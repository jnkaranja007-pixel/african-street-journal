Cameroon truckers resume Central African Republic fuel deliveries

Road traffic between Cameroon and the Central African Republic has restarted after a two-week strike by truckers protesting harassment by security forces.

Source: Radio Ndeke Luka · 2026-10-22 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Cameroonian transporters resumed their activities on October 20, ending a two-week strike that had halted the flow of fuel to the Central African Republic. The strike, which began in early October, was a protest against road harassment attributed to Central African security forces. During the stoppage, over 300 fuel tankers were immobilized at the border, impacting supply chains in both nations.

The resumption of traffic follows mediation talks held in Yaoundé between Cameroonian and Central African authorities. Both governments agreed to address the transporters' grievances to facilitate the return to normal operations. The first fuel tankers have since crossed the border at Cantonnier, heading towards Bangui, signaling a gradual restoration of fuel supplies.

Local officials, including the governor of Cameroon's East region and the sub-prefect of Baboua, met with transporters at Garoua-Boulaï to ensure a smooth restart. Assurances were given regarding the security of Cameroonian drivers in Central African territory, a key demand of the truckers. The transporters have committed to resuming their usual routes, aiming to re-establish the country's fuel supply.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic residents are affected by the resumption of fuel deliveries, which had been disrupted by the strike.
