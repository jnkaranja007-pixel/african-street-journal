Five Central African civil society leaders on hunger strike

Activists arrested on October 15 are now in gendarmerie custody, their health declining.

Five Central African civil society leaders arrested on October 15 in Bangui have begun a hunger strike in gendarmerie detention. Their deteriorating health has caused concern among investigators, who are reportedly angry with police colleagues over the transfer. The arrests occurred on October 15.

President Faustin-Archange Touadéra stated on Wednesday, October 21, that he would not intervene for the release of the five activists. He believes the decision rests with the justice system, which he expects to operate independently. The activists' current condition is worrying.

The leaders were detained in Bangui, the capital city. Their transfer to gendarmerie custody has led to anger among some investigators. President Touadéra's refusal to intervene highlights the justice system's expected autonomy.

Why it mattersFive civil society leaders are on a hunger strike in gendarmerie custody in Bangui.

Central African Republic forces attack rebels on home soil

Presidential spokesperson Albert Yaloké Mokpeme stated that operations against rebels occurred exclusively within Central African Republic territory.

Central African Republic's presidential spokesperson Albert Yaloké Mokpeme stated on October 22, 2026, that security operations against rebels occurred solely within the nation's borders. He affirmed there are no tensions with Chad, and the two heads of state communicate regularly to secure their shared frontier. Mokpeme specified that the FACA and their Russian allies acted against rebel leader Noureddine Adam's forces, supported by Chadian and Sudanese mercenaries, entirely within Central African Republic territory.

Mokpeme explained that the Defense and Security Forces effectively neutralized armed groups in the tri-border area of the Central African Republic, Chad, and Sudan, where large-scale attack preparations were discovered. He noted that FACA and their allies engaged in exchanges of fire, resulting in casualties on both sides, with some assailants fleeing into Chadian territory. The spokesperson warned that no bordering country will serve as a base for destabilizing forces.

"All those who attempt to disturb the peace of Central Africans will be severely dealt with by the FACA and their allies," Mokpeme declared. He emphasized that the operations were professional and aimed at neutralizing armed groups preparing for significant attacks.

Why it mattersThe presidential spokesperson confirmed that Central African Republic forces and their allies conducted military operations within the country's borders, directly affecting the security situation.

Central African Republic fuel traffic resumes after strike

Over 300 fuel trucks were held up for two weeks as Cameroonian transporters protested road harassment.

Cameroonian transporters resumed their routes on October 20 after a two-week strike over road harassment by Central African Republic security forces. The resumption follows mediation talks in Yaoundé between the two nations' authorities. Over 300 fuel tankers had been stranded at the border awaiting improved working conditions.

Yaoundé and Bangui agreed to the transporters' demands to facilitate the restart of fuel supplies to the Central African Republic. The first fuel trucks have now crossed the border at Cantonnier towards Bangui. Norbert Niéndé, sub-prefect of Baboua, stated that the situation had negative consequences for both countries.

The strike, which began in early October, disrupted the delivery of petroleum products to the Central African Republic. Amadou Bachirou, a transport union coordinator, welcomed assurances from authorities regarding the security of Cameroonian drivers in Central African territory. The governor of Cameroon's East region called for work to resume to restore fuel supplies.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic's fuel supply was disrupted for two weeks due to a strike by Cameroonian transporters.

Bangui hosts second African boxing tournament

The capital city will see boxers from Cameroon, Gabon, and Congo compete on October 31.

The Central African Republic capital, Bangui, is preparing to host the second African Boxing Tournament on October 31 at the Omnisports stadium. Organized by Bamara Boxe Pro Academy, Almadina Groupe, and Maison Russe, the event aims to promote boxing in the country and will feature professional, semi-professional, and amateur bouts. Athletes from Cameroon, Gabon, and Congo will compete alongside local boxers.

A key match will pit Central African fighter Kevin Dasso against Cameroon's Kola Sucré in the 75kg category. The program also includes two semi-professional and four amateur fights, showcasing young talents like Hergy Betoumbam and Merveil Amoloma. Maison Russe stated its commitment to supporting athletes' progress and increasing the sport's visibility.

Kevin Dasso expressed confidence and called for support from sponsors and fans. The tournament's ambition is to provide Central African boxers with an international platform and prepare them for future competitions, including the Olympic Games. The event is held under the patronage of President Faustin-Archange Touadéra.

Why it mattersCentral African boxers will compete for international recognition and Olympic preparation.

Touadéra advisor claims Paris plans assassination plot

Jules Njawé alleges France is orchestrating a media campaign to blame Rwanda for an assassination attempt on the president.

Jules Njawé, a presidential advisor in the Central African Republic, claimed on Freedom Africa TV that Paris is orchestrating a media campaign to prepare public opinion for an assassination attempt on President Faustin-Archange Touadéra. Njawé stated that France intends to blame Rwanda for the plot, escalating tensions between Bangui and Kigali.

These allegations follow reports from Corbeau News Centrafrique and an investigation by Africa Intelligence published on October 15. The Africa Intelligence report detailed the strained security alliances between Touadéra, Rwanda, and Kinshasa. A government member, speaking anonymously, confirmed that the palace felt compelled to respond to these reports.

Njawé's statements were made on the plateau of Freedom Africa TV, a platform he used to voice accusations against France. The specific details of the alleged assassination commando and the media campaign were revealed by Njawé during his televised appearance.

Why it mattersThe president of the Central African Republic is the subject of an alleged assassination plot.
