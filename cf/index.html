Central African Republic suspends classes at Lycée des Martyrs

Violence erupted at the school after a dispute over a stolen phone escalated, damaging buildings.

Classes at the Lycée des Martyrs in Bangui were suspended from November 5 to November 9 following violent incidents that saw official buildings vandalized. The violence began after a dispute between two students over a stolen mobile phone on November 2 escalated, drawing in outside individuals.

Minister of State for National Education, Aurélien Simplice Zingas Kongbelet, visited the school on Thursday, October 5, to assess the damage. He observed destroyed administrative offices, the library, and the headmaster's residence, with damaged furniture and scattered documents. The minister condemned the behavior, stating that the school must remain a place for learning and citizenship.

The minister announced that classes would officially resume on Monday, November 9, following a flag-raising ceremony. The temporary closure aims to secure the premises and prepare for the return of educational activities. The initial dispute involved one student allegedly seeking help from friends and acquaintances.

Why it mattersStudents and staff at the Lycée des Martyrs in Bangui are affected by the temporary closure of their school due to violence and vandalism.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Constitutional court validates Touadéra's election win

Faustin-Archange Touadéra's re-election with 77.9% of votes is confirmed by the constitutional court on January 19, 2026.

The Central African Republic's constitutional court validated Faustin-Archange Touadéra's re-election with 77.9% of the vote on January 19, 2026. This decision entrenches a presidential dictatorship in one of the world's poorest nations. The opposition boycotted the December 28, 2025, election, citing a lack of guarantees for a fair vote.

The National Election Authority announced Touadéra won with 76.15% on January 6, but opposition candidates Anicet-Georges Dologuélé and Henri-Marie Dondra contested the results, alleging manipulation of voter lists and ballot boxes. The court, presided over by Touadéra's uncle, adjusted the final score to 77.9% and dismissed appeals, leading the opposition to accuse the court of acting as a presidential tool.

A 2023 constitutional reform extended presidential terms from five to seven years and removed term limits, allowing Touadéra's third mandate until 2032. Russian Wagner mercenaries continue to operate, with reports of secret prisons in Bangui. Sporadic attacks by militias persist in the Haut-Mbomou and Mbomou regions.

Why it mattersThe constitutional court's validation of Faustin-Archange Touadéra's re-election and a 2023 reform extending presidential terms consolidate his power until 2032.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Central African army neutralizes Anti-Balaka leader Marcel Ndalé

Nine members of the Anti-Balaka group, including leader Marcel Ndalé, were killed in an operation supported by Russian allies.

Central African armed forces (Faca) killed nine Anti-Balaka members, including their leader Marcel Ndalé, in an operation on November 5 south of Pendéré in Nana-Mambéré. The defense ministry announced the successful operation on Friday, November 6, 2026, stating it was conducted with Russian allies.

The ministry accused Ndalé's group of robberies, murders of gold miners, traders, and other civilians. Marcel Ndalé, also known as Gbokaho Minang Sylvain and "King of the Bush," his brother Adamou Ndalé, and seven other fighters were killed. The group was allegedly preparing attacks on government forces and trucks from Cameroon on the Bouar–Béloko route.

The defense ministry stated the situation is under control but will continue sweeps to prevent fleeing fighters from regrouping. They urged residents to report any information useful to the zone's security. The group was also accused of planning incursions into Cameroon to provoke tensions and border closures.

Why it mattersThe operation against the Anti-Balaka group aims to stop violence against civilians and disrupt planned attacks on government forces and trade routes.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Civil society group defends planned November protests

The COSCIPAC group issued a statement on November 6, 2026, refuting Gbavini Ondimba's opposition to planned demonstrations.

The Coordination of Civil Society Organizations for Peace in the Central African Republic (COSCIPAC) released a statement on November 6, 2026, in Bangui, addressing Gbavini Ondimba. COSCIPAC is contesting Ondimba's stance against planned mobilization days and asserting the right of Central Africans to peacefully voice their discontent.

COSCIPAC specifically reproached Ondimba, who is reportedly still a member of their bureau, for attempting to discourage young Central Africans from participating in the mobilization days scheduled for November 9, 13, 16, and 18, 2026. The organization also questioned Ondimba's political history, listing his past affiliations with various political parties.

The statement details Ondimba's previous involvements with MLJC, François Bozizé's KNK, Charles Massi's FODEM, Cyriaque Gonda's PNCN, Alexandre-Ferdinand Nguendet's RPR, and Anicet-Georges Dologuélé's URCA in 2016, before he joined the ruling party Mouvement cœurs unis (MCU).

Why it mattersCentral Africans planning to participate in peaceful protests on November 9, 13, 16, and 18, 2026, are being supported by COSCIPAC against opposition from Gbavini Ondimba.

Culture · 2026-11-08

Central African Republic prepares for December 1st parade

Students in Bimbo practiced marching drills for the national day celebration on December 1, 2026.

Preparations for the December 1, 2026, national day parade are underway in the Central African Republic, with students in Bimbo, near Bangui, participating in marching practice sessions. The event commemorates the country's transition from Oubangui-Chari to the Central African Republic. Public and private schools mobilized students for the second preparatory session on Thursday, November 5.

Students, dressed in their school colours, marched to the rhythm of instruments. An educator from M'Poko Bac school, Brice Ngaïté, noted the good atmosphere but acknowledged the challenges in the early stages of preparation. Spectators observed some students struggling to maintain the marching rhythm, leading to a less harmonious display than in previous years, according to François Nglomoudé.

Despite the efforts of instructors, some students did not follow the marching cadence, prompting comments from attendees. Nglomoudé suggested that teachers need to demonstrate the correct marching rhythm. The preparations continue as the December 1st celebration approaches, with environmental beautification efforts yet to begin in Bimbo and its surrounding areas.

Why it mattersStudents and the general population are involved in preparing for a national day parade.
