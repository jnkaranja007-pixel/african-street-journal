Central African Republic threatens to ban BRDC platform

The government says the Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC) is acting illegally and will no longer be allowed to operate.

The Central African Republic government announced Monday it will no longer permit the Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC) to operate, citing its alleged illegal activities. The BRDC had issued a two-week ultimatum to the government with five demands, including the unconditional release of five civil society leaders and a reduction in fuel prices to under 850 FCFA per litre.

Government spokesperson Evariste Ngamana stated that the BRDC was created to obstruct the 2016 Constitution and should have dissolved after the 2023 referendum. He described the platform's continued actions as an act of rebellion, saying, "We cannot continue to accept someone who is in illegality continuing to disturb public order." The BRDC, through its coordinator Me Crépin Mboli-Goumba, responded that the government misunderstands the laws governing political parties in the country.

The BRDC's ultimatum followed a march against the high cost of living, where leaders presented their demands. If the government fails to respond, the BRDC has threatened to organize another demonstration. The government's decision to ban the platform comes amid ongoing political tensions and public discontent over economic conditions.

Why it mattersThe government's decision to ban the BRDC platform and its justification of rebellion directly impacts the freedom of expression and political organization for citizens.

Corridor 13 project nears 85% road completion

The 168 billion CFA franc project funded by the African Development Bank includes ports, schools, health centers, and roads linking Congo, CAR, and Chad.

The Corridor 13 project, connecting the Republic of Congo to the Central African Republic and Chad, is nearing 85% completion for its main road component. The African Development Bank (AfDB) is funding the 168 billion CFA franc initiative, which includes road works, port construction, schools, and health centers. A joint evaluation mission confirmed progress, with the section between Mbaïki and Gouga being the only one fully underway and showing concrete advances.

Former Minister of Equipment and Public Works, Éric Rokosse-Kamot, expressed satisfaction with most of the work, despite localized delays on unassigned lots. The project's seven components include the construction of the Moungoumba port with warehouses and economic activity zones, and support for its management. Related activities involve building schools, health centers, and a women's center to foster local development.

Future steps for Corridor 13 involve awarding contracts for the Gouga-Moungoumba section, which includes a 380-meter bridge over the Lobaye River, and a 30 km stretch to the Congolese border. Work is also planned for the Mbaïki-Bossongo section, though population compensation issues are delaying land acquisition. The project also includes rehabilitating the PK9 bridge for pedestrians and non-motorized users while constructing a new double-lane bridge for vehicles.

Why it mattersThe Corridor 13 project will improve transport and local development through new roads, a port, schools, and health facilities, with road works at 85% completion.

Bangui academic calls for calm amid tensions between government, opposition and civil s...

Arnaud Yaliki, a public law lecturer at the University of Bangui, urged citizens to respect differing opinions on the International Day of Tolerance.

Arnaud Yaliki, a lecturer in public law at the University of Bangui, called for calm on November 16, the International Day of Tolerance. He appealed amid tensions between the government, the opposition and some civil society organisations in the Central African Republic.

Yaliki said respect for divergent opinions is essential to building a lasting peace. He answered questions put to him by Christ Roi Jordan Bandéngbé for Radio Ndeke Luka.

Climate · 2026-11-17

Central African Republic presents six climate priorities at COP31

The nation advocates for climate justice, equitable financing, and recognition of indigenous peoples' roles.

The Central African Republic delegation presented its climate action plan at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, on November 15. The country's position focuses on six priorities, including climate justice, financing for adaptation, and valuing indigenous peoples. Minister of Environment and Sustainable Development Silla Sembala outlined Bangui's demands to international representatives, reaffirming the CAR's commitment to its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0) to preserve biodiversity.

Among the six priorities is climate justice, with the CAR, a low emitter of greenhouse gases, seeking recognition for the ecological role of the Congo Basin forests. The nation also calls for more accessible and equitable climate financing mechanisms tailored to its needs. Minister Sembala emphasized an approach that links environmental protection and biodiversity preservation with economic development and poverty reduction.

The valorization of indigenous peoples and their traditional knowledge for sustainable forest management is another key priority. Bangui also urges accelerated funding for climate adaptation, carbon finance, and tropical forest protection. COP31, which opened on November 9 and concludes on November 20, offers the CAR a platform to voice its environmental concerns internationally.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic is advocating for equitable climate financing and recognition of its forests' role in carbon capture at COP31, which concludes on November 20.
