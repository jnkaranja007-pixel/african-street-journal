President Touadéra dismisses legal advisor after protest criticism

William Ndjapou was removed from his post following public remarks criticizing the arrest of civil society leaders.

President Faustin-Archange Touadéra has dismissed William Freddy Kalmame Ndjapou, his legal advisor, according to a decree signed on Monday, October 19. This decision partially revokes a December 2021 decree that had appointed several individuals to the presidency. The dismissal specifically targets Ndjapou, who advised on legal reform and electoral process monitoring.

The action follows Ndjapou's public criticism on October 15 of the arrest of civil society leaders who had organized a "dead city" protest on October 12 against rising fuel prices. Ndjapou stated that the arrests were irregular and did not originate from the president, suggesting others around the head of state were responsible. He called for the leaders' release.

Five civil society leaders, including Elysée Nguémalé of the Observatory for Democratic Governance in Central Africa, were arrested by police as they prepared to hold a press conference. Their arrests occurred shortly after the "dead city" demonstration, which saw significant public participation. Other figures within the presidential camp, such as Special Advisor Fidèle Gouandjika and Minister Roméo Gribingui, had also voiced criticism of the arrests.

Why it mattersWilliam Ndjapou is affected because his criticism of government actions led to his dismissal from his presidential advisory role.

Newtech Institute Celebrates Student Success and Innovation

The institution awarded diplomas from Baccalaureate to Master's degrees and honored a robotics champion.

Newtech Institute in Bangui held a celebratory open house and solemn opening ceremony for its 2026 academic year on Saturday, October 17. The event included the graduation of the Émilie Béatrice Epaye class, with students receiving diplomas ranging from Baccalaureate to Master's levels. A special recognition was given to champion Girardot thanks Charline Reine for her achievements in a robotics competition held in South Korea.

Serge Singha Bengba, President and Founder of Newtech, congratulated the students on their excellent results in national exams for 2026. He emphasized education as a crucial investment for the youth and the nation's future, also acknowledging the dedication of teachers and parents. Charline Girardot, recognized as the institute's top student, was celebrated for her academic excellence and her representation of Central African youth at the international robotics event.

The institute's strategic choice to integrate robotics and artificial intelligence into its scientific curriculum from the high school level was highlighted. The promotion was named in honor of Émilie Béatrice Epaye, recognizing her distinguished career as a former deputy, minister, and her past achievements as an athlete and academic.

Why it mattersStudents in the Central African Republic are recognized for academic achievements and participation in international STEM competitions.

African Science Prize Awards Set for Bangui

The Faustin-Archange Touadéra Prize will honor five laureates in vital development sectors and award scholarships to Central African students.

The African Institute for Applied Multidisciplinary Research (IARPA) confirmed that the Faustin-Archange Touadéra Prize for African Scientific Excellence will take place in Bangui on November 26 and 27. The International Organizing Committee announced that this year's prize will recognize five laureates for their achievements in innovation and future technologies, sustainable agriculture and food sovereignty, public health, ecological transition, and green industrialization. Twelve candidates have been shortlisted for the awards.

In addition to the prizes, thirty scholarships will be offered to Central African students to pursue their studies in Côte d'Ivoire. Dr. Jean-Marie Koné, president of IARPA and the Organizing Committee, stated that this initiative aims to support the future of African scientific excellence by empowering its youth. The selection process for the scholarship recipients will involve a call for applications.

The committee also announced the upcoming launch of an international standard business school, "GESAO Business School Bangui," in January 2027. This project is being developed in collaboration with Dr. Henri-Marie Dondra. The prize, created under the patronage of the Head of State, was officially established on April 9.

Why it mattersThe event highlights scientific innovation and offers educational opportunities for Central African youth.

Bangui Hosts African Higher Education Agrégation Competition

Over 300 candidates from 15 African nations are competing in the 23rd CAMES aggregation contest, focusing on rigorous professional training.

Bangui is hosting the aggregation contest of the African and Malagasy Council for Higher Education (CAMES) for the first time. More than 300 candidates from 15 African countries are participating in this 23rd edition, which commenced on Monday. The competition spans five fields, including human medicine, pharmacy, and veterinary medicine, aiming to foster rigor and excellence in professional training.

The event is designed to promote high standards in the education of future professionals across the continent. Participants are vying for recognition in specialized areas, underscoring the importance of advanced academic and practical skills in these critical sectors.

This marks a significant occasion for the Central African Republic to showcase its capacity to host international academic events and contribute to the advancement of higher education standards across Africa.

Why it mattersThe competition affects professionals in medicine, pharmacy, and veterinary fields by promoting rigor and excellence in their training.

Wagner vehicles block Central African Republic road

Russian mercenary group's vehicles obstruct traffic and access to businesses in Zawa, complicating travel on National Road 1.

In Zawa, a town approximately 240 kilometers from Bangui on National Road 1, vehicles belonging to the Russian Wagner mercenary group are regularly blocking traffic lanes and circulation spaces. This haphazard parking significantly hinders the movement of travelers and impedes access to shops and offices situated along the route.

The town of Zawa is located between Yaloké and Bossemptélé on the main route connecting Bangui to western towns. Journalists observed that the obstruction is not a random occurrence. The armed individuals are parking their large vehicles and armored cars in the middle of the road, even positioning them in front of local transport agency facilities.

This situation disrupts the daily flow of transporters, motorcyclists, and all individuals who use this vital artery to travel between the capital and the western regions of the country. The presence of these vehicles creates a consistent impediment to local commerce and transit.

Why it mattersTravelers and local businesses in Zawa face daily disruptions due to blocked roads.
