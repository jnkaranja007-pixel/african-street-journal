Mboko residents denounce fees at Bangui checkpoint

Residents of Mboko in Bangui's 9th district report that defense and security agents at a checkpoint near the local town hall are demanding payments for certain procedures.

Residents in Mboko, a neighborhood in Bangui's 9th district, are expressing discontent over alleged fees imposed at a control barrier near their local town hall. According to testimonies gathered by Martial Stève Mbétissinga, agents from the defense and security forces stationed at this post are requiring payments for various formalities.

The reported practices at the checkpoint have caused dissatisfaction among a segment of the local population. These residents claim that the agents are leveraging their position to extract money for services that should not incur additional charges.

This situation highlights concerns about potential informal taxation and the conduct of security personnel at official checkpoints. The complaints from Mboko residents suggest a breakdown in trust and an economic burden on those who must pass through the barrier for daily activities.

Why it mattersResidents of Mboko are directly affected by having to pay unexpected fees for basic formalities, impacting their daily finances and trust in local authorities.

Student flees Bangui University after fake bank slip fraud discovery

Harol Beranger Toufeina, a student in anthropology, disappeared from the University of Bangui's Faculty of Letters after his alleged involvement in a fake bank slip scheme was.

A system of fake bank slips has been discovered at the Faculty of Letters and Human Sciences at the University of Bangui. Harol Beranger Toufeina, a student and cousin of Dean Jean-Claude Azoumaye, fled after the malversations were found in the schooling service, according to Corbeau News Centrafrique.

Toufeina's anthropology studies were nearing their end for almost a year when his cousin, Dean Jean-Claude Azoumaye, transferred him to the schooling service. The discovery of the fraudulent activity led to Toufeina's disappearance.

The scandal involves a widespread system of falsified bank slips, indicating a significant breach in financial procedures within the university's Faculty of Letters. The exact scale of the fraud and the number of affected students or transactions are not yet detailed.

Why it mattersStudents at the University of Bangui's Faculty of Letters are affected by potential financial irregularities and a breach of trust in the schooling service.

MSF withdrawal impacts Bangui Community Hospital maternity services

The director of Bangui Community Hospital, Abel Assaye, discussed the effects of Médecins Sans Frontières' departure on maternity ward operations and women's care.

The maternity ward at Bangui Community Hospital is experiencing changes in its operations and the care provided to women following the withdrawal of Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF). Hospital director Abel Assaye addressed these impacts in an interview with Astrid de Bonheur Kolengué.

Assaye detailed how MSF's departure has altered the hospital's capacity to manage maternity services. The organization had previously played a role in supporting the functioning of the ward and the provision of care for women seeking medical attention.

The discussion focused specifically on the practical consequences for the hospital's ability to maintain its previous level of service in the maternity department. The interview aimed to clarify the current situation for patients and staff at the facility.

Why it mattersWomen in Bangui who rely on the Community Hospital for maternity services are directly affected by potential changes in care provision and hospital operations.

Moloua's Security Plan Fails to Curb Rising Violence

Three months after Prime Minister Félix Moloua's security assurances, armed attacks, kidnappings, and killings continue to plague several regions of the Central African Republic.

Security in the Central African Republic has deteriorated, despite Prime Minister Félix Moloua's earlier commitment to deputies to restore peace using national forces, MINUSCA, and Russian and Rwandan allies. Recent events, including the June 29 killing of Vicar Crépin Monga in Haut-Mbomou by A Zandés Ani Kpi Gbé militiamen, highlight the ongoing instability.

The situation is particularly dire in the northeast, where a June 30 attack by the ASP rebel coalition on Amdafock resulted in at least 28 deaths and displaced nearly 18,000 civilians, according to the UN. Near Birao, unidentified armed men attacked Matala village, causing deaths and injuries, as reported by Moussa Radjab, deputy mayor of Birao.

Pockets of insecurity also persist in Ombella-Mpoko and Ouham-Fafa, where elements assimilated to the 3R terrorize residents. Near Bangui, a Swiss geologist and his aide were assassinated on July 30 at the Gbango-Carrière mining site. The government is responding by strengthening the national army, with 600 new recruits presented on August 10.

Why it mattersThe ongoing violence and displacement directly affect the safety and livelihoods of residents across multiple regions, including farmers abandoning their fields and civilians fleeing their homes.

Politics · 08/26/2026

Former Deputy Mara States CAR Lacks National Culture

Jean-Pierre Mara, former deputy of Mala, asserted that the Central African Republic has never developed a common cultural project since its modern state formation.

Former deputy Jean-Pierre Mara claimed on November seven, two thousand twenty-five, that the Central African Republic has not built its own national culture. He stated that the country's founding president, Barthélemy Boganda, initially envisioned a federal entity for French Equatorial Africa, but this project failed due to a lack of regional consensus. Boganda then named a limited territory the Central African Republic without a strong cultural foundation.

Mara argued that this fragile beginning led to a state based on strictly political logic, adopting imported institutions without deep connections to local practices. He noted that successive constitutions mirrored Western models, ignoring the languages, rites, and specific needs of the Central African populations.

According to Mara, a national culture requires a mastered language, shared norms, and artistic forms representing a collective. He concluded that the Central African Republic lacks these conditions, citing linguistic disunity, limited written Sango, and ethnic diversity as barriers to building a common cultural base, which hinders the state's ability to mobilize citizens and implement lasting public policies.

Why it mattersThe absence of a national culture, as described by Jean-Pierre Mara, prevents the state from effectively mobilizing its citizens and implementing durable public policies, directly affecting all residents.
