Gendarmerie destroys illicit drinks near Bangui

National gendarmerie and allied forces incinerated a large quantity of confiscated counterfeit beverages in Pani village.

National gendarmerie forces, supported by allied troops, destroyed a significant quantity of illicit alcoholic beverages this week in Pani village, north of Bangui. The confiscated drinks had been seized during various security operations and stored at the gendarmerie headquarters before their incineration.

Symphorien Ludovic Malékatcha reported on the operation, which saw the illicit products burned. The destruction took place at the village of Pani, located on the northern outskirts of the capital city.

The operation targeted counterfeit drinks that posed a public health risk. The gendarmerie stated these products were seized in prior security actions.

Why it mattersThe destruction of illicit drinks affects public health in the Bangui region.

Rotary Club aids polio fight in Bangui

Community health workers trained to find polio cases after two were detected in Ouango.

The Rotary Club Bangui Douain Rénové and partners are supporting the Central African Republic's Ministry of Public Health to combat polio after two cases were found in Ouango, Bangui. Community health agents received training on Monday, October 26, to improve surveillance and the search for suspected cases.

Émelien Gérard Nerguidima of the Rotary Club stated that the agents will now identify potential polio cases, such as acute flaccid paralysis in children, in their neighborhoods. This initiative aims to detect cases early and alert health centers, building on the club's commitment to fighting polio in the country. The World Health Organization and UNICEF are also involved.

Since the start of 2026, seven polio cases have been recorded in the Central African Republic, with two in Ouango. This training follows World Polio Day on October 24 and aims to sustain efforts beyond the awareness day. The global polio eradication initiative is a collaboration between the Ministry of Public Health, Rotary Club, WHO, and UNICEF.

Why it mattersThe detection of two polio cases in Ouango, Bangui, has led to the training of community health workers to identify and report suspected cases, enhancing public health surveillance.

Wagner-backed CAR forces eye Chad border

Bangui fears France will use Chad as a base to overthrow Central African Republic's government.

Tensions are high on the border between the Central African Republic (CAR) and Chad, with reports of imminent military confrontation. Observers note alarming signals of conflict between Bangui's forces, supported by Russian Wagner mercenaries and Sudanese FSR paramilitaries, and the Chadian army.

CAR's government, through advisor Jules Njawe and spokesperson Maxime Mokpeme Yaloké, claims France might use Chad as a rear base to destabilize the CAR. They believe N'Djamena is a springboard for a Western counter-offensive against the Russian presence in Africa, centered in Bangui.

This perceived threat has led Touadéra and his Russian allies to prepare for a decisive response. The populations on both sides of the artificial border are reportedly concerned about potential famine and forced displacement.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic citizens face potential death, famine, and displacement due to escalating tensions and possible conflict on their border with Chad.

Alumni donate 32 novels to Bangui school

The Association of LAENS Alumni provided literary works to the Lycée d’Application de l’ENS library.

The Lycée d’Application de l’École Normale Supérieure in Bangui received 32 literary novels on Monday, October 26, 2026. The donation came from the Association of Alumni of the Lycée d’Application de l’ENS (2AELAENS). This action follows the association's previous support for the school's library.

Nadège Edang-Ndi, president of 2AELAENS, stated that the 32 books cover all levels from sixth grade to final year. She expressed happiness with the collection, noting the books will aid students in research, understanding lessons, and passing exams. Arlette Djohodomo, a school official, thanked the alumni and urged students to preserve the novels.

The donated books include major literary works such as Émile Zola's Germinal and Aimé Césaire's La Tragédie du roi Christophe. The alumni association aims to improve study conditions and intellectual development for young students. This donation encourages reading, research, and cultural openness.

Why it mattersStudents at the Lycée d’Application de l’ENS in Bangui will benefit from new literary resources to aid their studies and cultural enrichment.

Luka-Luka links president's health to political conduct

A public figure claims hypertension medication and aphrodisiacs affect President Touadéra's political actions.

Madame Luka-Luka stated that President Faustin Archange Touadéra's physical health is directly connected to his political management in Bangui. She explained that regular hypertension medication often causes reduced libido and erectile dysfunction.

According to Luka-Luka, overcoming these physical issues necessitates the use of aphrodisiacs, including substances like Viagra. She suggested this biological and behavioral pattern, known to health specialists, is now influencing the highest levels of public office.

Luka-Luka made these remarks during a public address where she discussed aspects of power management in the capital. The specific details of the president's health and his alleged use of aphrodisiacs were presented as a factor in his political conduct.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic citizens may be concerned about the health and personal choices of their president affecting his governance.
