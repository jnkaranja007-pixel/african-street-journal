Central African Republic hosts 23rd medical exams

More than 600 participants from various African countries are in Bangui for the 23rd session of the Council of African and Malagasy Higher Education (CAMES) aggregation exams.

The Central African Republic is hosting the 23rd session of the aggregation exams in human and animal medicine, organized by the Council of African and Malagasy Higher Education (CAMES). The official launch ceremony took place in Bangui on October 19, 2026, with the exams running from October to November. Over 600 participants are attending, including nine candidates from the Central African Republic, an increase from previous years. Those who pass will attain the rank of university professor.

A delegation from the CAMES General Secretariat visited the Central African Republic from October 18 to 21, 2026, to assess teaching methods and quality at the University of Bangui. The delegation met with the rector of the university and the minister of Higher Education. Boniface Koffi, president of the jury coordination, noted the progress in the country's participation, highlighting the increased number of female candidates.

The exams aim to promote qualified teacher-researchers capable of training new generations, advancing research, and meeting human resource needs in health sectors. The process includes online preliminary tests and in-person evaluations in Bangui from November 10 to 18. The CAMES emphasizes ethical conduct and impartiality from the juries, with the goal of improving human and animal health sectors.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic is hosting an event that will advance the qualifications of its medical researchers and educators.

Civil society leaders on hunger strike in gendarmerie

Five Central African Republic civil society leaders are on a hunger strike in gendarmerie custody after being denied food and water by police.

Five Central African Republic civil society leaders arrested on October 15 in Bangui have begun a hunger strike at the gendarmerie. Their health is a concern for investigators, who are now angry with police colleagues. The leaders were detained by police for two days without food or water before being transferred to the gendarmerie in a weakened state.

The transfer of the five detained civil society leaders to the gendarmerie's Section de recherche et d’investigation (SRI) has angered several gendarmes. The detainees, including Quentin Claver Ngouando of the iGwé movement and Élysée Nguemale of the OGDC, are now refusing food and water in protest of their treatment.

The five individuals are Quentin Claver Ngouando, Élysée Nguemale, Stève Bendo Deny, Barry Mardios, and Igor Mokpem. Their detention and subsequent hunger strike highlight tensions between different branches of law enforcement and raise concerns about the treatment of civil society figures in the country.

Why it mattersCivil society leaders are affected by being arrested and denied basic necessities, leading to a hunger strike.

Cameroon truckers resume Central African Republic fuel deliveries

Road traffic between Cameroon and the Central African Republic has restarted after a two-week strike by truckers protesting harassment by security forces.

Cameroonian transporters resumed their activities on October 20, ending a two-week strike that had halted the flow of fuel to the Central African Republic. The strike, which began in early October, was a protest against road harassment attributed to Central African security forces. During the stoppage, over 300 fuel tankers were immobilized at the border, impacting supply chains in both nations.

The resumption of traffic follows mediation talks held in Yaoundé between Cameroonian and Central African authorities. Both governments agreed to address the transporters' grievances to facilitate the return to normal operations. The first fuel tankers have since crossed the border at Cantonnier, heading towards Bangui, signaling a gradual restoration of fuel supplies.

Local officials, including the governor of Cameroon's East region and the sub-prefect of Baboua, met with transporters at Garoua-Boulaï to ensure a smooth restart. Assurances were given regarding the security of Cameroonian drivers in Central African territory, a key demand of the truckers. The transporters have committed to resuming their usual routes, aiming to re-establish the country's fuel supply.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic residents are affected by the resumption of fuel deliveries, which had been disrupted by the strike.

Education · 2026-10-22

Newtech Institut graduates celebrate academic success

The Bangui-based institute awarded diplomas from bachelor's to master's degrees, recognizing top students and a robotics champion.

Newtech Institut in Bangui celebrated its 2026 academic year with an open house, solemn opening ceremony, and graduation for the Émilie Béatrice Epaye class on Saturday, October 17. Diplomas were conferred for studies ranging from Baccalaureate to Master's levels. The event also honored Charline Girardot, a student recognized for her achievements in a South Korean robotics competition.

Serge Singha Bengba, President Founder of Newtech, congratulated students on their excellent national exam results, emphasizing education as a crucial investment for the country's future. He also acknowledged the dedication of teachers and parents. Charline Girardot, the institute's top student, was highlighted for her academic excellence, including a Baccalaureate in the challenging Series C, and her representation of the Central African Republic in the international robotics contest.

The institute's strategic decision to integrate robotics and artificial intelligence into its scientific curriculum from the secondary level was reiterated. The promotion was named in honor of Émilie Béatrice Epaye, recognizing her distinguished career as a four-time elected deputy, minister, and her past achievements as a track and field athlete and academic.

Why it mattersStudents and their families are directly affected by the academic achievements and recognition of their educational progress.

Central African Republic hosts second African boxing tournament

Bangui prepares to welcome athletes from Cameroon, Gabon, and Congo for a multi-category boxing event.

The Central African Republic's capital, Bangui, is set to host the second edition of the African Boxing Tournament. The event, organized by Bamara Boxe Pro Academy, Almadina Groupe, and Maison Russe, will feature professional, semi-professional, and amateur bouts. Athletes from Cameroon, Gabon, and Congo will compete alongside local boxers, aiming to elevate the sport's profile in the nation.

A key highlight will be the 75kg professional fight between Central African Republic's Kevin Dasso and Cameroon's Kola Sucré. The program also includes two semi-professional and four amateur fights, showcasing emerging talents like Hergy Betoumbam and Merveil Amoloma. The tournament aims to provide Central African boxers with an international platform and prepare them for future competitions, including the Olympics.

The tournament is scheduled for October 31 at the Omnisports Stadium in Bangui. Kevin Dasso expressed confidence and called for support from sponsors and fans. Maison Russe, a partner, stated its commitment to athlete development and increasing the sport's visibility. The event seeks to establish the Central African Republic as a hub for sporting talent on the continent.

Why it mattersCentral African boxers will gain international exposure and preparation for major events like the Olympics.
