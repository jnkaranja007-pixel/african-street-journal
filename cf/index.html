Bangui protesters demand lower fuel prices

Hundreds marched in the capital against the high cost of living and fuel prices, issuing a 14-day ultimatum.

Several hundred people marched in Bangui on Friday, November 13, 2026, to protest the rising cost of living and fuel prices. The demonstration was organized by the Republican Bloc for the Defense of the Constitution (BRDC) and was monitored by national security forces and MINUSCA. The march began at the Marabéna crossroads, proceeded along the Avenue des Martyrs, and concluded at the Martyrs roundabout.

The BRDC is demanding lower fuel prices, the release of detained activists, and respect for public freedoms. The opposition group issued a 14-day ultimatum to the Central African Republic government to meet their demands. Five activists were arrested on October 15, following a call for a day of inactivity on October 12.

The protest aimed to denounce the high cost of living and the recent increase in fuel prices. The BRDC's demands include the liberation of detainees and a reduction in fuel costs. The mobilization started early on Friday morning.

Why it mattersThe BRDC has given the government 14 days to lower fuel prices and release activists.

Families of two young men abducted by Wagner mercenaries in Bangui still have no news

Khalifa, 28, and another young Central African were taken in August 2026 in Bangui, and nearly three months later their relatives do not know if they are alive.

Two young Central Africans, Khalifa, 28, and another young man, were abducted in Bangui in August 2026 by Russian mercenaries of the Wagner group. Nearly three months on, they have not returned to their families, who do not know whether they are alive or where they are being held.

Khalifa lived in the Golf neighbourhood in the PK11 area, on the northern outskirts of Bangui, and was part of a group of young Central Africans working for the Wagner mercenaries. Relatives of Khalifa told Corbeau News Centrafrique that they have received no word from him since he disappeared, and that no precise explanation has been given to them.

Corbeau News Centrafrique first revealed the case in August 2026. As of Wednesday, 11 November 2026, the families of both missing men still have no information about what happened to them.

Why it mattersRelatives of both men are left without any word on whether they are alive or where they are held.

UN Security Council extends MINUSCA mission in Central African Republic to 2027

The Security Council voted unanimously in New York on November 12 to keep the UN peacekeeping mission in the country until November 15, 2027.

The UN Security Council unanimously renewed the mandate of the UN Multidimensional Integrated Stabilization Mission in the Central African Republic (MINUSCA) on November 12 in New York. The mission is now authorised to operate in the country until November 15, 2027.

Yaye Nabo Sène, spokesperson for the mission, welcomed the decision and said it matters for the Central African Republic. In an interview with Radio Ndeke Luka, she went through the main issues raised by the renewal.

The report does not give further details on the mission's next steps or on the issues she raised, so the report ends there for now.

Why it mattersThe renewal keeps UN peacekeepers deployed in the country for another year, with the mandate now running to November 15, 2027.

Politics · 2026-11-14

Opposition march draws fewer than 400 people

The Bloc Républicain pour la Défense de la Constitution's planned mass demonstration in Bangui failed to attract significant public support.

Less than 400 people, many of them relatives of opposition leaders, participated in a march organized by the Bloc Républicain pour la Défense de la Constitution (BRDC) on the Avenue des Martyrs in Bangui. The demonstration, intended to be a major historical event, was described as a fiasco by observers, highlighting the limited popularity of opponents to President Faustin Archange Touadera's regime.

BRDC leaders had called on the population to join the march, but their hopes for a large turnout were unmet. The organizers failed to leverage the global rise in fuel prices as an argument against the government, instead blaming the conflict in the Middle East, which did not convince Central Africans to take to the streets. Citizen Magloire stated that marching with leaders responsible for the people's suffering would not solve the country's problems.

The low mobilization in Bangui suggests public disapproval of the BRDC, which many consider an organization that serves only its own selfish interests. Civil society organizations had asked citizens to go about their daily activities, and the BRDC's isolation from the public was confirmed by the event's outcome. The march proceeded without enthusiasm, with BRDC members posing for international television cameras.

Why it mattersThe BRDC's failed demonstration in Bangui indicates a lack of public support for opposition movements challenging President Touadera's government.

Youth cultivate vegetables for survival in Nzila

Young people in the Central African Republic are transforming hectares of land into vegetable farms to feed their families and pay for school.

In Nzila, Bangui-Fleuve sub-prefecture, vegetable farming has become a matter of survival for young people. Every afternoon, dozens of youths tend to hectares of land, growing vegetables to feed their families and pay for their education. The most common crop is amaranth, which grows quickly and is less expensive to cultivate.

Saddrack Mala, a young farmer, explained that while amaranth grows within three days, it takes three weeks to sell, with a plot costing 2,500 F CFA. Carrots take three months to mature and sell for 8,000 F CFA per plot, a price that discourages many. Preparing a 8m plot for carrots costs 8,000 F CFA, a significant expense for these young cultivators.

Despite the challenges, this horticultural activity sustains families and maintains vegetable cultivation in the Central African Republic. A mother, who wished to remain anonymous, complained that the effort is not adequately valued. The practice of growing vegetables is considered beneficial for health, according to medical advice.

Why it mattersYoung farmers in Nzila are cultivating vegetables to earn money for food and school fees, with plots costing up to 8,000 F CFA.
