Central African Republic expels students over school violence

Students involved in violence at Lycée des Martyrs in Bangui will be expelled and barred from re-enrolling.

Students identified as responsible for violence at Bangui's Lycée des Martyrs will be expelled and banned from enrolling in any other school in the Central African Republic. The announcement came on Saturday, November 7, from Minister of State for National Education, Aurélien Simplice Zingas, during a press conference in Bangui. He also condemned the destruction and looting of school property.

Zingas stated that perpetrators of these acts of vandalism would face sanctions. The minister's remarks were made during a press conference where he detailed the disciplinary measures following the unrest. The violence occurred the previous week at the Lycée des Martyrs.

The minister's announcement addresses the immediate aftermath of the violence at the school. The government has indicated that further sanctions will be applied to those found responsible for the damage to school infrastructure.

Why it mattersStudents in Bangui face expulsion and exclusion from further education due to their involvement in school violence.

Health · 2026-11-09

Batangafo mayor appeals for hospital aid

The mayor of Batangafo Centre has asked the government to help save the prefectural hospital as MSF prepares to leave.

The mayor of Batangafo Centre, Patrick Thierry Max Belleka, urgently appealed to the Central African government to prevent the collapse of the Batangafo prefectural hospital. Belleka requested the immediate return of the hospital's ambulance and an audit of medication management in a statement dated November 2, 2026. This plea comes as Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF), which has supported the facility's operations, is set to depart permanently.

Belleka, who is also the president of the Communal Health Committee, directed his appeal to the minister of Ouham-Fafa, the Minister of Health and Population, the Prime Minister, and President Faustin-Archange Touadéra. He seeks their intervention to ensure the continued provision of healthcare services and to safeguard the hospital's remaining resources.

The mayor's statement aims to secure government support to maintain the hospital's functionality following MSF's withdrawal. The departure of the medical aid group raises concerns about the future of healthcare access for the local population.

Why it mattersThe prefectural hospital in Batangafo faces potential service collapse, affecting local residents' access to healthcare.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Sudan conflict threatens CAR's Vakaga region

Refugee flows and arms trafficking fuel armed groups in the impoverished northern area, according to a new report.

The devastating three-and-a-half-year conflict in neighbouring Sudan is poised to turn the Vakaga region in the far north of the Central African Republic into a hub of regional destabilisation, the International Crisis Group stated in a report released Thursday, October 29.

In this remote and neglected mining region, the influx of refugees, incursions by fighters, and the circulation of weapons are enabling the reconstitution of Central African armed groups, the report claims.

The International Crisis Group's findings highlight the potential for spillover effects from the Sudanese conflict into the already fragile Central African Republic.

Why it mattersThe conflict in Sudan risks destabilising the Vakaga region of the Central African Republic by fostering armed groups.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Central African Republic government blocks protest march

The government cited the BRDC's lack of legal status for its decision to deny permission for the demonstration.

The Central African Republic government has refused to authorize a protest march planned for November 13 in Bangui by the Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC). The political platform intended to gather supporters to protest the high cost of living and fuel shortages across the nation.

Government spokesperson Évariste Ngamana stated on Saturday, November 7, that the BRDC does not possess the legal standing required to organize a public demonstration. He asserted that the platform is operating outside the established legal framework.

The BRDC's planned march aimed to highlight citizen concerns over rising prices and difficulties in obtaining fuel. The government's refusal means the demonstration will not proceed as organized by the BRDC.

Why it mattersThe government's decision prevents citizens from protesting the high cost of living and fuel shortages.

Politics · 2026-11-09

CPC denies general Ndalé's death after defence ministry announcement

The rebel group claims seven fighters died in an attack, contradicting official reports of the general's demise.

The Coalition des patriotes pour le changement (CPC) has denied the death of General Ndalé, an officer whose demise was announced by the Central African Republic's Ministry of Defence. The rebel movement stated that seven of its fighters, including two of the general's brothers, were killed during an attack on their camp.

Léopold Bara, the CPC's delegate for external relations, issued a statement refuting the official version of events. He asserted that General Ndalé is alive and well, and intends to continue his activities. The Ministry of Defence's director of cabinet had previously announced the rebel officer's death, a report that had been widely circulated by local media and social networks.

The CPC insists that the announcement of General Ndalé's death is false and that he successfully evaded the attack on his encampment. The group maintains that the general is in good health.
