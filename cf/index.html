Mboko residents denounce fees at checkpoint near town hall

Residents in Mboko, Bangui's 9th district, report that defense and security agents at a checkpoint near the local town hall are demanding payments for certain procedures.

Residents of Mboko are expressing discontent over alleged fees imposed at a checkpoint situated near their local town hall. According to their testimonies, agents from the defense and security forces stationed at this post are requiring payments for various formalities.

The situation has prompted complaints from a segment of the population in the 9th arrondissement of Bangui. These residents claim that the practices at the barrier are causing dissatisfaction among those who must pass through it.

The reports from Mboko residents highlight concerns about the conduct of security personnel at the checkpoint. Martial Stève Mbétissinga collected these testimonies, indicating a growing public grievance regarding the alleged imposition of fees.

Why it mattersResidents of Mboko are directly affected by having to pay additional, unexpected fees for unspecified formalities at a local checkpoint.

CEMAC graduates 37 customs agents in Bangui

The 40th class of the CEMAC Inter-State Customs School saw 37 of 47 trainees successfully complete their ten-month training in Bangui, receiving diplomas on Saturday.

Thirty-seven trainees from the 40th class of the CEMAC Inter-State Customs School graduated in Bangui on Saturday, marking a 79% success rate after a ten-month training program. The graduates, from Cameroon, Central African Republic, Congo, and Chad, received their diplomas at a ceremony attended by the CEMAC Commission president, Balthazar Engonga Edjo’o, and Central African authorities.

The training, which included 750 hours of instruction and a one-month practical internship, covered sixteen subjects, including ten customs-specific and six general knowledge courses. Forty-seven candidates were selected for the academic year 2025-2026 from 398 applicants for the agent level, following competitive exams held in CEMAC member states in October 2024.

President Edjo’o emphasized the importance of customs training for sub-regional economic integration, citing the need to optimize fiscal and budgetary revenues, facilitate sub-regional trade, and secure borders. He also acknowledged the Central African authorities for enabling the school's return to its statutory headquarters in Bangui.

Why it mattersThe graduation of these customs agents directly impacts trade and revenue collection across the CEMAC region, affecting businesses and citizens involved in cross-border commerce.

Harol Beranger Toufeina flees Bangui University after fraud discovery

A system of false bank slips at the Faculty of Letters in Bangui has been uncovered, leading to the disappearance of Harol Beranger Toufeina, a relative of Dean Jean-Claude.

Harol Beranger Toufeina, cousin of Dean Jean-Claude Azoumaye, has fled after the discovery of malversations within the enrollment service at the Faculty of Letters of Bangui University. Corbeau News Centrafrique reported a large-scale system of false bank slips, which came to light following Toufeina's disappearance.

Toufeina, an anthropology student whose studies were nearing completion, had been transferred to the enrollment service by his cousin, Dean Azoumaye. This transfer occurred despite his academic career being close to its end for nearly a year, according to Corbeau News Centrafrique.

The fraud involved a vast system of fake bank slips, impacting the administrative and financial integrity of the university's Faculty of Letters. The exact scale of the malversations and the number of affected students or transactions were not specified in the report.

Why it mattersStudents and the university community are affected by the discovery of fraud within the enrollment service, potentially compromising academic integrity and financial processes.

Abel Assaye, director of Bangui's Community Hospital, addressed the effects of Médecins Sans Frontières' (MSF) departure on the facility's maternity ward and women's care.

The withdrawal of Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) has impacted the operations of the maternity ward and the provision of care for women at Bangui's Community Hospital. Hospital director Abel Assaye discussed these effects in an interview with Astrid de Bonheur Kolengué.

Assaye's comments focused on the changes observed since MSF ceased its involvement. The discussion centered on how the hospital is now managing the services previously supported by the international organization.

The interview aimed to clarify the current situation regarding maternal health services and the broader care available to women at the Community Hospital following MSF's departure.

News · 2026-08-25

Moloua government faces security challenges across Central African Republic

Three months after Prime Minister Félix Moloua's appointment, armed attacks and kidnappings persist in several regions, despite efforts to improve security.

The security situation in the Central African Republic has not improved, with armed attacks, kidnappings, and killings continuing across multiple regions. Prime Minister Félix Moloua, who was reappointed on May fifteen, had expressed confidence in the national armed forces, MINUSCA, and Russian and Rwandan allies to restore peace. However, this hope is diminishing as violence escalates.

In the Haut-Mbomou region, exactions by A Zandés Ani Kpi Gbé militiamen led to the death of Crépin Monga, the vicar of Zémio parish, on June twenty-nine. Repeated kidnappings by the LRA near Obo and Bambouti are also increasing terror. An attack by the ASP rebel coalition on Amdafock on June thirty resulted in at least twenty-eight deaths and displaced nearly eighteen thousand civilians, according to the UN.

Pockets of insecurity also remain in Ombella-Mpoko and Ouham-Fafa, where elements of the 3R group reportedly terrorize residents of Boali, Bouca, and Batangafo. Near Bangui, a Swiss geologist and his aide were assassinated on July thirty near Damara. In response, the government presented nearly six hundred new recruits to the national army on August ten at Camp Kassaï in Bangui.

Why it mattersThe ongoing insecurity directly impacts residents through violence, displacement, and the disruption of daily life and economic activities.
