Central African Republic launches first National Tree and Forest Week

President Faustin Archange Touadera inaugurated the initiative in Bangui, aiming to promote afforestation and reforestation as a solution to climate change.

The Central African Republic launched its first National Tree and Forest Week (SNAF) on Monday, August 24, 2026, under the patronage of President Faustin Archange Touadera. The event, presided over by Prime Minister Félix Moloua, began with a tree planting ceremony on Bas-Oubangui hill in Bangui's seventh district, symbolizing a commitment to environmental health for residents and the planet.

This initiative, themed "Trees and forests for large-scale afforestation and reforestation: a solution for sustainable development," is part of a national strategy to combat climate change. Government officials emphasized that the SNAF aims to address deforestation and ecological fragility, which increase vulnerabilities in the country, by promoting collective mobilization.

The SNAF is intended to be an annual event, with activities planned throughout the week, including awareness campaigns, educational workshops, and community reforestation efforts. The government states its goal is to make every citizen an active participant in the fight against climate change, positioning environmental protection at the core of public policy for a greener future.

Why it mattersThe launch of the National Tree and Forest Week directly affects citizens by involving them in reforestation efforts and aiming to improve environmental health in the Central African Republic.

Bangui's Fondö residents decry months-long power outage

Residents in Bangui's Fondö district report being without electricity for several months, disrupting daily life and increasing insecurity concerns.

The Fondö district in Bangui's 3rd arrondissement has been without electricity for several months, according to residents. They attribute the ongoing power cut to Énergie centrafricaine (ENERCA), the national power utility, and state that the lack of power is severely impacting their daily routines and activities.

The prolonged outage has also heightened residents' fears regarding insecurity in the area. They are calling on ENERCA to restore electricity services promptly to alleviate the disruptions and address the safety concerns that have emerged due to the darkness.

This situation highlights a significant challenge for the community, as the absence of a reliable power supply affects both personal well-being and local commerce. The residents' appeal underscores the urgent need for intervention from the power company.

Why it mattersResidents of Fondö are directly affected by the lack of electricity, which disrupts their daily activities and increases their concerns about insecurity.

Central African Republic health sector faces persistent challenges

More than 100 days after the new government took office, citizens report ongoing issues with medicine shortages, insufficient medical staff, and high consultation costs.

The Central African Republic's health sector continues to struggle with significant difficulties, despite government efforts to rehabilitate some hospitals and announce new projects. Citizens across the country, including in Bangui and provincial cities, report that conditions for patient care have not substantially improved.

Patients frequently encounter medicine stockouts and a shortage of healthcare personnel in various health facilities. Simon-Pierre in Bangui acknowledged government efforts but stated that improvements remain insufficient, noting that the situation at the community hospital is largely unchanged since Médecins sans frontières departed.

Civil society organizations, such as "I Gué," led by Quentin Ngbouando, assess the outcomes as falling short of expectations. They highlight the high cost of care, maternal and neonatal deaths, inadequate medical equipment, and staff shortages as serious concerns, attributing these issues to the state's limited action in the sector.

Why it mattersCentral African citizens, particularly the vulnerable, are directly affected by the lack of affordable medicine, insufficient medical staff, and high costs of healthcare, impacting their well-being and access to essential services.

CZ Empire trains Bangui residents in gift and money bouquet creation

CZ Empire is conducting a three-day workshop in Bangui to teach practical skills in crafting surprise gifts and money bouquets, aiming to foster income-generating activities.

CZ Empire, led by CEO Carole Zokoua, launched a three-day training workshop on Monday, August 24, at the Federation of Associations of Women Entrepreneurs of Central Africa (FAFECA) headquarters in Bangui. The initiative focuses on teaching participants how to make surprise gifts and money bouquets, with the goal of transforming creativity into entrepreneurial opportunities.

Over twenty people registered for the training, and more than fifteen attended the first day despite heavy rains in Bangui. The workshop is open to everyone, but young people and women, especially young girls, represent the majority of participants. Carole Zokoua, who received similar training abroad, aims to share her knowledge to help others start their own businesses.

Participants will learn the various steps involved in creating these items, with the objective of enabling them to produce their own creations by the end of the training. Certificates of participation will be awarded, providing attendees with a skill that can become a source of income, according to Zokoua.

Why it mattersThis training directly affects young people and women in Bangui by providing them with practical skills to create their own income-generating activities.

Bangui Criminal Court sentences Jean Kolaga to 10 years hard labor

The court in Bangui convicted Jean Kolaga for fatal assault and ordered him to pay 5 million CFA francs in damages to the civil party.

Jean Kolaga received a 10-year sentence of hard labor from the Bangui Criminal Court on August 25 for committing fatal assault. This judgment was rendered during the first criminal session of the year in Bangui, as reported by Christ-Roi Jordan Bandengbe.

In addition to the prison sentence, Kolaga is required to pay 5 million CFA francs to the civil party. This financial compensation is intended for damages and interest related to the case.

The ongoing criminal session in Bangui continues to address legal matters within the Central African Republic's justice system, with this conviction marking a significant outcome for the court.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic's justice system is affected by this ruling, as it demonstrates the court's ability to prosecute and sentence individuals for violent crimes.
