CAR opposition leader criticizes foreign troops in election

Anicet-Georges Dologuélé says Russian Wagner and Rwandan forces secured the December 28, 2025 presidential vote.

Opposition leader Anicet-Georges Dologuélé analyzed the security conditions for the December 28, 2025 presidential and legislative elections in the Central African Republic. He criticized the government's absolute reliance on foreign military and paramilitary forces.

Dologuélé, leader of URCASA, stated that the joint deployment of Russian Wagner units and Rwandan special forces aimed to control the internal political space during the recent election. The presence of these external contingents has become a central debate.

The opposition leader's comments highlight concerns about foreign influence in the nation's electoral processes.

Why it mattersThe security and political landscape of the Central African Republic's elections are directly impacted by the presence and actions of foreign military groups.

Central African Republic activist questions floods' spiritual meaning

Sosthène Dingombé asks if deadly Bangui floods carry a message beyond natural disaster.

Central African activist Sosthène Marius Dingombé is questioning the spiritual meaning of deadly floods in Bangui. He published his thoughts on Facebook, expressing sympathy for victims of the heavy rains that hit the capital.

Dingombé, who calls himself "the man of the peasant bourgeoisie," believes the disaster prompts reflection on public authorities' responsibilities. He cited urban management, sanitation, and city planning as areas needing examination. The activist stated that Centrafricans' questions on these issues are legitimate and require concrete answers.

His statement, titled "Bangui under water: beyond the natural disaster, what message should we hear?", also touched on freedom of expression and respect for religious beliefs.

Why it mattersThe floods in Bangui directly affect its citizens and raise questions about public management.

Kaga-Bandoro residents struggle with economic hardship

Low incomes and few opportunities make daily survival a challenge for people in Nana-Gribizi.

Economic precarity continues to weigh on the daily lives of residents in Kaga-Bandoro, Nana-Gribizi. Low incomes and a lack of opportunities make it a significant challenge for people to make ends meet. The report immerses readers in the daily lives of those affected by these economic struggles. It highlights how residents are attempting to navigate their circumstances with limited resources.

Politics · 2026-11-02

Central African rebel leader gets 15-year sentence in Chad

The FDPC movement rejects the verdict and demands the release of General Abdoulaye Miskine, detained since November 2019.

The Front démocratique du peuple centrafricain (FDPC) movement is contesting the 15-year prison sentence handed down to its leader, General Abdoulaye Miskine, by Chadian justice. The FDPC issued a statement on October 29, 2026, from Gamboula, rejecting the trial conditions and calling for Miskine's fundamental rights to be respected. Miskine, whose real name is Martin Koumtamadji, has been held since November 2019.

The verdict, delivered on September 9, 2026, by the criminal chamber of the Court of Appeal in N’Djamena, found Miskine guilty of criminal association, grievous harm, torture, and assassination. Colonel Djimadoum Léon, the FDPC coordinator, signed the statement. Three co-accused received 10-year sentences each, and 30 million CFA francs in damages were awarded to each civil party.

Miskine's arrest in Chad in November 2019 occurred while he was travelling with companions to seek Chadian mediation regarding the February 2019 Khartoum peace agreement. This agreement was signed between Bangui and 14 armed groups. The FDPC is now appealing to international human rights organizations for intervention.

Why it mattersThe FDPC movement, a Central African political group, is directly affected by the conviction and sentencing of its leader in Chad.
