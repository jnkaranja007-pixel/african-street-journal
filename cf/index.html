Central African Republic fuel prices rise, rights group calls for dialogue

The OCDH expressed concern over the impact of rising fuel costs on living conditions and demanded the release of civil society leaders.

Fuel prices have increased in Bangui, prompting concern from the Central African Observatory for Human Rights (OCDH). In a statement on October 22, the OCDH highlighted the negative consequences of the price hike on the population's living conditions. The organization is calling for dialogue to achieve a reduction in fuel prices and the release of arrested civil society leaders.

The COSCIPAC also demanded the immediate and unconditional release of civil society leaders arrested after an October 12 "ville morte" day. In a declaration on October 23 in Bangui, the organization challenged President Faustin-Archange Touadéra's arguments and sought answers to the social crisis.

The political movement "I LEKE KÖDÖRÖ – LES BÂTISSEURS" (ILK-LB) noted that government and union agreements have not sufficiently eased social unrest over rising fuel prices and the cost of living. ILK-LB urged stakeholders to prioritize the general interest and dialogue to prevent a devastating social crisis.

Why it mattersRising fuel prices and the cost of living directly impact the purchasing power and food security of Central African citizens.

Pope urges dialogue in Cameroon speech

The Pope spoke of unemployment, exclusion, and frustrated youth, linking them to potential violence.

Pope Pope 14 arrived in Cameroon for an apostolic mission, delivering a speech focused on societal issues rather than theology. He addressed unemployment, exclusion, frustrated youth, and simmering violence, emphasizing the importance of dialogue. The message was discussed on Radio Ndeke Luka on April 19, 2026.

The Pontiff stated that persistent unemployment and exclusion breed frustration, which can lead to violence. He stressed that investing in youth, creating spaces for dialogue, and taking concrete peace actions are strategic choices to prevent worse outcomes. Former minister Laurent Gomina-Pampali noted the Pope's use of the word 'transformation,' which President Faustin Archange Touadéra also used in his March 30 investiture speech.

Elysée Nguémalé, coordinator of the OGDC, concluded that peace cannot be decreed, a point made by the Pope in Yaoundé. Bangui will heed this message when it chooses to.

Why it mattersThe Pope's message on dialogue and addressing issues like unemployment and exclusion directly relates to the Central African Republic's governance and potential for peace.

Audit finds failures at Bangui and Bouboui slaughterhouses

An audit presented on October 23 revealed sanitary and management shortcomings at two Central African Republic slaughterhouses.

An audit report on the management of the Bangui and Bouboui slaughterhouses by Djibro Service was presented at the Primature on October 23. The report details numerous failures in sanitary standards and management of these facilities.

The audit findings were contested by Djibro Service, the company responsible for operating both slaughterhouses. The report highlights several deficiencies in the operations of these key meat processing sites.

The presentation of the audit report indicates a formal review of the slaughterhouse operations. The next steps for addressing the reported issues have not yet been detailed.

Why it mattersThe audit report affects the management and sanitary conditions of slaughterhouses in the Central African Republic.

Central African Republic releases hostages after 10 months

Three officials kidnapped during elections returned to Bangui and met President Touadéra.

Three Central African Republic officials, kidnapped in December during general elections, arrived in Bangui on a UN flight after 10 months in captivity.

The released hostages, a local government official and two members of the National Election Authority, were kidnapped in Bambouti and were greeted by President Faustin-Archange Touadéra upon their return. "The period we spent in captivity was truly horrific," said Koumba Ndiaye, sub-prefect of Bambouti, describing their ordeal as "extremely difficult."

Their release followed months of negotiations by the MINUSCA peacekeeping force with the Azandé militants, an armed group that emerged in the Haut-Mbomou region in 2023 and was trained by the former Russian Wagner group.

Why it mattersThree Central African Republic officials experienced a horrific ordeal for 10 months after being kidnapped during elections.
