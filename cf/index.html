Central African justice minister orders prosecution of BRDC

The ministry says the political platform is absent from official party registers and has no authorisation after its 13 November march.

The Central African Republic’s justice ministry has ordered prosecutors to bring a case against the Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC), a political platform, after its 13 November march. In a note published on 17 November, the ministry challenged the group’s legal status.

Justice Minister Arnaud Djoubaye Abazène said checks by the territorial administration ministry and other competent services found the BRDC was not listed in official party registers and had no authorisation. He instructed public prosecutors to proceed against the platform.

The march took place in Bangui. The ministry’s note makes no further timetable for the prosecution public.

Why it mattersPublic prosecutors are instructed to bring a case against the BRDC after checks found it unregistered and without authorisation.

Security · 2026-11-18

Russian House chief Dimitri Sytyi claims ignorance of Ndassima gold revenue

Sytyi said on Radio Ndeke Luka on 22 September 2026 that mercenary presence in the Central African Republic was free of charge.

On 22 September 2026, Dimitri Sytyi, who runs the Russian House in Bangui, told Radio Ndeke Luka that Russia's support for the Central African Republic costs the state nothing. Pressed on the cost of the mercenary presence and on the mining of national resources, he said the work was free and claimed to know nothing of the revenue from the Ndassima gold deposit.

The Corbeau News Centrafrique report argues that Sytyi's role does not match his claims. The Russian House is a civil, cultural and social institution concerned with education, cooperation and the economy, not the Russian defence ministry or a military headquarters. Yet the report says Sytyi described deployments at Amdafock, the chain of command and security strategy in detail.

The report says he was fluent in military operations but claimed ignorance when questioned about economic activity, which it says should be central to a civil mission. The source gives no official response to the allegations.

Why it mattersResidents and investors in Bangui have a stake in who controls revenue from the Ndassima gold deposit, and the report challenges the claim that the Russian presence costs the state nothing.

Former Central African Republic prime minister Nicolas Tiangaye dies at 70

The lawyer and opposition figure died in Bangui on 16 November after a sudden illness, leaving a decades-long record in law and politics.

Nicolas Tiangaye, a former prime minister of the Central African Republic and former head of its lawyers’ association, died in Bangui on 16 November 2026 at age 70. Radio Ndeke Luka reported that he died after a sudden illness.

Tiangaye became prime minister in January 2013, appointed by President François Bozizé and retained after Bozizé was ousted in March. He resigned on 10 January 2014 alongside transitional leader Michel Djotodia. He also chaired the National Transitional Council in 2003, which helped shape the political transition and constitution.

In the early 1990s, Tiangaye helped establish the Central African Human Rights League and later founded the Republican Convention for Social Progress party. He had recently been active in the Republican Bloc for the Defence of the Constitution. Former transitional head Ferdinand Alexandre N’Guendet, described as his political ally since 2003, called on Central Africans to continue Tiangaye’s work for democracy.

Why it mattersTiangaye’s death leaves a vacancy in the country’s legal community, while his former ally Ferdinand Alexandre N’Guendet has urged Central Africans to continue his democratic work.

Security · 2026-11-18

ANAC equips Bangui-M’Poko airport and trains more than 70 staff

The Central African aviation authority handed out certificates and security equipment at the airport on Thursday 12 November, with funding from the African Development Bank.

The Central African Republic’s National Civil Aviation Authority (ANAC) trained more than 70 airport security staff at Bangui-M’Poko and gave them completion certificates on Thursday 12 November. The ceremony also included the handover of equipment to strengthen security at the airport.

The African Development Bank supplied security and screening equipment, sanitary equipment and logistical and office materials, funded at 126 470 800 FCFA. ANAC and the French embassy carried out the training; the embassy said it wants the international airport to meet international standards.

The training is intended to prevent children from entering aircraft parked on the tarmac, after one child was found in an aircraft there on two occasions recently. The work is part of two African Development Bank-funded projects to modernise the airport and support ANAC.

Why it mattersAirport users are to be protected by trained staff and new screening equipment funded at 126 470 800 FCFA.

Culture · 2026-11-18

Bangui festival closes after three days of Central African-Chinese arts

The event ran from 9 to 11 November under the arts ministry and the Chinese embassy, featuring performances, photography and martial arts.

A festival showcasing cultural and artistic exchanges between the Central African Republic and China ended on 11 November at a stadium in Bangui, after three days of activities. The event ran from 9 to 11 November under the slogan “Culture, friendship and cooperation between peoples.”

Michel Bamale Bongo, chief of staff at the Ministry of Arts, Culture and Tourism, presided over the closing ceremony. He said participants experienced dance, performances, photography, visual arts and an exhibition of Chinese martial arts. Zhang Wei, chargé d’affaires at China’s embassy in Bangui, said the festival offered an opportunity to share Chinese traditions and discover Central African culture.

Brice Talingano, a participant, said it was the first time photographers had been honoured at such an event. The festival was organised under the arts ministry in partnership with China’s embassy in Bangui.

Why it mattersArtists and photographers took part in a three-day programme that included an exhibition of Chinese martial arts and gave photographers a place of honour, according to participant Brice Talingano.
