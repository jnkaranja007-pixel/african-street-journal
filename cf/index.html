Pediatric Center Sees Fewer Sick Children After Care Costs Return

Parents are avoiding the Bangui Pediatric Complex due to renewed medical fees, leading to empty beds and a drop in patient numbers.

The Bangui Pediatric Complex is experiencing a significant decrease in patient visits following the suspension of some free healthcare programs. Parents are now hesitant to bring their sick children due to the reintroduction of certain medical fees, with some opting for self-medication or local remedies. Waiting rooms are noticeably less crowded, and many beds in Units A and B remain empty, a stark contrast to previous attendance levels.

Previously, targeted free care for children under five, pregnant women, and nursing mothers, supported by partners since 2016, meant many families could access services without cost. However, the return of consultation, examination, and medication fees has created a barrier for those lacking financial means. The director of the complex, Professor Jean Chrysostome Gody, stated that the government maintains targeted free care, shifting from total to selective coverage for specific demographics, a policy implemented in early 2026.

Beyond the financial strain, parents also report dissatisfaction with the reception and waiting times at the facility. One user anonymously described a lengthy wait for medical attention, even after paying for a medical record. The director attributed these reception issues to a lack of civility, while health officials are appealing to authorities and partners for support amidst these challenges.

Why it mattersSick children are not receiving timely medical attention because their parents cannot afford the reintroduced fees for consultations, examinations, and medications.

Miskine trial nears closing arguments in Chad

Central African leader Abdoulaye Miskine faces charges of insurrection, rape, and murder as his trial continues in N'Djamena.

The trial of Abdoulaye Miskine, leader of the Front démocratique pour le peuple centrafricain (FDPC), is set to resume on September 4 in N'Djamena, Chad, after a two-day suspension. Miskine and his co-accused face charges including creating an insurrectional movement, rape, assassination, and torture stemming from events in 2001 and 2002. The court has been hearing from civil parties and the accused, with chronological elements focusing on Miskine's alleged role in combating rebellion under President Ange-Félix Patassé.

Central African refugees from the Maro camp in Chad have requested to testify, aiming to provide evidence on crimes and violence attributed to Miskine's forces. Miskine's defense argues he was an official security commander fighting alongside the Central African army, not a rebel leader. The proceedings are scheduled to continue with the public prosecutor's arguments and pleas from the civil parties and the defense.

Miskine and three co-accused were arrested in southeastern Chad on November 19, 2019, and have been detained in N'Djamena for several years. Chadian authorities accuse them of creating an insurrectional movement, assassination, rape, torture, and sequestration. The Central African government's request for Miskine's extradition to Bangui has been unsuccessful.

Why it mattersCentral African refugees and victims of alleged crimes are seeking justice through the Chadian court system.

Bees force UN humanitarian plane to land in Bambari

A United Nations humanitarian flight en route to Bangassou made an emergency landing in Bambari on Monday after bees swarmed the aircraft's cabin.

A United Nations humanitarian plane traveling from Bangui to Bangassou was forced to divert its flight and land in Bambari on Monday, August 31, 2026. The diversion occurred after several bees entered the aircraft's cabin while it was flying over the Ouaka region.

The incident happened shortly after the plane departed from Bangui-M'Poko airport. The pilot decided to land in Bambari to ensure the safety of the passengers and to allow for the removal of the insects from the cabin. No passengers were reported injured during the event.

Following the evacuation of all occupants, the bees were removed from the plane. The aircraft then underwent a thorough cleaning and disinfection process before it was cleared to continue its journey to Bangassou.

Why it mattersThe event affected passengers and crew on a humanitarian flight, causing a delay and requiring safety measures due to the bee intrusion.

Farmer-herder clashes halt farming in Batangafo

Farmers abandon fields in Ouham-Fafa region due to armed herders, destroying crops and livestock amid ongoing tensions.

Tensions between farmers and armed herders have paralyzed agricultural activities in Batangafo, Ouham-Fafa. Farmers have fled their fields for several days, fearing the presence of armed herders who have devastated crops. This conflict, fueled by livestock destroying farms and herders' animals being killed, has resulted in over seven deaths and numerous injuries in the past three months.

The ongoing violence has forced residents like Madeleine Noukouma, a widow and mother of eleven, to abandon her farm in Diri village. She stated that the armed herders instilled fear, leading to their departure and the destruction of their livelihoods. Noukouma expressed a willingness to return if peace is restored and a solution is found.

Local authorities are attempting to de-escalate the situation. Fatimé Salet, president of the National Federation of Herders of CAR, urged herders to keep their cattle away from farmlands. Sub-prefect Robert Yekoua-Kette emphasized the need to respect the transhumance corridor and warned against cattle theft, while acknowledging the persistent fear among residents due to armed herders near the town.

Why it mattersFarmers are unable to access their fields and are experiencing crop destruction due to the presence of armed herders.

News · 2026-09-04

Man fatally stabbed in Zémio after months of calm

A resident of Koumboli, near Zémio, died from knife wounds sustained during a home invasion, ending a period of relative security.

A man was fatally stabbed in Koumboli, a village three kilometers from Zémio, during the night of September 1 and 2, 2026. The victim was attacked in his home by an assailant who entered the property with knives. Despite efforts to transport him to Zémio hospital, the victim succumbed to his injuries en route.

The perpetrator fled the scene immediately after the assault and, as of the article's writing, no arrests had been made. The precise reasons behind the attack and the assailant's motive remain unknown to authorities and the local community.

This incident marks a concerning resurgence of urban crime in the Haut-Mbomou region, following a period where security had improved. The attack has disrupted the fragile peace that had been established in the area.

Why it mattersResidents of Zémio and surrounding villages are affected by the return of violent crime, which threatens their personal safety and the recent period of security.
