Central African opposition gives government 14 days to meet demands

The BRDC coalition, which includes the former prime minister Martin Ziguélé, is demanding the release of detainees and lower fuel prices.

The Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC), an opposition coalition, marched through Bangui on Friday, 13 November 2026, ending the march with an ultimatum to the Central African government. Martin Ziguélé, a former prime minister, spoke for the coalition and gave the authorities 14 days to respond to its demands. If the response is not satisfactory, the opposition plans a new demonstration in the capital.

The march began around 6 a.m. at the Marabéna field, at the Omar-Bongo crossroads in the third district of Bangui. Protesters walked along the Avenue des Martyrs towards the Martyrs' monument roundabout, near the Lycée des Martyrs, and the gathering ended around 8 a.m. after about two hours on foot. The Ministry of the Interior had authorised the peaceful march in a letter dated 9 November 2026, addressed to Crépin Mboli-Goumba, president of the Patrie party and coordinator of the BRDC.

The authorisation came with strict conditions: organisers must keep the march peaceful and prevent violence or damage to people and property. The ministry noted that the BRDC is not among the organisations formally recognised in the territorial administration's register, but it still granted permission. Organisers and participants will be held responsible for any breach of those conditions.

UN Security Council renews peacekeeping mission in CAR

The UN Security Council has unanimously renewed the mandate for MINUSCA until November 15, 2027.

The United Nations Security Council has renewed the mandate of the UN peacekeeping mission in the Central African Republic (MINUSCA) until November 15, 2027. The decision was adopted unanimously in New York on Thursday.

MINUSCA, deployed in 2014 during a civil war, has already begun reducing its presence, closing or relocating 21 bases between January and June. The mission still has nearly 10,000 military personnel and 2,000 police officers. Despite improvements since the conflict's peak, armed groups remain active, particularly near borders with Chad, Sudan, and the DRC.

Attacks attributed to remnants of the Lord’s Resistance Army in the southeast reportedly killed four people and abducted 68 civilians, including 40 children, between June and September. In June, an attack in Am-Dafock, Sudan, resulted in 29 deaths and displaced 17,500 people. President Faustin-Archange Touadéra has called for a phased withdrawal of the mission.

Why it mattersThe UN Security Council's decision on MINUSCA's mandate renewal directly impacts the security situation for civilians in the Central African Republic.

Bangui hosts 352 candidates in CAMES medical aggregation exams

The second phase of the exams runs at the University of Bangui from November 10 to 18, with jury members urged to show rigour.

The second phase of aggregation exams in human medicine, pharmacy, dentistry, veterinary medicine and animal production was launched on Monday, November 10, at the National Assembly in Bangui. The exams, part of the 23rd edition, bring together 352 candidates from 13 member countries of the Conseil africain et malgache pour l’enseignement supérieur (CAMES), the body that coordinates higher education across French-speaking Africa and Madagascar, and will be held at the University of Bangui until November 18.

Gérard Gresenguet, the university's rector and interim president of CAMES, told jury members that their mission was decisive and asked them to show rigour, impartiality and transparency under the CAMES codes of ethics. Prime Minister Félix Moloua presided over the opening ceremony.

The written and in-person tests are split by discipline, with the largest group, 295 candidates, in human medicine. The exams are a major step for university teacher-researchers, who are assessed on scientific, teaching and professional skills.

Why it mattersTeacher-researchers taking part must pass these assessments to advance, and the results will shape the teaching staff at universities across the 13 member countries.

Bangui to host third school orientation fair for final-year pupils

Organisers say the two-day fair at the Alliance française will pair career talks with practical training and exhibitions from schools and partners.

The office of the Salon de l'éducation et de l'orientation scolaire announced the dates of its third edition at a press briefing in Bangui on Saturday, 7 November. The fair is scheduled to run on 12 and 13 November at the Alliance française in Bangui, aimed at final-year secondary pupils and university students choosing their fields of study.

The theme is "Guiding Central African youth for tomorrow's challenges: key training and future careers". Organisers say many children still choose their courses on their parents' instructions, and the event will combine speakers from home and abroad with orientation sessions, training and stands run by institutions and partners. Bienvenu Juvenal Rouheda, a member of the office, said students who attended the first two editions had made better-informed choices, and some now studying at the University of Bangui are expected to sit their licence exams.

Organisers want to take the fair to other cities and provinces, but they say funding is limited, even for this edition. They are asking partners and donors to help.

Central African religious leaders call for dialogue

Religious leaders in Bangui urged calm and dialogue amid socio-economic and political tensions.

Religious leaders in the Central African Republic called for calm and dialogue on Thursday, November 12, in Bangui. The Platform of Religious Confessions of the Central African Republic (PCRC) expressed concern over the deteriorating social fabric and tensions between the government, opposition, and civil society.

The leaders highlighted the fuel shortage and rising cost of living as urgent issues requiring government attention. Apostle Nicolas Guéret-Koyame noted the improving security but warned of a worsening socio-political climate, stating, "We are witnessing a widening crisis between political power, opposition parties, and civil society."

Cardinal Dieudonné Nzapalaïnga urged the youth and media to act responsibly and transparently. The PCRC announced a nationwide campaign for peace and coexistence to help ease social tensions.

Why it mattersReligious leaders are urging dialogue to address the rising cost of living and political tensions affecting most Central Africans.
