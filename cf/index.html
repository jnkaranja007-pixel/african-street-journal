Central African Republic denies border violation

The presidency stated that recent military operations against rebels occurred solely within its territory.

Central African Republic's presidency has affirmed that recent military operations involving its FACA forces and Russian allies took place exclusively within the nation's borders. Presidential spokesperson Albert Yaloké Mokpeme stated at a press conference in Bangui that there are no tensions with neighboring Chad, and the two heads of state communicate regularly to secure their shared border. He clarified that the operation against rebel leader Noureddine Adam, supported by Chadian and Sudanese mercenaries, occurred entirely on Central African soil without violating Chadian territory.

Mokpeme explained that the FACA and their allies acted to neutralize armed groups preparing a large-scale attack in the tri-border area of the Central African Republic, Chad, and Sudan. He reported that the clashes resulted in casualties on both sides, with some attackers fleeing into Chad. The spokesperson warned that any attempt to use neighboring countries as bases to destabilize the Central African Republic will be met with severe force by the FACA and their allies.

The presidency's statement aims to prevent any frontal country from serving as a rear base for negative forces seeking to destabilize Central African institutions. The FACA and their allies intervened effectively, leading to exchanges of fire that caused injuries on both sides.

Why it mattersThe presidency of the Central African Republic issued a statement to clarify the location of military operations and deny border violations, addressing potential diplomatic issues with Chad and Sudan.

Former CAR minister accuses Russia of exploitation

Marie-Reine Hassen stated the Central African Republic is more exploited now than during 66 years of French colonization.

Former Central African Republic minister Marie-Reine Hassen told a high-level conference in France that her country is currently more exploited than it was during 66 years of French colonization.

Hassen described the situation as a "raid" occurring with the complicity of Central African leaders, affecting villages, forests, mines, and the population.

She made these remarks at a conference dinner in the French Senate, comparing the current exploitation under Russian influence and Wagner mercenaries to the past colonial era.

Why it mattersThe former minister claims the Central African Republic is being exploited by Russian influence and Wagner mercenaries.

Central African Republic and DRC coordinate Ebola response

Health officials from Bangui and Kinshasa met in Bangui on October 22-23 to discuss epidemiological surveillance and prevention.

Health authorities from the Central African Republic and the Democratic Republic of Congo are working together to stop Ebola from spreading. Officials met in Bangui on October 22 and 23 to discuss how to monitor the virus and coordinate prevention efforts.

The meeting focused on epidemiological surveillance and the coordination of prevention measures. Astrid de Bonheur Kolengue reported on the discussions between the two nations' health bodies.

This coordinated response aims to curb the virus's propagation between the two neighboring countries.

Why it mattersHealth officials from the Central African Republic and the Democratic Republic of Congo met to coordinate efforts against the spread of the Ebola virus.

Central African Republic civil society leaders arrested

Organisations pleaded with President Touadéra for the release of five leaders detained after an October 12 protest.

Five civil society leaders remain detained in the Central African Republic following their arrest after an October 12 "dead city" day protest. Organisations met President Faustin-Archange Touadéra on October 21 to advocate for their release. The protest was organised to object to rising fuel prices.

The Minister of Interior, Bienvenu Zokoué, has invoked the events of 2013, suggesting that current mobilisations against fuel price hikes threaten the nation's stability. This statement follows the arrests of five civil society leaders in Bangui.

President Touadéra responded to the organisations by citing the principle of the separation of powers. The detained leaders were arrested after the October 12 demonstration against increased fuel costs.

Why it mattersFive civil society leaders were arrested in Bangui after protesting fuel price increases.

CAR advisor accuses France, Chad of plot

Jules Njawé claims 103 armed vehicles and French fighter jets are preparing an attack on the Central African Republic.

Jules Njawé, an advisor to Central African Republic President Faustin-Archange Touadéra, accused Chad and France on October 19, 2026, of plotting an attack against the country. Njawé claimed 103 heavily armed pick-up trucks, Sudanese fighters, and two French fighter jets were involved in the alleged offensive, seeking to divert attention from recent clashes.

These accusations follow an October 10 military operation in the Vakaga region where Russian Wagner group mercenaries reportedly attacked Front Populaire pour la Renaissance de la Centrafrique positions in Nabagaye-Tissi. During this operation, 16 Chadian soldiers were wounded on their own territory, according to reports.

The initial report from Corbeau News Centrafrique stated 14 wounded soldiers, later updated to 16 by October 13. Njawé made his claims during an October 19 broadcast hosted by Alain Foka.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic citizens are directly affected by accusations of a planned military offensive and the ongoing conflict.
