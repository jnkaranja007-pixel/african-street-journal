Fondö Residents Face Months Without Electricity

Residents of Bangui's Fondö neighborhood are calling for the urgent restoration of power from ENERCA after months of darkness.

Residents in the Fondö neighborhood, located in the 3rd arrondissement of Bangui, are protesting the prolonged absence of electricity supplied by Energie Centrafricaine (ENERCA). They have been without power for several months, a situation that significantly disrupts their daily lives and economic activities.

The lack of electricity is reportedly exacerbating concerns about security in the area, as darkness can embolden criminal elements. Residents are appealing to the energy company to resolve the issue and restore power services as quickly as possible, highlighting the essential nature of electricity for their community.

The continuous power outage affects various aspects of life in Fondö, from household chores to small businesses that rely on electricity for operation. The community's plea underscores the critical role of reliable energy infrastructure in maintaining normalcy and safety.

Why it mattersResidents of Fondö are affected by the prolonged absence of electricity, which disrupts their daily lives, economic activities, and increases security concerns.

Army Officer Detained Amidst Coup Allegations and Land Dispute

An army officer is held at the OCRB on suspicion of plotting a coup, a situation stemming from a family dispute over ancestral land.

Adjutant-chef Yves Junior Konzi, a non-commissioned officer in the Central African Armed Forces (FACA), has been detained for several days at the Office Central pour la Répression du Banditisme (OCRB) in Bangui. He faces accusations of preparing a coup d'état. The origins of this serious charge, however, are reportedly linked to a familial conflict concerning ancestral land located in Bangassou.

Konzi, whose father was a gendarmerie chief of staff killed in 2021, regularly travels to France for medical reasons and resides near Toulouse. During his most recent visit, he learned that his younger brothers had sold a piece of family property in southeastern Central African Republic. This discovery appears to be at the heart of the unfolding events.

The situation highlights what the outlet describes as the paranoia of the Bangui regime. Konzi's detention at the OCRB, a facility for combating banditry, places him at the center of a complex and unusual case that intertwines personal grievances with national security concerns.

Why it mattersAdjutant-chef Yves Junior Konzi is affected by being detained and accused of plotting a coup due to a family land dispute.

Central African Republic Orders Gold Sector Overhaul After Mine Collapse

President Touadéra directs the Mines Minister to regulate the gold industry following a deadly incident at the Zamboï mine.

Authorities in Bangui are working to bring order to the gold sector through inspections, oversight, and potential permit suspensions. This follows a collapse at the Zamboï gold mine on August 18, which resulted in approximately one hundred fatalities. President Faustin-Archange Touadéra has tasked the Minister of Mines and Geology, Rufin Benam Beltoungou, with taking charge of the situation and investigating the site.

The Zamboï mine is located in western Central African Republic, near the border with Cameroon. The government's efforts aim to address the challenges posed by the informal economy's impact on the mining industry. The minister visited the site the day after the incident to assess the situation and begin implementing regulatory measures.

The incident at Zamboï highlights the difficulties in managing the informal aspects of the gold trade. The government's response indicates a commitment to improving safety and oversight within the sector. Further actions, including potential permit reviews, are expected as the investigation progresses.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the government is implementing new regulations and oversight in the gold sector following a deadly mine collapse.

Ex-President Bozizé Accused of Ordering Arrest

A witness told the Special Criminal Court that former President François Bozizé ordered his imprisonment after a supermarket fire.

Bienvenu Ngaro, testifying at the Special Criminal Court (CPS) on August 27, stated that former President François Bozizé directly ordered his arrest and imprisonment following the fire at Rayan supermarket. Ngaro asserted that the accused currently before the court were not responsible for his detention. He recounted that his arrest documents cited charges of criminal association, arson, and violence.

Ngaro also testified that he did not suffer violence from Vianney Semndiro at the Bossembélé military center, describing an act of assistance instead. He confirmed that Bozizé regularly visited the military center but stated he never met him during his detention. The witness also claimed ignorance of Charles Massy, Firmin Junior Danboy, and Eugène Ngaïkosset.

The witness is seeking reparations, stating his imprisonment led to job loss and psychological distress. The CPS is hearing cases of crimes against humanity allegedly committed between February 2009 and March 2013 in Bossembélé, Bouar, and Bangui. The proceedings are set to resume with arguments from the prosecution, civil parties, and the defense.

Why it mattersThe testimony affects individuals accused of crimes against humanity, potentially impacting legal proceedings and reputations.

Judiciary Rejects Political Claims in Pabandji Case

The national magistrates' union stated that the proceedings against a communications advisor are not politically motivated.

The national magistrates' union has issued a statement regarding the case of Martial Pabandji, who is currently detained for contempt of court. In a communiqué released on August 27, the union explicitly denied any political dimension to the legal action taken against the High Communications Advisor.

Mauricette Monthé Psimhis, president of the national magistrates' union, addressed questions from Christ Roi Jordan Bandéngbé in a report. The union's stance aims to clarify the nature of the proceedings, emphasizing its judicial basis rather than political interference.

The case involves Pabandji, a senior communications advisor, who faces charges for allegedly showing disrespect to the judiciary. The magistrates' union's declaration seeks to ensure public understanding of the legal process and its separation from political agendas.

Why it mattersJudicial proceedings against a government communications advisor are being clarified by the magistrates' union to prevent political misinterpretations.
