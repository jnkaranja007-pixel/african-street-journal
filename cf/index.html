Central African Republic-China trade jumps 30% in nine months

Bilateral trade reached $123 million between January and September 2026, driven by China's zero-tariff policy for African nations.

Trade between the Central African Republic (CAR) and China surged by 30.0% to $123 million between January and September 2026, compared to the same period in 2025. This increase follows China's implementation of a zero-tariff regime for 53 African countries on May 1, 2026, which accelerates access to the Chinese market for CAR's exports.

The CAR's export basket to China remains heavily concentrated, with timber accounting for 99.9% of the $30.2 million shipped in the first nine months of 2026. Raw timber exports were $27.9 million, a slight increase of 6.7%, while sawn wood exports saw a significant rise of 263.3% to $2.0 million.

The CAR's total trade with Beijing in nine months nearly matched the $130 million recorded for all of 2025. The country now ranks 180th among China's trading partners, up from 183rd last year. Producers are expected to leverage the zero-tax regime to boost exports, provided they address local processing and meet Beijing's sanitary standards.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic producers and economic operators are expected to benefit from China's zero-tax regime to increase export volumes, with trade reaching $123 million in nine months.

Opposition coalition BRDC accuses CAR government of dictatorship

The Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution says the ruling party wants to remove every remaining counter-power after the 13 November march.

The Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC), led by Maître Crépin Mboli-Goumba, issued a harsh statement on Saturday, 14 November 2026, against the Central African government. The opposition coalition disputes the official account of the march held on 13 November and rejects a new consultation framework on the high cost of living.

The BRDC accuses the ruling Mouvement cœurs unis (MCU) party and President Faustin-Archange Touadéra of manipulating events and drifting towards dictatorship. It names the Minister of State for Communication, whose press conference prompted the reply, and says the government's communication strategy aims to neutralise any organisation able to challenge its decisions.

The statement follows a peaceful march by opposition leaders, led by the BRDC with a deputy for Bangui's 7th arrondissement at its head. The report does not say what the government has done in response.

Why it mattersOpposition parties and civil groups that organise marches now face the BRDC's charge that the government intends to silence them, with the coalition rejecting the cost-of-living consultation framework.

Business · 2026-11-16

Central African Republic negotiates new fuel deal with Russia

Officials have not disclosed how much fuel Russia would sell, at what price, or through which channel it would be delivered.

The Central African Republic and Russia are negotiating a new oil agreement, but the quantities of fuel, the prices and the delivery arrangements have not been disclosed. The signing is being considered for early 2027, as fuel supply difficulties and rising pump prices continue in the country.

The talks were first raised in September, during a visit by Russian Energy Minister Sergueï Tsivilev. Africa Intelligence reported on 27 October 2026 that the discussions concern imports of Russian petroleum products, within a wider energy cooperation between the two countries whose concrete commitments have not yet been detailed.

The report also cites a lack of transparency in the process and alleges that a criminal network is involved in the fuel trade, though it gives no detail on who is involved.

Why it mattersFuel buyers in the Central African Republic will pay whatever price the eventual contract sets, and no price has yet been published.

Security · 2026-11-16

Wagner mercenaries fail to dislodge rebels in Kwango

Russian Wagner Group fighters returned to Bangui without removing armed elements suspected of being former UPC fighters from the Kwango area.

In Kwango, Ouaka prefecture, an operation by Russian Wagner Group mercenaries against heavily armed rebels ended in failure. The mercenaries departed for Bangui without dislodging the armed men after responding to local reports of their presence.

Residents of Kwango had alerted the Russian mercenaries to the presence of several armed men, suspected of being former fighters of Ali Darassa's UPC movement, operating near the town. These individuals are regularly seen buying goods like cigarettes, causing concern among the population about a potential resurgence of violence.

The Wagner mercenaries arrived in the locality by helicopter, heavily armed, to investigate the armed groups' presence and intervene if necessary. The operation, however, did not result in the removal of the suspected rebels from the area.

Why it mattersResidents of Kwango are concerned about a potential resurgence of violence due to the continued presence of armed elements in their area.
