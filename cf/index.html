Hundreds march in Bangui against fuel price rise and high cost of living

The Bloc Républicain pour la Défense de la Constitution (BRDC) led the peaceful protest along Avenue des Martyrs on Friday morning.

Several hundred people marched in Bangui on Friday, 13 November 2026, to protest rising fuel prices and the high cost of living in the Central African Republic. The march was called by the Bloc Républicain pour la Défense de la Constitution (BRDC), a group of opposition parties, which said the government had raised fuel prices without consultation.

The demonstrators set off from the Marabéna crossroads, walked along Avenue des Martyrs and ended at the Martyrs roundabout. National security forces and the United Nations stabilisation mission, MINUSCA, escorted the march, which went ahead peacefully.

Organisers say protests of this kind are a legitimate way to contest government decisions and press for change in everyday living conditions.

Why it mattersHouseholds already short of cash now face dearer fuel and food, and the opposition has shown it can gather hundreds on the streets over the cost of living.

Wagner group intensifies gold mining in Central African Republic

While Ndassima gold activities draw political attention, Wagner's industrial gold extraction also expands in Yidéré.

The Russian Wagner group is intensifying industrial gold mining operations in Yidéré, located in the Nana-Mambéré prefecture of the Central African Republic. Field observations describe a large-scale mining site with crushing facilities and continuous activity, with helicopters and planes linked to Wagner making regular rotations to the site.

Corbeau News Centrafrique's field team estimates that the Yidéré installations may surpass those at Ndassima in scale, based on direct observations. While detailed public data on exploited areas or production volumes is unavailable, the Yidéré site exhibits characteristics of industrial exploitation, including significant logistical means and regular air traffic.

The Nana-Mambéré region hosts several foreign operators, including Chinese actors and interests linked to Wagner, according to information gathered by Corbeau News Centrafrique. Precisely identifying the companies present, their authorizations, and their respective responsibilities is necessary to understand the conditions under which the country's resources are being exploited.

Why it mattersThe Wagner group's industrial gold mining operations in Yidéré involve significant logistical means and regular air traffic, impacting the exploitation of the Central African Republic's natural resources.

Central African Republic-China trade jumps 30%

Bilateral trade reached 123 million dollars between January and September 2026, driven by China's zero-tariff regime.

Trade between the Central African Republic (CAR) and China surged by 30.0% in the first nine months of 2026, reaching 123 million US dollars. This increase follows China's implementation of a zero-tariff regime for 53 African countries on May 1, 2026, which accelerates access to the Chinese market.

The CAR's exports to China during this period were overwhelmingly dominated by timber, accounting for 99.9% of the 30.2 million dollars in exports. While raw timber sales saw a slight increase, sawn timber exports experienced a significant rise of 263.3%.

The CAR's total trade with Beijing in nine months nearly matched the 130 million dollars recorded for all of 2025. The country now ranks 180th among China's trading partners, up from 183rd last year.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic producers and economic operators should leverage China's zero-tax policy to boost exports, with timber remaining the primary commodity.

Security · 2026-11-15

Bangui court detains four teens over sex tape leak

Four adolescents are held in detention after being presented to the Bangui prosecutor's office.

Four adolescents have been placed under a detention order by the Bangui prosecutor's office following the circulation of sexually explicit videos on social media. The three boys, Georges Darlan, Gomez Darlan, and Benoît Obrou, were transferred to Ngaragba prison. The sole girl involved in the case was sent to the women's prison in Bimbo.

Sources close to the case confirmed the detention of the four individuals, who are presumed innocent until proven guilty. The exact circumstances of the videos' creation and distribution, as well as each person's alleged role, will be determined during the judicial proceedings. Women's rights organizations, who filed the initial complaint, have not yet commented publicly on the decision.

This judicial development occurs amidst growing concerns about adolescent protection and exposure to intimate online content. The prosecutor's decision follows the presentation of the accused to the court on Thursday. Further details regarding the ongoing legal process are expected as the investigation continues.

Why it mattersFour adolescents are detained in Bangui, facing judicial proceedings for alleged involvement in the distribution of explicit videos.
