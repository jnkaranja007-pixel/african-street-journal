BRDC calls November 13 protest in Bangui

The opposition bloc demands lower fuel prices and the release of civil society leaders.

The Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC) announced a peaceful march for November 13 in Bangui. The protest aims to demand lower fuel prices and the release of civil society leaders. Residents of Bangui, Bimbo, and Bégoua are called to gather at 6 AM.

The march will start from the Peace roundabout and proceed to the Martyrs monument via the Avenue des Martyrs. This demonstration follows an October 12 "ville morte" operation protesting the third fuel price increase in three years. BRDC stated they are responding to a proposal made by Faustin-Archange Touadéra during a meeting with trade unions.

The BRDC, a platform of the democratic opposition, made the announcement on October 30. The group is protesting the rising cost of living and fuel prices. The planned march is a response to the current government's policies.

Why it mattersCitizens in Bangui, Bimbo, and Bégoua will participate in a protest against the rising cost of living and fuel prices.

Politics · 2026-11-01

MLPC disowns Martin Ziguélé

The MLPC Bureau Exécutif stated Martin Ziguélé continues to act in their name despite being removed as party president.

The MLPC Bureau Exécutif in Bangui declared on October 20, 2026, that it disassociates itself from Martin Ziguélé. The party stated Ziguélé was removed as MLPC president by the Congress but continues to act and speak in the party's name without authorization.

MLPC President Tagotto Josephat signed the declaration, which also denounced a destabilization attempt involving opposition parties. The Bureau accused unnamed politicians of being 'traitors' who seek to destabilize the country and hinder financial aid from international bodies like the World Bank and IMF.

The party urged vigilance against these 'criminals' who have previously caused displacement and economic damage. The MLPC has informed administrative and judicial authorities about Ziguélé's actions, asserting he no longer controls the 'real MLPC'.

Why it mattersThe MLPC party is denouncing a former leader for actions that the party claims are destabilizing the Central African Republic.

Bangui bank clients demand access to funds

Customers of the Caisses mutuelles de Centrafrique in Bangui's 4th arrondissement are frustrated by delays in accessing their money.

Clients of the Caisses mutuelles de Centrafrique (CMCA) in Bangui's 4th arrondissement are demanding access to their money. After multiple unsuccessful meetings, their impatience is growing with a management that urges patience and promises a return to normalcy.

The customers expressed their frustration over the inability to retrieve their funds. Management has appealed for calm, stating that a return to normal operations is anticipated.

Alexia Félixia Ngbandi collected the complaints from the affected clients.

Bangui floods prompt spiritual and public responsibility questions

Activist Sosthène Marius Dingombé questions the spiritual meaning of the deadly floods in the capital.

Central African activist Sosthène Marius Dingombé is questioning the spiritual meaning behind the deadly floods that have struck Bangui. He also raised issues of public responsibility, freedom of expression, and respect for religious beliefs.

In a Facebook statement titled "Bangui under water: beyond the natural disaster, what message should we hear?", Dingombé, who calls himself "the man of the peasant bourgeoisie", expressed his sympathy for those affected by the heavy rains.

The activist believes the disaster necessitates a reflection on the government's role in managing urban development, sanitation, and planning in Bangui. He stated that the questions Central Africans are asking about these issues are legitimate and require concrete answers.

Why it mattersCentral African citizens are directly affected by the deadly floods and are questioning the government's management of urban development and sanitation.

Business · 2026-11-01

Sunu Assurance offers education sector protection

The insurance company met with private school and university leaders on October 29, 2026, to discuss risk prevention and student safety.

Sunu Assurance IARD Centrafrique and Sunu Assurance Vie Centrafrique hosted an event on October 29, 2026, for leaders of private educational institutions in the Central African Republic. The meeting focused on risk prevention and protection for schools, students, and families.

The insurance companies presented solutions including School Civil Liability, Education Insurance, accident coverage, and property protection for institutions. Mohamed Compaoré, director of Sunu Assurance, stated that education is the future of the nation and societies, prompting their focus on this sector. Didace Sabone, president-founder of Institut international Shoghi, expressed satisfaction with the initiative, noting the inherent risks in school operations.

This initiative aims to foster a culture of risk prevention within educational establishments and provide lasting support to families, students, and institutions. The gathering also served to strengthen ties between insurance professionals and education sector stakeholders.

Why it mattersEducational institutions and their students in the Central African Republic are offered new insurance solutions to mitigate risks.
