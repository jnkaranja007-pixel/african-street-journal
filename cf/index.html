Central African Republic minors involved in sexual videos

Social media videos show adolescents in sexual acts, prompting calls for justice and parental guidance.

Videos depicting sexual acts involving adolescents, some of whom were minors at the time, have begun circulating on social media in the Central African Republic. The footage also appears to show alcohol consumption and, in some sequences, the presence of a weapon, raising concerns about coercion. Investigations are underway to determine the exact circumstances and responsibilities.

The images have sparked public outrage and calls for justice from internet users and civil society groups focused on child protection. The association "SENI TI A WALI" condemned the alleged acts and urged the public to protect the dignity and privacy of the young people involved. Minister of Youth, Sports, and Civic Education, Héritier Doneng, acknowledged the situation and stressed the importance of parental supervision for adolescents.

Two of the three boys implicated in the case are currently being held at the Unit for Mixed Rapid Intervention and Repression of Sexual Violence Against Women and Children (UMIRR) for questioning. The ongoing investigation aims to clarify the facts, establish accountability, and understand how the videos were produced and disseminated.

Why it mattersAdolescents in the Central African Republic are directly affected by the circulation of explicit videos on social media, raising concerns about their safety and privacy.

Bangui women struggle to grasp breast cancer awareness goals

Many women in the capital city remain unaware of the importance of early detection for breast cancer.

Despite the conclusion of the October Rose campaign in Bangui, many women report a lack of full understanding regarding its breast cancer awareness objectives. Some women still do not know why early screening is important because they have not received enough information.

Edda Lotine Djiko spoke with women in the capital about their comprehension of the campaign. The women's statements indicate a gap in the effective communication of vital health information during the awareness drive.

The campaign aimed to educate women about breast cancer and the benefits of early detection. The ongoing lack of understanding highlights a challenge in reaching all segments of the population with public health messages.

Central African forces launch offensive operations

Army units, supported by Russian specialists, dismantled illegal checkpoints and neutralized armed group members.

Central African Republic's security forces are adopting a more offensive stance to reclaim and secure national territory. The army, police, and gendarmerie are increasing their operational presence across the country. This shift aims to enhance the nation's security apparatus to anticipate threats, protect citizens, and defend territorial integrity.

The national army is undergoing a transformation, moving from a defensive posture to a more mobile and offensive intervention capability. In October 2026, FACA forces, with Russian military specialists, conducted a preventive operation in the Vakaga prefecture near Nabagai-Tissi to thwart an incursion from the tri-border area. Operations in Vakaga on October 28 and 30 led to the dismantling of an illegal checkpoint and the discovery of an armed group's base.

In a separate operation on November 5, 2026, south of Pendéré in Nana-Mambéré prefecture, nine members of an Anti-Balaka group, including their alleged leader Marcel Ndalé, were neutralized with Russian support. This operation secured the Bouar-Béloko trade route. Security cooperation with Russia and Rwanda, including training and intelligence sharing, is bolstering national forces. In June 2026, MINUSCA provided logistical support for redeploying over 300 FACA elements to Vakaga.

Why it mattersCentral African citizens are affected as the national army shifts to a more proactive strategy to combat armed groups and secure territory.

Business · 2026-11-10

Fuel shortage sparks inflation in Bangui

Transport costs and food prices are rising as stations struggle with supply for three weeks.

Fuel shortages are disrupting daily life in Bangui, the capital of the Central African Republic. For three weeks, multiple service stations have faced supply difficulties, leading to a scarcity of transport options.

The lack of fuel has caused transportation fares to increase significantly. This situation is directly impacting the cost of living for residents who rely on these services for their daily commutes and movement of goods.

The rising prices extend beyond transport, with certain food items also experiencing an upward trend in cost. This inflation is a direct consequence of the ongoing fuel supply disruptions affecting the city.

Why it mattersResidents of Bangui face higher costs for transport and food due to a persistent fuel shortage.

Politics · 2026-11-10

CPC denies general Ndalé's death after defence ministry announcement

The rebel group claims seven fighters died in an attack, contradicting official reports of the general's demise.

The Coalition des patriotes pour le changement (CPC) has denied the death of General Ndalé, an officer whose demise was announced by the Central African Republic's Ministry of Defence. The rebel movement stated that seven of its fighters, including two of the general's brothers, were killed during an attack on their camp.

Léopold Bara, the CPC's delegate for external relations, issued a statement refuting the official version of events. He asserted that General Ndalé is alive and well, and intends to continue his activities. The Ministry of Defence's director of cabinet had previously announced the rebel officer's death, a report that had been widely circulated by local media and social networks.

The CPC insists that the announcement of General Ndalé's death is false and that he successfully evaded the attack on his encampment. The group maintains that the general is in good health.
