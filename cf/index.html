Education · 2026-10-28

Bangui schools paralysed by floods

Two schools in the capital remain submerged, disrupting classes since Monday.

Heavy rains last Sunday have disrupted school activities in some Bangui institutions. The Koudoukou and Castors schools, located in the 3rd district, are completely flooded, slowing down the progress of classes since the start of the week.

Radio Ndeke Luka reported that the flooding is affecting the normal functioning of these educational establishments. The full extent of the disruption to student learning is not yet clear.

Activities at these schools remain paralysed two days after the initial inundation.

Why it mattersStudents in Bangui are unable to attend classes due to flooded schools.

Climate · 2026-10-28

former minister criticizes CAR flood response

Marie-Reine Hassen says authorities lack lasting solutions for recurring deadly floods in Bangui.

Former Foreign Affairs Minister Marie-Reine Hassen has criticized the Central African Republic's authorities for their inaction on deadly, recurring floods in the capital, Bangui. In an analysis published on October 26, 2026, she pointed to successive public policies and demanded accountability for urban planning, sanitation, and investments meant to protect residents.

Hassen specifically questioned the management of urban development and sanitation projects, stating that these have failed to provide lasting solutions to the recurrent flooding that affects the population. She called for transparency regarding the funds allocated for disaster prevention and mitigation efforts.

The former minister's statement highlights ongoing concerns about the city's vulnerability to extreme weather events and the effectiveness of government measures. The report did not specify any immediate next steps or reactions from current officials.

Why it mattersResidents of Bangui face deadly recurring floods due to the authorities' inaction.

Health · 2026-10-28

Central African Republic government and FAO train on forest management

A two-day workshop in Bangui aims to link sustainable forest use to preventing zoonotic disease risks.

The Central African Republic's Ministry of Forestry, Wildlife, and Aquatic Resources, in partnership with the FAO, organized a two-day training workshop in Bangui from October 27-28. The event focused on educating participants about sustainable forest management as a method to prevent zoonotic disease risks, using the "One Health" approach.

The workshop, part of the INFORBIO project, highlighted the preservation of forest ecosystems as a way to stop new pathogens that could threaten human health. Achille Mapouka, a focal point for the ministry, stressed that economic, social, and ecological factors must be considered before any forest use, stating that sustainable exploitation is maintenance, not destruction.

Participants are expected to share the knowledge gained with others and promote practices that prevent poor forest management. The project views well-managed forests as "health infrastructures" that can help alert systems for zoonotic diseases, which are transmissible between animals and humans.

Why it mattersThe workshop aims to reduce the risk of human health threats by promoting sustainable forest management.

News · 2026-10-28

bangui floods destroy homes and streets

The capital city of the Central African Republic is submerged, with streets and homes destroyed by severe flooding.

Bangui, the capital of the Central African Republic, is submerged in water after catastrophic floods destroyed homes and streets in the city center and peripheral neighborhoods. Images circulating on social media show vehicles trapped and buildings damaged by the raging torrents.

The scale of the recurring disaster has led residents to question the cause, with some asking if it is a divine deluge or the result of an irresponsible regime. Calls to God are multiplying as the city grapples with the aftermath.

This year's flooding has transformed the capital's streets into furious torrents, engulfing houses and demolishing walls. The recurring nature of these events raises questions about past warnings that went unheeded.

Why it mattersResidents of Bangui are directly affected by the destruction of their homes and streets due to severe flooding.

Politics · 2026-10-28

Amnesty urges CAR to arrest minister Hassan Bouba

The rights group calls for continued funding and prosecution of officials for war crimes.

Amnesty International is pressing Central African Republic authorities to execute arrest warrants issued by the Special Criminal Court (CPS), including one for Minister Hassan Bouba. The organization's report, "The perpetrators are still here," also calls for sustainable funding for the court, which has faced financial crises.

The report, based on a mission to Bangui and interviews with 63 people, acknowledges the CPS's progress, citing 19 convictions and one acquittal in its first three cases. However, Amnesty states that prosecutions of high-ranking officials remain insufficient. Hassan Bouba, a former UPC leader, was arrested in November 2021 for war crimes and crimes against humanity in the Alindao case but was released a week later while still a minister, with the Ministry of Justice citing the expiration of detention periods.

Amnesty also highlighted that arrest warrants for suspects abroad, including former president François Bozizé in Guinea-Bissau and others in Chad, remain unexecuted due to a lack of cooperation from those states. The withdrawal of US funding in 2025 has worsened the CPS's crisis, leading to staff layoffs and a halt in new investigations. Amnesty recommends sufficient multi-year funding and administrative and financial management transfer to the CPS registry.

Why it mattersMinister Hassan Bouba and other officials face potential prosecution for war crimes and crimes against humanity.
