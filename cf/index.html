BRDC calls November 13 protest in Bangui

The opposition bloc demands lower fuel prices and the release of civil society leaders.

The Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC) announced a peaceful march for November 13 in Bangui. The protest aims to demand lower fuel prices and the release of civil society leaders. Residents of Bangui, Bimbo, and Bégoua are called to gather at 6 AM.

The march will start from the Peace roundabout and proceed to the Martyrs monument via the Avenue des Martyrs. This demonstration follows an October 12 "ville morte" operation protesting the third fuel price increase in three years. BRDC stated they are responding to a proposal made by Faustin-Archange Touadéra during a meeting with trade unions.

The BRDC, a platform of the democratic opposition, made the announcement on October 30. The group is protesting the rising cost of living and fuel prices. The planned march is a response to the current government's policies.

Why it mattersCitizens in Bangui, Bimbo, and Bégoua will participate in a protest against the rising cost of living and fuel prices.

Bangui bank clients demand access to funds

Customers of the Caisses mutuelles de Centrafrique in Bangui's 4th arrondissement are frustrated by delays in accessing their money.

Clients of the Caisses mutuelles de Centrafrique (CMCA) in Bangui's 4th arrondissement are demanding access to their money. After multiple unsuccessful meetings, their impatience is growing with a management that urges patience and promises a return to normalcy.

The customers expressed their frustration over the inability to retrieve their funds. Management has appealed for calm, stating that a return to normal operations is anticipated.

MLPC disowns Martin Ziguélé, denounces destabilization plot

The MLPC party accuses former leader Martin Ziguélé of acting in the name of the party despite being removed from his position.

The MLPC party in Bangui officially distanced itself from Martin Ziguélé on October 20, 2026, denouncing a destabilization attempt. The party's Executive Bureau, led by President Tagotto Josephat, stated its commitment to working with the President of the Republic through legal and peaceful means. The MLPC warned of external threats and internal "traitors" seeking to destabilize the country, referencing past disruptions that caused displacement and economic damage.

The MLPC specifically addressed Martin Ziguélé, noting investigations into opposition party involvement in recent destabilization attempts. The party informed administrative and judicial authorities about Ziguélé's alleged deceptions, stating he was removed as MLPC President by the supreme Congress but continues to act and speak for the party. The Executive Bureau asserts that Ziguélé no longer controls the "real MLPC."

The MLPC accused individuals operating in the shadows of preparing the ground for attacks that have harmed families and the economy. The party stated these actors falsely appeal for sympathy while allegedly asking international bodies like the World Bank, IMF, and European Union not to financially support the country. The MLPC called for collective responsibility to unmask these alleged criminals.

Why it mattersThe MLPC party is denouncing a former leader, Martin Ziguélé, for allegedly attempting to destabilize the Central African Republic and acting against the party's interests.

Central African Republic seeks Russian investment in key sectors

President Touadéra urged Russian partners to invest in agriculture, mining, energy, and forestry for local resource transformation.

Central African Republic President Faustin-Archange Touadéra called for Russian investment in the nation's mines, energy, and agriculture sectors. Speaking at the third Russia-Africa Summit, Touadéra emphasized transforming raw materials locally to create jobs and added value within the country. He outlined four key expectations: local resource processing, technology and skills transfer, transparent investment contracts, and market access for processed agricultural goods.

Touadéra specifically highlighted agriculture and agro-industry, noting the country's fertile land and favorable climate could make it a regional breadbasket. He requested mechanization, fertilizers, seeds, and processing units. The president also pointed to the country's mineral wealth, including gold, diamonds, and uranium, advocating for responsible exploitation. Forestry and energy, particularly hydroelectric and solar power, were also presented as areas open for partnership.

The president's address aimed to shift from a model where raw materials leave Africa unprocessed. He stressed that cooperation should sustainably benefit both parties, with Africa retaining value addition and employment. Touadéra's proposals come as regional tensions rise, with the Central African Republic positioning troops along its border with Chad following accusations between Niger and Chad.

Why it mattersPresident Touadéra wants Russian investment to boost the Central African Republic's economy through local processing of resources and job creation.
