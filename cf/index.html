FAFECA opens digital hub for women entrepreneurs

The new center in Bangui aims to connect women to online sales platforms and the digital economy.

The Federation of Associations of Women Entrepreneurs of Central Africa (FAFECA) officially opened its digital incubator headquarters in Bangui on Friday, October 30, 2026. Located at Benz-Vi, the incubator will help women access online sales platforms and integrate into the digital economy.

Minister of Small and Medium Enterprises, Hippolyte Robard Ngate, inaugurated the center, emphasizing the government's commitment to women's entrepreneurship. He stated that women need to add digital skills to their natural and traditional competencies to make their products and services competitive. Jessica Tengue Slowan, CEO of Slowan Digital Business and a FAFECA digital sector member, highlighted the incubator's role in empowering women entrepreneurs.

The new headquarters is ready to host training sessions for women seeking to develop their online businesses. FAFECA aims to adapt digital tools to the realities of each member entrepreneur.

Why it mattersWomen entrepreneurs in the Central African Republic can now access training and resources to develop their businesses online.

Central African Republic ranks 50th out of 54 African nations in governance index

The country scores 31.3 out of 100, falling below the continental average and showing a decline in key areas.

The Central African Republic (CAR) is ranked 50th out of 54 African countries in the latest Mo Ibrahim Foundation governance index, published in October 2026. With a score of 31.3 out of 100, the CAR falls significantly below the continental average of 50.1. The report, which analyzes data from 2025 based on trends since 2016, highlights a weakening of checks and balances and particularly poor performance in education and health sectors.

The CAR's governance score has declined by 0.9 points over the past decade, and it has fallen one place in the rankings. The Mo Ibrahim Foundation specifically notes an acceleration in governance deterioration since 2021. The report cites the removal of the constitutional court president by presidential decree in 2022 and the 2023 constitution removing presidential term limits as reasons for a 10.9-point drop in accountability and transparency since 2016.

In education, the CAR ranks last with 11.4 points, shared with Somalia, and in health, it is 53rd out of 54 countries with 40.7 points. While a 2024 law protecting human rights defenders was noted as encouraging, its effects remain to be seen. The index uses 96 indicators across security, participation, economic conditions, and human development to assess governance.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic citizens are directly affected by the country's poor performance in education and health services, as well as the weakening of institutions meant to hold leaders accountable.

Central African Republic fuel shortage continues

Long queues persist at Bangui petrol stations, disrupting daily life and commerce.

Fuel scarcity continues to grip Bangui, the capital of the Central African Republic, with long queues of vehicles still visible at petrol stations. This ongoing shortage is making it difficult for people to travel and is disrupting commercial activities across the city.

Motorcycle taxi drivers and car owners are expressing their worry over the persistent supply difficulties. Edouard Rogossé observed the situation firsthand, visiting several petrol stations within the capital to document the extent of the scarcity.

The lack of fuel is complicating essential journeys and hindering the normal flow of business. Residents are concerned about how this situation will impact their livelihoods and daily routines if the scarcity is not resolved soon.

Why it mattersResidents of Bangui face difficulties with transportation and business operations due to the ongoing fuel shortage.

Central African Republic leader uses new constitution for life term

President Touadéra's March 30, 2026 inauguration solidifies his grip on power, resetting previous mandates.

President Touadéra's inauguration on March 30, 2026, marks a definitive shift in Central African Republic's governance, utilizing a new constitution adopted on March 30, 2023. This move, referred to by citizens as the "MCU status," allows the executive leader to extend his tenure beyond standard limits, presented officially as a measure for national stability but seen by many as a method to prevent political alternation.

The new constitutional framework enables the president to reset previous mandates by designating himself as the first leader under this new phase, effectively erasing prior terms. This constitutional manipulation is described as turning the nation's rule into a tool for the ruling party's perpetuation, limiting the national vision to the survival of one man and his political organization.

The regime justifies its indefinite term by emphasizing a constant struggle against unrest, imposing strict control over public spaces through security services. While grand infrastructure projects are promised, they have remained unfulfilled for a decade, with public funds and resources reportedly prioritizing the security apparatus that supports the president's position. Families face increasing precarity despite claims of visible results.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic citizens face a government that prioritizes the leader's extended rule and security apparatus over social progress and visible improvements to their living conditions.

China trains Central African peacekeepers

Police and gendarmes from Bangui attend a global security seminar at Renmin University.

Central African peacekeepers from Bangui are attending a global security seminar at China's Renmin University to enhance their capabilities. The training program includes police and gendarmes, institutions tasked with ensuring public safety and upholding the law across the Central African Republic. Before arriving in Beijing, participants visited rural villages in Yunnan province, observing community-based security and governance.

The seminar focuses on contemporary security challenges such as transnational threats and population protection, aiming to broaden the officers' international perspective. China is sharing its experience and academic resources, enabling the Central African cadres to adapt new methods to their national context. This human resource cooperation aims to modernize security forces and build trust between them and citizens.

Upon their return, the trained officers will apply their acquired knowledge to improve security for Central Africans. This initiative represents an opportunity for the Central African Republic to update its security practices and strengthen the relationship between its security forces and the public.

Why it mattersCentral African police and gendarmes are receiving training in China to improve national security and public trust.
