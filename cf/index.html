Parliament's Lack of Opposition Raises Concerns for Public Representation

A debate is underway regarding the risk of a single-party national assembly and whether this absence of strong contradiction weakens the voice of the people.

The Central African Republic's national assembly faces scrutiny over the potential for a dominant political party, raising questions about its ability to represent the populace. This situation prompts a crucial discussion on whether a legislature lacking robust opposition can adequately address the needs of the citizens.

Jean Symphorien Mapenzi, the first vice-president of the National Assembly, and Yves Sanghamy, spokesperson for the Union for Renewal of Central Africa (URCA), are central figures in this ongoing debate. Their participation highlights the differing perspectives on the implications of a potentially unipolar legislative body.

The core issue revolves around the risk of a 'monocolor' assembly, where a lack of strong dissenting voices could diminish the influence and representation of the general public. This concern underscores the importance of diverse viewpoints in democratic governance.

Why it mattersThe public is affected because a parliament without solid opposition may not adequately represent their needs or voice their concerns.

Central African Republic Officials Enhance Public Finance Management

Government members and financial managers are attending a two-day seminar in Bangui focused on budget reforms and transparency.

Government members and financial managers are participating in a seminar in Bangui focused on budget reforms and transparency in public finance management. Organized by the Ministry of Finance and Budget with support from the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the event aims to strengthen fiscal discipline and resource management. Discussions cover the budget cycle, public spending, treasury operations, and accountability mechanisms.

The seminar is intended to consolidate the government's efforts to improve state resource management and enhance budgetary discipline, according to the Prime Minister. Authorities also aim to build the capacity of officials to promote more rigorous and transparent management of public funds. This initiative comes as international partners, including the IMF, World Bank, and African Development Bank, urge increased transparency and domestic resource mobilization.

The two-day meeting addresses reforms ahead of the implementation of program-based budgeting within the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC). Participants are exchanging views on the budget cycle, public expenditure management, treasury operations, and control mechanisms. The goal is to foster a more rigorous and transparent approach to public finances, reducing reliance on external funding.

Why it mattersGovernment officials and financial managers are affected by this seminar as it aims to improve their capacity for more rigorous and transparent management of public funds.

Campaign Mobilizes Citizens for Cleanup Ahead of Inauguration

The 'Kwa ti Kodro' operation saw ruling party affiliates cleaning public spaces in Grand Bangui and other cities.

The 'Kwa ti Kodro' operation, launched on Saturday, March 7, 2026, mobilized crowds in Grand Bangui and several provincial towns for garbage collection and street weeding. While the Ministry of Territorial Administration stated its coordinators supervised the events, photographic evidence and accounts primarily show militants from the United Hearts Movement (MCU) and women's associations linked to the government participating.

Activities included presidential palace staff sweeping grounds ahead of International Women's Day, MCU members and allied associations cleaning stadium areas, and party-aligned citizen action committees in markets taking up brooms. Minister coordinators focused their visits on these symbolic sites, with repeated references to March 30, the scheduled inauguration date for the Seventh Republic's leader.

In cities like Bria, Bossembélé, Berbérati, and Sibut, prefects and neighborhood leaders enlisted party youth, MCU women, and pro-presidency groups for the cleanup efforts. This mobilization occurred while Sibut residents were still dealing with the aftermath of high school disturbances, including student arrests and lingering anger.

Why it mattersThe operation affects citizens by mobilizing them for public cleaning activities, which are presented as civic duty but are linked to political events and party affiliations.

Opposition Leader Contests Security Claims

Raymond Adouma stated that the pacification claimed by President Touadéra exists only in speeches, not in reality for citizens.

Opposition figure Raymond Adouma directly challenged the government's security assertions, stating that the pacification claimed by President Faustin-Archange Touadéra exists only in speeches. Adouma argued that in the hinterlands and areas surrounding Bangui, the population cannot freely go about their activities due to armed groups obstructing movement and preventing normal circulation on roads.

Adouma pointed out that despite the national army having at least thirty-three thousand men, these forces are unable to secure areas near the capital or restore peace in villages, leaving towns isolated due to poor road infrastructure. He also criticized the presence of private militias, which he attributed to Minister Héritier Doneng, arguing that a republic cannot be built on unaccountable groups.

The former minister contested the president's recent investiture speech, highlighting a contradiction between claims of one hundred percent pacification and a new call for it. Adouma also questioned the disproportionate spending on foreign forces compared to the national army, suggesting it undermines security sovereignty. Minister Doneng defended the government's record, citing territorial reconquest and renewed community cohesion, but Adouma rejected these claims as not reflecting the population's reality.

Why it mattersCitizens in rural areas and around the capital are unable to move freely due to armed groups and poor infrastructure, contradicting official claims of security.

Powers Sécurité launches internal hunt for employees leaking information

The company's deputy general director is reportedly seeking to identify staff suspected of sharing details with the press.

Powers Sécurité has initiated an internal investigation to identify employees suspected of leaking information to Corbeau News Centrafrique. This comes as the company's deputy general director issued a response to the outlet contesting recent publications. The internal search for whistleblowers has intensified, creating a tense atmosphere within the organization.

Tensions escalated last Friday during a farewell ceremony in Bangui for a Powers Sécurité employee departing for the Bank of Central African States (BEAC). The event, held at a venue on De Gaulle Avenue near the French embassy, saw staff grouped by department and function, including human resources, controllers, inspectors, and managers.

The deputy general director, identified as Pasteur Osé Balezou, is overseeing the internal probe. The company's efforts to control information flow follow critical reports from Corbeau News Centrafrique, suggesting internal dissent or unauthorized disclosures are a significant concern for management.

Why it mattersEmployees of Powers Sécurité are affected by an internal investigation and potential disciplinary actions for sharing company information.
