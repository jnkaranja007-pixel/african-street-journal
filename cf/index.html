Bangui floods leave over 2,000 homeless

The Ministry of Humanitarian Action is distributing essential kits to those affected by recent heavy rains.

Heavy rains in Bangui and its outskirts have left over 2,000 people homeless. The Ministry of Humanitarian Action, with support from the UNHCR, is distributing essential kits to affected residents in the 4th arrondissement. These kits include items like tarpaulins, buckets, mats, and cooking pots.

Minister of Humanitarian Action, Lina Josiane Bemakassoui, stated on Monday in Bangui that efforts are underway to assist the victims, despite facing difficulties. Residents from sectors 4 I and 4 II gathered at the 4th arrondissement police station to receive aid. Wini Edoxie Yakona, a resident, described losing all her belongings when the Ngouya Bagara canal overflowed into her home.

The head of Dedengué 4 neighborhood, Dieudonné Ngofio, urged the government to build more canals to improve water flow and reduce future flood risks. He also noted that some flooding is caused by residents dumping waste into drainage channels. The Ministry has called on partners to provide further assistance to the affected population.

Why it mattersOver 2,000 people in Bangui are homeless and have lost their belongings due to severe flooding caused by heavy rains.

Central African Republic fuel prices rise, transport costs follow

Transport fares increase in Bangui one month after fuel prices went up at the pump.

Fuel prices have risen in Bangui, the capital of the Central African Republic, one month after the initial increase at the pump. This situation is linked to both the higher cost of fuel and its scarcity. Prices for transport, particularly for vehicles running on gasoline, are now negotiated individually.

Trade unionist Nicaise Ngouandji described the government's communication on the fuel price hike as a "return of the head tax that does not say its name." He criticized the decision as anti-social and imposed without consultation, making Central Africans the "financial and technical partner" of a struggling state.

The increase in transport costs affects the daily lives of citizens in Bangui. The government has not announced specific next steps regarding fuel availability or pricing adjustments.

Why it mattersCitizens in Bangui face higher transport costs due to increased fuel prices and scarcity.

Central African Republic governance score drops

The country ranks 50th out of 54 African nations in the 2026 Mo Ibrahim Foundation governance report.

The Central African Republic's governance has significantly declined, scoring only 31.3 out of 100 according to the 2026 Mo Ibrahim Foundation report. The country now ranks 50th among 54 African states, losing 0.9 points over the past decade.

This assessment covers most of President Faustin Archange Touadéra's tenure since 2016. While some economic and social progress was noted, it was outweighed by a deterioration in public freedoms, political participation, and institutional effectiveness.

The report, focusing on the 2016-2025 period, highlights that improvements in certain areas did not compensate for the decline in governance indicators.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic's governance has worsened, affecting public freedoms, political participation, and institutional functioning.

Four killed near Tiringoulou

Unidentified armed men killed four young men returning from a gold mine.

Four people were killed last Monday about 30 kilometers from Tiringoulou, on the Birao axis in the Vakaga region. The victims, young men returning from a gold mine, were killed by unidentified armed men. The mayor of Ouandja commune, Al-Abib Yaya, spoke about the circumstances of the killings.

Russian mercenaries from the Wagner group killed four young Central Africans of the Kara ethnic group between Tiringoulou and Délémbé. The bodies were decapitated and then burned with their motorcycles. One of the victims was identified as a member of the self-defense militia known as the Black Wagner.

The incident occurred near the village of Délémbé.
