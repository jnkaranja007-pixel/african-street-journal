Central African Republic army neutralizes Anti-Balaka leader

Nine members of the Anti-Balaka group, including chief Marcel Ndalé, were killed in an operation on November 5.

Central African Republic's armed forces, supported by Russian allies, killed nine Anti-Balaka members, including their leader Marcel Ndalé, on November 5. The operation took place south of Pendéré in the Nana-Mambéré region. The Ministry of Defence announced the successful operation on Friday, November 6, 2026.

The Ministry of Defence accused Ndalé's group of robbing and murdering gold miners, traders, and other civilians. Marcel Ndalé, also known as Gbokaho Minang Sylvain and "King of the Bush," was killed along with his brother Adamou Ndalé and seven other fighters. The ministry stated the group was preparing attacks against government forces and local authorities.

The communiqué also alleged the group planned to attack trucks on the Bouar–Béloko route and make incursions into Cameroon to create border tensions. Military authorities reported the situation is under control but will continue sweeps to find fleeing fighters. Residents are urged to report any useful security information.

Why it mattersThe operation against the Anti-Balaka group aims to stop violence and secure trade routes in the Nana-Mambéré region.

Central African Republic president attends UN reforestation launch

Faustin-Archange Touadéra joined Congo's president in Brazzaville for the 2027-2036 UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration.

Central African Republic President Faustin-Archange Touadéra arrived in Brazzaville, Republic of Congo, on Thursday evening to attend the official launch of the UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration, running from 2027 to 2036. The international initiative focuses on sustainable forest management amid growing concerns about deforestation and climate change. President Touadéra was invited by his Congolese counterpart, Denis Sassou-Nguesso, and will participate in the official ceremony on Friday, November 6, alongside the president of the 81st UN General Assembly session, Khalilur Rahman.

The launch event includes a tree-planting campaign at the ECO-NZAMBA project site, approximately 45 kilometers from Brazzaville. This decade-long program aims to mobilize states, international institutions, private companies, and civil society for concrete actions to restore forest cover. It also plans to launch the "Youth for the Decade" initiative, engaging young people in urban greening and the development of forestry and agroforestry businesses.

For the Central African Republic, participation in this event is particularly relevant due to its significant forest resources within the Congo Basin and shared ecosystem preservation challenges with the Republic of Congo. The president's agenda also includes a one-on-one meeting with President Sassou-Nguesso and other bilateral discussions, potentially addressing cooperation on environmental issues between Bangui, Brazzaville, and international partners.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic's participation highlights its commitment to environmental protection and sustainable resource management, potentially strengthening regional partnerships.

Centrafrican schools prepare for December 1st parade

Students in Bimbo practiced marching drills on November 5 for the upcoming national day celebration.

Schools in Bimbo, Central African Republic, held their second preparatory session on Thursday, November 5, for the December 1, 2026, national day parade. Students from public and private institutions gathered, dressed in their school colours, to practice marching routines. The event commemorates the country's transition from Oubangui-Chari to the Central African Republic.

Teacher Brice Ngaïté from M’Poko Bac school noted the good atmosphere among students, despite the early stage of preparations. However, some attendees, like François Nglomoudé, observed that students did not consistently follow the marching rhythm, leading to a less harmonious display. Nglomoudé suggested that teachers need to reinforce the marching cadence for better organisation.

The countdown has begun for the December 1 celebration, but the environment in Bimbo and its surrounding areas has not yet been decorated. Preparations for the parade are ongoing across various cities in the Central African Republic.

Why it mattersStudents are practicing for a national day parade on December 1, 2026, which marks the country's transition from Oubangui-Chari to the Central African Republic.

Politics · 2026-11-07

Bangui court issues questionable arrest warrant for MP

A document dated November 2, 2026, purportedly from a Bangui judge, orders the arrest of MP Élie-Allard Namtoli without specifying charges or citing immunity.

A document dated November 2, 2026, presented as an arrest warrant for Member of Parliament Élie-Allard Namtoli, is circulating and raising questions about its legitimacy. The warrant, attributed to second special investigating judge Doumely Nakoé Basile Donald of the Tribunal de grande instance of Bangui, orders international forces, bailiffs, and law enforcement to bring the MP before the magistrate.

The document, bearing the letterhead of the Court of Appeal of Bangui, the High Court, and special investigating chamber number 2, lacks any mention of the specific offenses attributed to the parliamentarian. It does not reference any procedure for lifting his parliamentary immunity.

This raises concerns about whether the document is a genuine judicial act or a fabricated instrument designed to provide a legal facade for an operation targeting a parliamentarian. The absence of specified charges and the disregard for immunity procedures are notable anomalies.
