Health · 2026-10-30

Rotary Club supports polio response in Bangui

Community health workers trained to identify and report suspected polio cases following detection of two cases in Ouango.

The Rotary Club Bangui Douain Rénové and partners are supporting the Central African Republic's Ministry of Public Health in response to two polio cases detected in Ouango, Bangui. A training session for community health agents took place on Monday, October 26, to enhance surveillance and active case finding.

Émelien Gérard Nerguidima, representing the Rotary Club, stated that the training aims to equip agents to better identify suspected cases in their neighborhoods and refer them to health facilities. These agents, living within the communities, are crucial for early detection and public awareness of polio prevention measures. Nerguidima noted that the presence of two cases suggests potentially more within the community.

Since the start of 2026, seven polio cases have been recorded in the Central African Republic, with two of these in Ouango. This initiative, extending beyond World Polio Day on October 24, involves the Ministry of Public Health, Rotary Club, WHO, and UNICEF in the global polio eradication effort.

Education · 2026-10-30

Alumni donate 32 novels to Bangui school

The Association of Former LAENS Students provided literary works to enhance the school's library resources.

The Lycée d’Application de l’École Normale Supérieure de Bangui received 32 literary novels on Monday, October 26, 2026, from the Association of Former Students of LAENS (2AELAENS). The donation aims to support youth and strengthen educational resources, following last year's contribution of scientific novels.

Nadège Edang-Ndi, president of 2AELAENS, stated the books would help students research, understand lessons, and pass exams, covering all levels from sixth grade to final year. Arlette Djohodomo, a school official, expressed gratitude, noting the novels would help students, particularly science students, improve their French language skills.

The donation includes works such as Émile Zola's Germinal and Aimé Césaire's La Tragédie du roi Christophe. The association encouraged the school administration and students to preserve the books for long-term educational and cultural use.

Why it mattersStudents at the Lycée d’Application de l’École Normale Supérieure de Bangui will benefit from new literary resources to aid their studies and cultural enrichment.

Politics · 2026-10-30

Amnesty International warns of special court closure

The Central African Republic's special court risks closure by 2028 due to a lack of funding.

Amnesty International has alerted that the Special Criminal Court (CPS) in the Central African Republic faces closure by 2028 due to insufficient funding. The organization stated that only the UN and EU are currently providing financial support to the court, which is tasked with prosecuting war crimes and crimes against humanity. This lack of funds has forced the court to halt investigations, leaving alleged perpetrators of international crimes potentially free.

The CPS relies exclusively on voluntary contributions from states and international organizations. In 2025, the United States withdrew its financial support, leaving over $4.15 million in unpaid commitments. This funding crisis led the court to dismiss a quarter of its staff, including foreign experts, between August and September 2025, impacting its witness protection unit.

Amnesty International is calling on Central African authorities, the UN, the EU, and partner states to provide or continue financial support for the CPS's operational budget. The court's national counterparts reportedly lack the resources and independence to handle these complex cases.

Why it mattersThe Special Criminal Court in the Central African Republic risks closure, potentially allowing perpetrators of war crimes and crimes against humanity to escape justice.
