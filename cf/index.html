Foreign firms accused of forest exploitation in CAR

The Sentry report details alleged tax evasion and illegal logging by Russian and Chinese companies.

An American NGO, The Sentry, released findings on November 10 detailing serious irregularities in the Central African Republic's forestry sector. The report accuses foreign companies, primarily Russian and Chinese, of non-payment of exploitation taxes, logging protected trees, and damaging forest ecosystems. Between 2021 and 2026, nearly 40% of forest concessions were reportedly controlled by Russian firms linked to the Wagner group and Chinese companies.

The Sentry's investigation highlights that these companies regularly violate the Central African Republic's forestry code by not paying taxes, disregarding their contractual obligations, and failing to generate economic benefits for local communities. Alex Bloom, who led the investigation, stated that potentially billions in taxes were not paid for exploitation permits, representing a significant loss for the Central African nation. The report also denounces the felling of trees protected under the CITES convention, including padouk, bibinga, and mahogany, with over 1500 instances of five protected species being exported.

The Sentry urges Central African authorities to revise the forestry code to strengthen sector regulation, improve governance, and ensure greater transparency. Recommendations include disclosing the real owners of forest concessions and reclaiming concessions belonging to the Wagner group. Neither the Central African authorities nor the implicated Russian and Chinese companies have yet commented on the investigation's findings.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic faces significant revenue loss and environmental damage due to alleged illegal forestry practices by foreign companies.

Health · 2026-11-12

Gambo hospital lacks basic facilities

A 1978 building in southeastern Central African Republic operates without an operating room or adequate medical supplies.

The hospital in Gambo, Mbomou prefecture, operates from a building constructed in 1978, lacking an operating room and essential medical resources.

Two qualified agents provide care amidst shortages of beds, mattresses, and electricity, forcing some patients to travel 75 kilometers to Bangassou for treatment, with reports of deaths during these journeys.

The facility houses multiple services including consultations, general medicine, pediatrics, and surgery within the same inadequate spaces, failing to meet the community's healthcare needs.

Why it mattersResidents of Gambo and surrounding areas lack adequate medical care due to the hospital's severe infrastructure and resource deficiencies.

BRDC rejects government claims on planned Bangui march

Master Crépin Mboli-Goumba of BRDC countered government spokesman Évariste Ngamana's arguments against a peaceful demonstration.

The Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC) firmly rejected arguments from government spokesman Évariste Ngamana regarding a planned peaceful march in Bangui. Master Crépin Mboli-Goumba, BRDC coordinator, stated on Monday, November 9, 2026, that Ngamana's legal points against the demonstration were unfounded. The march is scheduled for Friday, November 13, 2026, starting at 6 a.m. on Avenue des Martyrs.

The BRDC is calling on residents of Bangui, Bimbo, and Bégoua to participate in the mobilization against the policies of President Faustin-Archange Touadéra's administration. The government had previously agreed to secure the march, which aims to protest the high cost of living. The BRDC encourages attendees to use the event to demand better living conditions.

Master Crépin Mboli-Goumba, speaking on November 9, 2026, reminded the government spokesman of constitutional provisions governing public demonstrations. The BRDC's planned march is intended to denounce the rising cost of living and advocate for improved living standards for citizens.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic citizens are affected by the high cost of living and are organizing a march to demand better conditions.

Politics · 2026-11-12

Three judicial experts sworn in at Bangui Court of Appeal

Jean Louis Hubert Benimet, Véronique Vondo, and Akpekabou Maleombho Djetou Madina will now provide technical advice to magistrates.

The Court of Appeal in Bangui officially swore in three new judicial experts on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. Jean Louis Hubert Benimet, an expert in damages, Véronique Vondo, an expert in taxation, and Akpekabou Maleombho Djetou Madina, an expert in intellectual property, have joined the Central African Republic's official list of judicial experts.

These specialists will offer magistrates technical and specialized advice to aid in legal decisions, particularly for complex cases involving economic, fiscal, commercial, or intellectual property matters. Benimet, who waited nearly three years for this moment, aims to educate economic actors on the importance of damage assessment in resolving disputes related to transport and goods. Madina, a specialist in business and intellectual property law, sees her appointment as recognition for years of hard work and a commitment to serve her country with professionalism.

Benimet highlighted the crucial role of damage assessment in securing commercial exchanges across land, air, and river transport. He called for a sincere partnership among stakeholders to ensure the effectiveness of this function for economic operators and the justice system. Madina expressed her satisfaction, viewing the oath as a commitment to serve with rigor and integrity.

Why it mattersThe appointment of three new judicial experts strengthens the Central African Republic's legal system by providing specialized technical advice for complex cases.
