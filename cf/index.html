Central African Republic signs deals with Russia

Agreements cover communication, justice, transport, and energy sectors following Moscow summit.

The Central African Republic government announced on November 4, 2026, that it signed multiple accords and memorandums of understanding with Russia. These agreements, reached after the Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow from October 25-30, 2026, span the communication, media, justice, transport, and energy sectors. The government views these commitments as opportunities to accelerate national development priorities.

During the summit, Central African Republic's President Faustin Archange Touadéra met with Russian President Vladimir Putin. They discussed economic cooperation and the implementation of agreements from the first session of the intergovernmental commission held in Bangui in September 2026. Russia reaffirmed its readiness to support the Central African Republic through investments and cooperation in education, health, agriculture, industry, economy, finance, digital, and media.

The Central African Republic government expressed satisfaction with Russia's approach to cooperation, which respects national sovereignty and strategic choices. This partnership aims to build a more productive economy, strengthen national capacities, and consolidate economic independence. Officials believe the summit results will usher in a new phase for Russia-Central African Republic relations.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic government signed new agreements with Russia to boost development in key sectors.

Central African Republic distributes aid after floods

The Ministry of Humanitarian Action, with UNHCR support, provided essential kits to over 2,000 people displaced by heavy rains.

The Ministry of Humanitarian Action in the Central African Republic, supported by the UN High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), distributed essential kits to flood victims in Bangui's 4th arrondissement. The aid followed torrential rains that inundated the capital and its surroundings, leaving over 2,000 people homeless and three dead. Beneficiaries received items such as tarpaulins, buckets, mats, cooking pots, and soap at the 4th arrondissement police station.

Minister of Humanitarian Action, Lina Josiane Bemakassoui, stated that efforts are underway to assist the displaced, acknowledging difficulties and calling for partner support. Residents like Wini Edoxie Yakona, whose home near the Ngouya Bagara canal was flooded, expressed gratitude for the assistance, noting previous instances where aid was promised but not delivered. The head of Dedengué 4 neighborhood, Dieudonné Ngofio, also thanked the government but urged for improved water drainage systems to prevent future floods.

Despite the current aid, flood victims report that their needs remain significant and are appealing for further support from authorities and benevolent individuals. Some residents contribute to the flooding by dumping waste into drainage canals, exacerbating the problem. The ministry is working to address the immediate crisis while residents and local leaders call for long-term solutions, including the construction of better water channels.

Why it mattersOver 2,000 people in Bangui are left without shelter and essential supplies due to severe flooding caused by torrential rains.

Police and students clash in Bangui

Tension erupts at Lycée des Martyrs after police attempt to arrest a student over a phone theft.

Violent clashes erupted on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, near the Lycée des Martyrs in Bangui, as police and students engaged in a stone-throwing battle. The confrontations, marked by gunshots in the air and crowd surges, disrupted parts of the Central African Republic's capital. Several students experiencing distress were taken to the community hospital.

The conflict began when police attempted to arrest a student at the Lycée des Martyrs for alleged phone theft. According to reports, the law enforcement officers did not adhere to proper procedures during the apprehension. Upon learning of the arrest, fellow students left their classes and headed to the police station to demand explanations.

The incident occurred near the Compagnie Nationale de Sécurité (CNS). The students' protest at the police station escalated into the wider confrontation that paralyzed traffic and caused chaos in the city.

Why it mattersStudents at the Lycée des Martyrs in Bangui were directly affected by violent clashes with police, leading to some requiring hospital treatment.

CEMAC customs committees meet in Bangui

Experts from six member states are reviewing regulations to boost local production and cut import reliance.

Customs experts from the six member states of the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC) are meeting in Bangui from November 2 to 6, 2026. The technical meeting aims to establish regulatory foundations for a regional policy to reduce import dependency, strengthen local production, and enhance economic integration.

Seli Mbogo Ngabo, CEMAC Commissioner for the Common Market, opened the meeting, emphasizing the committees' role in harmonizing customs and fiscal regulations. The experts are examining conclusions from a July workshop in Brazzaville on implementing the regional import-substitution strategy adopted in March 2023. This strategy targets local production of rice, cassava, wheat, beef, and fish to decrease structural reliance on imports.

The strategy seeks to stimulate local output, reinforce regional value chains, and limit foreign currency outflows for food imports. The Bangui session will determine regulatory mechanisms for the effective application of these recommendations.

Bangui faces fuel shortage for over a week

Residents struggle with transport and rising food prices due to persistent fuel scarcity.

Fuel shortages have gripped Bangui for more than a week, disrupting daily life for residents. The difficulty in getting fuel at petrol stations has led to a lack of public transport options.

This scarcity has caused transport fares to increase and has also driven up the prices of essential food items. Citizens affected by this crisis are urging the government to take swift action to resolve the situation.

The ongoing fuel crisis is significantly limiting people's ability to move around the city.

Why it mattersResidents of Bangui are directly affected by the fuel shortage, facing difficulties with transportation and increased costs for food.
