Parents Avoid Bangui Pediatric Center After Care Costs Return

Fewer children are being brought to the Complexe pédiatrique de Bangui as some parents cite the reintroduction of medical fees and poor reception as reasons for seeking.

The Complexe pédiatrique de Bangui is experiencing a noticeable decline in patient numbers following the suspension of certain free care programs funded by partners. The return of some medical fees is causing families to hesitate before seeking treatment for their children at the facility. Reports of unwelcoming reception also contribute to this trend, though the management assures that care remains available.

Marceline, a mother whose daughter has measles, observed a significant drop in attendance, stating, "There are not enough sick people." She noted that waiting rooms and hospital beds are largely empty, a stark contrast to previous times. This situation arises after the government, with partner support, shifted from total free care to a targeted free care system in early 2026, covering children under five, pregnant, and breastfeeding women, though medication availability impacts coverage.

Beyond the reintroduction of some fees for consultations, exams, or medications, which pushes some families towards self-medication or local remedies, parents also complain about the reception process. An anonymous user described a long wait with a doctor unavailable due to fatigue. The director of the pediatric complex attributes reception issues to a lack of civility and has appealed to authorities and partners for support.

Why it mattersParents are affected by the reintroduction of medical fees and poor reception, leading them to avoid the pediatric center and seek alternative treatments for their children.

Business · 2026-09-03

ENERCA equipment appears on Bangui markets

The Central African electricity provider launched a subsidized connection program in July, providing equipment for 5,000 CFA francs.

The Central African electricity provider, ENERCA, has initiated an investigation into the discovery of its branded equipment on Bangui markets. The Section des recherches et d’investigations (SRI) is tasked with uncovering the circumstances surrounding these findings. ENERCA began offering subsidized connections in July, supplying customers with all necessary electrical components, including meters and circuit breakers, for a mere 5,000 CFA francs.

Despite the low cost and the intention to provide essential materials for new connections, these subsidized items have rapidly surfaced for resale. The Miskine market, located in the 5th arrondissement of Bangui, is among the locations where these ENERCA-stamped objects have been observed. The interim Director General of ENERCA, Bienvenu Mony Beya, has provided further details regarding the situation.

This development raises questions about the distribution and control of subsidized electrical materials intended for public use. The investigation by the SRI aims to determine how these items, meant for direct customer connections, ended up being sold on the open market, potentially undermining the subsidized program's objectives and ENERCA's efforts to expand access to electricity.

Why it mattersThe appearance of ENERCA equipment on markets affects investors by potentially indicating mismanagement or theft within the subsidized connection program, which could impact the company's financial operations and public trust.

Business · 2026-09-03

Chili Shortage Drives Prices Up in Bangui Markets

Consumers in the Central African Republic's capital are lamenting the scarcity of chili peppers, a staple spice, as prices have quadrupled.

Chili peppers, a highly prized spice for enhancing food flavor, are increasingly scarce in Bangui's markets, leaving consumers disappointed. For several weeks, the availability of this essential ingredient has dwindled, impacting daily meals and culinary practices across the city. The situation has led to widespread complaints from residents who rely on chili to add zest to their dishes.

The price surge is particularly notable. A 'Banco' sack, approximately five kilograms of chili, which previously cost 5,000 CFA francs, is now being sold for 20,000 CFA francs. This represents a significant increase, making the spice unaffordable for many households and altering purchasing habits.

This scarcity and the accompanying price hike are affecting the daily lives of Bangui's residents, who consider chili a fundamental part of their cuisine. The elevated cost poses a challenge for families trying to maintain their traditional diets and enjoy flavorful meals.

Why it mattersBangui consumers are affected by the scarcity and quadrupled price of chili peppers, making a popular spice unaffordable.

News · 2026-09-03

Central African Republic Authorities Offer Propaganda Amidst Flooding

Despite annual floods leaving citizens in mud, officials in Bangui issue empty promises and disconnected communication.

Annual rains continue to leave residents of the Central African Republic mired in mud, while authorities in Bangui engage in what critics call "pathetic propaganda" and "false promises." For a decade, the current administration has offered no tangible improvements in flood-affected areas of the capital. Official discourse, repeated annually, is delivered by leaders skilled in deception, who seem to believe citizens will accept mere words as a record of achievement.

The government's handling of floods reveals a systemic institutional inertia. Despite pronouncements about drainage systems and urban planning, the reality on the ground contradicts these claims. State structures prioritize public servant salaries to prevent strikes and maintain the regime, but beyond payroll, the treasury is empty, paralyzing the state's ability to fund significant public infrastructure.

When it comes to essential repairs or immediate relief, the government relies on international aid from partners like MINUSCA, the World Bank, the African Development Bank, the European Union, and France. Ministry officials, often appointed through nepotism, are incapable of independent policy-making and prefer media appearances. Recent statements blamed citizens' behavior and illegal construction for floods, deflecting from the state's urban planning failures. Humanitarian aid intended for flood victims has reportedly been diverted to private school construction projects owned by a minister.

Why it mattersCitizens are directly affected by the lack of infrastructure and aid, forcing them to live in mud and inadequate conditions due to government mismanagement and alleged corruption.

Eighteen Unions Accuse Masua Gogi of Dividing Labor Movement

A collective of 18 Central African labor unions claims Masua Gogi is undermining their demands presented to President Touadéra.

Eighteen Central African labor unions have accused Masua Gogi of actions intended to derail their official demands presented to President Faustin-Archange Touadéra. The collective voiced these accusations during a press conference in Bangui on August 21, 2026. The unions stated their grievances were formally submitted to the head of state on July 3, 2026, at the Cité des chefs d’État, with the Prime Minister and government members present.

The collective asserts that Masua Gogi's activities are aimed at challenging the legitimacy and progress of their negotiations with the government. Union representative Louis-Marie KOGRENGBO was among those speaking at the press conference. The group seeks to maintain a united front in their pursuit of specific labor demands.

The accusations highlight internal tensions within the Central African labor movement. The collective's press conference aimed to publicly address what they perceive as divisive tactics that could jeopardize their collective bargaining efforts with the presidency and government.

Why it mattersThe collective of 18 unions is affected by Masua Gogi's alleged actions which they claim are dividing the labor movement and undermining their demands to the president.
