Central African Republic Officials Enhance Public Finance Transparency

Government members and financial managers are attending a two-day seminar in Bangui focused on budget reforms and fiscal discipline.

Government officials and financial managers in the Central African Republic are participating in a two-day seminar in Bangui, organized by the Ministry of Finance and Budget with support from the International Monetary Fund (IMF). The meeting aims to strengthen public finance management and transparency, focusing on budget reforms ahead of the implementation of program-based budgeting within the CEMAC region.

Discussions cover the budget cycle, public spending management, treasury operations, and control mechanisms. The Prime Minister stated that the seminar is intended to consolidate government efforts to improve state resource management and bolster budgetary discipline. The initiative also seeks to enhance the capabilities of officials for more rigorous and transparent handling of public funds.

This event occurs as technical and financial partners, including the IMF, World Bank, and African Development Bank, consistently urge authorities to increase transparency in public finance and boost domestic resource mobilization to reduce reliance on external funding.

Why it mattersGovernment officials and financial managers are being trained to improve the transparency and rigor of public finance management.

Powers Sécurité hunts employees over media leaks

The company's deputy general manager is leading an internal investigation to find staff suspected of sharing information with a local news outlet.

Powers Sécurité is conducting an internal hunt for employees suspected of leaking information to Corbeau News Centrafrique, despite the company's deputy general manager issuing a response to contest recent publications. The internal tension escalated last Friday during a farewell ceremony for a departing employee who is moving to the Bank of Central African States (BEAC).

The ceremony, held at a venue on Avenue De Gaulle near the French embassy in Bangui's first arrondissement, saw staff grouped by department and function. Human resources personnel, controllers, inspectors, and managers were separated into distinct units as the search for the source of the leaks intensified.

The deputy general manager, identified as Pastor Osé Balezou, is reportedly overseeing the efforts to identify and discipline employees believed to be communicating with the press. This internal crackdown follows a series of publications by Corbeau News Centrafrique that the company is disputing.

Why it mattersEmployees at Powers Sécurité are facing internal investigations and potential disciplinary action for allegedly sharing company information with the press.

Central African Republic Ranks Among Last Three in Human Development Index

A United Nations Development Programme report reveals that nearly seven out of ten Central Africans live below the poverty line, with the nation at 191st out of 193 countries.

The Central African Republic ranks 191st out of 193 countries in the latest Human Development Index, according to the 2025 annual report by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP). The report highlights that nearly seven out of ten citizens live below the poverty line, with significant challenges in accessing basic social services. Only 8% of households have electricity, and 36% have access to safe drinking water.

Socioeconomic conditions remain precarious, with a 36% underemployment rate. Acute food insecurity affects three out of ten citizens. In education, 68% of women are not enrolled in school, and 42% of men are illiterate. The UNDP attributes these conditions largely to successive politico-military crises and states it continues efforts for economic recovery and improved living standards.

The government, however, points to its development initiatives, including the National Development Plan, which has an announced funding exceeding seven thousand billion CFA francs. Despite these efforts, the report underscores the deep-seated challenges in human development and access to essential services across the nation.

Why it mattersNearly seven out of ten Central Africans live below the poverty line, impacting their access to basic services like water and electricity, and contributing to food insecurity and illiteracy.

Humanitarian plane lands after bees swarm cabin

A United Nations humanitarian flight was diverted to Bambari after bees entered the passenger cabin mid-flight.

A United Nations humanitarian aircraft flying from Bangui to Bangassou was forced to land in Bambari on Monday, August 31, 2026, due to an infestation of bees inside the cabin. The incident occurred shortly after the plane departed from Bangui-M’Poko airport, prompting the pilot to divert to Bambari to ensure passenger safety and address the bee intrusion.

The decision to land in Bambari was made to allow for the safe removal of the insects and to conduct necessary checks on the aircraft. No passengers were reported to have sustained any injuries during the event. Following the evacuation of all occupants, the bees were successfully removed from the plane.

After the bee removal, the aircraft underwent a thorough cleaning and disinfection process before it was cleared to continue its journey to Bangassou. The diversion caused a delay in the flight schedule but ensured the integrity of the flight and the well-being of everyone on board.

Why it mattersThe diversion of a humanitarian flight affects the timely delivery of aid and services to the intended recipients in Bangassou.

Wounded fighter has leg amputated after rebel clash

A young Fulani fighter injured during an August 23 attack on a position in Nana-Mambéré has undergone a leg amputation at Bouar hospital.

A young Fulani fighter, injured during an August 23 attack on General Adamo Ndalé's position, had his leg amputated on Monday at the Beninga hospital in Bouar. Social media identified him as an active member of Ndalé's armed group, though he had left the CPC rebel coalition months before the offensive. He was reportedly arrested by the gendarmerie.

The attack on General Ndalé's position occurred on August 23. Earlier, on July 24, Russian mercenaries had launched an operation against CPC positions near Aba-Bogani, approximately 17 kilometers from Bouar in the Nana-Mambéré prefecture. During that operation, two residents were abducted and used as guides by the assailants.

The July operation quickly escalated into a confrontation with General Ndalé's fighters. The two captives managed to escape amidst the confusion. CPC forces reportedly destroyed one military vehicle belonging to the mercenaries during the exchange of fire.

Why it mattersA young fighter is affected by the conflict between armed groups and mercenaries, resulting in a severe injury and amputation.
