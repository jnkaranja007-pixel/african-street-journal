Central African Republic authorises opposition march

The government has approved a demonstration planned for November 13 in Bangui against the high cost of living.

The Central African Republic government has authorised a march by the Republican Bloc for the Defence of the Constitution (BRDC) scheduled for Friday, November 13, in Bangui. The demonstration aims to protest the high cost of living and rising fuel prices. This decision follows recent statements from the government spokesperson that suggested opposition to the planned mobilisation.

The Minister of Public Security formally notified the BRDC coordinator, Me Crépin Mboli-Goumba, on November 9, granting approval for the march. The Minister noted that the BRDC is not formally registered with the Ministry of Territorial Administration but cited a commitment to public liberties and democratic freedoms for the decision. The march is set to begin at 6 AM at the Marabéna roundabout, proceeding through specific avenues to the United Nations roundabout, with an emphasis on maintaining a peaceful and orderly event.

The BRDC plans to use the march to denounce the increased cost of living and fuel prices. They are also demanding the release of five civil society leaders detained since October 12, following a call for a 'dead city' operation. The Ministry of Public Security has assured the BRDC that law enforcement will secure the demonstration and expects adherence to public safety regulations and the free movement of citizens along the route.

Why it mattersThe BRDC plans to use the march to denounce the increased cost of living and fuel prices, and to demand the release of five detained civil society leaders.

Wagner and Chinese firms control 40% of CAR forests

A new report details lost revenue and violations of forestry law due to foreign control of concessions.

Russian group Wagner and Chinese forestry firms now control nearly 40% of commercial logging areas in the Central African Republic. This concentration of control is linked to significant irregularities, unpaid taxes, and unfulfilled social commitments, according to an investigation by The Sentry organization.

The Sentry's report, titled "Self-Service Wood: How the Central African Forestry Sector Became Prey to Corruption and Negligence," examines six of the country's 14 commercial forest concessions. Four of these concessions are managed by Chinese companies, while two are associated with the Wagner group.

The investigation highlights millions in lost revenue and violations of the country's forestry code, with a notable absence of oversight from Central African authorities. The full report is scheduled for publication on November 10, 2026.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic is losing millions in potential revenue and facing violations of its forestry laws due to the control of its forest concessions by foreign entities.

Lawyers warn of health decline among five detained Central African activists

Five civil society actors held at Bangui's Roux camp prison since October 15 face a worsening health situation, their lawyers say.

Five civil society activists remain held at the Roux camp prison in Bangui, nearly a month after their arrest on October 15. They were detained in the wake of a "dead city" day against rising fuel prices and the high cost of living.

Their lawyers say the health of some detainees has deteriorated. The activists are now waiting for the next step in the judicial procedure. Their arrests have drawn reactions from the opposition, civil society groups and some figures close to the government.

The packet does not say when the judicial procedure will move forward or what charges, if any, the five face.

Why it mattersThe five detainees and their families face continued custody while lawyers report declining health among some of them.

Health · 2026-11-11

Bangui women still unclear on October Rose breast cancer message

Radio Ndeke Luka reports that many women in Bangui say they do not fully grasp the campaign's aims, including early screening.

The October Rose campaign on breast cancer awareness has ended, but many women in Bangui say they do not fully understand what it was meant to achieve. Some are still unaware of why early detection matters, because they have not been given enough information.

Reporter Edda Lotine Djiko went to meet women in the capital and collected their views on the campaign. The gap between the awareness drive and what women actually understood is the central finding of the report.

The report does not say what follow-up information is planned for women in the capital.

Why it mattersWomen in Bangui who have not understood the campaign remain without the screening information it was meant to provide.
