Mediators from Central African Republic and Cameroon train to prevent herding conflicts

About 50 participants met in Bouar to review their work and strengthen coordination between mediator networks and local authorities.

Source: Radio Ndeke Luka · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

The Centre for Humanitarian Dialogue trained agropastoral mediators from the Central African Republic and Cameroon in Bouar, in the Nana-Mambéré prefecture, from 17 to 18 November. About 50 participants came from the Central African Republic's Mambéré and Nana-Mambéré prefectures and Cameroon's Adamaoua region to discuss preventing and managing community conflicts linked to seasonal livestock movements.

Héritier Brillant Ndakpanga, an operations officer at the centre, said participants would review their prevention work and build technical and operational skills. Apollinaire Issa, a participant from Cameroon, said the meeting showed them they must work with administrative and local authorities.

The meeting was part of a project launched in 2019 with European Union support to promote peaceful resolution of farmer-herder conflicts in Central African border areas. Jean-Norbert Ndiende, sub-prefect of Baboua, said the government backs the mediators' role in bringing conflicting parties together.

Why it mattersHerders and farmers in the border prefectures are now to settle disputes at a table with mediators, who are working with local officials.
