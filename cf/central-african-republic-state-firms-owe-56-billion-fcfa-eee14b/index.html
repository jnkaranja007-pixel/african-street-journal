Central African Republic state firms owe 56 billion FCFA

A World Bank report reveals that public companies in the Central African Republic have accumulated a debt of 56 billion FCFA.

Source: Corbeau News Centrafrique · 2026-11-04 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Public companies in the Central African Republic, including SODECA, SOCATEL, and ENERCA, have accumulated a debt of 56 billion FCFA, equivalent to over 4% of the nation's GDP. This financial deficit threatens the country's economy, with reports indicating that these state-owned enterprises are being used as personal financial resources by an oligarchy.

International experts have flagged these 14 commercial public companies as a significant source of budgetary risk. The World Bank's confidential report details how managerial corruption and political paranoia have led to this situation. Roméo Gribingui, as General Controller, allegedly also serves as President of the Board of Directors for these companies, collecting substantial monthly fees.

The current state of these national assets, which are supposed to benefit citizens and the public treasury, has led to widespread power outages and a collapse of national telecommunications. The report highlights that incompetent individuals, chosen for their loyalty rather than expertise, are appointed to their boards.

Why it mattersThe financial mismanagement of state-owned enterprises in the Central African Republic has resulted in a massive debt that impacts the national economy and public services.
