Six of nine Central African candidates pass regional teaching exam in Bangui

They were among 324 successful candidates from 368 who sat the competition, which covered health, pharmacy, dentistry and animal sciences.

Source: Radio Ndeke Luka · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

The 23rd session of the Council for African and Malagasy Higher Education (CAMES) agrégation competition ended on Wednesday in Bangui. Of the 368 candidates who sat the exams, 324 passed, a success rate of 88.04%.

Central African Republic, the host country, had put forward nine candidates, and six passed, three of them women. Rodrigue Romuald Elien Gagnan Yan Zaou Tou, an ophthalmology specialist among the new agrégés, said candidates trained with senior colleagues and teachers at the University of Bangui's Faculty of Health Sciences, and also studied individually.

The competition covered human health sciences, pharmacy, dentistry and animal sciences. Estelle Minka Ngom, an ear, nose and throat professor who came from Cameroon and passed, said the candidates had prepared for several months.

Why it mattersThe six Central African passes add to the country's pool of qualified university teachers in health and pharmacy subjects.
