Central African Republic threatens to ban BRDC platform

The government says the Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC) is acting illegally and will no longer be allowed to operate.

Sources: Oubangui Medias and Radio Ndeke Luka · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

The Central African Republic government announced Monday it will no longer permit the Bloc républicain pour la défense de la Constitution (BRDC) to operate, citing its alleged illegal activities. The BRDC had issued a two-week ultimatum to the government with five demands, including the unconditional release of five civil society leaders and a reduction in fuel prices to under 850 FCFA per litre.

Government spokesperson Evariste Ngamana stated that the BRDC was created to obstruct the 2016 Constitution and should have dissolved after the 2023 referendum. He described the platform's continued actions as an act of rebellion, saying, "We cannot continue to accept someone who is in illegality continuing to disturb public order." The BRDC, through its coordinator Me Crépin Mboli-Goumba, responded that the government misunderstands the laws governing political parties in the country.

The BRDC's ultimatum followed a march against the high cost of living, where leaders presented their demands. If the government fails to respond, the BRDC has threatened to organize another demonstration. The government's decision to ban the platform comes amid ongoing political tensions and public discontent over economic conditions.

Why it mattersThe government's decision to ban the BRDC platform and its justification of rebellion directly impacts the freedom of expression and political organization for citizens.
