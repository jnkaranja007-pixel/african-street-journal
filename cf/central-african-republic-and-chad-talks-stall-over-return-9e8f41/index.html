Central African Republic and Chad talks stall over return of rebel leaders

Bangui and N'Djamena are negotiating the return of three rebel chiefs in Chad, but Wagner's stakes in a gold mine are among the obstacles.

Source: Jeune Afrique · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

The governments of the Central African Republic and Chad are trying to negotiate the return of three rebel leaders who took refuge in Chad, but the talks have stalled, Jeune Afrique reported.

The leaders are Ali Darassa of the Union for Peace in the Central African Republic (UPC), Sembé Bobo of the Return, Reclamation and Rehabilitation group (3R), and Mahamat al-Khatim of the Patriotic Movement for the Central African Republic (MPC). The report says the interests of Wagner and its allies in operating a gold mine are one of the obstacles.

Bangui has for months been seeking to complete a new peace agreement with these armed groups, which are active on its territory and along its borders.

Why it mattersPeace in the north-east and along the Chad border depends on whether these three groups sign, and Wagner's gold-mine interests are now blocking that deal.
