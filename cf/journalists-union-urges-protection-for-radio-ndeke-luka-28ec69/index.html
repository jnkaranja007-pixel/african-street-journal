Journalists’ union urges protection for Radio Ndeke Luka after protest

The Union of Central African Journalists says complaints about media coverage should go through legal channels, not pressure on the station.

Sources: Oubangui Medias and Corbeau News Centrafrique · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

The Union of Central African Journalists called on authorities to protect Radio Ndeke Luka’s staff and facilities after young people demonstrated outside the station in Bangui on 17 November. The protesters accused the broadcaster of airing remarks they considered defamatory and disparaging toward government actions.

Union president Eustache Michael Mounzatela said disputes over media content should be taken to competent bodies. He cited a right of reply, a complaint to the High Council of Communication or court action as proper routes, and said the union had asked authorities to investigate what happened outside the station.

Mounzatela said journalists must verify information and report responsibly, while retaining a duty to question officials about public difficulties. He urged young demonstrators to use dialogue and legal mechanisms rather than actions that could intimidate the press.

Why it mattersRadio Ndeke Luka’s staff and facilities are the direct focus of the union’s request for protection, and authorities have been asked to investigate the 17 November protest.
