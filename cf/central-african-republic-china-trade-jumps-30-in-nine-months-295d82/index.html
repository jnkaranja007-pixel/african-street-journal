Central African Republic-China trade jumps 30% in nine months

Bilateral trade reached $123 million between January and September 2026, driven by China's zero-tariff policy for African nations.

Source: Oubangui Medias · 2026-11-16 · 1 min read

Trade between the Central African Republic (CAR) and China surged by 30.0% to $123 million between January and September 2026, compared to the same period in 2025. This increase follows China's implementation of a zero-tariff regime for 53 African countries on May 1, 2026, which accelerates access to the Chinese market for CAR's exports.

The CAR's export basket to China remains heavily concentrated, with timber accounting for 99.9% of the $30.2 million shipped in the first nine months of 2026. Raw timber exports were $27.9 million, a slight increase of 6.7%, while sawn wood exports saw a significant rise of 263.3% to $2.0 million.

The CAR's total trade with Beijing in nine months nearly matched the $130 million recorded for all of 2025. The country now ranks 180th among China's trading partners, up from 183rd last year. Producers are expected to leverage the zero-tax regime to boost exports, provided they address local processing and meet Beijing's sanitary standards.

Why it mattersCentral African Republic producers and economic operators are expected to benefit from China's zero-tax regime to increase export volumes, with trade reaching $123 million in nine months.
