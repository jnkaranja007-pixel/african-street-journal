Central African Republic fuel traffic resumes after strike

Over 300 fuel trucks were held up for two weeks as Cameroonian transporters protested road harassment.

Source: Radio Ndeke Luka · 2026-10-23 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Cameroonian transporters resumed their routes on October 20 after a two-week strike over road harassment by Central African Republic security forces. The resumption follows mediation talks in Yaoundé between the two nations' authorities. Over 300 fuel tankers had been stranded at the border awaiting improved working conditions.

Yaoundé and Bangui agreed to the transporters' demands to facilitate the restart of fuel supplies to the Central African Republic. The first fuel trucks have now crossed the border at Cantonnier towards Bangui. Norbert Niéndé, sub-prefect of Baboua, stated that the situation had negative consequences for both countries.

The strike, which began in early October, disrupted the delivery of petroleum products to the Central African Republic. Amadou Bachirou, a transport union coordinator, welcomed assurances from authorities regarding the security of Cameroonian drivers in Central African territory. The governor of Cameroon's East region called for work to resume to restore fuel supplies.

Why it mattersThe Central African Republic's fuel supply was disrupted for two weeks due to a strike by Cameroonian transporters.
