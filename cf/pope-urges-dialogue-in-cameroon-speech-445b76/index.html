Pope urges dialogue in Cameroon speech

The Pope spoke of unemployment, exclusion, and frustrated youth, linking them to potential violence.

Source: Corbeau News Centrafrique · 2026-10-26 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Pope Pope 14 arrived in Cameroon for an apostolic mission, delivering a speech focused on societal issues rather than theology. He addressed unemployment, exclusion, frustrated youth, and simmering violence, emphasizing the importance of dialogue. The message was discussed on Radio Ndeke Luka on April 19, 2026.

The Pontiff stated that persistent unemployment and exclusion breed frustration, which can lead to violence. He stressed that investing in youth, creating spaces for dialogue, and taking concrete peace actions are strategic choices to prevent worse outcomes. Former minister Laurent Gomina-Pampali noted the Pope's use of the word 'transformation,' which President Faustin Archange Touadéra also used in his March 30 investiture speech.

Elysée Nguémalé, coordinator of the OGDC, concluded that peace cannot be decreed, a point made by the Pope in Yaoundé. Bangui will heed this message when it chooses to.

Why it mattersThe Pope's message on dialogue and addressing issues like unemployment and exclusion directly relates to the Central African Republic's governance and potential for peace.
