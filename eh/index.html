Dakar rally to pass through occupied Western Sahara

The race will traverse Smara and reach Dajla between January 18 and 22, crossing territory claimed by Morocco.

The 2027 Dakar Rally will include stages in occupied Western Sahara, organizers announced on October 30. The race is scheduled from January 9 to 23, starting in Portugal and ending in Marrakech, Morocco. Five racing days will take place in the disputed territory, passing through Smara and near Dajla, which Morocco occupies.

ASO, the French organizing company, presents these locations as the "southernmost tip" of Morocco, despite the UN classifying Western Sahara as a non-self-governing territory awaiting decolonization. The European Union's Court of Justice has ruled it distinct from Morocco. The Polisario Front considers the ceasefire broken since November 2020, and projectiles fell on Smara in October 2023.

This inclusion follows the rally's departure from Saudi Arabia due to Houthi attacks, with ASO needing a desert location quickly. Morocco has used events like this to assert its claim over the territory it has occupied since 1975. The Dakar rally previously caused a crisis between Morocco and the Polisario Front in 2001 when it passed through Smara.

Politics · 2026-11-05

UN mission ends, Western Sahara risks escalation

Financial Times warns of unprecedented uncertainty and potential military confrontation after MINURSO's 35-year mandate expires.

The United Nations mission in Western Sahara, MINURSO, has ended after 35 years without achieving its goal of a referendum, raising fears of unprecedented uncertainty. The Financial Times reported on November 3, 2026, that the Security Council's failure to renew the mandate, due to US-Russia disagreements, could lead to increased military confrontation in a region already experiencing rising tensions.

Ricardo Fabiani of the International Crisis Group warned that the withdrawal of UN observers from the buffer zone could trigger an uncontrollable military clash from any miscalculation. Moroccan MP Lahcen Haddad called the end of MINURSO a "gift from heaven," believing Morocco can now advance its autonomy proposal without UN interference. However, this contrasts with expert warnings that the mission's absence removes a key military de-escalation mechanism.

Oubbi Bashraya Bashir, an advisor to Sahrawi President Brahim Gali, stated the Polisario Front will continue its political and military struggle for self-determination while maintaining restraint. He cautioned that MINURSO's departure exposes the region to dangerous escalation and potential catastrophe. UN Secretary-General António Guterres affirmed the Sahrawi people's right to self-determination remains, expressing concern over intensified hostilities.

Why it mattersThe end of the UN mission and potential military escalation directly affects the Sahrawi people's pursuit of self-determination and safety.

Politics · 2026-11-05

UN peacekeeping mandate for Western Sahara ends

MINURSO begins liquidation after Russian veto at Security Council blocks mandate renewal.

The United Nations peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara, MINURSO, has begun liquidation procedures following a Russian veto at the Security Council. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres reaffirmed the Saharawi people's inalienable right to self-determination.

An analysis of the UN Security Council vote revealed a gap between governments' statements on self-determination and their resolution support. The Security Council's decision means the end of MINURSO's mandate in the disputed territory.

The liquidation process for MINURSO is now underway. The UN chief's statement emphasized the enduring right to self-determination for the Saharawi people.

Why it mattersThe UN Security Council's decision to end the MINURSO mandate directly affects the people of Western Sahara by removing a peacekeeping presence.
