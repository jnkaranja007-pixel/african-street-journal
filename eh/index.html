Polisario Front calls general mobilisation as it accuses Morocco of massing troops

The movement's leadership, led by Brahim Ghali, made the call on 10 November along the defence wall that divides Western Sahara.

The Polisario Front, the movement seeking independence for Western Sahara, called for general mobilisation on 10 November. Its leadership, led by Brahim Ghali, accused Morocco of gathering troops along the defence wall.

The Sahrawi People's Liberation Army is organised into seven military regions and has infantry, armoured units and artillery, according to Jeune Afrique. The magazine describes the army's arsenal as ageing.

The report raises the question of how many men the movement can actually mobilise, and of the support it receives from Algeria. It gives no figures for troop strength, weapons or the scale of that support.

Why it mattersFighters and families in the camps and along the wall now face a formal mobilisation call, with no public figures yet showing how many people it would draw in.

Morocco jails four Sahrawi students for two years over peaceful protest

A Rabat court also fined each student 15,000 dirhams, about 1,400 euros, for joining a demonstration outside the prison administration in October.

The Rabat court of first instance on Friday sentenced four Sahrawi students, Brahim Babeit, Salah Eddine Essabar, El Hussein Chihab and Mohamed Lmrabet, to two years in prison and a fine of 15,000 dirhams each, about 1,400 euros. Their offence was joining a gathering on 12 October outside the Moroccan prison administration's headquarters in Rabat, to call for the release of Gdeim Izik prisoners on hunger strike.

Three others detained alongside them, human rights defenders, had their complaints dismissed and were freed within days. The four students were sent to Salé prison and held until sentencing. Two Spanish jurists who travelled to Rabat to follow the trial, representing the International Association of Lawyers for Western Sahara and the Spanish bar, were prevented from entering the courtroom.

Babeit refused to give a statement to the prosecutor until he received care and ended up in hospital. Sahrawi rights groups CODESA and CODAPSO said he was beaten during his arrest, and the prosecutor ordered a medical examination. Those medical records were among documents missing from the file, which had caused the trial to be postponed in October. He was convicted regardless.

Why it mattersThe four students now face two years in prison and must pay 15,000 dirhams each for a protest that the Gdeim Izik prisoners' families and supporters had backed.

Politics · 2026-11-18

EUCOCO closes in Madrid after speakers challenge diplomacy over Western Sahara

The 50th European conference ended with calls for another path as the United Nations mission in the territory entered liquidation on 1 November.

The 50th European Conference of Coordination of Support and Solidarity with the Sahrawi People (EUCOCO) met in Madrid on 13 and 14 November, ending with speakers saying diplomacy had failed and another path was needed. The gathering coincided with the 51st anniversary of the Madrid Accords.

Mansur Omar, the Polisario Front’s representative to Europe and European institutions, said the United Nations Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara had not protected Sahrawis and described it as a “silent witness”. The mission has been in liquidation since 1 November, after the Security Council failed to approve either of two draft resolutions put to a vote on 30 October.

Sidi Mohamed Dadda, a former Sahrawi prisoner and activist in the occupied territories, warned that the mission’s end was dangerous, saying it had never had a human-rights mechanism. He cited four Sahrawi students sentenced in Rabat on 13 November to two years in prison and a fine of 15,000 dirhams each. Spain was due to vote on 29 November.

Why it mattersThe four students sentenced in Rabat face two years in prison and a 15,000-dirham fine each, while Spain's vote was scheduled for 29 November.
