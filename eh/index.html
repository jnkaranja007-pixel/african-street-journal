Lavrov and Attaf discuss Western Sahara in Moscow

Russian and Algerian foreign ministers met amid UN Security Council talks on a US draft resolution.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov met Algerian counterpart Ahmed Attaf in Moscow on October 27, 2026, to discuss the Western Sahara issue. The meeting occurred on the sidelines of the Gas Exporting Countries Forum (GECF) summit.

The talks addressed bilateral relations and regional matters, including Western Sahara, during a sensitive diplomatic period. Consultations are ongoing at the UN Security Council regarding a revised US draft resolution on the territory and the MINURSO mandate.

Russia, a permanent Security Council member, maintains its stance for a just political solution within the UN framework, emphasizing self-determination. Moscow has abstained on Western Sahara resolutions since 2018, citing disagreements with the US approach to drafting them.

Why it mattersThe discussions between Russia and Algeria directly involve the political future of Western Sahara and its people's right to self-determination.

US revises Western Sahara resolution draft

Draft changes autonomy from 'only viable' to 'most viable' solution, retains it as negotiation base.

The United States has revised a draft UN Security Council resolution on Western Sahara, changing the description of Moroccan autonomy from the "only viable solution" to the "most viable solution." This change, detailed in an October 24 document, reduces expectations in Morocco but maintains autonomy as the sole basis for negotiation.

The Frente POLISARIO explained the changes on October 26, noting the revised text now includes conditions for a just, mutually acceptable political solution respecting UN principles, including self-determination. The document also proposes changing the name of the UN mission to MINUSO.

The Security Council is currently engaged in intense consultations over the revised proposal. The initial draft had been presented as a significant shift favoring Morocco, but the revised text aims to incorporate changes proposed by other council members.

Why it mattersThe people of Western Sahara are directly affected by the UN Security Council's deliberations on their right to self-determination and the future of the MINURSO mission.
