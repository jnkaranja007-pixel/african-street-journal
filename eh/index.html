UN Security Council votes on MINURSO future

US-drafted resolutions for the UN mission in Western Sahara have undergone changes after initial objections.

The UN Security Council will meet on Friday, October 30, to decide the future of the United Nations Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara (MINURSO). Several draft resolutions prepared by the United States have recently been leaked.

RFI has reviewed these draft resolutions, noting that an initial version had significantly angered the Polisario Front independence movement. The latest proposal presented has seen several important modifications.

The Security Council's decision will determine the ongoing mandate and operations of MINURSO in the region.

Why it mattersThe UN Security Council's vote will determine the future of MINURSO, impacting the political process in Western Sahara.

Polisario Front faces choices before UN vote

Tensions rise in Tindouf and Algiers over a new UN resolution framework for Western Sahara.

The Front Polisario faces decisions that could reshape its organization and leadership as the UN Security Council prepares to vote on a new resolution framework for Western Sahara. On October 26, 2026, five days before the vote, Sahrawi women and children demonstrated in the Tindouf camps against the American proposal. Media outlets linked to the Front Polisario circulated images of these protests, aiming to project an image of unity.

A recording attributed to Bachir Mustapha emerged the following day, adding to internal discussions. These reactions, observed in both Tindouf and Algiers, highlight emerging tensions and strategies that could alter the future of the Polisario Front.

The proposed UN resolution framework has placed the Front Polisario at a crossroads. The reactions from within the camps and from Algerian officials indicate a complex internal dynamic as the organization confronts this moment.

Why it mattersThe people of Western Sahara are directly affected by the UN's decisions regarding their territory and future leadership.

Politics · 2026-10-30

US revises Western Sahara resolution draft

Draft changes autonomy from 'only viable' to 'most viable' solution, adding self-determination conditions.

The United States has revised a draft UN Security Council resolution on Western Sahara, changing the description of Moroccan autonomy from the "only viable solution" to the "most viable solution." This adjustment, detailed in an October 24 document, also reintroduces conditions for a just and mutually acceptable political solution, including respect for the UN Charter and the principle of self-determination.

The Polisario Front explained these changes on October 26, noting that while Moroccan autonomy remains the sole basis for negotiation, the revised text softens previous expectations. The shift away from presenting autonomy as the exclusive option aims to reduce interpretations of a historical turn favoring Morocco.

The revised draft also modifies the mission's name, proposing a change to MINUSO, and abandons the automatic transformation of MINURSO. These amendments emerged from consultations with other Security Council members, leading to a "war of drafts" focused on autonomy, self-determination guarantees, and the mission's future.

Why it mattersThe people of Western Sahara are directly affected as the resolution impacts the negotiation basis and conditions for a political solution regarding their self-determination.

Politics · 2026-10-30

Lavrov and Attaf discuss Western Sahara in Moscow

Russian and Algerian foreign ministers met amid UN Security Council talks on a US draft resolution.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov discussed the Western Sahara issue with his Algerian counterpart Ahmed Attaf in Moscow on October 27, 2026. The ministers also reviewed bilateral relations and regional matters during their meeting on the sidelines of the Gas Exporting Countries Forum (GECF) summit.

The talks occurred as the UN Security Council held intensive consultations on a US draft resolution concerning Western Sahara and the MINURSO mandate. Lavrov expressed satisfaction with the trade, economic, and military-technical cooperation between Russia and Algeria.

Russia, a permanent Security Council member with veto power, maintains its traditional stance favoring a political solution within the UN framework, emphasizing self-determination. Moscow has abstained from voting on Western Sahara resolutions since 2018, citing concerns over the US-led drafting process.

Why it mattersThe foreign ministers of Russia and Algeria discussed the future of Western Sahara, a territory with disputed sovereignty.
