UN mission mandate expires in Western Sahara

The UN mission for a referendum in Western Sahara will end as resolutions failed to gain enough votes for renewal.

The mandate for the United Nations Mission for the Organization of a Referendum in Western Sahara (Minurso) has expired on Saturday. Two successive resolutions failed to secure the necessary votes for its renewal.

The failure to renew Minurso's mandate follows negotiations between the United States and Morocco on one side, and Russia and the Polisario Front on the other. The mission's purpose was to organize a referendum in Western Sahara.

The mandate's expiration means the planned referendum on self-determination for Western Sahara will not proceed under the current UN framework.

Why it mattersThe UN mission tasked with organizing a referendum in Western Sahara will cease operations, impacting the political future of the region.

Russia vetoes US-backed UN resolution on Western Sahara

Moscow blocks changes to MINURSO mandate, citing an unbalanced approach to negotiations.

Russia vetoed a United States-led draft resolution on October 30, 2026, that aimed to renew the mandate of the UN Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara (MINURSO). The decision by Moscow prevents the proposal from advancing in its current form and halts substantial changes to the mission's political and operational framework.

The US-backed resolution sought to rename MINURSO, removing the reference to a referendum, and to establish Morocco's autonomy plan as the primary negotiation framework. Russia stated the US text was unbalanced and could compromise the UN's impartiality in the political process, blocking the resolution's approval.

This veto creates a diplomatic setback for Washington, requiring them to revise their proposal to find an alternative that renews the mission's mandate without causing a diplomatic deadlock. The future of MINURSO and the international framework for resolving the Western Sahara conflict now faces renewed tension among Security Council members.

Why it mattersThe Russian veto prevents changes to the UN mission in Western Sahara, directly affecting the political process for the region.
