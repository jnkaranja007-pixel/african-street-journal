UN peacekeeping mission ends in Western Sahara

The United Nations' mission for Western Sahara ceased operations on Sunday after 35 years due to a US-Russia dispute.

The United Nations peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara ended Sunday after 35 years, following a US-Russia disagreement at UN headquarters concerning the territory's future. The mission's mandate officially ended, initiating a liquidation period starting November 1, 2026.

Stéphane Dujarric, spokesperson for the UN secretary general, stated that a smaller team will oversee the transport of assets from troop-contributing countries and the disposal of UN equipment. Morocco's royal palace welcomed the non-renewal, calling the mission an "instrument to internationalize the dispute."

The Polisario Front, supported by Algeria, described the decision as "unacceptable." The UN Security Council's failure to agree on a resolution, with Russia vetoing a US proposal and a Russian proposal being voted down, led to the abrupt termination of the force's mandate.

Why it mattersThe end of the UN peacekeeping mission raises concerns about stability in the disputed Western Sahara, a territory rich in minerals and fisheries claimed by Morocco and the Polisario Front.

UN mission ends, Western Sahara risks escalation

Financial Times warns of unprecedented uncertainty and potential military confrontation after MINURSO's 35-year mandate expires.

The United Nations mission in Western Sahara, MINURSO, has ended after 35 years without achieving its goal of a referendum, raising fears of unprecedented uncertainty. The Financial Times reported on November 3, 2026, that the Security Council's failure to renew the mandate, due to US-Russia disagreements, could lead to increased military confrontation in a region already experiencing rising tensions.

Ricardo Fabiani of the International Crisis Group warned that the withdrawal of UN observers from the buffer zone could trigger an uncontrollable military clash from any miscalculation. Moroccan MP Lahcen Haddad called the end of MINURSO a "gift from heaven," believing Morocco can now advance its autonomy proposal without UN interference. However, this contrasts with expert warnings that the mission's absence removes a key military de-escalation mechanism.

Oubbi Bashraya Bashir, an advisor to Sahrawi President Brahim Gali, stated the Polisario Front will continue its political and military struggle for self-determination while maintaining restraint. He cautioned that MINURSO's departure exposes the region to dangerous escalation and potential catastrophe. UN Secretary-General António Guterres affirmed the Sahrawi people's right to self-determination remains, expressing concern over intensified hostilities.

Why it mattersThe end of the UN mission and potential military escalation directly affects the Sahrawi people's pursuit of self-determination and safety.

UN begins withdrawing peacekeepers from Western Sahara

The UN mission in Western Sahara is closing its 13 sites after its mandate was not extended by the Security Council.

The United Nations has begun withdrawing its peacekeepers from Western Sahara. The UN mission, known as Minurso, started closing its 13 sites on Sunday because its mandate was not extended by the Security Council. This decision followed a diplomatic dispute between the United States and Morocco, and Russia and the Polisario.

Minurso was recognized as a stabilizing force in the region before its liquidation began. The withdrawal commenced on Monday, according to UN confirmation. The mission's closure is a direct result of the Security Council's failure to renew its mandate.

The diplomatic clash involved key international players, highlighting the complex political situation in Western Sahara. The closure of the 13 sites marks the end of the UN's peacekeeping presence under the current mandate.

Why it mattersThe UN mission in Western Sahara is being liquidated, affecting the region's stability and the people involved in the diplomatic dispute.
