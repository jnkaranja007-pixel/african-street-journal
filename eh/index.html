Russia vetoes UN resolution on Western Sahara

Moscow blocks US proposal to change MINURSO mandate, citing imbalance in negotiations.

Russia vetoed a United States-led UN Security Council resolution aimed at renewing the mandate of the UN Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara (MINURSO) on October 30, 2026. The decision by Moscow prevents the adoption of the US proposal in its current form, which sought to alter the mission's name and promote Morocco's autonomy plan as the primary negotiation framework.

Russian Foreign Minister Lavrov and his Algerian counterpart Attaf discussed the Western Sahara issue amidst UN consultations on the US draft. Russia stated the US proposal was unbalanced and could compromise the impartiality of the UN-sponsored political process. This veto halts a US initiative that aimed to transform the mission, established in 1991 to oversee a self-determination referendum.

The US proposal had raised concerns about potentially sidelining the option of independence for the Sahrawi people and exclusively focusing on Morocco's autonomy proposal. The Russian veto creates an obstacle to changing the UN's operational framework without broad consensus among Security Council members, leaving the future of MINURSO and the conflict resolution framework dependent on further diplomatic engagement.

Why it mattersThe veto by Russia affects the people of Western Sahara by blocking a UN resolution that could have significantly altered the mission's mandate and the negotiation framework for resolving the conflict.

UN mission mandate expires in Western Sahara

The UN Security Council failed to renew the mandate for the UN Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara.

The United Nations Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara (Minurso) has ceased operations after its mandate was not renewed at the UN Security Council. The mission's presence in the region, which lasted 35 years, ended on October 31, 2026, following two successive resolutions that failed to gain the necessary votes for renewal.

The failure to renew the mandate followed negotiations between the United States and Morocco on one side, and Russia and the Polisario Front on the other. The UN Security Council adjourned in surprise overnight on Friday, October 30, into Saturday, October 31.

Minurso was established to organize a referendum in Western Sahara. The mandate's expiration leaves the future of the region's political status uncertain.

Why it mattersThe failure to renew the UN mission's mandate directly impacts the political future and organization of a referendum in Western Sahara.

Sahrawi protesters reject UN resolution draft

Tindouf residents demonstrated against a US-backed UN proposal for Western Sahara on October 28, 2026.

Sahrawi residents in the Tindouf camps protested a proposed UN resolution on October 28, 2026. Women and children marched under security supervision against the American draft, with images shared on pro-Polisario media to show unity.

The demonstration occurred five days before the UN Security Council's vote on Western Sahara. Media close to the Polisario Front broadcast images of the protest, aiming to portray a unified movement.

The proposed resolution has created tensions and strategic debates within the Polisario Front, potentially reshaping the movement's future and leadership.

Why it mattersSahrawi residents in Tindouf camps are directly affected by the UN resolution vote and subsequent negotiations.
