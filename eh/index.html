Financial Times warns of escalation risk in Western Sahara

UN mission's mandate ends after 35 years, raising fears of military confrontation.

The Financial Times warned on November 3, 2026, that Western Sahara faces unprecedented uncertainty following the end of the UN's MINURSO mission after 35 years. The British newspaper reported that the UN Security Council's failure to renew the mandate, due to US-Russia differences, could lead to greater military confrontation in a region already experiencing rising tensions and an arms race.

Ricardo Fabiani of the International Crisis Group cautioned that the withdrawal of UN observers from the buffer zone could trigger an uncontrollable clash from any military miscalculation. Moroccan MP Lahcen Haddad called the mission's end a "gift from heaven," believing Morocco can advance its autonomy plan without UN interference, contrasting with expert warnings that the mission's absence removes a key de-escalation mechanism.

Oubbi Bashraya Bashir, an advisor to Sahrawi President Brahim Gali, stated the Polisario Front will continue its political and military struggle for self-determination while maintaining restraint to facilitate a political solution, warning the mission's departure risks a dangerous escalation. UN Secretary-General António Guterres stressed that the mission's end does not negate the Sahrawi people's right to self-determination and expressed concern over intensified hostilities.

Why it mattersThe end of the UN mission and potential military escalation directly affects the people of Western Sahara and their pursuit of self-determination.

UN mission begins withdrawal from Western Sahara

All 13 sites of the UN's Western Sahara mission will close as its chief meets Moroccan officials.

The United Nations mission for organising a referendum in Western Sahara, Minurso, is preparing to close all 13 of its sites within the territory. This follows a Russian veto on a US amendment at the UN Security Council four days ago. A small team will oversee the liquidation process.

The head of Minurso is currently in Morocco to discuss the mission's departure with relevant parties. The plan for the UN mission's withdrawal has been detailed following the Security Council meeting.

The mission's closure comes after a period of discussion regarding its liquidation. The specific timeline for the complete departure of the mission from its 13 locations has not yet been stated.

Why it mattersThe United Nations mission for organising a referendum in Western Sahara is closing its sites in the territory.

Politics · 2026-11-04

UN peacekeeping mission ends in Western Sahara

The United Nations' mission in the disputed territory ceased operations Sunday after 35 years due to a US-Russia UN Security Council dispute.

The United Nations peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara ended Sunday after 35 years of operations. The mission's mandate was abruptly terminated following a US-Russia disagreement at UN headquarters concerning the territory's future.

Stéphane Dujarric, spokesperson for the UN secretary general, stated that November 1, 2026, marked the beginning of the liquidation period. A smaller team will manage the orderly transport of assets from troop-contributing countries and the disposal of UN equipment.

The mission, established in 1991, had been repeatedly extended. Russia vetoed a US resolution supporting Morocco's sovereignty claim and ending the mission, while a subsequent Russian resolution to extend it was voted down, leading to its cessation.

Why it mattersThe end of the UN peacekeeping mission raises concerns about stability in the mineral and fisheries-rich Western Sahara, a territory claimed by Morocco and the Polisario Front.
