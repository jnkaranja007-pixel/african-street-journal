UN to discuss Minurso mandate after Russian veto

The UN General Assembly will debate the future of the UN mission in Western Sahara following Russia's Security Council veto.

The United Nations mission in Western Sahara, Minurso, will be discussed at the General Assembly on Tuesday, November 10. This comes nearly 10 days after Russia vetoed a resolution to renew the mission's mandate at the Security Council. Russia is expected to explain its opposition to the US-backed resolution.

Morocco and Algeria are anticipated to react strongly to the developments surrounding Minurso. The mission's mandate renewal was put forward by the United States at the Security Council.

The UN General Assembly's discussion on November 10 will address the ongoing situation concerning Minurso. The Security Council's decision followed a Russian veto that effectively ended the mission's current mandate.

Morocco deploys troops to Western Sahara wall

Thousands of Moroccan troops, tanks, and artillery have moved to at least nine points along the military wall dividing Western Sahara.

Morocco reinforced at least nine points along its military wall in Western Sahara, accumulating troops near the separation line. Thousands of soldiers, tanks, and self-propelled howitzers moved to the northern, central, and southern sectors. A battalion was sighted in Guelta-Zemmur and Amgala, where Morocco's elite army corps stationed thousands of troops.

A high-ranking Polisario Front official stated Morocco's military command moved troops to 'show muscle' due to continuous Polisario attacks. Sahrawi People's Liberation Army fighters attacked a mobile unit of the occupying army with appropriate weaponry, according to a military source.

Morocco's actions follow the UN General Assembly's scheduled debate on November 10 regarding a veto that led to the closure of MINURSO. A senior source told ECSaharaui that Rabat might try to exploit a security vacuum to expand its control over territories administered by the Polisario Front.

Why it mattersMoroccan military movements and potential expansion into Polisario-controlled territories raise the risk of open conflict for the people of Western Sahara.

Morocco marks Western Sahara occupation anniversary

Pro-Moroccan Sahrawis gathered in Laayoune to celebrate the 1975 Green March, while a UN mission concludes after 35 years.

Morocco commemorated the anniversary of the 1975 Green March on Friday, an event that led to its takeover of most of Western Sahara. Hundreds of pro-Moroccan Sahrawis gathered in Laayoune, waving Moroccan flags to mark the occasion.

The anniversary occurs as the United Nations peacekeeping mission concludes its 35-year mandate in the region. Layla Choraifi, a demonstrator, stated that the autonomy initiative for the Saharan provinces is the most appropriate proposal for resolving the issue.

For pro-independence Sahrawis, the day signifies the start of Morocco’s occupation and the displacement of thousands. This has resulted in families being divided between Moroccan-controlled cities and refugee camps located in Algeria.

Why it mattersThe anniversary of Morocco's takeover of Western Sahara and the winding down of a UN mission directly affects the people of Western Sahara.

European Parliament conference demands self-determination for Sahrawi people

Experts and MEPs in Brussels called for the decolonization of Western Sahara and warned of regional instability.

A conference at the European Parliament in Brussels on Wednesday called for the decolonization of Western Sahara and for the Sahrawi people to exercise their right to self-determination, freedom, and independence. Participants warned that prolonging the conflict could impact regional security and stability. Oubbi Bouchraya stated that the Sahrawi people would not renounce their right to self-determination and sovereignty over their natural resources.

The conference, titled "The plunder of Sahrawi natural resources and tourism under occupation," was organized by the European Parliament's Intergroup on Solidarity with the Sahrawi People and the Sahrawi Commission for Natural Resources and Legal Issues. It included Members of the European Parliament, lawyers, and international law experts. They emphasized that the UN's responsibility to complete the decolonization process continues despite the non-renewal of MINURSO's mandate.

A report presented at the conference, "Tourism under the context of occupation: the case of the occupied city of Dajla," detailed Morocco's continued exploitation of Sahrawi natural resources, particularly through illegal agreements with the EU. The report highlighted tourism investments, including Ryanair flights to Dajla, as a means to consolidate economic occupation and legitimize it through foreign investment.

Why it mattersThe Sahrawi people are directly affected by the ongoing conflict and the exploitation of their natural resources, as highlighted by the conference and report.
