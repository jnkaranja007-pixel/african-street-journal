European Parliament conference demands Sahrawi self-determination

Experts and MEPs gathered in Brussels to discuss resource exploitation and tourism under occupation.

A conference at the European Parliament in Brussels on Wednesday called for the decolonization of Western Sahara and demanded that the Sahrawi people be granted their right to self-determination, freedom, and independence. Participants warned of the consequences the prolonged conflict could have for regional security and stability. Oubbi Bouchraya stated, "The Sahrawi people will not renounce their right to self-determination and sovereignty over their natural resources."

The conference, titled "The exploitation of Sahrawi natural resources and tourism under occupation," was organized by the European Parliament's Intergroup on Solidarity with the Sahrawi People. Attendees, including MEPs, lawyers, and international law experts, recalled that the Sahrawi issue is a decolonization process on the UN agenda. They emphasized that the non-renewal of MINURSO's mandate does not absolve the UN of its responsibility to end the occupation.

The event also presented a report on tourism in occupied Dajla, detailing Morocco's continued exploitation of Sahrawi resources through illegal agreements with the EU, including Ryanair flights. The report highlights how tourism is used to legitimize economic occupation with foreign investment.

Why it mattersThe Sahrawi people are directly affected by the call for self-determination and the ongoing exploitation of their natural resources and territory.

Morocco marks Western Sahara occupation anniversary

Hundreds of pro-Moroccan Sahrawis gathered in Laayoune to celebrate the Green March anniversary.

Morocco commemorated the 1975 Green March on Friday, an event that led to its takeover of most of Western Sahara. In Laayoune, hundreds of pro-Moroccan Sahrawis gathered to celebrate the anniversary, waving Moroccan flags.

The anniversary occurs as a United Nations peacekeeping mission concludes after 35 years, entering a new diplomatic phase for the territory's conflict. Pro-Morocco demonstrator Layla Choraifi stated that the autonomy initiative for the Saharan provinces is the most appropriate proposal for resolution.

For pro-independence Sahrawis, the day signifies the start of Morocco’s occupation and the displacement of thousands, dividing families between Moroccan-controlled cities and refugee camps in Algeria. Western Sahara remains Africa’s longest-running territorial conflict.

Why it mattersThe anniversary of Morocco's takeover of Western Sahara and the ongoing territorial conflict directly affects the Sahrawi people.
