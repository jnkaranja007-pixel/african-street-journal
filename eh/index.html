US proposes UN mission changes in Western Sahara

A US draft resolution suggests ending MINURSO peacekeeping by April 2027 and shifting to a political mission.

A United States draft resolution proposes significant changes to the UN mission in Western Sahara, suggesting an end to its peacekeeping functions by April 30, 2027. The mission would then transition into a special political mission with a mandate extending to October 31, 2027. The proposal aims to reduce the military component by at least 50% before January 31, 2027, and close some bases.

The draft reaffirms support for political negotiations and proposes adopting Morocco's autonomy plan as the basis for talks. It calls for a just, lasting, and acceptable solution aligned with UN principles, including self-determination. The UN Secretary-General is asked to submit detailed proposals for the new political mission's structure and functions, which would include supporting the political process and mine action.

The resolution urges cooperation with UN envoy Staffan de Mistura and the US in facilitating negotiations between Morocco, the Polisario Front, Algeria, and Mauritania. It also calls on the UN High Commissioner for Refugees to complete the registration of refugees in Tindouf within six months and ensure unimpeded access and freedom of movement.

Why it mattersThe proposed changes affect the United Nations Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara (MINURSO) and the political process for the territory.

US envoy urged Spain to grant Sahrawi nationality

Michael Waltz told Spain's foreign minister the law could reduce refugee camp populations and aid a political deal.

United States Ambassador to the United Nations Michael Waltz proposed to Spanish Foreign Minister José Manuel Albares that Spain grant nationality to tens of thousands of Sahrawis. Waltz suggested this measure could help reduce the population in Sahrawi refugee camps and facilitate a political agreement. The conversation reportedly occurred on February 10, following negotiations involving Morocco, the POLISARIO Front, Algeria, and Mauritania.

The proposed law, initiated by the Sumar party, passed the Congress of Deputies on September 10 with PSOE support but lapsed when Spain's Cortes dissolved on October 5. Sahrawi leaders expressed cautious support; Brahim Ghali stated in July that it could alleviate hardship but should not overshadow self-determination, while Bachir Mustafá Seyed called it a step towards justice.

Some Sahrawi media outlets interpreted Washington's interest as a strategy to alter the political balance in the camps and pressure the POLISARIO Front. This view aligns with Brahim Ghali's earlier caution that no initiative should eclipse the central goal of self-determination for the Sahrawi people.

Why it mattersThe US envoy's proposal to Spain regarding Sahrawi nationality directly affects the Sahrawi people by potentially altering their living conditions and political standing.
