Morocco marks Western Sahara takeover anniversary

Hundreds of pro-Moroccan Sahrawis gathered in Laayoune to celebrate the 1975 Green March.

Morocco observed the anniversary of the Green March on Friday, commemorating the 1975 mobilization that led to its control over most of Western Sahara. In Laayoune, hundreds of pro-Moroccan Sahrawis gathered to celebrate, waving Moroccan flags.

The anniversary occurs as a U.N. peacekeeping mission concludes after 35 years, entering a new diplomatic phase for the territory's conflict. Pro-Morocco demonstrator Layla Choraifi stated, “He proposed the autonomy initiative for the Saharan provinces, which, in our view, is the most appropriate proposal for resolving this issue.”

For pro-independence Sahrawis, the day signifies the start of Morocco’s takeover and the displacement of thousands. Families remain divided between Moroccan-controlled cities and refugee camps in Algeria.

Why it mattersThe anniversary marks the beginning of Morocco's takeover and the displacement of thousands of Sahrawis, dividing families.

Morocco deploys troops to Western Sahara wall

Thousands of Moroccan troops, tanks, and artillery have moved to at least nine points along the military wall dividing Western Sahara.

Morocco reinforced at least nine points along its military wall in Western Sahara, accumulating troops near the separation line. Thousands of soldiers, tanks, and self-propelled howitzers moved to the northern, central, and southern sectors. A battalion was sighted in Guelta-Zemmur and Amgala, where Morocco's elite army corps stationed thousands of troops.

A high-ranking Polisario Front official stated Morocco's military command moved troops to 'show muscle' due to continuous Polisario attacks. Sahrawi People's Liberation Army fighters attacked a mobile unit of the occupying army with appropriate weaponry, according to a military source.

Morocco's actions follow the UN General Assembly's scheduled debate on November 10 regarding a veto that led to the closure of MINURSO. A senior source told ECSaharaui that Rabat might try to exploit a security vacuum to expand its control over territories administered by the Polisario Front.

Why it mattersMoroccan military movements and potential expansion into Polisario-controlled territories raise the risk of open conflict for the people of Western Sahara.

European Parliament conference demands Sahrawi self-determination

MEPs and experts met in Brussels to discuss the exploitation of natural resources and tourism under occupation.

A conference at the European Parliament in Brussels on Wednesday called for the decolonization of Western Sahara and demanded that the Sahrawi people be guaranteed their right to self-determination, freedom, and independence. Participants warned of the consequences of prolonging the conflict for regional security and stability. Oubbi Bouchraya stated that the Sahrawi people will not renounce their right to self-determination and sovereignty over their natural resources.

The conference, titled "The exploitation of Sahrawi natural resources and tourism under occupation," was organized by the European Parliament's Intergroup on Solidarity with the Sahrawi People and the Sahrawi Commission for Natural Resources and Legal Affairs. Attendees, including MEPs, lawyers, and international law experts, recalled that the Sahrawi issue is a decolonization process on the UN agenda. They emphasized that the UN's responsibility to complete decolonization and end the occupation remains, even with the non-renewal of MINURSO's mandate.

The meeting also presented a report on "Tourism under the context of occupation: the case of the occupied city of Dajla," which details Morocco's continued exploitation of Sahrawi resources, particularly through illegal agreements with the EU. The report examines tourism investments, including Ryanair flights between Madrid and Dajla, highlighting tourism's role in consolidating economic occupation and attempts to legitimize it through foreign investment.

Why it mattersThe Sahrawi people are directly affected by the ongoing conflict and the exploitation of their natural resources and territory.
