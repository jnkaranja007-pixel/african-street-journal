Rabat court jails four Sahrawi students for two years over protest

The students were convicted for a peaceful gathering outside a Rabat prison administration office, and Spanish jurists were kept out of the courtroom.

A court in Rabat sentenced four Sahrawi students, Brahim Babeit, Salah Eddine Essabar, El Hussein Chihab and Mohamed Lmrabet, to two years in prison and a fine of 15,000 dirhams, about 1,400 euros, each on Friday. They were arrested on 12 October after gathering outside the Moroccan prison administration headquarters in Rabat to demand action for Gdeim Izik prisoners on hunger strike.

Three other people detained the same day, human rights defenders, had their complaint dropped and were released within days. The four students were sent to Salé prison and held until sentencing. Two Spanish jurists who had travelled to Rabat to observe the trial, representing the International Association of Jurists for Western Sahara and the Spanish bar, were not allowed into the courtroom.

Brahim Babeit refused to give evidence to the prosecutor until he received care and ended up in hospital. Sahrawi rights groups CODESA and CODAPSO say he was beaten during arrest, and the prosecutor ordered a medical examination. The medical papers were missing from the file, which had led to the trial being postponed in October, but he was convicted all the same.

Why it mattersThe four men must each serve two years in prison and pay the fine, and the convictions stand despite the missing medical evidence.

Front Polisario issues mobilisation call after Morocco troop movements

The independence movement accuses Morocco of massing forces near its defensive wall.

The Front Polisario, a Sahrawi independence movement, launched a general mobilisation call on November 10. The group's leadership gathered around Brahim Ghali to announce the step.

Brahim Ghali accused Morocco of massing troops along the defensive wall. The Sahrawi People's Liberation Army is organised into seven military regions featuring infantry, armoured units and artillery.

The movement maintains its determination in the face of these military deployments. Jeune Afrique reported details on the group's forces and equipment on November 17.

Why it mattersThe Front Polisario has issued a general mobilisation call following reported troop movements along the defensive wall, affecting regional security arrangements.

Politics · 2026-11-17

EUCOCO conference concludes in Madrid as delegates prepare to move to Algiers

The fiftieth meeting closed after two days of debates following the liquidation of the UN mission in Western Sahara.

The fiftieth European Coordination Conference of Support and Solidarity with the Sahrawi People, known as EUCOCO, took place on 13 and 14 November in Madrid. Pierre Galand, president of the EUCOCO Task Force, opened the event by criticising the Moroccan autonomy plan, while an Algerian delegation attended alongside representatives from the Polisario Front, the movement fighting for the territory's independence. Mansur Omar, the Polisario representative to Europe, stated that the United Nations Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara, or MINURSO, had failed to protect the population before its liquidation on 1 November.

Sidi Mohamed Dadda, a former Sahrawi prisoner and activist, called the end of the UN mission a dangerous turning point during speeches on Saturday. He detailed ongoing persecution in the occupied territories, noting that four Sahrawi students were condemned on 13 November in Rabat to two years in prison and 15,000 dirhams of multa each. Abdulah Arabi, the Polisario representative in Spain, addressed the delegates from the occupied territories during the closing session.

The conference coincided with the fifty-first anniversary of the Madrid Accords and preceded Spain's upcoming vote on 29 November. Organizers announced that future coordination meetings will move to Algiers. The Algerian National Committee for Solidarity with the Sahrawi People reiterated its support for the Polisario Front and the Sahrawi Republic during the proceedings.

Why it mattersDelegates and activists from the solidarity conference must adjust their operations as meetings move to Algiers following the dissolution of the UN mission.
