Financial Times warns of escalation risk in Western Sahara

UN mission's mandate ends after 35 years, raising fears of military confrontation.

Source: ECS Saharaui · 2026-11-04 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The Financial Times warned on November 3, 2026, that Western Sahara faces unprecedented uncertainty following the end of the UN's MINURSO mission after 35 years. The British newspaper reported that the UN Security Council's failure to renew the mandate, due to US-Russia differences, could lead to greater military confrontation in a region already experiencing rising tensions and an arms race.

Ricardo Fabiani of the International Crisis Group cautioned that the withdrawal of UN observers from the buffer zone could trigger an uncontrollable clash from any military miscalculation. Moroccan MP Lahcen Haddad called the mission's end a "gift from heaven," believing Morocco can advance its autonomy plan without UN interference, contrasting with expert warnings that the mission's absence removes a key de-escalation mechanism.

Oubbi Bashraya Bashir, an advisor to Sahrawi President Brahim Gali, stated the Polisario Front will continue its political and military struggle for self-determination while maintaining restraint to facilitate a political solution, warning the mission's departure risks a dangerous escalation. UN Secretary-General António Guterres stressed that the mission's end does not negate the Sahrawi people's right to self-determination and expressed concern over intensified hostilities.

Why it mattersThe end of the UN mission and potential military escalation directly affects the people of Western Sahara and their pursuit of self-determination.
