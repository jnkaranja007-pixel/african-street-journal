Morocco jails four Sahrawi students for two years over peaceful protest

A Rabat court also fined each student 15,000 dirhams, about 1,400 euros, for joining a demonstration outside the prison administration in October.

Source: ECS Saharaui · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

The Rabat court of first instance on Friday sentenced four Sahrawi students, Brahim Babeit, Salah Eddine Essabar, El Hussein Chihab and Mohamed Lmrabet, to two years in prison and a fine of 15,000 dirhams each, about 1,400 euros. Their offence was joining a gathering on 12 October outside the Moroccan prison administration's headquarters in Rabat, to call for the release of Gdeim Izik prisoners on hunger strike.

Three others detained alongside them, human rights defenders, had their complaints dismissed and were freed within days. The four students were sent to Salé prison and held until sentencing. Two Spanish jurists who travelled to Rabat to follow the trial, representing the International Association of Lawyers for Western Sahara and the Spanish bar, were prevented from entering the courtroom.

Babeit refused to give a statement to the prosecutor until he received care and ended up in hospital. Sahrawi rights groups CODESA and CODAPSO said he was beaten during his arrest, and the prosecutor ordered a medical examination. Those medical records were among documents missing from the file, which had caused the trial to be postponed in October. He was convicted regardless.

Why it mattersThe four students now face two years in prison and must pay 15,000 dirhams each for a protest that the Gdeim Izik prisoners' families and supporters had backed.
