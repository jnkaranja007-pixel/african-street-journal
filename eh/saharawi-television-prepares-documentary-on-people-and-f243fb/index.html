Saharawi television prepares documentary on people and resource extraction

“Nazif Samit” examines Saharawis living under Moroccan occupation and the resources taken from the territory, according to its authors.

Source: ECS Saharaui · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Saharawi National Television in the occupied territories is preparing to premiere “Nazif Samit” (“Silent Bleeding”), an investigative documentary made by young Saharawi journalists with a group monitoring resources and protecting Western Sahara’s environment.

The film has two parts, according to its authors. One addresses repression, economic pressure and threats to Saharawi livelihoods under Moroccan occupation; the other documents the territory’s resources, extraction and employment at each site and sector. Mohamed Dchira leads the credits, which include six other names.

The authors say the film examines resource extraction in an unresolved legal and political conflict without the Saharawi people’s consent. The European Union’s Court of Justice ruled in October 2024 that Western Sahara is separate from Morocco and that exploiting its resources requires Saharawi consent. The documentary is due to air on Saharawi television before going to festivals and other Saharawi institutional events.

Why it mattersSaharawi viewers will see the film first on national television, with later screenings planned at festivals and Saharawi institutional events.
