Experts call for emergency decree on Kinshasa drainage after floods

Heavy rains on November 18 turned avenues into rivers and cut power poles, prompting engineers to demand urgent infrastructure fixes.

Source: Radio Okapi · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Following heavy rains on November 18, severe flooding in Kinshasa transformed main avenues into rivers and paralyzed traffic across several major roads. In some neighborhoods, erosion destroyed homes, leaving families displaced, while fallen Snel power poles severed routes in multiple locations.

Michel Uyumbu, president of the National Corporation of Civil Works Engineers, stated that the city's rainwater drainage system is failing. Experts argue that declaring a state of emergency for drainage infrastructure is necessary to prevent further damage during the remaining rainy season.

The discussion focused on preventive measures for the end-of-year period, when rainfall typically increases. Radio Okapi reported these exchanges between Uyumbu and journalist Jody Daniel Nkashama as the capital prepares for continued wet weather.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in Kinshasa must navigate closed roads and damaged homes until drainage systems are repaired or an emergency decree is issued.
