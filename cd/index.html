DR Congo and Israel Agree to Boost Security and Agriculture Cooperation

Presidents Tshisekedi and Herzog met in Jerusalem to expand partnerships in key sectors including technology, health, and investment.

DR Congo President Félix Tshisekedi and Israeli President Isaac Herzog met in Jerusalem on September 1 to reaffirm their commitment to strengthening bilateral cooperation. The discussions focused on enhancing collaboration in security, agriculture, food security, water management, health, technology, training, and investment. This meeting follows Herzog's visit to Kinshasa in November 2025 and aims to develop a mutually beneficial partnership between the two nations.

Both leaders identified agriculture and food security, water, health, technology and innovation, security, training, and investment as priority areas for deepening their cooperation. President Tshisekedi emphasized the importance of translating these agreements into tangible results, stating a desire to build a modern, pragmatic, and beneficial partnership with Israel that is measured by implementation and outcomes for both peoples.

The Congolese presidency described the meeting as part of President Tshisekedi's working visit to Israel. The leaders expressed a shared ambition to give a new dimension to the relationship between the Democratic Republic of Congo and Israel, aiming for concrete achievements beyond signed accords. Discussions also touched upon high technologies and security matters.

Why it mattersDR Congo and Israel plan to strengthen cooperation in agriculture, food security, water, and investment, directly impacting farmers and potential investors.

ADF Attacks Disrupt School Year in Mambasa

Armed group violence in Ituri province threatens to keep children out of classrooms as the new academic year begins.

The start of the 2026-2027 school year in the Democratic Republic of Congo is facing significant challenges in several localities within Mambasa, Ituri province. While millions of students across the country officially returned to classes on September 1, armed attacks attributed to the ADF are preventing many children in Mambasa from attending school.

The ongoing insecurity means that a substantial number of students in these affected areas risk remaining outside of educational facilities, despite the national efforts to ensure a smooth academic resumption. The situation highlights the stark contrast in provincial realities across the DRC as the new school term commences.

This disruption impacts the educational continuity for countless young learners in Mambasa. The national school year aims to enroll nearly 31 million students, but the security situation in specific regions like Ituri casts a shadow over these broader figures.

Why it mattersChildren in Mambasa are unable to attend school due to ongoing armed attacks by the ADF, disrupting their education.

Prime Minister Launches Ten-Year Anti-Erosion Strategy

The government aims to protect populations and infrastructure from erosion and flooding with a new national strategy.

The Congolese government has launched a ten-year national anti-erosion strategy to safeguard communities and infrastructure as the rainy season approaches. Prime Minister Judith Suminwa Tuluka led a strategic meeting with ministers of Economy, Budget, Infrastructure, and Finance to address the risks of erosion and flooding impacting Kinshasa and other provinces.

The strategy, detailed by Infrastructure Minister John Banza, is divided into three phases: an immediate one-year emergency response for critical sites, a one-to-five-year medium-term phase for urban planning updates, and a long-term phase up to ten years for structural solutions and rational land use. The Prime Minister is personally overseeing prevention efforts, emphasizing local adaptation and project completion.

Hundreds of erosion sites threaten homes, roads, and essential infrastructure in cities like Kinshasa and Kikwit. The government stated that sporadic interventions are insufficient and must be combined with a gradual reorganization of urbanization to provide a lasting response to these environmental challenges.

Why it mattersThe national anti-erosion strategy aims to protect populations and infrastructure from the risks of erosion and flooding, particularly in urban areas like Kinshasa and Kikwit.

Two soldiers sentenced to death for killing commander in Walikale

The Goma military court handed down sentences ranging from ten months to capital punishment for ten FARDC soldiers.

A military court in Walikale has sentenced two soldiers to death for the assassination of their commander. The tribunal, sitting in Walikale-centre, also imposed sentences of ten months imprisonment on eight other soldiers from the FARDC.

The Goma military garrison court convened an extraordinary session to hear the case. The convictions stem from the killing of a commanding officer within the FARDC ranks. Details regarding the motive or specific circumstances of the assassination were not provided.

This judicial action highlights internal disciplinary issues within the armed forces operating in the Walikale region. The sentences underscore the severe consequences for military personnel involved in serious offenses against their superiors.

Why it mattersThe convictions of soldiers for assassinating a commander may affect the stability and security of military operations in the region.

Kinshasa Subscribers Face Internet Data Validity Dilemma

Telecommunication operators in Kinshasa have reduced the validity period for some internet packages, forcing users to adapt their data consumption habits.

Subscribers in Kinshasa are reacting strongly to changes in the validity period for certain internet packages. A one gigabyte package, previously valid for twenty-four hours, now expires at 23:59 on the day of activation. This alteration compels many users to adjust their consumption patterns, with some opting for longer-validity packages, such as those lasting seventy-two hours, to avoid losing unused data.

The student parliament conducted a citizen review in August involving the ARPTC and major sector players. According to its president, André Kakubu, this initiative addressed several user concerns, including the establishment of a task force to monitor service quality. The ongoing discussion about the poor quality of telecommunication services in the capital highlights the need for operators to adapt their offers to user needs.

Jean-Marc, a subscriber encountered in Gombe, stated that the reduced validity period presents an additional challenge in managing daily consumption. The issue of declining telecommunication service quality remains a significant topic of conversation among users in Kinshasa, as they weigh the evolving offers against their actual requirements.

Why it mattersKinshasa subscribers are affected by reduced internet data validity periods, forcing them to either consume data faster or purchase longer-validity packages.
