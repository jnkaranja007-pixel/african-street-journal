DR Congo army seizes weapons from Mai-Mai camps

The FARDC recovered military equipment from suspected Mai-Mai Bakata Katanga camps in Haut-Katanga.

The FARDC recovered a significant cache of military equipment from camps believed to belong to the Mai-Maï Bakata Katanga militia in Haut-Katanga, southeastern DR Congo. The operation took place on October 27, 2026.

FARDC spokesperson reported the seizure of the arsenal. The exact quantity of recovered equipment was not specified, but it is described as substantial.

In a separate incident on the same day, FARDC forces repelled an attack by the CRP militia on their position in Bule, Ituri province.

DR Congo aims for 62% electricity access by 2030

The nation's energy compact targets increased access despite a low current rate and vast hydropower potential.

The Democratic Republic of Congo aims to raise its electricity access rate to 62% by 2030, according to the Congolese Energy Compact. This target comes as the nation currently has an access rate of only 21.5%, despite possessing an estimated 94,000 megawatts of hydropower potential.

The International Energy Agency (IEA) reported that Africa as a whole is projected to represent just 2.8% of global electricity consumption in 2026, consuming 843 terawatt-hours (TWh). This figure highlights a significant disparity, with China alone expected to consume over 12 times the African continent's total.

The African Development Bank (AfDB) and the World Bank are supporting the 'Mission 300' initiative to connect an additional 300 million Africans by 2030. These institutions have already provided electricity access to over 50 million people across 40 African countries as of June 2026.

Why it mattersCitizens in the Democratic Republic of Congo will experience increased access to electricity by 2030.

DR Congo Ebola cases surpass 10,000

The Democratic Republic of Congo has recorded over 10,000 confirmed Ebola cases and nearly 5,000 deaths since the outbreak began.

More than five months after the Ebola epidemic was declared in the Democratic Republic of Congo, the country has surpassed 10,000 confirmed cases. As of October 25, health ministry data shows 10,060 cases and 4,887 deaths. The Ituri province remains the epidemic's epicenter, with North Kivu also heavily affected.

On October 25 alone, health services confirmed 96 new cases, including 58 in North Kivu, 32 in Ituri, and six in Tshopo province, alongside 46 additional deaths. Ituri accounts for over 70 percent of infections with more than 7,200 cases and 3,370 deaths. North Kivu has over 2,300 cases and nearly 1,300 deaths.

The disease has spread to 66 health zones across eight provinces since May 15. While over 2,600 patients have recovered, response teams face challenges tracking contacts, especially in remote areas. In Ituri, two women with suspected symptoms could not be transferred to a treatment center due to a lack of available ambulances.

Why it mattersThe Ebola epidemic has affected over 10,000 people in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with nearly 5,000 deaths recorded.

DR Congo finishes Kasai river navigation markers

Navigation markers are now complete along 605 kilometers of the Kasai River, improving safety for transport.

Work to install navigation markers on a 605-kilometer stretch of the Kasai River between Kwamouth and Ilebo is finished. Brinto Desami, hydrography manager for Grand Bandundu, announced the completion on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, stating the project aims to boost navigation safety and reduce river accidents.

The new markers will help boats navigate more easily and safely along this waterway. Desami urged river users to follow the markers' guidance to prevent incidents. He also stressed the need for regular maintenance due to constant changes in water levels and riverbeds that affect boat passage.

The Kasai River is a vital transport route in the DRC's west-central region, used daily for moving people and goods between provinces. The boat 'Kauka', used for the marking work, is currently docked at the ONATRA port in Bandundu-Ville.

Why it mattersThe Kasai River is a vital transport route for people and goods in the DRC.

DR Congo government disburses $404 million to financial agencies

The government allocated 71.6% of the planned budget for financial agency operations by August 2026.

The Democratic Republic of Congo government disbursed 889.2 billion Congolese francs, approximately $404 million USD, to financial agencies by the end of August 2026. This amount represents 71.6% of the budgeted funds for these retrocessions during that period. These figures come from provisional budget monitoring reports published by the Directorate-General for Budgetary Policies and Programming (DGPPB).

The DGPPB reported that 889.2 billion CDF were executed out of a planned 1.242.8 billion CDF for retrocessions, leaving a shortfall of 353.6 billion CDF. These retrocessions support the operational functioning of agencies involved in collecting public revenue. The overall budget execution rate for the general budget reached 78.7% by August 2026, with spending on internal resources at 83.9% but external resource spending at only 26%.

Provisional data from the DGPPB for August 2026 shows a 78.7% overall expenditure execution rate, primarily driven by internal resources. Retrocessions to financial agencies achieved a 71.6% realization rate. The budget monitoring reports highlight a significant difference in the pace of public spending execution between internal and external funding sources.

Why it mattersThe DR Congo government has disbursed $404 million USD to its financial agencies by August 2026.
