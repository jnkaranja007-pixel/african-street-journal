DRC, Mota-Engil Sign Lobito Corridor Deal

The United States has welcomed the agreement, stating it will boost American and allied investments in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

The Democratic Republic of Congo and Mota-Engil have signed a concession contract and collaboration agreement for the Lobito Corridor project. This marks a significant step forward for the initiative, which aims to improve infrastructure and trade routes.

Washington has publicly endorsed the agreement, with officials stating that it is expected to stimulate further investment from the United States and its allies within the DRC. The project is seen as a key development for regional economic integration.

The Lobito Corridor is a vital transport route connecting the DRC to the Atlantic coast. The agreement with Mota-Engil is anticipated to enhance its operational efficiency and capacity, facilitating the movement of goods and people.

UN Chief Denounces Ebola Indifference Amidst DRC Outbreak

António Guterres calls for urgent international action against the largest Ebola epidemic in the Democratic Republic of Congo's history.

United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres has issued an urgent appeal to the global community to intensify efforts against the ongoing Ebola epidemic in the Democratic Republic of Congo. He characterized the international response as suffering from a "virus of indifference," highlighting the severity of the outbreak, which is now the most significant in the nation's recorded history. The call comes as health officials grapple with containment and treatment strategies.

The epidemic's scale necessitates a robust and coordinated international response, Guterres stated, emphasizing that the "virus of indifference" hinders effective containment. While the primary focus remains on the health crisis, separate reports from South Kivu's Kitutu village highlight persistent security concerns. Civil society there is denouncing the continued presence of military personnel and Wazalendo fighters in mining sites, a situation that persists despite a presidential order for their withdrawal.

This persistent presence of armed groups in mining areas, particularly in the Mwenga territory of South Kivu, raises concerns about stability and the potential for further conflict or disruption. The situation in Kitutu, where military forces remain in mining zones a month after President Félix Tshisekedi's directive for their removal, underscores ongoing challenges in implementing presidential orders and maintaining security in resource-rich regions.

Why it mattersThe UN chief's call for action affects the Democratic Republic of Congo by highlighting the severity of the Ebola epidemic and the need for global support.

UNC Deputies Pledge Support for National Dialogue

Lawmakers from the A/A UNC and AVK 2018 political groups met in Kinshasa to affirm their commitment to the president's national dialogue initiative.

Deputies from the A/A UNC and AVK 2018 political groups met in Kinshasa on Friday, August 29, following President Félix Tshisekedi's announcement of a national dialogue. The lawmakers gathered at the Palais du peuple to discuss the country's political situation and reaffirmed their support for the initiative, expressing a strong willingness to participate actively in the upcoming discussions.

Claude Misare Mugomberwa, president of both political groupings, stated that as the second-largest political force within the parliamentary majority, they are committed to actively contributing to the dialogue. He added that their aim is to propose recommendations that can be translated into concrete political decisions for the benefit of the Republic and the future of the Congolese people.

The elected officials are calling on all vital forces of the nation, including political actors, civil society, traditional leaders, religious confessions, women's organizations, and youth groups, to support this process. They emphasized the importance of prioritizing the nation's superior interest, national cohesion, and the pursuit of lasting peace, all while respecting the Democratic Republic of Congo's sovereignty and territorial integrity.

Why it mattersThe UNC deputies are affected by the national dialogue as they are pledging to actively participate and propose recommendations for concrete political decisions.

DRC, Tanzania, UNHCR Plan Refugee Returns

A tripartite commission agreed to facilitate voluntary repatriation of Congolese refugees from Tanzania based on resource availability and safe zones.

A joint commission involving the Democratic Republic of Congo, Tanzania, and the UNHCR has outlined a plan for the voluntary return of Congolese refugees residing in Tanzania. The eighth edition of the tripartite commission, held in Lubumbashi, concluded with an agreement to progressively repatriate refugees who wish to return to the DRC. This process will be implemented based on the availability of resources and the establishment of appropriate reception facilities.

The commission emphasized that all returns must be voluntary and free from pressure. Repatriation will be facilitated to areas identified by the Congolese government as safe, accessible, and suitable for sustainable reintegration. The UNHCR representative in the DRC, Patrick Kirya, noted that insecurity in parts of North Kivu, South Kivu, and Ituri currently prevents safe returns to these regions, and such returns are not encouraged.

The Congolese government, through the Vice Prime Minister and Minister of Interior, Jacquemain Shabani, assured that efforts are underway to create favorable conditions for the reception of returning refugees. The government is committed to facilitating the return of its citizens living in Tanzania, working to establish the necessary infrastructure and security measures for their reintegration.

Why it mattersCongolese refugees in Tanzania face uncertainty regarding their return due to ongoing insecurity in parts of their home country.

Mobondo militia withdrawal leaves Popokabaka with damaged fields and economy

Three months after the withdrawal of Mobondo militiamen, the territory of Popokabaka in Kwango province still suffers from damaged livestock, vandalized fields, and a collapsed.

The territory of Popokabaka, located in the Kwango province of the Democratic Republic of Congo, has not yet recovered from the occupation by Mobondo militiamen. Three months after their evacuation from villages where they had entrenched themselves, the region continues to bear the scars of their presence. The economic infrastructure is in ruins, and the livelihoods of residents have been severely impacted.

The aftermath of the Mobondo militia's presence includes widespread destruction. Livestock has been damaged, and agricultural fields vandalized, directly affecting the local food supply and the primary source of income for many families. The economic activities in the area have come to a standstill, leaving residents struggling to rebuild their lives and their community.

The lingering effects of the Mobondo phenomenon continue to plague Popokabaka. The violation of young girls and the overall devastation have left deep, unhealed wounds on the community. Efforts to restore normalcy and rebuild the shattered economy are ongoing, but the path to recovery remains long and arduous for the affected population.

Why it mattersThe people of Popokabaka are affected by the destruction of their livestock and fields, the collapse of the local economy, and the trauma of violence.
