Congolese Shipping Lines Cut Port Operations Amid Export Decline

Lignes maritimes congolaises (LMC) has reduced its ship rotations to five per year from twenty-two in 2017-2018 due to falling export demand.

Lignes maritimes congolaises (LMC) is experiencing a significant reduction in its activities at the port of Boma, with fewer ship rotations. This downturn is attributed to a decline in export demand over recent months, according to LMC's deputy director, Dieudonné Kasereka. The company has seen its annual voyages drop from twenty-two in 2017 and 2018 to just five currently, severely impacting its operations.

Kasereka highlighted that local economic operators and producers are facing considerable challenges. The port of Boma was originally established for agricultural product exports, but obtaining clearance for even small shipments now takes two to three months. He suggested that reviving old companies or supporting farmers to increase production could boost export volumes.

To sustain its operations and cover monthly expenses, such as fifteen thousand dollars for ship rental, LMC is advocating for a revival of local agricultural production and exports. Kasereka believes this is essential for LMC to generate enough revenue to cover its operating costs and ensure its long-term viability.

Why it mattersFarmers and economic operators are affected by the reduced export demand, leading to difficulties in shipping agricultural products and potentially impacting their livelihoods.

President Tshisekedi Inaugurates Religious Sites, Inspects Infrastructure in Mbuji-Mayi

President Félix Tshisekedi arrived in Mbuji-Mayi to inaugurate religious buildings and review ongoing infrastructure projects, including airport and road upgrades.

President Félix Tshisekedi is in Mbuji-Mayi, the capital of Kasaï-Oriental province, for the inauguration of several religious structures. The new church of the Notre-Dame de l’Espérance University Parish, the Saints Félix and Antoine presbytery, and the Notre-Dame du Balai sanctuary are set to be officially opened. The President will also inspect construction sites for key infrastructure, including the Bipemba international airport and national road number 1.

The city is experiencing increased activity with the President's arrival. Security has been heightened with the deployment of Republican Guard elements and national police. Roadworks on Avenue Inga, which has been transformed into a dual-carriageway leading to the President's temporary residence, are accelerating. The N'Sele military camp is also undergoing significant renovations. Notably, women sweepers have returned to the main streets after a months-long absence due to unpaid bonuses.

Mbuji-Mayi is bustling with delegations, including the Union sacrée and national deputies from the Grand Kasaï region, who are staying in fully booked hotels. The governor has urged the population to provide a warm welcome to the head of state. The influx of visitors for the event is palpable, with arrivals at Bipemba airport occurring day and night.

Why it mattersThe President's visit and infrastructure focus directly impacts the capital city and its residents through security presence and accelerated public works.

Butembo Students Demand Health Measures Before School Reopens

Students in Butembo are worried about Ebola and calling for improved sanitation in schools before classes begin September 1.

Students in Butembo are demanding enhanced sanitary measures in schools ahead of the September 1 reopening, citing concerns over the ongoing Ebola epidemic. Kambale Malyabwana, the student committee coordinator, urged authorities and aid organizations to bolster health protocols within educational facilities. Key issues include poor toilet conditions in over 100 schools, insufficient access to water, and overcrowded classrooms, with 70 to 100 students per room, all of which could facilitate the spread of the virus.

Malyabwana stated that student representatives have been advocating for better sanitation and water access since July. He noted that the introduction of free primary education has led to a significant increase in student numbers, but without adequate health measures, this influx could exacerbate the Ebola outbreak. Official responses from school administrators regarding these demands have not yet been received.

The students' concerns highlight a potential public health challenge as the academic year commences. The lack of basic sanitary infrastructure and overcrowding in schools present a significant risk, particularly given the heightened alert due to the Ebola epidemic in the Nord-Kivu region. The situation remains unresolved as the start date for classes approaches.

Why it mattersStudents in Butembo are affected by the potential spread of Ebola due to poor sanitation and overcrowding in schools.

UNC MPs Pledge Support for National Dialogue

Lawmakers from the A/A UNC and AVK 2018 political groups met to affirm their commitment to contributing to peace and state rebuilding.

Deputies from the A/A UNC and AVK 2018 political groups met on August 29 in Kinshasa to discuss the nation's political situation following President Félix Tshisekedi's announcement of a national dialogue. The lawmakers reaffirmed their support for the initiative and expressed a strong desire to actively participate in the upcoming discussions.

Claude Misare Mugomberwa, president of both political groups, stated that as the second-largest force within the parliamentary majority, they are committed to contributing recommendations that can be transformed into concrete political decisions for the Republic's benefit and the Congolese people's future. He urged all national stakeholders, including political actors, civil society, traditional leaders, religious bodies, women's organizations, and youth groups, to support this Congolese-led process.

The elected officials emphasized the importance of prioritizing the nation's superior interest, national cohesion, and the pursuit of lasting peace, all while respecting the Democratic Republic of Congo's sovereignty and territorial integrity. Their participation signals a unified front from a significant parliamentary bloc in addressing the country's challenges through dialogue.

Why it mattersThe UNC MPs are affected by the national dialogue as they are pledging to contribute to peace and state rebuilding, which directly impacts the governance and future of the country.

Health · 2026-08-30

Community Resistance Hinders Ebola Response in Ituri

Ebola response efforts in Ituri face ongoing challenges due to population resistance, leading to continued community deaths and disease spread.

The fight against the Ebola virus in Ituri province remains obstructed by resistance from a segment of the population. Despite health teams' efforts, community deaths are still being recorded in some Bunia neighborhoods and surrounding areas, which fuels the epidemic's spread. In areas where prevention measures are followed, authorities observe a decline in new cases. However, in other sectors, some individuals continue to deny the disease's existence or refuse to cooperate with response teams.

Affected neighborhoods include Ndibe, Simbilyabo, and parts of Lembabo, along with peripheral localities such as Miala, Centrale Soleniama, Nizi, and Iga-Barrière, located about ten kilometers north of Bunia. To counter this trend, response teams and their partners are intensifying awareness campaigns and increasing the involvement of community leaders. A women's organization in Miala conducted a campaign on prevention measures.

Last Thursday, teams met with customary chiefs from the Baboa Bokoe chiefdom. Following discussions, eight group chiefs signed a commitment to support the response. Health authorities urge other opinion leaders to follow this example to strengthen community mobilization and help end an epidemic that has claimed over two thousand seven hundred lives.

Why it mattersThe population's resistance to Ebola prevention measures in specific areas of Ituri is directly hindering the effectiveness of the health response, leading to continued community deaths and disease propagation.
