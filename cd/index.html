Kinshasa Train Derails Days After Relaunch

The urban train in Kinshasa derailed on Wednesday evening, halting service just fifteen days after its official resumption.

The Kinshasa urban train ceased operations Thursday morning after derailing Wednesday evening in the commune of Masina. The incident occurred fifteen days after the train's official relaunch, raising concerns about the condition of the aging infrastructure managed by the National Transport Office (ONTRA). Works are underway to potentially allow the train to return to its starting point in Masina by Thursday evening.

The derailment happened around six-twenty in the evening as the train traveled from the city center to N'djili Airport. Fortunately, the locomotive only left the rails by approximately one meter, resulting in no fatalities, injuries, or major damage. According to ONOTRA, sand and garbage obstructed the railway, with trucks allegedly dumping sand illegally and residents discarding waste.

This derailment follows the August 19, 2026, relaunch of the Kinshasa urban train by the National Transport Office after a fifteen-year hiatus. The service was intended as an alternative to the city's persistent traffic congestion. Train conductor Santos Nguizani anticipates traffic will resume by Thursday evening, though some residents are calling for authorities to secure the tracks.

Why it mattersThe derailment halts the urban train service, affecting commuters who relied on it to bypass Kinshasa's traffic jams.

DR Congo soldiers sentenced to death for murder

Two FARDC soldiers received the death penalty for the assassination of Captain Musanga Bebeto in Walikale, North Kivu.

A military court in Goma has sentenced two FARDC soldiers to death following a trial in Walikale, North Kivu. The convictions stem from the assassination of Captain Musanga Bebeto, an incident that occurred in October 2025. The two soldiers were found guilty of murder, criminal participation, and the dissipation of war munitions.

In total, ten soldiers from the 3415th FARDC regiment faced sentencing for various offenses, including cowardice before the enemy and the misuse of military supplies. Three soldiers received ten-year prison sentences, while five others were given ten-month sentences with significant mitigating circumstances for their involvement in the dissipation of war munitions.

Major Dieudonné Kasereka, spokesperson for the 34th military region, confirmed the sentences. The implicated soldiers were reportedly engaged in operations against AFC/M23 rebels in the Masisi territory when they allegedly fired upon their company commander, Captain Musanga Bebeto, in the village of Lukole.

Why it mattersMilitary personnel and their families are directly affected by these sentences, impacting morale and discipline within the FARDC.

DR Congo Mines Ministry Probes Illicit Operations in South Kivu

An ad hoc commission will audit mining companies in Mwenga, Fizi, Uvira, Baraka, and Shabunda following numerous complaints of illegal exploitation.

The national Ministry of Mines has established an ad hoc commission to verify the compliance of companies operating in South Kivu province. This initiative, which began on Monday, August 31, targets mining operations in Mwenga, Fizi, Uvira, Baraka, and Shabunda. The move comes amid a surge in complaints from civil society groups regarding ongoing illicit mineral exploitation in the region.

Sources close to the ministry indicate that mining operators are being summoned for hearings to present documentation proving their legitimacy. National deputies elected from South Kivu will attend these sessions, which aim to scrutinize company records and ensure adherence to mining laws. The ministry stated that a report will be compiled after the verifications to determine appropriate measures.

This action follows multiple reports of overlapping mining titles in Mwenga. Lugushwa Mining recently alleged that local cooperatives are being used as fronts for shell companies to illegally extract gold. The company also implicated some local authorities in this alleged illicit network, though provincial officials have declined to comment on the matter.

Why it mattersMining companies operating in South Kivu are subject to audits to ensure compliance with regulations, potentially impacting their operations and legal standing.

President Tshisekedi Calls National Dialogue to Address Crises

A national dialogue initiated by President Félix Tshisekedi aims to find solutions to the country's crises, including insecurity in the East.

President Félix Tshisekedi announced on August 27 that a national dialogue will soon be convened to allow Congolese people to collectively seek solutions to the nation's crises. The ongoing insecurity in the eastern part of the country is a primary concern to be addressed during these discussions.

The dialogue process is set to commence with consultations held in provincial capitals. Following these initial meetings, a final phase of the dialogue is scheduled to take place in Kinshasa, the nation's capital. This approach aims to gather input from across the country before concluding.

Minister of Communication and Media, Patrick Muyaya, serving as the government spokesperson, is addressing all questions and concerns related to this national dialogue process. His role involves clarifying the objectives and procedures for the upcoming consultations and the subsequent Kinshasa meeting.

Why it mattersThe national dialogue initiated by President Tshisekedi aims to address the country's crises, including insecurity in the East, affecting all Congolese citizens.

Motorbike taxi drivers report police extortion in Kinshasa

Drivers in Sakombi say frequent, unjustified payments to police are draining their income and disrupting their work.

Motorbike taxi drivers from the Sakombi stop in Kinshasa's Ngaliema commune are protesting what they describe as police harassment through excessive road checks and illegal fees. The drivers, who gathered on Thursday, claim that many of these checks result in payments they deem unwarranted. They are calling for authorities to intervene and establish better oversight of these police operations.

According to the drivers, these road checks have become a significant source of financial loss for their businesses. They cite repeated payments, what they term arbitrary arrests, and searches conducted during nighttime police patrols. Éléphant Nzevu, president of the Sakombi motorbike taxi drivers, appealed directly to the head of state for intervention, stating that money is being lost for no reason and that those collecting taxes are pocketing the funds.

The drivers are requesting that authorities strengthen the regulation of road checks and actively combat what they consider illegal collections. They also desire improved working conditions for operators who adhere to regulations and seek measures to end the practices they are denouncing. Radio Okapi had not yet received a response from police officials at the time of publication.

Why it mattersMotorbike taxi drivers are affected by alleged illegal fees and arbitrary arrests during police checks, reducing their income and disrupting their work.
