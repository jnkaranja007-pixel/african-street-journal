DR Congo prime minister submits 2027 budget to assembly

Judith Suminwa Tuluka presented the draft finance law for the upcoming fiscal year on October 31, 2026.

Prime Minister Judith Suminwa Tuluka presented the draft finance law for the 2027 fiscal year to the National Assembly on October 31, 2026. She stated that every Congolese franc mobilized should lead to real progress in citizens' lives and every decision made should be a promise kept for the future of Congo.

The submission of the 2027 budget project occurred on October 31, 2026. The prime minister's statement emphasized the connection between mobilized funds and tangible improvements for the Congolese people. The proposed budget is now under examination by the National Assembly.

Separately, the President of the Democratic Republic of Congo participated in the third Russia-Africa Summit held in Moscow from October 28 to 29, 2026. This summit focused on strengthening political, economic, and diplomatic cooperation between Russia and African states.

Kinshasa parents worry over children's sugar intake

Sweet treats sold for as little as 100 Congolese francs are easily accessible near schools.

Excessive sugar consumption among children is causing concern for parents and health professionals in Kinshasa. Sweets and sugary drinks, available from 100 Congolese francs in shops and from street vendors, are readily accessible to young children, particularly near schools. This easy availability makes it difficult for some parents to limit these products in their children's diets.

One mother reported that her 10-year-old son consumes sweets 90% of the time, actively seeking them out despite her health warnings. Another parent expressed frustration, stating that even if she forbids sweets, her child will consume them when unsupervised outside the home. Dr. Louise Hanyange, deputy director of the anti-diabetes program, warned on October 29 that this excessive intake leads to dental cavities, overweight, obesity, and potentially type 2 diabetes.

Dr. Hanyange recommended that parents reduce their children's daily sugar intake and encourage water, fruits, and unsweetened beverages instead. The health risks highlighted include dental problems, weight gain, and the onset of type 2 diabetes.

Why it mattersChildren in Kinshasa are at risk of dental cavities, obesity, and type 2 diabetes due to easy access to cheap sugary snacks.

DR Congo families urged to rebuild state values

Academics and NGO leaders call for families to instill civic and moral values for national reconstruction.

The reconstruction of the Congolese state must begin within families, according to Professor Dezzy Mukebayi, dean of the Faculty of Law at the Official University of Mbuji-Mayi. Speaking at the 6th interdisciplinary colloquium in Ngandajika, Kasaï-Central, he urged parents to transmit civic and moral values to children. Mukebayi stated that Congolese families are weakened by socio-economic crisis and insecurity, hindering their ability to teach respect, responsibility, integrity, sharing, and solidarity.

Professor Mukebayi emphasized that the family is the primary space for learning societal rules and civic values, calling it a political and civic space. He encouraged parents to set examples and appealed to public authorities to better support families in their educational mission. Nancy Ngalula, founder of the NGO ADAAYE, added that national rebuilding also requires restoring cultural and moral values that form Congolese identity.

Ngalula highlighted the urgency of rehabilitating principles of dignity, respect, and social cohesion, citing observed violence and brutality within some families, including those committed by relatives. The colloquium focused on the family's role as a pillar in the state's refoundation.

Why it mattersCongolese families are directly affected by socio-economic crisis and insecurity, impacting their ability to transmit essential values for national reconstruction.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Kinshasa assembly considers funeral management law

A proposed law in Kinshasa aims to regulate funeral organization, morgue operations, and cemetery management.

The Kinshasa provincial assembly declared a proposed law on funeral sector reorganization admissible on October 16, 2026. Initiated by provincial deputy Steve Mulumba, the 50-article text sets a 30-day maximum for organizing funerals and limits corpse viewings to two hours. It also seeks to regulate morgues, cemeteries, and exhumations, requiring prior authorization and technical standards to protect public health.

Mulumba stated the initiative aims to prevent disease spread, ensure human dignity, and respect cultural values by curbing prolonged wake-keeping and ceremonies. Some violations under the proposed law could lead to prison sentences of up to five years.

Radio Okapi listeners raised concerns about the law's affordability for low-income families and its practical implementation. The proposal must still pass legislative procedures before potential adoption, with the economic realities of households being a key consideration.

Why it mattersA proposed law in Kinshasa seeks to regulate funeral practices, potentially affecting public health and cultural traditions.

Four killed in attack on army position in Ituri

A soldier and three militia members died in the Thursday, October 29 attack in Bule-centre.

A soldier from the FARDC and three militia members of the Convention for the Popular Revolution (CRP) were killed on Thursday, October 29. The attack targeted an army position in Bule-centre, located in the Djugu territory of Ituri province.

Security sources reported that seven other militia members sustained serious injuries during the confrontation. Socio-economic activities in the affected locality were disrupted following the incident.

The FARDC is the armed forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo. The CRP is a militia group operating in the region.

Why it mattersSocio-economic activities were disrupted in Bule-centre following the attack.
