DR Congo police arrest over 20 corrupt and fake officers

Kinshasa operation uncovers officers involved in extortion and arbitrary detention.

The General Inspectorate of the Congolese National Police (IG-PNC) arrested over 20 corrupt officers and several fake police officers in Kinshasa during a three-day operation. Nearly 70 inspectors conducted checks at police stations from October 28 to 30.

The IG-PNC stated the operation aimed to identify officers involved in law violations, including arbitrary detentions, and individuals illegally performing police duties. Many arrested lacked service cards or official documents, with some allegedly recruited irregularly by commanders and equipped with police uniforms and gear.

These fake officers are accused of extortion and other infractions, sometimes using civilians for illegal activities. The IG-PNC is also searching for commanders who facilitated the infiltration of these fake officers, and controls will continue across all police units.

Armed men cause fear in Lubero

Residents of Mwenye, in Lubero territory, Nord-Kivu, are living in fear due to the presence of armed men.

A new security concern has gripped residents of Mwenye, within the Baswagha chiefdom in Lubero territory, Nord-Kivu. The presence of armed men in the Mwenye community has revived fear among the inhabitants.

In Kayna, a commune in southern Lubero territory, a climate of fear has prevailed since October 28. The area, occupied by the Front pour la refondation de la nation (FRN), formerly AFC/M23, has experienced a series of arrests and violent acts following the assassination of three FRN members on October 18.

The FRN's occupation of Kayna has led to significant unrest and insecurity for the local population.

US midterm elections may alter Washington's DRC agreements

The November 3rd US midterm elections could change power dynamics in Washington, affecting future deals.

The US midterm elections on November 3rd may reshape the balance of power in Washington. This shift could influence agreements between the United States and Kinshasa.

The election results will determine party control of the US Congress, impacting the president's ability to advance foreign policy. Changes in congressional leadership could lead to a review of existing bilateral accords.

The specific implications for agreements between Washington and Kinshasa depend on the final vote tallies and the legislative agenda.

Why it mattersThe DR Congo's agreements with Washington could be impacted by the US midterm election results.

Kolwezi residents protest mining pollution

Residents of Dilungu and Bel Air districts are demanding compensation for housing damage and health issues.

Residents of the Dilungu and Bel Air neighbourhoods in Kolwezi, Lualaba province, demonstrated on Friday, October 30, to protest environmental and health problems they attribute to Ruashi Mining's operations.

The protesters are seeking reparations for damages, including cracks in their homes, dried-up crops, and an increase in reported health ailments.

The demonstration highlights ongoing concerns about the impact of mining activities on local communities and their environment in the region.

DR Congo health campaign finds 43 suspected cancer cases

A health campaign in South and North Kivu screened 113 women and raised awareness among nearly 600 people.

Health workers in the Democratic Republic of Congo's South and North Kivu provinces identified 43 suspected cases of breast and cervical cancer during an October campaign. The initiative, led by the Bunyakiri health zone and the Clubs des filles reporters de la RDC, provided 113 free consultations and raised awareness among over 580 people across three health zones.

Simon Mihona, coordinator for the Clubs des filles reporters, reported that 421 people were informed in Bunyakiri, 90 in Kalonge, and 25 community leaders in Itebero. Medical consultations included 61 in Bunyakiri, 29 in Itebero, and 23 in Kalonge, leading to the identification of the 43 potential cases, one of which was considered probable in Bunyakiri.

Dr. Marquis Chiralo, head of the Bunyakiri health zone, stated that individuals with suspicious signs require further tests to confirm or rule out cancer. He urged partners and donors to support such actions, while Kasese Maene, head of the Gender and Family office in Bunyakiri, called for more resources to ensure women receive accurate health status assessments.

Why it mattersWomen in South and North Kivu are affected by the identification of 43 suspected cases of breast and cervical cancer requiring further medical follow-up.
