UN condemns murder of Ebola nurse in Bafwasende

The UN Ebola response office called for an investigation into the killing of a nurse on October 24.

The United Nations Ebola response office condemned the murder of a nurse on Saturday, October 24, in Bafwasende, Tsho Province. The UN office, led by Julien Harneis, issued a strong condemnation of the killing and called for a thorough investigation into the incident.

The nurse was engaged in the response efforts against the Ebola virus disease in the region. The killing occurred amidst rumors related to Ebola in Bafwasende, according to Radio Okapi.

The UN has urged for swift action to bring those responsible to justice and ensure the safety of health workers operating in affected areas.

Why it mattersA nurse involved in the Ebola response was murdered in Bafwasende.

DR Congo's Banana SEZ gets expert approval

The project in Muanda, Kongo Central, secured a favorable opinion with over $100 million investment planned.

Experts have given a preliminary green light to the Banana Special Economic Zone (SEZ) project in Muanda, Kongo Central. The project, led by ARISE IIP, received this positive assessment after an October 21-23, 2026, commission meeting. This development aims to transform the province into a new industrial and logistics hub for the Democratic Republic of Congo.

The Banana SEZ project plans to mobilize $104 million in investments across approximately 984 hectares. ARISE IIP's request for the industrial infrastructure was approved by the Special Economic Zone Status Award Commission. Director General of the Special Economic Zone Agency (AZES), Auguy Bolanda, stated the initiative is expected to create 50,000 direct and indirect jobs.

The SEZ is designed to attract businesses in logistics, agro-industry, and construction materials. Its proximity to the Banana deep-water port, currently under construction, is seen as a key advantage. The project also includes plans for training centers to develop necessary skills for companies within the zone.

Why it mattersThe Banana SEZ project in DR Congo is expected to create 50,000 jobs and attract $104 million in investment.

Health · 2026-10-26

DR Congo research institute signs health accords

The Institut One Health pour l'Afrique (INOHA) and two national health programs agreed to collaborate on research and public health.

The Institut One Health pour l'Afrique (INOHA), a research and training body at the University of Kinshasa, signed two agreements on Saturday, October 24. INOHA partnered with the National Mental Health Program and the National Dracunculiasis Elimination Program, both under the Ministry of Public Health.

These accords aim to strengthen the link between scientific research and public health initiatives to better address health challenges. Professor Didier Bompangue, INOHA's deputy director, stated the goal is to bridge the gap between researchers and public health services. Dr. Delphin Antwisi, a 'One Health' specialist, believes the partnerships will improve collaboration between scientific institutions and public health administrations.

The agreements intend to promote the use of research findings in decision-making and policy development within the Democratic Republic of Congo. This collaboration seeks to produce data-driven solutions for the population's health needs.

Why it mattersThe agreements will improve the use of research in decision-making and the development of public health policies in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Tshisekedi orders audit of state computer systems

DR Congo president seeks to identify security flaws and protect sensitive state data amid rising cyber threats.

President Félix Tshisekedi ordered an audit of public digital infrastructure on October 23, 2026, to find security weaknesses and safeguard state data. International alerts about cyber threats, including espionage and sabotage, prompted the move. Ministers of Posts and Telecommunications, and Digital Economy, will lead the audit with specialized bodies to identify critical vulnerabilities and propose urgent protection measures.

The Ministries of Interior and National Defense will enhance surveillance of their sensitive computer systems, supported by security and intelligence services. The Ministry of Finance and the Central Bank of Congo (BCC) must secure public finance management platforms, payment systems, and critical IT infrastructure. Tshisekedi also called for permanent early warning mechanisms, coordinated incident response, and public agent awareness.

This initiative addresses limited digital modernization in DR Congo, which ranked 179th out of 193 countries in a 2024 UN e-government survey. Interpol's 2026 African Cyberthreat Assessment Report noted AI facilitates 55% of reported cybercrimes across 36 countries, with financial losses doubling to $484 million USD since 2024. Cybersecurity is a pillar of the 2026-2030 National Digital Plan, backed by $1 billion USD in public resources and over $500 million USD in external aid.

Why it mattersThe audit aims to protect sensitive state data from cyber threats like espionage and sabotage, affecting public services and financial platforms.

Kolwezi police arrest 58 after mining protests

Security services presented 58 alleged offenders to the Lualaba interior minister for vandalism linked to artisanal miners' unrest.

Security services presented 58 alleged offenders to the Lualaba provincial interior minister on Friday, October 23, 2026. These individuals are accused of vandalism and public disorder in Kolwezi this week.

The suspects were arrested in the Luilu, Kapata, Kasulo, Bel-Air, and Chabula neighborhoods. Authorities believe they exploited protests by artisanal miners to commit crimes and create insecurity. One alleged fetish priest with amulets was also presented, suspected of colluding with some offenders.

The unrest began after an inspection operation by a Kinshasa commission closed several mineral processing units. Artisanal miners stated these closures halted their activities, leading to protests that escalated into vandalism. Provincial authorities continue investigations to identify all perpetrators and restore order.

Why it matters58 alleged offenders were arrested in Kolwezi following protests by artisanal miners that led to vandalism and public disorder.
