Sport · 2026-11-01

DR Congo boxers win eight African titles

Congolese women secured all eight of their final matches at the amateur boxing championship held in Kinshasa.

The Democratic Republic of Congo has excelled at the amateur boxing Championship of Africa, hosted in Kinshasa from October 19 to November 1, 2026. Congolese female boxers achieved a perfect record, winning all eight of their finals. Several Congolese male boxers also secured continental titles.

Sulamite Tshimwanga won the over 81 kg category, while Déborah Boisabu defeated Zambia's Catherine Mwape in the 52-54 kg division. Ruth Yamfu beat Cameroon's Paule Alliance in the 54-57 kg category. Bénédicte Diyoka earned her second African title in the 45-48 kg final against Bénie Ndoudi of Congo-Brazzaville. Munga Zalia Modestie also claimed the 57-60 kg title after previous silver and bronze medals.

Merveille Mbalayi, Monique Bampala, and Jorbelle Malewu also contributed to the medal haul, defeating opponents from Senegal and Cameroon. In the men's finals, Kolongo Gloire beat Cape Verde's David Daniel Pina in the 51-54 kg category, and Beny Njibu Njibu won the 60-63.5 kg title against Cape Verde's Kevin Admolson.

Why it mattersCongolese boxers, both men and women, have won multiple titles at the continental championship held in their capital city.

Health · 2026-11-01

DR Congo campaign finds 43 suspected cancer cases

Health workers screened 113 women and raised awareness among nearly 600 people in South and North Kivu.

Health workers in the Democratic Republic of Congo identified 43 suspected cases of breast and cervical cancer during an October campaign in South and North Kivu provinces. The initiative, led by the Bunyakiri health zone and the Clubs des filles reporters, also provided free consultations to 113 women and raised awareness among over 580 people across three health zones.

Simon Mihona, coordinator for the Clubs des filles reporters, reported that 421 people were informed in Bunyakiri, 90 in Kalonge, and 25 community leaders in Itebero. Medical staff conducted 61 consultations in Bunyakiri, 29 in Itebero, and 23 in Kalonge, leading to the identification of the 43 potential cases, including one probable case in Bunyakiri.

Dr. Marquis Chiralo, head of the Bunyakiri health zone, stated that individuals with suspicious signs require further examinations to confirm or rule out cancer. He urged partners and donors to support such actions, while Kasese Maene, head of the Gender and Family office in Bunyakiri, called for more resources to ensure women receive accurate health status checks.

Why it mattersWomen in the Kalehe and Walikale territories of South and North Kivu need further medical examinations for suspected breast and cervical cancer.

Health · 2026-11-01

Kolwezi residents protest Ruashi Mining pollution

Residents of Dilungu and Bel Air neighbourhoods cite health problems and property damage, demanding compensation.

Residents of Dilungu and Bel Air in Kolwezi, Lualaba province, demonstrated on Friday, October 30, to protest environmental and health issues they attribute to Ruashi Mining's operations. They are seeking reparations for damages including cracks in their homes, dried-up crops, and increased health ailments.

The protesters marched to the company's facilities and submitted a memorandum detailing their grievances. They stated they have been alerting authorities to their situation since October 2025 without a satisfactory response. The memorandum specifically mentions cracked homes, damaged roofs, and withered plantations as consequences of the mining activities.

The residents also reported an increase in respiratory illnesses, skin irritations, and nosebleeds. They expressed deep concern and called for serious investigations to determine causes and responsibilities. The protesters regret the lack of concrete measures from authorities despite their repeated efforts since October 2025.

Politics · 2026-11-01

DR Congo families urged to rebuild state values

Professor Dezzy Mukebayi calls for civic and moral values to be taught within families to strengthen the nation.

The reconstruction of the Congolese state must begin within families, stated Professor Dezzy Mukebayi, dean of the Faculty of Law at the Official University of Mbuji-Mayi. He spoke at the 6th interdisciplinary colloquium in Ngandajika, Lomami province, urging parents to transmit civic and moral values to their children. Mukebayi highlighted that Congolese families are weakened by socio-economic crises and insecurity, hindering their ability to teach respect, responsibility, integrity, sharing, and solidarity.

Professor Mukebayi described the family as the primary space where children learn societal rules and civic values, calling it a political and civic space. He encouraged parents to set good examples and appealed to public authorities to better support families in their educational mission. Nancy Ngalula, founder of the NGO ADAAYE, added that restoring cultural and moral values is essential for national identity, noting the urgency to rehabilitate dignity and social cohesion due to observed violence within families.

The colloquium focused on the family as a pillar for rebuilding the Congolese state. Professor Mukebayi's call for state support for families' educational roles and Ngalula's emphasis on restoring cultural values aim to address the societal challenges affecting the nation's foundation.

Why it mattersThe citizens of DR Congo are affected by the call to rebuild state values through family education, which is essential for national reconstruction.
