Ituri Youth Parliament requests school year delay due to Ebola epidemic

The Youth Parliament of Ituri has asked the government to postpone the school year, officially scheduled for September 1, 2026, citing the ongoing Ebola epidemic.

The Youth Parliament of Ituri is advocating for a delay in the upcoming school year, which is set to begin on September 1, 2026. This request follows concerns about the persistent Ebola virus disease epidemic in the region, which continues to spread across six provinces of the country.

The call for postponement comes as the World Health Organization (WHO) has urged warring parties in northeastern DR Congo to implement a six-month humanitarian truce. This truce would allow medical teams to access affected populations and halt the transmission of the Ebola disease.

The inter-union body previously made a similar request, highlighting the widespread impact of the epidemic. The Youth Parliament's appeal underscores the growing concern among local organizations regarding the safety of students and educators amidst the health crisis.

Why it mattersStudents, parents, and educators in Ituri are directly affected by the potential delay of the school year, impacting their educational plans and daily routines.

Health · 2026-08-25

Cholera outbreak kills 27 in Popokabaka, Kwango province

Médecins sans frontières (MSF) reported 756 cholera cases and 27 deaths in Popokabaka health zone between June 1 and August 24, 2026.

A cholera outbreak in the Popokabaka health zone of Kwango province has led to 27 deaths and 756 recorded cases since June 1, according to figures released by Médecins sans frontières (MSF) on Monday, August 24. The epidemiological services of MSF have been tracking the resurgence of the disease in the area.

In response to the escalating situation, a Cholera Treatment Center (CTC) has been established at the Popokabaka General Referral Hospital to admit and manage patients. Additionally, MSF has set up four peripheral treatment units in other health facilities to bring care closer to affected communities and support the main center.

MSF teams are collaborating with local health personnel to combat the epidemic. Their efforts include supplying medical care, managing patients, and providing support to the teams engaged in the response to control the spread of the disease in the region.

Why it mattersResidents of Popokabaka are directly affected by the cholera outbreak, facing illness and death, while local health services are strained by the increased patient load.

Business · 2026-08-25

Seven bodies recovered after minibus plunges into abandoned mine in Kakanda

Seven bodies, including two customary chiefs, were retrieved from the waters of the former Kabamba Nkola mine in Kakanda, Lualaba, after a minibus accident.

Seven bodies were recovered from the abandoned Kabamba Nkola mine in Kakanda, Lualaba, after a minibus carrying a delegation of customary chiefs plunged into its waters. Six bodies were pulled from the water on Monday, August 24, following the recovery of customary chief Kuvumbi's body on Sunday, August 23, according to local sources. The bodies were in an advanced state of decomposition, having been submerged for at least five days.

The minibus, which was also recovered, reportedly fell into the mine's waters last Wednesday. The delegation of customary chiefs was en route to a mining site to perform traditional ceremonies, aiming to allow artisanal miners to operate there. The NGO Action for Integral and Sustainable Development (ADID) reported this information.

According to a witness testimony gathered by ADID, the driver lost control of the vehicle on a downhill slope, causing the minibus to be projected into the old mine's waters. The accident resulted in seven fatalities and three survivors. Search operations successfully located the vehicle before the victims were retrieved, an operation observed by a crowd, as confirmed by the Kakanda delegate.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects artisanal miners and customary chiefs by disrupting their plans for mining operations and causing fatalities.

DR Congo Basketball Team Focuses on World Cup Qualification

The national basketball team of the Democratic Republic of Congo is in a state of maximum concentration two days before the final window of the FIBA World Cup 2026 qualifiers.

The DR Congo national basketball team is intensifying its focus as it prepares for the fourth and final qualifying window for the FIBA World Cup 2026. The team's objective is to secure qualification for the global tournament, according to Shekinah Munanga.

The atmosphere within the national selection is described as one of maximum concentration, with the crucial matches approaching rapidly. This final window represents the last opportunity for the team to earn a spot in the World Cup.

The qualifiers are set to begin in two days, marking a critical period for the Congolese basketball program. The team aims to achieve its goal of qualification, which would represent a significant accomplishment for the nation's sports sector.

News · 2026-08-25

Environmentalists denounce illegal fishing of fry in Lake Kivu

The Environmental Civil Society of Kalehe reports that the continued illegal fishing of fry in Lake Kivu threatens food security for local residents.

Illegal fishing of fry persists in Lake Kivu, according to the Environmental Civil Society of Kalehe in South Kivu. This practice endangers the food security of residents, as reported on Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, two thousand twenty-six.

Madadju Devote, president of the citizen structure, cited Tchibanda village near Ishovu, where some fishermen capture up to seven basins of fry using prohibited nets. He warned that this activity is dangerous for aquatic fauna and leads to food insecurity.

Environmental experts regularly alert communities about the effects of this illicit fishing, especially as the population grows. The Sambaza, small fish popular in Goma and Bukavu, struggle to reproduce in large numbers on the Congolese side due to the destruction of spawning grounds by uncontrolled fishing activities.

Why it mattersResidents of Kalehe and surrounding areas face food insecurity due to the depletion of fish stocks in Lake Kivu.
