DR Congo justice agents protest unpaid bonuses

Over 100 ministry staff blocked roads and burned tires in Kinshasa demanding their permanent bonuses.

Over 100 agents from the Ministry of Justice in Kinshasa protested on Tuesday, November 3, demanding payment of their permanent bonuses. The agents blocked roads and burned tires in front of the Ministry of Public Service, disrupting activities in the area. Police used tear gas to disperse the crowd.

The protesters stated their action began the previous day at the Ministry of Finance. They claim to have only received a portion of their bonuses, covering the first quarter, and are protesting an administrative blockage preventing the release of the remaining funds. One agent declared they were demanding their rights to bonuses that already exist within their budget.

The agents believe the non-payment of these bonuses further deteriorates their living conditions and contradicts the social policy promoted by the head of state. Officials from the Ministries of Finance and Public Service had not yet responded to requests for comment at the time of reporting.

Why it mattersJustice ministry agents in Kinshasa are protesting because they have not received their full bonus payments, impacting their living conditions.

Fire disrupts Kinshasa medical Centre

Emergency services evacuated patients from Centre Médical Diamant after a generator fire on November 4.

A fire disrupted patient care on Wednesday, November 4, at the Centre Médical Diamant in Kinshasa's Ngaliema commune. The blaze, which started around 6 a.m., forced staff to urgently evacuate patients, including those in intensive care.

The medical facility showed signs of damage, with broken windows from the flames. Medical teams worked outside to manage the aftermath, while thick smoke rose into the sky hours after firefighters from the Kinshasa City Hall and MONUSCO controlled the fire. Consultations were temporarily stopped.

Witnesses suggested a generator left running in the scanner room, at the moment the national electricity company (SNEL) restored power, may have caused the fire, though official confirmation is pending. No injuries or deaths were reported due to the rapid evacuation and emergency response.

Why it mattersPatient care was disrupted at the Centre Médical Diamant in Kinshasa due to a fire that required emergency evacuations.

DR Congo women and youth seek greater role in peace processes

A meeting in Kinshasa marked the 26th anniversary of UN Resolution 1325, discussing participation in decision-making.

Women and youth in Kinshasa gathered on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, to mark the 26th anniversary of UN Resolution 1325. The meeting aimed to increase their involvement in decision-making bodies and in building lasting peace.

Participants highlighted challenges in implementing Resolution 1325 and Resolution 2250 across the country. The event, part of the TUFAULU PAMOJA project, also explored leadership, entrepreneurship, and funding opportunities.

Minister of State for Agriculture and Food Security, Muhindo Nzangi Butondo, stressed the importance of agricultural investment to support women and youth. Patrick Kabata, project manager for TUFAULU PAMOJA, stated the initiative encourages entrepreneurship using DR Congo's arable land and fertile potential, despite financial barriers and access to credit.

Why it mattersWomen and youth in the DR Congo are seeking increased participation in peace and development processes, facing financial and structural obstacles.

Five arrested after customary conflict in Maï-Ndombe village

Police transferred five individuals to Mushie after clashes erupted in Mba village over a customary dispute.

Police arrested five people in Mba village, located in the Mushie territory of Maï-Ndombe province, following clashes related to a customary conflict. The arrested individuals were transferred to Mushie town, the administrative center of the territory.

The arrests occurred early in the week, according to reports from Actualite.cd. The customary dispute in Mba village led to confrontations that prompted police intervention.

The situation in the village of Mba remains under observation as authorities process the arrested individuals in Mushie.
