Pinga Health Zone launches polio, measles, rubella vaccination campaign

After two postponements, a vaccination campaign against poliomyelitis, measles, and rubella began today, August 27, 2026, in the Pinga health zone.

The vaccination drive, covering poliomyelitis, measles, and rubella, commenced in the Pinga health zone, located in Walikale territory, North Kivu. This launch follows two previous delays, as reported by Actualite.cd on Thursday, August 27, 2026.

The campaign aims to protect residents in the region from these three diseases. The initiation of the program addresses a public health need that had been pending due to the earlier postponements.

The start of the vaccination effort marks a significant step for public health in the Pinga area, providing access to essential immunizations for the local population.

Why it mattersResidents in the Pinga health zone are directly affected by gaining access to critical vaccinations against polio, measles, and rubella.

DR Congo opens Lonshi border post to boost trade with Zambia

The new Lonshi border post, located 90 kilometers from Sakania in Haut-Katanga, aims to strengthen control of migratory flows and facilitate exchanges between the DRC and Zambia.

Vice-Prime Minister Jacquemain Shabani Lukoo inaugurated the Lonshi border post on Wednesday, August 26, in the Sakania territory of Haut-Katanga. This new infrastructure includes 17 administrative offices, 22 dormitories for agents, and a water borehole, according to Radio Okapi.

The construction of the post followed a request from Sabwe Mining, which sought to simplify the import and export of its mining equipment. The Ministry stated that the facility is expected to improve control operations at the shared border and streamline cross-border commercial exchanges between the two nations.

During the ceremony, the Directorate General of Migration (DGM) urged Sabwe Mining to comply with current laws and regulations in the DRC. The DGM also called on the mining company to enhance collaboration with local communities and politico-administrative authorities, emphasizing adherence to specifications and community development actions.

Why it mattersThe new border post affects businesses by streamlining trade and migration, while also requiring a mining company to comply with regulations and engage with local communities.

Business · 2026-08-27

New program helps Congolese prepare for retirement through agriculture

The Sustainable Agriculture Pre-Retirement and Retirement Program (PROPRAD) launched in Kinshasa to offer professional retraining and economic empowerment through farming.

A new initiative aims to help individuals nearing the end of their professional careers in the DR Congo prepare for retirement or career change through agricultural entrepreneurship. The Sustainable Agriculture Pre-Retirement and Retirement Program (PROPRAD) was launched on August eight in Kinshasa by the Bilanga ya Betu Agricultural Cooperative, in collaboration with the Grouping for Food Self-Sufficiency and Biodiversity (RATALBI).

The program's stated ambition is to use agricultural entrepreneurship as a means to gradually prepare for retirement, promote professional retraining, and foster economic empowerment. It encompasses various agricultural activities such as market gardening, livestock farming, fish farming, and agricultural processing, offering multiple possibilities for participants.

PROPRAD also includes provisions for savings mechanisms, financing, support, and insurance, along with connecting beneficiaries to various partners. The program seeks to address the question of whether agriculture can effectively serve as a means for retirement planning in the DR Congo, according to Radio Okapi.

Why it mattersThis program offers a new pathway for individuals to secure their financial future and transition into new careers, directly affecting their economic stability and professional options.

Ituri Education Director Confirms School Reopening Despite Ebola

Schools in Ituri province will resume classes on September one, 2026, as planned, despite ongoing concerns about the Ebola epidemic.

The 2026-2027 school year will begin on September one in Ituri province, confirmed Yvon Muke Abwasele, director of the Ituri I educational province, on Wednesday, August twenty-six. This decision comes amidst persistent anxieties among parents regarding the health safety of students.

Provincial education authorities have implemented measures to secure school environments, according to Abwasele. School principals have received training and awareness sessions to enforce barrier measures against Ebola. Necessary kits and exercise books for distance learning are also available to address potential emergencies.

Abwasele emphasized that the provincial education system is prepared for the reopening, stating that distance learning would be activated for any school experiencing a resurgence of cases. Parents in Ituri remain divided, balancing financial constraints with fears of their children's exposure to the virus, and are requesting enhanced sanitary provisions in all schools.

Why it mattersParents and students in Ituri province are directly affected by the decision to reopen schools amidst an Ebola epidemic, impacting their daily routines, financial situations, and health safety.

Education · 2026-08-27

Mukwege condemns M23 academic appointments, school closures

Nobel Peace Prize laureate Dr. Denis Mukwege denounced the M23's imposition of academic authorities and Kinshasa's suspension of academic activities in occupied territories.

Dr. Denis Mukwege, a Nobel Peace Prize laureate, condemned the armed movements AFC/M23/RDF for appointing new academic authorities in higher education institutions within occupied zones. He also criticized the Kinshasa regime's decision to suspend academic activities in these areas, as stated in a communiqué on Wednesday, August twenty-sixth.

Mukwege highlighted that the closure of universities, following restrictions on airports, banks, humanitarian aid, and population movement, further tightens the grip on twelve million Congolese citizens in North and South Kivu. He described denying young people the opportunity to learn and train as an unacceptable "red line."

The doctor called on all state and non-state actors involved in resolving the crisis to prioritize the education of Congolese children. He demanded an end to military interference in educational management and a secure resumption of classes across the entire territory, including occupied areas, emphasizing that youth's future should not be a political bargaining chip.

Why it mattersTwelve million Congolese citizens in North and South Kivu are directly affected by the closure of universities and the imposition of new academic authorities, impacting their access to education and future opportunities.
