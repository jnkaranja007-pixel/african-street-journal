Lawmaker announces no-confidence vote against agriculture minister

A national lawmaker plans to introduce a motion of no-confidence against the Minister of State for Agriculture and Food Security over alleged embezzlement of $57 million.

National Assembly member Hermione Bolumbe announced on October 21, 2026, that she will soon file a motion of no-confidence against Muhindo Nzangi, the Minister of State for Agriculture and Food Security. The motion is based on suspicions of embezzlement totaling $57 million.

The announcement comes amid broader discussions in Congolese media about the country's security and political crises. Other headlines on October 22, 2026, included calls for peace from the LUCHA movement directed at former president Joseph Kabila and current president Félix Tshisekedi, as well as statements from Belgium regarding the FRN and threats made by Fils Mukoko.

The specific allegations against Minister Nzangi have not yet been detailed in public reports. The motion, if filed and passed, could lead to the minister's removal from office and potentially trigger a cabinet reshuffle.

Why it mattersThe agriculture minister faces a no-confidence vote, potentially impacting the ministry's operations and leadership.

Nurses' strike leaves 60 patients without care in DR Congo

Around 60 patients at Onema Ototo hospital in Sankuru are not receiving treatment after nurses began a strike over pay and risk bonuses.

Around 60 patients at the Onema Ototo general reference hospital in the Sankuru province are without care following a nurses' strike that began last week. The nurses are demanding administrative and financial support from the government, along with payment of their risk premiums. Patients previously in various wards are now consolidated in one pavilion due to the lack of nursing staff.

Patients who underwent surgery before the strike reported that their wounds are not being dressed and they are not receiving injections. One patient described the situation as precarious, with surgical wounds unmaintained and a lack of essential medical care, urging authorities to intervene. The head of the Ototo health zone, Dr. Mathieu Lotshilenge, confirmed that the zone faces a shortage of medical personnel and administrative and financial difficulties.

Dr. Lotshilenge stated that the health zone has only three doctors, with one on leave. Of the remaining two, only he receives a premium but is not salaried, while the rest of the staff are neither salaried nor receive premiums. The approximately 60 patients remain in precarious conditions at the hospital, awaiting a government response to the healthcare workers' demands to resume medical services.

Why it matters60 patients in the Sankuru province are not receiving necessary medical treatment due to a strike by nurses demanding better pay and conditions.

Kolwezi protests leave two injured and property damaged

Artisanal miners demonstrated for two days in Kolwezi, paralyzing economic activity and causing injuries and material damage.

A gradual calm returned to several neighborhoods in Kolwezi, Lualaba province, on Wednesday, October 21, following two days of violent demonstrations by artisanal miners. Local sources reported that the unrest disrupted socio-economic activities and resulted in several injuries and significant material damage.

The protests, which lasted for two days, brought normal life to a standstill in the affected areas. The scale of the material damage is still being assessed, but initial reports indicate it is substantial. The number of injured has been confirmed by local sources, though specific details about their conditions were not immediately available.

The demonstrations by artisanal miners have had a direct impact on the local economy, halting daily business and trade. The return to calm suggests a de-escalation of the situation, though the full extent of the consequences of the two-day unrest is yet to be determined.

Why it mattersArtisanal miners are affected by the disruption of socio-economic activities and potential damage to their livelihoods during the protests.

Politics · 2026-10-22

DR Congo official banned from media over threats

Fils Mukoko, an official of the ruling UDPS party, faces media ban and judicial inquiry for inciting violence against Western nationals.

Congolese authorities have reacted strongly to controversial statements made by Fils Mukoko, a cadre of the UDPS party. Mukoko allegedly posted threats on social media to arm youth against Western nationals following searches of residences near the presidential family. In response, the Superior Council of Audiovisual and Communication (CSAC) banned Mukoko from all media and social networks for 88 days, citing violations of human life sanctity and respect for diplomatic missions.

Mukoko has also been removed from his position as deputy director general of the Kinshasa Sanitation Authority (RASKIN) by the city's governor. The Ministry of Justice has initiated a judicial investigation, with the Minister of State for Justice instructing the prosecutor general to examine allegations that Mukoko spoke of distributing weapons and threatening Western residents.

The government stated that the gravity of Mukoko's declarations warrants rigorous verification by judicial authorities. The Ministry emphasized that freedom of expression does not justify threats, intimidation, or statements endangering others' lives, regardless of constitutional guarantees.

Why it mattersFils Mukoko's alleged threats and subsequent ban affect Western nationals living in DR Congo and the country's political stability.

Education · 2026-10-22

DR Congo students receive school supplies after floods

Nearly 500 students in Luiza, Kasaï-Central, received stationery to continue their studies after heavy rains destroyed their belongings.

490 students in Luiza, Kasaï-Central province, have received school supplies following torrential rains on September 27. The local community mobilized to provide this assistance, aiming to ensure the children can resume their education despite the damage caused by the severe weather.

The initiative addresses the immediate needs of students whose learning materials were lost or damaged. This support is crucial for maintaining educational continuity in the affected region, where many families are still awaiting aid.

More than a thousand households in Luiza were also impacted by the floods, and they are still in need of further assistance. The distribution of school supplies marks a first step in helping the community recover from the disaster.

Why it mattersStudents in Luiza are directly affected by the floods, which destroyed their school supplies and disrupted their education.
