OIF leader stays on as DR Congo blocks third term

The Francophonie summit in Cambodia ended without a new secretary-general, extending Louise Mushikiwabo's term by 12 months.

The 20th Summit of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) concluded in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, on November 16, 2026, without appointing a new secretary-general. A diplomatic standoff over the succession of Louise Mushikiwabo resulted in a compromise: the Rwandan leader will remain in her position for an additional 12 months, ineligible for a third term. Observers noted this outcome as a partial success for the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and a partial setback for Rwanda, or vice versa.

The Congolese diplomacy opposed Mushikiwabo's reappointment, arguing that she had not sufficiently defended the organization's core values of peace, democracy, and human rights. The compromise is seen as a partial failure for Kigali, which temporarily retains leadership of the OIF. Four candidates had vied for the position: Juliana Amato Lumumba from the DRC, Coumba Ba from Mauritania, Dacian Cioloș from Romania, and the incumbent Louise Mushikiwabo seeking a third term.

The agreed-upon terms stipulate Mushikiwabo's continued tenure for 12 months, her ineligibility for future terms, and Rwanda's exclusion from presenting a candidate in the next selection process. While Kinshasa achieved its immediate goal of preventing a third term for the former Rwandan foreign minister, it did not secure Mushikiwabo's immediate departure or the appointment of its preferred successor. The OIF summit's outcome highlights the power dynamics among member states.

Why it mattersThe Democratic Republic of Congo's diplomatic efforts at the OIF summit have secured a 12-month extension for the current secretary-general, preventing her immediate departure and blocking her bid for a third term.

MSF reduces staff in Bunyakiri amid clashes

Médecins Sans Frontières has temporarily reduced its personnel in Bunyakiri health zone, South Kivu, due to armed confrontations.

Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) has temporarily reduced its staff presence in the Bunyakiri health zone in South Kivu. The decision follows armed clashes between the Congolese army and the FRN. This reduction affects the operations of the medical humanitarian organization in the region.

The clashes between the army and the FRN have created an unstable security situation in Bunyakiri. MSF's action is a precautionary measure to ensure the safety of its personnel. The organization has not provided specific numbers on how many staff members have been withdrawn or for how long.

The situation in eastern DR Congo remains volatile, with ongoing security concerns impacting humanitarian operations. Bunyakiri is one of the areas affected by the persistent instability in the Kivu provinces.

Congo columnist warns loose talk is undermining national development

Writing in Congo Independant, the columnist argues that the flood of unchecked commentary is pulling energy away from solving concrete problems.

In a column published by Congo Independant on Sunday, the author says the Democratic Republic of Congo has become a society where talk takes up a disproportionate share of daily life. He describes people gathering in improvised spaces to comment on politics, celebrities and personal rivalries, and says some offices lose working hours to lengthy conversations.

The columnist says social media has amplified the trend, letting anyone present themselves as a political commentator, diplomatic expert or military analyst without prior study. He stresses that freedom of expression is essential to democracy, but argues it does not remove the duty to inform a debate before joining it, especially on subjects that require specialist knowledge.

The column ties the problem to what it calls political distraction as an obstacle to development. It argues that societies advance through the quality of their knowledge, the soundness of their decisions and the effectiveness of what they deliver, not through the volume of words they produce.

Why it mattersOffice hours and public energy absorbed by commentary are, in the columnist's account, time no longer spent on research, production or problem resolution.

Kinshasa road crews start concrete and drainage work on Lumumba Boulevard

The Office of Roads and Drainage (OVD) began rehabilitation work on Thursday, November 12, in the communes of Limete and Kalamu.

Work to rehabilitate a section of Lumumba Boulevard and the Saint-Christophe and des Maçons avenues started on Thursday, November 12, in the Limete and Kalamu communes of Kinshasa.

The Office of Roads and Drainage (OVD), the state agency that builds and maintains the capital's roads and drains, is carrying out the concrete and drainage work. The project is scheduled to last 45 days.

Residents and drivers on these routes should expect the works to continue until the 45-day schedule is complete.

Why it mattersDrivers and residents on Lumumba Boulevard and the two avenues will face the works for the next 45 days.
