JICA delivers infection control supplies to DR Congo's Ebola response

The Japanese agency provided infection prevention and control materials to the Division of Surveillance in Kinshasa to bolster efforts against the ongoing Ebola outbreak.

The Japanese International Cooperation Agency (JICA) officially handed over infection prevention and control (IPC) supplies to the Division of Surveillance (DSE) in Kinshasa on Thursday, August 27, 2026. This delivery aims to strengthen the ongoing response to the Ebola epidemic in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

The handover occurred as the UN Secretary-General, Antonio Guterres, called for increased international mobilization against the virus. The epidemic has seen over five thousand seven hundred cases and two thousand seven hundred deaths since its official declaration three years ago.

With available resources potentially depleting within weeks, the international community's enhanced engagement is crucial. The provided IPC materials are intended to support frontline health workers and facilities in their critical efforts to contain the spread of the virus.

Why it mattersThe delivery of infection control supplies directly supports health workers and facilities combating the Ebola epidemic, aiming to prevent further spread.

DR Congo Opens Lonshi Border Post to Boost Trade with Zambia

The new facility in Haut-Katanga aims to improve migration control and streamline commercial exchanges, following a request from a mining company.

The Lonshi border post in the Sakania territory of Haut-Katanga has been inaugurated, marking a significant step in enhancing control over flows between the Democratic Republic of Congo and Zambia. Vice-Prime Minister Jacquemain Shabani Lukoo officially opened the facility on Wednesday, August 26, alongside the interim governor of Haut-Katanga and other senior officials. The new infrastructure is designed to strengthen the management of migratory movements and facilitate trade between the two neighboring countries.

Located approximately 90 kilometers from Sakania, the Lonshi border post is expected to improve control operations along the shared border. Its construction was initiated following a request from the mining company Sabwe Mining, which sought to simplify the import and export processes for its equipment. The facility includes 17 administrative offices, 22 dormitories for border agents, and a water borehole, aiming to create a more efficient operational environment.

During the inauguration ceremony, the Directorate General of Migration (DGM) urged Sabwe Mining to adhere to all DRC laws and regulations. The company was also encouraged to strengthen its collaboration with local communities and authorities, with a specific emphasis on respecting contractual obligations and undertaking development actions for the benefit of the community. This call highlights the DGM's commitment to ensuring compliance and fostering positive community relations alongside trade facilitation.

Why it mattersThe new Lonshi border post will facilitate the import and export of mining equipment for Sabwe Mining, potentially impacting operational costs and efficiency.

DRC Program Links Agriculture to Retirement Planning

A new initiative in Kinshasa aims to make sustainable farming a viable path for professional reconversion and economic autonomy for those nearing retirement.

The Cooperative agricole Bilanga ya Betu, in partnership with RATALBI, launched the Sustainable Agriculture Retirement and Pre-Retirement Program (PROPRAD) on August 8 in Kinshasa. This initiative seeks to transform agricultural entrepreneurship into a method for individuals to gradually prepare for retirement, facilitate career changes, and achieve economic independence.

PROPRAD envisions multiple agricultural avenues, including market gardening, livestock, fish farming, and agricultural processing. The program also incorporates savings and financing mechanisms, support services, and insurance options, alongside connecting participants with various partners to ensure comprehensive development.

The program addresses the question of whether agriculture can truly support retirement in the DR Congo. It offers a structured approach to generating income and maintaining autonomy through farming activities, moving beyond simply waiting for pension payments.

Why it mattersThis program offers a pathway for individuals nearing retirement to generate income and maintain autonomy through agricultural entrepreneurship.

Education · 08/27/2026

Mukwege Denounces M23 Academic Appointments and Course Suspensions

Nobel laureate Denis Mukwege condemned armed group appointments and Kinshasa's suspension of university classes, calling it an unacceptable blow to youth aspirations.

Nobel Peace Prize winner Dr. Denis Mukwege has condemned the M23 armed group's appointment of new academic authorities in occupied areas and the Kinshasa regime's suspension of university courses. Mukwege stated that the closure of universities, following restrictions on airports, banks, and humanitarian aid, further isolates 12 million people in North and South Kivu.

He described the disruption of education as an unacceptable violation, stating that crushing the hopes of Congolese youth to learn, train, and serve society is a red line. Mukwege urged all state and non-state actors involved in resolving the crisis to prioritize the education of Congolese children and ensure meaningful youth participation in decisions affecting them.

Mukwege demanded an end to military interference in educational institutions and the secure resumption of classes across the territory, including occupied zones. He emphasized that the future of youth should not be a bargaining chip in political or territorial disputes, nor should it be treated as mere collateral damage.

Why it mattersThe suspension of academic activities and the imposition of new authorities by armed groups directly impacts the education and future prospects of Congolese youth.

News · 08/27/2026

FARDC Drone Downs Coalition Aircraft Near Minembwe

An aircraft allegedly supporting the AFC-M23-RDF coalition was shot down by FARDC forces near Minembwe, South Kivu, on Thursday.

Congolese soldiers shot down an aircraft near Minembwe, South Kivu, on Thursday, according to Fizi territory administrator Samy Badibanga. The plane, reportedly carrying military supplies and food, was allegedly supporting the M23 rebels and their regional allies. Badibanga stated the aircraft was attempting to land at the Minembwe airfield when it was neutralized by a FARDC drone.

The administrator described the incident as another violation of DRC airspace by Rwanda and its allies. He claimed the downed aircraft was en route to Minembwe, a strategic resupply point for the AFC/M23 and Rwanda, where looted minerals from the Mitumba mountains are transported. Badibanga stated that the aircraft was carrying food and munitions and was departing loaded with red wood and gold.

Security sources confirmed the drone strike occurred in an area experiencing ongoing clashes between government forces and rebels. Badibanga commended the FARDC, supported by Wazalendo fighters, for their sacrifices in restoring peace to eastern DRC. Attempts by Radio Okapi to reach FARDC command in the area were unsuccessful.

Why it mattersThe downing of the aircraft disrupts the supply of military equipment and food to rebel forces, potentially impacting ongoing conflict dynamics.
