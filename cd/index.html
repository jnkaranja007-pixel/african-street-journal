DR Congo launches judicial digitalisation project

The justice ministry aims to modernise the system with support from the EU and UNDP.

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Justice Ministry officially began digitalising judicial revenues and interconnecting justice services on Thursday, November 5, 2026. This initiative connects courts, public prosecutor offices, and penitentiary institutions across the country.

The reform, led by the Justice Ministry, receives technical assistance from the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) and financial backing from the European Union (EU). Officials stated the goal is to modernise the judicial system and improve citizen services.

This digitalisation process is intended to enhance transparency and streamline services for citizens interacting with the justice system. The project was launched in Kinshasa.

Why it mattersCitizens will experience improved and more transparent services when dealing with the judicial system.

Daniel Bumba Lubaki met with technical study firms to modernize the city's urban transport system.

DR Congo rangers attack Ugandan goat herders

Tensions rise between Sarambwe reserve and Bwindi park communities after April goat seizure.

Tensions persist between communities bordering the Sarambwe Nature Reserve in DR Congo's Rutshuru territory and those near Uganda's Bwindi Impenetrable National Park. Local activist Muhindo Kibunga Elias reported on Thursday, November 5, that Ugandan eco-guards violently assaulted several Sarambwe community trackers on Monday.

The incident follows a dispute that began in April when Sarambwe trackers seized about 30 goats believed to belong to Ugandan herders inside the protected area. The goats were taken to a reserve surveillance post, and their owners are still seeking information on their fate from Sarambwe reserve managers.

Elias stated that the lack of clear explanations from reserve managers fuels the tensions, warning that the situation could escalate. He called for swift intervention from nature conservation authorities on both sides, urging the ICCN and Uganda Wildlife Authority to find a joint solution and involving the Greater Virunga Transboundary Collaboration.

Why it mattersCommunities in DR Congo and Uganda bordering nature reserves face escalating tensions due to a dispute over seized goats and alleged assaults.

Kinshasa academy seeks dialogue on art restoration

The Academy of Fine Arts in Kinshasa hosted a scientific conference on art conservation and restoration.

The Academy of Fine Arts in Kinshasa organized a scientific conference on Wednesday, November 4, focusing on the conservation and restoration of artworks. The event aimed to foster dialogue between indigenous knowledge and contemporary practices in art preservation.

Art professionals and academics gathered to discuss the complementarity of traditional methods with modern techniques for restoring artworks. The conference explored how to integrate these diverse approaches to safeguard cultural heritage.

This initiative seeks to ensure valuable artworks are maintained using a blend of historical wisdom and current scientific approaches. The discussions focused on practical applications for preserving artistic works for future generations.

Why it mattersThe Academy of Fine Arts in Kinshasa is working to preserve artworks by blending traditional and contemporary restoration methods.
