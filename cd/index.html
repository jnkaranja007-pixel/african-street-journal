DR Congo women mark 26 years of UN Resolution 1325

More than 200 women from various groups gathered in Kinshasa to celebrate the anniversary of the UN resolution on women, peace, and security.

Over 200 women from structures including Tufaulu Pamoja, Rien Sans les Femmes, and Justice et Paix au Congo (JPC) gathered in Kinshasa on Tuesday to celebrate the 26th anniversary of the UN Security Council's Resolution 1325.

Isabelle Pendeza stated that peace cannot be negotiated without women, highlighting that the resolution's application remains a challenge in the Democratic Republic of Congo 26 years after its adoption.

The anniversary event focused on the UN resolution concerning women, peace, and security.

Why it mattersWomen in the Democratic Republic of Congo are affected by the ongoing challenge of applying UN Resolution 1325 on women, peace, and security.

DR Congo assembly suspends prepaid meter installation

Bandundu's provincial assembly halted the rollout of new electricity meters due to tensions.

The provincial assembly in Bandundu, DR Congo, has suspended the installation of prepaid electricity meters. This action follows rising tensions surrounding the new payment system in the city.

The decision to halt the installation was made by the provincial assembly, according to reports from Actualite.cd. The specific reasons for the tensions and the exact number of meters affected were not detailed in the provided information.

Separately, the national assembly adopted a reform of the country's 50-year-old road traffic code on November 2. Initiated by deputy Tobie Kayumbi, the reform aims to improve road safety and increase penalties for violations, and has been sent to the Senate.

Why it mattersResidents of Bandundu are directly affected by the suspension of new electricity meter installations.

DR Congo justice agents protest unpaid bonuses

Over 100 agents blocked roads and burned tires in Kinshasa demanding their permanent bonuses.

More than 100 agents from the Ministry of Justice protested Tuesday, November 3, in Kinshasa, blocking roads and burning tires. They demanded payment of their permanent bonuses, which they consider additional payments they are owed.

The protest, which began outside the Ministry of Finance on Monday, disrupted activities near the Ministry of Public Service. Agents reported receiving only part of their bonuses for the first quarter and cited administrative blockages preventing the rest of the funds from being disbursed. Police used tear gas to disperse the demonstrators.

One agent stated they were demanding their rights and existing bonuses, not new money. The protesters urged authorities to intervene and find a solution, arguing that non-payment worsens their living conditions and contradicts the head of state's social policy. Officials from the Finance and Public Service ministries had not commented by the time of reporting.

Why it mattersJustice ministry agents in Kinshasa are protesting because they have not received their full bonus payments, impacting their living conditions.

DR Congo conservationist wins award for gorilla protection

Jackson Kabuyaya Mbeke received the 2026 Carole Noon Award for his work with Grauer's gorillas in North Kivu.

Jackson Kabuyaya Mbeke, director of the Gorilla Rehabilitation and Conservation Education Center (GRACE), received the 2026 Carole Noon Award from the World Federation of Animal Sanctuaries (GFAS). The award was presented on October 30th, recognizing his dedication to protecting and rehabilitating Grauer's gorillas in Lubero territory, North Kivu.

This honor highlights conservation efforts for the Grauer's gorilla, an Eastern Democratic Republic of Congo endemic species and one of the world's most endangered primates. Kabuyaya Mbeke leads GRACE, caring for gorillas affected by poaching and other wildlife threats.

Kabuyaya Mbeke credited his teams and local communities for their support, stating the award could help secure necessary funding for GRACE's projects. The GRACE center, located in the Batangi chiefdom, has been working for nearly 20 years to protect these gorillas.

Why it mattersThe award could help GRACE secure funding for its projects protecting endangered Grauer's gorillas.
