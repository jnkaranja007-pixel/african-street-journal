Food insecurity hits 28.8 million people in DR Congo

The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification report puts 3.6 million people in emergency conditions, with conflict and Ebola among the causes.

About 28.8 million people in the Democratic Republic of Congo, roughly 23% of the population, are in acute food insecurity, according to the 25th analysis of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) in the country. The report was presented on Thursday, 12 November, in Kinshasa.

The findings attribute the crisis to armed conflict and the Ebola epidemic. Of those affected, 3.6 million are in emergency situations, the most severe level of the IPC scale.

The analysis covers the food situation from September to December 2026 and projects trends for the first half of 2027.

Why it mattersHouseholds facing emergency-level hunger in conflict zones and Ebola-hit areas will depend on food aid through the coming months, the report's projection period.

Ebola victims' bodies sprayed with fuel in DR Congo

False information about fuel disinfecting bodies is causing a new surge in Ebola cases in the Mangala health zone.

In the Mangala health zone of Ituri province, people are spraying the bodies of Ebola victims with gasoline, believing it kills the virus. This dangerous practice, fueled by false community information, is leading to a resurgence of the epidemic.

Dr. Jacques Savo, the Mangala health zone chief, expressed concern on Wednesday, November 11, that families are resuming risky funeral rituals. The health area of Mandje has seen 24 suspected cases in one day and about 25 confirmed cases in a week.

Liliane Malossi, coordinator for the NGO PRODAWI, highlighted logistical challenges including poor roads and lack of transport for response teams. Health workers are calling for a general mobilization to increase awareness and combat misinformation.

Why it mattersThe resurgence of Ebola in the Mangala health zone, with 24 suspected cases in one day, directly affects the health of communities in Ituri province.

DR Congo rights group demands fair trial in $9 million case

The Congolese Association for Access to Justice (ACAJ) is calling for a thorough investigation into alleged embezzlement of over $9 million in Kongo-Central province.

The Congolese Association for Access to Justice (ACAJ), a human rights group, is calling for a thorough investigation into alleged embezzlement of more than $9 million in Kongo-Central province. The call follows a provisional decision by the Court of Auditors implicating several provincial officials.

In a statement published on Wednesday, the ACAJ said the case will test the mechanisms for controlling public finances and fighting corruption. According to the Court of Auditors' provisional findings, the governor, the provincial finance minister and the provincial delegated authorising officer are subject to the ongoing investigations.

The ACAJ said the process must respect the independence of the judiciary and the rights of the defence, while establishing the truth. It also called for recovery of public funds if the competent bodies establish that they were misused.

Why it mattersOfficials named in the Court of Auditors' findings, including the governor, face an investigation into more than $9 million in alleged misused public funds.

Business · 2026-11-14

Hamisi Ngalula Kakese shares lessons from running iPhone Congo in Lubumbashi

Many young graduates in DR Congo struggle to find work, and some are turning to their own businesses instead.

In Lubumbashi, in Haut-Katanga province, Hamisi Ngalula Kakese runs iPhone Congo, a smartphone business, after choosing entrepreneurship over waiting for a job. Radio Okapi reports that he is now sharing the obstacles he faced and the lessons he took from building the company.

Each year thousands of graduates enter the job market in DR Congo, but many struggle to find a first job because of a lack of opportunities, employer demands and no work experience. The report says that a lack of funding, insufficient preparation and little knowledge of the market are among the main difficulties facing young entrepreneurs.

The report argues that support for young people, including project preparation, market knowledge, financial management and perseverance, can help a business last. It concludes that the wider challenge is to help young people keep their ventures going, not only to encourage them to start.

Why it mattersYoung Congolese graduates who want to start businesses face shortages of funding and market knowledge, and the report calls for support to help their ventures last.
