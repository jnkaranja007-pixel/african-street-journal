DR Congo Football League Kicks Off With Eight Matches

The 2026-2027 Linafoot-Ligue 1 season commenced on Tuesday with eight games across two groups.

The national football championship, Ligue 1, for the 2026-2027 season began on Tuesday, October 20, in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Eight matches were scheduled for the opening day across Group A and Group B. The competition will continue on Wednesday, October 21, with additional games, and three other matches have been rescheduled.

In Group A, FC Lupopo hosted AS New Soger at the Kibasa Maliba stadium in Lubumbashi, while CS Don Bosco faced AS Malole at the TP Mazembe stadium in the same city. In Kolwezi, AS Simba played AS Saint-Luc, followed by CS Manika against Sanga Balende. FC Tanganyika met Blessing FC in Kalemie.

Group B saw AS Dauphin Noir of Goma play AC Rangers of Kinshasa in Kindu. Kinshasa hosted two matches at the Père-Raphaël stadium: Céleste FC versus FC MK, and Aigles du Congo against Étoile du Congo. The match between AS Maniema Union and Novakin FC is yet to be rescheduled, and FC Étoile du Kivu has a bye for this first round.

Electricity cut paralyzes water utility, hinders cholera response in Kalemie

Kalemie faces a dual crisis as cholera resurges and water distribution falters due to a prolonged electricity outage.

The city of Kalemie, capital of Tanganyika province, is grappling with a resurgence of cholera, compounded by disruptions to its drinking water supply. The water utility, Regideso, has been paralyzed by a significant electricity cut, severely impacting its ability to distribute potable water to residents.

This power failure has created a critical situation where the very infrastructure needed to combat the cholera outbreak is compromised. The lack of reliable electricity directly affects Regideso's operations, making it difficult to pump and deliver clean water, which is essential for hygiene and preventing the spread of waterborne diseases.

The dual challenge of a cholera outbreak and a crippled water system presents a serious public health concern for Kalemie. The inability of Regideso to function effectively means that efforts to control the cholera epidemic are significantly hampered by the lack of a fundamental service.

Why it mattersResidents of Kalemie are at risk of contracting cholera due to unreliable access to clean drinking water caused by an electricity outage affecting the water utility.

DR Congo Minister Urges Great Lakes States to Act on Peace Commitments

DR Congo's Minister for Regional Integration called on member states of the International Conference on the Great Lakes Region to turn their pledges into concrete actions.

Floribert Anzuluni Isiloketshi, the Democratic Republic of Congo's Minister of Regional Integration, has urged member states of the International Conference on the Great Lakes Region (ICGLR) to translate their commitments into tangible actions for peace.

The minister's call emphasizes the need for practical steps to address the ongoing challenges within the Great Lakes region, highlighting a gap between stated intentions and on-the-ground implementation.

This appeal comes as the region continues to grapple with various security and stability issues, underscoring the urgency for collective and decisive measures from all ICGLR member states.

Why it mattersThe Minister of Regional Integration of the DR Congo is urging ICGLR member states to take concrete actions for peace, affecting regional stability.

Census Takers Receive Welcoming Reception in Kinshasa's N'Djili

Population and local authorities in N'Djili report a generally favorable climate for the ongoing census mapping, despite minor security issues.

Census enumerators in Kinshasa's N'Djili commune are being met with a largely positive reception as they conduct mapping operations for the upcoming 2027 general census. Local officials and residents have expressed satisfaction with the deployment of teams, although some security challenges persist in specific neighborhoods. The mapping, which is expected to last one month, aims to gather essential data for the national census, the first in 42 years.

In areas like Équateur, local authorities highlight the significant interest shown by residents in the operation. The presence of familiar local agents alongside the enumerators is credited with fostering trust and encouraging cooperation. These local guides help explain the purpose of the census and address any concerns from the population, ensuring a smoother process.

Supervisors overseeing operations in several N'Djili districts confirm the generally positive reception, noting that communication efforts are proving effective. Teams are clarifying that the mapping is free of charge and distinct from administrative censuses that may involve fees. Community outreach, including the use of public announcers and neighborhood experts, continues to inform residents outside of the enumerators' working hours.

Why it mattersThe census mapping affects residents by clarifying the purpose and cost of the operation, ensuring they understand it is free.
