Butembo civil society warns Ebola strike by care workers could hurt response

Health workers in Butembo and Katwa are striking for two months of unpaid wages, a revised pay scale and better equipment.

Civil society in Butembo, a city in North Kivu province, has raised alarm over a strike by health care providers involved in the response to Ebola. The stoppage affects treatment and transit centres in the Butembo and Katwa health zones.

Mathe Saanane, president of Butembo's civil society, said patients admitted to health facilities could suffer if care stops. "Some could even see their condition deteriorate for lack of adequate follow-up," he said.

Saanane said the epidemic is not yet fully under control, with some contacts still untraceable, and he urged the parties to reach a compromise quickly.

Why it mattersPatients in the Butembo and Katwa treatment and transit centres are left without care for as long as the strike continues.

MP Gratien Iracan warns DR Congo could lose control of Grand Inga project

The National Assembly declared admissible on Wednesday, 11 November, a draft law that will govern how the Grand Inga hydropower project is carried out.

Gratien de Saint-Nicolas Iracan, a member of parliament, has raised reservations about several clauses in the draft law on implementing the Grand Inga project, warning that the Congolese state risks losing control over it. The National Assembly declared the bill admissible on Wednesday, 11 November.

Radio Okapi reported that the bill is meant to frame how the project is put into effect, and that its admissibility marks a new legislative step for the scheme. Iracan's concerns centre on the risk that state oversight of the project could slip away, according to Actualite.cd.

The bill has now cleared the admissibility stage in the National Assembly. The sources available do not detail the specific clauses Iracan objects to, nor the timetable for the bill's next steps.

Why it mattersIf the clauses Iracan objects to stand, Congolese state oversight of the Grand Inga project and its future revenues would be weakened.

Politics · 2026-11-15

France welcomes UN sanctions listing of Kabila's FRN movement

Paris reaffirms its support for DR Congo's sovereignty and territorial integrity in the east.

France has welcomed the United Nations' decision to add the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation (FRN) to its sanctions list. The FRN is a politico-military movement led by former president Joseph Kabila Kabange, and the UN measures also cover the entire leadership of the AFC/M23.

France said the designation reflects its commitment to the sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Democratic Republic of Congo. The UN sanctions now apply to the FRN as a group and to its leaders, who were named in the listing.

Separately, the UNICEF associate director for public health emergencies, Dr Douglas Noble, visited Bunia this week and praised progress against the seventeenth Ebola outbreak in the country. He pledged the agency's continued support for the response in the city.

Why it mattersIndividuals and entities on the list face UN sanctions, which can restrict their dealings and movements in the region.

Politics · 2026-11-15

DR Congo minister seeks genocide recognition in London

Human rights minister Samuel Mbemba urged UK institutions to sanction Paul Kagame and recognize Congolese genocides.

DR Congo's Human Rights Minister Samuel Mbemba urged the United Kingdom on Thursday, November 13, to take strong measures against Rwanda and recognize Congolese genocides. Speaking in London, Mbemba called on human rights defenders and civil society activists to follow the example of Edmond Morel, who exposed Belgian King Leopold II's atrocities in DR Congo.

Mbemba stated that the situation in eastern DR Congo, where he estimates over 10 million people have died, mirrors the historical injustices Morel denounced. He asked UK institutions to impose sanctions on Paul Kagame and pass resolutions acknowledging the genocides. "We are therefore here in England as the cradle of modern human rights," Mbemba said, adding that "women and children are terrorized by forces supported by the Government of Rwanda" in eastern Congo.

The DR Congo government, through the CIA-VAR and FONAREV under the Ministry of Human Rights, has established a scientific and technical team of professors and researchers. This team aims to achieve official recognition of the Congolese genocides and secure Rwanda's condemnation.

Why it mattersHuman Rights Minister Samuel Mbemba is seeking UK sanctions against Paul Kagame and recognition of Congolese genocides, impacting diplomatic relations and potential future actions.

Culture · 2026-11-15

JB Mpiana returns to Paris's Accor Arena for 40-year career concert

Congolese music star Jean Bedel Mpiana Tshituka was scheduled to perform at the Accor Arena in Paris on Saturday, 14 November, marking 40 years in music.

Jean Bedel Mpiana Tshituka, leader of the band Wenge BCBG and known to fans as "Papa Chéri", was due to perform at the Accor Arena in Paris on Saturday, 14 November. The venue, formerly known as Bercy, was the site of his last show there 25 years earlier.

In an interview with Radio Okapi from Paris, Mpiana said he was happy to return to the stage and thanked his fans for their support. He said the preparations had gone well, crediting several years of hard work. He was to share the stage with former collaborators, and singer Deborah Ntoya was to pay tribute to his idol and mentor, the late Papa Wemba.

Mpiana urged fellow musicians to pursue excellence and love of their work, and called on Congolese people to put love of their homeland first.

Why it mattersCongolese fans in Paris were due to see a concert marking four decades of one of the country's best-known musicians.
