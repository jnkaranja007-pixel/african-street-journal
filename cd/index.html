Fayulu denounces detention of Lamuka spokesperson

Martin Fayulu condemns the arrest of Prince Epenge, demanding respect for fundamental rights.

Martin Fayulu Madidi, president of ECIDé, denounced the detention of Prince Epenge, spokesperson for the Lamuka coalition. Epenge, who is also the second vice-president of Lamuka and president of ADDCongo, was arrested on Sunday at N'djili International Airport.

Fayulu, speaking on Wednesday, October 28, described the situation as "justice with two speeds." He is demanding that Epenge's fundamental rights be respected following his arrest.

Epenge is also a member of the C64 Coalition. His arrest occurred as he was attempting to depart the country.

Why it mattersOpposition politician Martin Fayulu is denouncing the arrest of Prince Epenge, a key figure in the Lamuka coalition.

Kinshasa parents worry over children's sugar intake

Health professionals in Kinshasa warn of dental cavities, overweight, and type 2 diabetes risks from excessive candy consumption.

Excessive sugar consumption among children is causing concern for parents and health professionals in Kinshasa. Candies and sugary drinks, sold for as little as 100 Congolese francs near schools, are easily accessible to young children, making it difficult for some parents to limit their intake.

One mother reported that her 10-year-old son consumes sweets 90% of the time, actively seeking them out. Another mother expressed frustration, stating that even if she forbids sweets, her child will consume them when unsupervised. Dr. Louise Hanyange, deputy director of the diabetes control program, highlighted risks including dental cavities, overweight, obesity, and type 2 diabetes.

Dr. Hanyange recommended that parents reduce their children's daily sugar intake and offer water, fruits, and unsweetened beverages instead. The health concerns were shared on Thursday, October 29.

Why it mattersChildren in Kinshasa are at risk of dental problems, becoming overweight, and developing type 2 diabetes due to easy access to and high consumption of sugary treats.

DR Congo professor proposes special economic zones for minerals

Arthur Kaniki believes these zones will speed up local processing of the country's mineral wealth.

Professor Arthur Kaniki proposed creating special economic zones in the Democratic Republic of Congo to boost local mineral processing. He made this suggestion at the 10th edition of the Alternative Mining Indaba on Thursday, October 29, 2026.

Kaniki stated that these tailored zones are a sustainable solution for industrializing the country. The proposal aims to accelerate the transformation of minerals within the DRC.

This initiative seeks to encourage domestic value addition to the nation's mineral resources.

Why it mattersProfessor Arthur Kaniki's proposal aims to foster industrialization by processing minerals locally, which could affect the DRC's economic development.
