Congolese Influencer Arrested in Tanzania

Tanzanian authorities arrested Denise Dusauchoy on August 30, 2026, following an international arrest warrant issued by Congolese judicial authorities.

Congolese influencer Denise Mukendi Dusauchoy was arrested in Tanzania on August 30, 2026, by Tanzanian authorities. The arrest occurred following an international arrest warrant issued by judicial authorities in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The Ministry of Justice of the DRC confirmed the arrest on September 1, 2026, stating it was part of an international judicial cooperation procedure.

The Ministry of Justice released a statement on its social media channels detailing the event. It specified that Dusauchoy was apprehended by Tanzanian authorities in execution of the warrant. The government of the DRC has acknowledged and commended the cooperation of the Tanzanian authorities in this matter.

While her arrest has been confirmed, the Ministry of Justice also reminded the public that Dusauchoy benefits from the presumption of innocence. Further details regarding the specific charges or the ongoing judicial process were not provided in the initial statements.

Artisanal brickmakers boost Boma's economy

Young people in Boma's KM 3 neighborhood are earning a living and contributing to local development through fired brick production.

The production and sale of fired bricks provide livelihoods for many young people in Boma's KM 3 neighborhood, Kongo-Central province. This area is the main hub for this activity in the city, with numerous artisans who have made it their trade and primary source of income. Beyond the revenue generated, this work creates jobs and offers an alternative to unemployment, with these young individuals actively contributing to the city's economic development through their labor and entrepreneurial spirit.

On production sites, brickmakers work daily to secure their livelihoods. Daniel Nkiama, one of the artisans, highlighted the importance of this work for his survival and that of many others in the neighborhood. "Each year, during the dry season, we produce bricks here. Since there is no employment, instead of stealing, we earn a little money here in our neighborhood," he stated. This activity has also enabled individuals like Célestin Bilonji to fund their education and support their families.

Despite satisfactory results, local entrepreneurs in brick manufacturing aim to expand their operations. They are appealing to authorities for adequate material support to enhance their production capacity. This initiative not only sustains local economies but also offers a path to self-sufficiency and personal advancement for those involved in the trade.

Why it mattersYoung people in Boma are finding employment and earning income through artisanal brick production, contributing to their personal well-being and the local economy.

DR Congo Youth Urged to Combat Health and Security Challenges

Young Congolese are called to move beyond observation and become active participants in addressing public health, security, and citizen engagement issues.

In the Democratic Republic of Congo, youth face significant challenges impacting community life, from public health and security to misinformation and national cohesion. Leaders are urging young people to transition from passive observers to active problem-solvers, leveraging their community ties and mobilization skills to tackle these issues.

In Beni, North Kivu, the fight against Ebola is hampered by rumors and resistance to response teams, with some incidents reportedly involving young people. Youth leaders like Azarias Mokonzi are encouraging their peers to seek credible information and avoid spreading rumors that foster mistrust. Master Fabrice Muluwahali stressed that violence against response teams weakens the epidemic's containment efforts and called on youth to act as prevention relays.

Community sensitization actor Master Omar Kavota highlighted that past Ebola outbreaks offer lessons on the dangers of misinformation. He emphasized the need for youth to verify information before sharing and to prioritize reliable sources to counter the rapid spread of false narratives that can fuel suspicion. This collective call aims to foster responsible engagement among the youth.

Why it mattersYoung people in the DR Congo are affected by health crises and misinformation, which can be exacerbated by their actions or inaction.

DR Congo Agencies Boost Congolese Firm Benefits in Subcontracting

The ARSP and DGDA have strengthened their collaboration to increase advantages for Congolese companies operating in subcontracting sectors.

The Director General of the Authority for the Regulation of Subcontracting in the Private Sector (ARSP), Beleshayi Kasanda Juan Ted, and his counterpart from the General Directorate of Customs and Excise (DGDA), Bernard Kabese Musangu, met on Monday. Their meeting focused on enhancing collaboration between the two state bodies.

This strengthened partnership aims to bolster the benefits and opportunities available to Congolese businesses engaged in subcontracting activities across various sectors. The initiative seeks to ensure that local companies receive preferential treatment and greater advantages within the subcontracting framework.

The collaboration between the ARSP and DGDA is expected to create a more favorable environment for domestic enterprises, promoting their growth and competitiveness within the national economy. This move aligns with efforts to support and empower Congolese businesses in key economic areas.

Why it mattersCongolese companies involved in subcontracting will receive increased benefits and advantages, potentially improving their operational capacity and market position.
