Agriculture · 2026-10-23

DR Congo minister questioned over youth agriculture funds

National Assembly deputy Hermione Bolumbe Bakando seeks details on 29.6 million USD from African Development Bank for youth entrepreneurship.

National Assembly deputy Hermione Bolumbe Bakando has questioned the Minister of Agriculture and Food Security, Muhindo Nzangi, regarding the use of funds disbursed for the Youth Entrepreneurship Project in Agriculture and Agribusiness (PEJAB). She is seeking clarification on financing for young entrepreneurs, created businesses, and the Congolese state's contribution, with the project initially set to end in December 2025.

The inquiry focuses on 29.6 million USD already released by the African Development Bank, according to a June 2025 report cited by the deputy. Bolumbe Bakando also wants to know if the Congolese state's expected one million dollars contribution has been mobilized and how it was used. She is asking for the number of young people trained and financed, and the overall project progress.

The deputy also requested an assessment of the PEJAB's social impact in pilot provinces, including the number of companies created with legal documents from incubation centers. This information aims to evaluate the program's effectiveness in transforming beneficiaries into operational entrepreneurs and creating income-generating activities.

Why it mattersYoung Congolese entrepreneurs and the agricultural sector are affected by the accountability of funds allocated to the PEJAB program.

News · 2026-10-23

Tshikapa airport flights suspended after rain damage

Air traffic halted since last weekend at Tshikapa airport due to runway damage from heavy rains.

Air traffic has been suspended at Tshikapa airport in the Kasaï province since last weekend after heavy rains damaged the runway. The interruption is disrupting economic activities and complicating the transfer of sick patients to cities with specialized care.

The Directorate of Air Navigation (RVA) halted all aircraft movements while repairs are underway, forcing travelers to use alternative transport like roads. The president of the Federation of Businesses of Congo (FEC) in Kasaï stated that road transport incurs extra costs and risks for goods, impacting the local diamond sector.

Health sector concerns are also rising as urgent patient transfers to cities with specialized medical facilities are now more difficult. Local civil society is urging the RVA to speed up runway repairs to resume air traffic in Tshikapa.

Why it mattersFlights are suspended at Tshikapa airport, affecting economic activities and the transfer of sick patients.

News · 2026-10-23

Bandundu residents fear rising crime

Nightly thefts, assaults, and robberies are increasing, prompting calls for more security patrols.

Insecurity is growing in Bandundu, the capital of Kwilu province, with residents reporting more nightly thefts, assaults, and robberies. Criminals are reportedly breaking into homes by piercing walls to steal valuables. Taxi-moto drivers are also targeted, with some being drugged before their motorcycles are stolen.

The latest incident occurred Tuesday night into Wednesday in the Office neighborhood, where unknown individuals entered a business and took merchandise, causing significant losses. Me Désiré Iyemvela Eminga, a provincial assembly reporter and elected official from Bandundu, expressed deep concern and urged security services to increase police patrols and operations to dismantle criminal networks.

Eminga believes a stronger police presence in sensitive areas could prevent banditry and restore public confidence. Residents are awaiting concrete actions from authorities for a more effective security system to ensure public peace and allow daily activities in a safe environment.

Health · 2026-10-23

ebola closes two health centers in lubero

two new cases among quarantined health workers prolong closure of alimbongo facilities.

Two health facilities in Alimbongo, Lubero territory, North-Kivu, remain shut after Ebola cases were confirmed among their staff. The Kitsombiro and Kasingiri health centers have had their agents in quarantine since October 3, with the closure extended after two new Ebola cases were confirmed among these quarantined health workers.

Over 50 health providers had contact with the initial nine Ebola cases in Kitsombiro and Kasingiri. Colonel Kiwewa Mitela Alain, overseeing the territorial response, cited community resistance, lack of logistics and finances, and occupation of part of the area by ex-AFC/M23 as obstacles to Ebola operations.

As of October 22, Alimbongo health zone recorded 19 Ebola cases, including 14 deaths. The arrival of the World Health Organization and International Medical Corps offers hope, with an Ebola treatment center under construction and staff capacities being strengthened.

Why it mattersTwo health centers in Alimbongo, DR Congo, remain closed due to Ebola outbreaks among staff, affecting local healthcare access.
