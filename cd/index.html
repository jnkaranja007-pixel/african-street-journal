Cholera response strained by shortages and strikes in Equateur

The Equateur provincial health division reports 142 cases and 17 deaths amid a lack of medicine and striking staff.

The fight against cholera in Equateur province faces severe challenges due to a shortage of medical supplies and ongoing strikes by healthcare workers. Treatment centers are struggling with limited beds, and the availability of essential medicines and chlorine stocks has been depleted. This situation complicates the care for an increasing number of cholera patients.

The provincial health division highlighted critical shortages of chlorine, sampling kits, and vital medicines in its November 1st epidemiological update. Both the Bolenge and Wangata cholera treatment centers report being overwhelmed by rising patient numbers. Healthcare providers are also on strike, demanding unpaid bonuses that have been due since the epidemic began.

A national healthcare worker strike, active since October 15, further disrupts health services across the province. The Equateur province has recorded 142 cholera cases, resulting in 17 fatalities. Health authorities are appealing to the government and international partners for increased support to secure medical supplies and bolster response operations.

Why it mattersCholera cases and deaths are rising in Equateur province due to a lack of essential medicines, supplies, and striking healthcare workers.

Bill Clinton Foundation warns of Ndolo prison overcrowding

The foundation urges Congolese authorities to take urgent action to alleviate severe overcrowding at the Ndolo military prison.

The Bill Clinton Peace Foundation (BCPF) has raised concerns about detention conditions at the Ndolo military prison in Kinshasa. In a statement released on October 30, the organization called on Congolese authorities to implement immediate measures to reduce the prison's significant overcrowding.

The foundation's statement, reported by Radio Okapi on November 2, specifically targets the severe population density within the military facility. The BCPF is urging for urgent action to address the situation.

The organization's appeal highlights the critical state of the Ndolo prison. The BCPF's call for urgent measures underscores the immediate need for intervention by the Congolese government.

Adfs free nearly 50 hostages in Ituri

Nearly 50 people held for weeks by ADF rebels have regained their freedom in the Malembi forest.

Nearly 50 people freed from ADF rebel captivity have emerged from the Malembi forest in Ituri. The hostages had been held for several weeks before their release. Some rejoined Congolese army positions while others returned directly to their home villages.

The freed individuals were held in the Malembi forest, located approximately 130 kilometers from Mambasa-centre in the Mambasa territory. Their release marks the end of their captivity by the ADF rebels.

The ADF rebels have been active in the Ituri region. The exact number of hostages freed was reported as around 50.
