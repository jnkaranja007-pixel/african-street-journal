The Sentry calls for probe into DRC conflict gold

The Sentry report links DRC gold to Ugandan networks and ADF financing.

The Sentry, an investigative organization, is calling for an investigation into conflict gold circuits connecting the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) to Ugandan networks. The group also urges sanctions against individuals involved in laundering this gold to benefit the Allied Democratic Forces (ADF). The Sentry's findings are detailed in a report titled 'Brutality backed by gold: The Islamic State-affiliated Allied Democratic Forces in the Democratic Republic of Congo'.

The report highlights the ADF's involvement in the DRC's gold trade. The Sentry's analysis aims to uncover the financial flows that support the ADF's operations. The organization seeks to hold accountable those who facilitate the laundering of conflict gold.

Separately, in Ituri province, a military position in Bule-centre, Djugu territory, was attacked on October 29. One FARDC soldier and three Convention for the Popular Revolution (CRP) militia members were killed. Seven other militia members sustained serious injuries, and local socio-economic activities were disrupted.

Why it mattersThe Sentry report calls for sanctions against actors involved in laundering conflict gold that finances the ADF.

Kinshasa parents worry over children's sugar intake

Health professionals warn of dental cavities, overweight, and type 2 diabetes risks from sweets sold near schools.

Excessive sugar consumption among children is causing concern for parents and health workers in Kinshasa. Sweets and sugary drinks, available from 100 Congolese francs near schools and in local shops, are easily accessible to young children, making it difficult for some parents to limit their intake.

A 10-year-old boy consumes sweets 90% of the time, actively seeking them out despite his mother's health advice. Other parents struggle with enforcement, acknowledging that children will consume sweets when unsupervised outside the home. Dr. Louise Hanyange, deputy director of the diabetes control program, highlighted risks including dental cavities, overweight, and obesity, which can lead to type 2 diabetes.

Dr. Hanyange recommends parents reduce children's daily sugar intake and offer water, fruits, and unsweetened beverages instead. The health professionals' warnings were shared on Thursday, October 29.

Why it mattersChildren in Kinshasa are at risk of dental problems, becoming overweight, and developing type 2 diabetes due to easy access to sugary snacks.

Health · 2026-10-30

26 deaths reported in Bunia amid Ebola fears

Community leaders in Bankoko, Bunia, report over a hundred contacts and 26 deaths in three weeks.

Community leaders in Bunia's Bankoko neighborhood reported 26 deaths and over a hundred contacts in nearly three weeks, raising fears of an Ebola resurgence. The figures, as of October 28, have not been confirmed by health authorities, who stated they are implementing preventative measures.

The deaths reportedly began after a woman returned from Djugu territory, where she attended a funeral. She died after being taken to a private clinic, and her body was returned home without precautions before burial, leading to further deaths among those in contact with the deceased.

Bunia's health zone chief, Dr. Prosper Ukurfwa, did not confirm the reported numbers but assured that surveillance, contact identification, patient transfer to treatment centers, and public awareness of barrier measures are underway.

Why it mattersResidents of Bunia's Bankoko neighborhood face a potential Ebola outbreak following 26 reported deaths and over a hundred contacts.

Education · 2026-10-30

DR Congo minister seeks education and energy investment

Raïssa Malu told a conference in Sicily that electricity access is vital for learning conditions in the DR Congo.

DR Congo's Minister of National Education Raïssa Malu called for simultaneous investments in education and energy at the AGORA conference in Sicily, Italy, on October 26-27. She stated that access to electricity is essential for improving learning conditions for the country's nearly 30 million students.

Speaking to decision-makers and experts, Malu emphasized the need to strengthen energy sector investments to support educational efforts. She explained that electricity access facilitates the use of digital tools and strengthens school capacities, linking education development with energy expansion for economic and social progress.

The AGORA conference, held as part of the Mission 300 initiative, gathered international partners including the World Bank and the Italian Ministry of Economy and Finance. The meeting focused on development, energy, and education issues across Africa.

Why it mattersDR Congo's education system, serving nearly 30 million students, needs investment in both education and energy infrastructure.
