DR Congo parliament forms road concession inquiry

National Assembly deputies will investigate financial transparency and legal compliance of road contracts.

Deputies in the DR Congo's National Assembly have established an investigative committee to scrutinize road concessions and toll stations. The committee will examine financial transparency and legal compliance related to these contracts.

The decision to form the inquiry commission was adopted through a project of resolution by the national deputies. This move signals a parliamentary effort to ensure accountability in the management of the country's road infrastructure projects.

The investigation will cover the management, financial transparency, and legal conformity of all road concessions and toll posts across the Democratic Republic of Congo. No specific timeline for the investigation's completion was provided in the announcement.

Kindu nurses' strike halts health services

Health centres close and hospital visits drop as nurses strike in Kindu, Maniema province.

Nurses in Kindu, Maniema province, began a strike that has disrupted healthcare services. Several health centres have closed, and patient numbers at the Kindu General Reference Hospital have fallen significantly. This situation is causing distress for patients and their families, especially with a recent increase in suspected cholera cases in the province.

Activities are nearly stopped at many Kindu health facilities, with some remaining closed and many patients struggling to get basic care. The strike also affects childhood vaccination services, forcing some parents to travel long distances to get their children immunised. One mother reported travelling to the Ngondombou health centre because local centres had no vaccines.

The Kindu General Reference Hospital is seeing a sharp decline in patients, with only about 20 currently receiving care, a much lower number than usual. Medical teams are operating with reduced staff due to the strike.

Why it mattersPatients in Kindu are struggling to access essential healthcare, including vaccinations, due to a nurses' strike.

DR Congo launches digital leadership meeting

Minister Augustin Kibasa Maliba opened the second annual gathering for public sector IT and digital heads in Kinshasa.

The second edition of the days for directors of information systems and digital leaders from public institutions opened in Kinshasa on Monday, November 9. Minister of Digital Economy Augustin Kibasa Maliba officially launched the event.

The meeting brings together IT and digital heads from across public institutions to discuss technology strategies. This event aims to advance digital governance within the country's public sector.

The gathering is scheduled to continue, focusing on improving digital systems for government operations. No further details on specific outcomes or future dates were provided in the initial report.

Kinshasa deploys mixed brigade to ease traffic

A new joint unit will regulate traffic and enforce road rules on main routes, including the airport road.

Kinshasa authorities launched a special operation on Monday, November 9, to improve traffic flow in the capital. A mixed brigade, comprising members of the provincial government, the National Road Safety Commission, and the National Service, under the coordination of the National Police of Congo, will patrol major roads. This team's mission is to manage traffic and ensure strict adherence to the Highway Code.

Particular attention will be given to the expressways leading to N'djili International Airport, where certain vehicle types are now prohibited. Drivers who violate this restriction will face penalties according to current regulations. The operation aims to reduce congestion and enhance urban mobility within Kinshasa.

The mixed brigade's deployment is part of a broader effort by Congolese authorities to tackle the city's persistent traffic challenges. The specific measures, including the airport road restrictions, are intended to create a more efficient transportation system for residents.

Climate · 2026-11-10

Expert urges stronger fight against deforestation in DR Congo

An environmental expert in Muanda calls for increased action against tree felling due to heavy rains.

An environmental expert in the Muanda territory of Kongo-Central, DR Congo, urged intensified action against deforestation on Saturday, November 7. Honoré Bowana Bokili stated that the loss of forest cover worsens erosion and increases people's vulnerability to natural disasters, especially during the current rainy season.

Heavy rains and strong winds have caused damage to homes, schools, health centers, and roads in many areas. Environmental specialists believe that uncontrolled tree cutting reduces the soil's ability to withstand intense rainfall, exacerbating these effects.

Bokili emphasized that public awareness campaigns on environmental education are vital for preserving forests and biodiversity. He also recommended that all tree cutting require official authorization and that reforestation systematically follow any felling.

Why it mattersResidents of Kongo-Central face increased vulnerability to natural disasters like floods and erosion due to deforestation.
