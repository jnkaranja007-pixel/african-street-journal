Ebola and insecurity worsen food crisis in eastern DRC

A UN food agency report shows the combined health and security crises have severely impacted agriculture and livelihoods.

The Ebola epidemic and ongoing insecurity have severely worsened food security and agricultural livelihoods in the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo provinces of Ituri, North Kivu, and South Kivu. A September 2026 study by the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), whose report was reviewed on November 11, concluded that health restrictions, market disruptions, and travel difficulties intensified an already precarious situation.

The DIEM-Impact assessment, based on interviews with 224 informants, found that the Ebola outbreak amplified the effects of years of armed conflict. In August 2026, the Bundibugyo strain spread rapidly, reaching nearly 500 new cases weekly, primarily in Ituri and North Kivu. North Kivu is particularly affected, facing both the epidemic and insecurity, with 1.9 million people needing aid even before the disease appeared. Between January 2024 and March 2026, insecurity reduced access to farmland and increased cattle theft from 15% to 28%.

The FAO recommends strengthening emergency humanitarian aid while supporting upcoming agricultural campaigns. The agency advocates for priority support to farming and livestock activities in vulnerable territories like Irumu and Aru in Ituri, Masisi and Rutshuru in North Kivu, and Kalehe in South Kivu. The cost of living has also risen sharply in Bunia, Bukavu, and Goma due to trade barriers and increased transport costs.

Why it mattersEastern DRC residents face worsening food insecurity and livelihood challenges due to the Ebola epidemic and persistent insecurity.

DR Congo president signs referendum law

The new law sets conditions for a public vote on a potential new constitution.

President Félix Tshisekedi has signed into law a bill that establishes the conditions for organizing a referendum in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The presidential ordinance, dated November 9, 2026, was read on national television on November 11, 2026. This legislation outlines the rules for the referendum's object, procedure, initiative, and convocation.

Roger Kalenga, deputy spokesperson for the head of state, explained that the law defines a referendum as an expression of the people's sovereignty through a direct consultation. Citizens will vote yes or no on a specific text or question. Participation is considered a civic duty for all registered voters aged 18 and above who are in possession of their civil and political rights.

André Mbata, secretary general of the presidential majority, stated that the new referendum law opens the way for adopting a new constitution. He indicated that this process is now possible without being imposed. The promulgation of this law was a major focus in media reports on November 11, 2026.

Climate · 2026-11-12

Kinshasa floods disrupt traffic after heavy rain

Heavy rainfall on Wednesday, November 11, caused widespread flooding in several Kinshasa neighbourhoods.

Heavy rains lashed Kinshasa on Wednesday, November 11, causing significant flooding in multiple city districts. The downpours led to disruptions in road traffic and damaged some homes, according to residents.

Testimonies from the Congolese capital described flooded streets and damaged properties following the intense rainfall. The full extent of the damage and the number of people affected are still being assessed.

The flooding occurred on a Wednesday in November. Further details on the specific neighbourhoods impacted and the official response were not immediately available in the report.

Why it mattersResidents of Kinshasa experienced disruptions to their daily lives and property damage due to flooding caused by heavy rains.

UN adds FRN to DR Congo sanctions list

The UN Security Council's sanctions committee added the Front for the Nation's Refoundation as another name for the Alliance Fleuve Congo.

The UN Security Council's sanctions committee added the Front for the Nation's Refoundation (FRN) as another name for the Alliance Fleuve Congo (AFC) on November 11, 2026.

This addition means the FRN is now subject to the sanctions previously imposed on the AFC. The committee did not provide further details on the FRN's activities or its connection to the AFC.

The sanctions list is maintained by the UN body overseeing measures against entities and individuals deemed a threat to peace and security in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Why it mattersThe FRN, now under UN sanctions, is directly affected by this decision.

Sport · 2026-11-12

DR Congo hosts Sierra Leone in CAN 2027 qualifier on Thursday

The Leopards host the Leone Stars at the Martyrs stadium in Kinshasa, with Sierra Leone still seeking their first points in the group.

DR Congo's senior men's Leopards host Sierra Leone's Leone Stars on Thursday, November 12, at the Martyrs stadium in Kinshasa. The match, kicking off at 17:00 Kinshasa time, is the third matchday of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers.

Sierra Leone's coach, Didier Gomes da Rosa, a Frenchman, said his team is bottom of the group with zero points after two games. He said his side had mounted a poor start and believes it could have taken four points with more concentration.

Da Rosa said the Leone Stars still believe in their chances against the Congolese, whom he described as one of the continent's strongest sides. The Leopards, who have started the campaign well, will look to consolidate their position in front of their home crowd.

Why it mattersSierra Leone must take points from this match to avoid falling further behind in Group qualifying, while the Leopards aim to extend a good start before their home supporters.
