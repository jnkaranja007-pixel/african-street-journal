DR Congo finance inspection plans 2026-2028 strategy

Academics and experts in Kinshasa reviewed a three-year plan for systemic financial control and digitalization.

The Inspection Générale des Finances (IGF) in the Democratic Republic of Congo is modernizing public finance control with its 2026-2028 strategic plan. From October 21 to 22, 2026, academics and scientists met in Kinshasa to discuss the plan's focus on systemic control, risk prevention, and digital procedures. This reform aims to integrate university and scientific communities into the IGF's work.

At the Pullman Grand Hotel, professors, researchers, and experts debated the shift from traditional compliance checks to a systemic, preventive, and digital approach. IGF Inspector General Christophe Bitasimwa Bahii stated the institution will use data analysis and risk mapping to anticipate irregularities. Participants noted challenges like poor digital infrastructure, limited electricity, and funding for research.

Discussions also covered public sector ethics, agent responsibility, and performance evaluation for transparency. Contributions came from academics like Ekolo Adiaka and Claude Sumata Motukula, and officials including Olga Kavira Kinyamusiti. Examples from Estonia, Denmark, Rwanda, and Kenya were cited for their digital governance benefits.

Why it mattersAcademics and public officials in the Democratic Republic of Congo are involved in reforming the nation's financial oversight system.

new boat arrives in Bandundu-Ville

the MB PANU 2 vessel docks at ONATRA port, raising hopes for better transport.

The MB PANU 2 boat arrived on Thursday, October 22, at the ONATRA port in Bandundu-Ville, Kwilu province. Its arrival offers hope for improved transport of people and goods in a region where river navigation is vital for movement and trade.

ONATRA's Bandundu-Ville director, Tsasa Mambu Eloi, stated that the new vessel will facilitate travel and cargo delivery, providing better comfort and safety. He invited travelers to buy tickets at the Bandundu-Ville port for reliable journeys.

New navigation rules, banning wooden boats from the Congo River and its tributaries, will take effect on November 15, 2026. Residents welcomed the boat, anticipating easier travel and support for their businesses reliant on river transport.

Why it mattersThe arrival of the MB PANU 2 boat and upcoming navigation rules will affect residents and businesses in Bandundu-Ville who rely on river transport.

DR Congo's SNEL seeks debt restructuring with six banks

The state-owned electricity company aims to finance four new projects through international financial institutions.

The Société nationale d’électricité (SNEL) is discussing debt restructuring and financing for four electricity projects with six international banks. SNEL's Director General, Teddy Lwamba, met with representatives from JPMorgan, HSBC, Standard Chartered, Absa Group, Dubai Islamic Bank, and EXIM Bank of India on October 22, 2026, in Dubai. These discussions occurred on the sidelines of the Invest Africa forum.

SNEL currently dedicates 60% of its revenue to repaying commercial and financial debts, leaving only 15% for operations, maintenance, and investments. The company also faces challenges collecting payments from public institutions, which owe approximately 23.07 million USD for 2025, with only 3.88% of the expected 24 million USD paid. This situation highlights SNEL's financial fragility due to high debt servicing and poor revenue collection.

Financial analysts suggest that debt restructuring could improve SNEL's cash flow through rescheduled payments. However, they caution that new investments require better collection rates, sustainable pricing, and careful resource management. The outcome of the Dubai discussions will depend on the agreements reached and their ability to increase electricity generation and transport capacity.

Why it mattersThe state-owned electricity company SNEL is seeking to restructure its debt and secure financing for new projects, impacting its financial operations and future investments.

DR Congo assembly passes referendum law

Parliamentary opposition boycotted the vote, calling it a forceful move that could harm national dialogue.

The National Assembly in the Democratic Republic of Congo adopted a law on organizing a referendum on Friday, October 23. The assembly considered reservations from the Constitutional Court before passing the text, which now returns to the Senate for a second review. The bill will then go to the president for promulgation.

The parliamentary opposition boycotted the debate and passage of the law, denouncing what they called a forceful move. Christian Mwando, head of the opposition group Ensemble pour la République, warned that passing the law without opposition participation would shut down dialogue. He stated the majority would bear responsibility for the law, which he claimed would be solely backed by the Union sacrée.

Majority deputies praised the law's adoption as a sign of democratic function and majority rule, asserting the opposition's absence did not halt proceedings. President Félix-Antoine Tshisekedi had previously sent the law back to parliament for reconsideration after the Constitutional Court raised concerns.

Why it mattersThe adoption of the referendum law by the National Assembly affects national dialogue and political stability in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

DR Congo plans 370km road upgrade with Arab Contractors

Prime Minister Judith Suminwa met with Arab Contractors Sub-Sahara to discuss the Ilebo-Kananga route modernization project.

Prime Minister Judith Suminwa met with a delegation from Arab Contractors Sub-Sahara on October 22, 2026, in Kinshasa to discuss the construction and modernization of the 370-kilometer Ilebo-Kananga road. This strategic route aims to improve access to the Kasaï region, facilitating the transport of goods and agricultural products. The project also includes the construction of four bridges.

Hassan Mohamed Hassoun, regional director for Arab Contractors, stated that the company and its financial partners, including Trade and Development Bank (TDB) and United Bank for Africa (UBA), have submitted their offer. The road is intended to boost economic development by improving trade with Kinshasa and supporting agricultural activities. Arab Contractors plans to support local agricultural cooperatives, particularly those led by women.

The company hopes to begin construction in early 2027, pending administrative procedures, which could take two to three months to finalize. This infrastructure development aligns with the government's 2024-2028 action plan priorities for transport, territorial access, and agricultural support.

Why it mattersThe 370km road project between Ilebo and Kananga will improve transport and trade for the Kasaï region.
