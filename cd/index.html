DR Congo, Israel Presidents Agree to Boost Cooperation

Félix Tshisekedi and Isaac Herzog met in Jerusalem to expand partnerships in security, agriculture, technology, and investment.

The presidents of the Democratic Republic of Congo and Israel have committed to strengthening bilateral cooperation across several key sectors. Félix Tshisekedi and Isaac Herzog met in Jerusalem on Tuesday, September 1, to discuss expanding partnerships in security, agriculture, food security, water, health, technology, training, and investment.

This meeting, part of President Tshisekedi's working visit to Israel, follows discussions held during President Herzog's visit to Kinshasa in November 2025. Both leaders affirmed their desire to develop a "mutually beneficial" partnership, aiming to translate agreements into concrete results for their respective populations.

The leaders identified agriculture, food security, water management, health services, technological innovation, security initiatives, and vocational training as priority areas for enhanced collaboration. President Tshisekedi emphasized the ambition to build a modern and pragmatic partnership with Israel, measured by the implementation and outcomes of joint projects.

Why it mattersThe presidents of DR Congo and Israel agreed to strengthen cooperation in agriculture, technology, and investment, potentially impacting farmers and investors.

Five Ebola Patients Recover and Leave Beni Treatment Centers

The largest group of recovered Ebola patients, including a caregiver, have been discharged from treatment centers in Beni, North Kivu.

Five patients, two children, two women, and a caregiver, have been released from Ebola treatment centers in Beni, North Kivu, after recovering from the virus. This marks the largest single group of recoveries discharged simultaneously from the two treatment facilities in Beni since the 17th Ebola epidemic began in May. The recoveries bring the total number of discharged patients from Beni's treatment centers to eleven since the outbreak started.

Dr. Jérémie Muhindo, head of the Ebola response commission in Beni, stated that these recoveries should reassure the public, emphasizing that prompt medical care leads to successful treatment. Survivors expressed immense joy and encouraged the population to trust healthcare workers and seek immediate consultation if symptoms appear. One survivor shared, "I came very weak, but now I am truly in shape. Thank you to the healthcare staff and may God bless them!"

Another recovered patient stated, "I am happy. I came in a sick state, today I am cured. The big message is that the population must become aware that the disease exists and that they should come for treatment." The discharged patients are now in good health and have left the treatment centers, offering hope and encouraging community engagement with healthcare services.

Why it mattersFive Ebola patients have recovered and been discharged, indicating successful treatment and potentially reducing fear of the disease among the population.

DR Congo Census Uses Digital Tools and Satellite Imagery

The technical coordinator of the Central Census Bureau announced that the operation relies on digital tools and satellite imagery to map the population.

The Democratic Republic of Congo is undertaking a nationwide mapping operation to better understand its population and plan for development. Henri Marie Kazadi, technical coordinator for the Central Census Bureau, stated that the census is essential for national progress and that development cannot wait. This initiative employs digital tools and satellite imagery, marking a significant technological shift for the country's demographic data collection.

Kazadi emphasized the need for broad public participation, calling it a guarantee for the success of the census operations. This census is the first to be conducted in 42 years, following the one organized in 1984 during the era of Zaire. The current operation aims to establish a reliable demographic database for the Congolese population.

Participants in a Radio Okapi broadcast, hosted by Jean Marc Matwaki Mofelele, stressed the importance of transparency and the reliability of the results. They also highlighted the necessity of large-scale public awareness campaigns to ensure the accuracy and completeness of the demographic data being collected.

Why it mattersThe census operation affects all Congolese citizens by aiming to create a reliable demographic database for future development planning.

Congolese Influencer Arrested in Tanzania on International Warrant

Tanzanian authorities apprehended Denise Dusauchoy on August 30, 2026, following a request from the Democratic Republic of Congo's justice ministry.

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Ministry of Justice confirmed the arrest of Congolese influencer Denise Dusauchoy, also known as Denise Mukendi, in Tanzania on August 30, 2026. The arrest occurred as part of an international judicial cooperation procedure. Tanzanian authorities detained Dusauchoy based on an arrest warrant issued by Congolese judicial authorities.

This operation follows judicial actions initiated in criminal cases under investigation since 2025. The Ministry of Justice highlighted the "diligent collaboration" of Tanzanian authorities. The ministry also stated that the arrest is a procedural step and does not prejudge the individual's guilt, emphasizing her right to the presumption of innocence until a final judicial decision.

The proceedings leading to the arrest began in 2025 when the Prosecutor General near the Court of Cassation sent three international rogatory letters to Belgian judicial authorities. These requests pertained to criminal cases opened at the Kinshasa/Gombe High Court of Justice, involving Dusauchoy and others, for which international arrest warrants had been issued.

Why it mattersThe arrest of Denise Dusauchoy affects individuals involved in ongoing criminal investigations and highlights international judicial cooperation between the DRC and Tanzania.
