US sends DR Congo list of agencies receiving over $400 million for Ebola

Washington has handed Kinshasa the names of the agencies that will receive its Ebola response funding, estimated at more than $400 million.

The United States has passed to the Congolese government a list of the agencies that will receive its contributions to the Ebola response in the Democratic Republic of Congo, which are estimated at more than $400 million.

The move follows warnings from Dr Jean Kaseya, director general of Africa CDC, the African Union's continental disease control agency, about how money meant for the Ebola response in the DRC was being managed.

The list is the first step in tracking where the money goes once it reaches the agencies responsible for the response.

Why it mattersAgencies named on the list will control how more than $400 million in U.S. money is spent on the Ebola response in the DRC.

DR Congo minister lobbies London for recognition of Congolese genocides

Human Rights Minister Samuel Mbemba is asking UK institutions and rights defenders to take firm measures against Rwanda.

Samuel Mbemba, DR Congo's Minister of Human Rights, is pressing for official recognition of the Congolese genocides, saying the fight is far from over while Kinshasa has not yet won its demand. He made the case on Thursday, 13 November, in London.

In London, Mbemba is meeting British institutions and human rights defenders to mobilise them behind strong measures against Rwanda. He is asking them to act rather than only to acknowledge the past.

The report does not describe any further meetings or decisions, so the outcome of his lobbying remains unreported.

Lutundula threatens DR Congo suspension from OIF if Mushikiwabo is re-elected

Former foreign minister Christophe Lutundula says Kinshasa could suspend its participation in the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) if Kigali keeps Louise.

Christophe Lutundula, a former vice-prime minister and foreign minister of the Democratic Republic of Congo, said on Saturday, 14 November, that Congo could suspend its participation in the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) if Louise Mushikiwabo is re-elected to lead it. He spoke during a live discussion organised by the journalist Stanis Bujakera Tshiamala.

Lutundula hardened his tone after Rwanda refused to withdraw Mushikiwabo's candidacy. Her candidacy is the central issue of his remarks, and he presented the threat as a response to Kigali's refusal rather than as a settled decision.

The same week, the OIF and the Alliance des Patronats Francophones released a business barometer in Phnom Penh, on 14 November, covering 53 member states. It found that productive investment in the French-speaking space rose to 301 billion dollars in 2025, above the 289 billion dollars received by Commonwealth countries, while public services to businesses lagged the global median.

Why it mattersCongo's participation in the OIF, an organisation that counts 53 member states, is at stake, and the outcome of the leadership vote will decide whether Kinshasa stays in it.

DR Congo provinces reject separatism

Representatives from Tshopo, Bas-Uele, Haut-Uele, and Ituri reaffirmed national unity at a meeting in Kisangani.

Representatives from four provinces in the Democratic Republic of Congo's Grande-Orientale region have rejected any plans to divide the country. The leaders from Tshopo, Bas-Uele, Haut-Uele, and Ituri met in Kisangani on Sunday, November 15, to reaffirm their commitment to national unity and territorial integrity.

Organized by the Dynamique Grande-Orientale, the gathering also saw participants pledge to support integrated development projects for the region. Professor Jean-Paul Nemoyato stated that a community project financing mechanism could help combat poverty and underdevelopment in the four provinces.

National deputy Geneviève Inagosi emphasized the importance of increasing women's involvement in the region's development efforts. The representatives also called for the establishment of a funding mechanism to support community projects facing economic and structural challenges.

Why it mattersLeaders from four provinces met to reject separatism and advocate for integrated development projects.

Politics · 2026-11-16

Congo Independant columnist warns of verbal excess in DR Congo

In an opinion column, the writer argues that a flood of public commentary is outpacing analysis and results, and links it to the candidacy of Juliana Amato Lumumba to the OIF.

In an opinion column published on Congo Independant, the writer argues that DR Congo talks too much and produces too little thought. He says public discussion now covers politics, celebrities and personal rivalries, and that social media lets anyone pose as a political commentator, diplomatic expert or military analyst without study.

He says the problem also reaches offices, where working hours are taken up by long conversations. He does not dispute the right of citizens to speak, but says freedom of expression carries an intellectual duty. He argues that when a subject demands specific expertise, personal opinion must be separated from reasoned analysis.

The column is framed around the candidacy of Juliana Amato Lumumba for the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF), the organisation of French-speaking countries. The writer uses that diplomatic bid to argue that national debate should be judged by its results rather than its volume.

Why it mattersCitizens who follow the candidacy of Juliana Amato Lumumba to the OIF are told that its success depends on the quality of public debate around it.
