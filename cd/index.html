Ituri Youth Parliament requests school reopening delay due to Ebola

The Youth Parliament of Ituri has asked the government to postpone the scheduled September 1, 2026, school reopening because of the ongoing Ebola epidemic in the region.

The Youth Parliament of Ituri has called for a delay in the start of the school year, currently set for September 1, 2026. This request comes as the Ebola virus disease epidemic continues to affect the region, raising concerns about student safety.

The youth body's appeal follows a similar request from the inter-union, highlighting widespread concern among local stakeholders. The Ministry of National Education had previously announced distance learning options, but practical arrangements for these programs are still pending in Ituri, the epicenter of the Ebola outbreak.

With the school year approaching rapidly, the lack of clarity on remote education modalities adds to the urgency of the Youth Parliament's request. The delay aims to protect students and staff from further exposure to the virus while alternative learning methods are established.

Why it mattersStudents and educators in Ituri are directly affected by the potential delay in school reopening and the uncertainty surrounding distance learning options due to the Ebola epidemic.

Health · 08/26/2026

Cholera outbreak kills 27 in Popokabaka, Kwango province

A cholera outbreak in Popokabaka, Kwango province, has resulted in 756 cases and 27 deaths between June 1 and August 24, 2026, according to Médecins sans frontières.

The health zone of Popokabaka is experiencing a resurgence of cholera, with Médecins sans frontières (MSF) epidemiological services reporting 756 cases and 27 fatalities. These figures, released on Monday, August 24, cover the period from June 1 to August 24, 2026.

In response to the escalating disease, a Cholera Treatment Center (CTC) has been established at Popokabaka General Referral Hospital to admit and care for patients. Additionally, MSF has set up four peripheral treatment units in local health facilities to bring care closer to affected communities and support the main center.

MSF teams are collaborating with local health personnel to manage the epidemic. Their efforts include supplying care, treating patients, and providing support to the teams involved in the response to the outbreak in the Kwango province.

Why it mattersThe cholera outbreak directly affects residents of Popokabaka, who are experiencing illness and death, and are now receiving care at newly established treatment centers.

Politics · 08/26/2026

Congolese government refers 62 suspected land fraud cases to justice system

The Congolese government has escalated its fight against state property spoliation in Kinshasa by submitting 62 suspicious files to the judiciary.

The Congolese government has forwarded 62 suspected cases of state property spoliation in Kinshasa to the justice system. This action, detailed in a communiqué from the Minister of Justice's cabinet dated Monday, August 24, marks an intensified effort against alleged land fraud.

The cases involve a suspected network creating false land titles. This network reportedly includes ministerial officers and administrative executives, according to the communiqué. The government is now moving to a higher gear in addressing these issues.

This development indicates a focused push to address corruption and illegal appropriation of state assets. The involvement of officials suggests a complex challenge for the justice system as it investigates these numerous allegations of fraud.

Why it mattersThis action directly affects citizens and businesses in Kinshasa by addressing the integrity of land ownership and potentially recovering public assets.

Culture · 08/26/2026

Grand Tambour Nears Two-Year Mark, Raises Operational Questions

The Centre Culturel et artistique pour les pays de l’Afrique centrale, known as Grand Tambour, approaches its second anniversary with questions about its full operational status.

The Grand Tambour, inaugurated on December fourteen, two thousand twenty-four, is nearing two years of existence. This cultural complex, located in Kasa-Vubu commune opposite the Palais du Peuple, spans some ninety-three thousand square meters and was opened by President Félix-Antoine Tshisekedi.

The facility includes a two thousand-seat performance hall, an eight hundred-seat amphitheater, recording studios, a dance hall, and accommodation rooms. It also houses the campus of the National Institute of Arts (INA), aiming to foster artistic creation, training, and cultural promotion for the Democratic Republic of Congo and Central Africa.

Despite its ambitious goals and extensive infrastructure, questions remain regarding whether the center's facilities are fully operational and accessible, and if it meets the expectations of artists and the public nearly two years after its opening, according to Radio Okapi.

Why it mattersArtists and the public in the Democratic Republic of Congo and Central Africa are affected by the operational status and accessibility of the Grand Tambour, which was intended to support their cultural activities and education.
