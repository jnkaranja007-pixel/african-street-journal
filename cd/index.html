Congolicious Foundation Pitches Cultural Development to Minister

The foundation presented initiatives like Congo Food Week and Congolicious Fest to the Minister of Culture, Arts and National Heritage.

The Congolicious Foundation met with Yolande Elebe, the Minister of Culture, Arts and National Heritage, to discuss its programs. The organization aims to leverage cultural activities as a means for economic development within the Democratic Republic of Congo.

During the working session, the foundation outlined several key initiatives, including the Congo Food Week, Mama Malewa, and Congolicious Fest. These events are designed to showcase and promote Congolese culture, fostering a stronger connection between heritage and economic growth.

The meeting underscored the foundation's commitment to integrating cultural promotion with tangible development strategies. Minister Elebe was briefed on how these initiatives are intended to create opportunities and stimulate economic activity through the arts and cultural sector.

Why it mattersThe Congolicious Foundation is working to make culture a tool for economic development, which could affect artists and cultural entrepreneurs.

Fire Destroys Bukavu Schools, Leaving Over 500 Students Without Study Space

An unknown blaze consumed the Uhuru primary school and Fariala institute in Bukavu's Cahi neighborhood overnight, displacing hundreds of students.

Over five hundred students in Bukavu are now without a place to study after a fire ravaged the Uhuru primary school and the Fariala institute. The blaze occurred during the night of Wednesday, September 2, to Thursday, September 3, 2026, in the Cahi neighborhood of South-Kivu. The cause of the fire remains unknown.

Civil society members in the Cahi neighborhood fear the fire may be linked to criminal activity, noting that the affected schools are not connected to the electrical grid or equipped with kitchens. Shukuru Lwekyya, president of the local civil society group, urged authorities to conduct serious investigations to determine the origin of the incident and called on residents to remain vigilant and report suspicious activity.

The civil society president also commended the efforts of local residents who intervened to help limit the spread of the fire. He emphasized that the security of all inhabitants depends on vigilance and collaboration. The community is now facing the challenge of finding alternative study locations for the displaced students.

Why it mattersOver 500 students in Bukavu are affected by the destruction of their schools, leaving them without a place to continue their education.

Business · 2026-09-05

DR Congo Plans Housing Bank Amidst Worsening Shelter Crisis

The government aims to establish a housing bank to address a deficit of approximately 4 million units and curb informal construction in hazardous areas.

The Democratic Republic of Congo faces a severe housing shortage, with an estimated deficit of 4 million units according to UN-Habitat. Many Congolese struggle to afford formal housing, leading to rising rents and exploitative landlord demands. Consequently, numerous families resort to building makeshift homes in flood-prone zones and on hillsides, exposing them to environmental dangers.

In response to these widespread housing challenges, the Congolese government is proposing the creation of a dedicated housing bank. This initiative is intended to provide citizens with a pathway to secure decent and safe housing, a goal that has remained elusive for many due to financial constraints and the high cost of construction and land.

The proposed bank aims to facilitate access to housing finance, potentially offering loans or other financial instruments for home construction and acquisition. This move comes as a direct response to the growing crisis, where informal settlements are becoming increasingly common due to the lack of affordable and formal housing options available to the population.

Why it mattersThe government's plan to create a housing bank directly affects citizens by aiming to provide access to decent housing, which is currently difficult to obtain.

Politics · 2026-09-05

President Tshisekedi Announces National Dialogue to Address Crises

The Congolese leader plans to convene a national dialogue aimed at finding solutions to the country's ongoing crises, including eastern insecurity.

President Félix Tshisekedi announced on August 27 that a national dialogue will soon be convened. The purpose of this dialogue is to allow Congolese people to collectively seek solutions to the crises affecting the nation, with a particular focus on the insecurity plaguing the eastern regions.

The process is set to begin with consultations held in the capitals of various provinces. Following these initial discussions, a final phase of the dialogue is scheduled to take place in Kinshasa, the nation's capital.

This initiative was discussed by Patrick Muyaya Katembwe, the Minister of Communication and Media and government spokesperson, who addressed questions and concerns related to the dialogue process.

Why it mattersThe national dialogue announced by President Tshisekedi aims to address the country's crises, including insecurity in the East, potentially impacting the stability and governance of the nation.

Health · 2026-09-05

Overcrowded School Lacks Water, Ebola Prevention Gear

More than one thousand displaced students in Bunia face health risks due to insufficient water and a lack of Ebola prevention equipment.

Saint-Luc school in Bunia is currently hosting one thousand one hundred students, more than three times its intended capacity. This overcrowding, combined with a severe shortage of water and essential Ebola prevention supplies, has raised serious concerns about the potential spread of the virus. Reporters observed classrooms with nearly one hundred students sharing a single room, and up to four children sharing a small bench, making it difficult to implement preventative measures.

The school has some handwashing stations, but water supply is critically low, with only two canisters of water provided daily from the displaced persons' site, which itself is experiencing a shortage. This means students cannot wash their hands regularly after using the toilet or during breaks. The school also lacks temperature scanners and hand sanitizer, further increasing the risk of disease transmission.

School administration believes these conditions put students and teachers at significant risk, especially as suspected and confirmed Ebola cases have been reported at the Kigonze displaced persons' site. Similar issues are reported in other schools accommodating displaced children in Bunia's outskirts and across Ituri province. The Ebola response team and provincial government have not yet responded to these urgent concerns.

Why it mattersOver one thousand displaced students are exposed to Ebola risks due to overcrowding and a lack of water and hygiene facilities.
