Tshisekedi promulgates law setting conditions for referendum

President Félix Tshisekedi signed law n° 26/045 on November 9, setting the rules for a referendum in DR Congo.

President Félix Tshisekedi promulgated law n° 26/045 on November 9, which sets the conditions for organising a referendum in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Media reports on November 11 gave the promulgation top billing, alongside the house arrest of Aubin Minaku, a member of the PPRD party and former president of the National Assembly.

The law is the legal step needed before a referendum can be organised in the country.

Why it mattersThe law sets the legal conditions under which a national referendum in DR Congo can be organised, as reported in the packet.

Fayulu blames authorities after health crisis of jailed opposition figure Prince Epenge

Martin Fayulu, president of the ECiDé party, says Prince Epenge is in extreme weakness at Makala central prison after a crisis on Sunday evening.

Martin Fayulu, president of the ECiDé party, visited Prince Epenge, an opposition figure currently detained at Makala central prison in Kinshasa, on Monday, November 9. Fayulu said Epenge suffered a crisis on Sunday evening and is now in a state of extreme weakness.

Fayulu, who is also a member of the C64 presidium, described Epenge's health as extremely worrying and said he holds the authorities fully responsible for what happens to him. The packet does not give further medical details.

Other media on Tuesday also covered a legal dispute between Moïse Katumbi and his brother Raphaël Katebe Katoto before the Court of Cassation, and a national round table on transport opened in the country.

Why it mattersEpenge's family and supporters now depend on prison authorities to provide care, and Fayulu says he will hold the state accountable for any further harm.

DR Congo war disrupts river trade

Conflict in eastern DR Congo slows goods transport between Kisangani and Kinshasa, idling boats.

War in eastern DR Congo has severely reduced river transport of goods between Kisangani and Kinshasa. Persistent insecurity in North Kivu has disrupted the flow of merchandise to Kisangani, leading to less cargo for Kinshasa-bound boats. Vehicles, beans, and potatoes, often sourced from the Kivu region, are among the most affected products.

At Kisangani port, several metal barges have been docked for months awaiting sufficient cargo. Fernand, marketing manager for the Apolo barge, stated that the war has drastically cut the arrival of goods, with packages now arriving in small quantities. This situation is complicating voyages for ship owners and workers whose incomes depend on transported volumes.

Operators report that many producing or transit areas in Kivu are affected by fighting or rebel control, slowing the supply chain to Kisangani. Michel noted that waiting times at the quay have doubled, extending round trips between Kisangani and Kinshasa from three months to five. Ship owners are waiting for larger loads to avoid financial losses, further delaying departures.

Why it mattersThe conflict in eastern DR Congo is disrupting trade routes, affecting the availability and delivery of goods like vehicles, beans, and potatoes to major cities.

Kinshasa census mapping nears four-week mark

Officials report on cartographic operations for the second general population and housing census.

Census agents have spent nearly four weeks mapping Kinshasa's streets and neighbourhoods. These cartographic operations are preparatory steps for the second general population and housing census in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Henri Marie Kazadi, Technical Coordinator of the Central Census Bureau, discussed the progress and conditions for the census's success. This marks the country's second census, following the one conducted in 1984.

The operations aim to gather essential data for the upcoming census. Officials are assessing the current situation and outlining requirements to ensure the census is completed effectively after a 42-year interval.

Why it mattersThe census operations in Kinshasa will provide updated demographic data for the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Health · 2026-11-11

13 Ebola patients leave Beni treatment centre

Health officials report that vigilance remains necessary as new cases continue to be recorded.

13 people who recovered from the Ebola virus left the treatment centre at Beni General Reference Hospital in North Kivu on Monday. This recovery marks a success for response teams and local communities in a city that remains a major centre for the epidemic.

Health officials stated that these recoveries demonstrate the effectiveness of medical care when patients are referred to specialised facilities promptly. However, they also noted that the two treatment centres in Beni are still recording new cases, partly due to population movements between the city and insecure areas.

The Ebola response teams are urging continued adherence to prevention measures and prompt consultation of health facilities for any suspicious symptoms. The first Ebola recovery case in Beni was recorded on Monday, June 29, 2026, with five more added on August 31 and eight on October 7.

Why it mattersThe recovery of 13 Ebola patients in Beni offers hope, but ongoing cases and population movements complicate containment efforts.
