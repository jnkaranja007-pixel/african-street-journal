Visa RDC launches Android tap-to-pay in Kinshasa

The feature lets users pay at terminals with their phones as the country prepares to ban commercial cash and foreign-currency transactions in April 2027.

Visa RDC launched Tap to Pay on its Visa Pay app in Kinshasa on Monday, enabling users with Android phones to make contactless payments at payment terminals.

Sophie Kafuti, Visa RDC's managing director, said the service lets consumers use a phone as a bank card. The central bank governor, André Wameso, said digital payments are central to the planned reform and to expanding financial inclusion.

The ban on commercial cash and foreign-currency transactions is due to take effect in April 2027. Visa RDC and the central bank said they would run public-awareness activities to encourage people to adopt the payment method.

Why it mattersShoppers and traders who pay by card can now tap their Android phones at terminals before cash payments are banned for commercial transactions.

Bunyakiri faces harder access to care amid cholera and malaria

Fighting between the FRN and the army has worsened insecurity in the South Kivu health zone as cholera spreads and malaria cases peak.

Insecurity following clashes between the FRN and the army is complicating access to health care in Bunyakiri, a health zone in South Kivu, where cholera is spreading and malaria cases have peaked.

The deteriorating security situation is adding to pressure on health services as the two diseases affect the area, Actualite.cd reported on Wednesday. Its report did not provide figures for cases or deaths, or details of steps by health authorities.

The combination of cholera, a malaria surge and insecurity has made the health situation in Bunyakiri more concerning, according to the report.

Why it mattersPeople seeking care in Bunyakiri face access difficulties as cholera and a malaria surge affect the health zone.

UN Women brings private sector into Ebola response in Kinshasa

Politics · 2026-11-18

Congolese youth urged to take a bigger role in peacebuilding

At a Kinshasa conference, organisers asked young people to prevent conflict, back social cohesion and carry their pledges into their communities.

Young Congolese were called on Monday, 16 November, to play a more active role in promoting peace and security in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The appeal came at a conference in Kinshasa organised by Change Your World, the Kinshasa Youth Parliament and the United Nations peacekeeping mission in Congo, MONUSCO, under the theme "Youth in action for peace."

Sarah Tambwe, coordinator of Change Your World, said young people should be placed at the centre of the peace process and given spaces to speak and take part. Youth parliamentarian Jennifer Sarty said young people must not stay on the sidelines of the security challenges facing the country, and urged them to act against violence targeting women and girls.

Organisers asked participants to turn their discussions and pledges into concrete action, and to pass the message on to their peers as awareness leaders.

Why it mattersYoung Congolese are being asked to prevent conflict and act against violence against women and girls in their own communities.
