Congo Independant columnist warns of verbal excess in DR Congo

In an opinion column, the writer argues that a flood of public commentary is outpacing analysis and results, and links it to the candidacy of Juliana Amato Lumumba to the OIF.

Source: Congo Independant · 2026-11-16 · 1 min read

In an opinion column published on Congo Independant, the writer argues that DR Congo talks too much and produces too little thought. He says public discussion now covers politics, celebrities and personal rivalries, and that social media lets anyone pose as a political commentator, diplomatic expert or military analyst without study.

He says the problem also reaches offices, where working hours are taken up by long conversations. He does not dispute the right of citizens to speak, but says freedom of expression carries an intellectual duty. He argues that when a subject demands specific expertise, personal opinion must be separated from reasoned analysis.

The column is framed around the candidacy of Juliana Amato Lumumba for the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF), the organisation of French-speaking countries. The writer uses that diplomatic bid to argue that national debate should be judged by its results rather than its volume.

Why it mattersCitizens who follow the candidacy of Juliana Amato Lumumba to the OIF are told that its success depends on the quality of public debate around it.
