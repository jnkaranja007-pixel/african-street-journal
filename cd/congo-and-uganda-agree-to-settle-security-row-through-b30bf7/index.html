Congo and Uganda agree to settle security row through diplomacy

Kinshasa had summoned Uganda's mission chief after posts by Uganda's army chief about eastern Congo, and Uganda has announced it will withdraw troops from Lubero.

Source: Radio Okapi · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Congo's foreign minister, Thérèse Kayikwamba Wagner, spoke by phone on Friday, 27 November, with her Ugandan counterpart, Adonia Ayebare. According to the Congolese foreign ministry, they discussed recent public statements on regional and bilateral security, and agreed to handle such issues through official and diplomatic channels.

The call followed Kinshasa's summoning, on 24 November, of the head of Uganda's embassy mission in Kinshasa. The ministry said that move came after posts on X by Ugandan army chief Muhoozi Kainerugaba about the security situation in eastern Congo, some of which showed support for the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, a political movement led by former president Joseph Kabila. Kinshasa had asked Ugandan authorities for explanations.

The ministers reaffirmed respect for each country's sovereignty and territorial integrity, and said they would strengthen cooperation in defence, the economy and public health. Uganda has also announced the coming withdrawal of its troops from Lubero, in North Kivu, where they have been deployed since January 2025 alongside Congolese forces against the Allied Democratic Forces.

Why it mattersUgandan soldiers leaving Lubero, in North Kivu, will change the military presence in a district where they have fought the Allied Democratic Forces since January 2025.
