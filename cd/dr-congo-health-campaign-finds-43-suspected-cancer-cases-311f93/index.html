DR Congo health campaign finds 43 suspected cancer cases

A health campaign in South and North Kivu screened 113 women and raised awareness among nearly 600 people.

Source: Radio Okapi · 2026-11-02 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Health workers in the Democratic Republic of Congo's South and North Kivu provinces identified 43 suspected cases of breast and cervical cancer during an October campaign. The initiative, led by the Bunyakiri health zone and the Clubs des filles reporters de la RDC, provided 113 free consultations and raised awareness among over 580 people across three health zones.

Simon Mihona, coordinator for the Clubs des filles reporters, reported that 421 people were informed in Bunyakiri, 90 in Kalonge, and 25 community leaders in Itebero. Medical consultations included 61 in Bunyakiri, 29 in Itebero, and 23 in Kalonge, leading to the identification of the 43 potential cases, one of which was considered probable in Bunyakiri.

Dr. Marquis Chiralo, head of the Bunyakiri health zone, stated that individuals with suspicious signs require further tests to confirm or rule out cancer. He urged partners and donors to support such actions, while Kasese Maene, head of the Gender and Family office in Bunyakiri, called for more resources to ensure women receive accurate health status assessments.

Why it mattersWomen in South and North Kivu are affected by the identification of 43 suspected cases of breast and cervical cancer requiring further medical follow-up.
