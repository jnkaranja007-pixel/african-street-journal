Wooden-barge owners urge Congo ministry to suspend river ban

Owners in Kinshasa want each vessel inspected and upgraded gradually, warning that a blanket ban could cost jobs and disrupt riverside supplies.

Source: Radio Okapi · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Owners of wooden barges protested in Kinshasa on Thursday, 19 November, against the transport ministry’s ban on their vessels navigating the Congo River. The ministerial order, signed on 10 November, prohibits the boats from operating on the river.

In a memorandum to Prime Minister Judith Suminwa, the owners asked the government to suspend and review the order. They say it risks jobs and supplies for riverside communities, and affects thousands of people who rely on the trade. Owner Makala Roger called for technical inspections of individual boats and gradual upgrades instead of a general ban.

The owners also want a joint commission to assess the vessels and government support for their modernisation. They say they are ready to work with officials on river safety and are awaiting a response to their suspension request.

Why it mattersThe thousands of people who depend on river transport face possible job losses and disrupted supplies while the owners await the government’s response to their suspension request.
