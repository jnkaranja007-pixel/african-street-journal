OIF leader stays on as DR Congo blocks third term

The Francophonie summit in Cambodia ended without a new secretary-general, extending Louise Mushikiwabo's term by 12 months.

Sources: Congo Independant and Radio Okapi · 2026-11-17 · 2 min read

The 20th Summit of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) concluded in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, on November 16, 2026, without appointing a new secretary-general. A diplomatic standoff over the succession of Louise Mushikiwabo resulted in a compromise: the Rwandan leader will remain in her position for an additional 12 months, ineligible for a third term. Observers noted this outcome as a partial success for the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and a partial setback for Rwanda, or vice versa.

The Congolese diplomacy opposed Mushikiwabo's reappointment, arguing that she had not sufficiently defended the organization's core values of peace, democracy, and human rights. The compromise is seen as a partial failure for Kigali, which temporarily retains leadership of the OIF. Four candidates had vied for the position: Juliana Amato Lumumba from the DRC, Coumba Ba from Mauritania, Dacian Cioloș from Romania, and the incumbent Louise Mushikiwabo seeking a third term.

The agreed-upon terms stipulate Mushikiwabo's continued tenure for 12 months, her ineligibility for future terms, and Rwanda's exclusion from presenting a candidate in the next selection process. While Kinshasa achieved its immediate goal of preventing a third term for the former Rwandan foreign minister, it did not secure Mushikiwabo's immediate departure or the appointment of its preferred successor. The OIF summit's outcome highlights the power dynamics among member states.

Why it mattersThe Democratic Republic of Congo's diplomatic efforts at the OIF summit have secured a 12-month extension for the current secretary-general, preventing her immediate departure and blocking her bid for a third term.
