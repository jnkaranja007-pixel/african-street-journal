Djibouti urges Francophonie to reinvest in peace at Phnom Penh summit

Djibouti's foreign minister told heads of state that lasting prosperity requires justice, dialogue and solidarity, not just the absence of conflict.

Source: Agence Djiboutienne d'Information · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Djibouti's foreign minister, who is also the government spokesman, spoke at the Francophonie summit in Phnom Penh on behalf of President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh. The gathering, of heads of state and government from French-speaking countries, was held under the theme "Reinvesting in peace for shared prosperity and lasting stability."

The minister, Abdoulkader Houssein Omar, said peace means more than the absence of conflict. He said reinvesting in peace meant investing in education, health, employment and young people, and refusing to let inequality and exclusion become the breeding ground for division and instability.

The summit is due to name the next secretary-general of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) for the 2027-2030 term. Louise Mushikiwabo of Rwanda has held the post since 2019. Djibouti's minister asked for a more visible, inclusive and results-driven Francophonie and said Djibouti was fully available to help.

Why it mattersInvestment in schools, clinics and jobs is the policy Djibouti is asking the French-speaking bloc to back, and the new secretary-general will decide how far that agenda moves.
