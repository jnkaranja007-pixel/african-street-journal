Djibouti researcher publishes regional food-security book

The 274-page study sets out field-based approaches to degraded land, irrigation and heat-resistant seeds for Djibouti and the Horn of Africa.

Source: Agence Djiboutienne d'Information · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

A Djiboutian environmental-science researcher has published a 274-page book on food security, arid land and climate change in Djibouti and the Horn of Africa. The publisher describes it as the first scientific work on the subject in Djibouti and the region.

The researcher, Sougueh Cheikh, works at Djibouti’s Centre for Studies and Research, the country’s leading scientific institution. The book examines proven methods to restore degraded land, irrigate without making soil saline and select heat-resistant seeds, and gives central attention to rural women, young people and herders facing repeated droughts.

Cheikh argues that food security starts at home and is strengthened by cooperation among Horn of Africa countries. The book is directed at decision-makers, funders, technical partners and students; Djibouti imports almost all its food.

Why it mattersRural women, young people and herders are the book’s central audiences, with methods for restoring land, irrigating without salinisation and choosing heat-resistant seeds.
