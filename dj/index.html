Djibouti launches emergency medical service

The new SAMU aims to bring hospital care to patients when emergencies demand it.

Djibouti has launched a new emergency medical service, SAMU, following a week of training with Hospices civils de Lyon. The service will operate with medical regulation, mobile teams, and adapted transport, focusing on permanence, resilience to disasters, and territorial equity.

Health Minister Mouna Osman Aden, who led the launch ceremony, stated the ambition is to "bring the hospital to the patient when the emergency demands it." The initiative involves the University Hospital, the National Social Security Fund, and the Military Hospital, which presented its mass casualty preparedness plan.

The SAMU's operational framework includes three commitments: constant availability, resilience in the face of catastrophes, and equitable access across the country. Minister Aden emphasized that "no Djiboutian should be less protected because they live far from a hospital."

Why it mattersDjiboutians, especially those living far from hospitals, will receive more equitable emergency medical care.

Djibouti signs deal to clear rainwater collectors

The National Office for Water and Sanitation and the Urban Renewal Agency will work on flood prevention in four city districts.

The National Office for Water and Sanitation (ONEAD) and the Urban Renewal Agency (ARULoS) signed an agreement to clear rainwater collectors in Djibouti-ville. Mohamed Fouad Abdo of ONEAD and Abdourahman Ali Ahmed of ARULoS signed the deal at the Ministry of City. The work will focus on districts 3, 4, 5, and 6 to improve rainwater drainage, reduce water stagnation, and prevent flooding.

The agreement aims to restore the evacuation capacity for rainwater and protect residents and homes from floods. The Ministry of City, led by Amina Abdi Aden, and the Ministry of Agriculture, Water, Fisheries, and Livestock, led by Radwan Abdillahi Bahdon, will combine their skills and resources. This initiative is part of the government's efforts to better manage rainwater and strengthen urban infrastructure against bad weather.

Authorities stated the agreement shows a commitment to coordinating public institutions for sanitation, flood risk prevention, and population protection. The two agencies will use their operational structures to carry out the clearing work.

Why it mattersResidents of districts 3, 4, 5, and 6 in Djibouti-ville face reduced flood risk due to the agreement.

Djibouti launches first Beach Festival

The three-day event on Heron beach aims to showcase the nation's coastline and youth potential.

Djibouti's first Beach Festival opened on November 5 at Heron beach, featuring cultural, sporting, and economic activities. Minister of Commerce and Tourism Orbis Helaf Orbis presided over the opening ceremony, attended by government members, the president of the Ports and Free Zones Authority, and the prefect of Djibouti-ville, Abdi Hassan Ahmed.

Organized by IN'PACT Social under the institutional patronage of the Ministry of Commerce and Tourism, the festival runs until November 7. Stands and exhibition spaces are set up to display local creativity and highlight opportunities along the country's maritime facade.

Authorities aim to promote national maritime heritage, stimulate tourism, and provide a platform for local talent. The event is themed 'Djibouti Blue Generation' and focuses on the country's coastline and its young population.

Why it mattersDjiboutians will experience cultural, sporting, and economic activities designed to promote their maritime heritage and showcase local talent.
