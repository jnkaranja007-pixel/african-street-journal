Djibouti prepares World Bank business climate assessment

Officials and businesses will review indicators for the 2027 B-READY evaluation.

Djibouti is preparing for the World Bank's B-READY assessment, a process aimed at improving the business climate and national attractiveness. The evaluation examines regulatory frameworks, public services for businesses, and rule enforcement across 10 areas including company creation, finance, employment, and international trade. Ali Houmed Aboubaker, secretary-general of MIDDP, presided over a meeting to launch this preparation.

Various administrations, banks, essential service providers, professional organizations, and companies are participating in this initiative. Their role involves understanding the B-READY methodology, analyzing specific indicators and questionnaires, and appointing focal points responsible for data collection.

Following data collection, the process will move to processing the 2027 edition questionnaires. The next steps include identifying priority reforms and developing a roadmap to implement them.

Why it mattersDjibouti's business climate and attractiveness are being assessed through the World Bank's B-READY evaluation, involving government and private sector participants.

Djibouti police academy trains 17 African risk prevention officers

The ESIPC concluded a risk prevention course for trainees from nine African nations at its police academy.

The ESIPC concluded a risk prevention training on October 29, graduating 17 trainees from nine African countries including Djibouti. The course, held at the "Idriss Farah Abaneh" Police Academy, focused on enhancing skills in analyzing, anticipating, and managing threats to people and property. French lieutenants Colonel Jules Mazart and Olivier Ménard led the instruction.

The graduates came from Benin, Cameroon, Ivory Coast, Djibouti, Gabon, Guinea, Madagascar, Mauritania, and Senegal. The ceremony was attended by Djibouti's National Director of Civil Protection, Abdoulaker Moussa Abayazid, Police Commander Mahdi, and ESIPC Director Commander Hassan Ibrahim Ismail. This session aimed to improve capabilities and share best practices among African civil protection institutions.

The ESIPC stated the training is part of a skills development and cooperation initiative between African civil protection services. The school intends to strengthen international partnerships and improve operational capacities through such programs. The certificate awarding marked the culmination of the course.

Why it matters17 officers from nine African nations, including Djibouti, received training in risk prevention, enhancing their skills to protect people and property.
