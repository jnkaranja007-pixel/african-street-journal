RPP Leaders Convene in Ali Sabieh for Future Strategy Discussions

The ruling party is holding a three-day retreat to address national, regional, and global challenges following the presidential election.

The RPP party has opened a political retreat in Ali Sabieh, focusing on future challenges and national aspirations. Prime Minister Abdoulkader Kamil Mohamed emphasized the importance of being close to the people to understand their expectations and to face upcoming challenges. The retreat, themed "What party facing new issues and national, regional, and global challenges?", aims to foster reflection and preparation among party officials.

This gathering follows the April presidential election, which saw the re-election of President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh. The RPP's discussions are set to examine the evolving regional and international landscape and its implications for Djibouti, a strategically located country at the entrance to the Red Sea. The Prime Minister highlighted that a great party serves its people and nation, not itself.

The retreat, scheduled from September 3 to 5, is being held with the support of local authorities, regional council, security forces, and mobilized teams. The Prime Minister acknowledged the warm welcome from the authorities and the population, particularly the youth. The objective is to develop orientations that will enable the party to better meet the population's needs and expectations.

Why it mattersThe RPP party is holding a retreat to discuss future challenges and national strategies, which could influence the country's direction.

Djibouti Drafts Artificial Intelligence Strategy

The nation is developing a roadmap for AI governance, cybersecurity, training, and innovation, with a focus on practical implementation.

Djibouti has begun implementing its national artificial intelligence strategy, focusing on governance, cybersecurity, training, research, innovation, and inclusion. Following an initial day on national vision and readiness assessment, the second day concentrated on the practical conditions for strategy execution. The UNESCO RAM report on Djibouti's AI readiness was presented, featuring discussions with UNESCO representative Hugue Ngandeu Ngatta, Dr. Mahamed Nawar of ESCWA, and digital transformation expert Ismail Douksieh Boulaleh.

Discussions centered on AI and data governance, digital security, institutional mechanisms, and national priorities. Participants emphasized that the success of this transformation hinges less on the technology itself and more on the country's ability to cultivate appropriate skills. Key topics included evolving educational programs, teacher training, research development, computing infrastructure, and university partnerships.

The overarching goal is to prepare the public and private sectors with necessary skills while fostering the emergence of researchers, entrepreneurs, and professionals in emerging technologies. Five panels were held to further explore these strategic directions and ensure the effective development and deployment of AI capabilities within the nation.

Why it mattersDjibouti's focus on AI skills development and infrastructure will affect its public and private sectors, potentially creating new job opportunities and driving economic growth.

Comoros Seeks Djibouti Social Protection Model

A Comorian delegation is examining Djibouti's social security system and healthcare management, including the new Al Shifa hospital.

A delegation from the Comoros is in Djibouti to study the nation's social protection and health systems. The visitors are particularly interested in the operations of the National Social Security Fund (CNSS), which oversees the recently opened Al Shifa hospital. Discussions have focused on governance, social coverage, and human resource development within these sectors.

Djibouti's Minister of Labour, Yonis Ali Guedi, presented the country's social protection reforms and welcomed the Comorian interest, emphasizing the potential for South-South cooperation. The CNSS's management of the Al Shifa hospital, inaugurated in late June, is a key area of inquiry for the visiting officials.

The two nations also explored cooperation in medical and paramedical training and skills development. Djibouti's Ministry of Labour and the CNSS have offered to continue discussions and identify specific areas for future collaboration, aiming to adapt successful models to each country's unique circumstances.

Why it mattersComorian officials are affected by learning about Djibouti's social protection and healthcare management systems to potentially improve their own.
