Djibouti athlete Ayanleh Souleiman promotes Grand Bara race

Participatory activities and an information booth are part of preparations for the international desert race.

Preparations for the international Grand Bara race are advancing with sports challenges and quizzes about the desert event. An information space is available to assist future runners with registration.

Djiboutian athlete Ayanleh Souleiman, the patron for this edition, met with participants, offering locals and international runners a chance to interact with the athletics star. These events aim to showcase the race through sport, exchanges, and information.

The communication cell of the FFDj stated these animations allow the Grand Bara race to be discovered from a new perspective, associating sport, exchanges, and information.

Why it mattersDjiboutians and international runners have opportunities to engage with a world-class athlete and learn about the Grand Bara race.

Djibouti minister attends ITU conference in Doha

Djibouti aims to advance its digital connectivity and innovation priorities at the quadrennial meeting.

Djibouti's Minister of Communication and Postal Services, Abdi Youssouf Sougueh, arrived in Doha, Qatar, on Sunday for the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) Plenipotentiary Conference. He is accompanied by Dr. Kassim Issak Ousman, president of the ARMD regulatory authority, and Ambassador Dayib Doubad Robleh.

The conference brings together government delegations and major players in telecommunications and digital technology to discuss the ITU's strategic directions. Minister Sougueh participated in the inaugural ministerial meeting, where discussions focused on accelerating digital transformation, deploying high-speed infrastructure, and reducing the digital divide.

In a Facebook message, Minister Sougueh emphasized international cooperation for equitable access to new technologies and reaffirmed Djibouti's commitment to an innovative and inclusive digital environment. Djibouti intends to defend its priorities in connectivity, digital development, and innovation during the conference, which defines the ITU's policies and elects its leaders every four years.

Why it mattersDjibouti's minister is attending a global conference to advance the country's digital development and connectivity goals.

Djibouti plant produces 40 million m³ of desalinated water

The Doraleh desalination plant has reached a significant production milestone, contributing to the nation's water supply.

The Doraleh desalination plant in Djibouti has produced 40 million cubic meters of water. Mohamed Fouad Abdo, the director general of ONEAD, attended a meeting at the plant with its staff.

Abdo praised the plant workers for their daily efforts, emphasizing teamwork and operational precision. He highlighted the plant's vital role in a country where fresh water is scarce, noting that desalination helps meet the population's drinking water needs.

ONEAD considers desalination a core strategy for increasing access to water. The agency aims to expand the availability of this essential resource across Djibouti.

Why it mattersDjibouti's population benefits from increased access to drinking water through the Doraleh desalination plant's output.

Djibouti beach festival concludes with local and diaspora artists

The three-day event featured international hits, traditional dances, and performances by artists from Djibouti and its diaspora.

Djibouti's first Beach Festival concluded after three nights of seaside music, drawing crowds who sang and danced to a mix of international hits and local creations. The singer Wafa and her band, along with Sharmarke and his dancers in traditional costumes, performed contemporary rhythms including songs by Beyoncé and Sharma Boy. Traditional Arabic, Afar, and Somali dances were also featured.

The festival welcomed back Djiboutian music figures Saad Coolio and Racine J, who performed 25 years after their initial success, earning recognition from younger audiences. Rapper Waydara Boy, a recent national discovery, shared the stage with established artists like Fafy, Yahya, Ikhran, Harbouy, Houssein Hayle, and Abayazid Ali. Artists from the Djiboutian diaspora, including Danka Boy and Arido, also performed, alongside Ethiopian guest Haymanot.

Arido paid tribute to Nasser Abdallah by performing his song "Yinti Yinti," a moment described as the festival's most emotional. Beyond the music, the site offered family activities, children's games, and stands for crafts and entrepreneurship. Organizers reported the event proceeded without incident, with technical teams quickly fixing minor power outages on the second night.

Why it mattersThe festival provided a space for cultural expression, entertainment, and economic activity for Djiboutian residents and artists.

Tech · 2026-11-10

Djibouti parliament discusses cybersecurity lessons

National Security Director Hassan Said Khaireh and parliament officials reviewed digital threats and best practices.

Djibouti's National Assembly held a session on cybersecurity, opened by Assembly President Dileita Mohamed Dileita. Director General of National Security Hassan Said Khaireh addressed attendees on digital threats.

The meeting, attended by the Director General of the National Assembly Ibrahim Yonis Omar and the Public Prosecutor, focused on recommendations from a recent campaign. Discussions covered digital hygiene and the parliament's role in fostering cybersecurity culture.

Participants emphasized that protecting the digital space requires collective mobilization and shared responsibility. The session concluded with a reminder that cybersecurity is a concern for everyone.

Why it mattersDjibouti's parliament and national security officials are working to strengthen the nation's digital defenses against online threats.
