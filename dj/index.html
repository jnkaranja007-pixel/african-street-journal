Health · 2026-11-06

Djibouti launches emergency medical service

The new SAMU aims to bring hospital care to patients when emergencies demand it.

Djibouti has launched a new emergency medical service, SAMU, following a week of training with Hospices civils de Lyon. The service will operate with medical regulation, mobile teams, and adapted transport, focusing on permanence, resilience to disasters, and territorial equity.

Health Minister Mouna Osman Aden, who led the launch ceremony, stated the ambition is to "bring the hospital to the patient when the emergency demands it." The initiative involves the University Hospital, the National Social Security Fund, and the Military Hospital, which presented its mass casualty preparedness plan.

The SAMU's operational framework includes three commitments: constant availability, resilience in the face of catastrophes, and equitable access across the country. Minister Aden emphasized that "no Djiboutian should be less protected because they live far from a hospital."

Why it mattersDjiboutians, especially those living far from hospitals, will receive more equitable emergency medical care.

Djibouti launches beach festival to showcase youth talent

The three-day event, themed 'Djibouti Blue Generation,' features cultural, sports, and economic activities on Heron beach.

Djibouti's first Beach Festival opened on November 5, 2026, on Heron beach, with the Minister of Commerce and Tourism, Orbis Helaf Orbis, presiding over the launch ceremony. The event, running until November 7, aims to highlight Djibouti's coastline and the potential of its youth. It includes cultural, sports, and economic activities, alongside stands for artisans and young entrepreneurs.

Organized by IN'PACT Social under the institutional sponsorship of the Ministry of Commerce and Tourism, the festival is designed to be a showcase for local creativity and the opportunities presented by the country's maritime facade. Government officials, including the president of the Ports and Free Zones Authority and the prefect of Djibouti-ville, Abdi Hassan Ahmed, attended the opening.

Authorities intend for this initiative to promote national maritime heritage, stimulate tourism, and provide a new platform for local talents. The festival's theme, 'Djibouti Blue Generation,' underscores the focus on engaging and empowering the nation's young people.

Why it mattersDjiboutian artisans and young entrepreneurs have a new platform to display their creativity and products at the Beach Festival.

Politics · 2026-11-06

Djibouti agency and French development bank review urban projects

The Agence Djiboutienne de Développement Social and the French Development Agency visited Balbala to inspect completed and ongoing urban development works.

The Agence Djiboutienne de Développement Social (ADDS) and the French Development Agency (AFD) reviewed urban development projects in Djibouti. AFD director Mme Belliot visited the ADDS technical teams to see progress on the Integrated Urban Development Project (PDUI2 Ter) and the Shebelley Technical Landfill Centre. The landfill project is co-financed by AFD and the European Union for 16 million euros.

In the Balbala district, the delegation inspected facilities completed under PDUI2, including a gendarmerie brigade, market halls, sports fields, and a Community Development Centre. They also observed ongoing work for PDUI2 Ter, which continues urban transformation in the area. ADDS Director General highlighted the long-standing partnership with AFD and thanked French taxpayers for their contribution to projects totaling 24 million euros.

Mme Belliot praised the ADDS teams for their work quality on the projects. These initiatives are part of the 'Zero Slum' Programme, led by the Ministry of City, aiming for inclusive and sustainable urban development for residents.

Why it mattersDjiboutian residents in Balbala will benefit from improved urban infrastructure and community facilities funded by international partners.
