Djibouti and Somalia sign parliamentary cooperation deal

Djibouti's Dileita Mohamed Dileita and Somalia's Abdoulkadir Mohamed Nour agreed to share expertise and build institutional partnerships.

Djibouti and Somalia signed a memorandum of understanding on February 11, 2026, to strengthen parliamentary cooperation. The agreement aims to deepen relations through exchanges of experience and expertise, and the establishment of institutional partnerships between the two legislative assemblies.

Dileita Mohamed Dileita, president of the Djiboutian assembly, praised the discussions with the Somali delegation led by his counterpart. He highlighted positive perspectives for both nations emerging from the talks. The Somali president of the People's Assembly expressed Mogadishu's commitment to reinforcing parliamentary ties with Djibouti.

"We intend to strengthen the links between the two nations through a process of greater exchanges of expertise and partnerships between the two legislative assemblies," stated Abdoulkadir Mohamed Nour. He also thanked Djibouti for the welcome and hospitality extended to his delegation.

Why it mattersDjibouti's parliament and Somalia's parliament will share expertise and build partnerships, deepening relations between the two countries.

Mafia leader Haibado Ismail Omar reportedly takes charge

Haibado Ismail Omar is named as the new leader of Qawlaysato, Africa's oldest mafia organization.

Haibado Ismail Omar has reportedly been installed as the new leader of Qawlaysato, described as Africa's oldest and most significant mafia organization. Rumors of a leadership change and secret meetings have circulated for months on social media and within political circles.

The organization's structure is said to mirror that of Sicily's La Cosa Nostra, with a hierarchical system based on territorial control and a strong emphasis on family ties, both biological and adopted. This structure relies on strict selection criteria for members and uses rituals and sponsorship to foster loyalty.

Qawlaysato's operational principles include a strict code of silence, known as omertà, which is easier to maintain within a tightly-knit family unit. The organization's leadership selection process involves conclaves and assemblies of district leaders, similar to traditional mafia practices.

Why it mattersDjiboutian Haibado Ismail Omar is reportedly taking leadership of a major African mafia organization.

Djibouti prepares World Bank business climate assessment

Djibouti's government is preparing for the World Bank's B-READY assessment to improve its business environment.

Djibouti is preparing for the World Bank's B-READY assessment, chaired by MIDDP Secretary-General Ali Houmed Aboubaker, to improve the business climate and country attractiveness. The B-READY evaluates the regulatory framework, public services for businesses, and rule enforcement in 10 areas: company creation, public utilities and financial services, employment, taxation, international trade, competition, dispute resolution, and insolvency.

Administrations, banks, essential service operators, professional organizations, and businesses are participating in this preparation. They must adopt the methodology, review indicators and questionnaires, and designate focal points for data collection.

The process will then involve processing questionnaires for the 2027 edition, identifying priority reforms, and developing a roadmap. This aims to enhance the business environment and country attractiveness.
