Parents, Teachers Address Teen Vaping

Parents and school officials are working to prevent minors from using e-cigarettes, which are being shared among students and obtained from vendors who disregard age restrictions.

The use of e-cigarettes among minors is a growing concern, with some young people obtaining devices left unsupervised by parents or shared by peers at school. Others acquire them from sellers who do not enforce the ban on sales to minors, contributing to their circulation among younger age groups.

Authorities state that prevention is a collective responsibility. Parents are urged to keep e-cigarettes out of reach of children and to monitor for behavioral changes. Educators and school administrators are encouraged to raise awareness and emphasize the dangers of vaping to students.

The message for young people is to avoid e-cigarettes, regardless of perceived harmlessness, peer pressure, or flavor. Officials stress that e-cigarettes are not toys and should not become a habit, advocating for information, vigilance, and dialogue between parents, teachers, and youth to combat the normalization of vaping among minors.

Why it mattersThis story affects parents, teachers, and young people by highlighting the risks of e-cigarette use and the need for collective action to prevent it.

Djibouti hosts C STAR program for young African entrepreneurs

The fourth edition of the C STAR program, focusing on youth entrepreneurship, launched in Djibouti with 32 participants from 19 African countries selected from 641 applicants.

Djibouti is hosting the fourth edition of the C STAR program, a pan-African initiative for young entrepreneurs. The program, which began in 2022, has trained over 1,500 participants from more than 50 African countries, with 45% being women. This year, 32 young individuals from 19 African nations, including Algeria, Cape Verde, Côte d'Ivoire, and Djibouti, were chosen from 641 applications across 44 countries.

The launch ceremony included high-profile attendees such as Aboubaker Omar Hadi, President of the Djibouti Ports and Free Zones Authority (DPFZA), and Miao Jianmin, President of the China Merchants Group. Djiboutian ministers of Infrastructure and Equipment, Economy and Finance, and Digital Economy and Innovation also participated, alongside China's Ambassador to Djibouti, Hu Bin, and the UNDP Deputy Resident Representative, Philippe Clerc.

C STAR aims to cultivate a new generation of African leaders in innovation and sustainable growth, implemented with the China Merchants Charity Foundation, UNDP, and the CLE Center. The program aligns with the Beijing Action Plan of the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation (FOCAC) for 2025-2027, connecting young entrepreneurs with networks, investors, and economic opportunities across the continent. Djibouti's role as host highlights its entrepreneurial ecosystem and its position as a platform for African and international exchanges.

Why it mattersYoung entrepreneurs from 19 African countries are directly affected by gaining access to training, networks, investors, and economic opportunities through the C STAR program.

Ministry of Labor launches digital platform "Djibouti Emploi"

The Ministry of Labor introduced a new digital platform, "Djibouti Emploi," to centralize and simplify access to its services for citizens, workers, and employers.

The Ministry of Labor has launched a new digital platform named "Djibouti Emploi" to modernize and digitally transform its services. This initiative aims to bring public services closer to citizens, workers, and employers, according to the Agence Djiboutienne d'Information.

The platform will serve as a single portal to make services more accessible, simpler, and closer to users. It is designed to facilitate professional integration, assist companies with recruitment, and support public administration.

A key function of "Djibouti Emploi" is to centralize and streamline access to the Ministry of Labor's services, while also interconnecting various institutions. This move is part of a broader effort to enhance the efficiency and reach of public services.

Why it mattersCitizens, workers, and employers are affected by gaining a single, simplified portal for professional integration and recruitment services.
