Djibouti's telecoms regulator meets Morocco, France and Romania in Doha

The head of Djibouti's telecoms regulator held three bilateral talks at the ITU's Plenipotentiary Conference in the Qatari capital this week.

Djibouti's telecommunications regulator, Dr Kassim Issak Osman, held three bilateral meetings this week in Doha with counterparts from Morocco, France and Romania. The talks took place while the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), the United Nations agency for telecoms, was holding its Plenipotentiary Conference in the Qatari capital.

Dr Osman first met the director general of Morocco's National Telecommunications Regulatory Agency (ANRT) to strengthen an existing partnership and find new areas of joint work. He then spoke with a representative of France's Electronic Communications, Postal and Press Distribution Regulatory Authority (ARCEP) about current regulatory challenges and ways to share expertise and good practice. Last, he met the vice-president of Romania's Regulatory Council to compare how their authorities operate.

The conference opened on 9 November and meets every four years to set the ITU's strategic direction and elect its leaders. The regulator said the meetings aim to anchor Djibouti in international cooperation networks as the telecoms sector changes quickly.

Djibouti military trains 91 soldiers

The army chief presided over the graduation of 91 soldiers, including 23 women, after a year of training.

Djibouti's army graduated 91 soldiers, 23 of them women, from training courses at Camp Cheikh Osman. The ceremony marked the end of the 2025-2026 academic year and was led by the Chief of General Staff of the Armies, Lieutenant General Zakaria Cheikh Ibrahim. A formation of 150 soldiers, including the trainees, presented arms to the chief of staff before he reviewed the troops.

Three of the four courses focused on armored warfare, leading to elementary technical certificates in tank investigation, pilot investigation, and the piloting of wheeled armored vehicles. The fourth course was dedicated to military music. According to the general staff, these programs aim to enhance the technical and professional skills of personnel and strengthen the operational capabilities of the Djiboutian Armed Forces (FAD).

The training courses were designed to improve the technical and professional abilities of the soldiers. The FAD's operational capacities are expected to be consolidated through these programs.

Djibouti urges Francophonie to reinvest in peace at Phnom Penh summit

Djibouti's foreign minister told heads of state that lasting prosperity requires justice, dialogue and solidarity, not just the absence of conflict.

Djibouti's foreign minister, who is also the government spokesman, spoke at the Francophonie summit in Phnom Penh on behalf of President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh. The gathering, of heads of state and government from French-speaking countries, was held under the theme "Reinvesting in peace for shared prosperity and lasting stability."

The minister, Abdoulkader Houssein Omar, said peace means more than the absence of conflict. He said reinvesting in peace meant investing in education, health, employment and young people, and refusing to let inequality and exclusion become the breeding ground for division and instability.

The summit is due to name the next secretary-general of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) for the 2027-2030 term. Louise Mushikiwabo of Rwanda has held the post since 2019. Djibouti's minister asked for a more visible, inclusive and results-driven Francophonie and said Djibouti was fully available to help.

Why it mattersInvestment in schools, clinics and jobs is the policy Djibouti is asking the French-speaking bloc to back, and the new secretary-general will decide how far that agenda moves.
