Djibouti secures direct Hajj flights and medical care for 2027 pilgrims

Agreements with Saudi providers also cover lodging, meals and ground transport in Mecca and Medina, plus airport help for older and less mobile travellers.

Djibouti’s delegation has signed three agreements for the 2027 Hajj, covering ground services, medical care and direct flights to Medina and Jeddah. The contracts follow a memorandum on the season signed by Djibouti’s minister and Saudi Hajj Minister Tawfiq Al-Rabiah.

Saudi company Al Rifadah will provide lodging in Mecca and Medina, meals, ground transport and reception at Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifa. Al Abeer Medical Group will offer round-the-clock emergency consultations, specialist care, tests, imaging, hospital treatment and medicines through facilities in Mecca’s Al Aziziyah district and Medina. Care will be coordinated with Djibouti’s medical mission, with older people and those with chronic illnesses prioritised.

Saudi airline Flynas has agreed to direct flights to Medina and Jeddah at preferred times, simplified check-in and checked-baggage handling for five-litre Zamzam water containers. It also agreed to assist older passengers and people with reduced mobility at airports and on board.

Why it mattersOlder pilgrims and people with chronic illnesses are prioritised for medical services, while Flynas has agreed to assist older and less mobile passengers at airports and on board.

Djibouti recommends four more coaching days for 24 farm cooperatives

A review of the pilot backed by the International Finance Corporation proposes extra coaching, stronger trainer preparation and new teaching materials.

Djibouti’s agriculture value-chain project unit hosted a review of the Agricultural Leadership Programme, backed by the International Finance Corporation, the World Bank’s private-sector arm. The programme’s pilot covered 24 cooperatives across the country.

The review mission recommended extending the pilot, with four additional days of coaching for each cooperative, more training for trainers and new learning materials. Abdoulkader Issé, the programme’s focal point at the project unit, said the programme was intended to strengthen cooperatives and their economic weight.

Participants discussed which institution would lead the programme and how responsibilities and coordination would be organised. A second phase could start in early March 2027; they also raised a national study of cooperatives and the programme’s links to legal reforms underway.

Why it mattersThe 24 cooperatives covered by the pilot are slated for four more coaching days each under the mission’s recommendation.

Security · 2026-11-18

Six survivors reach Berbera after cargo boat from Djibouti sinks

The Somaliland Ports Authority said two crew members died and medical teams are caring for the six rescued survivors at the port.

Six crew members rescued after the cargo boat Alfatah sank near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait arrived at Somaliland’s Berbera Port on Nov. 17. The boat had sailed from Djibouti, according to Horn Diplomat.

The Somaliland Ports Authority said medical personnel and emergency teams were providing care to the survivors. Two other crew members died when the boat sank.

The arrival brought the six survivors to safety at Berbera, the port authority said. The report did not give further details about the boat or its voyage.
