Djibouti funds 15 community development projects

The Community Development Fund has now supported 52 local initiatives since its launch.

Djibouti's Agency for Social Development (ADDS) signed agreements today with 15 associations to fund local development projects. The initiative, backed by 20 million Djiboutian francs (FDJ), aims to support local initiatives and boost resident participation in neighborhood improvements.

The ceremony was attended by parliamentarians, local officials, and representatives from the French Development Agency (AFD). Speakers praised the selected projects and urged careful fund management and effective execution by the winning associations.

This marks the third call for projects, bringing the total number of funded initiatives by the Community Development Fund to 52, with a cumulative investment of 57.97 million FDJ since its inception.

Djibouti minister visits China for talks

Djibouti's foreign minister met with China's Wang Yi to deepen political and economic cooperation.

Djibouti's Minister of Foreign Affairs, Abdoulkader Houssein Omar, arrived in Beijing for an official visit at the invitation of Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi. The visit, scheduled to conclude on October 24, aims to strengthen political dialogue and deepen cooperation between the two governments.

Discussions focused on political, economic, and institutional partnership, as well as infrastructure, investments, and trade prospects. The ministers also addressed regional and international issues of mutual concern, agreeing to continue consultations in bilateral and multilateral forums. Omar was accompanied by a delegation including Djibouti's ambassador to China and senior officials from various ministries.

The delegation includes key figures such as the ambassador Abdallah Abdillahi Miguil, the secretary general of the foreign ministry Mohamed Ali Hassan, and the secretary general of the budget ministry Simon Mibrathu.

Djibouti ports authority signs training deal

The Djibouti Ports and Free Zones Authority will partner with the DRTC to enhance maritime and port professional skills.

Djibouti's Minister of Infrastructure and Equipment, Said Nouh Hassan, oversaw the signing of a partnership between the Djibouti Regional Training Centre (DRTC) and the Port Management Association of Eastern and Southern Africa (PMAESA). The agreement, signed by DRTC Executive Director Mina Houssein and PMAESA Secretary General Colonel André Ciseau, aims to improve port efficiency, sustainability, and resilience through enhanced training.

The cooperation framework will cover capacity building, technical expertise, and knowledge sharing in areas including port and maritime security, crisis management, environmental protection, and innovation. Colonel Ciseau stated the accord marks the start of a long-term partnership focused on developing human capabilities and stronger, more competitive institutions. Aboubaker Omar Hadi, president of the Djibouti Ports and Free Zones Authority (APZFD), highlighted that maritime trade accounts for 80% of global exchanges and that 10 landlocked countries rely on PMAESA ports.

The PMAESA, which includes Djibouti among its 19 member states, operates under a 2024-2028 strategic plan to foster port cooperation and maritime connectivity. Minister Said Nouh Hassan reaffirmed Djibouti's commitment to the DRTC's development and the full utilization of its expertise. He expressed hope for rapid implementation of joint training and practical initiatives to meet member states' needs.

Why it mattersDjibouti's ports authority is partnering with a regional body to train professionals, aiming to make ports more efficient and sustainable.

tropical storm rain risk for Djibouti

A tropical disturbance in the Arabian Sea is influencing weather in the Horn of Africa, with potential rain for Djibouti.

Djibouti could experience rain and thunderstorms on Friday and Saturday. A tropical disturbance formed on October 19 in the southwest Arabian Sea and moved towards Somalia, influencing weather in the Horn of Africa.

Cloud formations associated with the disturbance have reached the Gulf of Aden, opening the possibility of rainfall across Djibouti. The intensity and geographical distribution of the rain remain uncertain, depending on the disturbance's trajectory and local atmospheric conditions.

Meteorological services are monitoring the situation and may revise forecasts as the disturbance evolves. The coming days will identify zones susceptible to being hit.
