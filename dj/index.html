Djibouti clears fires from Ambouli cemetery

Minister Moumin Hassan Barreh announced the successful completion of a large-scale cleanup and security operation.

Djibouti's Minister of Waqf Affairs, Moumin Hassan Barreh, announced on November 7, 2026, that a large-scale cleanup and security operation at the Ambouli cemetery has successfully eliminated recurring fire hazards. The operation, ordered by President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh, aimed to restore the sacred character of the burial ground.

The cemetery, spanning over 700,000 square meters across Ambouli's districts 7 and 7 bis, and Djebel, was cleared of thick vegetation that fueled fires. Homeless individuals who had occupied the space and turned it into a public dump were also removed. The Ministry of the Interior supported the Waqf department in this effort.

Minister Barreh stated that the operation preserves the memory of citizens laid to rest in the cemetery, which is intimately linked to Djibouti's history. He urged the public to cooperate with authorities in maintaining the site's cleanliness and security.

Why it mattersThe Ambouli cemetery, a place of rest for Djiboutians and a site linked to the nation's history, was cleared of fire hazards and squatters.

Djibouti signs deal to clear rainwater collectors

The National Office for Water and Sanitation and the Urban Renewal Agency will work on flood prevention in four city districts.

The National Office for Water and Sanitation (ONEAD) and the Urban Renewal Agency (ARULoS) signed an agreement to clear rainwater collectors in Djibouti-ville. Mohamed Fouad Abdo of ONEAD and Abdourahman Ali Ahmed of ARULoS signed the deal at the Ministry of City. The work will focus on districts 3, 4, 5, and 6 to improve rainwater drainage, reduce water stagnation, and prevent flooding.

The agreement aims to restore the evacuation capacity for rainwater and protect residents and homes from floods. The Ministry of City, led by Amina Abdi Aden, and the Ministry of Agriculture, Water, Fisheries, and Livestock, led by Radwan Abdillahi Bahdon, will combine their skills and resources. This initiative is part of the government's efforts to better manage rainwater and strengthen urban infrastructure against bad weather.

Authorities stated the agreement shows a commitment to coordinating public institutions for sanitation, flood risk prevention, and population protection. The two agencies will use their operational structures to carry out the clearing work.

Why it mattersResidents of districts 3, 4, 5, and 6 in Djibouti-ville face reduced flood risk due to the agreement.
