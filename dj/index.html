118 Djiboutian youths receive vocational training certificates

The Agence Djiboutienne de Développement Social (ADDS) awarded certificates to 118 young people in Balbala.

118 young people in Balbala received vocational training certificates on November 9. The training is part of the Integrated Urban Development Program 2 Ter (PDUI2 Ter), supported by the French Development Agency (AFD). The ceremony was attended by the Minister of Social Affairs and Solidarity, Ouloufa Ismaïl Abdo, and the Minister of Youth and Culture, Fardoussa Moussa Egueh.

The ADDS views this certification as recognition of the importance placed on human capital. The agency considers investing in skills a way to fight vulnerability and promote socio-economic inclusion, especially for youth in disadvantaged areas. The graduates received encouragement to apply their new skills and seize future opportunities.

These trainings aim to equip young people with practical qualifications matching labor market needs, facilitating their access to employment and economic autonomy. The ADDS stated that urban development should include strengthening capacities and creating sustainable economic opportunities, not just infrastructure.

Why it matters118 young people in Balbala have gained practical skills and certifications that will help them find employment and achieve economic independence.

Somaliland ports body denies Djibouti claim on Berbera staff training

The Somaliland Ports Authority wants Djibouti's port agency to withdraw its claim and publish a correction.

11 senior staff at Berbera Port were said by Djibouti's port authority to have completed six months of engineering and operational training in Djibouti. The Somaliland Ports Authority has rejected that account and called it false.

In a statement posted on its official X account, the authority demanded that the Djibouti Ports & Free Zones Authority (DPFZA) withdraw the claim and issue a public correction. The dispute centres on the training record of port personnel at Berbera.

The packet does not report any further response from the DPFZA or any other body.

Why it mattersThe 11 senior Berbera Port staff named in the DPFZA claim are the people whose training record is now disputed in public.

Djibouti hosts IGAD meeting on regional migration fund

The sixth annual meeting, held November 10 and 11, 2026, reviewed the fund's progress and future strategy.

Djibouti hosted the sixth annual meeting of the IGAD Regional Migration Fund on November 10 and 11, 2026. Ambassador Guelleh Idris Omar, Director of International Organisations at the foreign ministry, opened the meeting on behalf of Djibouti, which holds the IGAD presidency.

Omar said migration in the Horn of Africa needs joint responses built on solidarity and shared responsibility. He praised the partnership with Germany's federal ministry for economic cooperation and development and the KfW development bank, which he described as essential to the fund's work. He noted investments in Ali-Sabieh and Ali-Addeh since the fund launched in 2019 and called for their sustainability.

Participants will review progress, operational constraints, financial and audit reports, and member states' responsibilities to make recommendations for the fund's next phase. IGAD's Deputy Executive Secretary, Muhammed Abdi Ware, speaking for Executive Secretary Workneh Gebeyehu, thanked Djibouti and said the meeting came two months after infrastructure built with fund support in Ali-Sabieh and Ali-Addeh was inaugurated and handed over.

Why it mattersMigrants, refugees and host communities in Ali-Sabieh and Ali-Addeh depend on the infrastructure and livelihood projects the fund finances, and the meeting will decide recommendations for its next phase.

Climate · 2026-11-12

Djibouti defends climate priorities at COP31

Djibouti's delegation, led by the Environment Minister, is in Antalya, T extü rkiye, for climate talks.

Djibouti is participating in the 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, T extü rkiye, which opened on Monday, November 9. The conference brings together global delegations to discuss climate change mitigation.

The Djiboutian delegation is led by SEM Mohamed Ahmed Awaleh, the Minister of Environment and Sustainable Development, and includes the Minister of Energy, SEM Dr Djama Mohamed Hassan, and other national officials. Djibouti, which is highly exposed to climate change impacts, aims to highlight its priorities on adaptation, energy transition, and climate finance.

Through its presence at COP31, Djibouti seeks to reaffirm its commitment to strengthening international partnerships and translating climate commitments into actions for sustainable development.

Why it mattersDjibouti is attending a global climate conference to advocate for its adaptation, energy transition, and climate finance needs.

Politics · 2026-11-12

Djibouti president inspects new conference centre

The new international conference centre features a main hall, press galleries, and four summit rooms.

President Guelleh visited the new Djibouti International Conference Centre on Tuesday, accompanied by his government ministers. He toured the modern facility, which incorporates the latest technologies. The centre is built to international standards and includes a large hall for plenary sessions and events.

The main hall has press galleries overlooking the debate area, allowing journalists to film proceedings. In addition to the plenary hall, the centre has four rooms specifically for heads of state and government meetings. It also contains five rooms for smaller, private meetings.

The building is equipped with an independent generator for continuous power supply. It also features a kitchen offering a variety of meals. The centre aims to host excellent and continuous work.

Why it mattersThe new conference centre will host meetings for heads of state and government.
