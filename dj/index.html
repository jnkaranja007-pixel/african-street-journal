Agriculture Minister Inspects Damerjog Export Center Operations

Minister Radwan Abdillahi Bahdon reviewed facilities and staff performance at the Damerjog quarantine and export center, emphasizing animal health and regional trade.

Djibouti's Minister of Agriculture, Radwan Abdillahi Bahdon, visited the Damerjog quarantine and export center, inspecting its bacteriology and virology laboratories, autopsy, incineration, and slaughter units, as well as enclosures for large and small ruminants. The visit also included the date palm section of the facility.

Minister Bahdon acknowledged the work of veterinary doctors, technicians, and ministry teams, commending their diligence in animal health surveillance, export livestock control, and public health risk prevention. He stressed the importance of coordination among services involved in livestock management for effective quarantine, sanitary control, and export operations.

The Minister encouraged the center's teams to continue their efforts to strengthen existing systems and enhance Djibouti's position in regional animal trade, while adhering to sanitary standards. This focus aims to ensure the country's role in livestock exports meets international health requirements.

Why it mattersThe Minister's visit directly affects livestock exporters and veterinary staff by reinforcing operational standards and coordination for animal trade.

Djibouti urges parents to secure e-cigarettes from youth access

Parents are encouraged to keep electronic cigarettes out of reach of children and monitor behavioral changes to prevent underage vaping.

Djibouti authorities are calling for increased vigilance from parents and educators to combat the growing normalization of vaping among minors. The Agence Djiboutienne d'Information reports that young people are acquiring e-cigarettes directly, finding unattended devices, or receiving them from peers who obtained them from vendors disregarding age restrictions.

The issue extends beyond direct purchases, as some minors share e-cigarettes found or left by parents in school settings. This circulation among the youth highlights a collective responsibility for prevention, with parents asked to secure devices and observe their children for any changes in behavior.

Schools are also tasked with a crucial role, as teachers and administrators can reinforce awareness and educate students about the dangers of vaping. The message to young people emphasizes that e-cigarettes are not toys and should not become a habit, regardless of perceived harmlessness, peer influence, or flavor.

Why it mattersThis story affects parents and educators who are being asked to take specific actions to prevent underage vaping, and young people who are the target of prevention efforts.

Djibouti students gain admission to French universities

Twenty-three students from Djibouti's Lycée d’Excellence will pursue higher education in several French cities following two years of intensive preparation.

Twenty-three students from the Lycée d’Excellence in Djibouti have been admitted to major French universities. These students are preparing to travel to cities including Brest, Nice, Clermont-Ferrand, Saint-Quentin, Saint-Omer, Douai, and Strasbourg to continue their studies.

This achievement follows two years of dedicated work and perseverance by the students, supported by an accompanying program at the Lycée d’Excellence. The preparatory classes at the school receive assistance from France, contributing to the students' success.

Officials from the institution praised the commitment of families and teachers, along with the support from the Ministry of National Education and Vocational Training (MENFOP), the French Institute of Djibouti, and Campus France Djibouti, for these results.

Why it mattersThis event directly impacts the 23 students and their families by providing access to international higher education opportunities.

Djibouti Port welcomes largest ever container ship

The port of Djibouti successfully docked the "Benjamin Franklin," a container ship with a capacity of 18,000 twenty-foot equivalent units (EVP), marking the largest vessel ever.

Djibouti's ports demonstrated their capability to handle very large ships by accommodating the "Benjamin Franklin," a container ship measuring 400 meters in length. This operation required the mobilization of four latest-generation tugboats, each with a traction power of 65 tonnes, and was overseen by a Djibouti port pilot.

The President of the Ports and Free Zones Authority of Djibouti (APZFD) stated on X that the port is capable of handling and maneuvering large vessels for clients, attributing this success to the skills, know-how, and expertise of their human capital. The tugboats Omar Dankali, Barkat Hamadou, Farah Hassan, and Saad Warsama were specifically deployed for this maneuver.

The successful docking of the "Benjamin Franklin," which is among the world's largest container ships, showcases the nautical and human resources available at Djibouti's ports. This event highlights the port's operational capacity for accommodating significant maritime traffic and demonstrates its ability to manage complex shipping operations.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects shipping companies and port workers by demonstrating Djibouti's capacity to handle larger vessels, potentially increasing trade volume and operational demands.

Djibouti police detain over 200 people in security operation

Police in Djibouti arrested more than 200 individuals and seized bladed weapons during an operation aimed at reinforcing security measures following recent public disturbances.

Djibouti police have detained over 200 people and confiscated bladed weapons as part of intensified security operations across various neighborhoods. The police stated these actions are a response to recent public order disturbances and involve round-the-clock interventions.

Targeted operations are being conducted in areas identified as sensitive, with police announcing a continuous presence 24 hours a day, seven days a week. The individuals apprehended were transported to the Nagad transit center for identity verification.

For those concerned, procedures for repatriation to the border are expected to follow the identity checks. This initiative reflects a broader effort by authorities to enhance security and address public order issues within the city.

Why it mattersResidents of Djibouti are directly affected by increased police presence and arrests, potentially impacting their daily routines and freedom of movement.
