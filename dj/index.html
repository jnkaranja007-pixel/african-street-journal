Djibouti revises labour code with social partners

Minister Yonis Ali Guedi opened talks on November 8 to update labour laws for modern employment realities.

Djibouti's Minister of Labour, Yonis Ali Guedi, opened a four-day revision of the country's labour code on November 8. The workshop, involving government, union, and employer representatives, aims to create a modern legal framework for employment.

The revision addresses evolving job markets and new professional relationships, seeking a balanced law that protects workers' rights and employers' interests while encouraging investment and job creation. International partners, including the European Union and the International Labour Organization, pledged support for this modernization effort.

Discussions focus on worker and employer obligations, conflict prevention, and enforcement of social legislation. The final recommendations will lead to the code's finalization and institutional adoption process.

Why it mattersThe revision of the labour code will affect all workers and employers in Djibouti by updating their rights and obligations.

Somaliland Ports Authority rejects Djibouti training claim

The Somaliland Ports Authority demanded a public correction from Djibouti's authority over staff training allegations.

The Somaliland Ports Authority has rejected a claim by the Djibouti Ports & Free Zones Authority (DPFZA) that 11 senior Berbera Port staff received six months of engineering and operational training in Djibouti.

The Somaliland authority demanded the withdrawal of the claim and a public correction in a statement posted on its official X account.

This dispute arises from allegations made by the DPFZA regarding training provided to Berbera Port personnel.

Djibouti president tours new conference centre

President Guelleh inspected the modern facility equipped with new technologies and a main hall for large meetings.

President Guelleh visited Djibouti's new international conference centre on November 10, 2026, accompanied by his government.

The modern infrastructure includes a main hall for large gatherings, a press gallery, and four rooms for heads of state and government meetings. It also has five rooms for smaller meetings, spacious offices, and equipped stands for translators.

The centre is connected to an autonomous generator and features a kitchen offering a variety of meals.

Why it mattersThe new international conference centre will host state and government meetings, impacting diplomatic engagements.

118 Djiboutian youth receive vocational training certificates

The certificates were awarded to young people in Balbala as part of a development program supported by France.

118 young people in Balbala received their vocational training certificates on November 10, 2026. These trainings are part of the Integrated Urban Development Program 2 Ter (PDUI2 Ter), supported by the French Development Agency (AFD). The ceremony was attended by the Minister of Social Affairs and Solidarity, Ouloufa Ismaïl Abdo, and the Minister of Youth and Culture, Fardoussa Moussa Egueh.

The ADDS, represented by its Director General Mahdi Mohamed Djama, views the awarding of these certificates as a recognition of the importance placed on human capital. The agency stated that investing in skills is a way to combat vulnerability and promote socio-economic inclusion, especially for youth in disadvantaged neighborhoods. The goal of the training is to equip young people with practical skills needed by the job market to facilitate their employment and economic autonomy.

The PDUI2 Ter aims to go beyond infrastructure development, focusing on improving living conditions through capacity building and creating sustainable economic opportunities. For the 118 recipients, the diploma represents acknowledgment of their efforts and a step towards new professional prospects. The French Ambassador to Djibouti, Jérôme Bresson, and the mayor of Balbala, Mohamed Hassan Said, were also present.

Why it matters118 young Djiboutians in Balbala have gained vocational skills and certificates that will help them find employment and achieve economic independence.

Djibouti gendarmes arrest extortion gang

Three units apprehended suspects who used weapons to steal money and goods.

Djibouti's Gendarmerie arrested an alleged extortion gang after operations involving three units: the PK12 Nord Territorial Brigade, the Road Safety Squadron (ESR), and the Research and Documentation Section (SRD). The gang operated in the PK12, PK13, Nagad, and Barwaqo 2 areas, threatening victims with weapons to take their money or belongings.

During the operation, gendarmes seized several bladed weapons and one firearm, which was found without a magazine or ammunition. The suspects were presented to the justice system following the investigation and have been placed under a detention order at Gabode civil prison.

The SRD, which specializes in complex judicial investigations, supported the inquiries that led to these arrests. The Gendarmerie's action targeted a group accused of preying on residents in several Djibouti neighborhoods.

Why it mattersResidents in the PK12, PK13, Nagad, and Barwaqo 2 areas are no longer directly threatened by this alleged extortion gang.
