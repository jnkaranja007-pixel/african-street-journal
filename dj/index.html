Djibouti opens Al-Shifa hospital on Route de Venise

The public hospital is run by a private operator and aims to keep patients from being sent abroad for treatment.

Djibouti has opened the new Al-Shifa hospital on the Route de Venise in Djibouti-Ville, a public project entrusted to private management. The state wants the facility to modernise the country's medical offer and reduce emergency medical evacuations to other countries.

The project follows two earlier reforms: the creation of the National Social Security Fund (CNSS) in 2008 and the launch of universal health insurance (AMU) in 2014. The hospital's opening in 2026 came during the long rule of President Ismaël Omar Guelleh.

The government presents the hospital as a regional medical hub for the sub-region. The report does not give the number of beds, the cost of the project or the date services begin for patients.

Djibouti hosts African water and sanitation experts for a 98th council session

Water utility leaders, regulators, researchers and donors are meeting in the capital to discuss desalination, digital networks and wastewater reuse across the continent.

Djibouti's capital is hosting the 98th session of the Strategic and Technological Council (CST) of the African Water and Sanitation Association (AAEA), which runs until November 17. The theme is "Innovative solutions for sanitation and water security in Africa." Agriculture, Water, Fisheries, Livestock and Marine Resources Minister Radwan Abdillahi Bahdon opened the sessions, alongside the council's president, Mahmood Lutaaya, the AAEA's executive director, Olivier Gosso, and Mohamed Fouad Abdo, director general of Djibouti's National Water and Sanitation Office (ONEAD).

Delegates include executives of public water companies, regulators, researchers and donors, who all point to the same pressures: climate change, population growth and fast urbanisation are straining already fragile water resources. The first day covered seawater desalination powered by renewable energy, and the digitising of networks through the internet of things, remote meter reading and pipeline monitoring sensors. Later sessions are due to cover the safe reuse of treated wastewater for irrigation and industry, and sanitation systems designed for African conditions rather than imported models.

The association's eight specialist groups, covering data, water loss, climate, sanitation, financing, water quality, service management and rural water, are taking part. A forum for affiliate members and a round table on strategic dialogue complete the programme. Djibouti has very little natural fresh water and has relied on desalination and renewable energy to supply its population.

Health · 2026-11-17

Djibouti ministers launch anti-sedentary campaign

Health, Labour, and Sports ministers took health checks to combat office-based inactivity.

Djibouti's Minister of Labour Yonis Ali Guedi, Health Minister Mouna Osman Aden, and Secretary of State for Sports Fatouma Ali Abdallah launched a campaign against sedentary lifestyles in the workplace on Sunday. The initiative, supported by the World Health Organization (WHO) representative in Djibouti, aims to address risks associated with inactivity, such as hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular diseases. Stands were set up in the Ministerial City for agents and visitors to measure blood pressure and check blood sugar levels.

The three ministers underwent health checks themselves as a signal to civil servants to monitor their health more closely. Organizers intend to promote regular physical activity and encourage the adoption of healthy habits in professional daily life. The government stated that linking the ministries of Labour, Health, and Sports reflects a commitment to making worker health a core component of improving working conditions.

The campaign is scheduled to run until Thursday, November 19. Non-communicable diseases, including cardiovascular conditions, cancers, diabetes, and chronic respiratory illnesses, account for approximately three-quarters of global deaths, according to the WHO. The initiative seeks to promote physical activity and good habits within the professional environment.

Why it mattersCivil servants in Djibouti can now have their blood pressure and blood sugar checked at their workplace as part of a new health campaign running until November 19.
