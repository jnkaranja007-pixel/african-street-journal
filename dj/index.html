Health · 2026-11-16

Djibouti opens Al-Shifa hospital in Djibouti-Ville to cut medical evacuations

The public project, run with private partners, is meant to make Djibouti a medical hub for the subregion.

Djibouti has opened the new Al-Shifa hospital on the Route de Venise in Djibouti-Ville, a public project entrusted to the private sector. The government says the hospital will modernise the country's health services and reduce the number of patients sent abroad for treatment.

The opening comes during the long rule of President Ismaël Omar Guelleh. Health reforms in past decades included the creation of the National Social Security Fund (CNSS) in 2008 and the launch of universal health insurance (AMU) in 2014.

The hospital is designed to serve as a regional medical centre, according to the report, which describes Djibouti's aim of becoming a subregional health hub.

Djibouti's telecoms regulator meets counterparts from Morocco, France and Romania

The meetings took place in Doha during the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) Plenipotentiary Conference, which opened on 9 November.

Djibouti's telecommunications regulator, led by Dr Kassim Issak Osman, held three bilateral meetings in Doha with counterparts from Morocco, France and Romania. The aim is to anchor the regulator in international cooperation networks while the telecoms sector changes quickly.

Dr Osman first met the director general of Morocco's National Telecommunications Regulatory Agency (ANRT), and the two sides said they want to strengthen an existing cooperation, share experiences and find new areas to work together. He then discussed current regulatory challenges with a representative of France's electronic communications regulator, ARCEP, including ways to step up exchanges of expertise and good practice.

He also spoke with the vice-president of Romania's Regulatory Council, comparing the two authorities' experience on major telecoms regulation issues. The ITU Plenipotentiary Conference, the union's supreme body, meets every four years to set its direction and elect its leaders, and delegations also use it to forge partnerships outside plenary sessions.

Why it mattersDjibouti's regulator is building partnerships with three peer regulators while it takes part in the ITU conference that sets the agency's direction and elects its leaders.

Politics · 2026-11-16

Djibouti urges Francophonie summit to reinvest in peace

Djibouti's foreign minister told heads of state at the Francophonie summit in Phnom Penh that lasting prosperity needs justice, dialogue and solidarity.

Djibouti's foreign minister, who is also the government spokesman, spoke on behalf of President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh at the Francophonie summit, held under the theme "Reinvesting in peace for shared prosperity and lasting stability." He told leaders that peace means more than the absence of conflict, and that no lasting prosperity is possible without justice, dialogue and solidarity.

The minister said reinvesting in peace means investing in education, health, employment and young people, and refusing to let inequality and exclusion become the seeds of division and instability. Abdoulkader Houssein Omar urged the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) to become a more visible, inclusive and results-focused political, cultural and human space, and said Djibouti stands ready to contribute.

The summit is due to name the next secretary-general of the OIF for the 2027-2030 term. Louise Mushikiwabo, from Rwanda, has held the post since 2019.

Why it mattersDjibouti's call for education, health and jobs spending at the summit sets out the priorities it will press within the OIF's next leadership.
