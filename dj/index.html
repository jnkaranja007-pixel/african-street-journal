Djibouti parliament approves cancer centre loan

Djibouti's parliament has adopted nine legislative texts, including a loan for a new cancer treatment centre.

Djibouti's parliament convened for its second ordinary session, with President Dileita Mohamed Dileita opening the plenary meeting attended by Prime Minister Abdoulkader Kamil Mohamed and government ministers. Lawmakers welcomed the new Minister of Defence, Aden Ali Mahamade, who was present for the first time since his appointment. The assembly's agenda included a reform of the associations' regime, the ratification of a loan agreement with the Islamic Development Bank (IDB), and the amended finance law for 2026.

The parliament also approved the financial statements for seven public institutions, covering 2024 for the ADR, SIAF, and Electricité de Djibouti (EDD), and 2025 for ADDS, AID, ARULOS, and the Diwan of Wakf Properties. Deputies questioned the executive on the new framework for associations and the conditions of the IDB financing. Ministers responded to all inquiries regarding these matters and the management of public entities' accounts.

Prime Minister Abdoulkader Kamil Mohamed highlighted the future oncology centre, intended to improve early cancer detection and treatment locally, thereby reducing costly overseas medical evacuations. He acknowledged the crucial role of the National Social Security Fund (CNSS) as a partner in this health initiative. Discussions also covered energy production, hydrocarbons, and renewable energy development, with an update on the improved financial standing of SIAF and Electricité de Djibouti.

Why it mattersThe approved loan will fund a new cancer centre, aiming to improve local treatment and reduce expensive overseas evacuations.

Djibouti puts its connectivity figures before the ITU in Qatar

Around 70% of the population now uses the internet, and 350 kilometres of fibre are being laid towards the north of the country.

At the plenipotentiary conference of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), held in the Qatari capital, Djibouti's Minister of Communication, in charge of Posts and Telecommunications, Mohamed Abdoulkader Moussa, told delegates that developing countries should be full actors in designing new technologies, not simply users of them.

He said telecommunications have become a strategic lever for development and independence for states. The minister linked the sector to the national Vision 2035 led by President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh, and recalled that the National Assembly adopted a digital code in June 2025 covering electronic communications, personal data protection, cybersecurity, e-commerce, online administration and consumer rights.

The minister acknowledged that closing gaps between regions and reaching the most remote areas remains a challenge. Djibouti has also adopted a national emergency telecommunications plan (NETP) to keep communications running during crises, with satellite technology presented as a complement for isolated areas.

Why it mattersGaps in access between regions and remote areas are the problem the government must still close, and the emergency plan is meant to keep communications running when crises hit.

Djibouti and Qatar discuss data centre cooperation in Doha

Djibouti's communications minister, Helem, held talks with his Qatari counterpart, Al Mannai, on the sidelines of an ITU conference.

Djibouti's minister Helem met Qatari minister Al Mannai in Doha, the Qatari capital, while attending the Plenipotentiary Conference of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), the United Nations agency for digital technologies, which opened on Monday. The two sides reviewed the state of their cooperation on three topics: capacity building, communications and the development of data centres.

Helem praised the quality of the cooperation between the two countries and reaffirmed Djibouti's commitment to consolidating the partnership. Al Mannai praised the close and productive ties and said the Qatari government was determined to strengthen the cooperation further. Djibouti's ambassador to Qatar, Dayib Doubad Robleh, attended the meeting.

The ITU conference takes place every four years and is the union's supreme decision-making body, bringing together representatives of its 194 member states at the exhibition and congress centre in Doha. Delegates are due to elect the organisation's leadership for the next four years.

Why it mattersDelegates at the Doha conference will choose the ITU's leadership for the next four years, a decision that sets the agency's digital priorities for that period.
