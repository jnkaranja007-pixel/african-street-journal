Djibouti allocates 1.8 billion francs to youth entrepreneurship

The government approved a new organic law to modernize state budget management and support young entrepreneurs.

Djibouti's President chaired a Council of Ministers meeting on October 27, 2026, where 1.8 billion Djiboutian francs were exceptionally allocated to the Djibouti Economic Development Fund (FDED). This funding aims to boost national entrepreneurship, combat youth unemployment, and diversify the economy by supporting projects in agriculture, livestock, fishing, tourism, renewable energy, logistics, digital services, and the circular economy.

The President emphasized facilitating young entrepreneurs' access to finance and stressed the FDED's role in providing training, technical assistance, and follow-up. Success will be measured by the viability of created businesses, job generation, and their contribution to national prosperity.

The council also approved a new organic law on finance laws (LOLF) to modernize state budget preparation, execution, and control. This reform, aligned with Djibouti Vision 2035 and the ADEEG 2025-2030 national development plan, replaces a 2000 law and aims to enhance budget discipline, public spending efficiency, and financial transparency.

Why it mattersDjiboutian youth seeking to start businesses will receive financial support and guidance.

Djibouti youth train in digital governance

Nearly 30 young Djiboutians aged 17-24 are learning digital decision-making and negotiation skills.

A bootcamp to train Djiboutian youth in digital governance and telecommunications diplomacy began on October 27 and runs until October 29 at the Ayla Grand Hôtel. The Ministry of Communication, Posts and Telecommunications, in partnership with the Arab Regional Office of the International Telecommunication Union, organized the training for nearly 30 participants aged 17 to 24 from Djiboutian higher education institutions.

Aicha Mohamed Robleh, advisor to the minister, opened the event, joined by Hawa Ali from the University of Djibouti and Feycal Kaireh Chirdon, Director of ICT Development at the MCPT. The program aims to equip young people with knowledge of multi-stakeholder digital governance and the diplomatic aspects of telecommunications, including policy development and negotiation techniques.

The bootcamp also features sessions with the national digital ecosystem, where participants will meet Djiboutian entrepreneurs and learn about local initiatives like Limo and Hoom. The D-Money payment solution was also presented. Organizers believe it is essential to involve youth in decision-making processes as digital technologies grow in importance in international relations and public policy.

Why it mattersDjiboutian youth aged 17-24 are being trained in digital governance and negotiation skills.

Djibouti premier invites Chinese investment in ports

Prime Minister urges Chinese firms to invest in Djibouti's ports and industry, highlighting strategic location.

Djibouti's Prime Minister has called on Chinese companies to invest in the nation's ports and industrial sectors. The appeal was made ahead of a meeting scheduled for November 7 in Jinhua, Zhejiang province.

The Prime Minister, speaking for the Djiboutian government and people, described China as Djibouti's most reliable partner. He noted the strong diplomatic ties established in 1979 and elevated to a global strategic partnership in 2024, referencing the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation summit's impact.

Djibouti, positioned as the "guardian of the Bab-el-Mandeb strait," aims to leverage its strategic maritime location for port logistics and trade services, the pillars of its national economy under the "Vision 2035" plan. The country also seeks to develop solar and geothermal energy for a low-carbon transition.

Why it mattersDjiboutian businesses and the national economy will be affected by Chinese investment in ports and industry.

Djiboutian students graduate from security university

The Naif Arab University for Security Sciences updated its strategic plan to prepare graduates for modern security challenges.

Djiboutian students were among those who graduated from the Naif Arab University for Security Sciences in Saudi Arabia. The ceremony was attended by Djibouti's Ambassador to Saudi Arabia, Diya-eddine Saïd Bamakhrama, who also serves as the dean of the diplomatic corps.

University President Abdulmajid ben Abdallah Al-Bunyan highlighted the institution's nearly 50 years of work in Arab security cooperation. He noted the university's programs are recognized internationally and it is part of the United Nations Network of Institutes. The university's Supreme Council also approved an updated strategic plan.

This updated plan aims to better equip graduates for new security challenges and promote responsible use of modern technologies, including artificial intelligence, while adhering to ethical principles.

Why it mattersDjiboutian students have graduated from a security sciences university.

Djibouti minister consults on new labour code

Social partners will refine the draft code during a workshop from November 8 to 11.

Djibouti's Minister of Labour met with social partners on October 26 to discuss the new Labour Code. The updated code aims to modernise labour relations and adapt to the evolving job market. The minister invited feedback on the draft text to incorporate worker and employer concerns.

A workshop is scheduled from November 8 to 11 to further discuss contributions and prepare the draft for the CONTESS committee. This initiative is part of a broader legal reform including civil servant regulations and a new collective agreement. The minister stressed the importance of dialogue and cooperation.

The Ministry of Labour is committed to modernising labour laws and strengthening social dialogue. This process seeks to ensure professional relations are based on mutual respect for rights and obligations.

Why it mattersDjibouti's workers and employers will be directly affected by the new labour code and collective agreement.
