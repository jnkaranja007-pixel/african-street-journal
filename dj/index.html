Djibouti hosts EU security and defence ambassadors

Djibouti's foreign minister and interim interior minister met with EU envoys to discuss security cooperation.

Djibouti hosted ambassadors from the EU's Council of the European Union for Political and Security Committee (COPS) on April 11, 2026. Minister of Foreign Affairs Abdoulkader Houssein Omar and interim Minister of Interior Omar Abdi Said received the delegation led by Ambassador Lene Vensild at the Diplomatic Studies Institute.

The EU COPS delegation represents the bloc's central body for common security and defence policy. Discussions focused on deepening security and military cooperation between Djibouti and the EU, and on regional and international challenges. Both sides described the talks as "in-depth."

Djibouti, located at the entrance to the Bab el-Mandeb strait, is a long-standing partner for Europeans in the Horn of Africa. The visit signifies the importance both Djibouti and the EU place on their partnership and joint initiatives for regional peace and stability.

Djibouti oil jetty begins operations

The Damerjog Liquid Bulk Port facility aims to become a redistribution hub for East Africa.

The Damerjog oil jetty, operated by Damerjog Liquid Bulk Port (DLBP), a subsidiary of GHIH, has begun operations. Commercial oil storage depots are also developing within the Damerjog industrial park.

French group Rubis, which specialises in oil storage and distribution, visited the site to discuss cooperation. The goal is to make Damerjog a redistribution platform for the Horn of Africa and beyond.

Djibouti is developing infrastructure for liquid bulk at Damerjog, with the French group Rubis already active in East Africa.

Djibouti receives 40% of Heron Island naval base from France

Djibouti's interim defense minister accepted the keys to the naval base installations from the French ambassador.

Djibouti has taken back 40% of the Heron Island naval base, with interim Defense Minister Omar Abdi Said receiving the keys from French Ambassador Jérôme Bresson on March 11, 2026. The handover occurred at the general staff headquarters of the armed forces, attended by Chief of Staff General Zakaria Cheick Ibrahim.

This transfer aligns with the defense cooperation treaty between France and Djibouti and follows joint technical studies by the French forces stationed in Djibouti and the Djiboutian army. A financing agreement signed at the ceremony will fund infrastructure projects to boost the Djiboutian army's operational capabilities.

The defense relationship, strengthened since the treaty's signing on July 24, 2024, saw its first strategic dialogue on June 11, 2026. This dialogue resulted in a two-year partnership plan for 2026-2028.

Why it mattersDjibouti's military capabilities are being enhanced through French cooperation and infrastructure projects.

Somali parliament speaker tours Doraleh terminal

Djibouti's port authority details cooperation with Somali ports, including equipment donations and training.

Somali Parliament Speaker Abdoulkadir Mohamed Nour visited Djibouti's Doraleh Container Terminal and the DIFTZ on March 11, 2026. He was accompanied by Djiboutian National Assembly President Dileita Mohamed Dileita and met with Aboubaker Omar Hadi, president of the Port and Free Zones Authority of Djibouti (APZFD).

At the Doraleh terminal, officials from the Terminal Management Company (SGTD) showcased infrastructure, equipment, and operational capabilities, emphasizing Djibouti's role as a logistics hub in the Horn of Africa. Discussions at the DIFTZ focused on strengthening cooperation between the APZFD and Somali ports.

Djibouti has previously donated two tugboats, the 'Bab el Mandeb' and 'Moussa Bouh', along with container handling equipment to Mogadishu. A Djiboutian technical team was deployed for two years, and port agents received training. In Bossaso, the APZFD conducted bathymetric surveys, improved navigation aids, and provided a tugboat and pushcat, alongside studies on port tariffs and regulations.

Why it mattersSomali port officials and professionals are receiving training and equipment from Djibouti to improve their operations.
