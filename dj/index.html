Djibouti Minister Urges African Energy Skills Growth

Djibouti's Energy Minister Djama Mohamed Hassan advocated for a sustainable capacity-building approach to Africa's energy transition, emphasizing skills development.

Djibouti's Minister of Energy, Djama Mohamed Hassan, has called for a stronger focus on developing skills across Africa to support the continent's energy transition. Speaking at a meeting organized under the patronage of Zimbabwean President Emmerson Mnangagwa, Hassan argued that successful energy transitions require more than just infrastructure deployment. He stressed the need to build lasting institutional capacities that extend beyond short-term training programs.

The minister proposed a shift from individual training to establishing a comprehensive ecosystem for continuous learning. This ecosystem would involve collaboration between universities, public institutions, and development partners. Discussions at the event, which included energy ministers, experts, and investors from African Alliance member countries, centered on financing solar projects, rural electrification, and technological innovation.

The gathering also explored ways to enhance South-South cooperation and leverage solar energy for economic development. Key discussion areas included strengthening skills through STAR-C centers, solarizing agriculture, financing projects via the Africa Solar Facility, and advancing technological innovation in areas like artificial intelligence and digitalization, alongside electric mobility and energy storage.

Why it mattersDjibouti's call for enhanced skills development in the energy sector directly impacts investors by highlighting the need for skilled labor to implement and maintain solar projects and infrastructure.

Djibouti Red Cross Transfers Funds to Storm Victims

The Djibouti Red Cross has disbursed thirteen million, four hundred forty thousand Djiboutian francs to households in Mouloud affected by recent violent winds.

The Djibouti Red Cross has mobilized thirteen million, four hundred forty thousand Djiboutian francs to aid residents of Mouloud following severe wind damage. Each affected household received eighty thousand Djiboutian francs to assist with roof repairs and rehabilitation. This financial assistance is part of a multisectoral response to the violent winds in Mouloud, implemented by the Djibouti Red Cross with support from the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies.

The disbursement followed extensive field work by Red Cross teams and volunteers, who coordinated with local authorities and community structures. They assessed damaged homes, identified eligible households, and verified data, prioritizing vulnerable families such as those headed by women, large families, the elderly, people with disabilities, and households with limited economic resources.

The ceremony marking the transfer was attended by various officials, including the Secretary General of the Djibouti Red Cross, Amina Houssein Farah, and local representatives from the Dikhil region. This initiative aims to provide direct financial support for rebuilding damaged roofs and restoring shelter for affected families.

Why it mattersFarmers may be indirectly affected if damaged roofs impact storage or processing of agricultural goods, though the aid is for household repairs.

Djibouti Minister Inspects Quarantine Center, Boosts Export Trade

Minister Radwan Abdillahi Bahdon reviewed animal health facilities and stressed coordination to enhance Djibouti's regional livestock trade position.

Minister Radwan Abdillahi Bahdon visited the Damerjog quarantine and export center, inspecting key facilities including animal laboratories, autopsy units, and livestock enclosures for ruminants. The minister also reviewed operations related to date palm cultivation at the center. He acknowledged the diligent work of veterinarians and ministry teams in sanitary surveillance and export control.

During his visit, the minister emphasized the critical need for inter-service coordination within the livestock management chain. He stated that this cooperation is vital for ensuring the effectiveness of quarantine, sanitary control, and export processes. The minister encouraged the center's staff to maintain their efforts in strengthening existing systems.

Minister Bahdon urged the teams to continue their work to consolidate current measures and bolster Djibouti's standing in regional animal commerce. He highlighted the importance of adhering to sanitary standards in these operations. The visit aimed to assess and reinforce the country's capacity for safe and efficient livestock exports.

Why it mattersThe minister's focus on export controls and regional trade positioning directly impacts the potential for increased livestock sales and market access for Djiboutian businesses.

Djibouti Health Minister Meets New UNFPA Chief

The meeting focused on strengthening cooperation in maternal and reproductive health, family planning, and youth health services.

Djibouti's Minister of Health held a first meeting with the new head of the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) office to review cooperation prospects. The UNFPA is a long-standing partner of the Djiboutian government.

Discussions centered on maternal and reproductive health, family planning, and the health of adolescents and young people. Both sides explored ways to enhance access for women, girls, and communities to quality health services.

The Minister praised the UNFPA's commitment and reaffirmed the authorities' desire to consolidate the partnership to sustainably improve the population's health and well-being. The new UNFPA representative pledged organizational support for national priorities and ongoing actions.

Why it mattersThe meeting between the Minister of Health and the UNFPA chief aims to improve access to quality health services for women, girls, and communities, impacting reproductive health and family planning.

Djibouti Students Gain Admission to French Higher Education Institutions

Twenty-three students from the Lycée d’Excellence de Djibouti have been accepted into prestigious French universities, marking a significant academic achievement.

Twenty-three students from Djibouti's Lycée d’Excellence have secured places in prominent French higher education institutions, with plans to commence their studies in various French cities including Brest, Nice, Clermont-Ferrand, Saint-Quentin, Saint-Omer, Douai, and Strasbourg. This success follows two years of intensive preparation by the students, characterized by hard work and perseverance. The achievement also highlights the support systems in place at the lycée, particularly its preparatory classes which receive backing from France.

The results were acknowledged by the school's leadership, who expressed gratitude for the dedication of families and teachers. Support from the Ministry of National Education and Vocational Training (MENFOP), the French Institute of Djibouti, and Campus France Djibouti was also recognized. These institutions play a crucial role in facilitating such academic pathways for Djiboutian students.

The students are expected to depart for France in the coming days to begin their advanced studies. This influx of Djiboutian students into the French education system underscores the strong educational ties between the two nations and the effectiveness of specialized preparatory programs in achieving international academic success.

Why it mattersDjiboutian students are affected by gaining access to higher education in France, which offers advanced academic opportunities and international exposure.
