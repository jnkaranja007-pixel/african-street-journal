Djibouti President Pushes Economic Reforms and Infrastructure Investment

President Guelleh emphasized engagement, action, and results for the government's return to work amidst regional uncertainties.

President Guelleh has called for a new government phase focused on engagement, action, and results, particularly in light of regional tensions affecting the Red Sea. He stressed the need for vigilance and determination while maintaining focus on national priorities. The President highlighted that investments in port, rail, road, energy, and telecommunications infrastructure have strengthened Djibouti's strategic position and attractiveness.

Efforts to consolidate the macroeconomic framework and continue public finance reforms aim to enhance efficiency, transparency, and accountability. The President also emphasized diversifying the economy by strengthening logistics, telecommunications, and services, while developing new growth sectors like light industries, digital technology, tourism, renewable energy, and fishing.

Innovation, entrepreneurship, and attracting productive investments are crucial for job creation and boosting national economic competitiveness and resilience. In response to the geopolitical context, the President also called for reinforcing internal security, protecting vital infrastructure, controlling borders, and addressing new threats like cybercrime and disinformation.

Why it mattersDjibouti's government is prioritizing infrastructure and economic diversification to attract investment and create jobs.

Djibouti Customs Modernizes with Electronic Single Window

A new electronic single window system aims to simplify customs procedures, reduce delays, and improve transparency in trade operations.

Djibouti's national customs administration is accelerating its modernization by establishing an electronic single window system. This initiative, developed with partners including the Ministry of Commerce, the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) Secretariat, and the Korean agency Cupia, seeks to streamline trade processes. Cupia has prior experience implementing similar systems in Ethiopia, Tanzania, and Ghana.

The launch of this project was marked by a workshop held at the People's Palace, attended by key figures such as Mr. Ali Abdou Daoud, Secretary General of the Ministry of Commerce, and Mr. Abdourahman Izzi Awad, a senior customs official. Representatives from AfCFTA and Cupia were also present, underscoring the collaborative nature of the project.

According to Mr. Izzi, the goal extends beyond enhancing the current SYDONIAWORLD system. The electronic single window is intended to simplify customs and trade formalities, cut down processing times, boost transparency, and increase the predictability of operations. This aligns with the AfCFTA's objectives of fostering economic integration and facilitating intra-African trade.

Why it mattersBusinesses involved in import and export will experience simplified customs procedures, reduced delays, and increased predictability in their operations.

Djibouti AI Adoption Accelerates Beyond Measurement

The nation is transitioning from discovering artificial intelligence to integrating it into daily life and administration.

Djibouti is moving beyond the initial discovery phase of artificial intelligence and is now increasingly integrating AI into daily life, administration, higher education, and businesses. However, the pace of AI adoption is outpacing the country's ability to measure its impact, according to Dr. Houssein Ahmed Assoweh. A national survey to precisely quantify AI usage, its applications, and effects among Djiboutians is currently lacking, making it scientifically challenging to provide exact figures.

Favorable conditions for AI diffusion are present, including strong international connectivity, the expansion of digital public services, increasing use of digital platforms, and administrative modernization. The University of Djibouti, through structures like the Centre for Research in Mathematics and Digitalization, is developing skills in mathematical modeling, data science, and artificial intelligence. Scientific work is underway on areas such as Somali language processing, climate modeling, and machine learning applications relevant to the country.

Dr. Assoweh emphasizes that Djibouti must shift from merely consuming AI tools developed elsewhere to becoming a producer of data, skills, research, and AI solutions tailored to national priorities. This requires strengthening existing digital, legal, scientific, and institutional foundations, which are currently fragmented. Recommendations include integrating AI and ethics into curricula, enhancing specialized training, and establishing a national AI center of excellence with dedicated research funding to build a critical mass of local expertise.

Why it mattersDjiboutians are increasingly using AI tools in their daily lives and administration, but the nation needs to develop its own AI capabilities to meet national priorities.

Djibouti Government Warns Traders on Expired Food Risks

Djibouti's minister of commerce urged economic operators to uphold food safety rules and remove expired products from markets.

Djibouti's government is raising awareness among traders about the health risks associated with selling expired food products. A recent consultation meeting emphasized economic operators' obligations regarding food safety, highlighting potential health dangers for consumers when products unfit for consumption enter the market. The minister stressed the importance of adhering to proper storage and conservation regulations for foodstuffs.

Officials also called for the immediate withdrawal of any expired items or those posing a sanitary risk. Discussions during the meeting addressed the responsibilities of all parties involved in the commercialization chain. The minister urged participants to demonstrate vigilance and a strong sense of responsibility in their operations.

The meeting concluded with a shared commitment to enhance collaboration between public authorities and food business operators. This initiative aims to bolster food security and ensure that only safe products reach Djiboutian consumers, fostering a more responsible marketplace.

Why it mattersDjiboutian traders are directly affected by new government directives on food safety and the removal of expired products from sale.

Djibouti, US Discuss Tech Cooperation

Djibouti's minister and the U.S. ambassador met to explore strengthening ties in telecommunications and new technologies.

Djibouti's Minister Helem met with the U.S. Ambassador to Djibouti to discuss the state of bilateral cooperation and ways to enhance it, particularly in telecommunications and new technologies. The discussions focused on the growing importance of digital infrastructure and services for economic development.

The meeting also served as a platform to examine the future prospects for cooperation in these key sectors. Both nations aim to leverage digital advancements to foster economic growth and improve services.

Djibouti and the United States maintain close relations, built on a foundation of collaboration across several strategic areas. This meeting underscores the ongoing commitment to deepening these partnerships.

Why it mattersDjibouti and the United States are exploring ways to consolidate cooperation in telecommunications and new technologies, which are crucial for economic development.
