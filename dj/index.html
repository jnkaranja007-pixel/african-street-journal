Djibouti athlete Ayanleh Souleiman promotes Grand Bara race

Participatory activities and an information booth are part of preparations for the international desert race.

Preparations for the international Grand Bara race are advancing with sports challenges and quizzes about the desert event. An information space is available to assist future runners with registration.

Djiboutian athlete Ayanleh Souleiman, the patron for this edition, met with participants, offering locals and international runners a chance to interact with the athletics star. These events aim to showcase the race through sport, exchanges, and information.

The communication cell of the FFDj stated these animations allow the Grand Bara race to be discovered from a new perspective, associating sport, exchanges, and information.

Why it mattersDjiboutians and international runners have opportunities to engage with a world-class athlete and learn about the Grand Bara race.

Djibouti's Doraleh plant produces 40 million m³ water

The desalination facility contributes to meeting the country's drinking water needs.

The Doraleh desalination plant in Djibouti has produced 40 million cubic meters of water. The director general of the National Office for Water and Sanitation (ONEAD), Mohamed Fouad Abdo, presided over a meeting with plant teams to mark the production.

Abdo praised the staff's daily efforts, highlighting teamwork and operational rigor for running the infrastructure. In Djibouti, where fresh water is scarce, seawater desalination is a strategy for ONEAD to expand access to drinking water for the population.

The ONEAD director general led the relaxed meeting with plant staff. The facility's output helps meet the population's drinking water needs in a country facing water scarcity.

Why it mattersDjibouti's population benefits from increased access to drinking water through the Doraleh desalination plant's production.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Aden Ali Mahamade installed as defence minister

Mahamade, previously ambassador to Eritrea, takes over from Hassan Omar Mohamed Bourhan who died in office.

Aden Ali Mahamade was officially installed as Djibouti's new defence minister on November 5, 2026. President Ismaïl Omar Guelleh appointed Mahamade by decree to the position. He succeeds Hassan Omar Mohamed Bourhan, who passed away while serving as minister.

The handover ceremony was attended by the Minister of Interior, Omar Abdi Said, who had been acting as interim minister. Mahamade is familiar with diplomatic matters, having previously held responsibilities in international cooperation. He also served as Djibouti's ambassador to Eritrea.

Mahamade's prior experience includes representing Djibouti as ambassador to Eritrea and working in international cooperation.

Culture · 2026-11-09

Djibouti beach festival ends with music and dance

The three-day event featured local and diaspora artists, traditional dances, and family activities.

Djibouti's first Beach Festival concluded after three nights of seaside music, drawing crowds who danced to international hits and local creations. Performers included singer Wafa and her band, Sharmarke and his dancers in traditional costumes, and reunited music figures Saad Coolio and Racine J. The festival also showcased national talents like rapper Waydara Boy, alongside artists Fafy, Yahya, Ikhran, Harbouy, Houssein Hayle, and Abayazid Ali.

The event included artists from the Djiboutian diaspora, such as Danka Boy and Arido, and an Ethiopian guest, Haymanot. Arido paid tribute to Nasser Abdallah by performing his song « Yinti Yinti », a moment described as the festival's most moving. Beyond the music, the festival site offered a family space with children's games, craft stalls, and entrepreneurship exhibits.

Organizers reported the event proceeded without incident, with minor electricity issues on the second night quickly resolved. The festival, blending sport, leisure, tourism, culture, and creative economy, served as a platform for local talent and a gathering point for youth. Observers noted the public's enthusiasm indicated a need for more leisure and creative spaces for young people.

Why it mattersDjiboutian youth and families can enjoy new leisure and creative spaces.
