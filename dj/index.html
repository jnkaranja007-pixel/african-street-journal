Djibouti reviews $30 million digital projects

Two projects, DFN and EARDIP, aim to boost internet access and expand digital infrastructure across the country.

Djibouti's government reviewed two digital projects totaling $30 million on October 25, 2026, with a call to accelerate their implementation. The Digital Francophone Network (DFN) project has mobilized $10 million, and the East Africa Digital Integration Programme (EARDIP) has secured $20 million, both supported by the International Development Association.

According to Hawa Youssouf Kayad, the coordinator for both projects, 26 out of 35 DFN activities are complete, with 80% of resources committed and a 56% disbursement rate, having already improved public institution internet access. EARDIP plans include extending cross-border terrestrial links, deploying 12 4G mobile sites, and building fiber optic loops to Dikhil, Tadjourah, and Obock, pending commercial, environmental, and social study validations.

Minister Mohamed Abdoulkader Moussa urged institutions and partners to accelerate remaining activities, remove constraints, and ensure investments yield concrete results, emphasizing rigorous monitoring of adopted recommendations. The DFN project is scheduled to close on June 30, 2027.

Why it mattersDjibouti citizens will benefit from improved internet access and expanded digital infrastructure.

Djibouti condemns Jordan church attack

Djibouti expresses solidarity with Jordan and rejects all forms of violence and extremism.

Djibouti has strongly condemned an attack on a church in Jordan's Karak governorate. The Republic of Djibouti stated its categorical rejection of all violence, terrorism, and extremism, and any attacks on civilians and places of worship.

The Djiboutian government expressed its full solidarity with the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan during this difficult time. Djibouti extended its sincere condolences to the families of the victims and to the Jordanian government and people, wishing a speedy recovery to the injured.

Djibouti's condemnation reiterates its stance against acts that harm innocent people and sacred sites.

Why it mattersDjibouti's government has condemned an attack in Jordan, expressing solidarity with the affected nation.

Djibouti records varied rainfall across regions

Tadjourah received up to 40 mm of rain, while other areas saw much less.

Djibouti experienced uneven rainfall on October 24, 2026, with Tadjourah recording up to 40 mm. Sagallou received 10 mm and Obock 9 mm, while Medeho recorded 2 mm in the same northern zone.

In the capital, the international airport measured 5 mm of rain, with higher amounts recorded within the city. Damerjog received 4 mm, Douda 3 mm, and Boulé 2 mm in the surrounding areas. Along the road, PK 23 recorded 7 mm, with only traces of rain at PK 20 and PK 50.

Wea and Randa each received 5 mm, and Lake Assal recorded 2 mm. The day's rainfall showed significant disparities, with clear showers in the north and center of the country and lighter, sometimes symbolic, precipitation in inland areas.

Why it mattersDjibouti residents experienced varied rainfall amounts across different regions on October 24, 2026.

Djibouti opens maritime rule of law training center

The new center aims to enhance technical skills and regional cooperation for maritime security.

Djibouti inaugurated a new center for maritime rule of law training on October 24, 2026. The ceremony was attended by the Minister of Infrastructure and Equipment, Saïd Nouh Hassan, alongside other civil and military officials. Also present were the U.S. Ambassador to Djibouti, Cynthia Kierscht, and a representative from the UN Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC), Mostafa Elbanna.

The center is designed to build technical and operational skills among personnel and foster expertise sharing between partner institutions. It addresses the increasing challenges to maritime security and safety in the strategic waters near the Gulf of Aden, Bab el-Mandeb Strait, and the Red Sea. Djibouti, the U.S., and UNODC reaffirmed their commitment to maritime law and fighting illicit activities.

Djibouti intends to strengthen its role in maritime security through specialized training and partnerships. The facility will support cooperation with national, regional, and international actors to consolidate its position at the crossroads of key maritime routes.

Why it mattersDjibouti's new maritime training center will improve its capacity to manage security in its strategic waters.
