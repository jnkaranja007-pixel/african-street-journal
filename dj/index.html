Djibouti urges collective action against youth vaping

Parents, teachers, and school officials are called upon to increase vigilance and awareness regarding the dangers of electronic cigarettes among minors.

Djibouti authorities are addressing the growing issue of youth vaping, emphasizing that electronic cigarettes are not harmless and should not be shared among peers. The Agence Djiboutienne d'Information reports that minors are obtaining these devices by finding them unattended, receiving them from friends, or purchasing them from vendors who disregard age restrictions.

The call for action highlights a collective responsibility, urging parents to keep electronic cigarettes out of children's reach and to monitor for behavioral changes. In schools, teachers and administrators are encouraged to reinforce awareness campaigns and educate students about the associated risks of vaping.

The message to young people is direct: do not use electronic cigarettes because they appear innocuous, are offered by a friend, or simply because of their taste. Preventing the normalization of vaping among minors requires a combination of information, vigilance, and open dialogue between parents, educators, and youth.

Why it mattersThe report directly affects parents, teachers, and young people by outlining their roles and responsibilities in preventing the use of electronic cigarettes among minors.

Djibouti hosts C STAR program, develops young African entrepreneurs

The fourth edition of the C STAR program, focused on youth entrepreneurship, launched in Djibouti, bringing together 32 young participants from 19 African countries.

Djibouti is hosting the fourth edition of the C STAR program, a pan-African platform for developing entrepreneurial skills and leadership among young people. The program, launched in 2022, has already trained over 1,500 participants from more than 50 African countries, with 45% being women. This year, 32 young people from 19 African nations were selected from 641 applicants across 44 countries.

The ceremony in Djibouti included several dignitaries, such as Aboubaker Omar Hadi, President of the Djibouti Ports and Free Zones Authority (DPFZA), and Miao Jianmin, President of China Merchants Group. Also present were Djiboutian ministers Said Nouh Hassan (Infrastructure and Equipment), Ilyas Moussa Dawaleh (Economy and Finance), and Safia Mohamed Ali Gadileh (Digital Economy and Innovation).

The C STAR program, implemented with the China Merchants Charity Foundation, UNDP, and the CLE Center, aims to foster a new generation of African leaders in innovation, entrepreneurship, and sustainable growth. It aligns with the Beijing Action Plan of the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation (FOCAC) for 2025-2027, connecting young entrepreneurs with networks, investors, and economic opportunities across the continent.

Why it mattersYoung entrepreneurs from 19 African countries are affected by gaining access to training, networks, and investors through the C STAR program hosted in Djibouti.

Djibouti Labor Ministry launches new digital platform "Djibouti Emploi"

The Ministry of Labor introduced "Djibouti Emploi," a new digital platform designed to centralize and simplify access to its services for citizens, workers, and employers.

The Ministry of Labor has launched "Djibouti Emploi," a new digital platform aimed at modernizing and digitally transforming its services. This initiative seeks to bring public services closer to the population, including workers and employers, by providing a single portal for various labor-related needs.

The platform's primary function is to centralize and facilitate access to the Ministry of Labor's services, while also interconnecting different institutions. It is intended to offer more accessible, simpler, and user-friendly services for professional integration, assisting businesses with recruitment, and serving public administration.

This digital tool is part of the Ministry's ongoing effort to enhance service delivery and streamline processes for job seekers and companies alike. The goal is to improve professional integration and support the recruitment efforts of businesses across the country.

Why it mattersWorkers and employers in Djibouti are affected by the new platform as it aims to simplify their access to labor services and recruitment processes.
