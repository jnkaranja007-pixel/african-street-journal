US lifts sanctions on Eritrea

The removal of US sanctions, imposed for Eritrea's role in the Tigray War, may destabilize the opaque state by creating new openings for predatory officials.

Source: African Arguments · 2026-10-22 · 2 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The United States lifted sanctions on Eritrea on September 19, 2026, ending measures imposed in 2021 due to the country's involvement in Ethiopia's Tigray War. Human rights groups have criticized the decision, citing Eritrea's record of forced labor and torture under the ruling People’s Front for Democracy and Justice (PFDJ). The Eritrean government, however, welcomed the move, calling the sanctions 'illicit and immoral' and arguing they hindered national development.

While Eritrea's government celebrates the removal of sanctions, observers suggest the benefits will likely accrue to the PFDJ's key financial and military apparatus, and two senior officials, rather than the impoverished population. The sanctions had targeted the ruling party's core structures, which are instrumental in maintaining control over political expression, capital flows, and national service conscripts. Past experience, following the removal of UN sanctions in 2018, indicates that the PFDJ's internal power dynamics are unlikely to change significantly.

The lifting of sanctions may create a paradoxically destabilizing effect within Eritrea, potentially empowering predatory officials seeking new political and economic opportunities. The PFDJ's strategy relies on repression to maintain control, and the removal of external constraints could exacerbate internal power struggles. Speculation also exists about a potential US-Eritrean partnership against Houthi forces in Yemen, following recent territorial gains by the Houthis.

Why it mattersEritrea's impoverished population may not benefit from the sanctions removal, while predatory officials could exploit new openings.
