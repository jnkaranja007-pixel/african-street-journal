Eritrea Boosts Measles Vaccine Coverage to 93 Percent

The nation has implemented a two-dose vaccination strategy, significantly increasing protection against the contagious disease.

Eritrea has made substantial progress in protecting its children against measles, achieving 93 percent coverage for the first dose and 85 percent for the second dose in 2023. This advancement is attributed to the country's sustained investment in routine immunization and community health services, including the introduction of a second measles vaccine dose in 2012. The two-dose approach is crucial as up to 15 percent of children may not develop adequate immunity after a single dose.

The national immunization program has strengthened significantly, with children now receiving two doses of the combined measles-rubella vaccine since 2018. This strategy has led to considerable progress toward measles elimination, with vaccination coverage rates now considerably higher than continental and global averages. While formal certification of elimination is still pending, the country's efforts demonstrate the impact of improved vaccination coverage on disease reduction.

Measles remains a significant global threat, with the World Health Organization estimating nearly 11 million cases worldwide in 2026, predominantly in Africa and Asia. The disease is highly contagious and preventable through vaccination, yet many children globally remain unprotected. Complications from measles can include deafness, blindness, encephalitis, and death, particularly for undernourished or medically underserved populations.

Why it mattersEritrean children are protected from the severe complications of measles, including blindness and death, due to increased vaccination rates.

Eritrea Marks 50 Years of Revolution School

National and international events will celebrate the school's legacy of integrating theory with practice and social justice.

Eritrea will celebrate the 50th anniversary of the Revolution School from September 17 to 20 with events themed "Vibrant Commemoration: New Impetus for Nation-Building." Ambassador Zemede Tekle, Commissioner of Culture and Sports, stated the celebrations aim to document the school's history and legacy for future generations. The EPLF's education system, which combined theory and practice with social justice, will be highlighted.

Activities planned include photo exhibitions, musical concerts, seminars, sports competitions, and a symposium. The official program will take place in Asmara, with regional areas organizing similar events. A week-long photo exhibition opens on September 17, followed by a concert and cultural carnival on September 18. Seminars on nationalism and nation-building are scheduled for September 19.

The concluding symposium and ceremony are set for September 20 at the Asmara Palace Hotel. Celebrations have already occurred in countries like Sweden and Canada, aiming to foster a conducive environment for Eritrean diaspora contributions. The events underscore the school's role in nurturing nationalism and advancing the nation-building process through education.

Why it mattersEritrean nationals in the Diaspora are invited to strengthen their contribution to nation-building through participation in celebratory events.

Modern Medical Equipment Enhances Adi-Keih Hospital Services

New digital equipment installed since 2023 has increased patient and staff satisfaction at the facility.

Modern medical equipment installed at Adi-Keih Hospital is significantly improving healthcare services, according to beneficiaries and health professionals. The hospital's Medical Director, Dr. Tsegazeab Kiflezgi, stated that the government is investing heavily in equipping health facilities and training personnel, leading to better public healthcare. He noted that the introduction of digital equipment since 2023 has boosted satisfaction among both patients and staff.

Dr. Kiflezgi also urged the public to adopt healthier lifestyles, including proper diet and regular exercise, to manage non-communicable diseases like diabetes and hypertension, and advised against consuming canned foods. Mr. Teshome Tekle from the physiotherapy unit confirmed that the hospital serves patients from Adi-Keih and Senafe sub-zones, as well as the Northern Red Sea Region, and that the new equipment is crucial for effective treatment.

Beneficiaries expressed their gratitude to the government for the modern medical equipment, reporting that they are receiving effective and timely healthcare. The hospital's enhanced capabilities are a direct result of these investments, aiming to provide improved health outcomes for the surrounding communities.

Why it mattersPatients and health professionals in the Adi-Keih and Senafe sub-zones, as well as the Northern Red Sea Region, are receiving more effective and timely healthcare services.

Eritrea Health Official Urges Public to End Harmful Practices

Awareness campaigns are underway in 11 areas of the Teseney sub-zone to combat female genital mutilation and underage marriage, which cause maternal deaths.

Mr. Gebrehiwet Tekeste, head of healthcare services in Eritrea's Teseney sub-zone, has called for increased public participation in eradicating harmful practices like female genital mutilation and underage marriage. He stated that these practices persist in some regions, prompting awareness activities across 11 administrative areas. Mr. Gebrehiwet stressed the crucial role of religious leaders and local administrators in these efforts, highlighting that the consequences of these practices can lead to maternal deaths during childbirth.

Ms. Hafisa Mohammed from the sub-zone's prosecutor’s office confirmed that female genital mutilation is illegal under Proclamation No. 158/2007, and underage marriage is prohibited by Article 581 of the Civil Code. Both offenses carry legal penalties. Religious leaders have pledged to integrate awareness messages into their sermons, noting that female genital mutilation is not religiously sanctioned. Sub-zone administrator Mr. Seium Gebreyesus urged active public involvement and regular follow-up by administrators and village coordinators.

Participants in the discussions expressed their commitment to intensifying efforts against harmful practices and controlling malaria prevalence. The call for reinforced participation aims to address deeply rooted social issues that impact public health and safety. Legal frameworks are in place to prosecute offenders, and community leaders are being mobilized to support awareness and enforcement initiatives.

Why it mattersThe public in the Teseney sub-zone is affected by awareness campaigns and legal measures aimed at eradicating female genital mutilation and underage marriage.
