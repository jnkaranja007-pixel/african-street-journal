Eritrea Calls for Stronger African Conflict Resolution Mechanisms

The nation's delegation urged African states to prioritize national institutions and equitable resource use for peace and security.

Eritrea's delegation has called for a strengthening of Africa's conflict prevention and resolution mechanisms, stating that current systems have not been sufficiently effective. Speaking at the 21st Extraordinary Session of the Assembly of the African Union in Luanda, Angola, the delegation highlighted that internal and external factors continue to fuel violent conflicts across the continent. They emphasized that African agency is undermined by competing, externally driven initiatives.

The delegation stressed that effective conflict prevention must begin at the national level, with African states taking primary responsibility for peace and security. This requires strong national institutions, a move away from divisive identity-based politics towards citizenship-based politics, and the equitable utilization of national resources for the benefit of all citizens. The concentration of resources in the hands of a few was identified as a contributing factor to instability.

Furthermore, the Eritrean statement called for a thorough evaluation of existing sub-regional and continental peace and security architectures to identify shortcomings and improve effectiveness. A critical point raised was the need to address the African Union's unsustainable dependence on external funding, advocating for strengthened domestic resource mobilization and prudent, transparent, and accountable utilization of available resources to safeguard the Union's independence.

Why it mattersAfrican nations and their citizens are affected by the call for stronger national institutions and equitable resource distribution to prevent and resolve conflicts.

Eritrean Coffee Ceremony Unites Communities Through Ancient Ritual

The revered domestic tradition spans one to two hours, emphasizing hospitality and community ties across diverse ethnic groups.

The Eritrean coffee ceremony, a cornerstone of domestic life, serves as an ancient social ritual that prioritizes hospitality and deliberate conversation. This multi-stage process involves roasting and hand-grinding green beans, slow-brewing the coffee, and engaging guests in meaningful dialogue. The ceremony unfolds over one to two hours, incorporating sensory elements like burning frankincense and the aroma of roasting beans, culminating in three distinct rounds of coffee: Awel, Kalay, and Bereka.

This tradition is distinguished by its unique spatial arrangements, cultural omens, and specialized utensils, including the spherical clay Jebena pot and small ceramic Finjal cups. Hosts brew coffee over hot charcoal, often infusing it with ginger, and pour it in a continuous stream from a height to create a delicate froth. The rich roast is accompanied by traditional snacks like roasted barley or popcorn, offering a complete cultural experience rooted in generosity and respect.

Having traveled across Eritrea from Karora to Rahayta, the coffee ceremony stands as the most revered domestic tradition, retaining a unified core of warmth, social prestige, and unyielding hospitality regardless of geographical or linguistic differences. It acts as a seamless thread binding diverse communities under a shared heritage, serving as the highest mark of respect for visitors and functioning as a non-negotiable anchor of daily family life for genuine togetherness and spirited dialogue.

Why it mattersThe Eritrean coffee ceremony is a vital social ritual that strengthens community ties and fosters hospitality among all ethnic groups within the nation.

Eritreans in Saudi Arabia Mark Independence Struggle Anniversary

Nationals in Riyadh, Jeddah, and Al-Namas gathered on August 27 to commemorate the 65th anniversary of the start of Eritrea’s armed struggle for independence.

Eritrean communities in Saudi Arabia marked the 65th anniversary of the beginning of the nation's armed struggle for independence on August 27. Events were held in Riyadh, Jeddah, and Al-Namas, featuring patriotic gatherings and cultural performances.

In Riyadh, Mr. Tesfay Tekle, chairman of the Eritrean community, discussed the historical background of the struggle, emphasizing the responsibility of current generations to preserve independence. Ms. Weini Gerezgihier, Chargé d’Affaires at the Eritrean Embassy, highlighted the 1961 armed struggle as a pivotal moment in the nation's pursuit of freedom.

At the Jeddah event, Eritrea’s Consul General, Mr. Abdurahman Osman, spoke about the day's significance and urged attendees to contribute to the National Martyrs Trust Fund and support martyrs' families. The commemorations included artistic displays reflecting the historical importance of the independence struggle.

Why it mattersEritrean nationals residing in Saudi Arabia are reminded of their nation's history and encouraged to support national funds and families of martyrs.
