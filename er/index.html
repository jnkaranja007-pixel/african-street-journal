Ethiopia-Eritrea conflict threatens Red Sea shipping

Renewed hostilities near Eritrea's Assab port could increase war-risk costs for global commerce.

Renewed fighting between Ethiopia and Eritrea near the Eritrean border could threaten Red Sea shipping lanes. Eritrean troops have allegedly crossed into Ethiopia’s northern Tigray region, leading to clashes with Ethiopian forces. This conflict has the potential to extend toward Eritrea’s port of Assab, a critical maritime corridor.

Military activity near Assab could increase war-risk costs for global commerce, exacerbating the existing threat from the Houthis in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. The United States is urged to use its influence with both governments to prevent Ethiopian forces from advancing on Assab and to secure an Eritrean withdrawal from Tigray.

Global commerce faces a potential second shock to Red Sea shipping, as Saudi Arabia reroutes oil exports through this route due to Strait of Hormuz disruptions. Weekly vessel transits through the Bab el-Mandeb have already fallen from 38 to 25 since July due to Houthi attacks.

Why it mattersGlobal commerce and shipping routes are threatened by potential conflict escalation near Eritrea's coast and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

Eritrea faces choice between war and peace

The nation's modern history is defined by sacrifice, but recent conflicts have been wars of choice, not necessity.

Eritreans understand sacrifice for country, having fought one of Africa’s longest liberation wars to achieve national independence. This struggle united people across identities, driven by the conviction that Eritrea belonged to its people.

The nation's modern history is marked by sacrifice, with generations giving everything for an independent Eritrea. However, the current regime has drawn the country into conflicts over the past three decades that were wars of choice, not necessity, prioritizing the ruler's authority over citizens' lives.

As tensions escalate along the Eritrea-Ethiopia border with reports of artillery exchanges and drone strikes, the critical question for Eritreans is not their capacity to fight, but whether another war is necessary or if other choices exist.

Eritrea president meets Putin at Kremlin

Russian President Vladimir Putin and Eritrean President Isaias Afwerki discussed trade and economic cooperation in Moscow.

Eritrean President Isaias Afwerki met Russian President Vladimir Putin at the Kremlin on the sidelines of the 2026 Russia-Africa Summit. The leaders discussed bilateral relations and ways to increase trade between their countries. Putin highlighted potential sectors for cooperation including energy, mining, infrastructure, agriculture, and fisheries.

Putin stated that Russia is providing assistance to enhance Eritrea's food security. He also expressed gratitude for Eritrea's support of Russian initiatives at the United Nations and other multilateral forums. Afwerki observed Putin's strength and active engagement in summit issues, finding his clarifications inspiring.

The meeting occurred in October 2026. Several Russian officials, including Deputy Prime Ministers Andrei Nikitin and Alexander Novak, attended the discussions. Both leaders acknowledged shared positions on many international issues.
