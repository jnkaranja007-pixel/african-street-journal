Eight Ethiopian opposition groups form armed alliance, US report warns of war with Eritrea

A Congressional Research Service report says fighting has escalated in Tigray and Ethiopia has broken ties with Eritrea.

Ethiopia's internal conflict is spreading and the country may be heading toward war with Eritrea, according to a new Congressional Research Service (CRS) report. The CRS says eight opposition groups have formed an armed alliance against the government of Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, and that the overlapping rebellions threaten a wider regional conflict.

Fighting resumed in early August and escalated, with increasingly frequent drone strikes in Tigray. On September 20, the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF) and six other groups announced the Alliance for Survival. In early October, Ethiopia broke ties with Eritrea and expelled an Egyptian diplomat, then accused Eritrea of moving troops into Tigray. Eritrea denied the allegation and framed it as a declaration of war.

Abiy has announced a caretaker government for Tigray and pledged elections there within six months, which the TPLF has rejected as illegitimate. In Washington, the administration waived restrictions under the Child Soldiers Prevention Act and ended the arms embargo from the earlier war. Sanctions on Eritrea's military and ruling party ended with the lapse of the Ethiopia-related national emergency.

Why it mattersTigray residents face continued drone strikes and a contested election window, while the Tigray government's authority is rejected by the TPLF.

Ethiopia-Eritrea clashes raise fears for Red Sea shipping lanes

Eritrean troops allegedly crossed into Ethiopia's Tigray region, the first fighting between the two countries in over 25 years.

Eritrean troops allegedly crossed into Ethiopia's northern Tigray region along the Eritrean border and clashed with Ethiopian forces, bringing the two countries to the brink of their first war in more than 25 years. The fighting could extend toward Eritrea's Red Sea port of Assab if it spreads.

Military activity near Assab could raise war-risk costs for ships using the corridor, adding to the threat Houthi attacks already pose in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the narrow waterway between Yemen and Djibouti. The Small Wars Journal argues that the United States should use its ties with both capitals to secure an Eritrean withdrawal from Tigray and keep Ethiopian forces away from Assab.

Saudi Arabia is rerouting about 4 million barrels of oil a day through the Red Sea, most of it bound for Asia, and those tankers must pass 600 miles of Eritrean coast. Weekly vessel transits through Bab el-Mandeb have already fallen from about 38 to 25 since the Houthis renewed attacks in July.

Why it mattersTankers carrying Saudi oil must sail past 600 miles of Eritrean coast, so fighting there would put a route that is already shrinking at further risk.

Yale students celebrate Ethiopian and Eritrean jazz

The second annual collaboration between student groups highlighted jazz traditions of the Habesha community.

The Yale Ethiopian and Eritrean Students Association (YEESA) and the Yale Undergraduate Jazz Collective (YUJC) co-hosted their second annual jazz evening on Saturday in the Davenport College common room. The event showcased the jazz traditions of Ethiopia and Eritrea, East African neighbours.

YEESA President Liana Markos said jazz is deeply ingrained in the Habesha community's culture, prompting the idea to incorporate it at Yale. The collaboration began last year when YEESA approached YUJC, which readily agreed to participate. YEESA handled logistics like booking space and arranging food, while YUJC provided musical promotion and performance.

The evening featured jazz performances alongside an open mic for spoken-word poetry and songs, creating a calm and welcoming atmosphere. Attendees noted the music's authenticity, reminiscent of jazz clubs in Addis Ababa. Organizers plan to expand the event to a larger venue and reach more of the Yale community.

Why it mattersStudents from the Habesha community in the US are celebrating their cultural heritage through jazz music.
