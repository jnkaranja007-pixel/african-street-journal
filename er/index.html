Ethiopia and Eritrea mass troops as Red Sea access dispute deepens

Ethiopian and Eritrean forces have built up along their shared border amid a dispute rooted in Addis Ababa's push for sea access, according to analysts tracking the standoff. The International Crisis Group says the risk of hostilities involving Ethiopia, Tigray and Eritrea remains high three years after the last major war in the area, and has urged outside powers to open back channels to defuse tensions. Ethiopia has accused Eritrea of backing armed factions inside Ethiopian territory, while relations have deteriorated since early 2025 over Eritrea's bid to regain access to the Assab port.

Why it mattersA slide toward renewed war would disrupt trade routes, displace civilians and draw in regional and Gulf powers.

Isaias uses Independence Day speech to criticise US policy, sign Egypt maritime deal

President Isaias Afwerki used his 24 May Independence Day address marking Eritrea's 35th anniversary to question the policies of the Trump administration, particularly interventions in Iran and Venezuela, while cautioning against premature conclusions, according to the International Crisis Group. He went on to criticize decades of what he called reckless U.S. hegemony. Separately, Asmara and Cairo signed a maritime agreement.

Why it mattersThe speech signals Asmara is hedging between wary engagement with Washington and closer ties with Cairo on Red Sea security.

Politics · 2026-08-05

UK courts clear deportation of Eritrean asylum seekers to France under new pact

Britain's High Court and Court of Appeal have ruled against Eritrean asylum seekers contesting removal to France under the UK-France "one in, one out" migration scheme, with the first such deportation carried out after a man who fled Eritrea in 2019 over forced conscription lost his case. A second Eritrean man was removed days later after judges found the Home Office had sufficient grounds to reject his trafficking claim. Prime Minister Keir Starmer's government has called the removals proof the scheme works and says it plans to scale up returns.

Why it mattersThe rulings set a legal precedent that is likely to accelerate returns of Eritrean Channel migrants to France.

Business · 2026-08-05

China's Zijin expands Bisha mine output with new pits as copper, zinc keep exports flowing

Zijin Mining says its Bisha copper-zinc operation in western Eritrea, the country's largest mining project, extracts 2.9 million tonnes of ore a year and in 2025 completed pre-stripping at the new Hambok open pit while starting production at the Asheli underground mine. Bisha has produced more than 1 million ounces of gold since output began, alongside 16,000-18,000 tonnes of copper and roughly 117,000 tonnes of zinc annually as of 2023, according to industry data. Mining accounts for over 90% of Eritrea's exports, leaving the economy exposed to global metals prices.

Why it mattersContinued expansion at Bisha underpins Eritrea's main source of foreign exchange and government revenue.

Business · 2026-08-05

Colluli potash project advances under Chinese ownership after Danakali exit

Control of the Colluli potash project, one of the world's largest sulphate-of-potash deposits with reserves estimated at 1.1 billion tonnes, has passed to China's Sichuan Road and Bridge Group after Australia's Danakali sold its 50% stake for $135 million plus a $31 million shareholder loan. The project, jointly held with Eritrea's state mining company ENAMCO, is designed to run for roughly 200 years and was projected to help lift Eritrea's GDP growth toward 3.3% by 2026 once production ramps up.

Why it mattersA running Colluli mine would diversify Eritrea's export base beyond gold and copper into fertiliser inputs for regional agriculture.

Hundreds of Eritreans forcibly returned from Ethiopia face treason claims, UN says

Amnesty International reports that between December 2024 and 27 February 2025, more than 600 Eritreans who had sought refuge in Ethiopia were forcibly returned to Eritrea, where the government regarded their asylum claims as evidence of treason. The UN Special Rapporteur on Eritrea told the Human Rights Council that Asmara has taken no steps to reform its indefinite national service programme, a key driver of outward migration. Rights groups say the conscription policy continues to push large numbers of Eritreans to flee the country.

Why it mattersThe returns policy raises safety risks for the region's largest refugee-producing population and pressures Ethiopia's asylum system.

Girmay leads NSN's Tour de France bid as Eritrea's cycling profile grows

Eritrean sprinter Biniam Girmay heads an eight-rider NSN Cycling Team squad at the 2026 Tour de France, his first appearance for the team after signing a three-year contract that began this season. Girmay enters the race off his best early-season form since 2022, having taken a stage win and two top-10 finishes at the Baloise Belgium Tour, following his 2024 Tour campaign in which he won three stages and the green points jersey as the first African to claim any Tour classification.

Why it mattersGirmay's results keep Eritrea in global sports coverage and continue to draw young riders into the country's cycling programs.

Business · 2026-08-05

Eritrea's mining sector stays reliant on Chinese capital amid Western sanctions

Eritrea's international isolation continues to limit financial inflows into its mineral sector, with sanctions and human rights concerns having deterred Western investment and left China as the dominant player, according to bne IntelliNews. State-backed Chinese firms including Zijin Mining and Sichuan Road and Bridge now control the country's key gold, copper, zinc and potash projects. The country's gold reserves are estimated at 2 million ounces, with mining contributing about 20% of GDP in 2021.

Why it mattersHeavy reliance on Chinese capital shapes the terms of Eritrea's resource development and its limited access to diversified financing.
