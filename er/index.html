Eritrea hosts conference to advance agri-food systems for economic growth

The Ministry of Agriculture organized a two-day National Conference on Eritrean Agricultural Studies in Asmara, focusing on sustainable agri-food systems.

Around 300 participants, including ministers, government officials, researchers, academics, and agri-food producers, gathered in Asmara from August 20–21 for the National Conference on Eritrean Agricultural Studies. The Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) organized the event under the theme “Advancing Sustainable Agri-Food Systems for National Economic Growth,” aiming to foster inter-sector solutions for national challenges.

The conference highlighted the importance of collaboration across various sectors, recognizing that improving agri-food systems requires expertise beyond agriculture. Representatives from government institutions, academia, development partners, and producers exchanged knowledge and networked, demonstrating the value of looking beyond institutional boundaries for solutions.

A notable aspect of the conference was the active participation of young professionals and researchers, many holding master’s degrees or higher. This engagement reflects the MoA’s commitment to human-resource development and suggests that such platforms are crucial for building scientific capacity through practice, mentorship, and collaboration.

Why it mattersThe conference directly affects agri-food producers and various professionals by providing a platform for knowledge exchange and networking to improve agricultural practices and economic growth.

Eritrean Officials Brief Diaspora on National Development and Regional Security

Eritrean embassy officials held seminars in Switzerland and Austria to update nationals on the country's security program, social justice principles, and sustainable development.

Eritrean nationals in Switzerland and Austria received updates on national and regional developments from August 16 to 23. Mr. Ismail Mohammed-Nur, Head of Public and Community Affairs at the Eritrean Embassy in Switzerland, conducted seminars in Zug, Schwyz, Schaffhausen, and St. Gallen. He stated that the Government of Eritrea is monitoring developments in the Horn of Africa and collaborating with other nations to prevent potential dangers.

Mr. Ismail also noted the government's efforts to implement its national security program, social justice principles, and sustainable development initiatives to improve citizens' livelihoods. He urged nationals in Switzerland to enhance their awareness and organization to contribute to national development programs, emphasizing the strong position of the Eritrean people due to their unity.

In Vienna, Austria, Mr. Kibreab Tekeste, Eritrea’s Consul General in Frankfurt, and Mr. Teame Haile, Head of Public and Community Affairs at the Eritrean Embassy in Germany, briefed nationals on the homeland's situation, regional and global developments, and the importance of strengthening organizational capacity. They also provided information on consular services and timely responsibilities for nationals abroad.

Why it mattersEritrean nationals in Switzerland and Austria are directly affected by the government's call to participate in national development and maintain their organizational capacity.

Eritrea plans national celebration for Revolution School's 50th anniversary

The Commission of Culture and Sports announced a four-day national celebration from September 17 to 20, marking the 50th anniversary of the Revolution School's establishment.

Eritrea will host a national celebration for the 50th anniversary of the Revolution School from September 17 to 20, under the theme, “Vibrant Commemoration: New Impetus for Nation-Building.” Ambassador Zemede Tekle, Commissioner of Culture and Sports, stated the celebration aims to document the school's history and legacy, highlight the EPLF’s education system, and encourage contributions from Eritrean nationals abroad.

The celebratory events will take place both inside the country and internationally, featuring photo exhibitions, musical concerts, seminars, sports competitions, and a symposium. Official programs are scheduled for Asmara, with regional activities mirroring these events. A photo exhibition will open on September 17 in front of the Central Region Administration building, remaining open for one week.

Further events include a concert and cultural carnival on September 18 along Asmara’s main streets, involving all thirteen sub-zones of the region. Seminars on September 19 at the Expo compound will focus on the Revolution School's role in nationalism and education's contribution to nation-building, alongside a cultural program at Cinema Roma. The celebration will conclude with a symposium and ceremony at the Asmara Palace Hotel on September 20.

Why it mattersThe celebration directly affects Eritrean nationals by providing cultural events and educational seminars, highlighting national history and encouraging diaspora engagement.

Eritrean Neuroscientist Promotes National Beauty at Miss Universe

Snit Habteab, an Eritrean neuroscientist, Montessori educator, and disability rights advocate, is representing Eritrea at Miss Universe in Vietnam, highlighting the nation's.

Snit Habteab is showcasing Eritrean beauty on the global stage at the Miss Universe competition in Vietnam. Her participation promotes the country's cultural richness and the resilience of Eritrean women, drawing parallels between their endurance and the strength of coral reefs.

The representation emphasizes the concept of “unity in diversity,” likening the intricate beauty of Eritrean women from nine distinct ethnic groups to the varied species within a coral reef. Each group contributes unique aesthetic, cultural, and traditional values to the national identity.

Examples include the classic elegance of Tigrinya women in white cotton gowns, the intricate silver adornments of Tigre and Saho women, the vibrant veils of Rashida women, and the bold beadwork of Afar women. Women from Kunama, Nara, Bilin, and Hedareb ethnic groups are noted for their natural hair textures and earthy bodily ornamentations.

Why it mattersThis story affects Eritrean women by showcasing their diverse beauty and cultural heritage on an international platform, potentially influencing perceptions and appreciation of their identity.
