Eritrea Boosts Measles Vaccine Coverage to 93 Percent

The nation has implemented a two-dose vaccination strategy, significantly increasing protection against the contagious disease.

Eritrea has made substantial progress in protecting its children against measles, achieving 93 percent coverage for the first dose and 85 percent for the second dose in 2023. This advancement is attributed to the country's sustained investment in routine immunization and community health services, including the introduction of a second measles vaccine dose in 2012. The two-dose approach is crucial as up to 15 percent of children may not develop adequate immunity after a single dose.

The national immunization program has strengthened significantly, with children now receiving two doses of the combined measles-rubella vaccine since 2018. This strategy has led to considerable progress toward measles elimination, with vaccination coverage rates now considerably higher than continental and global averages. While formal certification of elimination is still pending, the country's efforts demonstrate the impact of improved vaccination coverage on disease reduction.

Measles remains a significant global threat, with the World Health Organization estimating nearly 11 million cases worldwide in 2026, predominantly in Africa and Asia. The disease is highly contagious and preventable through vaccination, yet many children globally remain unprotected. Complications from measles can include deafness, blindness, encephalitis, and death, particularly for undernourished or medically underserved populations.

Why it mattersEritrean children are protected from the severe complications of measles, including blindness and death, due to increased vaccination rates.

Eritrean Expert Develops Organic Pesticides from Local Plants

Zenawi Zakarias created two natural pesticides using Alqe and Andel plants to combat crop pests and reduce chemical use.

Zenawi Zakarias, a plant protection expert at the Ministry of Agriculture in Eritrea's Southern Region, has developed two organic pesticides from local plant materials. The innovation aims to provide farmers with an alternative to chemical pesticides, which are harmful and often their only option. Zakarias was motivated by his concern for the negative effects of chemical pesticides and the ministry's strategy to promote organic agriculture.

The organic pesticides are derived from two local tree species, Alqe (Cissus quadrangularis) and Andel (Capparis tomentosa). Their leaves and tender stems were used to prepare the natural insecticides. Zakarias tested the products on crops such as cabbages, oranges, tomatoes, and lemons, as well as on fruit trees. The tests showed success in controlling pests like aphids and leaf miners.

Zakarias, who specializes in plant protection, was recognized in May 2026 by the National Union of Eritrean Youth and Students for his work. He grew up on a farm and has a deep connection to agriculture, believing it to be the backbone of the nation's economy. His research focused on creating effective, natural alternatives to chemical pesticides that damage crops and spread diseases.

Why it mattersFarmers in Eritrea can benefit from these organic pesticides as a safer and potentially more accessible alternative to chemical treatments for crop protection.

Improved Potato Seeds Yield Abundant Harvests for Eritrean Farmers

Exemplary farmers in Dubarwa sub-zone report successful cultivation of new potato varieties, leading to increased market supply.

Improved potato seeds distributed to exemplary farmers in Eritrea's Dubarwa sub-zone are yielding abundant harvests, according to Mr. Medhanie Okbai from the Agriculture Office. The new varieties are noted for their pest and disease resistance, contributing to their wide acceptance among growers. In June, 160 quintals of these seeds were planted across five hectares, demonstrating a significant step in agricultural development.

Farmers involved in the program have expressed satisfaction with the results, noting their ability to produce substantial quantities of potatoes for market. They attribute this success partly to the training provided, which they believe will enable further increases in their production. This initiative aims to bolster local food security and agricultural output through the adoption of advanced seed technology.

The distribution of these seeds represents a targeted effort to enhance farming practices and productivity in the region. By focusing on exemplary farmers, the program aims to create a ripple effect, encouraging broader adoption of improved agricultural techniques. The positive outcomes reported suggest a promising future for potato cultivation in Eritrea.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly benefiting from improved potato seeds that produce abundant yields and are resistant to pests and diseases, allowing them to supply more to the market.

Official Calls for Cultural Heritage Preservation

Seminars in Barentu, Gogni, and Mogolo urged public participation in safeguarding traditions and passing them to future generations.

Seminars held between August 21 and 24 in Barentu, Gogni, and Mogolo sub-zones have issued a call for the proper preservation of cultural heritage and traditional ways of life. Mr. Idris Saleh, Director General of Culture and Sports in the Gash-Barka Region, stated that his department is actively working with partners towards this objective. He encouraged the public to deepen their understanding of cultural heritage's importance and to actively engage in its preservation and documentation.

During the seminars, Mr. Adam Abushush of the Intangible Cultural Heritage Research and Studies Unit provided insights into the significance, objectives, and challenges of cultural festivals, with a particular focus on the Kunama and Nara ethnic groups. Sub-zone administrators acknowledged their responsibility in ensuring thorough research and documentation of cultural festivals and the transmission of heritage to younger generations, pledging increased participation.

Participants at the events expressed their commitment to enhancing efforts to revive cultural heritage that is showing signs of diminishing distinctiveness due to modern influences. The call emphasizes the need for collective action to ensure cultural traditions are maintained and passed on effectively to future generations.

Why it mattersThe call for cultural heritage preservation affects the public by urging active participation in documenting and safeguarding traditions, particularly for the Kunama and Nara ethnic groups.

New Medical Equipment Enhances Adi-Keih Hospital Services

Modern digital equipment installed since 2023 has increased patient and staff satisfaction at Adi-Keih Hospital.

Modern medical equipment installed at Adi-Keih Hospital is significantly improving healthcare services, according to beneficiaries and health professionals. The hospital's Medical Director, Dr. Tsegazeab Kiflezgi, stated that government investments in equipping facilities with advanced technology and qualified personnel are enhancing public healthcare. He noted that the introduction of digital equipment since 2023 has boosted satisfaction among both patients and staff.

Dr. Kiflezgi also urged the public to take proactive steps in managing non-communicable diseases like diabetes and hypertension through increased awareness, proper diet, and regular exercise, advising against the consumption of canned foods. Mr. Teshome Tekle from the physiotherapy unit confirmed that the hospital serves patients from Adi-Keih and Senafe sub-zones, as well as the Northern Red Sea Region, with the new equipment enabling more effective treatment.

Beneficiaries expressed their gratitude to the government for the modern medical equipment, reporting that they are receiving effective and timely healthcare services. The hospital's enhanced capabilities are a direct result of these recent upgrades, contributing to better health outcomes for the surrounding communities.

Why it mattersThe installation of modern medical equipment at Adi-Keih Hospital directly benefits patients and health professionals by improving the quality and timeliness of healthcare services provided.
