Abiy says Eritrea rejected power, road and port offers as tensions grow

Ethiopia's prime minister accuses Asmara of arming forces hostile to Ethiopia and warns that destruction will fall upon Eritrea if it does not change course.

Source: Eritrea Focus · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed accused Eritrea of arming forces hostile to Ethiopia and warned of grave consequences if Asmara did not change course. Speaking to parliament on November 26, he set two conditions for peaceful coexistence: that Eritrea stop arming and collaborating with those forces, and that it join joint development, with Ethiopia contributing the ideas, the money and the labour.

The state-run Ethiopian News Agency reported that Ethiopia had offered Eritrea electricity at Ethiopian domestic tariffs and proposed joint road and port projects, and that Eritrea had rejected the initiatives. Abiy said relations deteriorated after Eritrean forces entered Tigray during the 2020-22 war, accusing them of killing young people, destroying infrastructure and looting property.

Eight days earlier, Reuters reported that the two sides had massed forces along their shared border, bringing the armies within striking distance of each other. Eritrean Information Minister Yemane Gebremeskel dismissed the reports as propaganda.

Why it mattersEritrea must now decide whether to accept Ethiopia's power, road and port offers or face the consequences Abiy has threatened, with armies already within striking distance.
