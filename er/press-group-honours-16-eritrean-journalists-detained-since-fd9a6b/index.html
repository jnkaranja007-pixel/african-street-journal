Press group honours 16 Eritrean journalists detained since 2001

The Committee to Protect Journalists gave its International Press Freedom award to the journalists in absentia at a New York ceremony on 19 November.

Source: Eritrea Focus · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

The Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ), a press-freedom organisation, honoured 16 Eritrean journalists in absentia at a New York ceremony on 19 November, 25 years after they disappeared into Eritrea’s prison system. The government shut down the country’s newspapers on 18 September 2001, the day known as Black Tuesday.

The journalists’ families and colleagues do not know whether they are alive. At the African Investigative Journalism Conference in Nairobi, Eritrean journalist Fisseha Yirgalem called for them not to be forgotten. CPJ chief executive Jodie Ginsberg said the award could not replace truth, freedom or justice, and that families had been denied answers, including proof their loved ones were alive.

Yirgalem fled Eritrea in March 2018 after six years in military prisons. CPJ announced in August that it would give the journalists its International Press Freedom award.

Why it mattersFamilies in Eritrea and elsewhere remain without answers about their loved ones, and authorities face calls to disclose the 16 journalists’ fate and whereabouts.
