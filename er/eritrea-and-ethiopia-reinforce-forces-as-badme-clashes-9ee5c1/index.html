Eritrea and Ethiopia reinforce forces as Badme clashes raise war risk

A conflict monitor reports troop reinforcements and artillery exchanges near the town claimed by both countries.

Source: Eritrea Focus · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Eritrean and Ethiopian forces have exchanged artillery and clashed around Badme, a disputed border town, in a standoff analysts say risks escalating into full-scale war. The Armed Conflict Location and Event Data Project (ACLED), a conflict monitor, said Eritrea retook Badme last week after its army had been forced out of the town the previous month.

A foreign security source in Ethiopia told AFP that Ethiopian forces entered Eritrean territory during clashes last week before withdrawing. ACLED reported Eritrean troops and heavy military equipment deployed to Gual Gemhalo and Sembel, villages near Badme, and said the opposing forces were positioned close together. The two governments have not responded to AFP's questions.

The clashes follow a sharp deterioration in relations, with both countries cutting diplomatic ties in October. Eritrea backed a rebel offensive in Ethiopia's Tigray region launched in September, which the Ethiopian army pushed back. The two countries fought a border war from 1998 to 2000 that killed tens of thousands, according to the report.

Why it mattersEritrean troops deployed near Badme face opposing forces at close range, with ACLED reporting artillery exchanges and heavy reinforcements.
