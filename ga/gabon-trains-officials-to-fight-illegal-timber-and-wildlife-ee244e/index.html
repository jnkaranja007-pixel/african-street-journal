Gabon trains officials to fight illegal timber and wildlife trade

A five-day workshop aims to improve the detection, investigation, and prosecution of organized criminal networks involved in exporting protected natural resources.

Source: Gabon Review · 2026-10-22 · 2 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Gabon's customs and indirect rights directorate, with support from the UN Office on Drugs and Crime, is hosting a national workshop in Libreville to combat illegal timber and wildlife trafficking. The training, which began on October 19, 2026, focuses on enhancing the capabilities of law enforcement and judicial bodies to disrupt organized criminal operations that threaten the country's natural heritage. 30 participants from various national administrations are attending the event, which is funded by the European Union through the Guard Wildlife program.

The workshop addresses critical areas such as identifying fraudulent permits, recognizing timber species, inspecting cargo, and tracing illicit financial flows. Participants will engage in practical exercises, including at the port of Owendo, to refine their skills in controlling goods and building stronger cases for prosecution. The initiative aims to improve the entire chain of repression, from detection to judicial proceedings, to better tackle the economic, ecological, and judicial challenges posed by these illegal activities.

The training emphasizes institutional cooperation between customs, forestry services, investigators, and judicial authorities. This collaborative approach is seen as crucial for effective intelligence sharing, tracking resource flows, and dismantling the financial networks supporting illegal trade. The workshop is part of ongoing efforts in Gabon to secure its significant forest and wildlife resources, which are vital national assets.

Why it mattersGabon's natural resources are threatened by organized criminal networks involved in illegal timber and wildlife exports.
