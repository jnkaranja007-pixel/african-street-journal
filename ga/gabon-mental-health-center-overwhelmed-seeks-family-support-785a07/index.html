Gabon Mental Health Center Overwhelmed, Seeks Family Support

The National Mental Health Center in Mélen faces overcrowding due to insufficient beds and a call for increased family involvement in patient care and reintegration.

The National Mental Health Center (CNSM) in Mélen, Libreville, is struggling with its capacity, which has remained at 100 beds since its 1995 establishment. Officials are requesting greater family participation in supporting patients and their return to society. The center is forced to keep patients whose treatment is complete or advanced because they cannot yet return home, reducing bed availability for urgent cases.

This situation highlights broader challenges in Gabon's mental health system, including a lack of specialized facilities, insufficient hospital beds, and a shortage of qualified professionals. While families play a crucial role in recovery and reintegration, their support alone cannot compensate for the existing capacity limitations. The CNSM's appeal also signals a need for public authorities to strengthen infrastructure and training.

In a related effort to manage public safety and patient care, the CNSM has launched a campaign urging the public and facility managers to report individuals with psychiatric disorders wandering in public spaces. A toll-free number, 1324, has been established for these reports, particularly focusing on areas near schools ahead of the academic year's start, following a recent incident where a person with apparent psychiatric issues caused traffic disruption.

Why it mattersThe National Mental Health Center in Mélen is over capacity, impacting its ability to admit new patients and highlighting a systemic need for more resources and family support in mental healthcare.
