Gabon plans to regulate revival churches

The interior minister announced new rules for revival churches, drawing parallels with stricter measures in Rwanda and Angola.

Source: Gabon Review · 2026-10-22 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Gabon's new interior minister, Edgard Moukoumbi, has announced plans to regulate revival churches, stating that authorities will "look into what is happening inside them." This move follows similar actions in other African nations, where governments have tightened control over religious organizations.

In Rwanda, over 8,000 places of worship were closed by August 2024 as part of a crackdown that began in 2018. The country has implemented stringent legal recognition requirements, building compliance standards, and administrative fees for religious organizations. Angola, meanwhile, requires confessions to gather 60,000 signatures, with at least 1,000 from each of the country's 18 provinces, alongside notarized documents and financial disclosures for legal recognition.

These international examples highlight the potential for strict oversight in Gabon's religious sector. While the stated aim is to protect congregants and ensure safety, the experiences of Rwanda and Angola also raise concerns about potential impacts on freedom of worship and the administrative burden placed on religious groups.
