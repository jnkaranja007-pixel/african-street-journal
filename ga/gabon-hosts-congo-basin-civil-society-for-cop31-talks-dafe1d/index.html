Gabon hosts Congo Basin civil society for COP31 talks

Over 50 participants from six countries met in Libreville to forge a common stance on climate finance and local rights.

Source: Gabon Review · 2026-10-31 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Libreville, Gabon's capital, became the center for green diplomacy on October 28, 2026, hosting the third regional conference for Congo Basin civil society organizations. Over two days, more than 50 participants from Cameroon, Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Central African Republic, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Gabon gathered to build a unified position before COP31.

The conference, themed "A resilient Congo Basin: forests, populations, finances and nature," focused on the role of local communities and indigenous peoples in climate action. Participants, including representatives from women-led organizations and researchers, aimed to ensure the Congo Basin, the world's second-largest rainforest, presents a coordinated front at the upcoming climate summit.

Key demands from indigenous peoples included effective participation in decision-making, formal recognition of their rights, reduced extractive industries on their territories, and direct access to climate finance. They highlighted that only 2.1% of climate funds currently reach forest guardians, underscoring the need for equitable financial flows.
