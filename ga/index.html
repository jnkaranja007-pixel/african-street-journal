Port-Gentil Men Detained for Cannabis Seizure

Two Gabonese men are in custody after authorities found 127 packages of cannabis in Ndougou.

Hendrick Mbina and Patrice Moussavou Mouloungui were placed in provisional detention on August 28 in Port-Gentil's central prison. They face charges related to the seizure of 127 packages of Indian hemp discovered in Ndougou, Ogooué-Maritime province. The men were arrested on August 23 during an operation where investigators also found paper fragments possibly used for preparing the drug.

Mbina admitted to possessing cannabis but denied trafficking, claiming it was for personal consumption and use during agricultural work. Mouloungui, present in Ndougou for professional reasons, stated the cannabis was not in his room and belonged to his brother, who acknowledged ownership. Investigators found six rifle cartridges in Mouloungui's room.

The two men were presented to an investigating judge on August 28 and subsequently detained. The investigation aims to determine the origin and intended destination of the 127 packages, as well as the precise roles of Mbina and Mouloungui. Authorities will decide if the quantity was for personal use or part of a wider distribution network.

Why it mattersTwo Gabonese men are detained, facing charges related to a significant cannabis seizure, impacting their freedom and potentially leading to legal penalties.

Gabon Leaders Pray for Nation, Courted for Sahel Diplomacy

Religious leaders held a national prayer on August 30, while President Oligui Nguema met with an African Union envoy discussing regional crises.

Gabonese religious leaders, including Pastor Bruno Georges Ngoussi, organized a national prayer service on Sunday, August 30, 2026, at the Nazareth Church. The event, coinciding with the Fête de la Libération, aimed to imbue the national holiday with spiritual significance and focus on prayer and thanksgiving for the nation. Organizers stated the gathering was intended for reflection on the country's progress, challenges, and future, urging the faithful to pray for Gabon.

The religious leaders emphasized that August 30 holds a spiritual dimension beyond its political commemoration, calling for unity among church components. Pastor Ngoussi referenced scripture to highlight prayer's role in national healing and offered a message of hope, stating that the visible unity of the church signifies divine action. The gathering also aimed to encourage leaders to pursue decisions beneficial for the country's development and to seek divine wisdom for them.

On the same day, August 30, 2026, in Makokou, President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema received Mamadou Tangara, the African Union's special representative for Mali and the Sahel. Tangara suggested that the Gabonese leader possesses the capacity to offer solutions to the Sahel region's crises, potentially positioning Libreville for a greater diplomatic role. Tangara praised Gabon's transition as a model and highlighted the president's political wisdom and pragmatism.

Why it mattersReligious leaders and an African Union envoy met with the Gabonese president on August 30, 2026, to discuss national spiritual well-being and potential diplomatic solutions for the Sahel.

Activists demand justice for 2016 election violence victims

Ten years after post-election unrest, Tournons La Page-Gabon calls for truth, justice, and recognition of victims.

Tournons La Page-Gabon (TLP-Gabon) is demanding accountability a decade after violent events surrounding the 2016 presidential election. The coalition is calling for truth, justice, and recognition for victims, stating that lasting reconciliation cannot be built on silence. The group highlighted the deaths, injuries, arrests, disappearances, and trauma that occurred, marking the day as one of the darkest in recent Gabonese history.

The national coordinator for TLP-Gabon, Blanche Simonny Abegue, emphasized that the passage of time should not lead to forgetting these events, as doing so would betray the fight for democracy and the rule of law. The organization is questioning who gave orders, what responsibilities have been established, the whereabouts of victims' bodies, and which trials have taken place, asserting that these questions remain unanswered and prevent the nation from moving past this painful period.

TLP-Gabon is urging for the opening of all necessary investigations, access to case files, and justice without statute of limitations or forgetting. The coalition is also launching a campaign called "10 ans, 10 visages" (10 years, 10 faces) to give names and stories to each victim and plans to engage national and international bodies to prevent institutional oblivion. They insist that national reconciliation requires judicial truth and that "turning the page" means writing the next chapter with truth, not turning away.

Why it mattersThe activists' demands for justice and truth affect victims and their families by seeking answers and recognition for the violence they endured.

CNSS Begins School Allowance Payments Via Bank Transfer

The National Social Security Fund will disburse 2026-2027 school year allowances starting September 10, using electronic transfers nationwide.

The National Social Security Fund (CNSS) announced it will begin paying school year allowances for 2026-2027 on September 10. Payments will be made via bank transfer across the country to employers, beneficiaries, and their dependents. Companies needing to collect payment slips must provide the company's bank account details, a signed authorization, and a valid ID for the representative.

Beneficiaries who previously received allowances in cash or by check will now have funds directly deposited into their bank accounts. The CNSS urges those who collected payments at a counter and have not yet submitted their bank details to do so promptly through their personal space on the e-CNSS portal to ensure timely disbursement.

This shift to electronic payments aims to streamline the distribution of school allowances and prioritize digital transactions for the benefit of all recipients. The CNSS stated that this process facilitates the payment of school allowances and encourages electronic payment for the benefit of insured persons and their dependents.

Why it mattersThe CNSS is transitioning to electronic payments for school allowances, affecting employers and beneficiaries by requiring bank details for direct transfers.

Gabon Sugar Company Faces Financial Crisis, Turkish Investors Warn

The Sucreries du Gabon is experiencing severe financial difficulties, prompting calls for increased state support to prevent the company's collapse.

The Sucreries du Gabon is in a critical financial state, with its general director, Ousmane Sall Mabignat, warning that the company's financial health is "very, very, very difficult and very threatened." Mabignat stated that the majority shareholder feels "totally abandoned" by the Gabonese administration. He indicated that investments made over the past two years may not be sustainable long-term without greater visibility and commitment from the state.

This alert comes as the company's industrial and agricultural operations depend heavily on a consolidated partnership. The director expressed concern that the Turkish investors might disengage if the Gabonese state does not provide adequate support. He described the situation as a plea for the administration to bolster the operator before it "runs out of steam and leaves."

Haut-Ogooué governor Jacques Denis Tsanga visited the Franceville plant on August 28, 2026, to assess the situation. He assured company officials of the state's commitment, promising that "the state's commitment will be respected so that this tool, once again, is preserved." The governor called for collective engagement to ensure the company's future, which was taken over by Turkish investors two years ago.

Why it mattersTurkish investors are affected by the financial difficulties of Sucreries du Gabon, risking disengagement without increased state support.
