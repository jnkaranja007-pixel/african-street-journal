Gabon connects Kinguélé Aval dam to Libreville grid

Eight days of work to connect the 35 MW Kinguélé Aval hydroelectric plant to the Libreville power network.

The Société d’énergie et d’eau du Gabon (SEEG) began final connections for the Kinguélé Aval hydroelectric plant to Libreville’s 225 kV power line on November 6. The work, conducted by Sinohydro until November 13, aims to integrate 35 MW of new electricity into the capital region’s supply.

This connection process requires temporary reductions in power output from other plants, leading to rotating power outages in Grand-Libreville for eight days. SEEG estimates a deficit of 40 to 50 MW during peak evening hours, imposing approximately three-hour blackouts.

Once operational, Kinguélé Aval will provide an additional 35 MW, representing about 13% of Grand-Libreville’s energy needs. The plant, built on the Mbei river, is Gabon’s first public-private hydroelectric partnership and is expected to produce around 200 GWh of renewable electricity annually.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in Grand-Libreville will experience rotating power outages for eight days while the Kinguélé Aval hydroelectric plant is connected to the main power grid.

Gabon goalkeeper Junior Bekale suffers mid-air seizure

The national football team, the Panthers, faces squad changes before their match against Lesotho due to player illness and injury.

Gabon's national football team, the Panthers, is adjusting its squad for an upcoming match against Lesotho in Franceville on Thursday. Goalkeeper Junior François Bekale experienced a seizure on a flight to join the team, leading to his withdrawal for medical evaluation. Orphé Mbina is also unavailable due to a persistent adductor issue.

The Gabon Football Federation (Fegafoot) reported on November 8, 2026, that Bekale suffered a five-minute convulsive seizure on an Africa United flight from Libreville to Mvengué. Medical staff on board provided immediate assistance, and his condition reportedly improved. He is being evacuated to Hôpital d’instruction des armées Omar Bongo Ondimba in Libreville for further tests, and the team's medical staff has decided he should rest.

Joël Mbore, a goalkeeper for Mangasport, has been called up to replace Bekale, marking his first national team selection. Alan Do Marcolino will replace Mbina, who requires a week's rest for his left adductor problem. These changes come amid heightened medical caution following Bekale's incident.

Why it mattersGabon's national football team is experiencing player withdrawals due to medical issues ahead of an important match.

Gabon ministry prepares church synod to end crisis

The Ministry of Interior plans a national synod in December 2026 to restore leadership legitimacy and unity within the Evangelical Church of Gabon.

Gabon's Ministry of Interior, Security, and Decentralisation (MISD) has outlined plans for a national synod of the Evangelical Church of Gabon (EEG) in early December 2026. The aim is to restore legitimacy to the church's leadership and foster unity after a period of division. Preparatory work, involving consultations with current church bodies and stakeholders, concluded on November 6, 2026.

The proposed synod follows a decision made on August 14, 2026, and endorsed by the Council of Ministers on September 18, 2026. It prioritises organising a new national synod over extending the mandate of existing, contested leadership. The current EEG bodies will convene the synod to ensure procedural regularity, with a focus on electing new delegates from parishes and synodal regions for inclusive representation.

The governance of the EEG is also set for renewal, with plans to establish a new National Council and its bureau. Candidates for the position of President Pastor will be open to all, with consensus being the preferred method for selection. Voting will only be considered if two opposing candidacies emerge. These measures aim to provide a fresh start and lasting unity for the church.

Politics · 2026-11-10

Gabon minister inspects resettlement site after demolitions

Minister Millot Lambert Mouvinde visited Malibé 2, a 15-hectare site for families displaced by urban modernization projects.

Gabon's Minister of Public Works and Construction, Millot Lambert Mouvinde, inspected the Malibé 2 resettlement site on November 6. The 15-hectare area is designated to house families displaced by demolitions that began on November 4 in Okala, Lac Bleu, and Diba-Diba as part of the Grand-Libreville modernization. Mouvinde's visit aimed to assess progress and reassure affected residents.

On site, the minister reviewed the preparation of the Malibé 2 area, which is intended to accommodate households affected by the clearing of land for new roads. "With reinforced logistics, teams are accelerating operations and working to prepare the zone to allow our compatriots to be relocated with dignity," Mouvinde stated, emphasizing adherence to deadlines for road projects. He also discussed the importance of the projects for improving urban mobility and access to strategic infrastructure.

The urban renewal operation, launched by Vice-President Hermann Immongault, has raised concerns among residents who seek more than just housing. They are requesting essential services like roads, clean water, electricity, schools, health centers, and economic opportunities. The government's approach at Malibé 2 seeks to balance land acquisition, resettlement, and urban development for social acceptance.

Why it mattersGabon residents displaced by urban development projects face uncertainty regarding their relocation and the provision of essential services.

Education · 2026-11-10

Gabon prepares to teach national languages in schools

Experts and officials met in Libreville to review a study on integrating local languages into the curriculum.

Gabon is moving closer to introducing its national languages into the education system. On November 7, experts and officials in Libreville reviewed a study on teaching mother tongues in schools. This initiative, supported by the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF), aims to improve children's understanding of lessons and preserve linguistic heritage.

The study, conducted in three provinces, involved 175 school heads and focused on pedagogical and institutional conditions for integrating local languages. Experts like Pr Marie-France Andeme Allogho suggest a gradual approach, starting with a limited number of languages and schools. The OIF will assist with curriculum development and teacher training in collaboration with the Ministry of National Education.

Currently, three languages – Fang, Nzèbi, and Myènè – have emerged as priorities from the study. An experimental phase is planned to assess deployment conditions and necessary adaptations before any wider rollout. The project seeks to both preserve national languages and foster better learning environments for Gabonese students.
