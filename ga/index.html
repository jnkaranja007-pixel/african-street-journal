Gabon's Power Struggle Unearths Billions in Assets

A patrimonial war over seized properties, offshore accounts, and companies reveals a complex financial landscape three years after a political transition.

Gabon's political transition three years ago has evolved into a significant patrimonial conflict, with authorities and the former presidential family locked in a dispute over vast assets. Investigations reveal accumulated debits of two thousand nine hundred two billion CFA francs and identified assets exceeding one thousand five hundred billion CFA francs. This includes numerous companies, residences, aircraft, hotels, oil permits, and offshore accounts, painting a picture of a complex financial empire.

The battleground includes seized properties like the Nam residence, estimated at eighty billion CFA francs, and the expansive Oyo domain. While some properties have been allocated to children of the former president, others remain in dispute. Legal proceedings have led to court orders for the seizure of assets from twenty-two real estate companies and stakes in other entities, highlighting the scale of the financial claims.

Financial intelligence reports point to around thirty companies, with twenty-two registered abroad, involved in these financial movements. The former presidential family disputes these findings, but the ongoing legal and financial scrutiny underscores the deep-seated issues surrounding wealth and property in the country. The conflict extends beyond political lines, focusing on the control and distribution of significant national wealth.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the seizure of assets and the ongoing disputes over companies and properties, impacting the financial landscape and potential for investment.

Gabon's 'Liberation Coup' Marks Three Years of Avoided Violence

A former war reporter reflects on the three-year anniversary of the 2023 'Coup de Liberation,' viewing it as a moment that prevented a descent into civil conflict.

Three years ago, on August 30, 2023, Gabon's history took a sharp turn. While often labeled a coup d'état, many Gabonese refer to it as the 'Coup de Liberté.' For a photojournalist with experience covering conflicts across Africa, this event signifies more than a change in power; it represents a critical juncture where the nation narrowly avoided irreparable violence.

The author draws on years of observing how electoral disputes, accumulated frustrations, resource mismanagement, growing poverty, and ethnic divisions can escalate into coups, rebellions, and civil war. Gabon, despite periods of intense political tension in 1990, 1993, 2009, and 2016, had previously steered clear of widespread conflict. The author questions how the country avoided such a fate, particularly in 2009 and 2016.

Reflecting on the decade preceding August 30, 2023, the author notes that political, social, and economic tensions had deeply fractured the nation, fostering widespread frustration, anger, and mistrust. This division had even permeated families, turning political differences into deep rifts. The author believes that without the actions of General Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema and his peers on that day, Gabon might have faced a violent and unpredictable change.

Why it mattersThe event is viewed as having prevented widespread violence and civil conflict, which would have had devastating consequences for the nation and its people.

MORENA Criticizes Government Over Social and Health Issues

The opposition party MORENA has voiced strong criticism regarding the current social conditions, public health, and governance in Gabon.

The Movement for National Redress (MORENA) in Libreville has issued a sharp critique of the current government's performance, citing widespread social difficulties, a concerning health situation, and issues with public finance management and individual liberties. Party president Luc Bengono Nsi highlighted the precarious daily lives of Gabonese citizens, struggling to make ends meet due to high living costs, unemployment, poor sanitation, inadequate housing, and limited access to water and electricity. The party also expressed alarm over the proliferation of drugs among youth, underscoring the persistent social challenges.

MORENA is particularly concerned about national health statistics, including recent mortality rates and maternal deaths. The party is demanding a rigorous and independent evaluation of the health sector, emphasizing that preventable deaths occur due to insufficient medical care. They question the efficacy of government slogans about restoring dignity when citizens are dying in undignified health conditions, urging immediate action and resource allocation to reverse this trend.

The party also addressed the public service reform, asserting that it must involve civil servants. MORENA noted that thousands of public employees are awaiting administrative regularization, reclassification, advancement, or permanent appointments. They called for an exceptional regularization of careers and a transparent review of pending cases, stating that reforms should not be conducted against, but with, the affected workers.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens are affected by precarious living conditions, inadequate healthcare, and stalled public service careers.

Gabon hires 248 volunteer teachers into public service

Two hundred forty-eight teachers who previously volunteered in Ogooué-Ivindo province have been officially integrated into the civil service.

The formal integration of 248 teachers into Gabon's public service was marked by a ceremony in Makokou on Friday, August 28, 2026. These educators had been volunteering in public schools in the Ogooué-Ivindo province for years, often under precarious administrative conditions. The event, attended by Minister of State for National Education and Civic Instruction, Camélia Ntoutoume Leclercq, involved the distribution of certificates and initial payment vouchers, recognizing their commitment, training, and qualifications.

This regularization process acknowledges the teachers' dedication and the essential role they played in the education system despite administrative uncertainties. Their integration into the civil service signifies a tangible fulfillment of promises, transitioning them from volunteer status to fully recognized and salaried public employees. Minister Ntoutoume Leclercq emphasized the professional recognition this change represents, stating they are no longer volunteers but certified educators.

The provincial director of education for Ogooué-Ivindo, Jean Lin Essié Effé, expressed gratitude to the government for its efforts in the education sector, specifically highlighting the regularization of these former volunteers. Beneficiaries shared their long-awaited satisfaction, noting that this recognition of their years of work provides renewed motivation to serve Gabonese schools with commitment and responsibility. The state aims to strengthen its teaching staff with these qualified and integrated professionals.

Why it mattersTwo hundred forty-eight volunteer teachers in Gabon have been officially hired into the public service, securing their employment and regular salaries.
