Gabon President Orders 75 New Homes and 500 Land Parcels in Makokou

President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguéma announced housing and land development plans during the Fête de la Libération celebration in Makokou.

President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguéma announced a significant expansion of housing and land development projects in Makokou during his address on the third anniversary of the Fête de la Libération. The plan includes the construction of 75 new housing units and the development of 500 plots of land for future construction. This initiative aims to extend residential development beyond the capital region.

The housing initiative comprises 50 F4-type homes, each approximately 145 square meters, built by the company Le Roi des Chantiers in the Mayiga area. An additional 25 homes are under construction by the Société Nationale Immobilière (SNI). The land development program will see the SNI prepare 500 plots, each 500 square meters, an increase from the initial 300 plots.

The President emphasized the importance of the 'Spirit of the Liberation' in correcting societal imbalances and building a disciplined nation. The celebration, held in Makokou, a region historically known for resistance, also served as a platform to highlight national unity and progress. The specifics regarding the cost, rental prices, and accessibility criteria for the new housing remain undisclosed.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the announcement of new housing construction and land development, which could signal opportunities for real estate and construction firms.

China Plans Naval Base in Gabon

Discussions with Beijing regarding a Chinese naval base in Port-Gentil remain open, signaling deepening military ties.

Gabon and China are expanding their military cooperation, with a potential Chinese naval base in Port-Gentil being a key point of discussion. This collaboration includes past agreements for military equipment donations and training, with a significant escalation in scope noted in April 2023. Recent events, such as the arrival of a Chinese defense attaché and military vehicle displays, underscore the strengthening security relationship.

The military partnership between the two nations has a long history, dating back to a 1996 agreement for military equipment and further solidified by a 2012 protocol for military assistance. In 2016, plans were discussed for a military attaché, equipment provision, and scholarships for Gabonese students. The most significant shift occurred in April 2023 with a joint declaration to enhance army cooperation in personnel training, combating cross-border crime, military technology, and joint exercises. Chinese naval forces have also conducted port calls and operational exchanges.

The prospect of a Chinese military installation in Port-Gentil represents a strategic development in this bilateral relationship. A French Senate report from January 2025 indicated that an agreement for such a base was reached prior to the August 2023 coup. Current discussions acknowledge that the possibility of a Chinese naval base in Gabon is still being actively considered by both governments.

Why it mattersGabon's government is deepening its military ties with China, which could lead to the establishment of a Chinese naval base.

Gabon Opens 24-Bed Hospital in Booué

The new facility aims to reduce long travel distances for medical care and improve access to specialized services for local populations.

Booué now has a new 24-bed hospital, complete with an operating theater, medical imaging, and neonatal care, inaugurated by President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema. This facility is intended to strengthen healthcare services and decrease the need for medical evacuations to Makokou and other centers. The 850 m² establishment will provide the populations of Lopé with closer access to medical attention, ending the necessity of traveling hundreds of kilometers for basic procedures like ultrasounds.

Previously, patients requiring specialized care or facing emergencies had to travel significant distances, a situation particularly challenging for pregnant women and newborns. The hospital features a surgery block with a laboratory, an administrative and consultation wing, and a medical imaging service. A separate wing houses the 24 beds, including a dedicated space for neonatology. Three generators ensure operational continuity, with one exclusively powering the operating theater, addressing potential electricity access issues in the region.

The project, funded by SETRAG as part of its corporate social responsibility, represents an investment of nearly 1.5 billion FCFA, with 1.1 billion FCFA allocated to construction. Two residences for the lead physician and their deputy are also included to facilitate staff retention. The government plans to ensure the hospital's effective operation, with medical staff availability and consistent supply of equipment being key challenges for meeting community expectations.

Why it mattersResidents of Booué and the Lopé region will benefit from reduced travel times and improved access to essential medical services, including specialized care and emergency treatment.

Gabon Launches $200 Million Fund for Conservation and Community Development

The 'Gabon Infini' project aims to mobilize up to $200 million over ten years to support rural communities and protect unique ecosystems.

Gabon has officially launched "Gabon Infini," a $200 million financing mechanism designed to bolster rural community development and enhance ecosystem conservation. The initiative, also known internationally as a Project for Permanence Financing (PFP), was formalized on August 29, 2026, in Makokou. The project brings together the Gabonese government, The Nature Conservancy (TNC), Enduring Earth, and other partners, with the goal of mobilizing funds over a decade to benefit local populations and biodiversity.

This program aligns with Gabon's National Growth and Development Plan for 2026-2030, emphasizing a human-centered approach to development. Vice-President Hermann Immongault stated the vision is to place people and communities at the core of progress, creating local economic opportunities and strengthening livelihoods. The project also supports the Community and Conservation Development Plan (PDCC), which includes expanding protected areas by an additional 3.7 million hectares, adding to the existing 3.8 million hectares already under protection.

The initiative seeks to create a development model that balances natural space conservation with improved living conditions for nearby populations. Jennifer Morris, CEO of The Nature Conservancy, highlighted the necessity of local involvement for successful conservation, stating that sustainable conservation requires respecting and integrating the communities protecting these areas. Gabon aims to protect 30% of its terrestrial, freshwater, and marine ecosystems by 2030.

Why it mattersThis initiative directly impacts rural communities by creating economic opportunities and strengthening livelihoods through conservation efforts.

Gabon Lawmaker Reports on Legislative Session to Owendo Constituents

Jeanne Mbagou Matsougou detailed the adoption of 23 texts, including nationality and social media reforms, during a public meeting.

Jeanne Mbagou Matsougou, the representative for Owendo's second district, met with her constituents to present the outcomes of the 14th legislature's first ordinary session. The meeting focused on the 23 legislative texts definitively adopted by the National Assembly between March 1 and June 30, 2026, out of 34 examined. These included ratified ordinances and parliamentary initiatives aimed at regulating congressional meeting protocols.

Key reforms discussed by the lawmaker covered the Nationality Code, social media usage, and land laws, alongside ratified ordinances. Mbagou shared a personal anecdote about being falsely reported dead on Facebook to highlight the need for responsible social media use. She emphasized her role as a representative of all constituents, not just a political party, aiming for transparency and regular dialogue.

The event, attended by community leaders and residents, aimed to bridge the perceived gap between parliamentary work and daily realities. Mbagou intends to make these reporting sessions a regular occurrence, with another planned for December. This initiative seeks to foster better understanding and maintain consistent communication between the elected official and her district.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens in Owendo are affected by the legislative updates on nationality, social media, and land laws discussed by their representative.
