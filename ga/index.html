Gabon health ministry warns of late stroke treatment

Nearly 80% of stroke patients arrive at hospitals after the window for life-saving thrombolysis treatment has closed.

In Gabon, almost 80% of stroke patients reach hospitals too late for effective treatment, according to the Ministry of Health. This delay means victims often miss the critical window for thrombolysis, a procedure that can limit disability and save lives. The Centre hospitalier universitaire de Libreville (CHUL) saw its stroke admission rate rise from 42% in 2004 to 62.5% in 2025.

Minister of Health, Pr Elsa Nkana Joséphine Ayo épouse Bivigou, urged the public to recognize stroke symptoms and call for immediate help. A dedicated neurovascular unit at CHUL, established in 2021, has performed about 20 thrombolysis interventions. Hospital mortality for stroke patients has decreased from 9.5% in 2005 to 3.9% in 2026, but access to treatment remains limited by cost, as it is not covered by CNAMGS.

The ministry is advocating for more equitable access to stroke treatments and improved care throughout the entire chain, from initial alert to rehabilitation. Key risk factors include high blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, inactivity, overweight, and excessive alcohol consumption. The campaign's slogan emphasizes recognizing signs, alerting emergency services, and acting without delay.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens are at risk of severe disability or death from strokes because they are not receiving timely medical intervention.

Politics · 2026-11-01

Gabon civil society demands land deal explanation

11 groups want parliament to question ministers over a plot given to the ruling party.

A coalition of Gabonese civil society groups has asked the National Assembly to question government officials about land allocated to the ruling party. The Union for Democracy and Builders (UDB) received a plot in Libreville's Plaine-Orety area. This site was previously declared for public utility, and residents were displaced in 2025.

The groups are demanding answers from Vice-President Hermann Immongault and Habitat Minister Mays Mouissi regarding the land's allocation. They seek clarity on the legal basis, size, and value of the land, as well as the decision-maker's identity. The UDB stated on October 24, 2026, that the land was acquired from competent authorities, but this has not resolved concerns.

The coalition also questions potential site reclassification and conflicts of interest, as the UDB's secretary-general holds a government position in the housing sector. They requested documentation of procedures and information on other beneficiaries. The groups also asked for a temporary halt to construction if necessary and clarification for displaced populations.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens are concerned about the transparency and legality of government land allocation processes, particularly when it affects public utility sites and displaced populations.

Business · 2026-11-01

Gabon minister asks BEAC to ease property loan rules

Housing Minister Mays Mouissi met BEAC Governor Yvon Sana Bangui to discuss high interest rates and access to credit.

Gabon's Housing Minister Mays Mouissi met with the governor of the Bank of Central African States (BEAC), Yvon Sana Bangui, in Libreville on October 30, 2026. They discussed ways to make it easier for Gabonese households to get loans for buying property. The minister specifically raised concerns about the high interest rates charged by banks and difficulties in accessing financial information for loan applicants.

Governor Yvon Sana Bangui explained how the structure of bank deposits influences interest rates and the cost of credit in the region. The two officials agreed on the need to further explore options for making property loans more accessible. They considered measures to better regulate interest rates, which could improve borrowing conditions for families.

This meeting, held during the Open House for Housing event in Libreville from October 29 to 31, 2026, aims to support housing development by addressing financial barriers. The ministry is working with financial sector players to find solutions tailored to household budgets and facilitate property ownership.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens seeking to buy homes face difficulties due to high interest rates and access to credit, as discussed by the housing minister and the central bank governor.

Health · 2026-11-01

Gabon health minister inspects Mouila health facilities

Minister Elsa Nkana Bivigou reviewed services from regional health direction to a provincial training school.

Gabon's Health Minister Elsa Nkana Bivigou inspected health facilities in Mouila on October 29, 2026. The minister visited the Regional Health Direction, the Ambulatory Treatment Centre, the urban health centre, maternal and child health services, the epidemiological base, and the provincial school for health workers. Her goal was to assess operations, identify issues, and improve care for the population.

During her tour, Minister Bivigou examined how services function, staff working conditions, and facility capacities. Discussions with managers revealed several shortcomings, leading to recommendations and requests for further explanation on operational reports. Working sessions are planned to determine corrective measures.

The minister also focused on the presence of specialist doctors redeployed to regional hospitals, a policy aimed at bringing specialized care closer to rural populations. Initial observations suggest this initiative is encouraging, with facilities seeing significant patient numbers seeking medical expertise now available in the provinces.

Why it mattersGabon's populations in Mouila will receive improved healthcare quality and access to specialist doctors.
