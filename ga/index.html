Teenage Daughter Convicted of Mother's Involuntary Homicide in Okondja

A 13-year-old girl, G.M., received a 12-month prison sentence, with seven months suspended, and a 3 million CFA franc fine for her mother's death after she put camphor in her.

A court in Franceville has convicted a 13-year-old girl, identified as G.M., of involuntary homicide in the death of her mother, Esther Diki Nzoghet. The verdict, delivered on August 19, included a sentence of 12 months in prison, with seven months suspended, and a fine of 3 million CFA francs. The incident occurred on June 13, 2024, in Okondja, Haut-Ogooué province.

G.M. admitted to investigators that she put camphor in her mother's juice, intending to make her sleep so she could go out at night without parental supervision. Her mother, Esther Diki Nzoghet, fell ill after consuming the drink and died shortly after being taken to the city's medical center. The court considered her young age and consistent statements, leading to the involuntary homicide conviction instead of poisoning.

The teenager had been held at Franceville central prison for approximately six months before being granted provisional release. Her lawyer, Maitre Yoni Andy Nkum’Enguie, acknowledged her guilt but argued for a suspended sentence, while the public prosecutor requested leniency due to her age at the time of the events.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects families in Gabon, particularly those with teenagers, by highlighting the tragic consequences of parental control disputes and the judicial system's response to juvenile offenses.

Ogooué-Ivindo installs electric fences, revives rural agriculture

The province of Ogooué-Ivindo has deployed 485 mobile electric fences across 190 villages, reducing elephant incursions and boosting local food production.

Ogooué-Ivindo, previously affected by repeated elephant incursions into plantations, is experiencing a period of calm. The province now has the largest national deployment of 485 mobile electric fences in 190 villages, a measure that helps secure crops, enhance food security, and gradually restart agricultural activity.

This initiative follows public responses to community protests, including a "ville morte" operation in Mékambo in 2021 and a blockade in Batouala in April 2023 by women denouncing elephant damage. Since 2022, the Ministry of Water and Forests has received technical support from the NGO Space for Giants to install these solar-powered fences, which deter elephants without harming them.

The impact is visible in places like Lata, where residents have returned and agricultural activities resumed after fences were installed in 2024. Markets in Makokou now show increased availability of local cassava and bananas, which had declined in recent years, indicating a revival of the rural economy.

Why it mattersRural communities in Ogooué-Ivindo are directly affected by improved food security and renewed agricultural opportunities due to reduced elephant damage.

US Judge Invalidates Visa Suspension for 75 Nations

A New York federal judge invalidated the Trump administration's decision to suspend immigration visa processing for citizens of 75 countries, including Gabon.

A federal judge in New York, Jeannette Vargas, ruled on Friday, August 21, 2026, that the US government overstepped its authority by suspending certain immigration visa applications. This decision impacts citizens from 75 nations, including Gabon, who were previously unable to process their visa applications at US embassies in their home countries.

The judge determined that the policy, implemented in January, lacked sufficient legal basis and that the executive branch exceeded its rights by targeting applicants based on financial criteria. This ruling means that visa refusals based solely on this policy can no longer be upheld, potentially allowing the US Embassy in Libreville to resume visa issuance.

Previously, applicants from affected countries, such as Nigeria, Afghanistan, Somalia, Brazil, and Iran, were required to travel to third countries like Equatorial Guinea or DR Congo to apply for visas. This judicial decision marks a significant setback for the Trump administration's immigration policies, which have faced repeated challenges in federal courts.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens seeking to immigrate to the United States may now be able to process their visa applications directly at the US Embassy in Libreville, avoiding the need to travel to other countries.

Education · 08/25/2026

Gabon introduces national languages in schools after English primary rollout

Gabon's Ministry of National Education is preparing to progressively introduce national languages into classrooms, following the recent implementation of English in primary.

The Ministry of National Education plans to integrate national languages into the school system, a project already assigned to a technical committee. This initiative aims to strengthen social cohesion and promote the country's cultural heritage, according to Minister Camélia Ntoutoume Leclercq.

This move follows the experimental introduction of English in primary schools, which included teacher training, curriculum development, and the creation of the first Gabonese English textbook for the first year of primary school. The English textbook incorporates familiar Gabonese elements like names, faces, fruits, places, and monuments.

Minister Leclercq stated that the introduction of national languages is a continuation of an educational ambition that balances cultural identity with international openness. This reform aligns with the 2010 General States of Education, Gabon's 2022 accession to the Commonwealth, and the 2024 Curricular Orientation Framework.

Why it mattersThis change affects all Gabonese students by introducing national languages into their curriculum, aiming to connect them with their cultural heritage while also providing English language skills.

Business · 08/25/2026

Maripoma Gabon mobilizes 144 machines for Makokou-Mékambo-Ekata road

Maripoma Gabon presented 144 new machines and vehicles on August 24, 2026, to accelerate work on the 260-kilometer Makokou-Mékambo-Ekata road project.

The Makokou-Mékambo-Ekata road project, extending to the Congo-Brazzaville border, is entering an operational phase following the presentation of 144 machines by Maripoma Gabon. The equipment, including 42 pick-ups, 32 trucks, 12 compactors, 9 graders, 6 bulldozers, 6 tank carriers, 6 tank trucks, and 4 hydraulic excavators, was showcased on August 24, 2026, at Angondjé.

Public Works and Construction Minister Edgard Moukoumbi attended the presentation, highlighting the project's importance for the Ogooué-Ivindo region. He stated that the mobilization of these machines signals the effective start of the project, which aims to open up territories and improve mobility for residents of Makokou and Mékambo.

The 260-kilometer road is considered a significant infrastructure project for the province, with the government aiming for it to support heavy transport. The project is also expected to create two thousand direct jobs and one thousand two hundred indirect jobs, alongside plans for boreholes, electrical infrastructure, public lighting, and the construction or renovation of schools and dispensaries.

Why it mattersResidents of Makokou and Mékambo will benefit from improved access and new infrastructure, including schools and dispensaries, as well as job opportunities.
