Chinese firm plans two manganese plants and 800 jobs in Gabon

Inner Mongolia Xinchuang Metallurgical Co. Ltd, a silicomanganese producer, wants to mine manganese in Okondja and build two processing units in Gabon.

Inner Mongolia Xinchuang Metallurgical Co. Ltd plans to invest in mining and processing manganese in Gabon, with two industrial units planned: one for silicomanganese and one for ferromanganese. The company already takes 35% of its manganese from the Moanda mine in Gabon.

Mines Minister Sosthène Nguema Nguema visited the company's facilities in Wulanchabu, Inner Mongolia, on 8 November 2026 to check its technical capacity and environmental standards. Company officials say the investment could create about 800 direct jobs, 200 in mining and 600 in the processing units.

The company holds a manganese research permit in Okondja, in Haut-Ogooué, and will apply for an exploitation permit once resources are assessed. Construction of the processing units is planned to start in June 2027 and take two years, with production targeted for December 2027.

Gabon launches guide to track public spending

A new national guide aims to harmonize government project oversight and curb wasteful spending.

Gabon officially launched a national guide in Libreville on November 11 to standardize how government actions are monitored. The Guide national de suivi et d’évaluation des politiques publiques, developed with the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), will track projects under the 2026-2030 National Growth and Development Plan (PNCD). This tool aims to improve investment steering and project oversight.

The guide seeks to ensure public resources produce measurable results and limit unproductive investments, often called 'white elephants'. The Ministry of Planning and Prospective will coordinate its implementation with sector ministries, harmonizing tools and consolidating information. Each administration remains responsible for its investments and the accuracy of its data.

A training program will follow the guide's presentation to ensure stakeholders can use it effectively. The goal is to make monitoring and evaluation a regular management tool, not just a post-project exercise. This initiative is part of the PNCD's effort to improve public investment execution rates.

Why it mattersThe government has introduced a new guide to monitor public spending and ensure projects deliver results, starting November 11.

Gabon seeks fairer EU raw materials partnership

Gabon's National Assembly president called for local processing, technology transfer, and job creation in producer countries.

Gabon's National Assembly president, Michel Régis Onanga Ndiaye, called for a more equitable economic partnership with the European Union during the second EU-ACP Parliamentary Assembly in Brussels. He advocated for increased local transformation of raw materials, technology transfers, and job creation in producer nations, emphasizing industrial sovereignty in cooperation.

Ambassador Régis Immongault stated that access to strategic raw materials should not be the sole objective of the EU-ACP partnership. The Gabonese delegation, also including Senate First Vice-President Serge Maurice Mabiala, pushed for a better distribution of economic benefits from natural resource exploitation. Gabon wants productive investments, technology, local industries, and skilled jobs, not just raw material supply.

This demand aligns with Gabon's economic diversification goals, aiming to move beyond exporting unprocessed raw materials, as shown by the planned ban on raw manganese exports from January 1, 2029. Gabon also seeks participation in the future Africa-EU Parliamentary Working Group on critical raw materials to defend producers' interests in resource governance.

Why it mattersGabon's National Assembly president demanded that producer countries be fully involved in the value addition of their resources, seeking investment and job creation.

Gabon gives security forces human rights manual

Justice Minister Augustin Emane presented the training manual to defense and security forces in Libreville on November 10.

Gabon has officially adopted a new training manual on human rights for its defense and security forces. The Minister of Justice, Augustin Emane, presented the document in Libreville on November 10, on behalf of the Vice-President of the government, Régis Immongault. The ceremony was attended by the interim UN Resident Coordinator, Dr. Xavier Crépin, and senior military officials.

The manual, presented by the Director General of Human Rights, Edna-Paola Biyogou-Minko, focuses on five pillars: alignment with Gabon's international commitments, regulating the use of force, protecting fundamental freedoms, combating cross-border crime, and non-discrimination. Minister Emane emphasized that lasting security cannot be built at the expense of fundamental rights and urged the forces to use the manual for professional transformation.

The document, handed over by Dr. Crépin to representatives of the Gendarmerie, National Police, and Penitentiary Security, is now entering its implementation phase. The manual aims to translate universal norms into practices suitable for operational realities, guiding security actions with respect for human dignity, necessity, and proportionality in the use of force.

Why it mattersThe defense and security forces will now use a new manual to guide their actions, aiming for respect for human dignity and fundamental rights.

Gabon and Burkina Faso renew cooperation ties

14 years after their first meeting, officials from Gabon and Burkina Faso met in Libreville to boost bilateral relations and create tangible benefits for their populations.

Gabon and Burkina Faso opened the second session of their Joint Cooperation Commission in Libreville on November 11, aiming to transform their long-standing relationship into an operational partnership. This meeting, 14 years after the first in Ouagadougou, seeks to create measurable benefits for the populations of both nations.

The session was officially launched by Valentin Louemba Bayonne, Secretary General of the Gabonese Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and Hermann Yerigouin Toé, his Burkinabe counterpart. Both officials emphasized the need to move beyond intentions and build a pragmatic cooperation with achievable, mutually beneficial projects.

The three-day meeting, held on November 12 and 13, will focus on updating the legal framework, examining about 20 cooperation instruments, and addressing key sectors including agriculture, trade, economy, professional training, higher education, research, culture, youth, sports, communication, security, and consular affairs.

Why it mattersOfficials from Gabon and Burkina Faso are meeting to update legal frameworks and explore cooperation in sectors like agriculture and trade, aiming for tangible benefits for their populations.
