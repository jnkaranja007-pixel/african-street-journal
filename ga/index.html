Gabon Central Purchasing Agency expands market access in Makokou

The Gabon Central Purchasing Agency (CEAG) launched a three-day Mega Market in Makokou, Ogooué-Ivindo, offering essential goods at accessible prices ahead of the August 30.

The Gabon Central Purchasing Agency (CEAG) held a Mega Market in Makokou from August 24 to August 26, providing residents with access to food and essential products at what it described as accessible prices. This initiative aimed to help counter high living costs in the region, coinciding with preparations for the National Liberation Day celebration on August 30.

The market's opening was attended by Senate President Huguette Nyana and Ogooué-Ivindo Governor Christiane Leckat. CEAG's Director General, Théophile Boutamba, stated that the agency's presence in Makokou demonstrates its commitment to bringing its offerings closer to populations outside of Greater Libreville.

This operation in Makokou follows similar deployments in Akanda, Nzeng-Ayong, Owendo, and PK areas in Estuaire, as well as in Port-Gentil and Minvoul. The CEAG aims for these Mega Markets to allow more people to access a diverse range of products at affordable prices, though the long-term challenge remains establishing regular supply mechanisms beyond temporary events.

Why it mattersResidents in Makokou benefited from reduced prices on essential goods, directly impacting their household budgets during the three-day market event.

Road accidents kill four people in Gabon in one day

Two separate road accidents on Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, 2026, resulted in four fatalities, prompting calls for increased vigilance on Gabonese roads.

Four people died in two distinct road accidents across Gabon on Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, 2026. The first incident occurred in the afternoon on the Pegnoundou-Tchibanga road in Nyanga province, where Papy Donald Boussougou, a thirty-seven-year-old Gabonese man, died in a motorcycle accident. Judicial sources reported that he was a manager at the Mougoutsi Departmental Council.

Initial findings suggest Boussougou lost control of his two-wheeler while traveling at high speed, leading to a violent fall. Authorities noted significant head trauma and nasal hemorrhage. His body was transported to a funeral home after the scene was processed.

The second accident, which also claimed two lives, took place in Essasssa on the same day. Investigations have been opened by competent authorities to determine the precise circumstances of both incidents, highlighting the need for greater caution, especially among two-wheel vehicle operators who are particularly vulnerable in crashes.

Why it mattersFour individuals lost their lives in two separate road accidents, directly impacting their families and communities.

Nelyo criticizes artists' silence after Big Monster's assault

Gabonese artist Nelyo has publicly condemned the lack of solidarity among local artists following the assault of fellow artist Big Monster, whose video circulated widely online.

Gabonese artist Nelyo has voiced his indignation over the perceived silence of other artists after a video showing Big Monster being assaulted went viral. Nelyo, a Gabonese artist, stated, "Where are the Gabonese artists? No solidarity," and called for mobilization to find the alleged attackers.

Big Monster, a comedian and web-humorist, reported on his official page that the incident occurred at PK9 after returning from a filming session. He stated that an individual he did not know accused him of insults during a live session, then repeatedly slapped and humiliated him before calling for backup.

Big Monster, who has dwarfism, later announced that his alleged attacker had been found and that the matter was with the gendarmerie. The case is now in the hands of the justice system, according to Gabon Actu.

Why it mattersThe Gabonese artistic community is affected by the call for greater solidarity and the legal proceedings following the assault of a prominent artist.

Gabonese journalist Rudy Hombenet dies two months after top award

Rudy Hombenet Anvingui, a journalist for L'Union newspaper, died in Libreville on August 27, 2026, just two months after being named Best Print Journalist at the Makongonio 2026.

Rudy Hombenet Anvingui, a prominent journalist with the daily newspaper L'Union, passed away in Libreville during the night of August 26 to August 27, 2026. Her death occurred two months after she received the award for Best Print Journalist at the Makongonio 2026, the Gabonese press awards, on June 28. She had worked for L'Union since 2015, following her studies at Omar-Bongo University.

Hombenet, who was born in Moanda, had over ten years of experience in journalism and was known for her focus on social issues, education, and health. In recent months, her columns in "Espace Santé" addressed concrete health topics relevant to daily Gabonese life, including tuberculosis, depression, malaria, self-medication, and hospital operations.

Her passing is particularly impactful as she was actively working and had recently gained significant public recognition. According to Gabonactu.com, she died at the Polyclinique El Rapha in Libreville two days after giving birth to a daughter, though the circumstances following the birth were not specified by a colleague.

Why it mattersThe death of Rudy Hombenet affects readers of L'Union and the broader Gabonese public who followed her reporting on critical social and health issues, as her voice advocating for the voiceless is now absent.
