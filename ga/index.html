Gabon receives 96 tonnes of Chinese apples

A shipment of Huaniu apples arrived in Libreville following a trade deal at a Madrid exhibition.

Gabon has received its first shipment of 96 tonnes of Chinese Huaniu apples in Libreville. The fruit departed from China's Gansu province on October 30, 2026, and is expected to take 70 days to reach its destination by sea. This marks the initial export of fresh agricultural products from Gansu to Gabon via cross-border e-commerce.

The trade agreement for these apples was finalized in October at the Fruit Attraction 2026 trade fair in Madrid, Spain. Li Zhi, general manager of a Tianshui international trade company, stated that African markets show stable demand for quality fresh fruit. This export aims to increase the direct sales of fresh fruit from Gansu, where apple production reached 2.03 million tonnes this year.

The arrival coincides with Gabonese President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema's state visit to China, which began on November 3, 2026. This venture opens new commercial prospects between China and Central Africa for fresh produce.

Why it mattersGabon is receiving a new type of fruit for its market.

Gabon firms sign iron ore rail deal

REMINAC and SETRAG will negotiate transporting iron ore from Baniaka to Owendo port.

REMINAC and SETRAG signed a memorandum of understanding on October 28, 2026, in Libreville to discuss transporting iron ore by rail. The agreement aims to structure the logistics for the Baniaka iron ore project, moving future production to the port of Owendo.

The discussions will cover ore flow planning, rail transport capacity on the Transgabonais line, potential infrastructure needs, and pricing. Christian Magni, SETRAG's general director, stated the Transgabonais railway supports Gabon's economic sectors and has undergone significant track upgrades since 2016.

Thierry Makando, Genmin's country director, called the MOU a major step for the Baniaka project, emphasizing the need for a reliable and competitive logistics solution. Genmin, which is linked to the project, has already invested approximately $120 million in its development.

Why it mattersGabon's mining and transport sectors will be affected by the agreement to move iron ore by rail.

Makokou school graduates first health workers

The EPFASS in Makokou has awarded diplomas to 500 students in nursing and social work after its 2008 closure and 2025 reopening.

The first class of the Makokou School for Health and Social Action (EPFASS) received their diplomas last weekend, marking the school's first graduation since its closure in 2008. The school was rehabilitated and reopened on March 21, 2025, by President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema. The Senate President, Huguette Yvonne Nyana Ekoume-Awori Onanga, served as the godmother for the graduating class, represented by her cabinet director, Silvère Landry Boubala.

A total of 500 students completed their training across two specializations. In the nursing-assistant track, 371 out of 407 registered students were admitted, with 20 eligible for further assessment and 12 failing. The social work program saw 90 out of 97 candidates admitted. These graduates are expected to help address a shortage of healthcare personnel in the Ogooué-Ivindo province's hospitals and clinics.

The graduates are now prepared to enter the workforce and bolster the staffing levels of health facilities, particularly in the Ogooué-Ivindo province. This initiative contributes to Challenge 4 of the National Growth and Development Plan. The godmother's representative, Silvère Landry Boubala, stated that being a godmother involves commitment and support for the students' futures. The best-performing students received kits from the godmother.

Why it mattersThe graduation of 500 health and social workers from the EPFASS in Makokou aims to fill critical personnel shortages in Gabon's healthcare sector, particularly in the Ogooué-Ivindo province.

Gabon receives four bodies from Mayumba barge disaster

Three Gabonese and one Croatian national were recovered after the oil barge sank off the coast of Mayumba.

Port-Gentil received four bodies on Tuesday, November 3, following the shipwreck of an oil barge near Mayumba. The recovered individuals included three Gabonese citizens and one Croatian national. Five government ministers, the governor of Ogooué-Maritime, and local officials were present at the funeral home where the bodies were taken.

Minister of Petroleum and Gas, Clotaire Kondja, conveyed the government's condolences to the grieving families, reiterating presidential instructions for support and transparency. He stated that the bodies of the three Gabonese would be prepared at Gasepga for their families, while the Croatian national would be repatriated. An official tribute for the oil industry is scheduled for Friday at the Port-Gentil Fair.

Search operations continue for the fifth missing person from the Mayumba incident, according to Vice-Admiral Charles Huber Bekalé-Meyong, head of the National Navy's staff. The final toll of the disaster has not yet been determined, leaving families in painful anticipation.

Why it mattersFamilies of the recovered victims are receiving support and official condolences from the Gabonese government following the oil barge disaster.

Gabon hosts Italian business forum in November

Around 20 Italian companies will explore investment and trade partnerships in Libreville on November 18-19, 2026.

Libreville will host the first Italy-Gabon Business Forum on November 18 and 19, 2026. The meeting aims to boost economic exchanges between the two nations in strategic sectors. Italy's ambassador, Stefano Moscatelli, presented the event to Vice President Alexandre Barro Chambrier on November 2, 2026.

Approximately 20 Italian companies are interested in various sectors, according to Ambassador Moscatelli. Vice President Chambrier stated on Facebook that the forum will focus on agriculture, energy, water, tourism, and health, areas prioritized by President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema. The event is organized by the Italian embassy, the bilateral chamber of commerce, and the Italian Trade Agency.

The forum, titled 'Italy-Gabon: New Paths for Growth,' seeks to identify joint investment projects and foster direct connections between business operators. This initiative supports Gabon's efforts to diversify economic partnerships and attract new investments, potentially addressing areas where Gabon has significant needs.

Why it mattersGabon seeks to attract new investments and diversify economic partnerships through this forum with Italian companies.
