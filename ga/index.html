Gabon seeks balanced economic partnership with Russia

Vice-President Hermann Immongault called for mutual interests and state sovereignty at the Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow.

Gabon's Vice-President Hermann Immongault, representing President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema, proposed a more balanced economic partnership with Russia at the 3rd Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow on October 29, 2026. He emphasized mutual interests, state sovereignty, and effective commitments in bilateral cooperation.

Immongault highlighted key areas for increased collaboration, including support for SMEs, special economic zones, trade in national currencies, and modernizing port and rail infrastructure. He also pointed to energy prospects in hydropower, natural gas, and civil nuclear power, alongside accelerating local processing of raw materials like manganese before a January 2029 export ban.

The vice-president also advocated for enhanced security cooperation against terrorism and piracy in the Gulf of Guinea, and discussed challenges with student scholarship fund transfers. Gabon is also seeking Russian support in artificial intelligence for digital sovereignty and inclusive development.

Why it mattersGabon's vice-president outlined priorities for economic, security, and technological cooperation with Russia, affecting the nation's development and international relations.

Gabon hosts Congo Basin civil society for COP31 talks

Over 50 participants from six countries met in Libreville to forge a common stance on climate finance and local rights.

Libreville, Gabon's capital, became the center for green diplomacy on October 28, 2026, hosting the third regional conference for Congo Basin civil society organizations. Over two days, more than 50 participants from Cameroon, Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Central African Republic, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Gabon gathered to build a unified position before COP31.

The conference, themed "A resilient Congo Basin: forests, populations, finances and nature," focused on the role of local communities and indigenous peoples in climate action. Participants, including representatives from women-led organizations and researchers, aimed to ensure the Congo Basin, the world's second-largest rainforest, presents a coordinated front at the upcoming climate summit.

Key demands from indigenous peoples included effective participation in decision-making, formal recognition of their rights, reduced extractive industries on their territories, and direct access to climate finance. They highlighted that only 2.1% of climate funds currently reach forest guardians, underscoring the need for equitable financial flows.

Health · 2026-10-31

Gabon health ministry warns of late stroke arrivals

Nearly 80% of stroke patients reach hospitals beyond the critical window for treatment.

Gabon's Ministry of Health has raised an alarm regarding the late arrival of stroke patients at hospitals, with nearly 80% reaching facilities after the window for effective thrombolysis treatment has closed. This delay significantly hinders efforts to limit lasting damage and save lives, as every minute is critical following a stroke. The Centre hospitalier universitaire de Libreville (CHUL) saw hospital admissions for strokes rise from 42% in 2004 to 62.5% in 2025.

Minister of Health, Pr Elsa Nkana Joséphine Ayo épouse Bivigou, urged the public to recognize stroke symptoms and call for immediate emergency assistance. The CHUL has operated a neurovascular unit since 2021, performing about 20 thrombolysis interventions. Hospital mortality rates for strokes have decreased from 9.5% in 2005 to 3.9% in 2026, but access to treatment is limited by cost, as it is not covered by the CNAMGS.

The ministry advocates for more equitable access to stroke treatments and improved care across the entire chain, from initial alert to rehabilitation. Key stroke symptoms include sudden facial distortion, limb weakness, speech difficulties, and vision or balance problems. The ministry warns that delaying treatment or attempting home remedies can reduce survival and recovery chances. High blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, inactivity, excess weight, and heavy alcohol consumption are identified risk factors.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens are at risk of severe disability or death from strokes due to late hospital arrivals and limited access to time-sensitive treatments.

Gabon votes to lift US embargo on Cuba

The UN General Assembly voted on October 28, 2026, to end the US embargo on Cuba.

Gabon voted on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, in favor of a United Nations General Assembly resolution calling for the end of the United States embargo against Cuba. The vote took place in New York, with 130 countries supporting the resolution, 17 opposing, and 25 abstaining.

The resolution urges Washington to lift the economic, commercial, and financial sanctions that have been in place for over six decades. It also calls on other nations not to implement similar measures, citing violations of UN Charter principles and denouncing the extraterritorial effects of the 1996 Helms-Burton Act.

This diplomatic action occurs as Cuba faces severe economic and energy shortages, with power outages affecting essential services like hospitals. The vote shows a decline in international support compared to previous years, with 165 votes in favor in 2025 and 187 in 2024.

Why it mattersGabon's vote at the UN affects its diplomatic relations and stance on international sanctions.

Business · 2026-10-31

Gabon signs Accra Convention to speed up trade

The UN agreement aims to digitize transport documents, cut costs, and simplify import-export operations.

Gabon signed the UN Convention on Negotiable Documents for Cargo on October 26, 2026, in Accra, Ghana. This move is part of modernizing trade procedures and facilitating the movement of goods internationally. Minister of State for Transport Ulrich Manfoumbi Manfoumbi signed the agreement for Gabon.

The convention seeks to harmonize rules for issuing and using cargo documents in both paper and electronic formats across all transport modes. Gabon expects the dematerialization of transport documents to reduce administrative formalities, processing times, and transaction costs. This is intended to improve goods traceability and the security of commercial operations.

Gabon's economic ambitions rely on efficient infrastructure and fluid trade, making this convention a potential tool for competitiveness. The agreement, signed by 26 states, reflects a shared interest in legal instruments adapted to digital trade changes. Gabon now needs to implement operational measures to achieve concrete benefits for economic operators.

Why it mattersGabon's businesses and trade operations will benefit from simplified import-export processes and reduced transaction costs.
