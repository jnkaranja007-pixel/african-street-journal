Gabon spends 35 billion CFA on fuel subsidies

The government plans adjustments to fuel prices in the coming months, impacting businesses.

The Gabonese state spent 35 billion CFA francs in November to maintain fuel prices, with further adjustments expected soon. While consumers focus on pump prices, the move will increase costs for transport operators, subcontractors, and small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) without allowing them to raise their own prices.

Ike Ngouoni Aila Oyouomi, president of AILA, questions the cost of these reforms for businesses, highlighting that while the state anticipates savings, companies may absorb increased expenses. Fuel subsidies cost Gabon 1% of its GDP in 2024, with 96% benefiting the wealthiest half of the population, according to the World Bank. The price of diesel at 575 CFA per litre makes Gabon the cheapest in the CEMAC region, a difference covered by the state budget.

Businesses that face higher fuel costs see their operating results decrease, reducing the profit on which they pay taxes. This indirect subsidy means the state recovers a fraction of the increased cost through lower tax revenue. If rising prices hinder activity, they can affect employment, consumption, and ultimately state revenue. Industrial companies often source fuel outside the network at different rates, while transport and service providers, along with SMEs, typically operate on fixed prices, particularly in cities like Port-Gentil.

Business · 2026-11-16

FlyGabon aborts takeoff after alarm

A technical alert during departure on Friday, November 13, 2026, halted a FlyGabon flight from Libreville to Franceville.

A FlyGabon ATR 72-600 aircraft aborted its takeoff from Libreville on Friday, November 13, 2026, after a technical alarm sounded during the departure phase. The pilots immediately halted the plane, which had not yet left the ground, prioritizing passenger safety. The national airline stated that the incident, which occurred on the 10:30 flight to Franceville, was handled according to established procedures.

An inspection revealed a leak caused by a ruptured seal, which was promptly replaced. Passengers were then transferred to a replacement aircraft of the same type and continued their journey to Franceville without further incident. FlyGabon emphasized that rejecting a takeoff, known as RTO, is a planned procedure for which pilots are trained and regularly rehearsed on simulators.

The airline highlighted its fleet's average age of 4.2 years for its ATR aircraft and its maintained IOSA certification since 2020, an international audit assessing maintenance, operations, and safety management systems. FlyGabon stated that the incident demonstrates the effectiveness of its safety culture, which relies on vigilance, responsiveness, and transparency.

Why it mattersPassengers traveling on FlyGabon flights between Libreville and Franceville experienced a delay due to a technical issue, with a replacement aircraft provided.

Gabon's UDB party readies 200 organisers for its first congress

The Union Démocratique des Bâtisseurs holds its first ordinary congress at Libreville's Palais des Congrès on 5 December, under the theme Enracinement et Consolidation.

The Union Démocratique des Bâtisseurs (UDB), the party founded by President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema, gathered about 200 members of its organising committee on Friday 13 November at La Sablière in Libreville. The meeting reviewed the organisational structure, the responsibilities of each team and the operational calendar for the party's first ordinary congress.

Dr Séraphin Akouré-Davain, who chairs the organising committee, called the congress a decisive rendezvous with history and said it is not a simple statutory formality. He said the party must anchor itself in every province, department, canton and neighbourhood, and urged members to show cohesion, solidarity and unity of action.

The congress is scheduled for 5 December at the Palais des Congrès in Libreville. The party says its aims are to give the organisation, its territorial presence and its political project a new dimension.

Why it mattersParty members across Gabon's provinces and neighbourhoods are expected to carry the organisation's plans to local level once the congress sets its direction.
