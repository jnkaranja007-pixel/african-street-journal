Gabon seeks national savings for development plan

The government aims to leverage domestic capital for the 2026-2030 National Growth and Development Plan.

The Gabonese government is initiating a strategy to harness national savings as a key financial tool for the country's development. Minister of Economy Thierry Minko announced at the first National Savings for Development Forum (FONED) in Libreville that the nation must increase its domestic capital mobilization to fund the 2026-2030 National Growth and Development Plan (PNCD). While not excluding foreign investment, the focus is on building financial sovereignty through internal resources.

Minko emphasized that the PNCD requires significant investment in infrastructure, production, human capital, and wealth creation, necessitating diversified funding sources. He stated that economic sovereignty is built and financed, not decreed. The strategy involves integrating national savings more prominently alongside international capital, public-private partnerships, and capital markets, with the goal of attracting and multiplying the impact of foreign investment.

The potential for mobilizing savings from households, banks, insurance, pension funds, financial markets, and the diaspora is considerable. However, creating an environment of trust, ensuring investment security, and implementing efficient financial instruments are crucial. The Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (CDC) is designated to play a central role in this new financial architecture, aiming to transform mobilized savings into long-term resources for national development. The forum also introduced OKIRA 241, a CDC initiative to connect savers with investment opportunities.

Why it mattersThe Gabonese government aims to use national savings to fund its development plan, affecting businesses and individuals who can contribute to this capital.

Gabon Party Praises President's Activity, Demands Proof of Results

The RPM party congratulated President Oligui Nguema for his August efforts, focusing on health and provincial development, while urging tangible outcomes.

The Rassemblement pour la Patrie et la Modernité (RPM) party has commended President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema for his intensive national activities between August 9 and 30. The party highlighted new health sector measures, concrete achievements, the president's consistent field presence, and attention to provincial development. Specifically, the RPM noted a 55 billion CFA franc aid package for health, including funds for national technical capacity, hospital restaurants, and social security services.

The RPM also pointed to the inauguration of various health, commercial, administrative, and defense infrastructures in Grand Libreville, alongside 17 major projects in the Ogooué-Ivindo province. The party cited the Mayiga housing program, with 50 units completed and 25 under construction, as an example of development extending beyond the capital. The party echoed the president's call for a shift from promises to proof, emphasizing the need for government initiatives to demonstrably improve daily life.

Furthermore, the RPM welcomed the visit of Congolese President Félix-Antoine Tshisekedi Tshilombo and the signing of three cooperation agreements. The party encouraged the government to maintain this momentum, focusing on project execution, sustainable infrastructure operation, and youth employment. The RPM reaffirmed its support for initiatives promoting national unity and modernization, while stressing the importance of concrete impacts on citizens' lives.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens are affected by the government's focus on improving health services, infrastructure, and housing, with the RPM party urging for tangible proof of these initiatives.

Magistrates Union Blames State for Judicial Failures

The national magistrates union cited chronic underinvestment and neglected infrastructure as reasons for justice system dysfunction.

The National Union of Magistrates of Gabon (Synamag) has criticized the state's handling of the justice system, pointing to neglected infrastructure and a lack of resources following disciplinary actions against several magistrates. Synamag president Landry Abaga Essono stated that the degraded image of Gabonese justice is not due to inherent flaws but to a cumulative result of state failures, including underinvestment and prioritizing sanctions over reforms.

The union highlighted the dilapidated state of judicial buildings, noting that apart from the Libreville Palace of Justice, which recently underwent extensive renovations, no other courthouses have been built or renovated. Synamag also contested disciplinary sanctions against magistrates, deeming some disproportionate and expressed readiness to support appeals. They also raised concerns about security incidents and the non-execution of court orders by law enforcement.

Further grievances include the delayed convening of the Superior Council of the Magistracy, which was expected in July but had not met by early September, leaving magistrates uncertain about their assignments. Synamag also pointed to the lack of implementing texts for the magistrates' status and issues with the traceability of judicial revenues, suggesting potential misuse of funds.

Why it mattersMagistrates are affected by the state's failure to provide adequate infrastructure, resources, and timely administrative processes, impacting their ability to perform their duties.

Minister Inspects University Construction Sites Ahead of Academic Year

Professor Charles Edgar Mombo reviewed progress on new and renovated university buildings in Greater Libreville to ensure readiness for the 2026-2027 academic year.

Minister of Higher Education, Professor Charles Edgar Mombo, inspected university construction and renovation projects in Greater Libreville on September 2. The tour included the Cap Estérias University Complex, Omar Bongo University (UOB), and the University of Health Sciences (USS). The objective was to assess the progress of work, ensure compliance, and confirm that the infrastructure will meet student needs upon the resumption of classes.

At the Cap Estérias University Complex, the first phase involves fourteen buildings, including pedagogical and administrative facilities, student and professor housing, and a modern security post. At UOB, the minister observed the advancement of a new pedagogical hub with classrooms, a multipurpose hall, a multimedia room, and an auditorium that is completed and awaiting inauguration. The USS projects, however, are reportedly experiencing delays despite assurances from the contractor.

The visit aimed to accelerate the delivery of modern university infrastructure, which is a priority for the President. The new facilities are expected to provide improved conditions for students, faculty, and administrative staff. Officials assured the minister that recent recommendations from the Head of State are being implemented, with ongoing monitoring to ensure all requirements are met for the effective handover of these university construction sites.

Why it mattersStudents and staff will benefit from improved learning and working conditions as new and renovated university facilities are prepared for the upcoming academic year.

Gabon Summons 21 Firms Over 7 Billion CFA Francs in Unfinished Projects

Government officials are demanding explanations from companies that received funds for infrastructure projects in Nyanga province.

Gabonese authorities have summoned 21 companies to explain the use of 7 billion CFA francs allocated for infrastructure projects in the Nyanga province. The summons follows an investigation into numerous unfinished construction sites, with the government seeking to understand how the funds were utilized and why projects remain incomplete. This action is part of a broader government initiative to ensure accountability in public fund management.

The companies, including Speednet, KM BTP, and Société des bâtiments et des distributions (SBD), are required to appear at the Vice-Presidency's cabinet on Thursday, September 3rd. Their mandatory presence aims to clarify the status of the projects and the expenditure of the special presidential endowment. This move intensifies scrutiny on businesses that secured contracts funded by this significant allocation.

The investigation into the Nyanga projects has been ongoing, with previous audits and field reports highlighting concerns about project execution. The government, under President Brice Coltaire Oligui Nguema, is emphasizing accountability for public funds. The public in Nyanga has expressed frustration over the lack of completed infrastructure despite the substantial investment.

Why it mattersTwenty-one companies are affected as they must explain their use of 7 billion CFA francs for infrastructure projects that remain unfinished in Nyanga province.
