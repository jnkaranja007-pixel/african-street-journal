Gabon leasing association launches to boost business finance

The new professional body aims to increase leasing's role in funding productive investments and company growth.

The Gabonese Association of Leasing Actors (AGA CB) has officially formed in Libreville, supported by the International Finance Corporation (IFC). This initiative aims to make leasing a key tool for financing investments, developing companies, and transforming the Gabonese economy. The association's creation is seen as a significant step in structuring a sector with substantial potential but currently underutilized.

During its constituent general assembly, members reviewed statutes and internal regulations to establish a framework for the new association. Ruffin Nzoghé Ndong Simore, elected president, stated that the AGA CB's creation addresses the major challenge of giving leasing a more prominent role in financing the Gabonese economy. In 2021, leasing finance stood at 22.3 billion CFA francs, representing only 2.6% of private sector financing, despite a market potential estimated between 180 and 200 billion CFA francs annually.

Leasing allows businesses, particularly small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), to acquire necessary equipment without significant upfront capital, thereby supporting modernization, job creation, and economic diversification. Serge Bertrand Abessolo, representing the Minister of Economy, emphasized that leasing is a vital instrument for supporting private investment, especially for SMEs. The ongoing reform process, initiated in 2021, seeks to establish an appropriate legal, fiscal, and institutional framework for the sector.

Why it mattersGabonese businesses, particularly SMEs, are affected by the launch of the AGA CB as it aims to make leasing a more accessible and significant tool for acquiring necessary equipment and financing growth.

Gabon plans to regulate revival churches

The interior minister announced new rules for revival churches, drawing parallels with stricter measures in Rwanda and Angola.

Gabon's new interior minister, Edgard Moukoumbi, has announced plans to regulate revival churches, stating that authorities will "look into what is happening inside them." This move follows similar actions in other African nations, where governments have tightened control over religious organizations.

In Rwanda, over 8,000 places of worship were closed by August 2024 as part of a crackdown that began in 2018. The country has implemented stringent legal recognition requirements, building compliance standards, and administrative fees for religious organizations. Angola, meanwhile, requires confessions to gather 60,000 signatures, with at least 1,000 from each of the country's 18 provinces, alongside notarized documents and financial disclosures for legal recognition.

These international examples highlight the potential for strict oversight in Gabon's religious sector. While the stated aim is to protect congregants and ensure safety, the experiences of Rwanda and Angola also raise concerns about potential impacts on freedom of worship and the administrative burden placed on religious groups.

Why it mattersRevival church leaders and members in Gabon may face new restrictions and administrative hurdles in practicing their faith.

Gabon trains officials to fight illegal timber and wildlife trade

A five-day workshop aims to improve the detection, investigation, and prosecution of organized criminal networks involved in exporting protected natural resources.

Gabon's customs and indirect rights directorate, with support from the UN Office on Drugs and Crime, is hosting a national workshop in Libreville to combat illegal timber and wildlife trafficking. The training, which began on October 19, 2026, focuses on enhancing the capabilities of law enforcement and judicial bodies to disrupt organized criminal operations that threaten the country's natural heritage. 30 participants from various national administrations are attending the event, which is funded by the European Union through the Guard Wildlife program.

The workshop addresses critical areas such as identifying fraudulent permits, recognizing timber species, inspecting cargo, and tracing illicit financial flows. Participants will engage in practical exercises, including at the port of Owendo, to refine their skills in controlling goods and building stronger cases for prosecution. The initiative aims to improve the entire chain of repression, from detection to judicial proceedings, to better tackle the economic, ecological, and judicial challenges posed by these illegal activities.

The training emphasizes institutional cooperation between customs, forestry services, investigators, and judicial authorities. This collaborative approach is seen as crucial for effective intelligence sharing, tracking resource flows, and dismantling the financial networks supporting illegal trade. The workshop is part of ongoing efforts in Gabon to secure its significant forest and wildlife resources, which are vital national assets.

Why it mattersGabon's natural resources are threatened by organized criminal networks involved in illegal timber and wildlife exports.

Eramet reinforces cervical cancer screening in Haut-Ogooué

The mining group donated diagnostic and treatment equipment to two hospitals as part of the "Octobre Rose" campaign.

The Eramet Group has donated equipment for diagnosing and treating cervical lesions to the Amissa University Hospital Center in Franceville and the Marcel Abéké Hospital (HMA) in Moanda. The handover, which took place on October 19, is part of the "Octobre Rose" campaign aimed at strengthening local health facilities' capacity for prevention, early detection, and management of human papillomavirus (HPV), a primary risk factor for cervical cancer.

This initiative follows a pilot study conducted from March to June 2025 in Haut-Ogooué, under the patronage of the Ministry of Health, which screened over 200 women aged 35 to 65. The study revealed a 19% overall HPV prevalence, indicating that nearly one in five women tested positive, though not all had cancerous lesions. Awareness campaigns in Moanda, Mounana, Bakoumba, and Sucaf also engaged hundreds to encourage screening.

Eramet acquired a digital colposcope and a thermocoagulator in 2026 to enhance diagnosis and treatment. These donations to Franceville and Moanda follow an earlier provision to Libreville's CHU in summer 2026. The company also plans to fund rapid test kits and midwife training, aligning with its "Act for Positive Mining" CSR roadmap to ensure lasting local capacity building.

Why it mattersWomen in the Haut-Ogooué region are affected by a higher than expected prevalence of HPV, increasing their risk of cervical cancer.

Gabon revokes mining permits after audit

The Ministry of Mines has cancelled research and exploitation permits for several companies for failing to meet legal requirements.

Gabon's Ministry of Mines and Geological Resources has revoked mining permits from multiple operators following an audit conducted between June 29 and October 16, 2026. The decision, announced on October 19, 2026, returns these permits to state control due to non-compliance with legal and regulatory standards. This action follows a June 22, 2026, suspension of small-scale gold mining activities nationwide.

The audit specifically examined adherence to Law No. 037/2018 of June 11, 2019, which governs the mining sector in Gabon. The ministry stated that controls revealed breaches of legal and regulatory requirements by several permit holders. Consequently, permits for research and exploitation across various provinces, including Ogooué-Ivindo, Woleu-Ntem, and Ngounié, have been cancelled. Affected companies include Abice Mining Gabon, Afrique Resources SA, and SMO, among others.

The Ministry of Mines urges interested parties to contact its services for further information regarding the implementation of this decision. The authorities aim to enforce the regulatory framework for mining activities and strengthen oversight of issued titles. The ministry also warned that non-compliant entities could face legal prosecution.

Why it mattersMining companies operating in Gabon are affected by the revocation of their permits due to non-compliance with legal and regulatory requirements.
