CEAG brings affordable goods to Makokou ahead of Liberation Day

The Central Purchasing Agency of Gabon (CEAG) held a three-day market in Makokou offering food and essential items at accessible prices, aiming to combat the high cost of living.

The Central Purchasing Agency of Gabon (CEAG) has expanded its operations into the country's interior, launching a "Mega Market" in Makokou on August 24th. This initiative, which concluded on August 26th, aimed to provide residents with food and essential products at prices described as accessible, coinciding with preparations for the August 30th Liberation Day festivities. The event saw participation from local officials, including the Senate President and the Governor of Ogooué-Ivindo province.

CEAG's Director General, Théophile Boutamba, stated that the Makokou market is part of a broader strategy to bring the agency's offerings closer to populations outside of Libreville. This follows similar operations in other locations across Gabon. The agency aims to ensure that a wider range of products are available to more people at affordable prices, a key objective in their fight against the rising cost of living.

While the "Mega Market" addresses immediate supply needs, the long-term challenge for CEAG lies in establishing regular supply mechanisms in interior cities. Factors such as transportation costs, logistical constraints, and distance from major supply centers contribute to higher prices for essential goods. The agency's success will depend on moving beyond temporary events to create sustainable supply chains that can durably impact household expenses.

Why it mattersThe CEAG's initiative in Makokou offers residents access to essential goods at reduced prices, directly addressing the impact of the high cost of living on their purchasing power.

Fortescue Belinga Advances Exploration, Plans 2030 Ore Shipment

The Belinga iron ore project in Gabon has completed its initial drilling phase and is expanding exploration, aiming for its first ore shipment by 2030, according to company.

The Belinga iron ore project is central to Gabon's industrial ambitions, with Fortescue Belinga reporting significant progress in exploration activities. The company has completed over two hundred twenty-five thousand meters of reverse circulation drilling and more than seventeen thousand five hundred meters of diamond drilling. This work aims to define the extent of mineral resources and refine the deposit's potential for future development decisions. Exploration is expanding beyond the primary Belinga site to areas like Batouala, where drilling began in May, and Boka Boka.

Fortescue Belinga's Chief Executive Officer, Edward Kalajzic, stated at the Ivindo Economic Forum in Makokou that the project has the potential to support Gabon's economic diversification. The company is emphasizing local development through employment and skills training, with a program called 'We Train for Gabon'. Currently, the project supports one thousand two hundred forty-six direct and indirect jobs, with eighty-two percent being Gabonese nationals, and fifty-seven percent from the Ogooué-Ivindo province.

The project's development is planned in stages, aligning with technical studies, intensified exploration, and the necessary local skill development. Fortescue Belinga aims for a first ore expedition by the horizon 2030. The forum highlighted the project's role in transforming the Ogooué-Ivindo region and diversifying the Gabonese economy through mining infrastructure and local value creation.

Why it mattersThe Belinga iron ore project's progress and planned ore shipment by 2030 directly impact investors by outlining future resource development and economic diversification opportunities in Gabon.

Gabonese Journalist Rudy Hombenet Dies Suddenly After Childbirth

Rudy Hombenet Anvingui, recognized as the best print journalist in 2026, passed away in Libreville on August 27, just two days after giving birth.

Rudy Hombenet Anvingui, a journalist at the daily L’Union and a recent recipient of the Makongonio 2026 award for best print journalist, died in Libreville on the night of August 26-27. Her death occurred only two months after her professional recognition and shortly after she gave birth to a daughter.

Hombenet, who joined L’Union in 2015, focused her work on social issues, education, and particularly health. Her recent articles explored topics such as tuberculosis, depression, malaria, and hospital operations, bringing daily realities to readers. She had previously expressed a strong aversion to injustice, stating her journalism aimed to give voice to the voiceless.

Her career, spanning over a decade, was marked by a commitment to factual reporting and rigorous investigation. Hombenet held a Master's degree in Communication from Omar-Bongo University. Colleagues remember her for her seriousness, rigor, and dedication to journalistic principles.

Why it mattersThe sudden death of Rudy Hombenet affects her family and the Gabonese press corps, impacting the continuity of her reporting on critical social and health issues.

Bosch Capital Demands $330 Million Debt Payment from Gabon

South African fund Bosch Capital has set a September 3 deadline for Gabon to pay a debt of over $300 million, threatening to damage the nation's financial reputation.

Bosch Capital, a South African fund that acquired the rights to a debt from Paramount Group, is demanding over $300 million from Gabon. The fund has given Libreville until September 3, 2026, to settle the debt. In a letter dated August 24, Bosch Capital warned of actions that could negatively impact Gabon's financial standing. This debt originates from a 2014 contract for maintenance and support of military equipment, with the current amount reflecting accumulated sums and legal developments.

The fund, through its legal counsel Seven Pillars Law, is pressuring the Gabonese government to resolve the financial dispute. Bosch Capital has indicated it may inform international financial system actors if its demand is not met, extending the threat beyond legal action. This could affect Gabon's sovereign risk perception among rating agencies, banks, and international investors, impacting its financial credibility and access to external financing.

The dispute traces back to a relationship between Gabon and Paramount Group, a South African defense and aerospace firm, which began before 2014. In 2006-2007, Gabon acquired Mirage F1AZ aircraft from Paramount Group and Aerosud, previously part of the South African air force. Paramount Group also assisted Gabon with the acquisition, maintenance, and operation of these aircraft, indicating a long-standing cooperation in military aviation capabilities.

Why it mattersBosch Capital's demand and threat to report to international financial actors directly impacts Gabon's financial reputation and access to capital.

Gabon Concert Gathers 26 Singers, Congolese Star Dena Mwana

The first national gospel concert, 'Gabon Action de Grâce,' will feature over 26 Gabonese artists and international guest Dena Mwana at the Palais des Congrès Omar Bongo Ondimba.

The inaugural 'Gabon Action de Grâce' gospel concert is scheduled for Thursday, August 27, 2026, at the Palais des Congrès Omar Bongo Ondimba in Libreville. The event aims to unite gospel artists from across Gabon on a single stage for a celebratory experience. International artist Dena Mwana from the Democratic Republic of Congo will be a special guest, highlighting her past connections with Gabon as an early supporter of her international ministry.

Organizers anticipate that the presence of Dena Mwana will be a significant highlight of the evening. The concert, which opens its doors at two in the afternoon for a five o'clock start, is designed to be a major gathering for Gabonese gospel music. This event marks a new initiative in the national gospel concert landscape, seeking to foster community among artists and the public.

Tickets for 'Gabon Action de Grâce' are available at prices of ten thousand, thirty thousand, fifty thousand, and one hundred thousand FCFA. A 24-hour promotion offers the thirty thousand FCFA ticket for fifteen thousand FCFA. Ticketing options include QR code access and sales points near the Rond-point de la Démocratie on the day of the event.

Why it mattersThe concert offers a cultural event for Gabonese citizens and attracts international artists, impacting local arts and entertainment.
