Gabon Customs Targets Illegal Timber and Wildlife Trafficking Networks

A five-day workshop aims to enhance national agencies' ability to combat organized crime and illegal resource exploitation.

Gabon's Directorate General of Customs and Indirect Duties, with support from the UN Office on Drugs and Crime, is hosting a national workshop in Libreville focused on combating illegal timber exports and wildlife crime. The training, which began on October 19, 2026, aims to strengthen the capacities of law enforcement agencies and improve coordination against criminal organizations involved in these illicit activities.

The workshop brings together 30 participants from various national administrations and is part of the EU-funded Guard Wildlife program. It seeks to improve the detection of offenses, the conduct of investigations, and the judicial processing of cases related to the trafficking of timber and wildlife, which contribute to biodiversity loss, state revenue decline, and the growth of organized criminal networks.

Training modules include identifying fake permits, recognizing timber species, inspecting cargo, and tracking illicit financial flows to better detect infractions and dismantle trafficking rings. Practical exercises at the port of Owendo are designed to equip participants with enhanced investigation skills and improve the compilation of cases for prosecution.

Why it mattersThe workshop aims to improve the detection of illegal timber and wildlife trafficking, which impacts state revenue and supports organized crime.

Gabon Revokes Mining Permits After Regulatory Audit

The Ministry of Mines has canceled permits for multiple mining companies for failing to meet legal requirements.

Gabon's Ministry of Mines and Geological Resources has revoked mining exploration and exploitation permits from several companies following a comprehensive audit. The audit, conducted from June 29 to October 16, 2026, found that these operators failed to comply with legal and regulatory standards. This action is part of a broader effort to strengthen oversight of the national mining sector.

The revoked permits affect operations across multiple provinces, including Ogooué-Ivindo, Woleu-Ntem, Ngounié, Haut-Ogooué, Ogooué-Lolo, Estuaire, Moyen-Ogooué, and Nyanga. Companies named include Abice Mining Gabon, Afrique Resources SA, Azingon Gabon, Les Mines du Gabon, Somine Gabon, Gabon Gold Mining, Ivindo Mining, Jin He Mining, SMO, Société des mines et carrières du Gabon (SMCG), Société Perle Gabonaise, Agil Gabon, Élie Resources Gabon, and Global Mine Development (GMD).

The ministry stated that the decision aims to enforce regulatory compliance and reinforce control over issued mining titles. Companies are advised to contact the ministry for further information, and non-compliance with the decision may lead to legal proceedings. This move follows a suspension of small-scale gold mining activities nationwide, decided on June 22, 2026.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because multiple mining companies have lost their permits due to non-compliance with regulations.

Gabon Explores Electronic Monitoring to Ease Prison Overcrowding

Gabon's Justice Minister visited Senegal to study the electronic bracelet system as a way to reduce prison populations.

Gabon's Minister of Justice, Augustin Emane, is in Dakar this week to examine Senegal's use of electronic monitoring as an alternative to incarceration. The visit, from October 18 to 21, focuses on understanding the mechanisms of the electronic bracelet system, which Senegal employs to combat prison overcrowding and improve detention conditions. Emane, accompanied by technical security officials, met with his Senegalese counterpart, Me Moussa Saar, to discuss judicial cooperation and the implementation of electronic surveillance.

The Gabonese delegation aims to gather technical and practical data to adapt Senegal's model to Gabon's judicial system. This initiative aligns with Gabon's high authorities' priority to improve the conditions of detained individuals. Beyond alleviating prison congestion, the electronic bracelet system could modernize sentence execution, diversify judicial responses, and potentially generate new state revenue, according to a ministerial note.

Senegal's electronic surveillance system, supported by companies like GEOSATIS, offers Gabon a practical framework for implementation. The goal is to provide a credible alternative to imprisonment for certain defendants or convicts, allowing them to serve sentences under strict supervision outside of correctional facilities. This move signifies an effort to humanize justice and enhance the effectiveness of criminal penalties in Gabon.

Why it mattersGabon is exploring the electronic bracelet system to reduce prison overcrowding and modernize its justice system.

Gabon Launches 114 Billion FCFA Cement Plant

A new cement factory and clinker production line are set to reduce import dependency and boost national construction material output.

Gabon has initiated its industrialization strategy with the groundbreaking ceremony for the Mondah Green Beach cement production line and factory on October 20, 2026. This project, backed by an investment of at least 114 billion FCFA and developed with Chinese partners, aims to decrease reliance on imported materials, strengthen domestic production of construction supplies, and generate over 200 direct jobs. The initiative is part of Gabon's "Structuring Industries" plan, building on economic cooperation agreements established in 2024.

The Mondah Green Beach facility will feature a clinker production line with a capacity of one million tons annually, alongside a cement plant capable of producing the same amount. This venture is a collaboration between Hope Global Investment and Hong Kong-based Conch Investment Holding. The project is intended to secure the national supply of cement, support the building and public works sector, and potentially open avenues for regional exports, contributing to economic diversification.

Beyond industrial output, the project emphasizes job creation and skills transfer, with a focus on employing Gabonese labor and engaging local businesses. Training programs and technology transfer initiatives are planned to ensure local communities acquire the necessary expertise for operating the plant. This development is a tangible outcome of economic diplomacy discussions held between Gabon and China.

Why it mattersThe new cement plant will affect investors by providing opportunities in a key industrial sector and potentially influencing regional trade dynamics.

Gabon, US Bolster Air Security Cooperation

Gabon's civil aviation agency and the U.S. embassy met to enhance airport security and state safety through API-PNR system implementation.

Gabon's National Civil Aviation Agency (ANAC) and the U.S. Embassy in Libreville are strengthening their collaboration on airport and state security. A meeting on October 16th focused on technical assistance and expertise sharing as Gabon deploys the API-PNR system, designed to enhance air traffic control and threat detection. The system utilizes passenger identification and reservation data to anticipate risks and identify suspicious travel patterns, aiding in the fight against terrorism, drug trafficking, and transnational crime.

The discussions, led by ANAC Director General Éric Tristan Franck Moussavou and U.S. Embassy Regional Security Director Michael Lee, aimed to bolster national capabilities in airport security and territorial protection. The API-PNR system requires effective coordination among administrations and proficiency in data analysis tools, making the exchange of experience and technical support crucial for Gabon. Both parties expressed a commitment to deepening their relationship and developing concrete cooperation aligned with Gabon's priorities.

This enhanced partnership is particularly relevant as Gabon advances the implementation of the API-PNR system. The goal is to consolidate national expertise and better integrate airport security measures with national protection imperatives. The collaboration seeks to translate the shared willingness into operational actions to meet the growing demands of civil aviation security.

Why it mattersGabon's aviation security is being enhanced through a partnership with the U.S. Embassy, impacting border control and travel data management.
