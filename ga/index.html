Hospitals Screen 500 Infants for Hearing Deficiencies

Port-Gentil's Ntchengué Regional Hospital and Edelweiss Clinic launched a free screening campaign for babies aged zero to six months.

A free hearing screening campaign for infants aged zero to six months began on September 1 in Port-Gentil. The initiative, a collaboration between Ntchengué Regional Hospital and Edelweiss Clinic, aims to test at least 500 babies in its initial phase. The goal is to detect potential hearing impairments early, which can significantly impact a child's language acquisition and overall development.

Dr. Danielle Fengui Kacmeni, an ENT specialist, explained that the screening involves simple auditory tests to determine if an infant can hear. While the initial results are not a definitive diagnosis, they serve to identify potential issues. Infants with suspicious results are referred for further, more in-depth examinations at specialized centers, a process currently concentrated in Libreville but with hopes of decentralization.

The campaign, scheduled to run until September 3, intends to examine as many children as possible, exceeding the initial target of 500. Organizers aim to establish regular hearing checks as a routine practice for families in Port-Gentil. Parents are encouraged to take advantage of this free opportunity to assess their infant's hearing and receive guidance if further medical attention is needed.

Why it mattersInfants aged zero to six months in Port-Gentil are directly benefiting from early detection of potential hearing impairments, which could affect their development.

Gabon French Voters Increase Support for National Rally

The percentage of French expatriates in Gabon voting for the National Rally party has risen from 14% in 2017 to 17.5% in 2022, according to data presented by Privat Ngomo.

French citizens residing in Gabon have shown a growing inclination to vote for the National Rally, a party whose platform heavily features anti-immigration policies targeting African nationals. This trend, highlighted by political activist Privat Ngomo, suggests a disconnect between the hospitality received in Gabon and the voting choices of some French expatriates.

Ngomo, a known critic of the Gabonese government, questions the reciprocity of this situation, stating that French citizens living comfortably in Gabon, or other Francophone African nations, appear to disregard the welcome they are afforded when they cast ballots for a party that makes immigration a central issue. The National Rally, formerly the National Front, has roots in far-right ideologies.

The National Rally's electoral base has expanded significantly, reaching an estimated 22% and extending beyond mainland France and its overseas territories to include African nations. This rise in support for a party with a history of controversial stances on race and immigration is a point of concern for Ngomo, who sees it as a challenge to the principles of mutual respect.

Why it mattersFrench expatriates in Gabon are affected by the rise of the National Rally, a party whose anti-immigration stance is a core part of its platform.

Business · 2026-09-03

Fortescue Belinga Trains Entrepreneurs, Upgrades Vocational Center

The mining project developer conducted a week of activities in Makokou focused on local capacity building and infrastructure.

Fortescue Belinga conducted a series of initiatives in Makokou from August 25 to 30, including entrepreneur training, economic dialogue, and vocational education. The company provided equipment to the Makokou Vocational Training and Professional Education Center, a partnership aimed at enhancing practical skills and workplace safety. This program is designed to prepare local economic actors for potential opportunities arising from the Belinga project's development.

The company's CEO, Edward Kalajzic, emphasized the importance of local skills development, stating that Fortescue Belinga hopes the project will strengthen businesses and create jobs in the region. A program concluded that trained 20 local entrepreneurs, with Kalajzic encouraging them to leverage their new skills for economic growth. Fortescue Belinga also participated in the Ivindo Economic Forum, discussing the project's prospects and potential benefits.

Exploration activities for the Belinga iron ore project are ongoing and have expanded beyond the initial site, with preliminary drilling work underway in Batouala and planned exploration in Boka Boka. These studies are intended to inform future development decisions. Fortescue Belinga also supported the renovation of the Alexandre Sambat Stadium as part of its community investment efforts.

Why it mattersLocal entrepreneurs and the vocational training center in Makokou are directly affected by Fortescue Belinga's initiatives to build capacity and provide resources.
