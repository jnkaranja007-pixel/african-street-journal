Oligui Nguema meets Gabonese youth in Beijing

Gabonese students and young professionals in China expressed their desire to return and contribute to national development.

Gabon's President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema met with Gabonese students and young professionals in Beijing on November 7, 2026, before returning to Libreville. The meeting, held after a five-day state visit to China, focused on national economic issues.

Some attendees voiced their wish to return to Gabon to start businesses and create wealth, while others aimed to enhance their skills in strategic sectors like mining, new technologies, digital services, and artificial intelligence. President Nguema acknowledged their aspirations, highlighting opportunities from his visit in mining, oil, gas, and agro-industry.

The president also mentioned support mechanisms for project leaders, simplified business creation procedures, and SME financing. He urged the diaspora to maintain solidarity and respect Chinese laws and the Gabonese embassy's guidance, reaffirming their integral role in the nation.

Why it mattersGabonese students and professionals in China want to use their acquired skills for national development, and President Nguema discussed support for their return and entrepreneurial endeavors.

Gabon minister inspects resettlement site after demolitions

Millot Lambert Mouvinde visited Malibé 2 on November 6 to check progress on housing for families displaced by urban renewal projects.

Gabon's Minister of Public Works and Construction, Millot Lambert Mouvinde, inspected a 15-hectare site in Malibé 2 on November 6. The site is designated for families displaced by demolitions that began on November 4 in Okala, Lac Bleu, and Diba-Diba. These demolitions are part of a plan to modernize Grand-Libreville and create future road access.

Mouvinde reviewed the site's readiness and the resources allocated to speed up its preparation for the affected households. He stated that teams are working to ensure the area can accommodate the relocated citizens with dignity. The minister also stressed the importance of meeting deadlines for both the demolitions and the resettlement preparations.

The resettlement plan aims to balance the clearing of land with the rehousing of families and urban modernization. Residents have expressed concerns, seeking more than just housing, requesting essential services like roads, water, electricity, and schools. The government's goal is to improve traffic flow, urban mobility, and access to strategic infrastructure.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens are being displaced from their homes for urban development projects and are concerned about the provision of adequate housing and essential services.

Gabon survey finds 80% support gender equality

Despite widespread approval for equal rights, women face workplace discrimination and fewer resources for community development.

Nearly 80% of Gabonese people support gender equality in rights, opportunities, and political participation, according to Afrobarometer Round 11 survey results presented on November 6 in Libreville. The study, implemented by the University Omar Bongo's CERGEP, surveyed a representative sample of the adult population.

While citizens back legal intervention for equal pay, workplace discrimination and harassment persist. Women are seen as equally legitimate for elected office, yet they encounter greater difficulty accessing resources for their communities, highlighting a gap between principle and practice.

Researchers noted the need to question the notion of equality against African cultural realities, with one academic advocating for "equity" where individuals are recognized for their true worth. Civil society representatives see popular support as a call to accelerate reforms and address the disconnect between field realities and current laws.

Why it mattersGabonese women, despite broad support for equality, experience ongoing discrimination and have less access to resources for community development.

Gabon launches artisanal fishing support centre

A new support centre in Donguila will assist 700 to 800 Gabonese fishermen with financing and social coverage.

Gabon's government is launching a program to support artisanal fishing, starting with a new support centre under construction in Donguila. Minister of Fisheries Georges Christian Waza visited the site on November 7, 2026, to inspect progress and discuss new support mechanisms for 700 to 800 Gabonese fishermen. This initiative aims to formalise fishing activities and improve access to financing, social coverage, and regulatory compliance.

The minister met with fishermen in Donguila, Estuaire province, to explain the new support system. Representatives from the Directorate General of Fisheries and Aquaculture, the National Agency for Investment Promotion, the National Health Insurance and Social Guarantee Fund, the National Social Security Fund, and the Bank for Commerce and Entrepreneurship of Gabon were present. They provided information on administrative, financial, and social procedures to help fishermen formalise their activities and access benefits.

This program, part of the national gabonisation of artisanal fishing, will expand to Cocobeach, Gamba, and Mayumba after Donguila. The initiative seeks to structure the sector, which faces challenges in formalisation and requires fishermen to declare their catches and sell them in approved centres.

Gabon writer's book launched at African writers day

The Association du Festival international du livre gabonais et des arts presented Carolina Korouna's "Eden-Vie : Les visages de la femme" in Libreville.

The 34th International African Writers Day on Saturday, November 7, 2026, in Libreville featured the presentation of "Eden-Vie : Les visages de la femme" by Gabonese writer Carolina Korouna. The Association du Festival international du livre gabonais et des arts (Filiga) organized the literary event, which also included a book signing.

Filiga president Rosny Le Sage Souaga stated that the day celebrates African writers, cultural identity, and the transmission of knowledge, emphasizing literature's role in Africa telling its own story. The book "Eden-Vie : Les visages de la femme," published by Éditions Filiga, explores femininity, identity, emancipation, and ancestral values through three figures.

Filiga also announced the 6th edition of its festival, scheduled for May 28-30, 2027, at Baie des Rois. The Democratic Republic of Congo will be the guest of honor, with China as a special guest, expecting approximately 20 countries.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens, particularly women, are directly affected by the celebration and exploration of their cultural identity and literary contributions.
