SEEG repairs 20 kV cable after outages in Libreville and Akanda

The underground line serving several neighbourhoods failed on 15 November; the cable had been in service for more than 28 years.

A fault in a 20 kV underground cable cut electricity to several neighbourhoods in Libreville and Akanda from about 3:45 a.m. on Sunday, 15 November. The Société d’énergie et d’eau du Gabon (SEEG), Gabon’s electricity and water company, traced the failure to the Gué-Gué feeder near the Charbonnages interchange.

SEEG said the cable had been installed more than 28 years ago. Its repair teams located the fault and began work, which requires digging to reach the damaged section. Heavy road traffic in the area is making access and repairs more difficult, the company said.

The affected areas include Tri Postal Charbonnage, École Bas de Gué-Gué, Mairie Haut de Gué-Gué and Cité Kalikac. SEEG had announced that power would be restored on Monday afternoon, 16 November, and said its teams remained at work.

Why it mattersSubscribers in the named Libreville and Akanda neighbourhoods faced a power interruption while SEEG worked to repair the cable, with restoration scheduled for Monday afternoon, 16 November.

Kossy consults farmers and entrepreneurs on Gabon's 2026-2030 food plan

The agriculture minister heard calls on land, finance, training and market access as he seeks input for the national strategy.

Gabon’s agriculture minister, Pacôme Kossy, met agricultural professional organisations, women and young entrepreneurs in Libreville on 16 November to gather proposals for the 2026-2030 Food Systems Transformation Plan (PTSA).

Participants raised barriers to agricultural enterprise, including access to land and finance, training, competitiveness, certification and marketing local produce. Kossy presented planned support mechanisms and asked them to submit recommendations, according to Gabon Review.

Consultations were due to continue on 18 November at the Palais des Congrès Omar-Bongo-Ondimba in the Cité de la démocratie. The plan is intended to modernise production, organise value chains, attract investment and create jobs, while reducing reliance on food imports and expanding local processing by 2030.

Why it mattersAgricultural entrepreneurs raised land, finance, training and marketing barriers, and were asked to submit recommendations for the plan.

Business · 2026-11-18

Eurofinsa seeks freeze on Gabon’s Bank of New York Mellon funds

The Spanish contractor wants access to nearly 18 million dollars tied to Libreville’s unfinished Omar-Bongo stadium project.

Eurofinsa asked a federal judge in New York on 12 November 2026 to stop Gabon from transferring public funds held at the Bank of New York Mellon. The Spanish construction group is seeking payment of an arbitration award over the stalled rehabilitation of Libreville’s Omar-Bongo stadium.

A federal judge in Washington ordered Gabon to pay 17 898 152,10 dollars on 17 January 2025, after the country did not respond to Eurofinsa’s enforcement case. The award followed a 15 October 2020 arbitration ruling requiring Gabon to pay more than 12 million euros, with annual interest of 3,66%; Eurofinsa’s 437 400 euros owed to the state was deducted.

Eurofinsa, which Madrid’s Commercial Court No. 17 declared bankrupt in January 2023 with liabilities of 306 million euros, is pursuing the funds through its court-appointed administrator, Baker Tilly. The dispute began with a 2009 stadium contract; the stadium’s rehabilitation remains unfinished.

Why it mattersGabon faces a freeze request over public funds at the New York bank, with Eurofinsa pursuing enforcement of the 17 January 2025 award.
