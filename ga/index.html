Gabon launches first digital week in Libreville

Experts from 50 African countries will attend the event focused on digital transformation and public services.

Gabon launched its first Digital Week on October 26, 2026, in Libreville, with Vice-President Alexandre Barro Chambrier initiating the event. The week-long event will feature experts from 50 African Union member countries, focusing on the national digital transformation program.

Vice-President Chambrier stated that digital transformation is a key to modernizing the state and diversifying the economy, aiming to improve administration efficiency, economic competitiveness, and create jobs for youth. The Gabon Digital program, funded by the World Bank at approximately $68.5 million, is in its final 18 months and focuses on legal frameworks, an ePasseport platform, and IT infrastructure.

Steeve Ewore, Coordinator of the Gabon Digital Program, explained the project aims to facilitate public service access for all citizens, enhance cybersecurity, and strengthen IT infrastructure. The program intends to develop systems locally to meet Gabonese needs, enabling access to services like civil registration, recruitment, and health for those in rural areas.

Why it mattersGabon's digital week aims to improve public service access and economic diversification for its citizens.

Arnaud Kombila Mussavu to audit Gabon's agricultural company

The new interim administrator has 90 days to restructure the Société d’agriculture et d’élevage du Gabon after a governance crisis.

Arnaud Kombila Mussavu officially took charge of the Société d’agriculture et d’élevage du Gabon (SAEG) on October 26, 2026, with a 90-day mandate to audit and restructure the company. His appointment follows a governance and performance crisis that has weakened the agricultural and livestock enterprise, which is strategic for Gabon's food security. This transition period aims to restore the company's operational capacity after institutional changes on October 8 made previous dissolution plans void.

The handover ceremony, presided over by Serge Rufin Okana from the Ministry of Agriculture, marked the start of the provisional administration. Mussavu's tasks include conducting a strategic review, securing state assets, and identifying conditions for sustainable recovery. The government also ordered the urgent payment of one-third of accumulated salary arrears for SAEG and AGROPAG employees, with the remainder to be settled later.

Gabon aims to reduce import dependency, including a planned halt to broiler chicken imports by January 1, 2027. The SAEG's ability to support national production is under scrutiny. The 90-day period is expected to yield a clarified governance structure, preserved assets, and a credible operational strategy to test the coherence between food sovereignty ambitions and institutional means.

Why it mattersGabon's food sovereignty and national production capacity are directly affected by the restructuring of the Société d’agriculture et d’élevage du Gabon.

Gabon launches digital platform for state assets

The new Fichier unique des participations (FUP) aims to centralize data and improve financial transparency.

Gabon launched the Fichier unique des participations (FUP) in Libreville on October 26, a digital platform to centralize state asset data. The tool aims to improve public portfolio tracking, financial transparency, and decision-making. Marc Abeghe, the Minister Delegate for Economy, Finance, Debt, and Participations, officially launched the FUP.

The FUP, developed by the Directorate General of Participations, seeks to address the scattered nature of information on state-held assets. It will provide a consolidated map of public holdings through centralized, structured, and secure data. This centralization is intended to enhance the monitoring of revenues generated from state investments.

Anne Nkene, Director General of Participations, stated the tool will modernize public portfolio management and strengthen financial transparency. The initiative aligns with national priorities for economic recovery and digital transformation of administration, as outlined in the national growth and development plan.

Why it mattersGabon's government launched a new digital platform to track state assets, aiming for better financial transparency and management.

Gabon agency faces business creation complaints

Entrepreneurs report ongoing technical issues at the ANPI-Gabon single window, hindering company registration.

Entrepreneurs in Gabon are facing persistent technical difficulties at the ANPI-Gabon single window, delaying the formalization of new businesses. Sylvie Kotha épouse Nzamba, a municipal councilor, highlighted these ongoing issues on Facebook, noting that problems first signaled in January 2025 remain unresolved in October 2026.

These technical failures at the Agency for the Promotion of Investment (ANPI-Gabon) risk slowing down projects and causing entrepreneurs to miss market opportunities due to delayed documentation. Nzamba has called for explanations regarding the causes of these persistent malfunctions and the solutions being implemented.

The complaints arise as the government, supported by the UNDP and Comilog, launches a three-month study across Gabon's nine provinces to identify obstacles to entrepreneurial initiative. The study aims to understand economic, sociological, psychological, and ecological barriers, but faces criticism for not immediately addressing administrative hurdles.

Why it mattersEntrepreneurs in Gabon are experiencing delays in registering their businesses due to technical problems at the ANPI-Gabon single window.

Gabon Connect consults data protection authority

Gabon Connect project leaders met with the Authority for the Protection of Personal Data and Privacy on October 27, 2026.

Gabon Connect project leaders met with the Authority for the Protection of Personal Data and Privacy (APDPVP) on October 27, 2026, to ensure the digital transformation of public services protects user privacy. Michel Tsamba, director of Gabon Connect, stated the goal was to consult the institution responsible for personal data to guarantee legal compliance in information processing.

The Gabon Connect platform, presented on October 22, aims to centralize and digitize procedures for taxes, customs, land registry, and social security. This digital shift requires careful handling of sensitive personal information. Tsamba explained the need to understand what can and cannot be done with collected data.

The APDPVP's role is to ensure organizations collecting personal data comply with legal requirements. This consultation is a step towards ensuring citizen trust in digital public services, focusing on regulatory compliance, access control, and data security.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens and businesses will have their personal data handled according to legal requirements as public services become digital.
