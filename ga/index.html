Gabon's social security fund faces rescue plan

A task force has proposed 23 measures to address the CNAMGS's financial crisis and governance issues.

Gabon's National Social Security and Health Insurance Fund (CNAMGS) is undergoing a recovery plan due to severe financial problems, governance failures, and significant debt to healthcare providers. A steering committee met on Monday, November 16, 2026, in Libreville, chaired by Vice-President Hermann Immongault, to review 23 proposals aimed at stabilizing the organization. The CNAMGS is struggling with accumulated debts, particularly for medical evacuations, and operational dysfunctions that impact service quality for its members.

After four months of work, the interministerial Task Force has identified critical issues including financial fragility, governance and control shortcomings, and legal constraints. Vice-President Immongault stressed that the task force is meant to provide concrete, high-level state responses to the CNAMGS's crisis. The Minister of Social Affairs, Raïssa Nana Ghogho, stated the meeting aimed to prioritize proposals and set an execution timeline to lift the CNAMGS out of its known 'quagmire'.

The proposed solutions fall into three phases: short-term actions to restore cash flow and ensure care continuity; medium-term goals focusing on modernizing governance and accelerating the Unique Social Registry; and long-term strategies for resource diversification through behavioral taxation and an environmental health contribution based on the polluter-pays principle. The task force expects 23 decisions to translate these directions into operational measures.

Why it mattersHealthcare providers are awaiting payment of accumulated debts to ensure the continuity of care for insured individuals.

Gabon's labour ministry hands draft strike law to employers and unions

Employers and unions have at least three working days to review the bill and send back amendments and suggestions.

On Monday, November 16, in Libreville, Colonel Bernard Obie, secretary-general of the Ministry of Labour, formally handed the draft law on the exercise of the right to strike to employers and trade unions. The text is being put forward under Convention 144 of the International Labour Organization (ILO), which governs tripartite consultation.

Rubain Paul Zoukoué Ella, president of the national confederation of public and private sector workers (Cosynap), said the meeting was only the official handover, so members could study the document. Joseph Mendene Assoumou, president of the Confederation of Gabonese Enterprises (CEG), said employers would use the three days to set out their position, aiming for productive social dialogue and more flexibility to attract local and foreign investors.

Proposals will be examined with the government and the labour administration to reach a consensual text. A further meeting with the minister, who was in Switzerland and absent from the handover, is due to take place on her return before the legislative process continues.

Why it mattersEmployers and unions must return amendments within three working days, so the wording of any future limits on strikes will be shaped by their replies.

Politics · 2026-11-17

Gabon president funds teen's medical evacuation

President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema visited the injured boy and will cover all costs for his emergency medical transfer.

President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema visited 13-year-old Dylan on Monday, November 16, 2026, at the CHU Mère-Enfant in Libreville. The boy was shot on November 13 at the Monédan stadium. The president decided to fully fund Dylan's urgent medical evacuation to ensure he receives specialized care.

Dylan, a student in third grade, was seriously wounded by two bullets during a football match. President Oligui Nguema offered comfort to the boy and his parents, expressing the nation's solidarity. This presidential visit follows earlier trips by the Justice and Interior ministers.

An investigation into the shooting is ongoing to determine the exact circumstances and responsibilities. Jean-Boniface Assélé, implicated in the case, was arrested and was to appear before the Libreville prosecutor on Monday. The medical team reported Dylan's condition as stable after surgery.

Why it mattersPresident Oligui Nguema will cover the full cost of Dylan's emergency medical evacuation, with the procedure to be expedited.

Business · 2026-11-17

Gabon arrests suspects in counterfeit tissue ring

Authorities detained about 10 expatriates involved in selling fake Lotus brand tissues, following a complaint from the trademark owner.

Judicial units arrested approximately 10 expatriates in Libreville on November 12, suspected of involvement in a network manufacturing and selling counterfeit Lotus brand tissues. These arrests follow a criminal complaint filed by Satoga, the brand's owner, which has faced a proliferation of fake products on the Gabonese market for months. The suspects, primarily Malian and Chinese nationals, allegedly operated in Libreville, Franceville, Moanda, and Port-Gentil.

The investigation began in September 2026 when Satoga filed a complaint to stop the trade, leading to the seizure of counterfeit Lotus tissues from stalls at the Petit-Paris market in Libreville. The seized products were destroyed in October, and the suspects were to face justice. Satoga stated that the counterfeit sales constitute serious unfair competition, causing significant financial loss and compromising quality control standards, directly impacting the 116 jobs at the company.

Satoga highlighted that each counterfeit pack sold replaces a product made in Gabon by Gabonese employees, threatening the 116 jobs and investments in a new production line. Beyond the economic threat, the counterfeit tissues pose a health risk due to a lack of guaranteed sanitary safety standards. Satoga has temporarily halted local production, placing several employees on technical leave, while the arrested individuals await trial.

Why it mattersSatoga has temporarily halted local production, placing several employees on technical leave, due to the threat of counterfeit goods.
