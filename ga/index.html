Gabon lifts social media ban after eight months

The High Authority for Communication ended the suspension on October 24, 2026, citing new laws and platform cooperation.

Gabon's High Authority for Communication (HAC) lifted the eight-month suspension of social media access on Saturday, October 24, 2026. The regulator, led by Germain Ngoyo Moussavou, announced the decision in Libreville, formally ending the measure that began on February 17, 2026.

The HAC stated the suspension aimed to allow the government to adapt laws for better oversight of digital platforms and to encourage constructive dialogue for content moderation. This action followed a rise in defamatory, hateful, and obscene content circulating online, impacting human dignity and national security.

A new law, ratified on October 5, 2026, now governs administrative and judicial responses to digital misinformation, including content amplified by artificial intelligence. The HAC also reported developing active collaboration with platforms like TikTok, Meta, and YouTube.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens can now access social media platforms after an eight-month suspension.

Gabon transport ministry seeks 44.56 billion CFA francs for 2027

The ministry's 2026 budget execution stands at 33.91% cash basis with 7.93 billion CFA francs paid.

Gabon's Ministry of Transport is requesting 44.56 billion CFA francs for 2027, nearly double the 23.39 billion CFA francs budgeted for 2026. Of the 2026 budget, only 7.93 billion CFA francs have been paid, representing a 33.91% cash execution rate.

Minister of State Ulrich Manfoumbi Manfoumbi presented the 2026 performance to the National Assembly's finance commission on October 23, 2026. He noted that while 54.68% of the budget was committed, actual spending on investments was only 2.05%. The minister assured deputies that social dialogue is ongoing to resolve issues at the Société nationale immobilière (SNI) in Port-Gentil and that planned funds for air and sea transport aim to reorganize routes and negotiate lower fares.

The 2027 budget proposal is divided into 17.65 billion CFA francs for land transport, 22.34 billion CFA francs for air and maritime sectors, and 4.57 billion CFA francs for institutional steering. Deputies also raised concerns about traffic congestion, with the minister stating that traffic flow improvements are a priority under the National Road Safety Plan but require impact studies and adherence to public procurement procedures.

Why it mattersGabon's citizens are affected by the low execution rate of the transport budget and the high cost of air and boat tickets.

Gabon politician demands former PM's release

Francis Hubert Aubame criticizes the silence of the judiciary on the detention of Alain-Claude Bilie-By-Nze.

Francis Hubert Aubame, president of the PSE-PP party, is demanding the immediate and unconditional release of former Prime Minister Alain-Claude Bilie-By-Nze, who has been detained since April 17. Aubame published a statement on October 24, criticizing the Superior Council of the Magistracy (CSM) for its silence on the case and calling for the government to act on its reform promises.

Aubame highlighted concerns over access to the case file, delays in the investigation, and the contested grounds for Bilie-By-Nze's preventive detention. He questioned why the CSM, responsible for overseeing the judiciary, has not publicly addressed these issues, especially given the institution's stated commitment to a diligent and impartial justice system. The CSM met in Port-Gentil on September 16.

The PSE-PP leader insists that a person's liberty should not be held up by prolonged discussions. He is calling for concrete actions to demonstrate consistency between institutional commitments and the handling of sensitive judicial matters in Gabon.

Why it mattersFrancis Hubert Aubame and Alain-Claude Bilie-By-Nze are directly affected by the ongoing detention and the political criticism of the judicial process.

Gabon represented in global song project

Gabonese singer SeBa joined 242 musicians from around the world for "The United Song."

Gabonese singer-songwriter SeBa has represented Gabon in "The United Song," a global music project featuring 242 artists from 242 countries. The collaborative song, developed over six years online, was released in September to celebrate cultural diversity and promote peace.

SeBa, who was contacted in February, expressed pride in representing her nation on the international stage. "The United Song" aims to use music as a universal language to unite people with a message of unity in diversity, bringing together various voices and sounds.

The artist sees her participation as a continuation of her work promoting Gabonese cultural heritage, following her April album "Pō Pō." SeBa stated that the song shows what can be achieved when people unite for a common cause, highlighting the power of artists to cross borders.

Why it mattersGabonese singer SeBa participated in a global music project to promote peace and cultural diversity.

Gabon Senate to assess local government capacity

Senate President Huguette Nyana Ekoumé presented plans for field missions to President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema in Libreville.

Gabon's Senate will conduct field missions to evaluate the capacity of local governments to assume transferred responsibilities. Senate President Huguette Nyana Ekoumé presented this initiative to President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema on Friday, October 23, in Libreville. The goal is to accelerate the effective implementation of decentralization by understanding local realities and constraints.

These missions aim to assess the administrative and financial capabilities of local authorities. Senators will identify needs in human resources, funding, and budget management. The discussions also highlighted the necessity of training and professionalizing local agents, particularly in tax collection and revenue generation.

The initiative seeks to enable local governments to mobilize more resources and gain financial autonomy. This evolution is considered essential for them to develop projects tailored to population needs and ensure transparent public resource management. The Senate's planned visits could bring decision-making closer to local realities.

Why it mattersGabon's local governments will be assessed for their capacity to manage transferred responsibilities and resources.
