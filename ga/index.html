Gabon signs Accra Convention for digitized cargo documents

The treaty aims to simplify trade by allowing electronic transfer of ownership rights and reducing paperwork for multimodal transport.

Gabon signed the United Nations Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents, known as the Accra Convention, in Accra, Ghana, on Monday, October 26, 2026. The Ministry of Transport announced that the treaty establishes common rules for issuing and using cargo documents in paper or electronic formats across all transport modes.

This convention allows ownership rights in goods to be transferred by their representing document, facilitating the sale of cargo in transit and trade finance. It also permits a single negotiable document for multimodal transport, reducing administrative burdens and the risk of lost or forged paperwork for Gabonese businesses.

The agreement supports ongoing reforms, including a proposal to digitize administrative documents and improve road freight tracking discussed in January 2026. Gabon must ratify the convention and incorporate its provisions into national law, with further steps needed to integrate systems and ensure business adoption of new tools.

Why it mattersGabonese businesses will benefit from a reduced administrative burden and more secure, predictable logistics operations.

Gabon firms sign iron ore rail deal

REMINAC and SETRAG will negotiate transport of Baniaka iron ore to Owendo port.

Gabon's Baniaka iron ore project has moved forward with a memorandum of understanding signed on October 28, 2026, in Libreville. REMINAC, which is developing the project, and SETRAG, the railway operator, agreed to negotiate the transport of future iron ore production by train to the port of Owendo. This agreement aims to structure the logistics for the project, which is expected to boost Gabon's mining potential.

The deal will lead to discussions on ore flow scheduling, transport capacity on the Transgabonais railway, potential infrastructure work, and pricing and commercial terms. SETRAG's director general, Christian Magni, stated the collaboration aligns with the Transgabonais' role in supporting the country's economic sectors.

Genmin, the group overseeing the Baniaka project, has invested approximately $120 million USD in its development. Thierry Makando, Genmin's country director, stated the signing is an important step towards realizing the Baniaka project, emphasizing the need for a reliable and cost-effective logistics solution to support production startup.

Why it mattersGabon's mining sector development is progressing with an agreement to transport iron ore.

Gabon hosts Italian business forum

Libreville will welcome around 20 Italian companies for a two-day economic exchange event in November 2026.

Libreville will host the first Italy-Gabon Business Forum on November 18-19, 2026. The event, presented to Vice-President Alexandre Barro Chambrier by Italy's Ambassador Stefano Moscatelli, aims to boost economic ties. Approximately 20 Italian companies are expected to attend, seeking investment and commercial partnerships.

The forum, titled "Italy-Gabon: New Paths for Growth," is organized by the Italian Embassy, the bilateral chamber of commerce, and the Italian Trade Agency. Ambassador Moscatelli stated that the invited companies are interested in sectors including agriculture, energy, water, tourism, and health, which are priorities for Gabon's President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema.

This initiative seeks to move from intentions to concrete investments, with a focus on identifying joint projects. For Gabon, the forum represents an effort to diversify economic partnerships and attract new investments, potentially addressing areas of significant national need.

Why it mattersGabonese businesses and the national economy stand to benefit from new investment and commercial partnerships with Italian companies.

Gabon signs carbon credit deal for 6 million tonnes

Gabon becomes the first African nation to sell certified forest preservation credits eligible for international aviation emissions compensation.

Gabon has signed its first agreements to sell jurisdictional carbon credits, committing to approximately 6 million tonnes of CO2 with Germany's state-owned energy company SEFE. The deals, announced on November 3, 2026, by the Presidency's spokesperson Théophane Nzame-Nze Biyoghe, aim to generate new revenue streams from forest preservation. First deliveries are expected by the first quarter of 2027.

These credits are certified under the ART-TREES standard and are eligible for CORSIA, the international aviation emissions compensation mechanism. The transactions are described as 'jurisdictional', meaning they are accounted for at a national or administrative territory level. Gabon's government stated that the country is not selling its land or forests, but rather carbon units, with ownership and management remaining with the state.

The financial details of the agreements, including the price per tonne and the total expected revenue, were not disclosed in the announcement. The net sums returning to the state will depend on the negotiated financial terms and distribution arrangements. The revenues will be held in a dedicated account until a national regulatory framework is established.

Why it mattersGabon's government aims to generate revenue for economic diversification through the sale of carbon credits derived from forest conservation.

Libreville cuts century-old Iroko for road expansion

The Libreville mayor's office removed a large Iroko tree in Okala-Auberge on November 3 to clear land for new roads.

The Libreville mayor's office removed a century-old Iroko tree in Okala-Auberge on November 3. The tree was cut down to clear space for future road developments in the capital's first district.

Teams from the municipality's environment department led the operation, which was overseen by Essono Bienvenu, director of Prevention and Risks. The tree's large size and location near homes required careful execution, with no incidents reported during the removal.

This action clears the necessary space for widening roads in the area. The municipality thanked Okala-Auberge residents for their cooperation and stated it will maintain security vigilance for future land clearing operations.
