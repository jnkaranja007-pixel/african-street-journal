President Inaugurates New Hospital in Booué

The new departmental hospital in Booué, inaugurated by President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema, aims to improve healthcare access for local populations.

President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema officially opened the Booué Departmental Hospital Center on Friday, an event attended by Health Minister Pr Elsa Nkana Joséphine Ayo épouse Bivigou. This new facility, spanning 850 square meters, is equipped with 24 beds and offers services in surgery, maternity, and general medicine, alongside an operating room, laboratory, and medical consultations. The establishment of this hospital represents a significant investment of nearly one and a half billion FCFA.

The project was funded through SETRAG's corporate social responsibility policy, with the explicit goal of enhancing health infrastructure in this region of Gabon. Local health workers and residents have expressed their satisfaction with the new center, which is expected to reduce the need for patients to travel to other towns for medical care. The hospital is now better equipped to address various medical needs within Booué and its surrounding areas.

This development provides the inhabitants of Booué with improved medical facilities, ensuring more immediate and accessible healthcare. The presence of essential services within the community is anticipated to alleviate pressure on existing, more distant healthcare providers and improve overall public health outcomes.

Why it mattersResidents of Booué and surrounding areas are affected by the improved access to medical care and specialized services provided by the new hospital.

Bosch Capital Demands $330 Million Debt Payment from Gabon

The South African fund threatens to damage Gabon's financial reputation if the debt from military equipment purchases is not settled by September 3.

Bosch Capital, a South African fund that acquired rights to a debt from Paramount Group, is demanding over $300 million from Gabon. The fund has set a September 3 deadline for Libreville to pay. In a letter dated August 24, Bosch Capital warned of actions that could negatively impact Gabon's financial standing. This debt originates from maintenance and support services for military equipment, stemming from a 2014 contract, and has accumulated to approximately $330 million.

The fund, represented by Seven Pillars Law, is attempting to resolve a financial dispute by pressuring the Gabonese state. Bosch Capital has indicated it may inform international financial system actors if its demand is not met. This threat extends beyond legal action, potentially affecting Gabon's sovereign risk perception among rating agencies, banks, and international investors.

The debt is linked to past military equipment transactions. In 2006-2007, Gabon acquired Mirage F1AZ aircraft from Paramount Group and Aerosud, which were previously part of the South African Air Force. Bosch Capital's claim is rooted in a 2014 contract for maintenance and support of military equipment, with the current amount reflecting accumulated sums and litigation developments.

Why it mattersGabon's financial reputation and access to international financing are at risk due to the potential impact on its sovereign risk perception.

TotalEnergies EP Gabon Profit Plummets 87% Despite Favorable Oil Market

The company's net profit fell to six million dollars in the second quarter of 2026, impacted by stock position changes and increased operating costs.

TotalEnergies EP Gabon reported an 87% drop in net profit for the second quarter of 2026, reaching six million dollars, despite a favorable oil market. The company's revenue increased by thirteen percent to one hundred eleven million dollars, driven by rising crude prices. The average Brent crude price rose twenty-eight percent to one hundred three point eight dollars per barrel during this period, while TotalEnergies EP Gabon's commercialized crude averaged one hundred point two dollars per barrel, up seven percent.

The significant profit decline was primarily attributed to a negative stock position variation of sixty-nine million dollars. Additionally, higher operating expenses and non-operational financial charges reduced earnings by six million dollars. These impacts were partially offset by revenue growth, a twenty-one million dollar decrease in tax charges, and a five million dollar reduction in depreciation.

For the first half of 2026, TotalEnergies EP Gabon maintained a solid net profit of fifty-one million dollars. Revenue for the semester was two hundred nine million dollars, a four percent decrease year-on-year, mainly due to a reduced export schedule. Crude oil production saw a slight increase to fifteen point six thousand barrels per day in the first half.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the significant drop in net profit, indicating potential challenges in translating market conditions into earnings.

Gabon hires 248 volunteer teachers into public service

Two hundred forty-eight teachers who previously volunteered in Ogooué-Ivindo province have been officially integrated into the civil service.

The formal integration of 248 teachers into Gabon's public service was marked by a ceremony in Makokou on Friday, August 28, 2026. These educators had been volunteering in public schools in the Ogooué-Ivindo province for years, often under precarious administrative conditions. The event, attended by Minister of State for National Education and Civic Instruction, Camélia Ntoutoume Leclercq, involved the distribution of certificates and initial payment vouchers, recognizing their commitment, training, and qualifications.

This regularization process acknowledges the teachers' dedication and the essential role they played in the education system despite administrative uncertainties. Their integration into the civil service signifies a tangible fulfillment of promises, transitioning them from volunteer status to fully recognized and salaried public employees. Minister Ntoutoume Leclercq emphasized the professional recognition this change represents, stating they are no longer volunteers but certified educators.

The provincial director of education for Ogooué-Ivindo, Jean Lin Essié Effé, expressed gratitude to the government for its efforts in the education sector, specifically highlighting the regularization of these former volunteers. Beneficiaries shared their long-awaited satisfaction, noting that this recognition of their years of work provides renewed motivation to serve Gabonese schools with commitment and responsibility. The state aims to strengthen its teaching staff with these qualified and integrated professionals.

Why it mattersTwo hundred forty-eight volunteer teachers in Gabon have been officially hired into the public service, securing their employment and regular salaries.

Culture · 2026-08-29

Children Learn Galoa Language to Preserve Cultural Heritage

An interactive workshop in Ntyatanga village aims to teach young people the ancestral Galoa language, fostering cultural memory and identity.

In Ntyatanga village, Moyen-Ogooué province, children recently participated in a Galoa language learning workshop. The initiative uses participatory methods to encourage the transmission of this ancestral tongue, aiming to empower youth as guardians of cultural heritage. The workshop focused on oral and written practice in a relaxed environment, emphasizing progress through shared correction and confidence-building.

Led by Francis Akita, Victor Vériany, and Arthur Lamie Akendengue, the workshop covered basic communication, sentence structure, pronouns, vocabulary, numbers, and body parts. Interactive methods like role-playing, debates, and pronunciation exercises were employed to engage children and instill a desire to speak, understand, and write Galoa. The organizers believe that preserving traditional languages is crucial for maintaining collective memory, values, and knowledge.

The event highlighted the ongoing challenge of ensuring the Galoa language remains actively used within families and by younger generations. Potential future actions include more frequent workshops, intergenerational gatherings, and the creation of educational materials to support the language's continuity. The initiative underscores that a language's survival depends on its active use, teaching, and valorization by those who will carry it forward.

Why it mattersChildren in Ntyatanga are learning the Galoa language to ensure its survival and preserve their cultural heritage.
