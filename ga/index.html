Gabon Senate questions 2027 budget for public works

Public works funding drops 29% while water, energy, and oil budgets face scrutiny.

Gabon's Senate Finance Committee questioned ministers on Tuesday, November 10, about the 2027 budget, focusing on public works, water, energy, and oil sectors. The Minister of Public Works requested 268.27 billion CFA francs for infrastructure, a 29% decrease from 2026's 379.15 billion CFA francs. Senators raised concerns about the execution of announced projects and the delivery timelines for infrastructure works.

Ministers for Water and Energy, and Petroleum defended their 2027 budget proposals before the Senate commission. The Water and Energy ministry seeks 154.79 billion CFA francs, an increase from 129.76 billion CFA francs previously. The Petroleum sector's budget is set to decrease by over 13 billion CFA francs to 20.02 billion CFA francs, impacting plans for digitizing oil data and awarding new permits.

Senators also questioned the ministers on job localization, access to essential services like water and electricity nationwide, and corporate social responsibility in oil-producing areas. The government's infrastructure and energy ambitions must now contend with budget constraints and public demands for essential services.

Foreign investors plan five ecolodges in Gabon's national parks

A plan presented to President Oligui Nguema would build five ecolodges in national park zones and offer cheaper tourist services to Gabonese residents.

A group of foreign investors specialising in ecotourism plans to build five ecolodges in the zones of Gabon's national parks. Tourism Minister Axel Jason Ayenoue presented the project to President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema on November 10, together with representatives of African Landscape, a company that develops ecotourism projects.

The company says it has more than 50 years of experience in the sector and aims to offer visitors experiences built around the country's biodiversity, forest landscapes and natural resources. The investors also want to reduce the cost of some services so that Gabonese residents can visit national destinations.

According to the government, ecotourism could diversify the economy if tourism development is balanced with preserving ecosystems. The packet says success will depend on securing financing, the quality of infrastructure, site access and the involvement of local communities.

Why it mattersGabonese residents would gain access to cheaper tourist services, while the ecolodge plan depends on financing, infrastructure, site access and local community involvement.

Gabon council reviews urban renewal and jobs

The interministerial council met on November 10 to discuss urban land reclamation and employment tracking.

Gabon's interministerial council convened on November 10, chaired by Vice-President Hermann Immongault, to address urban renewal and employment policies. The meeting focused on reclaiming land in Grand Libreville, including areas like Okala, Diba-Diba, and Lac Bleu, to improve urban modernization, traffic flow, and emergency service access. Immongault stressed the need for these urban transformations to respect affected populations and involve administrative consultation.

The council also prioritized employment, mandating sector-specific ministries to directly monitor jobs created by their activities through the "Pass Emploi" program. This aims to produce more accurate annual job creation statistics and assess economic policy impacts. A Delivery Unit will be established under the vice-president's supervision to oversee these efforts. Additionally, a draft law on recovering illicit assets was reviewed for harmonization among concerned administrations.

Preparations for national discussions on Iboga, a plant resource with scientific, economic, and heritage importance, were also on the agenda. These meetings will define a national strategy for preserving, valuing, and regulating Iboga. The council's decisions aim to strengthen public policy monitoring and refine institutional reforms.

Why it mattersThe decisions made by the interministerial council will affect urban planning, employment tracking, and the management of the Iboga plant resource in Gabon.

Gabon prepares national household living conditions survey

The National Statistics Institute is raising awareness among economic actors before collecting data on spending and food habits.

The National Statistics Institute (INSTAT Gabon) is preparing to launch a national survey on household living conditions across Gabon. This initiative aims to gather precise data on the economic and social realities of Gabonese populations. Teams will collect information on household spending, dietary habits, market prices, and non-standard measurement units used in agricultural production.

A mission to raise awareness among economic actors, including traders and agricultural producers, recently took place in Port-Gentil. Edwige Nfoume Ella-Mbeke led this mission to explain the survey's modalities and encourage collaboration. The collected data will help assess living standards, current expenses, and difficulties faced by households.

These statistics are intended to inform public policy development and evaluation, particularly in areas like poverty reduction, food security, and social protection. The survey's success depends on the active participation of the public and economic stakeholders. Awareness campaigns will continue in other provinces before data collection begins.

Why it mattersThe survey will provide data to help the government develop and evaluate policies on poverty, food security, and social protection.

Libreville marks 86 years since the 1940 Gué-Gué battle

Soldiers, sailors and civilians killed in the 1940 fighting were honoured at a ceremony before the Wall of Remembrance in Libreville.

On Monday, November 9, 2026, the Compagnons mémoriels gathered at the Gué-Gué bridge in Libreville to remember the men who died in the 1940 battle, 86 years earlier. In November 1940, forces loyal to the Vichy regime clashed in Gabon with those who had joined Free France, so soldiers fought soldiers under different flags.

The ceremony at the Wall of Remembrance brought together civil and military officials, religious leaders, memorial companions and children. A wreath was laid at the foot of the monument, a call to the dead was sounded, and the Gabonese and French anthems were played. The event also marked the 56th anniversary of General de Gaulle's death, and Didier Lespinas gave the opening address.

The Wall of Remembrance is meant to honour more than commanders. It also names African riflemen, health workers, sailors, aviators and civilians caught in the fighting. The organisers say the dead live on only while the living keep remembering them.

Why it mattersFamilies and descendants of those killed, along with the memorial companions who maintain the Wall, are the people the commemoration is directly meant to serve.
