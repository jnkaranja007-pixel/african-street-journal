Gabon launches digital platform for public services

Gabon Connect aims to simplify administrative tasks, secure public revenue, and improve the business environment.

Gabon launched its digital platform, Gabon Connect, on Thursday, October 22, 2026, in Libreville. The platform integrates services like Déclaration unique des salaires (DUS) and TrésorPay to streamline administrative processes and enhance public revenue collection. Vice-president Hermann Immongault presented the initiative, which aims to simplify procedures, reduce processing times, and improve the overall business climate.

The Gabon Connect platform will progressively consolidate digitized public services onto a single online interface. It includes six key services: eTax for taxes, eEmployeur for social formalities, TrésorPay for non-fiscal revenues, eDouane for customs, and ePropriété for land registry. The full deployment is scheduled to conclude by December 2027, with the goal of reducing physical travel and procedural complexity for citizens and businesses.

Beyond administrative simplification, Gabon Connect is designed to improve oversight of fiscal, customs, and non-fiscal revenues. The introduction of DUS and TrésorPay specifically targets the security of collections and transparency in public fund management. This digital transformation is seen as a strategic lever to de-silo services, ensure reliable information, and boost public action efficiency, according to Immongault.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens and businesses will experience simplified administrative procedures and improved transparency in public service delivery.

Gabon youth fight female cancers in Mouila

Over 300 young people learned about prevention and risks during a three-day awareness marathon.

In Mouila, Gabon, the Jeunes Capables collective organized a three-day awareness marathon in secondary schools to combat female cancers. The initiative, which concluded on October 17, aimed to educate over 300 young people about prevention and associated risks. Dr. Aubin Djembi informed students about the causes, risk factors, and consequences of breast and cervical cancers, stressing the importance of early detection and regular medical follow-ups.

Philippe Arnaud Nziengui, president of Jeunes Capables, expressed satisfaction with the high participation of the target audience. The event highlighted the ongoing challenge of promoting early detection for improved recovery chances in Gabon, where diagnostic delays and limited access to specialized exams persist. Cultural barriers and fear of results can also deter women from seeking medical care.

Globocan estimates that Gabon records approximately 295 new cases of breast cancer and 146 new cases of cervical cancer annually. The initiative by Jeunes Capables shows that the fight against female cancers requires information and community mobilization, alongside effective access to healthcare. Medical specialists emphasize that screening should extend beyond October, with any unusual symptoms warranting a consultation.

Why it mattersYoung people in Mouila learned about the prevention and risks of female cancers, including breast and cervical cancers.

PDG plans 13th ordinary congress for November

Gabon's ruling party will hold its congress from November 27-29 to restructure and revitalize its organization.

The Gabonese Democratic Party (PDG) will hold its 13th ordinary congress from November 27 to 29, 2026. Preparations for the event, aimed at revitalizing and restructuring the party, were initiated on October 22 in Libreville. A committee for organization must be in place by October 27.

Party leaders, including Blaise Louembé, met on October 22 at the party headquarters to validate the preparation steps. The congress is intended to translate ambitions for renewal into concrete decisions and strengthen the party's structure. The PDG faces the challenge of redefining its place in the Gabonese political system.

Proposals from five thematic commissions, formulated during an October 9-11 workshop, were presented to officials. The upcoming congress is seen as a critical moment for the PDG to clarify its direction, reorganize its structures, and re-mobilize its members.

Why it mattersThe PDG, Gabon's ruling party, is holding a congress to restructure and redefine its political direction.

Politics · 2026-10-24

Gabon warns against illegal land sales at Cap Santa Clara

The Ministry of Housing alerts residents to irregular occupations that threaten government development projects.

Gabon's Ministry of Housing, Habitat, Urbanism, and Land Registry issued a warning on October 23 regarding illegal land sales and occupations at Cap Santa Clara. The ministry stated that these irregular practices threaten government urbanization and development projects in the area. The alert aims to preserve the integrity of public land operations and remind citizens of the rules governing land transactions and construction.

The ministry detailed that land sales or transfers must be conducted by legally authorized individuals with proper land titles or administrative acts. Occupying a plot of land also requires legal standing, and any construction must adhere to existing regulations, including obtaining building permits. The warning emphasizes that fraudulent sales, illegal occupations, and unauthorized constructions can lead to land evacuation and building demolition without compensation.

This official statement serves as a deterrent against dubious transactions and aims to protect buyers from losing their investments. The government is urging the public to remain vigilant and refrain from any anarchic initiatives that could result in demolitions and evictions. The ministry's action is intended to safeguard planned public developments at Cap Santa Clara and prevent land disputes.

Why it mattersGabon's government is warning citizens about illegal land sales and occupations that could lead to financial losses and the demolition of their properties.
