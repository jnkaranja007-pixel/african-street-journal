Gabon seizes nearly 500 cannabis bundles in Woleu-Ntem

Security services dismantled a cross-border trafficking network in Bitam, arresting several suspects, including a woman who says she has sold cannabis for 17 years.

Security and intelligence services seized nearly 500 bundles of cannabis, estimated at about 16 million FCFA, during a special operation in the Woleu-Ntem region. Several people suspected of supplying a cross-border sales network were also arrested.

Investigators in Bitam, in the Méyo-Kyé district, found two large bundles holding more than 453 cannabis bundles, reportedly grown, packed and stored near homes. The alleged owner fled, and her son indirectly acknowledged the facts. A Gabonese woman and a Cameroonian man are suspected of drug sales and illegal ammunition sales. A woman close to 70 and a 22-year-old high school student were also detained.

Prosecutor Léance Mba Mihindou, of the specialised national prosecution office, said the authorities maintain a zero-tolerance policy on narcotics. The network reportedly linked Méyo-Kyé, Kiossi in Cameroon and Equatorial Guinea, and traffickers had shifted some activity inland as the Grand Libreville market dried up.

Why it mattersResidents of Bitam and surrounding districts face arrests and the seizure of a large cannabis stock, and the network reportedly reached across the Cameroon and Equatorial Guinea borders.

SOS Prisonniers Gabon advocates for inmate rights in five provinces

The NGO launched a tour on November 9 to raise awareness about detention conditions and prisoner rights.

SOS Prisonniers Gabon began a tour on November 9 from Lambaréné to advocate for the rights of detained individuals. The organization aims to inform administrative and judicial authorities about detention conditions and the protection of rights for those deprived of liberty. This initiative, part of the "Dignity in Detention" project, will visit five penitentiary establishments across five provinces.

The tour will take the delegation to Mouila, Tchibanga, Oyem, and Franceville, in addition to Lambaréné. They plan to discuss issues such as prison overcrowding, respect for visitation rights, the use of pre-trial detention, and strengthening the role of the sentencing judge. SOS Prisonniers Gabon will present its advocacy notes and recommendations at each location.

Beyond institutional discussions, the organization is also providing humanitarian aid, distributing food and hygiene kits to inmates in Lambaréné. The project, implemented with Agir Ensemble pour les Droits Humains and supported by the European Union in Gabon, combines institutional dialogue with direct assistance to detainees.

Why it mattersDetainees in Gabon face issues like overcrowding and limited rights, which SOS Prisonniers Gabon is addressing through advocacy and aid.

Gabon justice ministry to host business security symposium

The November 13 event in Port-Gentil aims to build investor confidence through judicial security.

Gabon's Ministry of Justice will host a symposium in Port-Gentil on November 13 focused on strengthening the legal framework for businesses. The event, under the patronage of President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema and supervised by Minister Augustin Emane, seeks to improve the business climate by fostering dialogue between the justice sector and private operators. Approximately 100 participants, including magistrates, lawyers, and entrepreneurs, are expected to attend.

The symposium, themed "Justice and business climate: building trust based on judicial security," aims to make judicial security a tool for economic competitiveness. Minister Emane stated that businesses should not be above the law but must receive legal protection. Discussions will cover contractual security, commercial dispute resolution, and adherence to Ohada law, which harmonizes business legal rules across several African states.

The meeting intends to identify improvements for the legal and judicial system. By ensuring predictable court decisions, the initiative aims to consolidate investor confidence in Gabon. The event will also address social relations and legality within the judicial institution.

Why it mattersGabon's business environment and investor confidence will be directly affected by discussions on legal and judicial security.

Gabon's Panthers lose two players before Lesotho match

Goalkeeper Junior François Bekale had a convulsive episode on a flight, and Orphé Mbina has been ruled out with a thigh injury.

Gabon's national football team has lost two players ahead of its match against Lesotho, set for Friday, November 13, in Franceville. Goalkeeper Junior François Bekale suffered a convulsive episode on a flight from Libreville to Mvengué, and Orphé Mbina is also out for physical reasons.

The Gabonese Football Federation (Fegafoot) said in a statement on November 8 that the seizure lasted about five minutes and that the team's medical staff on board gave immediate help. Bekale is being evacuated by air to the Omar Bongo Ondimba Army Instruction Hospital in Libreville for further examinations, and no conclusions have yet been drawn. Mangasport goalkeeper Joël Mbore has been called up to replace him, making his first senior national team appearance.

Mbina has an intermittent discomfort in his left adductor and will rest for one week, according to the medical staff of his club, Sabah FK. The technical staff has called up Alan Do Marcolino to replace him.

Why it mattersBekale is under medical evaluation and Mbina will rest for a week, leaving the squad with new goalkeeper Joël Mbore and Alan Do Marcolino.

Gabon football task force submits final report

The Task Force for the renewal of Gabonese football presented its findings on governance, financing, and professionalization.

A Task Force focused on reforming Gabonese football submitted its final report to the Minister of Sports, Paul Ulrich Kessany Zategwa, on Monday, November 9, 2026. The report, developed over five days of work, addresses the governance, financing, and professionalization of the sport in the country.

The Task Force for the renewal of Gabonese football convened from October 22 to 26 at the Sino-Gabonese Friendship Stadium. Participants included national football governing bodies, first and second division clubs, women's football representatives, club associations, and experts who discussed structural challenges.

The report's recommendations aim to provide a new impetus for the reform process initiated by sports authorities. These conclusions will inform future orientations and measures for the sustainable development of Gabonese football, aligning with a national sports modernization initiative.

Why it mattersGabonese football stakeholders are affected by the proposed reforms in governance, financing, and professionalization.
