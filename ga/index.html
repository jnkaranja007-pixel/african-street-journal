CEAG Offers Affordable Goods in Makokou Ahead of Liberation Day

The Central Purchasing Agency of Gabon (CEAG) deployed a Mega Market in Makokou, offering food and essential items at accessible prices during the Liberation Day festivities.

The Central Purchasing Agency of Gabon (CEAG) has expanded its operations into the interior of the country with its Mega Market initiative aimed at combating the high cost of living. Launched in Makokou on August 24 and running until August 26, the market provided a range of food products and essentials at prices described as accessible in anticipation of the Liberation Day celebrations on August 30.

The initiative, which saw the presence of Senate President Huguette Nyana and Ogooué-Ivindo Governor Christiane Leckat, aims to bring CEAG's offerings closer to populations outside of Libreville. This follows similar operations in various locations, with CEAG's General Director Théophile Boutamba stating the goal is to allow more people access to a diverse selection of products at affordable prices.

Beyond the immediate supply, the CEAG faces the challenge of ensuring the long-term availability of goods in provincial towns, where transport costs and logistical constraints can increase prices. The agency's success will depend on establishing regular supply mechanisms to create a lasting impact on household budgets, moving beyond the temporary nature of the Mega Markets.

Why it mattersGabonese consumers in Makokou can access food and essential items at lower prices, easing household budgets.

Gabonese Journalist Rudy Hombenet Dies After Awards Recognition

Rudy Hombenet Anvingui, recognized as best print journalist in 2026, passed away in Libreville on August 27, 2026, after a decade-long career focused on social issues, education.

Journalist Rudy Hombenet Anvingui, recently honored as the best print journalist at the Makongonio 2026 awards, died in Libreville on the night of August 26-27, 2026. Her passing comes just two months after receiving this significant recognition, marking the end of a career spanning over ten years at the daily newspaper L’Union.

Hombenet, who joined L’Union in 2015, had focused her work on social issues, education, and more recently, health. Her reporting often tackled concrete topics such as tuberculosis, depression, malaria, and the functioning of hospitals, bringing these issues to the daily realities of Gabonese citizens. She had previously expressed a strong aversion to injustice, stating that journalism allowed her to give voice to the voiceless.

Her recent health-focused columns were noted for their direct, interrogative, and critical tone. In the weeks leading up to her death, she raised concerns about tuberculosis patients being lost to follow-up and alerted readers to a resurgence of malaria and weakening prevention efforts. She also wrote about the responsibility of resident physicians within the healthcare system.

Why it mattersThe death of Rudy Hombenet Anvingui affects the journalism profession and the public who followed her reporting on social issues, education, and health in Gabon.

Children Learn Galoa Language to Preserve Gabonese Heritage

An interactive workshop in Ntyatanga village aims to teach ancestral Galoa language to youth, fostering cultural transmission and safeguarding collective memory.

In Ntyatanga village, children recently participated in a Galoa language learning workshop, engaging with the ancestral tongue through participatory methods. Organizers aim to strengthen the transmission of the language, positioning the youth as key players in preserving cultural heritage. The sessions focused on oral and written practice in a relaxed environment, encouraging children to speak without fear of making mistakes.

The workshop covered essential language elements such as self-introduction, sentence construction, personal pronouns, vocabulary expansion, numbers, and human anatomy. Interactive techniques including role-playing, debates, reading, pronunciation exercises, and situational practice were employed to immerse children in the language. This approach seeks to build confidence and foster a desire to speak, understand, and write Galoa.

Led by Francis Akita, Victor Vériany, and Arthur Lamie Akendengue, the initiative is founded on the belief that traditional language preservation relies on transmission to new generations. Galoa carries narratives, values, and collective memory, and its decline risks a loss of generational knowledge. The long-term goal is to integrate Galoa into daily family life through continued workshops and intergenerational exchanges.

Why it mattersChildren in Ntyatanga are learning the Galoa language to ensure its survival and connect with their cultural heritage.

New Road Connects Kinguélé Market to Belle Peinture

Construction advances on a secondary road linking Kinguélé market to Belle Peinture, aiming to ease traffic congestion in Libreville's 3rd arrondissement and offer an alternative.

Work on the secondary road connecting the Kinguélé market to Belle Peinture is progressing, with the new infrastructure set to improve accessibility in the 3rd arrondissement of Libreville. This route is intended to provide an alternative for drivers facing traffic jams on main roads. The road also strengthens the secondary network in the affected neighborhoods and is expected to facilitate population movement.

According to Justin Rodrigue Mbagangoye, the deputy for the 2nd seat of the 3rd arrondissement, this project fulfills a long-standing initiative dating back decades. He stated that the current leadership has instructed a company to open this secondary route, which runs from "the two poles" directly to Kinguélé, to alleviate congestion and enable security forces to access underserved areas.

Residents anticipate improved travel conditions, with easier access to transportation and smoother circulation. Angéla Nkoghé, a local inhabitant, noted the benefits for young people regarding safety and mobility, allowing them to catch taxis closer to home. However, the presence of electrical poles along some sections requires attention to ensure the road's full functionality, safety, and fluid use.

Why it mattersThis new road will improve accessibility and reduce travel times for residents and businesses in Libreville's 3rd arrondissement.

Bosch Capital Demands $330 Million Debt from Gabon

South African fund Bosch Capital has set a September 3 deadline for Gabon to pay a debt of over $300 million, threatening to damage the country's financial reputation.

Bosch Capital, a South African fund that acquired rights to a debt from Paramount Group, is demanding over $300 million from Gabon. The fund has given Libreville until September 3 to comply, warning of actions that could impact Gabon's financial standing. This debt originates from a 2014 contract for maintenance and support of military equipment, with the current claim accumulating to $330 million.

The fund, represented by Seven Pillars Law, is threatening to inform international financial actors if Gabon fails to meet its demand. This action targets Gabon's sovereign risk perception among rating agencies, banks, and international investors. The stakes extend beyond the debt amount for a nation seeking to maintain financial credibility and access to external funding.

Gabon's relationship with Paramount Group and its affiliate Aerosud dates back to 2006-2007, when the country acquired Mirage F1AZ aircraft from South Africa's former air force. Paramount Group was involved in the acquisition, maintenance, and operation of these aircraft, indicating a long-standing cooperation in military aviation capabilities.

Why it mattersBosch Capital's demand and threat to report Gabon to international financial actors could negatively affect the country's credit rating and access to capital.
