Gabon forms leasing association with SFI support

The new AGA CB aims to boost leasing finance for businesses in Gabon, currently only 2.6% of private sector funding.

The Gabonese Association of Leasing Actors (AGA CB) was officially formed on October 21 in Libreville. Serge Bertrand Abessolo, representing the Minister of Economy, supervised the constitutive general assembly. The International Finance Corporation (IFC) supported this initiative to structure the leasing sector.

Ruffin Nzoghé Ndong Simore, director general of Alios Gabon, was elected president of the AGA CB. He stated the association aims to make leasing an essential lever for investment financing and business development. In 2021, leasing finance in Gabon was 22.3 billion CFA francs, representing just 2.6% of private sector funding.

The potential annual market for leasing in Gabon is estimated between 180 and 200 billion CFA francs. Leasing allows companies, especially SMEs, to acquire necessary equipment without large immediate capital outlay. This mechanism supports modernization, job creation, and economic diversification.

Why it mattersGabonese businesses, particularly SMEs, can now access a more structured leasing sector to acquire equipment.

Gabon ranks 119th in rule of law index

The World Justice Project's 2026 report shows Gabon improved less than 1% globally.

Gabon ranks 119th out of 143 countries in the World Justice Project's (WJP) 2026 Rule of Law Index, scoring 0.40 overall. The country is among a minority that showed progress, with its global rule of law score increasing by less than 1% this year. Despite this improvement, Gabon ranks 23rd out of 34 countries in sub-Saharan Africa and 42nd out of 44 in upper-middle-income countries.

The WJP index, published on October 21 in Washington, assesses adherence to laws, checks and balances, fundamental rights, and justice system functionality. While Gabon saw improvements in access to and affordability of civil justice, along with the absence of discrimination in civil justice, challenges persist regarding public freedoms and institutional balance. The report notes that 62% of countries globally have seen a decline in the rule of law, with Gabon experiencing limitations on government powers, governmental transparency, and fundamental rights.

Globally, Denmark leads the index, while Venezuela is among the lowest-ranked nations. In sub-Saharan Africa, Rwanda is the highest-ranked country. The WJP highlights that over 86% of countries have experienced a sustained five-year decline in at least one safeguard against authoritarian regimes, a trend that includes Gabon.

Why it mattersGabon's ranking in the rule of law index affects its citizens' fundamental rights and governmental transparency.

Gabon launches digital platform for public services

Gabon Connect aims to simplify administrative tasks, secure public revenue, and improve the business environment.

Gabon launched Gabon Connect on Thursday, October 22, 2026, in Libreville, a digital platform designed to centralize public services online. Vice-president Hermann Immongault presented the new phase which includes the deployment of the Déclaration unique des salaires (DUS) and TrésorPay portals. The initiative seeks to streamline administrative procedures, secure public finances, and enhance the business climate through a single digital window.

The Gabon Connect platform will gradually integrate six key services: eTax for taxes, eEmployeur for social formalities, TrésorPay for non-fiscal and local government revenues, eDouane for customs, and ePropriété for land registry. Immongault stated that the digital transformation is a strategic lever to de-silo services, ensure reliable information, and boost public action efficiency. The full deployment is scheduled to conclude in December 2027.

Beyond administrative simplification, Gabon Connect aims to improve the management of fiscal, customs, and non-fiscal revenues. The introduction of DUS and TrésorPay specifically targets the security of collections and transparency in public fund management. This digital shift is expected to facilitate investments and increase business competitiveness by making public services more fluid and predictable.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens and businesses will experience simplified administrative processes and improved transparency in public revenue collection.
