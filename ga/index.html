Gabon seeks Chinese investment for industrialisation

President Oligui Nguema invited Chinese investors to the Nkok special economic zone on November 5, 2026.

Gabon's President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema urged Chinese investors to develop local processing of raw materials during the 9th China International Import Expo (CIIE) in Shanghai on November 5, 2026. Gabon, the guest of honour, participated with a national pavilion for the first time, aiming to attract industrial partnerships. The president stated that Gabon is a platform for investment to build a new future.

Speaking as the only African president at the opening ceremony, Nguema highlighted the long-standing friendship between China and Gabon and the concrete results of their trade agreements. He invited investors to the Nkok special economic zone, describing it as a strategic gateway to Central Africa with 200 million consumers. He also pointed to opportunities in timber, manganese, iron, energy, water, health, digital, and agriculture sectors, noting five million hectares of arable land.

The Gabonese leader presented Gabon as offering political stability, legal security, a new investment code, free profit transfer, and reforms in labour, mining, and petroleum codes. He mentioned the digitalisation of administration, allowing for online company creation in 48 hours. The expo showcased local products and highlighted Gabon's potential in green economy, carbon credits, renewable energy, and pharmacopoeia.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens are affected by the call for investment to create jobs and develop local processing of raw materials.

Gabon president visits Shanghai robot valley

Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema seeks to position Gabon in global tech revolution by integrating AI and robotics.

Gabon's President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema visited the Zhangjiang Robot Valley in Shanghai on November 4, 2026, expressing a desire for the nation to actively participate in the global technological revolution. He aims to integrate artificial intelligence into public services and promote robotization, ensuring Gabon is part of this transformation rather than a passive observer.

During his visit, President Oligui Nguema observed robots performing surgical procedures, patient rehabilitation, and managing industrial operations, noting that this is current reality, not science fiction. He emphasized the urgency of preparing young Gabonese for these emerging technologies, stating, "Gabon will not watch this revolution from afar."

Gabon is already taking steps toward digital transformation, including the completion of preparatory work for its first national Artificial Intelligence Strategy on September 30, supported by the UNDP. The country also inaugurated its first national Data Center in Nkok on July 3, 2026, a Tier III certified facility designed to host and secure strategic data within the nation.

Why it mattersPresident Oligui Nguema's visit and stated ambitions aim to equip Gabon's youth and public services with AI and robotics, positioning the nation for future technological development.

Gabon aviation fees rise 7.97%

Aviation revenue reached 1.547 billion FCFA by September 2026, up from the previous year.

Aviation fees in Gabon generated 1.547 billion FCFA by September 2026, an increase of 7.97% compared to the same period in 2025. Other operating revenues also grew to 316 million FCFA, a 5.93% rise. These figures were presented on November 4, 2026, in Libreville during the 46th session on national aviation activities.

The overall budget realization rate stood at 59.2% as of September 30, 2026. Joachim Tchissambou M’Boundou, cabinet chief for Asecna Gabon's director general, reported that out of 12 decisions reviewed, four were fully executed, four are in progress, three partially executed, and one remains unexecuted. Two recommendations have not been acted upon.

Beyond financial indicators, participants discussed improving aviation service quality, safety, and continuity. The partnership between Gabon and Asecna, based on a management delegation contract signed on February 5, 2019, emphasizes shared responsibility. Enhancing airport facilities and ensuring decisions are implemented are priorities for strengthening public aviation services.

Why it mattersAviation revenue in Gabon has increased, impacting the national budget and the efficiency of aviation services.

Politics · 2026-11-06

Gabon ex-PM questioned over detention

Alain-Claude Bilie-By-Nze faced his judge for the first time since his April 16 arrest.

Former Gabonese Prime Minister Alain-Claude Bilie-By-Nze was questioned by an investigating judge in Libreville on Thursday, November 5. Assisted by his lawyer, Me Sarah-Ognyane, Bilie-By-Nze appeared before the judge for the first time since his preventive detention began on April 16. The hearing followed months of requests from his defense team.

The interrogation, which started at 9 a.m., focused on statements previously made by a woman named Baba Ramatou on April 27. Me Sarah-Ognyane stated that her client provided clarifications on the accusations against him and described the proceedings as having gone "very well." The lawyer deemed the audition late but necessary for respecting the defense's rights.

The next steps in the procedure may include a confrontation between Bilie-By-Nze and Baba Ramatou, followed by a moral investigation and defense submissions. The judge will then decide whether to dismiss the case, leading to Bilie-By-Nze's release, or to send him to trial. His lawyer reported that Bilie-By-Nze is in good physical and psychological condition despite his detention.

Why it mattersFormer Prime Minister Alain-Claude Bilie-By-Nze is undergoing a judicial process that could lead to his release or trial.
