Chinese firm CCECC seeks new Gabonese infrastructure contracts

After renovating the Senate building, the China Civil Engineering Construction Corporation presented road, rail, and mining projects to the government.

The China Civil Engineering Construction Corporation (CCECC) is seeking to expand its presence in Gabon by securing new infrastructure contracts. The Chinese firm, currently involved in the renovation of the Omar Bongo Ondimba palace, which houses the Senate, met with the vice-president of the government, Hermann Immongault. During the meeting, CCECC representatives showcased their expertise and presented several projects in the road, rail, and mining sectors.

This initiative follows discussions held in Australia between government officials and CCECC representatives. The company aims to leverage its ongoing work on the Senate building as a reference point for future development in the country. CCECC is particularly interested in strategic sectors aligned with Gabon's infrastructure development ambitions, including the Bélinga iron ore project, which requires significant infrastructure for mineral transport.

CCECC plans to engage with several Gabonese ministries, including Transport, Public Works, Energy, and Mines, to explore cooperation possibilities. The company intends to build upon its experience in African infrastructure projects, especially in rail and aviation, to position itself for future major construction projects in Gabon. The renovation of the Senate building serves as a foundation for broader engagement.

Why it mattersThe China Civil Engineering Construction Corporation is pursuing new contracts in Gabon's road, rail, and mining sectors, potentially impacting infrastructure development and investment.

Gabon Food Agency Finds Erectile Dysfunction Drug in Coffee

The Gabonese Food Security Agency warns consumers about "Bio Herbs Coffee" due to undeclared tadalafil, a prescription medication.

The Gabonese Food Security Agency (AGASA) has issued a warning regarding the sale of "Bio Herbs Coffee," also marketed as "Bio Herbs Café Docteur Secret" under the DR’S Secret brand. Although presented as a 100% natural dietary supplement, AGASA's investigations revealed the presence of tadalafil, an active pharmaceutical ingredient used to treat erectile dysfunction. This substance was not declared on the product's labeling, posing a significant health risk to consumers.

AGASA highlighted potential adverse effects from consuming the coffee, including dangerously low blood pressure, vision or hearing disturbances, and in severe cases, priapism. The agency emphasized the heightened risk when tadalafil is combined with nitrate medications, commonly prescribed for cardiovascular conditions, which can lead to a potentially fatal drop in blood pressure. The presence of undeclared tadalafil in products marketed as natural supplements is a documented health concern in other countries.

Consequently, AGASA strongly advises against the consumption of "Bio Herbs Coffee" and has instructed all relevant operators to immediately remove the product from all points of sale, including physical stores, boutiques, and online platforms. The agency urges anyone possessing the product or aware of its distribution to report it to their services, ANMAPS, or by calling the toll-free number 1411, to enhance vigilance around "natural" products sold as dietary supplements.

Why it mattersConsumers are at risk of serious health complications, including dangerously low blood pressure and priapism, from consuming coffee containing undeclared erectile dysfunction medication.

Gabon Water Funds Vanish Amidst Project Shortfalls

An investigation into 400 billion FCFA allocated for Libreville's water supply under Ali Bongo reveals a significant gap between announced funds and actual expenditure.

Gabon Review's investigation into the water supply for Grand Libreville has failed to locate 400 billion FCFA reportedly allocated between 2009 and 2016. While major projects like the Ntoum-Libreville pipeline and the Ambowé-Agondjé conduit were documented with costs around 90 billion FCFA, the Ntoum 7 water treatment plant, contracted for 33 billion FCFA, faced interruptions. State accounts from 2015 show only 7.2 billion FCFA mobilized against a planned 81.2 billion FCFA for water and electricity sectors, with a significant portion of Ntoum 7 funding unreleased.

Further complicating the accounting is the PIAEPAL program, which officially lists 77 billion FCFA for its water component, including network upgrades and pump stations. Although the African Development Bank still considers it an ongoing project, the discrepancy between budgeted and disbursed funds, as seen in 2015, highlights a pattern of financial shortfalls. The total sum of 400 billion FCFA remains untraceable through documented market awards, announced credits, and actual disbursements.

The search for the 400 billion FCFA highlights a complex financial landscape involving numerous contracts, credits, and disbursements, making a clear accounting difficult. Despite substantial sums announced for infrastructure and program components, the evidence suggests that not all funds were spent as intended, leaving Libreville's water supply issues unresolved and the allocated billions unaccounted for.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the lack of transparency and potential mismanagement of significant public funds allocated to infrastructure projects, raising concerns about financial accountability.

ANINF Deploys Public Wi-Fi and Digital Services in Makokou

The National Agency for Digital Infrastructure and Frequencies has installed public Wi-Fi hotspots and a digital driver's license center in the provincial capital.

Makokou now has enhanced digital access with the recent deployment of public Wi-Fi by the Agence nationale des infrastructures numériques et des fréquences (ANINF). Two Wi-Fi zones have been established at the Place de l'Indépendance and the Alexandre Sambat stadium, aiming to boost internet access and digital administrative services. This initiative extends digital infrastructure beyond Libreville, supporting Makokou's development by providing youth, entrepreneurs, and job seekers with tools for information, training, and professional opportunities.

Beyond public Wi-Fi, ANINF has also set up a technical center for issuing digitized driver's licenses. This facility includes a local network, dedicated internet, and a secure electrical system with UPS and voltage regulators. The project aligns with President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema's vision for digital transformation, administrative modernization, and balanced territorial development, as advocated by ANINF Director General Alberto Wenceslas Mounguengui Moudoki.

This expansion of digital services in Makokou is seen as crucial for the city's current development phase, which is experiencing growth from various investments and projects. Improved connectivity is considered essential to support this transformation, making digital tools accessible to the wider population and enhancing daily life through easier access to information and basic services.

Why it mattersResidents of Makokou benefit from improved access to information, training, and online services through new public Wi-Fi and digital administrative centers.
