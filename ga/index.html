Gabon navy finds three bodies in Prospector 5 wreck

Two people remain missing after the Prospector 5 vessel sank, with recovery operations underway.

Gabon's navy located three bodies inside the sunken Prospector 5 shipwreck on Wednesday, October 28, 2026. The discovery occurred four days after the vessel sank, with two individuals still unaccounted for. Underwater inspections using a remote-operated vehicle (ROV) led to the finding of the bodies within the submerged wreck.

Vice-Admiral Charles Hubert Bekale-Meyong, Chief of Staff of the National Navy, announced the discovery. Identification of the bodies is pending further examination. Recovery operations are being prepared by technical teams with specialized personnel. A total of 110 people have been rescued since the shipwreck, with naval, air, and oil company resources mobilized.

An investigation into the circumstances of the disaster was opened on October 25, 2026, by the prosecutor of the Tchibanga Court of First Instance. The priority remains the recovery and identification of the three bodies and the continued search for the two missing persons. BW Energy Gabon SA is jointly conducting operations with the rescue command.

Why it mattersThe recovery and identification of three bodies from the Prospector 5 shipwreck and the ongoing search for two missing individuals are the immediate consequences.

Gabon votes to lift US embargo on Cuba

The UN General Assembly passed a resolution calling for the end of the US embargo on Cuba, with Gabon supporting the measure.

Gabon voted on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, at the United Nations General Assembly in New York to support the resolution demanding the lifting of the United States embargo against Cuba. This vote occurred amidst international tensions between Washington and Havana, with the resolution adopted by 130 votes in favour, 17 against, and 25 abstentions.

The resolution, presented by Cuba, urges other countries not to apply similar measures and denounces the extraterritorial effects of the 1996 Helms-Burton Act. This diplomatic action comes as Cuba faces severe economic and energy shortages, with electricity outages impacting essential services like transport, water distribution, and hospitals.

The vote reflects a declining international support for the resolution compared to previous years, with 165 votes in favour in 2025 and 187 in 2024. The US ambassador to the UN contested the existence of a blockade, while Havana accused Washington of economic strangulation. The adopted resolution remains non-binding.

Why it mattersGabon's vote at the UN General Assembly supports the lifting of the US embargo on Cuba, a measure that has led to severe economic and energy shortages on the island.

World Bank reviews Gabon's public finances

The financial institution presented its 2026 economic outlook to the CESEC on October 28, 2026.

The World Bank presented its 2026 economic outlook for Gabon on October 28, 2026, to officials and advisors of the Economic, Social, Environmental and Cultural Council (CESEC). The report details the country's public finances and economic prospects, highlighting the challenge of translating analysis into concrete reforms for diversified, productive, and job-creating growth.

Sylvain Kakou, the World Bank's resident representative, led the delegation that reviewed Gabon's public finances with CESEC members. This meeting renews the cooperation framework between Gabon and the World Bank, aligning with the Gabonese authorities' ambition to structurally transform the national economy as outlined in the 2026-2030 National Growth and Development Plan.

The review focused on six areas: the macroeconomic and budgetary context, public revenues, the quality and efficiency of state spending, and public finance management. It also examined revenues from extractive sectors like oil, mines, and forests, alongside public investment management, budgets, treasury, and debt, including social spending on education, health, and social protection.

Why it mattersGabon's public finances and economic prospects are being reviewed by the World Bank to guide reforms for stronger, more diversified, and job-creating growth.

Climate · 2026-10-29

Gabon hosts Congo Basin civil society for COP31 talks

Over 50 participants from six countries met in Libreville to forge a common stance on climate finance and local rights.

Libreville, Gabon, became the center for green diplomacy in the Congo Basin on October 28, 2026, as over 50 participants from six countries gathered for a two-day conference. Environmental civil society organizations from Cameroon, Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of Congo, and Gabon convened to build a unified position before COP31.

The conference, themed "A resilient Congo Basin: forests, populations, finances and nature," aims to ensure the region, the planet's second-largest green lung, arrives at COP31 united and coordinated. Key discussions focused on the role of local communities and indigenous peoples, with participants demanding effective decision-making participation and direct access to climate finance. Indigenous peoples presented nine demands, including formal recognition of their rights and reduced extractive industries on their territories.

Martial Dinang, executive secretary of Brainforest, emphasized that forest preservation should not compromise the rights of local populations, stressing the need to protect livelihoods and ensure their effective participation in decisions. Etienne Dourani Toko, representing local communities in Gabon, stated that nature cannot be preserved by marginalizing its guardians. Only 2.1% of climate funds reportedly reach forest guardians.

Why it mattersThe meeting in Libreville aims to ensure the Congo Basin's position is unified and coordinated for COP31, focusing on local community rights and climate finance.

Gabon police arrest three over failed explosive bank heist

Suspects accused of attempting to blow up a vehicle carrying Olam payroll funds near Bitam.

Three individuals have been arrested and detained in Gabon for allegedly attempting to rob a vehicle carrying payroll funds for Olam employees. The suspects are accused of using explosives in the failed heist on September 6, 2026, on the Bitam-Mongomo road in the Woleu-Ntem province.

The targeted vehicle belonged to Sodec, a subsidiary, and was transporting money for Olam-Batouri agents. The attackers reportedly placed an industrial explosive device the day before the incident. The blast damaged the vehicle, but its seven bank agents and one armed gendarme escaped unharmed.

The suspects face charges of attempted armed robbery with explosives and criminal association. Investigations by the Directorate General of Research (DGR) and local gendarmerie led to their arrest last week. They have been placed in detention at the Bitam Peloton pending trial.

Why it mattersThree individuals are facing legal proceedings in Gabon for an attempted explosive robbery targeting company payroll funds.
