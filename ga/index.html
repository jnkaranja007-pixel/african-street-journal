Gabon and China elevate ties to shared future community

President Nguema and President Xi Jinping signed a joint declaration in Beijing on November 6, 2026.

Gabon and China established a "shared future community" on November 6, 2026, elevating their diplomatic relationship during President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema's state visit to Beijing. The agreement, signed with President Xi Jinping at the Great Hall of the People, moves beyond the 2023 strategic partnership to focus on long-term economic development, sovereignty, and mutual interests.

China pledged to support Gabon's National Growth and Development Plan (PNCD) for 2026-2030, particularly in local resource transformation and infrastructure development. This commitment aims to help Gabon capture more value from its natural resources. Six memorandums of understanding were also signed, expanding cooperation into agriculture, digital economy, health, training, maritime security, and culture.

The two nations also plan to finalize an economic partnership agreement to sustain the duty-free access for Gabonese products to the Chinese market, effective since May 1, 2026. Modernizing the investment protection accord is another priority for both countries.

Why it mattersGabon's relationship with China has been upgraded, promising support for its national development plan and increased value capture from its resources.

Nina Abouna leads Gabon's international exhibition efforts

Nina Abouna, Gabon's General Commissioner for Exhibitions, guided the nation's participation in Osaka in 2025, earning a Golden Prize.

Nina Abouna, currently the General Commissioner for Exhibitions for Gabon, led the nation's delegation to the Osaka International Exhibition in 2025, which received a Golden Prize.

Abouna also presides over the Information and Communication Commission of the Bureau International des Expositions since November 2025, representing Gabon in this international body.

Her career includes roles in investment promotion and government, and she previously served as Director General of APIEX and ANPI-Gabon, where she contributed to establishing a one-stop shop for investment and digitizing the Commercial Register.

Why it mattersNina Abouna's work aims to increase Gabon's visibility and attractiveness through international exhibitions and investment promotion.

Gabon names university after pioneering cardiologist

The University of Health Sciences in Libreville will now be known as the Pierre André and Maryvonne Kombila Koumba University.

The University of Health Sciences (USS) in Libreville has been renamed the "Pierre André and Maryvonne Kombila Koumba University" to honour the late Professor Pierre-André Kombila Koumba, Gabon's first cardiologist and the first Cameroonian professor of cardiology. The ceremony on November 5, presided over by Gervais Oniane, personal representative of the President, also commemorated his wife, who was also a professor of medicine.

Professor Kombila Koumba, 85, died on October 15 from a stroke. The Health Minister, Elsa Nkana Joséphine Ayo-Bivigou, a former student, praised his scientific rigour and dedication to national cardiology, recalling his role in establishing the first cardiology department at the former General Hospital. He was posthumously awarded the National Order of Merit for his contributions.

Kombila Koumba also served as Minister of Technical Education and Vocational Training, advocating for it as a tool for youth development. The renaming decision was announced by Oniane on behalf of President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema, recognizing the couple's significant legacy in medicine and education.

Why it mattersGabon's health and education sectors are honouring a pioneering medical professional and his wife, who was also an academic.

Gabon Senate advances local powers transfer

Senators meet in Libreville to assess decentralisation progress and prepare for nationwide field missions.

Gabon's Senate is holding an information and exchange workshop in Libreville from November 5 to November 6 to review the decentralisation policy. The meeting, held at the Senate's provisional headquarters, brings together local elected officials and institutional actors to discuss the legal framework, implementation challenges, and future prospects of transferring powers to local authorities. This initiative aims to bring administration closer to citizens and empower local communities for territorial development.

The workshop's first day focused on the historical, political, and legal foundations of decentralisation, with academics tracing its evolution since 1990 and presenting key legal instruments. Difficulties in applying these laws were highlighted, alongside the issue of financing. The state plans to allocate between 5 and 10 percent of its budget to local authorities to ensure they have the necessary resources for their responsibilities and to improve local services.

The discussions are also preparing for a mission by senators to Gabon's interior to evaluate how local authorities are adopting the decentralisation policy. Medoux Yongui, deputy director of the Local Authority Equalisation Fund, addressed the perspectives for implementing the policy, particularly concerning the financial constraints faced by many local governments. The outcomes of these workshops are intended to enable local elected officials to better manage their territories.

Why it mattersGabon's local elected officials and citizens in territorial communities are directly affected by the progress and challenges in transferring powers and resources through the decentralisation policy.

Gabon plans new refinery to end fuel shortages

The government aims to boost storage and modernise refining to reduce reliance on imports.

Gabon's government is seeking financing for a new oil refinery to address recurring fuel shortages. The plan, discussed at an interministerial meeting on November 5, 2026, also includes strengthening fuel storage facilities and supporting the existing Sogara refinery. The current refinery, built in 1967, only meets about 30% of national demand, forcing the country to rely on imports.

Vice-president Hermann Immongault stated that the government wants to move from managing shortages to building a more resilient supply system. The existing Sogara refinery faces financial constraints related to price support mechanisms and its limited capacity. Short-term measures involve rehabilitating the Owendo fuel depot and increasing storage at SGPP.

The long-term solution involves building a new refinery designed for Gabonese crude to reduce dependence on foreign supplies. Financial partners have been sought for this project since 2024. The ultimate goal is to ensure regular availability of petrol and diesel for both households and businesses.

Why it mattersGabon's fuel supply chain is struggling with financial constraints and limited refining capacity, affecting the availability of fuel for households and businesses.
