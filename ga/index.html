Sunu Assurances Gabon promotes home insurance

The company presented home multi-risk and borrower death insurance products at the housing event.

Sunu Assurances Gabon showcased two insurance products, Multirisque habitation and Assurance décès emprunteur, at the housing open days event held from October 29-31, 2026. The company aims to make insurance a reflex for homeowners, protecting their investments against risks like fires, water damage, and material damages. The Multirisque habitation policy covers the dwelling, movable goods, and civil liability based on subscribed guarantees.

Lionel Vivien Mackaya, commercial director for Sunu Assurances IARD, stated that insurers should be involved in real estate projects from the outset to protect assets acquired through sacrifice. The Assurance décès emprunteur is designed for those with mortgage loans, covering the remaining debt in case of the borrower's death to prevent heirs from inheriting the financial burden. Martin Ondo Ovono, head of commercial department at Sunu Assurances Vie Gabon, explained this allows beneficiaries to keep the acquired home.

Sunu Assurances Gabon's participation aimed to raise public awareness about insurance mechanisms and their role in preserving family assets. The company emphasized that property ownership requires not only securing a loan but also implementing precautionary measures to safeguard the property and its financing.

Why it mattersHomeowners in Gabon are offered insurance products to protect their property and prevent families from inheriting debts in case of death.

Lee White criticises Gabon's election practices

Former minister Lee White, now active in Scottish politics, described Gabonese elections as requiring 'dancing to get elected'.

Former Gabon minister Pr Lee White stated on Facebook on October 31 that Gabonese elections require "dancing to get elected." White, who was Gabon's Minister of Water and Forests from 2019 to 2023 and a former PDG party candidate, made the comment while campaigning in Scotland.

White, who became a Gabonese citizen in 2008 and previously directed the National Parks Agency, is now involved in a new political venture in Scotland. He had previously run as a candidate for the PDG in July 2023 for a departmental councilor seat in Lopé. Following the August 30, 2023 coup, he was investigated for alleged public fund embezzlement and other charges.

Lee White left Gabon on October 7, 2023, for medical reasons with the new authorities' consent. He expressed a desire to return to Gabon as a scientist in February 2024. He has not yet appeared before Gabonese justice regarding the accusations.

Why it mattersGabon's political practices are being publicly critiqued by a former minister who is now pursuing a political career abroad.

Gabon defends inequality observatory at UN forum

Justice Minister Augustin Emane presented Gabon's plan to measure anti-racism policies at the 5th Global Forum in The Hague.

Gabon presented its strategy to combat racism and discrimination at the 5th Global Forum of the Unesco in The Hague on October 29. Justice Minister Augustin Emane detailed plans for an observatory of inequalities and a national action plan against hate speech. The minister stated that Gabon aims to move beyond declarations of principle to measurable results in public policies.

The observatory will audit structures, collect data, and assess inclusion policies, including a national survey to map socioeconomic disparities. This aims to improve access to citizenship, education, and justice for vulnerable and indigenous populations. The action plan includes prevention, sanctions, and the deconstruction of stereotypes in school programs.

Emane highlighted that Gabon's constitution guarantees cohesion among its diverse communities. He urged global leaders to make human diversity a foundation for a more just world. Gabon is committed to working alongside other nations in this endeavor.

Why it mattersGabon's justice minister presented national strategies to combat racism and inequality at an international forum, aiming for measurable policy outcomes.

Gabon begins resettlement for 100 families

Construction minister Lambert Mouvinde Millot launched work on a 15-hectare site in Akanda on October 31, 2026.

Gabon's Minister of Public Works and Construction, Lambert Mouvinde Millot, officially started the development of the Malibé 2 resettlement site in Akanda on October 31, 2026. The 15-hectare area will house 100 families displaced by road expansion projects in Okala, Lac Bleu, and Diba-Diba.

Mouvinde Millot visited the site to ensure work aligned with President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema's directives, confirming that earthmoving equipment and technical teams were already present. The project includes providing 100 serviced plots and essential community facilities like schools and clinics.

The government aims to balance infrastructure modernization with the needs of affected residents, with Malibé 2 serving as the initial phase of relocation. The plan also involves improving access roads and internal circulation within the new settlement.

Why it matters100 families in Gabon will be rehoused due to road construction projects.
