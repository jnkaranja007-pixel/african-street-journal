Teenager receives prison sentence for mother's involuntary homicide in Franceville

A 13-year-old girl, G.M., was sentenced to 12 months in prison, with seven months suspended, and a three million CFA franc fine for the involuntary homicide of her mother, Esther.

The Franceville Court of First Instance, sitting as a criminal court for children, convicted G.M. on August 19 for the death of her mother, which occurred on June 13, 2024, in Okondja. G.M. admitted to putting camphor in her mother's drink to make her sleep, intending to go out at night without parental supervision. Her mother, Esther Diki Nzoghet, fell ill after consuming the drink and later died at the city's medical center.

Investigations by the local gendarmerie brigade quickly focused on G.M., who remained consistent in her statements throughout the preliminary inquiry, instruction, and hearing. She confessed to the act, stating she no longer tolerated her mother's reprimands regarding her nighttime outings and romantic relationship. The court considered her young age and the absence of intent to kill, leading to a conviction for involuntary homicide rather than poisoning.

G.M. was placed in pre-trial detention at Franceville central prison on June 21, 2024, where she spent approximately six months before being granted provisional release. Her lawyer, Maitre Yoni Andy Nkum’Enguie, acknowledged her guilt and advocated for a suspended sentence, while the public prosecutor requested leniency due to her age.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects the family of Esther Diki Nzoghet and G.M., who are dealing with a tragic loss and a legal conviction.

Immongault advances forest and 'Gabon Infini' initiatives

Vice President Hermann Immongault led two working sessions to finalize funding for ecosystem preservation and address challenges in the timber sector.

Gabon's Vice President, Hermann Immongault, chaired two working sessions on Monday, August twenty-fourth, focusing on recommendations for the "Gabon Infini" financing project and issues within the timber industry. The government aims to finalize an agreement with funders and boost the competitiveness of Gabonese forest products in international markets.

The first session addressed recommendations from the sixth steering committee meeting for the "Gabon Infini" performance financing project. This project supports Gabon's commitment to preserve and enhance 30% of its coastal, terrestrial, and marine ecosystems, backed by a 200 million dollar fund, partly financed by the Gabonese state.

The second session focused on presidential directives for the timber sector, following a recent meeting between the President and industry operators. Immongault stated the goal is to restore sector competitiveness, enhance national resource value, save jobs, increase economic contribution, and establish the "Bois du Gabon" brand for quality.

Why it mattersGabonese timber operators and environmental stakeholders are directly affected by these government initiatives to restructure the forest industry and secure conservation funding.

US Judge Invalidates Visa Suspension for 75 Nations, Including Gabon

A New York federal judge on Friday, August 21, 2026, invalidated the Trump administration's decision to suspend immigration visa processing for citizens of 75 countries.

The ruling by Judge Jeannette Vargas halts a key part of the White House's migration policy, finding that the executive branch overstepped its authority by targeting applicants based on financial criteria. This decision affects individuals seeking to settle permanently in the United States, including those from countries like Nigeria, Afghanistan, Somalia, Brazil, and Iran.

The US administration had cited financial risk as the basis for the policy, considering some immigration candidates likely to depend on social benefits or burden public finances. Consular services, including the US Embassy in Libreville, were instructed to suspend procedures, even for applicants who met other requirements, forcing Gabonese applicants to travel to third countries like Equatorial Guinea or DR Congo.

The New York judge's decision means that visa refusals based solely on this policy can no longer be maintained. This judgment represents another setback for Donald Trump's efforts to reduce immigration to the United States, as his administration's policies frequently face challenges and suspensions in the courts.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens seeking to immigrate to the United States will no longer be denied visas solely based on the invalidated policy, potentially allowing the US Embassy in Libreville to resume visa issuance.

Four unions form new federation for extractive and energy workers

The Force ouvrière des industries extractives et énergétiques (FOIEE) was established in Libreville to represent workers in Gabon's oil, gas, water, electricity, and mining.

Four major trade unions in Gabon's extractive and energy sectors ratified the statutes of a new federation, the Force ouvrière des industries extractives et énergétiques (FOIEE), on August twenty-third, two thousand twenty-six, in Libreville. This new organization aims to consolidate the representation of workers in these strategic industries.

The founding members include ONEP for oil and gas, SYNTEE+ of SEEG for water and electricity, and SYLTRAC and SYNATIEX for mining workers at COMILOG. Sylvain Mayabi Binet was named Secretary General, leading an eight-member federal executive bureau. The federation intends to leverage the strong representation of its founding unions from recent professional elections.

According to Secretary General Sylvain Mayabi Binet, the election results confirm the influence of these organizations within the extractive, energy, and mining industries. This positions the FOIEE as a significant participant in social dialogue across these sectors, affecting thousands of workers in Gabon.

Why it mattersThis new federation will directly impact the working conditions, wages, and social protections of employees in Gabon's key extractive and energy industries.

Former Prosecutor Eddy Narcisse Minang removed from magistracy

Eddy Narcisse Minang, who led the Bongo trial in November 2025, has been struck from the judicial register due to alleged embezzlement and judicial interference.

Eddy Narcisse Minang, the former Attorney General and lead prosecutor in the high-profile Bongo trial, was removed from the magistracy this week. This decision follows accusations of embezzlement in a case involving a Chinese businessman and alleged judicial interferences, including in the Ministry of National Education's treasury bond affair, according to a source close to the Ministry of Justice.

Minang was among ten magistrates summoned by a disciplinary council on Tuesday but did not appear, leading to the decision being made without his testimony. He had been previously suspended from his duties by the Minister of Justice on June 9, 2026, over similar allegations. His relatives claim these accusations are a political maneuver linked to the Bongo trial.

During the Bongo trial, which was broadcast live on national television, Minang sought twenty years of criminal imprisonment for massive embezzlement of public funds against Sylvia and Noureddin Bongo. This sentence was subsequently pronounced in absentia against the mother and son duo.

Why it mattersThis event affects citizens by demonstrating the government's stance on judicial accountability, particularly for high-profile figures involved in corruption cases.
