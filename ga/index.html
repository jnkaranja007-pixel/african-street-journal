Gabon Forum Mobilizes Savings for National Development

The National Savings for Development Forum in Libreville aims to transform national and diaspora savings into long-term financing for economic growth.

Libreville will host the inaugural National Savings for Development Forum (FONED) on September 2 and 3, 2026. Organized by the Caisse des dépôts et consignations du Gabon (CDC Gabon), the event seeks to leverage national and diaspora savings as concrete tools for financing Gabon's development and financial sovereignty. This initiative aligns with the National Growth and Development Plan (PNCD) 2026-2030, which requires twenty-seven thousand billion FCFA in investments, with eighteen thousand billion FCFA expected from the private sector and national resources.

The forum will bring together public authorities, financial institutions, investors, the private sector, experts, and diaspora representatives to discuss transforming savings into long-term financing. Discussions will cover domestic resource mobilization, endogenous financing of the PNCD, productive investment, diaspora savings, and sectors like housing, infrastructure, agriculture, energy, digitalization, and financial inclusion. A study on diaspora savings and development will also be presented.

CDC Gabon aims to solidify its role in mobilizing and securing savings, transforming them into long-term financing and investments aligned with national priorities. The FONED is intended to permanently place savings at the center of national economic debate and foster innovative, sovereign financing models adapted to African development challenges, drawing on continental experiences like that of Morocco's Caisse de Dépôt et Gestion.

Why it mattersThe FONED event directly affects investors by seeking to mobilize national and diaspora savings for long-term investments within Gabon's development plan.

Kelly Ondo Obiang Honors Victims, Launches Memoir on Resilience

Ten years after post-election violence, Kelly Ondo Obiang marks the anniversary with a tribute and the release of his autobiographical essay.

Kelly Ondo Obiang paid tribute on August 31, 2026, to victims and families affected by Gabon's 2016 post-election violence, marking the tenth anniversary of the crisis. One year after his release from Libreville Central Prison under an amnesty law, he has dedicated himself to personal reconstruction. He announced the upcoming publication of his autobiographical essay, "L’Espoir doit mourir en dernier" (Hope Must Die Last), which aims to share his experience and reflect on resilience, justice, and rebuilding.

The essay, published by Éditions Filiga, is presented as a method for transforming hardship into leverage and injustice into a demand for change. Ondo Obiang stated, "I am not publishing to recount my pain. I am publishing to offer a method: that of transforming the ordeal into leverage, injustice into demand, and survival into a mission." The book, intended for African youth, seeks to contribute to discussions on overcoming adversity and reclaiming dignity.

The August 31st commemoration also served as a moment for personal reflection, a year after his release. Ondo Obiang described the past twelve months not as a pause but as "labor," dedicated to survival, reconstruction, and regaining his dignity as a citizen. The book's publication is positioned as a contribution to understanding the consequences of adversity and the inner strength required to move forward, even after profound challenges.

Why it mattersKelly Ondo Obiang's memoir offers a personal reflection on resilience and justice, potentially inspiring those who have faced adversity.

Gabon Debt Climbs to 94% of GDP Amidst Ambitious Spending

The International Monetary Fund projects Gabon's public debt to reach 94.3% of GDP by 2027 as infrastructure projects continue.

Gabon's public debt is projected to reach eighty-six point one percent of GDP in 2026 and ninety-four point three percent in 2027, according to the IMF. This surge follows three years of extensive construction across infrastructure sectors, including roads, buildings, and utilities. The nation faces a deficit nearing ten percent of GDP and recently secured a new bond issue of nine hundred twenty million dollars.

The government's initial budget for 2026 proposed significant investment spending, but this was later reduced due to financing realities and project readiness. Investment credits were cut from over three thousand three hundred billion FCFA to around one thousand one hundred sixty-nine billion FCFA in subsequent revisions. This adjustment reflects a recalibration of ambitious initial plans to align with available funding and project maturity.

Despite scaled-back investment figures, Gabon continues to seek capital on international markets. In late July, the country issued bonds worth nine hundred twenty million dollars, maturing in 2033, with an interest rate of nine point three seven five percent. This borrowing comes as the country grapples with financing its development agenda and managing its growing debt burden.

Why it mattersGabon's government is increasing its debt to fund infrastructure projects, potentially impacting future fiscal stability.

Senate Opens Session, Cites Population Needs

Gabon's Senate convened its second ordinary session, with the chamber's president urging focus on citizens' social and economic expectations.

Gabon's Senate commenced its second ordinary session of the year on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, at its temporary headquarters. Presided over by Huguette Yvonne Nyana-Ekoume Awori Onanga, the session's priorities include addressing the social expectations of the populace. The Senate president called for collective, responsible action in service of citizens, highlighting youth, employment, economic diversification, social justice, and good governance as key discussion points.

The Senate president emphasized that numerous expectations remain across various sectors and urged the government to prioritize socio-economic urgencies during the upcoming budget law examination. She stressed the need for particular attention to rehabilitating basic infrastructure, improving living conditions, and ensuring access to essential public services. The session also acknowledged the success of the National Liberation Day celebrations and paid tribute to victims of a recent tragic accident.

During the opening ceremony, attended by the Vice President of the Republic and a regional representative from Mali's Parliament, the Senate president set a tone of focused work and dialogue. She called on senators to evaluate legislation based on its impact on Gabonese citizens and to make the Senate a proactive force for territorial development, emphasizing rigor, openness, and the general interest.

Why it mattersGabonese citizens are affected as the Senate prioritizes their social and economic expectations, including employment, infrastructure, and essential services.

President wears military uniform at liberation celebration

Brice-Clotaire Oligui Nguéma's attire at the 3rd Liberation Day festival raises questions about the separation of military and political power.

President Brice-Clotaire Oligui Nguéma appeared in military uniform, complete with insignia, during the 3rd Liberation Day festival in Makokou. This choice of dress, despite his current role as head of state and supreme commander of defense and security forces, has sparked debate. Although no longer holding a rank within the military hierarchy, his attire evoked his past as a general, leading to discussions about the symbolic representation of power and the distinction between civilian and military institutions.

The president's office had previously stated that an elected president would permanently step down from military service. Nguéma's decision to wear military regalia has prompted interpretations ranging from a tribute to former comrades-in-arms to a potential blurring of lines between the army and politics. This visual choice raises questions about the nature of the post-transition regime and whether the image of a "general-president" should persist.

Separately, a police brigadier named Christian Rodrigue Assogo posted a video pleading for help from President Nguéma and his wife. Assogo, who is paralyzed and suffering from infected wounds, claims his salary and August 17th bonus were cut, leaving him unable to afford medical treatment. He stated that his superiors stopped his pay in July, despite his continued service and deteriorating health.

Why it mattersThe president's attire and a police officer's plea for aid highlight concerns about the relationship between military and political authority and the welfare of security personnel.
