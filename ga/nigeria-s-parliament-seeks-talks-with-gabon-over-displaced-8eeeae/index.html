Nigeria's parliament seeks talks with Gabon over displaced fishers

Its motion asks the federal government to consult Libreville, while four commissions investigate the living conditions of people displaced by demolitions.

Source: Gabon Review · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Nigeria’s House of Representatives adopted an emergency motion on 17 November calling for consultations with Gabon over demolitions of installations occupied by Nigerian nationals in Greater Libreville’s coastal areas and mangroves. The motion was presented by lawmaker Donald Ojogo and focuses in particular on Ilaje fishers from Ondo state, who have lived in Gabon for decades.

The lawmakers said the operations had caused forced displacement and harassment. They asked the federal government, through its foreign ministry and embassy in Libreville, to engage Gabonese authorities. Four parliamentary commissions were also tasked with investigating displaced people’s living conditions and reporting within 14 legislative days.

The demolitions followed security operations in Kinguélé, along the shore and in Soduco-Mindoubé. Gabon’s interior minister, Edgard Moukoumbi, said the operations were based on enforcing the law and protecting national sovereignty, and did not target any nationality.
