Gabon Explores Electronic Monitoring to Ease Prison Overcrowding

Gabon's Justice Minister visited Senegal to study the electronic bracelet system as a way to reduce prison populations.

Source: Gabon Review · 2026-10-20 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X

Gabon's Minister of Justice, Augustin Emane, is in Dakar this week to examine Senegal's use of electronic monitoring as an alternative to incarceration. The visit, from October 18 to 21, focuses on understanding the mechanisms of the electronic bracelet system, which Senegal employs to combat prison overcrowding and improve detention conditions. Emane, accompanied by technical security officials, met with his Senegalese counterpart, Me Moussa Saar, to discuss judicial cooperation and the implementation of electronic surveillance.

The Gabonese delegation aims to gather technical and practical data to adapt Senegal's model to Gabon's judicial system. This initiative aligns with Gabon's high authorities' priority to improve the conditions of detained individuals. Beyond alleviating prison congestion, the electronic bracelet system could modernize sentence execution, diversify judicial responses, and potentially generate new state revenue, according to a ministerial note.

Senegal's electronic surveillance system, supported by companies like GEOSATIS, offers Gabon a practical framework for implementation. The goal is to provide a credible alternative to imprisonment for certain defendants or convicts, allowing them to serve sentences under strict supervision outside of correctional facilities. This move signifies an effort to humanize justice and enhance the effectiveness of criminal penalties in Gabon.

Why it mattersGabon is exploring the electronic bracelet system to reduce prison overcrowding and modernize its justice system.
