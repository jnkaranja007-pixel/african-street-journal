Gabon spends 35 billion CFA on fuel subsidies

The government plans adjustments to fuel prices in the coming months, impacting businesses.

Source: Gabon Review · 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

The Gabonese state spent 35 billion CFA francs in November to maintain fuel prices, with further adjustments expected soon. While consumers focus on pump prices, the move will increase costs for transport operators, subcontractors, and small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) without allowing them to raise their own prices.

Ike Ngouoni Aila Oyouomi, president of AILA, questions the cost of these reforms for businesses, highlighting that while the state anticipates savings, companies may absorb increased expenses. Fuel subsidies cost Gabon 1% of its GDP in 2024, with 96% benefiting the wealthiest half of the population, according to the World Bank. The price of diesel at 575 CFA per litre makes Gabon the cheapest in the CEMAC region, a difference covered by the state budget.

Businesses that face higher fuel costs see their operating results decrease, reducing the profit on which they pay taxes. This indirect subsidy means the state recovers a fraction of the increased cost through lower tax revenue. If rising prices hinder activity, they can affect employment, consumption, and ultimately state revenue. Industrial companies often source fuel outside the network at different rates, while transport and service providers, along with SMEs, typically operate on fixed prices, particularly in cities like Port-Gentil.
