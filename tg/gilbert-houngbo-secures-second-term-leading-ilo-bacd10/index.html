Gilbert Houngbo secures second term leading ILO

The former prime minister won 51 votes after his sole challenger withdrew from the race.

Sources: 24heureinfo, Icilome and Jeune Afrique · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Gilbert Fossoun Houngbo, a former prime minister, will continue leading the International Labour Organization (ILO) after the governing body confirmed his reappointment on Monday, 16 November 2026. He secured 51 votes from the 58 eligible members on the board, which includes representatives from governments, workers, and employers. His new five-year term will officially begin on 1 October 2027 and run until 2032, following his initial election in 2022.

Yolanda Diaz, Spain's labor minister and second vice-president, withdrew her opposing candidacy shortly before the vote to preserve international union unity. Although she stepped down, Diaz criticized current management practices, alleging the institution experienced a deep crisis affecting social dialogue and workers' rights. She thanked several African and European countries, the Brazilian government, and Spanish unions for their support.

Following his re-election, the ILO director-general intends to focus on artificial intelligence as a central element for global social justice. 58 board members were authorized to participate in the electoral process, which concluded following multiple candidate withdrawals.

Why it mattersThe ILO director-general starts his new five-year term on 1 October 2027, following a vote of 51 out of 58 eligible board members.
