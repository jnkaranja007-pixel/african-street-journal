Togo launches maritime crime money hunt

National workshop aims to identify threats and strengthen coordination against illicit maritime finance.

Togo is hosting a national workshop until Friday to track illicit funds linked to maritime crime. The event will assess risks, identify threats, and enhance coordination among institutions. Recommendations will be formulated to combat financial crime at sea, supported by new legislation adopted in March 2026.

The workshop is organized under the auspices of the National Financial Information Processing Unit, with support from GIABA and the African Development Bank. This initiative follows the interception of Togolese-flagged vessels carrying significant cocaine loads, including the Blume and Orion V in 2023, and a fishing boat in the South Pacific in January 2026.

The port of Lomé is considered the economic engine of the nation, but questions remain about the transparency of its revenues, concessions, and contracts. The article suggests that an audit, rather than a workshop, is needed for port administration that lacks clear public accounting of its financial flows.

Why it mattersTogo's government is holding a workshop to combat financial crime related to maritime activities.

Togo roads crumble, hindering hospital access

Mud and giant potholes plague the Amégnran-Afagnan and Afagnan-Agomé-Glozou routes in Bas-Mono.

Two key roads in Togo's Bas-Mono prefecture, Amégnran-Afagnan and Afagnan-Agomé-Glozou, are in advanced states of disrepair. Mud, giant potholes, and missing asphalt make travel extremely difficult, especially during the rainy season.

The deteriorating condition of these routes significantly impacts local populations and complicates access to the Saint-Jean-de-Dieu hospital in Afagnan. The MMLK group has called on the government for urgent rehabilitation of these roads.

The poor state of the roads hinders the transport of agricultural products from Bas-Mono, causing losses for producers and transporters. Ambulances and other vehicles carrying patients also face severe difficulties reaching the Afagnan hospital.

Why it mattersResidents of Bas-Mono prefecture face difficulties accessing essential services like healthcare and transporting agricultural goods due to the poor condition of local roads.

Togo's development illusions persist amid trust deficit

A tribune argues that imported development models fail without citizen trust in institutions.

Development models and machines can be imported, but not the trust between citizens and institutions, according to an opinion piece published by Icilome. The author suggests that despite President Faure's global travels, development in Togo risks remaining an illusion due to this fundamental lack of trust.

The piece recounts an incident where a man, a victim of violence by four individuals, was presented as the offender in an official report. A captain offered a second report stating the aggressors were at fault, but after the victim signed the first report, the captain declared the procedure closed, leaving the victim feeling deceived.

This experience, the author claims, erodes trust in institutions by making citizens feel trapped rather than seeking truth and accountability. Such interactions foster doubt, frustration, and resentment, hindering sustainable development projects that rely on institutional credibility.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are affected by a lack of trust in their institutions, which hinders development.

Togo association holds mock trial on abortion

The Iyawo association used a simulated court case on October 23, 2026, in Lomé to discuss reproductive rights.

The Iyawo association organized a mock trial in Lomé on October 23, 2026, to address the taboos surrounding unwanted pregnancies and abortion in Togo. The event aimed to educate young people about their rights and highlight the dangers of clandestine abortions, despite a legal framework that permits abortion under certain conditions.

Elisabeth Apampa, president of Iyawo, stated that many taboos and silences surround these issues, necessitating a direct approach to inform youth about legal mechanisms. Dr. Abram Agossou, a pediatrician and public health specialist, noted that healthcare professionals face obstacles in implementing legal procedures for pregnancy termination, even when cases involve rape, incest, or risks to the woman's life.

Togo's 2007 law on reproductive health allows for pregnancy interruption in specific circumstances, yet implementation challenges persist. The mock trial sought to bridge the gap between legal provisions and their practical application, confronting participants with the realities faced by women and girls seeking safe reproductive healthcare.

Why it mattersWomen and girls in Togo face difficulties accessing safe abortion services due to legal and practical obstacles.

Togo scraps teaching aptitude exams

A new ministerial order ends the Certificate of Pedagogical Aptitude exams for teachers in Togo.

Togo has abolished the Certificate of Pedagogical Aptitude (CAP) exams for teachers. Minister of National Education Mama Omorou signed an order on October 23, 2026, cancelling the exams that were established in 1978.

The reform includes transitional measures for some educators. Holders of the elementary CAP for the first degree can still take the first-degree CAP exam. Secondary school teachers in specific subjects like art, physical education, home economics, and music can also apply if they hold a diploma equivalent to the second-degree CAP or the BAC 2.

The new order takes effect immediately upon its publication in the Official Gazette. The secretary-general of the ministry is responsible for its implementation.

Why it mattersThe Certificate of Pedagogical Aptitude exams for teachers in Togo have been abolished.
