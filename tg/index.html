Togo university hosts rights court debate

Lomé conference examines regional human rights courts and state sovereignty after constitutional change.

The University of Lomé hosted an international conference on October 29 and 30, 2026, discussing regional human rights courts, the rule of law, constitutional changes, and state sovereignty. The event followed a January 29, 2026, ruling by the ECOWAS Court of Justice.

Professor Pascal Kossivi Adjamagbo stated that Togolese sovereignty belongs to the people and cannot be seized by rulers in the name of state sovereignty. This discussion is particularly relevant following Togo's constitutional reform on March 25, 2024, which moved the country to its Fifth Republic.

The conference, organized by the Public Law Center at the University of Lomé, focused on the tension between popular sovereignty and the new institutional framework established by the recent constitutional reform.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are affected by the debate over who holds ultimate sovereignty following a constitutional reform.

Togo cyber unit warns of fake job scam

CERT.TG alerts citizens to social media recruitment drive using fake offers and suspicious links.

Togo's national cybersecurity incident response center, CERT.TG, has issued a warning about a fraudulent recruitment campaign circulating on social media. The scam, which impersonates the PRO-REPEM project, targets job seekers with fake offers and malicious links designed to steal personal data.

CERT.TG is urging the public to exercise caution and avoid clicking on suspicious links or sharing personal information. The center advises citizens to verify recruitment announcements with official sources before taking any action to prevent falling victim to cybercriminals.

This alert aims to protect individuals from falling prey to online scams that exploit the job search process. The CERT.TG recommends vigilance to safeguard personal data from unauthorized collection.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens seeking employment are at risk of having their personal data stolen through a fake recruitment campaign.

Togo consumers reject higher utility tariffs

The LCT demands a halt to potential price hikes for water and electricity amid frequent power cuts.

The League of Consumers of Togo (LCT) is protesting against recurring electricity outages and the possibility of increased water and electricity tariffs. The LCT is calling for a suspension of any price hikes and for national consultations to address these essential services.

Frequent power interruptions are damaging appliances and causing losses for households and businesses, according to the LCT. These cuts also increase expenses for generators and disrupt essential services like health centers and schools, while consumers are not compensated for the service failures.

The LCT recalls that a 12.5% tariff increase in March 2025 was partially suspended after public outcry. They insist that any future price adjustments must involve genuine consultation with all stakeholders, especially as the cost of living continues to rise.

Why it mattersTogolese consumers face deteriorating conditions due to frequent power cuts and potential price increases for essential services like water and electricity.

Togo group seeks delay for street clearing

The MMLK movement asks the government to postpone the operation until January 31, 2027.

The Mouvement Martin Luther King (MMLK), led by Pastor Edoh Komi, has asked the Togolese government to delay the clearing of public roadways until January 31, 2027. The operation was originally scheduled for November 3 to November 20, 2026.

In a statement on November 3, the MMLK acknowledged the need to clear public spaces for safety but warned that a hasty implementation could harm small businesses during the holiday season. The group also cited high commercial rents and excessive deposit demands as reasons for street occupation.

The MMLK advocates for the clearing to be accompanied by social measures and dialogue with affected populations, proposing a gradual and socially responsible transition. They stated, "Clearing, yes. But not in haste and not 45 days before the end-of-year holidays."

Why it mattersSmall business owners in Lomé face disruption to their livelihoods due to a planned street clearing operation.
