Ex-Minister Held Nearly a Year Awaiting Trial

Former Togolese Army Minister Marguerite Gnakadé faces charges of inciting uprising and remains in detention since September 2025.

Former Togolese Army Minister Marguerite Gnakadé has been detained for nearly a year since her arrest on September 17, 2025. She was taken from her Lomé home following public statements critical of the government. Four days later, she was formally charged and placed under arrest.

The justice system accuses Gnakadé of "incitement to uprising against authority." Her trial date has not yet been set, leaving her in prolonged pre-trial detention. The circumstances and conditions of her confinement are currently unknown.

Gnakadé previously served as the Army Minister under President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé. Her arrest and continued detention come after she made several public statements opposing the current administration.

Opposition Parties Announce Public Conference on Sociopolitical Situation

Four opposition parties, united under the CNCC, will hold a public conference on September 4 to discuss the country's sociopolitical landscape.

The National Framework for Concertation for Change (CNCC), comprising the ANC, ADDI, FDR, and PSR parties, is organizing a public conference on September 4 in Lomé. The event, scheduled to begin at 8:30 AM at the MUGET conference hall in Djidjolé, will focus on the nation's sociopolitical situation. The CNCC formally notified the Ministry of Territorial Administration, Local Governance, and Customary Affairs of their plans via a letter submitted on August 31, 2026.

This gathering follows a recent formalization of synergy between the two main opposition groups, marked by the release of a joint memorandum on August 25 proposing a resolution to the political crisis. The momentum for dialogue has been further bolstered by a ruling from the ECOWAS Court of Justice, which invalidated the constitutional revision adopted in Togo in 2024.

The CNCC aims to utilize this platform to present their analysis of current events and to continue mobilizing support around institutional issues that have divided the Togolese political class for several months. The conference serves as a key opportunity for opposition voices to articulate their perspectives and strategies.

Why it mattersOpposition parties are holding a public conference to discuss the sociopolitical situation and institutional issues dividing the political class.

SYNJIT Marks 15 Years of Media Unionism

Togo's independent journalists' union celebrated its crystal anniversary, reaffirming its commitment to professional advocacy and the media landscape.

The Independent Journalists' Union of Togo (SYNJIT) marked its 15th anniversary on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, at the Ministry of Communication and Media. The event celebrated 15 years of union struggle and commitment within Togo's media sector. The gathering, themed "At the heart of challenges: re-engagement, solidarity, mobilization for a strong SYNJIT," was presided over by Ambroise Klevor Yao Togbe, technical advisor to the Minister of Communication.

The ceremony recognized the union's enduring presence and its role in advocating for professional interests and contributing to national discourse on press freedom, democracy, and development. Attendees included prominent figures from the Togolese media landscape, such as Fabrice Pétchézi, President of OTM, and Aimé Ekpé, Director of the House of the Press, alongside union members and independent journalists.

SYNJIT used the occasion to reaffirm its dedication to the Togolese media environment, highlighting the significance of 15 years of professional engagement. The union views this crystal jubilee as a symbol of recognition, unity, and renewal, underscoring its capacity to sustain its actions and champion the cause of journalists.

Why it mattersThe independent journalists' union in Togo celebrated its 15th anniversary, signifying ongoing professional advocacy and engagement within the country's media sector.

Togo Institute Halts Key Viral Load Tests

The National Hygiene Institute faces technical issues, temporarily suspending crucial tests for hepatitis B, C, HIV, and HPV.

The National Hygiene Institute (INH) in Togo is experiencing a technical interruption that has temporarily halted several viral load analyses. The affected tests include those for hepatitis B, hepatitis C, HIV, and human papillomavirus (HPV). These examinations are vital for managing infected individuals, allowing for monitoring of viral presence, progression, and response to treatment.

According to the INH, technical measures are currently being implemented to restore these services. However, the institute has not provided a specific timeline for when the analyses will resume. An official communication is expected once normal operations are re-established.

The suspension impacts the ability to accurately track the status of viral infections, potentially affecting treatment efficacy and patient management. The INH is working to resolve the technical issues and resume its diagnostic capabilities as soon as possible.

Why it mattersPeople infected with hepatitis B, C, HIV, and HPV in Togo are affected because crucial viral load tests needed for monitoring their conditions and treatment responses are temporarily unavailable.

Togo, Belarus Expand Trade in Agriculture and Mining

Togo and Belarus have agreed to deepen cooperation in food security, agribusiness, and mining sectors, fostering private sector partnerships.

Togo and Belarus are set to enhance their partnership, focusing on key economic sectors including food security, agribusiness, agricultural mechanization, and mining. The agreement follows a meeting between Togolese President Faure Gnassingbé and Belarusian President Alexander Lukashenko in Kyrgyzstan on September 1, 2026. Both leaders expressed commitment to strengthening bilateral ties and implementing agreed-upon roadmaps.

Belarusian President Lukashenko highlighted his country's readiness to support Togo's development projects, particularly in agriculture and transport, and to facilitate partnerships between the private sectors of both nations. President Gnassingbé, in turn, presented Togo's investment opportunities, emphasizing the agricultural sector's potential and the country's strategic aim to diversify partnerships and increase Eurasian market access.

This expanded cooperation aims to drive investment, promote local processing, and add value to Togo's national economy. The discussions also touched upon the banking sector, signaling a comprehensive approach to bilateral economic engagement. The meeting underscores Togo's strategy to build robust international collaborations for economic growth.

Why it mattersTogo and Belarus are expanding cooperation in agriculture and mining, which could lead to new investment and trade opportunities for businesses in both countries.
