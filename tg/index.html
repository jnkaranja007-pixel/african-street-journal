UN Votes to Adopt New Map Reflecting Africa's True Size

The UN General Assembly has voted to replace the traditional Mercator map with one that more accurately reflects the size of Africa.

The United Nations General Assembly has voted to adopt a new world map that more accurately represents the size of Africa, a move championed by Togo and supported by African Union members. The resolution, passed with 164 nations voting in favor, aims to correct the long-standing inaccuracies of the Mercator projection, which has historically minimized the perceived size of equatorial regions.

The Mercator map, in use since the 16th century, has been criticized for distorting the relative sizes of continents, notably showing Africa as only slightly larger than Greenland, despite being 14 times its size. This misrepresentation has been linked to the downplaying of development needs and potential in countries located near the equator. The UN vote, though non-binding, is expected to influence map usage globally.

The "Correct the Map" resolution saw opposition only from the United States, which voted against the measure, while six nations abstained. The Mercator projection was originally created by Gerardus Mercator in 1569 to aid European explorers in navigation. Critics argue that its continued widespread use perpetuates a skewed global perspective.

Why it mattersThe UN vote to adopt a new map affects all nations by correcting the historical misrepresentation of Africa's size, which critics say minimized its development needs and potential.

Togo Launches Household Survey to Update Socioeconomic Data

The National Institute of Statistics will collect data on income, employment, education, and health across the country until December.

The National Institute of Statistics and Economic and Demographic Studies (INSEED) will begin a new data collection on household living conditions nationwide on September 7, 2026. This operation, scheduled to run until December 2026, is part of the third Harmonized Survey on Household Living Conditions (EHCVM). The initiative is supported by the West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA) and the World Bank.

Mobilized teams will gather information on household incomes and expenditures, employment status, educational attainment, and health indicators. The collected data aims to provide a more accurate measure of the socioeconomic realities faced by Togolese populations. This effort is crucial for understanding the current living standards and identifying areas for improvement.

The expected results will update national poverty statistics and equip public authorities with reliable elements to better target and direct development policies and programs. This updated information is vital for evidence-based decision-making and the effective allocation of resources to address development challenges.

Why it mattersThe survey will provide updated socioeconomic data that can inform development policies and programs, potentially affecting resource allocation and public services.

Jurists Debate ECOWAS Court Ruling on Togo's Constitution

Legal experts and political figures gathered in Lomé to analyze the ECOWAS Court's decision on Togo's 2024 constitutional reform.

The National Coordination Framework for Change (CNCC) hosted a public conference in Lomé on Friday to discuss the ECOWAS Court of Justice ruling concerning Togo's 2024 constitutional reform. The event featured legal analyses and discussions on the political implications of the court's decision, with participation from academics and legal professionals from across West Africa.

Presentations focused on the legal interpretation of the ruling, with contributions from public law professor Martial Zongo and private law professor Komi Wolou. Additionally, Mamadou Ismaïla Konaté, a lawyer and former Malian Minister of Justice, offered insights. The political dimensions were explored by journalist Armando Lona, former Beninese Justice Minister Victor Prudent Topanou, and civil society actor Ekoué David Dosseh.

Professor Komi Wolou stated that the court found the Togolese government's constitutional modifications to be unconstitutional, referencing the African Charter on Democracy, Elections and Governance. However, Me Ismaïla Konaté noted a potential discrepancy between the ruling's reasoning and its operative part, suggesting a need for the court to reconsider its decision. The conference concluded with a call for citizen mobilization to ensure the ruling's effective implementation.

Why it mattersLegal professionals and political actors are affected by the ECOWAS Court's ruling on Togo's constitutional reform, which could impact governance and legal precedents in the region.

Police Arrest Suspect in Togo Motorcycle Theft Attempt

A 20-year-old man is in custody after a foiled motorcycle robbery in Lankouvi, with two accomplices still at large.

Togolese national police arrested a 20-year-old man in connection with an attempted motorcycle theft on August 26 in Lankouvi. The suspect, identified as D. Sodjiné, a mason residing in Ghana, was apprehended on the same day in the Awatamé neighborhood. Two other alleged accomplices remain sought by authorities after fleeing the scene toward Ghana.

The incident occurred around 3:12 AM when three individuals, armed with a machete, attempted to steal a BAJAJ 125 BM motorcycle from its owner, A. Kodjo, a carpenter. The victim was saved by the timely arrival of a police patrol unit, prompting the assailants to abandon the motorcycle and flee. Officers recovered a mobile phone dropped by the suspects.

Investigators used information from a call received on the recovered phone to track down Sodjiné. The modus operandi involved one accomplice posing as a passenger to lure the victim to a location where two others waited. The police are continuing their search for the remaining suspects and are urging the public to remain vigilant and report any suspicious activity.

Why it mattersThe arrest of a suspect in a motorcycle theft attempt affects public safety and property security in Lankouvi.

Togo Pushes UN to Correct World Map

UN General Assembly adopts resolution to challenge Mercator projection, which Togo says distorts Africa's size.

The United Nations General Assembly adopted a resolution on September 4, with 164 votes in favor, to "Correct the Map: Rebalancing Global Cartographic Representation and Promoting Equitable Representation of the World's Regions, Especially Africa." The resolution, championed by Togo on behalf of the African Union, aims to challenge the widespread use of the Mercator projection, which is accused of diminishing the actual size of the African continent in global representations.

Togolese diplomacy stated that these cartographic distortions have had lasting cognitive, educational, cultural, geopolitical, and socioeconomic repercussions, contributing to a drastic reduction of Africa's size in the global collective imagination. The resolution frames this correction as an act of cognitive justice, memorial reparation, and historical truth. It encourages the adoption of equivalent projections, such as the Equal Earth projection recently adopted by the African Union, which offers a more accurate representation of continental dimensions.

The resolution calls on member states to disseminate these more accurate projections in educational and administrative tools and to integrate critical cartography teaching into school curricula. It also urges international organizations and technology companies to establish common geospatial standards and dialogue to incorporate more faithful maps into digital systems. The adoption aligns with the International Decade for People of African Descent and the Decade of Action for Justice and Reparations.

Why it mattersThe resolution affects people by promoting a more accurate representation of Africa's size, aiming to correct historical distortions in global maps and educational materials.
