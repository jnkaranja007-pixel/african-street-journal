Togo adds Ewe and Kabye as optional BAC II subjects from June 2027

The Baccalaureate Office says candidates can choose either national language, bringing the number of optional language subjects to seven.

Togo’s Baccalaureate Office will add Ewe and Kabye to the optional subjects in the second part of the baccalaureate (BAC II) from the June 2027 session. The office announced the decision on Monday, 16 November 2026.

In a circular to school heads, the office’s director general, Minlipe Martin Gangue, said candidates may choose either language under the same conditions as other optional subjects. The seven language options will be German, Arabic, Spanish, Ewe, Kabye, Latin and Russian.

The office asked school heads to identify and register specialist Ewe and Kabye teachers who want to help mark the exams. Registrations must follow criteria set out in a letter dated 9 November 2026.

Why it mattersStudents who sit BAC II from June 2027 can count their Ewe or Kabye skills in the exam, and teachers of those languages can register to help mark it.

Security · 2026-11-18

Togo orders more road checks on main routes and in towns

The Security Ministry says the operations will target all vehicle types and offences including speeding, dangerous overtaking and driving without a valid licence.

Togo’s Security Ministry has announced more checks on main roads and in towns, covering all vehicle types. The operations will target dangerous driving that authorities say threatens road users’ safety.

The ministry identified speeding, dangerous overtaking, driving against traffic and ignoring road signs among the offences. Checks will also focus on driving without a valid licence, using a phone at the wheel and driving under the influence of alcohol.

For motorcyclists, officers will check helmet use; motorists are expected to wear seat belts. The ministry said it also wants drivers to follow road rules, act cautiously and pay attention to other road users to prevent crashes.

Why it mattersMotorcyclists face checks for helmet use, while motorists are expected to wear seat belts as the operations expand across roads and towns.

Politics · 2026-11-18

Houngbo wins second five-year term leading UN labour agency

The Togolese former prime minister secured 51 of 56 votes after Spain’s labour minister withdrew as his only challenger.

Gilbert Houngbo of Togo was re-elected on 16 November to lead the International Labour Organization, the United Nations agency for labour issues, for a second five-year term. He received 51 of 56 votes cast.

Spain’s Labour Minister Yolanda Díaz Pérez withdrew her candidacy shortly before the vote, leaving Houngbo as the sole candidate. His new term is due to begin on 1 October 2027.

Houngbo said he would put artificial intelligence among the agency’s central challenges and argued that technological progress should serve people. Díaz Pérez said she withdrew after union leaders backed Houngbo, and criticised the agency’s financial position.

Why it mattersHoungbo’s new five-year term begins on 1 October 2027, with artificial intelligence among the issues he says the agency will address.

Sport · 2026-11-18

Togo beats Algeria 3-0 in 2027 Africa Cup qualifying

The Eagles scored in the 18th minute and secured the win at Kégué Stadium on Tuesday, 17 November.

Togo beat Algeria 3-0 at Kégué Stadium on Tuesday, 17 November, in a qualifying match for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. The result came in the return match between the national teams.

Togo opened the scoring in the 18th minute and added two more goals by the end of the 90 minutes, according to 24heureinfo. The outlet reported that Togolese supporters celebrated the win, including on social media.

Togo had lost the first match in Tizi Ouzou, Algeria, according to L-Frii. The return-match victory puts the team’s qualifying campaign back in focus, but the reports gave no further qualification details.

Why it mattersTogolese supporters celebrated the 3-0 home win after the team had lost the first match in Algeria.
