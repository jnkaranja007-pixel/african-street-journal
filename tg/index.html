Togo opposition calls for national strike on November 20

The 'Warning Patriotic Day' aims to show public frustration with the Faure Gnassingbé regime.

Opposition groups in Togo have launched a campaign for a "Warning Patriotic Day" on Friday, November 20, 2026. On Sunday, November 8, organizers distributed flyers outside churches, urging Togolese citizens to halt all activities on that day. The call comes from the Manifeste Génération Togo, a coalition including DMK-Originale, DMP, LDP, and the collective "Touche Pas À Ma Constitution."

These groups are asking for "empty streets, empty markets, empty offices, and empty workshops" to demonstrate the population's exasperation. They described the planned day as one of "sacrifice" intended as a warning to the regime of Faure Gnassingbé. The campaign seeks to mobilize the public to express their discontent.

The organizers aim to show the depth of public frustration through a widespread cessation of daily activities. This action is intended to serve as a clear message to the current government regarding the population's sentiments.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are urged to stop all activities on November 20 to express frustration with the government.

Togo signs cooperation deals in Russia

Agreements on education, culture, humanitarian action, and human rights were signed in Moscow.

Togo's President Faure Gnassingbé attended the third Russia-Africa Economic and Humanitarian Forum in Moscow on October 28, 2026. The meeting focused on shared growth drivers including trade, investment, energy infrastructure, digital transformation, and food security. Togo's stated priorities for cooperation with Russia are energy, agriculture, industrial transformation, and scientific/technical training.

During the forum's closing session at the Kremlin on October 29, Gnassingbé reiterated his vision of balanced partnerships based on sovereignty, peace, and economic transformation. He stated that cooperation should support African states' priorities and their own crisis prevention initiatives. He also highlighted that populations expect accessible food, reliable energy, and job prospects.

In addition to the forum, Togo signed two cooperation agreements in Moscow on October 27, 2026, covering education, culture, humanitarian action, and a memorandum of understanding on human rights. Gnassingbé had previously visited China from October 9 to 16, 2026, where discussions and agreements reportedly focused on trade, infrastructure, agriculture, customs, debt relief, and technological modernization.

Why it mattersTogo's president participated in international forums and signed cooperation agreements, affecting the nation's diplomatic and economic relationships.

Togo launches third male health campaign

Novembre Bleu Togo aims to reach rural communities with prostate cancer awareness and screening.

Togo has launched the third edition of its national "Novembre Bleu Togo" campaign, which began Friday in Lomé with a hybrid webinar. Running until November 30, 2026, the initiative, organized by Bébé Trésor and Smart Group Togo, focuses on male health, particularly prostate cancer, under the theme "From the city to the village, let's act for men's health!". This year, the campaign specifically targets rural communities.

Christelle Agnindom, coordinator of Novembre Bleu, highlighted the campaign's purpose, comparing it to "Pink October" for women and noting that male health initiatives are less numerous. She described prostate cancer as a "silent killer" often diagnosed late, leading to preventable deaths. Agnindom expressed satisfaction with progress since the campaign's 2024 start, observing increased partner engagement and men beginning to discuss their health and seek medical advice.

According to the Plan Cancer Togo 2022-2025, prostate cancer accounts for 18.5% of cancer cases in the country, with Hôpital Dogta-Lafié diagnosing over 500 new cases annually. Dr. Sokpoli Jule, a sexologist, identified age (over 60) and family history as unmodifiable risk factors, while diet, physical activity, and exposure to pollution are modifiable. He urged men to recognize symptoms like frequent urination and seek screening via a PSA blood test from age 40.

Togo police block CNCC convoy, seize phones

CNCC leaders faced roadblocks and detentions on their way to a meeting near Tsévié on November 8.

Police and gendarmes blocked a convoy carrying Cadre national de concertation pour le changement (CNCC) leaders on the national road no. 1 near Tsévié on Sunday, November 8. The blockade lasted over an hour and a half, with officials offering no justification for the action.

During the incident, phones were confiscated, and a delegation led by municipal councilor Emmanuel Avognon was taken to the gendarmerie in Tsévié. Activist Nadia Touré was also detained. Professor David Dosseh, en route to join them, missed the meeting. Organizers reported that a mayoral authorization for the meeting had been delayed for six weeks.

This event follows similar disruptions to a CNCC meeting in Vogan. The CNCC's ability to hold meetings is questioned despite the president's remarks on the need for opposition in Togo.

Why it mattersThe meeting of the CNCC, an opposition group, was obstructed by authorities, affecting their ability to assemble and express their views.

Togo evicts informal traders before holidays

Journalist Dieudonné Sewonou criticizes the timing and lack of alternatives for displaced vendors.

Journalist Dieudonné Sewonou reports that authorities in Lomé are clearing informal traders from public spaces ahead of the holiday season. This action displaces many small business owners and artisans who rely on year-end sales for income. The evictions are happening without immediate alternative solutions or social support for those affected.

Sewonou highlights the cruel timing of these evictions, occurring just before the holidays when many informal workers aim to make profits. These micro-activities are a last resort for families facing rising living costs and eroding purchasing power. Many have taken out loans to stock up for the festive period, and their displacement risks pushing them into severe debt.

The journalist also questions the follow-through on development plans for cleared sites, noting that many areas remain abandoned or in a state of disrepair since 2023. He observes that instead of resolving the issue, evicting informal vendors without providing sustainable solutions merely shifts the problem, leaving some formerly vibrant areas as derelict lots.

Why it mattersInformal traders and their families in Lomé are directly affected by evictions that threaten their livelihoods and financial stability, especially during the critical holiday period.
