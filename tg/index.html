Togo men urged to check for breast cancer signs

Less than one percent of breast cancers occur in men, but early detection is key for treatment.

Men in Togo are being urged to be aware of breast cancer symptoms, as the disease can affect them too. While October Rose campaigns focus on women, male breast cancer is rare, accounting for less than one percent of all cases. This rarity means men may not recognize signs like a lump or nipple changes, delaying diagnosis.

A lump or nodule near the nipple, nipple retraction, skin changes, or a swollen armpit can indicate breast cancer in men. These signs, though not always cancerous, warrant rapid medical evaluation. Men with a family history of breast cancer, genetic predispositions like BRCA mutations, or certain hormonal conditions face increased risk.

Consulting a health professional is important if any suspicious signs appear, regardless of age or perceived risk factors. Early diagnosis through clinical exams, mammography, or ultrasound, followed by biopsy, improves treatment outcomes for men affected by this uncommon but serious condition.

Why it mattersMen in Togo are directly affected by the need to recognize and seek treatment for breast cancer symptoms.

Togo journalists face arbitrary arrests

Independent media in Togo are targeted with arrests, fines, and exile.

Independent journalists in Togo face arbitrary arrests, hefty fines, and pressure to leave the country. These actions aim to silence critical voices and investigative reporting. The government's security apparatus is reportedly used to suppress dissent.

Journalists and human rights defenders in Togo operate under systemic repression, according to human rights bodies. Peaceful demonstrations are often banned and dispersed violently. Non-governmental organizations also face administrative hurdles and threats.

The report highlights Togo's authoritarian regime, which persists despite international human rights commitments. This environment stifles public scrutiny of government actions and limits freedoms guaranteed by national and international laws.

Togolese opposition plans Brussels meeting

Togolese democratic forces and diaspora groups will meet in Brussels from November 27 to 29, 2026.

Togolese opposition groups CODITOGO and FREEDOM-TOGO MLN announced a meeting in Brussels, Belgium, from November 27 to 29, 2026. The gathering aims to strengthen the unity of the Togolese opposition and foster a new dynamic for citizen-led action to rebuild democracy in Togo.

This initiative follows discussions at the International Forum of Montreal in August 2025 and takes into account the January 26, 2026, ruling by the ECOWAS Court of Justice. The Brussels Assises will focus on promoting collaboration among democratic opposition actors, establishing a strategic consultation framework, and creating a permanent body for decision-making and dispute resolution.

The stated goals include building a culture of collaboration based on mutual respect and collective responsibility. The meeting also seeks to define new strategies and strengthen the convergence of initiatives to unite democratic forces around a shared vision.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens and diaspora are affected by the opposition's efforts to rebuild democracy and end a long-standing dictatorship.

Togo names six new prefects

Colonel DJOSSOU Agossa Essevi and Colonel (R) AKAGNA Kodjo are among those appointed to lead prefectures.

Togo’s Council of Ministers appointed six new prefects on October 22, 2026. The appointments were made for the Avé, Bas-Mono, Est-Mono, Kloto, Wawa, and Yoto prefectures.

Colonel DJOSSOU Agossa Essevi will serve as the prefect of Avé, while Colonel (R) AKAGNA Kodjo takes on the role of prefect for Bas-Mono. Colonel PIDASSA Bakaï is the new prefect for Est-Mono, and Colonel FIAWOFIA DOAGBODJI Kodjo will lead Kloto prefecture. Monsieur LARE Bintiene Djimbondjo and Monsieur AHE Kokouvi Dodji were appointed prefects of Wawa and Yoto, respectively.

These appointments fall under the purview of the Ministry of Territorial Administration, Local Governance, and Customary Affairs. The six newly appointed officials are tasked with overseeing their respective prefectures.

Business · 2026-10-24

Togo gas transport to resume by October 27

West African Gas Pipeline Company completed valve replacements and will now dry the pipeline.

Gas transport on the Lomé lateral pipeline, suspended since October 12, is expected to resume by October 27. The West African Gas Pipeline Company (WAPCo) announced on October 20 that it had successfully replaced two valves without incident. The company is now proceeding with water evacuation, drying, and inspection of the 19-kilometer pipeline.

WAPCo stated that the replacement of the subsea valve and the station's inlet shut-off valve occurred between October 14 and 17. The next phase involves internal inspection using a magnetic flux leakage tool to assess the pipeline's condition before gas transport can safely restart. WAPCo acknowledged the temporary interruption's impact on Togo's electricity production.

The company expressed gratitude to the Togolese government, the Ministry of Mines and Energy, CEET, ContourGlobal Togo, and the public for their patience. The project aims to ensure the safety, reliability, and sustainable operation of the West African Gas Pipeline system.

Why it mattersThe suspension of gas transport has impacted Togo's electricity production.
