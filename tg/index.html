Togo opposition group plans rally in Tsévié

The National Coordination for Change will gather on November 8 to protest the 2024 constitutional reform.

The National Coordination for Change (CNCC) will hold a rally on Sunday, November 8, at the Atikoumé square in Tsévié. The group, comprising opposition parties ADDI, ANC, FDR, and PSR, along with civil society organizations, received official permission from the local mairie for the event.

The mairie confirmed in a letter dated October 22 that necessary arrangements are in place to provide the requested venue on the specified date. This follows an earlier administrative disagreement that caused the rally, initially scheduled for October 25, to be postponed.

The CNCC's central message is 'No to permanent coups, yes to the restoration of C92,' directly challenging the constitutional reform enacted in 2024.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens, specifically members of opposition parties and civil society, are directly affected by the CNCC's planned protest against constitutional changes.

Togo football coach faces media group demands

The MMLK movement demands public apologies from Patrice Neveu for his conduct towards a journalist.

The Mouvement Martin Luther King (MMLK) in Togo has condemned the behavior of national football team coach Patrice Neveu towards journalist Espoir Gato of Nana FM. The incident occurred during a press conference on November 3 at Kégué stadium in Lomé.

The MMLK, led by Pastor Edoh K. Komi, stated on November 4 that no journalist should face intimidation for asking questions, even if they are difficult. The organisation demands Neveu issue a public and personal apology within 48 hours to the journalist, the Togolese press, and the Togolese people.

The movement also called on the Togolese Football Federation (FTF) to ensure the safety of media professionals at press conferences. While acknowledging the FTF president's regrets, the MMLK insists these do not absolve the coach from offering his own apology.

Why it mattersThe MMLK movement is demanding accountability from the national football coach for his treatment of a journalist, affecting media professionals.

Togo traffic lights malfunction sparks safety concerns

The Togolese Consumers Association (ATC) urges caution and calls for repairs to faulty traffic signals.

Intermittent power outages in Lomé are disrupting traffic light operations, increasing accident risks at intersections. The Togolese Consumers Association (ATC) is warning road users to be extra careful.

In a statement released on November 4, 2026, the ATC also urged authorities to fix the malfunctioning lights and improve road signage. The association is calling on drivers and motorcyclists to strictly follow traffic laws.

The information was initially reported by the news site letabloid.tg.

Why it mattersRoad users in Lomé face increased accident risks due to malfunctioning traffic lights caused by power outages.

Togo players train in Tunisia for Algeria qualifier

The Togolese national football team will camp in Tunisia from November 9-12 before their CAN 2027 qualifier against Algeria.

The Togolese national football team, the Éperviers, will train in Tunisia from Monday, November 9, to Thursday, November 12. This camp prepares them for the first leg of their CAN 2027 qualifier against Algeria, scheduled for Saturday, November 14. The return match will be played on Tuesday, November 17.

Coach Patrice Neveu has selected 25 players to focus on physical, technical, and tactical improvements and to build team unity. The squad draws players from 18 different leagues. This camp marks the first call-up for defender Lilian Brassier, who plays for Eintracht Frankfurt and has Togolese heritage. Kennedy Boateng and Thibault Klidje return to the team, while Augustin Drakpe also receives his first call-up under Neveu. Kevin Boma is unavailable due to injury.

The selected players include Malcolm Barcola of Ittihad Riadi de Tanger in Morocco, Bastou Djibril of Yamoussoukro FC in Ivory Coast, Kodjo Fodoh Laba of Al Sharjah in the UAE, and Komi Arnaud Komlavi of Talaea Al-Gaish FC in Egypt. Brassier, who previously played for Stade Rennais, Brest, and Olympique de Marseille, is contracted to Eintracht Frankfurt until 2031.

Togo professor questions ECOWAS court ruling

Kossi Hounaké, president of the University of Lomé, stated the regional court's ruling on Togo's constitutional reform was 'empty'.

University of Lomé president Kossi Hounaké has described a ruling by the ECOWAS Court of Justice concerning Togo's 2024 constitutional reform as 'empty'. Speaking on Victoire FM on Monday, November 2, Professor Hounaké, a public law specialist, challenged the regional court's jurisdiction and the scope of its decision.

Professor Hounaké argued that the court's authority is limited to human rights violations, stating that the court itself concluded no human rights were breached in the Togolese case. He called the ruling 'laconique' and suggested the court should have stopped its findings there.

The professor's comments followed a university colloquium where the ECOWAS Court's decision was discussed. Hounaké specifically contested the court's competence in addressing the constitutional reform.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are directly affected by the constitutional reform and the legal challenges to its review.
