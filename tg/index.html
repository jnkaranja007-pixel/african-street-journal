Togo's Council of Ministers Adopts New Forest Code and Investment Agency Decree

The Council of Ministers, led by President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbe, approved a revised forest code and a decree for the Investment and Free Zone Promotion Agency (API-ZF).

Togo's Council of Ministers adopted a new forest code and a decree for the Investment and Free Zone Promotion Agency (API-ZF) during its meeting on Wednesday, August 26, 2026. The new forest code aims to strengthen the legal framework for sustainable natural resource management, address climate change, and preserve biodiversity, aligning with Togo's international commitments.

The revised forest code introduces innovations in governance and local actor participation in forest resource management. It redefines national forest domain categories, harmonizes management methods, and enhances territorial community involvement. The code also prioritizes the consultation of riparian populations and considers their rights and interests, particularly in forest classification procedures.

Additionally, the adopted decree expands the API-ZF's responsibilities to include the development, management, and provision of industrial zones. This move seeks to improve the effectiveness of investment promotion and enhance Togo's attractiveness for private investments, while also strengthening transparency and risk control in granting approvals and benefits.

Why it mattersThe new forest code affects local communities by granting them increased participation and consideration of their rights in forest management, while the API-ZF decree impacts businesses by streamlining investment processes and expanding industrial zone development.

Togo opposition forms front against 2024 Constitution, cites ECOWAS ruling

Togolese opposition parties have united in a common front, "Touche pas à ma Constitution," to challenge the 2024 Constitution, citing a January ruling by the ECOWAS Court.

Major opposition parties in Togo have formed a common front, "Touche pas à ma Constitution" (Don't Touch My Constitution), to contest the 2024 Constitution. Nathaniel Olympio, spokesperson for the front, stated that the opposition was informed of the ECOWAS Court of Justice's decision in June, which deemed the constitutional reform an "unconstitutional change of government." The opposition has intensified its protest since June, though it began contesting the Constitution in March 2024.

The opposition accuses the current authorities of changing the fundamental law to allow President Faure Gnassingbé, who has been in power for over twenty years, to remain indefinitely as "president of the Council" without term limits. The Togolese government rejects the ECOWAS Court's conclusions, asserting that the court overstepped its jurisdiction. The opposition, in an alliance described as unprecedented in years, proposes a "crisis exit" to restore constitutional order.

Olympio highlighted that the ECOWAS Court's decision, based on the African Union Charter, makes the Togolese regime "juridically illegitimate and illegal." He stated that all partners should recognize that Togolese authorities are disqualified from speaking on behalf of the Togolese people, viewing the current power as one imposed by force that must be opposed, similar to other exceptional regimes in the sub-region.

Why it mattersThe political instability and legal challenges to the government's legitimacy directly affect all Togolese citizens by creating uncertainty about their leadership and the rule of law.

Dr. Abdallah Ouédraogo becomes Health Clubs ambassador for Sahel States Alliance

Professor Serge Michel Kodom appointed Dr. Abdallah Ouédraogo as the new ambassador for SOS Docteur TV Health Clubs in the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) region.

Dr. Abdallah Ouédraogo, a public health specialist from Burkina Faso, was named ambassador for SOS Docteur TV Health Clubs for the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) by Honorary Professor Serge Michel Kodom. This appointment formalizes a long-standing collaboration between the NGO AIMES-AFRIQUE and Dr. Ouédraogo, who is also a singer, comedian, and writer.

The appointment recognizes Dr. Ouédraogo's ability to combine medical rigor with creative communication, particularly through health awareness capsules on social media. Professor Kodom, head of AIMES-AFRIQUE, stated that Dr. Ouédraogo's background exemplifies the resilience and community engagement the organization aims to foster among young people.

The ceremony also included the signing of an agreement to produce preventive videos and clips for secondary school students. This initiative extends the reach of AIMES-AFRIQUE's Health Clubs, which are already active in Togo's 39 prefectures, aiming to empower young people to create health content for their peers across the Sahel region.

Why it mattersThis event affects young people in the Alliance of Sahel States by providing them with health prevention information and empowering them to become content creators for their communities.

MMLK urges government to address Lomé rent and deposit abuses

The Martin Luther King Movement (MMLK) has called on the Togolese government to intervene urgently to ensure affordable and dignified housing in Lomé and its surroundings.

The MMLK, an advocacy group, highlighted a crisis in housing affordability, citing soaring rents and illegal security deposit demands. Despite existing laws that cap rental deposits, landlords in Lomé are reportedly requiring six, eight, twelve months, or even two years of rent as a deposit.

This situation makes it difficult for ordinary citizens, civil servants, students, and mothers to secure housing, according to the MMLK. The group stated that many face the inability to pay and the threat of eviction due to these practices.

The MMLK's open letter to the Togolese government and the Ministry of Urbanism emphasizes that the current housing market creates precarity, despair, and injustice, threatening the peace and dignity of Togolese families.

Why it mattersCitizens in Lomé are directly affected by the rising cost of housing and illegal deposit demands, making it difficult to find and maintain a home.

ECOWAS Commission reviews free movement obstacles on Abidjan-Lagos corridor

The ECOWAS Commission held a regional dialogue at the Togo-Ghana border to address administrative burdens and other challenges hindering the free movement of people and goods.

The ECOWAS Commission convened a regional dialogue on Monday, August 17, 2026, at Noépé-Akanu, a strategic border point between Togo and Ghana. The meeting focused on reviewing the free movement of people and goods along the Abidjan-Lagos corridor, which connects five ECOWAS member states and facilitates significant trade.

Damtien Tchintchibidja, Vice-President of the Commission, stated that the corridor is a key economic artery supporting millions of people and a substantial portion of the region's economic activity. Despite existing protocols and instruments like the 1979 Protocol on Free Movement, field actors, particularly women and young traders, face administrative hurdles, harassment, gender-based violence, and a lack of information regarding their rights.

Participants, including border agents, customs officials, ministry representatives, traders' associations, and development partners, reviewed tools such as the ENBIC biometric identity card, the SIGMAT transit system, and joint border posts. The objective is to transition from written policies to practical implementation to streamline border crossings and generate economic and social benefits.

Why it mattersThe dialogue directly affects traders and citizens by addressing the administrative and practical obstacles they encounter when moving goods and people across West African borders.
