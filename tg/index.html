Burkina Faso reinstates death penalty for economic crimes

Togo's proximity raises questions about applying similar strictness to its own economic crimes.

Burkina Faso reinstated the death penalty on October 20, 2026, with 71 lawmakers unanimously adopting a new penal code. Justice Minister Edasso Rodrigue Bayala stated the code targets terrorism, espionage, and treason, alongside economic and financial crimes that lead to death.

The new law also imposes life imprisonment and triple fines for embezzling public funds, with significantly increased fines for all offenses. This move by the military leader Captain Ibrahim Traoré follows his intention announced in December 2025.

Human rights groups like Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch condemned the law, fearing it could suppress dissent. The strictness of Burkina Faso's new code prompts questions in Togo, a neighbor, about how such laws would apply to its own history of unpunished economic crimes.

Why it mattersTogo's administration faces scrutiny over unpunished economic crimes, raising concerns about potential repercussions if similar laws were enforced.

Togo court hears regional review of constitution

Jurists gather at Lomé University to discuss the ECOWAS Court's January 29, 2026 ruling on Togo's March 2024 constitutional changes.

Jurists from across the sub-region are meeting at the University of Lomé on October 29 and 30, 2026, to discuss the role of regional courts. The conference, held in the Koffi Ahadzi-Nonou hall, focuses on African regional human rights courts and their oversight of constitutional changes. A key session examines the ECOWAS Court of Justice's January 29, 2026 decision concerning Togo's March 2024 constitutional reform.

The opposition views the ECOWAS Court's ruling as a victory, suggesting it invalidates the current Togolese government's legitimacy. However, the government disputes this, stating the Court lacks jurisdiction over internal constitutional matters and that its ruling does not mandate the repeal of the law or financial reparations. A political scientist noted that while the ECOWAS Court is not a constitutional court and did not order the law's withdrawal, it affirmed that constituent powers are not above international law.

The international colloquium includes panels on regional oversight of constitutional revisions and the enforcement of community court decisions. Speakers include Professor Alioune Sall, a former court judge, and Professor Dodzi Kokoroko, who will moderate a panel featuring academics from Benin, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, France, and Togo. Other participants include Dandi Gnamou, Seni Ouedraogo, Fabrice Hourquebie, and Augustin Loada.

Why it mattersThe ECOWAS Court of Justice's ruling on Togo's constitutional reform is being debated by legal experts, impacting the perception of the government's legitimacy.

Togo launches maritime crime money hunt

National workshop aims to identify threats and strengthen coordination against illicit financial flows.

Togo is hosting a national workshop until Friday to track illicit funds linked to maritime crime. The event, organized by the National Financial Information Processing Unit with support from GIABA and the African Development Bank, will assess risks, identify threats, and develop operational recommendations. This initiative follows the adoption of new legislation in March 2026, which aligns with international standards.

The workshop focuses on combating financial crime in maritime activities. Officials will work to enhance cooperation between institutions. The new legislation provides a framework for these efforts. The goal is to strengthen the country's ability to address financial threats originating from the sea.

Togo-flagged vessels have been intercepted carrying significant cocaine loads, including over four tons each on the Blume and Orion V in 2023, and nearly five tons on a fishing boat in the Pacific in January 2026. The port of Lomé, described as the national economy's lung, faces scrutiny over the transparency of its revenue, concessions, and contracts.

Why it mattersTogo is holding a workshop to combat maritime crime and financial illicit flows, impacting national security and economic transparency.

Togo influencer raises 2 million FCFA for house fund

Maa Bio's online fundraiser for her home project collected nearly 2 million FCFA in two hours.

Influencer Maa Bio launched a fundraiser to symbolically finance the construction of her house, collecting nearly 2 million FCFA in just two hours. She announced that 15% of the collected amount will be donated to autistic children.

Maa Bio explained that her subscribers wanted to continue contributing to her project, viewing their financial support as a symbolic way to gift her a house. She stated that contributions would continue.

The influencer humorously addressed her financial situation, suggesting that acquiring property might be part of her future. The fundraiser was conducted via the Tikerama platform.

Why it mattersTogo's influencer Maa Bio is raising funds for her house and donating a portion to autistic children.

Université de Kara opens new arts degree

The new plastic arts and drawing degree will train teachers for secondary schools.

The University of Kara will launch a new Bachelor's degree in Plastic Arts and Drawing starting in the 2026-2027 academic year. The program, offered by the ISPAU, aims to train professionals capable of teaching these subjects at the junior secondary level.

This new specialization will allow students to master fundamental plastic arts practices, develop their creative and analytical abilities, and learn effective pedagogical methods for teaching art. The university's president approved the creation of this degree.

The degree is part of the University of Kara's 2026-2030 Strategic Development Plan. The program seeks to promote and disseminate artistic culture and creation within Togo.

Why it mattersTogolese students will have a new educational opportunity to specialize in visual arts and pursue careers in teaching.
