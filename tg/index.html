Togolese opposition plans Brussels meeting

Forces démocratiques togolaises and diaspora groups will convene in Brussels from November 27-29, 2026, to discuss unity.

Togolese opposition groups CODITOGO and FREEDOM-TOGO MLN announced a meeting in Brussels, Belgium, from November 27 to 29, 2026. The gathering aims to strengthen the unity of the Togolese democratic opposition and foster a new dynamic for citizen-led action.

This initiative follows discussions at the International Forum of Montreal in August 2025 and is informed by a January 26, 2026, ruling by the ECOWAS Court of Justice. The Assises de Bruxelles will focus on promoting collaboration, strategic reflection, and establishing a permanent framework for consultation and coordination among democratic forces.

The meeting seeks to define new strategies for citizen struggle and build a shared vision to remobilize democratic forces against what organizers describe as a 60-year dictatorship in Togo.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens and diaspora members are affected by plans to unify the opposition and strategize for democratic change.

Togo movement aims to keep girls in school

Ladies Movement 3 will gather young women on October 31 in Lomé to discuss education and leadership.

The third Ladies Movement event was announced on October 10, 2026, in Lomé, Togo. Organized by IYAWO Association and led by its president, Elisabeth Apampa, the initiative prioritizes the education, protection, and leadership of young girls. The event is scheduled for October 31 at the Golden Plazza, aiming to empower girls to build their futures.

Elisabeth Apampa, who is also a UNICEF Togo Children's Champion, stated that the movement seeks to translate words into action. The forum aims to ensure Togolese girls can dream, learn, and succeed without facing barriers like early pregnancies or socioeconomic difficulties. This event follows International Day of the Girl, celebrated on October 11.

Ladies Movement 3 will provide a platform for girls to express themselves, learn, and develop concrete solutions. Apampa, founder of IYAWO Association in 2018, advocates for girls' education and prevention of gender-based violence. The event will focus on enabling girls to voice their challenges and expectations.

Why it mattersYoung girls in Togo will be directly affected by the Ladies Movement 3 event which focuses on their education and empowerment.

France 24 correspondent removed from Togo

Journalist Emmanuelle Sodji was sidelined by France 24 management in September, amid French diplomatic efforts to restore broadcast access.

France 24 journalist Emmanuelle Sodji, who covered both Togo and Benin, was suddenly removed from her post by management in September. This action follows over a year of France 24 and RFI broadcasts being banned in Togo. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot had previously advocated for the lifting of these suspensions in April 2026.

Off Investigation reported on Sodji's dismissal, which occurred as France's Quai d’Orsay seeks to reopen the stations closed by the Togolese government. Barrot had expressed hope in April 2026 that the broadcast suspensions would be lifted soon. The specific reasons for Sodji's sudden sidelining were not immediately clear.

The French media group France Médias Monde operates both RFI and France 24. The Togolese government shut down broadcasts from both outlets more than a year ago. Barrot met with Togolese authorities to discuss the issue.

Why it mattersJournalist Emmanuelle Sodji was removed from her position in Togo, affecting media access.

Togo opposition decries 'grotesque' academic conference

Le Manifeste Génération Togo group calls the October 29-30 Lomé event a bid to bypass a January 2026 ECOWAS court ruling.

Opposition groups in Togo are denouncing an upcoming international conference in Lomé scheduled for October 29 and 30. Le Manifeste Génération Togo calls the event, focused on regional courts and constitutional changes, a "grotesque" attempt to circumvent a January 29, 2026, ECOWAS Court of Justice ruling. The court had previously declared the establishment of the Fifth Republic in Togo an "unconstitutional change of government."

The opposition group expressed deep concern that the academic gathering is a deliberate effort to shift the discussion away from the legal implications of the court's decision. They argue that academics cannot substitute for judges and that the conference risks becoming a platform to contest the court's ruling and legitimize an "illegal" Fifth Republic. The group stated that this move follows the government's prior avoidance of public debate before constitutional changes.

Le Manifeste Génération Togo warned that attempts to discredit the ECOWAS Court could weaken confidence in regional and continental human rights institutions. They questioned how the government plans to ensure the rule of law and respect for the people's will, noting that past government reactions have included contesting the court's jurisdiction and calling for dialogue.

Why it mattersTogo's opposition fears a conference will undermine a court ruling on the country's constitutional changes.

Togo faces power cuts due to gas pipeline maintenance

Electricity outages will continue until October 27 as the CEET performs essential maintenance on the gas pipeline.

Togo is experiencing widespread electricity disruptions because of maintenance work on the gas pipeline that supplies power plants. The Compagnie énergie électrique du Togo (CEET) announced that these repairs are scheduled from October 12 to October 27, 2026.

CEET Director General Ilagou Ayeva stated that a gradual return to normal power supply is expected before the end of October. Despite efforts to use alternative energy sources, further power interruptions are possible during this period, and customers are advised to unplug appliances to prevent damage.

The CEET is asking subscribers to be cautious while awaiting improved electricity delivery across the affected regions. The maintenance aims to ensure the continued reliable operation of the gas infrastructure.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are experiencing power outages and risk damage to their electrical appliances.
