Togo's CNSS starts pensioner life checks

The social security agency will verify pensioners are alive from November 16 to January 17.

The National Social Security Fund (CNSS) in Togo has launched a life verification operation for all pensioners and annuitants. This campaign, running from November 16, 2026, to January 17, 2027, includes beneficiaries living both in Togo and abroad. The CNSS aims to update its records and improve the reliability of social benefit payments through this initiative.

This operation targets all pensioners and annuitants whose payments were processed up to the end of October 2026. Beneficiaries must complete the process digitally using the Biosecu application for biometric data collection. The CNSS warns that failure to comply by the deadline will result in the suspension of payments starting in January 2027.

The verification process replaces traditional paper-based life certificates with a digital system to prevent fraudulent payments, often called 'phantom pensions,' which occur after a beneficiary's death. This modernization aims to ensure social security resources reach rightful recipients and preserve the integrity of the social security system.

Why it mattersPensioners and annuitants in Togo and abroad must complete a digital life verification by January 17, 2027, or face payment suspension.

Togo launches three climate initiatives at COP31

The country presented the Togo Green Fund, a national carbon registry, and a zero-waste program.

Togo launched three environmental initiatives at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, on November 11, 2026. The country introduced the Togo Green Fund, a national carbon registry, and a zero-waste program to advance its ecological transition. These mechanisms aim to centralize climate financing, track greenhouse gas emissions, and promote a circular economy.

President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé attended the summit, themed 'Towards a Sustainable, Resilient, and Equitable Future.' The Togo Green Fund will mobilize national and international finance for environmental projects. The national carbon registry will enhance transparency in monitoring emission reduction efforts and valuing progress. The zero-waste program seeks to improve waste collection, management, and valorization.

Beyond these three initiatives, Togo plans to focus on renewable energy development, ecosystem preservation, and reforestation at COP31. These efforts align with Togo's commitments under the Paris Agreement and cover strategic sectors including energy, agriculture, and forestry. The Togolese delegation will continue discussions on these proposals until November 20, 2026.

Why it mattersTogo's environmental initiatives at COP31 will affect its energy, agriculture, and forestry sectors.

Université de Kara adds anti-corruption teaching to its courses

A kick-off lecture on Tuesday 10 November 2026 launched the permanent teaching of integrity and anti-corruption in the university's programmes.

The University of Kara in Togo has taken a new step in promoting integrity by making education on fighting corruption a permanent part of its training offers. An inaugural lecture held on Tuesday 10 November 2026 marked the start of the initiative, driven by the High Authority for Prevention and Fight Against Corruption and Related Offenses (HAPLUCIA), a state anti-graft body.

Professor Alosse Charles Grégoire, who gave the opening lecture, told students that the units on citizenship, transparency and anti-corruption should be treated as practical courses, not extra subjects. He said university leaders, lecturers and administrative staff must also model the values they teach, including good management of public service and putting the general interest first.

HAPLUCIA president ABA Kimelabalou argued that awareness campaigns, control mechanisms and sanctions are not enough on their own to eradicate corruption, and that education and training are essential. The lecture was held under the theme "Academic excellence through the lens of public ethics: the University of Kara, incubator of a culture of integrity".

Why it mattersStudents across the University of Kara's programmes will now receive permanent instruction on integrity and anti-corruption, as the initiative's organisers state.

Tech · 2026-11-12

Togo launches first cybersecurity event

The Togo Cyber Arena will gather public officials, companies, and experts on November 19-20 in Lomé.

Togo will host its first national cybersecurity gathering, the Togo Cyber Arena (TCA), on November 19 and 20, 2026, at the Hôtel Sarakawa in Lomé. The Agence Nationale de la Cybersécurité (ANCy), supported by the GIZ ProDigiT project, organized the event to unite the cybersecurity ecosystem.

The two-day event aims to promote cybersecurity careers, identify local talent, and establish Togo as a regional leader in digital security. It will address threats like phishing, mobile money fraud, ransomware, and identity theft, which are increasing with widespread digital use. The program includes conferences, technical workshops on penetration testing and cybercrime, and a national ethical hacking competition.

A public exhibition village will offer practical advice on data protection, phone security, and scam prevention. Commandant Gbota Gwaliba, Director General of the ANCy, stated that cybersecurity is no longer a specialist issue and invited startups to exhibit their solutions. Participation requires registration, and the event will be live-streamed on ANCy's digital channels.

Why it mattersThe event aims to address growing digital threats like phishing and ransomware affecting the general public and businesses.
