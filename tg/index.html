Togo Power Company Announces Service Interruptions Until September 21

The Compagnie Énergie Électrique du Togo (CEET) has announced potential electricity outages across the country due to maintenance work on the West African Gas Pipeline.

Electricity supply in Togo will experience intermittent interruptions nationwide from August 28 to September 21, 2026. The Compagnie Énergie Électrique du Togo (CEET) stated these disruptions are necessary for planned maintenance on the West African Gas Pipeline (WAGP).

To mitigate the impact on electricity provision, CEET intends to utilize liquid fuels, which come at a higher cost. Despite these measures, the company cautioned that sporadic power cuts may still occur during the specified period.

CEET advises all users to take precautions, including unplugging electrical appliances during outages. This recommendation aims to prevent potential damage from voltage fluctuations when power is restored.

Why it mattersBusinesses and households across Togo face potential disruptions to their operations and daily lives due to planned electricity outages.

Togo Police Extort Motorcyclists in Lomé Roadblocks

Mixed police and gendarme units in Lomé are reportedly setting up ambushes at traffic junctions, demanding cash payments from motorcyclists under arbitrary pretexts.

In Lomé and its suburbs, mixed detachments of police and gendarmes are reportedly conducting targeted raids on motorcyclists at strategic traffic points. Instead of managing traffic flow, these units are allegedly setting up ambushes, citing variable pretexts such as alleged traffic light violations, overloading, or lack of helmets and documentation. The practice has become a regular occurrence, with reports of motorcycles being impounded even when riders claim to be in compliance.

The process involves officers offering informal settlements on the spot for immediate release of the motorcycle, or directing riders to a collection point where a fixed sum of seven thousand five hundred CFA francs is demanded. This payment is required for the return of the vehicle, regardless of whether an infraction was officially documented. The absence of official receipts or proper legal documentation for these transactions suggests a system of generalized extortion.

Documented cases include a delivery rider who was forced to pay seven thousand five hundred CFA francs after being unable to immediately settle a five thousand CFA franc demand, losing two days of work. Another incident involved workers returning from factories whose motorcycles sustained damage during impoundment, with no initial vehicle condition report filed. Some owners have also reported their motorcycles disappearing after paying the arbitrary fees, with no clear record-keeping system in place.

Why it mattersMotorcyclists in Lomé are directly affected by arbitrary demands for payment and potential loss of their vehicles, impacting their livelihoods.

New Group Tackles Plastic Waste in Togo

ZDP-Togo launched to address widespread plastic pollution affecting waterways, markets, and the food chain, urging public action and enforcement of existing laws.

The association "Zéro Déchet Plastique-Togo" (ZDP-Togo) officially began its work on Thursday, August 27, aiming to combat the pervasive plastic pollution that has long marred Lomé's streets, drains, and coastline. The group, operating under the motto "Everything transforms!", seeks to address the accumulation of plastic bags in waterways and markets, as well as the invisible threat of microplastics entering the food chain.

ZDP-Togo President Gérard DJOSSOU highlighted the health risks associated with plastic waste, warning that microplastics can be ingested by marine life and ultimately poison humans, potentially leading to chronic illnesses and cancers. He called on public authorities to enforce existing environmental laws, including the 2008 framework law and subsequent decrees and regulations prohibiting non-biodegradable plastics, rather than creating new legislation.

The organization plans to organize collection efforts, leveraging local neighborhood development committees to tackle the issue. ZDP-Togo emphasizes that the accumulation of plastic waste exacerbates flooding risks in Lomé, particularly during rainy seasons. The group encourages individuals to reduce plastic consumption, avoid littering, reuse items when possible, and participate in recycling initiatives.

Why it mattersThe launch of ZDP-Togo and its focus on plastic waste collection and reduction directly impacts farmers by potentially mitigating pollution in waterways and on land, which can affect crops and livestock.

Togo Adopts New Forest Code to Boost Local Governance and Investment

The Council of Ministers approved a revised forest code aimed at strengthening legal frameworks for natural resource management, climate resilience, and local development.

Togo's Council of Ministers has adopted a new forest code designed to enhance the legal framework for managing natural resources, addressing climate change, and preserving biodiversity. The revised law seeks to better balance forest protection with local development needs and climate resilience, aligning with international commitments. It introduces significant changes in governance, including increased participation of local actors in forest resource management and a redefinition of national forest categories.

The updated code places greater emphasis on the rights and interests of local populations, mandating their consultation in forest classification procedures. It also formalizes principles for accessing genetic resources and sharing benefits equitably. Furthermore, it strengthens forest monitoring and enforcement against illegal activities such as poaching and trafficking of wild species. Mechanisms to incentivize agroforestry, private investment, and energy transition are also included to reduce pressure on forest resources.

In parallel, the Council approved a decree detailing the roles, organization, and operations of the Agency for Investment Promotion and the Free Zone (API-ZF). This move is part of broader efforts to streamline investment processes and encourage economic activity within designated zones. The adoption of these measures signifies the government's commitment to sustainable development and economic growth through improved regulatory frameworks and institutional support.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected by the new forest code which emphasizes local participation in resource management and incentivizes agroforestry, potentially impacting their land use and livelihoods.
