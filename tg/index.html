French Filmmaker Released for Medical Care in Togo

One of two French filmmakers detained in Togo since late July on charges of "false declarations" has been released under judicial supervision to receive medical treatment.

One of the two French filmmakers, Sebastian Perez Pezzani, arrested in Togo and accused of making "false declarations," has been released under judicial supervision to seek medical care. He and Gaël Mocaër, along with their Togolese colleague Fred Vedomey, were taken into custody on July 27 in northern Togo.

The trio was filming an episode for the documentary series "Les routes de l'impossible," which is broadcast on France Télévisions. The specific nature of the medical care required for Perez Pezzani was not detailed by RFI Afrique.

The other French filmmaker, Gaël Mocaër, and their Togolese collaborator, Fred Vedomey, remain detained. The arrests occurred while they were working on their documentary project in the northern region of the country.

Why it mattersThe release of one filmmaker for medical treatment affects the individuals involved by changing their legal status and access to healthcare, while the ongoing detention of others impacts their freedom and work.

Lomé Port Union Secures Reinstatement, Benefits for Dockworkers

The Autonomous Port of Lomé Agents' Union (SYAPAL) announced significant progress following discussions with port management, including the reintegration of dockworkers and new.

The Autonomous Port of Lomé Agents' Union (SYAPAL), affiliated with the National Union of Independent Unions of Togo (UNSIT), reported major advancements after meeting with the General Directorate of the Autonomous Port of Lomé. These include the reintegration of dockworkers who were not rehired and several social measures benefiting employees.

On August 18, 2026, SYAPAL secured an audience with the General Director of the port. During this meeting, the Director committed to examining 12 union demands, distributing face masks to mineral quay workers, providing milk to each worker per shift, and implementing a sanitation block project for staff.

Additionally, the port management pledged to reinstate all non-rehired workers by August 24, 2026, provide worker transport from assembly points to the quay within one month, cover one meal per shift, and establish a modern canteen soon. SYAPAL thanked the port's general directorate for these measures and reaffirmed its commitment to social dialogue.

Why it mattersDockworkers who were not rehired will be reinstated and receive new benefits, directly impacting their employment and working conditions at the port.

Togo President Reshuffles Security Apparatus Amid Jihadist Threat

President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé initiated a significant reorganization of the security forces on August 17, driven by the jihadist threat in the north and a desire to.

President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé deeply reshuffled Togo's security apparatus on August 17. This extensive reorganization of the military command, which had been anticipated for several weeks, follows a two-year cycle that became an unofficial rule after the assassination of Colonel Toussaint Bitala Madjoulba in May 2020.

The changes were prompted by the ongoing jihadist threat in the northern part of the country. Additionally, the President of the Council sought to consolidate his influence over the armed forces, according to reports.

The previous military command had been in place since 2024, completing its two-year term before this latest overhaul. The move reflects a strategic effort to address both external security challenges and internal power dynamics within the nation's defense structure.

Why it mattersThis security reshuffle directly affects Togolese citizens as it aims to address the jihadist threat in the north, which impacts their safety and stability.

Togo housing costs outpace wages, MMLK demands government action

Rent and associated fees in Lomé often exceed monthly salaries, leading the Mouvement Martin Luther King “La Voix des Sans Voix” to call for government intervention.

Housing costs in Lomé are increasingly unaffordable for many residents, with rent and associated fees often surpassing monthly incomes. An example from Agoè two Lions shows a two-room studio, lacking reliable running water, renting for twenty-five thousand FCFA per month, with an additional two thousand five hundred FCFA non-refundable viewing commission and a requirement for twelve months' deposit. This situation is common for thousands of Lomé residents weekly, according to Icilome.

The Mouvement Martin Luther King “La Voix des Sans Voix” (MMLK) highlighted this issue in an open letter to the government on August twenty-fourth. Its president, Pastor Edoh K. Komi, stated that despite a law capping rental deposits, the reality on the ground is that stronger parties dictate terms, with demands for six, eight, ten, or even twelve months of advance rent.

The MMLK is pushing for four key changes: control brigades to penalize deposit abuses, official rent scales by neighborhood to curb speculation, a free hotline for reporting issues without fear of reprisal, and faster social housing programs. This comes as household purchasing power in Togo declines due to rising petroleum product costs, while wages remain stagnant.

Why it mattersThis situation directly affects Togolese families, like Akossiwa, who find it impossible to afford basic housing due to high rents and unregulated fees that consume a significant portion of their income.

Clubs RFI-Togo launches campaign against counterfeit medicines in Kpalimé

The Coordination des Clubs RFI-Togo initiated its eighth awareness campaign in Kpalimé on August twenty-first to combat the sale of fake medicines.

The Coordination des Clubs RFI-Togo launched its eighth awareness campaign in Kpalimé on Friday, August twenty-first, targeting counterfeit medicines. The three-day operation in the Kloto prefecture involved administrative authorities, health professionals, professional organizations, and citizens, focusing on the dangers of medicines sold on the street.

Koudjo Kodjo, the secretary general of the prefecture, described contraband medicines as "silent weapons" that threaten thousands of lives daily and compromise health system efforts, particularly affecting the most vulnerable. Kossi Afelete Atigaku, mayor of Kloto 1, emphasized that awareness alone is insufficient and called for financially and geographically accessible quality medicines.

The campaign is supported by a coalition including the World Health Organization, the Order of Pharmacists, the Brazzaville Foundation, and fifteen private companies. Alfred Nadjere, spokesperson for Clubs RFI, stated that falsified or improperly stored medicines sold outside official channels become a deadly threat, urging people to protect their health by avoiding such products.

Why it mattersThousands of lives are threatened daily by counterfeit medicines, compromising health system efforts and affecting the most vulnerable.
