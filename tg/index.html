Togo Power Company Announces Scheduled Electricity Cuts Until September 21

The Compagnie Énergie Électrique du Togo (CEET) will implement intermittent power outages nationwide due to maintenance on the West African Gas Pipeline.

The Compagnie Énergie Électrique du Togo (CEET) has announced that electricity supply will be temporarily interrupted across the country from August 28 to September 21, 2026. These disruptions are attributed to scheduled maintenance work on the West African Gas Pipeline (WAGP). The CEET plans to use more expensive liquid fuels to mitigate the impact of these works on the electrical supply.

Despite these measures, the company warns that sporadic power cuts may still occur during the announced period. The CEET advises electricity consumers to take necessary precautions. Specifically, users are recommended to disconnect electrical appliances during outages to prevent potential damage from voltage fluctuations when power is restored.

The maintenance on the gas pipeline necessitates these temporary reductions in electricity provision. While the CEET is employing alternative fuel sources, the cost of these fuels is higher than usual. The company's advisory aims to help the public manage the inconvenience and protect their equipment from potential surges.

Why it mattersBusinesses and households across Togo will experience power interruptions, potentially disrupting operations and daily life.

Police and Gendarmes Extort Motorcyclists in Lomé

Mixed detachments of police and gendarmes are setting up ambushes at Lomé intersections to demand payments from motorcyclists.

In Lomé and its suburbs, police and gendarmes are reportedly setting up ambushes at strategic times, targeting motorcyclists. Instead of directing traffic, these mixed detachments allegedly stop riders for minor or fabricated infractions, such as running red lights, overloading, or lacking helmets. In some cases, motorcycles are impounded without clear justification, with officers then offering informal settlements on the spot for immediate release.

Those who cannot pay immediately are directed to a collection site the next day, where a flat fee of seven thousand five hundred CFA francs is demanded for the motorcycle's return, regardless of whether an offense was officially recorded. This practice, often conducted without official receipts or proper legal documentation, is described as a systematic extortion enterprise rather than a security mission.

Documented incidents include a delivery rider forced to pay seven thousand five hundred CFA francs after being told to pay five thousand FCFA on the spot, leading to two lost workdays. Workers returning from factories have also reported their motorcycles sustaining damage during impoundment, with no vehicle condition report filed. Some owners who paid the arbitrary fee have found their motorcycles missing from the impound sites, with no clear record of their seizure.

Why it mattersMotorcyclists in Lomé are affected by arbitrary impoundments and demands for payment, leading to financial loss and disruption of work.
