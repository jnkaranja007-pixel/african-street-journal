Burkina Faso reinstates death penalty for economic crimes

Togo's proximity raises questions about applying similar strictness to its own economic crimes.

Burkina Faso reinstated the death penalty on October 20, 2026, with 71 lawmakers unanimously adopting a new penal code. Justice Minister Edasso Rodrigue Bayala stated the code targets terrorism, espionage, and treason, alongside economic and financial crimes that lead to death.

The new law also imposes life imprisonment and triple fines for embezzling public funds, with significantly increased fines for all offenses. This move by the military leader Captain Ibrahim Traoré follows his intention announced in December 2025.

Human rights groups like Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch condemned the law, fearing it could suppress dissent. The strictness of Burkina Faso's new code prompts questions in Togo, a neighbor, about how such laws would apply to its own history of unpunished economic crimes.

Why it mattersTogo's administration faces scrutiny over unpunished economic crimes, raising concerns about potential repercussions if similar laws were enforced.

Togo lawyers debate regional court's power over constitution

Jurists gathered at the University of Lomé on October 29, 2026, to discuss human rights courts and state sovereignty.

Jurists from across the region met at the University of Lomé on October 29 and 30, 2026, to discuss the role of regional courts. The conference focused on how these courts interact with the rule of law, constitutional changes, and state sovereignty.

A key discussion point was the ECOWAS Court of Justice's January 29, 2026, ruling on Togo's March 2024 constitutional reform. Opposition parties and civil society groups brought the case, arguing the reform violated certain principles. The government contested the court's jurisdiction over internal constitutional matters.

The debate continues over the ruling's implications, with some opposition groups viewing it as a judicial validation of illegitimacy. However, the court did not order the repeal of the constitutional law or any financial reparations, and the government maintains the court lacks authority for such control.

Why it mattersA conference in Lomé on October 29, 2026, addressed the ECOWAS Court of Justice's ruling on Togo's constitutional reform, impacting the political legitimacy debate.

Togo influencer raises 2 million FCFA for house fund

Maa Bio's online fundraiser for her home project collected nearly 2 million FCFA in two hours.

Influencer Maa Bio launched a fundraiser to symbolically finance the construction of her house, collecting nearly 2 million FCFA in just two hours. She announced that 15% of the collected amount will be donated to autistic children.

Maa Bio explained that her subscribers wanted to continue contributing to her project, viewing their financial support as a symbolic way to gift her a house. She stated that contributions would continue.

The influencer humorously addressed her financial situation, suggesting that acquiring property might be part of her future. The fundraiser was conducted via the Tikerama platform.

Why it mattersTogo's influencer Maa Bio is raising funds for her house and donating a portion to autistic children.

Togo launches maritime crime money hunt

National workshop aims to identify threats and strengthen coordination against illicit financial flows.

Togo is hosting a national workshop until Friday to track illicit money from maritime crime. The event will assess risks, identify threats, and improve coordination between institutions. Recommendations for operational action will be formulated during the workshop.

The workshop is organized by the National Financial Information Processing Unit with support from GIABA and the African Development Bank. It operates under a new law adopted in March 2026, which has been declared compliant with international standards.

Togo-flagged vessels have been intercepted carrying large cocaine shipments, including the Blume and Orion V in 2023, and a fishing boat in the Pacific in January 2026. The port of Lomé's financial operations and concessions lack public, detailed reporting, according to the report.

Why it mattersThe Togolese government is holding a workshop to combat financial crime related to maritime activities, while critics question the transparency of state finances and port revenues.

Université de Kara opens arts degree in 2026

The new plastic arts and drawing degree aims to train teachers for secondary schools.

The University of Kara will launch a new Bachelor's degree in Plastic Arts and Drawing starting in the 2026-2027 academic year. The program, offered by ISPAU, will equip students with fundamental artistic practices and pedagogical skills.

This new course aims to prepare graduates to teach plastic arts and drawing at the junior secondary level. It also seeks to promote and disseminate artistic creation and culture within Togo.

The university's strategic development plan for 2026-2030 includes this new offering, which was established by a decree on September 18, 2025, appointing the university's president.

Why it mattersTogolese students will have a new specialized degree in arts education starting in the 2026-2027 academic year.
