Togo university hosts rights court debate

Lomé conference examines regional human rights courts and state sovereignty after constitutional change.

The University of Lomé hosted an international conference on October 29 and 30, 2026, discussing regional human rights courts, the rule of law, constitutional changes, and state sovereignty. The event followed a January 29, 2026, ruling by the ECOWAS Court of Justice.

Professor Pascal Kossivi Adjamagbo stated that Togolese sovereignty belongs to the people and cannot be seized by rulers in the name of state sovereignty. This discussion is particularly relevant following Togo's constitutional reform on March 25, 2024, which moved the country to its Fifth Republic.

The conference, organized by the Public Law Center at the University of Lomé, focused on the tension between popular sovereignty and the new institutional framework established by the recent constitutional reform.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are affected by the debate over who holds ultimate sovereignty following a constitutional reform.

Togo mayor deploys beauty brigade to clear streets

Golfe 5 municipality launches a two-week operation to remove obstructions from priority roads.

Golfe 5 mayor Kossi Aboka launched a special street clearing operation on November 2, 2026, in Lomé, using a newly formed 'beauty brigade' instead of bulldozers. The commune aims to clear sidewalks and priority roads of goods, containers, stalls, and other obstructions until November 20.

The operation follows a government decision to clear Grand Lomé's main routes, with Mayor Aboka stating that a city's beauty is its first impression. The brigade, led by the third deputy mayor Koubalo Badjamla, includes local officials, municipal agents, and security representatives. Prohibited items include market goods, fuel sales, and construction material deposits on sidewalks.

The goal is to create a cleaner living environment for residents, though the brigade has not publicly stated where displaced vendors will relocate. This initiative focuses on the aesthetic appeal and accessibility of the city's main thoroughfares.

Why it mattersResidents of Golfe 5 municipality will experience cleaner streets and sidewalks as obstructions are removed.

Côte d’Ivoire sets cotton prices for 2026-2027 season

The government announced 310 FCFA per kilogram for first-choice cotton and 285 FCFA for second-choice.

The Ivorian government has set the purchase prices for seed cotton for the 2026-2027 season. First-choice cotton will be bought at 310 FCFA per kilogram, and second-choice at 285 FCFA per kilogram. The state will also provide 29.3 billion FCFA to support producers through price supplements and subsidies for agricultural inputs.

Minister of Agriculture, Rural Development and Food Production, Bruno Nabagné Koné, announced these official tariffs on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, during a press conference. The prices were fixed for the upcoming cotton campaign.

The government also communicated the prices for key agricultural inputs intended for producers.

Togo opposition figure supports Gnassingbé regime

Me Tchassona Traoré, leader of the MCD, argues for reform and dialogue over rupture with the current government.

Me Tchassona Traoré, president of the small political party MCD, stated his commitment to social democracy through reform and dialogue rather than rupture. He believes in participating in dialogue frameworks, even with the current regime, to defend his convictions. Traoré made these remarks in an interview with the Togolese weekly "La Manchette" on October 21, 2026.

Traoré's stance has drawn criticism from some who argue that Togo is not a democracy and that the Gnassingbé regime is resistant to sincere dialogue. Critics point to a history of human rights violations, including political prisoners and repression of peaceful protests, as evidence of the regime's approach, suggesting Traoré's emphasis on non-violence is misplaced.

The MCD leader cited examples of grand coalitions and cohabitation in France and Germany as models for political cooperation. However, critics argue these European examples are not comparable to Togo's political situation, where they claim the Gnassingbé regime holds a monopoly on violence and resists genuine democratization efforts.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens, particularly those in the opposition, are affected by the debate on how to engage with the long-standing Gnassingbé regime.
