Togo government in exile demands ECOWAS court order enforcement

The legitimate government and DMK announced a forced execution order for a January 29, 2026 ECOWAS court ruling.

The legitimate government of Togo in exile and the Dynamique Monseigneur Kpodzro (DMK) announced on November 1, 2026, that Togolese authorities have been served with an order for the forced execution of an ECOWAS court ruling from January 29, 2026.

The joint statement noted that the notification from the ECOWAS Court of Justice's interim chief registrar, Dr. Ousmane Diallo, arrived nine months after the original ruling. This marks the end of a nine-month period granted for voluntary compliance with the court's decision.

The government in exile and DMK are calling on the international community to act and urging Togolese citizens to mobilize generally.

Why it mattersThe legitimate Togolese government and DMK are pushing for the enforcement of a regional court order against the current Togolese authorities.

Togo opposition figure supports Gnassingbé regime

Me Tchassona Traoré, leader of the MCD party, advocates for reform and dialogue within the current political framework.

Me Tchassona Traoré, president of the small political party MCD (Mouvement Citoyen pour la Démocratie et le Développement), stated his commitment to social democracy through reform, institutions, and dialogue rather than rupture. In an interview with "La Manchette" weekly on October 21, 2026, Traoré argued for participating in and improving existing dialogue frameworks, asserting his presence in the CPC (Cadre Permanent de Concertation) is a practical application of his party's ideas.

Traoré believes in permanent dialogue, which he sees as a way to advance reform, contrasting this with what he perceives as the opposition's tendency to flee dialogue and embrace rupture. He cited examples of cohabitation and grand coalitions in France and Germany as models for opposition figures holding responsibilities without abandoning their stance, though critics note these European nations have democratically elected leaders, unlike Togo.

Critics accuse Traoré of ignoring Togo's over 50-year father-son rule and its alleged intolerance for sincere dialogue, pointing to numerous human rights violations including political prisoners, suppression of peaceful protests, and exiles. They argue the Gnassingbé regime uses dialogue frameworks to delay genuine democratic reform, relying on terror and brute force instead of non-violence.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens, particularly those in opposition, are affected by the political strategy of Me Tchassona Traoré, who advocates for engagement with the Gnassingbé regime.

Togo mayor deploys beauty brigade to clear sidewalks

Golfe 5 commune launches a two-week operation to remove goods and structures from priority streets.

Golfe 5 Mayor Kossi Aboka launched a special street clearing operation on November 2, 2026, in Lomé. Instead of bulldozers, a new 'beauty brigade' will enforce government orders to clear priority roads in the Grand Lomé area until November 20.

The brigade, led by the third deputy mayor Koubalo Badjamla, includes local officials, city agents, and security representatives. Their task is to remove goods, containers, stalls, and other obstructions from sidewalks and road edges, a move aimed at improving the city's appearance.

The operation targets items like merchandise, fuel sales, and construction material deposits on public spaces. While the goal is a cleaner commune, the brigade has not yet publicly stated where displaced vendors will relocate.

Why it mattersResidents of Golfe 5 will experience cleaner streets, while vendors may face disruption to their livelihoods.
