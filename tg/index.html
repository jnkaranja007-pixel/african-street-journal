Togo, World Bank agree $1.5 billion partnership

The bank will support agriculture, energy, digital economy and job creation through 2029.

Togo's President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé met with Ousmane Diagana, the World Bank's vice president for West and Central Africa, in Lomé on Monday, November 9, 2026. They discussed strengthening their partnership, focusing on agriculture, energy, the digital economy, and job creation.

Diagana affirmed the World Bank's commitment to supporting Togo's development goals, aiming to create more and better jobs, especially for young people and women. Agriculture is a key priority, with the bank seeking to transform the sector into a driver of economic value and activity, empowering farmers as entrepreneurs.

The partnership, outlined in the 2025-2029 country plan, includes an indicative allocation of $1.5 billion. This framework prioritizes private sector job improvement, human capital development, and inclusive, resilient territorial development. Previous cooperation in July 2026 saw five financing agreements totaling $429 million for transport, energy, and social cohesion projects.

Why it mattersThe World Bank's $1.5 billion partnership aims to boost Togo's economy by creating jobs and improving living conditions.

Two missing after pirogue accident on Lake Togo

17 people were on board the vessel when it capsized on Sunday, November 8, 2026.

A pirogue carrying 17 people capsized on Lake Togo around 12:30 PM on Sunday, November 8, 2026. 15 individuals were rescued, but two remain missing. Search operations are ongoing.

The Ministry of Security confirmed the incident occurred in the Vo prefecture. Firefighters, coast guards, security forces, and local fishermen are involved in the search for the missing passengers. The Minister of Security praised the rescue teams and reminded users of waterways about safety measures.

The minister stressed the importance of wearing life jackets and respecting the maximum capacity of boats. Search efforts continue with the mobilization of various emergency services.

Why it mattersTwo people are unaccounted for following a pirogue accident on Lake Togo, prompting ongoing search efforts.

Togo boosts fuel subsidy by 20 billion FCFA

The government plans to increase fuel subsidies to 34.2 billion FCFA to shield consumers from rising global oil prices.

The Togolese government plans to significantly increase its fuel subsidy budget from 14.2 billion FCFA to 34.2 billion FCFA. This adjustment, part of the 2026 revised budget, aims to limit the impact of higher international oil prices on consumers.

This decision was adopted by the Council of Ministers on October 22. The increase of 20 billion FCFA is intended to absorb some of the increased costs associated with importing and supplying petroleum products, preventing a direct pass-through to pump prices.

The government aims to cushion households and economic actors from the effects of fluctuating global oil markets. Without this financial support, rising supply costs could lead to higher fuel prices at the pump.

Why it mattersTogolese consumers and businesses will be shielded from higher fuel costs due to the increased government subsidy.

Togo party demands halt to evictions

The Nouvel Engagement Togolais calls for operations to stop before deadlines and extend to February 2027.

The Nouvel Engagement Togolais (NET) political party in Togo is protesting current eviction and clearing operations in Lomé. The NET claims some operations began before the deadlines given to occupants had expired, leading to the destruction of kiosks, stalls, and workshops.

Jules A. Amim's NET stated that these actions raise social and legal questions, arguing that affected individuals need time to relocate and preserve their livelihoods. The party issued a statement, number 007/NET/BN/PN, calling for a halt to evictions that proceed before the set deadlines.

The NET is requesting authorities in the Grand Lomé Autonomous District to suspend ongoing eviction and clearing operations. They also demand an extension of these operations until February 1, 2027, and compensation for victims.

Why it mattersThe Nouvel Engagement Togolais is protesting eviction operations in Lomé, seeking a suspension of premature demolitions and an extension until February 2027.
