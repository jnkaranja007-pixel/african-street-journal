Togo appoints military prefects without explanation

The government has appointed military officers to lead several prefectures, sparking calls for transparency.

Togo has appointed military officers to head several prefectures without providing official explanations for the choices.

The newspaper Sika’a suggests these appointments may be linked to the delicate security situation in the sub-region, particularly the threat of terrorism. While military experience can be an asset against terrorism, leading a prefecture also requires administrative, local development, and population engagement skills beyond security concerns.

The government has been urged to account for its decisions, as citizens have a right to understand the motivations and expected outcomes of these appointments, which should prioritize effective governance and citizen well-being.

University of Lomé launches vocational training for students

The University of Lomé's new 'One Student, One Profession' program aims to combat graduate unemployment by offering vocational workshops.

The University of Lomé, led by President Professor Kossivi Hounake, announced the official launch of its "One Student, One Profession" program for the 2026-2027 academic year. This initiative, linked to the government's 2026-2031 roadmap, aims to tackle youth unemployment among graduates and promote entrepreneurship.

However, the program has sparked significant public and academic criticism, with many viewing it as a demeaning intellectual and social downgrade for students. Critics point to overcrowded lecture halls, inadequate teaching equipment, and a shortage of lecturers as pressing issues that need addressing before vocational training is introduced. Students are already struggling to access fundamental knowledge due to these conditions.

The university is proposing workshops in areas like laundry services, soap making, and hairdressing, which critics argue are redundant given existing technical and vocational training centers in Togo. This move is seen by some as a deliberate attempt to lower the ambitions of students, offering them basic subsistence activities as the outcome of higher education.

Why it mattersStudents at the University of Lomé will now spend afternoons in vocational workshops instead of focusing solely on academic studies.

Japan gives Togo 2.4 billion FCFA for road maintenance training

The funds will equip the regional road maintenance training center with new machinery and enhance its operational capabilities.

Togo will receive 2.4 billion FCFA from Japan to boost the capabilities of the Regional Training Center for Road Maintenance (CERFER). An agreement for the grant, equivalent to 615 million Japanese yen, was signed on November 12 in Lomé. This funding will be used to purchase, transport, and install new equipment at the center to improve its teaching, technical, and operational capacities in road maintenance.

Togo's Minister of Finance and Budget, Georges Essowè Barcola, and Japan's Ambassador to Togo, Gomakubo Junji, signed the documents. Minister Barcola stated the agreement reflects a shared desire to strengthen the partnership through actions addressing priority needs. He highlighted the strategic role of road infrastructure in connecting regions, fostering trade, and improving mobility.

The Japanese ambassador announced a future technical cooperation project for road maintenance capacity building in French-speaking West Africa, including expert secondments and training. JICA's Togo representative, Kato Koichi, described the support as an investment in human resources for future infrastructure development. Japan aims to contribute to local skill development and improve connectivity in Togo and the wider West African sub-region.

Why it mattersThe Japanese grant will provide new equipment and training for road maintenance, directly impacting infrastructure development and trade connectivity in Togo and West Africa.

Université de Lomé opens maritime institute in Aného

The institute, known as IHES2M, opened on 10 November with 220 students admitted from more than 400 applicants.

The University of Lomé officially opened the Higher Institute of Higher Studies in Maritime Sciences and Professions (IHES2M) on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, in Aného, in the Lacs 1 commune. The institute, formerly the Institute of Maritime Professions (I2M), trains qualified staff for maritime, port, logistics and industrial work.

The ceremony at the Lycée d'Aného-Ville brought together administrative, municipal, university and government officials. The mayor of Lacs 1, Maître Alexis Koffi Aquereburu, pledged to provide land for the institute's future buildings. The institute's director, Professor Bernard Atchrimi, said two professional bachelor's degrees are offered for 2026-2027: port operations and maritime supply chain management, and maritime risks, safety and insurance.

The institute is temporarily housed at the Lycée d'Aného-Ville until its own buildings are constructed. University of Lomé president Professor Kossivi Hounaké said the opening reflects the university's policy of bringing higher education to the regions, and Minister Delegate for Higher Education and Research Professor Gado Tchangbedji called for skills in logistics, security and marine environmental protection.

Why it mattersYoung Togolese who applied to study port, logistics or maritime risk can now enrol in Aného rather than in Lomé.

Algeria loses two more players to injury before Togo match

The Algerian football federation announced on Thursday, November 12, 2026, that Amin Chiakha and Rafik Belghali are out with injuries.

Algeria's national football team, the Greens, will face Togo without two more players due to injuries sustained in training. Amin Chiakha is out with a tibia injury, and Rafik Belghali has a knee injury, forcing them to miss the upcoming Africa Cup of Nations 2027 qualifiers.

The Algerian Football Federation (FAF) officially confirmed the players' unavailability on Thursday, November 12, 2026. Belghali's absence weakens the defensive balance, while Chiakha's injury reduces attacking options for the coach. Saâdi Radouani, a right-back for USM Alger, has been called up to replace Belghali on the right side of the defense.

These latest absences add to a growing list of unavailable players for Algeria. Before Chiakha and Belghali, four other players had already withdrawn from the squad. Bachir Belloumi, an winger for Hull City, is out for an estimated three weeks with a muscle injury, and left-back Jaouen Hadjam has also withdrawn. Midfielder Hicham Boudaoui of Nice was sent back to his club for treatment, with Adem Zorgane called up as his replacement.

Why it mattersAlgerian players are withdrawing from the national team before their Africa Cup of Nations qualifier against Togo, impacting squad selection.
