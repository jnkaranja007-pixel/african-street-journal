Togo opposition group announces new Lomé rally for 13 December

The CNCC says it will hold the meeting in Lomé or another location still to be specified, after reporting arrests and blocked participants at its Tsévié event.

The National Concertation Framework for Change (CNCC), a grouping of opposition parties and civil society organisations, announced on Friday 13 November 2026 that it will hold a new public meeting on Sunday 13 December 2026. The venue is Lomé or another locality yet to be decided, and the group said the details have not been communicated.

The CNCC, speaking at a press conference in Lomé about its previous rally in Tsévié, said militants were prevented from reaching the venue, notably at the Davié toll gate, and that some were pressured to stay away. The movement reported five arrests and six mobile phones seized, and it denounced administrative requirements for organisers, participant checks and the security deployment as limits on freedom of assembly and expression.

The group also urged President of the Council Faure Gnassingbé to respect fundamental freedoms and the place of popular sovereignty in the institutional order of the Fifth Republic. The December meeting is intended to press those demands on public liberties and political change.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens who want to attend the December 13 meeting will need to confirm the venue, which the CNCC has not yet announced.

Togo elites accused of compromising values for power

Intellectuals and citizens once seen as moral guardians now align with President Faure Gnassingbé's government for personal gain.

In Togo, political engagement is meant to be built on fundamental values like justice and the common good, but the country's political history shows many intellectuals and citizens who once championed freedoms now gravitate towards President Faure Gnassingbé's circles.

These individuals, formerly seen as moral watchdogs, are accused of compromising their convictions for status, sinecures, or protection, embodying a reality where a conscience is sold and integrity is deliberately abandoned.

This intellectual and moral compromise is identified as a central mechanism of an oppressive system that stifles dissent, exploits national wealth, and perpetuates poverty, with the allure of privileges and the fear of losing them driving this alignment.

Why it mattersIntellectuals and citizens are accused of abandoning their integrity for personal gain, contributing to a system that stifles dissent and perpetuates poverty.

University of Lomé opens maritime institute in Aného

The new institute will train qualified professionals in maritime, port, logistics, and industrial fields.

The University of Lomé officially opened the Higher Institute for Maritime Sciences and Professions (IHES2M) on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, in Aného. The institute aims to bring higher education closer to economic realities and regional potential, offering two professional licenses for the 2026-2027 academic year: Port Operations and Maritime Supply Chain Management, and Maritime Risks, Security, and Insurance.

Over 400 applications were received for the first intake, with 220 students selected. The IHES2M, formerly the Institute of Maritime Professions (I2M), is temporarily housed at the Lycée d’Aného-Ville while awaiting its own facilities. The mayor of the Lacs 1 commune pledged municipal support, including land for future infrastructure.

The Minister Delegate for Higher Education and Research, Professor Gado Tchangbedji, highlighted the strategic importance of developing skills for Togo's maritime economy, including logistics, security, and environmental protection. The University of Lomé's president, Professor Kossivi Hounaké, stated this opening reflects the institution's policy of regionalizing its training programs.

Why it mattersStudents seeking careers in maritime, port, logistics, and industrial sectors can now enroll in specialized professional licenses at the new institute in Aného.

Politics · 2026-11-15

Togo president attends Francophonie summit in Cambodia

Faure Gnassingbé joins world leaders in Phnom Penh to discuss peace, stability, and shared prosperity.

Togo's President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé is attending the 20th Summit of the Organisation Internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, which opened on Sunday, November 15, 2026. The summit's theme is "Reinvesting in peace for shared prosperity and lasting stability." Leaders will examine new directions for the Francophone space to promote peace and stability.

Discussions at the summit include strengthening multilateralism, institutional renewal of the OIF, political dialogue, economic cooperation, cultural exchanges, the role of youth in governance and innovation, and developing ties with the Asia-Pacific region. Member states aim to reinforce partnerships around shared values like diversity, inclusion, democracy, and human rights.

Togo intends to renew its commitment to peace and stability, and to valuing its youth's potential through innovative initiatives. The country has contributed to crisis prevention, mediation, and resolution efforts in West Africa and the Sahel. Togo hosts the OIF's first representation in Africa, established in 1982, and supports its influence in West Africa.

Why it mattersPresident Faure Gnassingbé is participating in international discussions on peace and stability, with Togo aiming to renew its commitment to these values.

Sport · 2026-11-15

Algeria beat Togo 1-0 in CAN 2027 qualifier in Tizi Ouzou

Defender Ramy Bensebaïni headed the only goal in the 76th minute, from a cross by Hadj Moussa, as Algeria took three points in front of their own fans.

Togo's national team, the Sparrowhawks, lost 1-0 to Algeria on Saturday, 14 November 2026, in the third round of qualifying for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. The match was played at the Hocine-Aït-Ahmed stadium in Tizi Ouzou.

The Togolese side held firm for much of the game before Algerian defender Ramy Bensebaïni headed in the only goal in the 76th minute, from a cross by Hadj Moussa. Algeria took the three points in front of their own fans, while the result left Togo's players with a bitter taste, despite a solid organisation that kept the match close for most of the game.

Algeria now lead Group I of the qualifiers, RFI Afrique reported. The Sparrowhawks must turn the page quickly and prepare for the next stage of their qualifying campaign, with their chances of reaching the tournament still at stake.

Why it mattersTogo must now recover from this defeat and prepare for the next qualifying matches, with its chances at the 2027 tournament still at stake.
