Sport · 2026-11-06

Togo football body demands apology from coach

The MMLK movement wants coach Patrice Neveu to apologize to a journalist within 48 hours.

The Movement Martin Luther King (MMLK) condemned the behavior of Togolese national football team coach Patrice Neveu towards journalist Espoir Gato of Nana FM. The incident occurred during a press conference on November 3 at Kégué stadium in Lomé.

MMLK, led by Pastor Edoh K. Komi, stated that no journalist should be intimidated for asking questions, calling such actions a destabilization attempt. The organization demands a public and personal apology from Neveu within 48 hours to the journalist, the Togolese press, and the Togolese people.

The movement also asked the Togolese Football Federation (FTF) to ensure the safety of media professionals at press conferences. While acknowledging the FTF president's regrets, MMLK insists Neveu must offer his own apology, emphasizing that dignity and respect are universal.

Why it mattersThe MMLK movement is demanding an apology from the national football coach for his treatment of a journalist, affecting the dignity of media professionals.

Health · 2026-11-06

Togo pharmacists warn of TikTok abortion drug sales

Online accounts openly promote and sell abortion-related products, bypassing strict medical regulations.

Pharmacists in Togo are raising alarms about TikTok accounts openly promoting and selling abortion-related products. These sales bypass the strict medical regulations and oversight required for such sensitive medications.

A local pharmacist expressed shock after witnessing a live TikTok broadcast advertising abortion products, with the seller explaining their usage. When questioned about legal authorization, the seller blocked the pharmacist. The purchase of these specific medications is tightly controlled even within pharmacies.

This issue extends beyond Togo, as a similar TikTok account in neighboring Benin, named "Avortement 2290," led to police intervention and account closure in 2025. Operators were arrested for offering abortifacient drugs without medical qualifications or legal permission.

Why it mattersPeople in Togo seeking abortion-related medications are at risk of obtaining unregulated and potentially dangerous products through social media platforms.

The Togolese Consumers Association (ATC) urges caution and calls for repairs to faulty traffic signals.

Business · 2026-11-06

Togo health insurance branch loses 106 billion CFA francs

African health insurers met in Lomé to address a sector growing at 10% annually but facing significant financial losses.

Africa's health insurance sector, despite growing by about 10% annually since 2014, is facing substantial deficits. The first African health insurance symposium, held in Lomé on November 4 and 5, revealed that the CIMA zone accumulated an operating deficit of 106 billion CFA francs between 2020 and 2024.

The symposium, organized by FANAF and APSAT, brought together health ministers, insurance directors, and industry leaders. Participants identified causes for the losses including insufficient data for pricing, price wars, unapplied adjustment clauses, poorly managed direct payment systems, medical overconsumption, and weak controls. Mamadou Koné, president of FANAF, questioned the sustainability of promising protection that is financed at a loss.

To address these issues, a roadmap for 2027-2030 is being developed. The Togolese Minister of Health highlighted the struggle of low-income citizens who must choose between food and medical care, emphasizing the need for insurers to innovate with affordable, accessible products that cover essential needs. The minister stressed that inclusion in insurance is not charity but requires adaptation to real needs.

Why it mattersTogo's health insurance sector is experiencing significant financial losses, impacting the ability to provide affordable coverage for citizens.

Education · 2026-11-06

Lokossa school denies viral video rumour

CEG 3 administration states no student by the name circulated is enrolled at the institution.

The administration of CEG 3 in Lokossa, Benin, has formally denied rumours circulating on social media about a private video allegedly involving one of its students. The school stated that the name mentioned in the viral posts does not appear in their student registers.

School officials issued a statement to counter the widespread online speculation, emphasizing that no such incident has occurred within the college premises. This denial aims to protect the reputation of the institution, its learners, and its staff from unverified information.

The CEG 3 management urged internet users to exercise caution and verify information before sharing it online. The school is located in Lokossa, Benin, and the rumour spread widely on social media platforms.

Why it mattersStudents and staff at CEG 3 in Lokossa, Benin, face reputational damage from unverified social media rumours.
