French filmmaker released for hospital care in Togo, passport confiscated

Sebastian Perez Pezzani, a French filmmaker arrested in Togo, has been released under judicial supervision to receive medical treatment, while his passport has been seized.

Sebastian Perez Pezzani, one of two French filmmakers arrested in Togo on July twenty-seventh, is now receiving medical care in a hospital after being released under judicial supervision. His lawyer, Martial Akakpo, confirmed the release and the confiscation of his passport. Pezzani, along with Gaël Mocaër and Togolese collaborator Fred Vedomey, was arrested in northern Togo while filming for a documentary series.

The three men face charges of "false declarations," with Togolese authorities citing "serious evidence" against them. Their production company maintains they were in compliance, and their lawyer states they deny the accusations. Gaël Mocaër and Fred Vedomey remain incarcerated in Lomé after their request for provisional release was denied.

Reporters Without Borders previously expressed concern over Sebastian Perez Pezzani's "serious health problems" and had called for his urgent medical evacuation to France, as well as the release of his colleagues. Togolese authorities also mentioned "several irregularities in the preparation and execution" of their mission, specifically regarding the use of a drone in the northern region, which is experiencing recurrent jihadist attacks.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects the individuals involved, their families, and the organizations they work for, impacting their freedom and health.

Togo Opposition Demands Political Transition After Court Ruling

A coalition of opposition parties and civil society organizations in Togo has called for a political transition, citing a 2024 ECOWAS Court of Justice ruling on constitutional.

Several Togolese opposition parties, political groups, and civil society organizations convened on Tuesday, August 25, 2026, at the ADDI headquarters in Lomé to present a joint memorandum. This document demands a political transition in Togo, bringing together key components of the CNCC and the DMP, alongside organizations such as the LTDH, ASVITTO, and CODITOGO.

The coalition bases its demand on a ruling by the ECOWAS Court of Justice concerning the 2024 constitutional changes. According to the signatories, this decision invalidates the constitutional reform and its resulting institutions, necessitating a return to the previous constitutional order.

The opposition is calling for a political transition to implement institutional reforms, restore the rule of law, address social and security emergencies, release political detainees, and facilitate the return of exiles. They have also urged ECOWAS, the African Union, the European Union, the United States, and the United Nations to support Togo in this process and encouraged citizens to mobilize to restore constitutional order.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are directly affected by the call for political transition, which aims to address social and security issues, and potentially change the governance structure of the country.

SYAPAL secures re-employment and social benefits for Lomé port workers

The Autonomous Port of Lomé management committed to re-employing dismissed dockers and implementing several social measures following discussions with the SYAPAL union.

The SYAPAL union announced significant progress after meeting with the Director General of the Autonomous Port of Lomé on August 18, 2026. This includes the re-employment of all dockers who were not renewed, with activities resuming by August 24, 2026. The meeting followed a request from a collective of non-renewed dockers on August 17, 2026, after a strike.

The port's Director General also committed to addressing 12 union demands. These include distributing face masks to mineral quay workers from August 19, 2026, providing a box of Peak milk per worker per shift, and constructing sanitary blocks for staff. Additionally, within one month, the port will arrange worker transport from assembly points to the quay and back, and cover one meal per shift.

Further commitments include the upcoming establishment of a modern canteen. SYAPAL, affiliated with the National Union of Independent Trade Unions of Togo (UNSIT), thanked the port management for these measures and reaffirmed its readiness for social dialogue to protect worker interests and ensure the port's smooth operation and national economic benefit.

Why it mattersPort workers, including those previously dismissed, will directly benefit from re-employment, improved working conditions, and new social provisions like meals and transport.

Mgr Edmond Amékusé joins Togo's Episcopal Conference

Mgr Edmond Amékusé will be ordained on August 29, 2026, becoming a full member of the Episcopal Conference of Togo (CET) and the youngest member of the body.

Mgr Edmond Amékusé, appointed by Pope Leo XIV on June 26, 2026, will be ordained on August 29, 2026. This ordination will integrate him into the college of successors of the Apostles and make him a full member of the Episcopal Conference of Togo (CET). He will also take charge of the Kpalimé diocese, succeeding Mgr Benoît Alowonou.

Amékusé is the first bishop in the CET to come from the Grand Séminaire Saint Jean Paul II in Lomé. His ordination is expected to draw many attendees, including bishops and priests from various locations, as well as former students from the Grand Séminaire Saint Jean Paul II and the Petit Séminaire Saint Pie X d’Agoe, including those from his 1979-1980 promotion.

The event will see participants traveling from countries such as Burkina Faso, Chad, France, Belgium, Canada, Switzerland, Gabon, and the USA. Mgr Amékusé is noted as the youngest member of the CET, bringing new leadership to the conference and the Kpalimé diocese.

Why it mattersThe appointment and ordination of Mgr Edmond Amékusé directly affects the Catholic community in Togo by changing the leadership of the Kpalimé diocese and the composition of the Episcopal Conference.

Gabon President Nguema warns developers over abandoned construction sites

President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema issued a stern message to property owners and developers with unfinished projects, threatening state requisition for public utility if work.

Gabon's President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema announced on Sunday, August sixteen, 2026, that the government will no longer tolerate abandoned construction sites, particularly those along major roads in the capital. He stated that owners have obligations to the community and must complete work within set deadlines, or the state will take responsibility.

The President indicated that the government would consider normative measures, including the requisition of sites for public utility, in strict adherence to the Constitution and property rights. This follows his inspections of construction sites, including one on June thirty, 2026, at the national memory museum, where he publicly criticized contractors for delays.

In a related development, architect Eric Moro Nguema was detained on August fourteen, 2026, in Libreville for significant delays and non-compliance with deadlines on a strategic public project he was committed to delivering before national holiday celebrations on August seventeen. Meanwhile, in Togo, abandoned road repair projects in areas like Bè, Akodessewa, Ahligo, Wetekome, and Ablogame have persisted for over four years, leading to serious consequences, including loss of life.

Why it mattersProperty owners and developers in Gabon face potential state intervention and requisition of their unfinished projects, while residents in Togo continue to suffer from abandoned infrastructure projects.
