Togo Adopts New Forest Code and Investment Agency Decree

The Council of Ministers approved a revised forest code aimed at sustainable resource management and a decree reorganizing the Investment Promotion and Free Zone Agency.

Togo's Council of Ministers convened on Wednesday, August 26, 2026, adopting a new forest code designed to strengthen legal frameworks for natural resource management, climate change adaptation, and biodiversity preservation. The revised code emphasizes local governance, integrates the rights and interests of riparian communities, and promotes agroforestry and private investment. It also aims to reduce pressure on forest resources by encouraging private sector involvement and energy transition initiatives.

The new forest code introduces significant changes, including redefined national forest domains and enhanced participation of local authorities in their management. It ensures consultation with local populations and acknowledges their legitimate rights and uses, particularly in forest classification procedures. The code also establishes principles for accessing genetic resources and sharing benefits equitably, while bolstering forest monitoring and combating illegal activities like poaching and wildlife trafficking.

In addition to the forest code, the Council approved a decree detailing the roles, organization, and functioning of the Agency for the Promotion of Investment and the Free Zone (API-ZF). This move is intended to streamline investment processes and further integrate Togo into global economic activities, aligning with national policies for reforestation and protected area management.

Why it mattersThe new forest code affects farmers by incorporating their rights and promoting agroforestry, while the API-ZF decree may attract private investment relevant to businesses.

ECOWAS Officials Discuss Abidjan-Lagos Corridor Obstacles

Despite protocols on free movement, traders face administrative hurdles and lack of information along the vital economic route connecting five West African nations.

Regional officials met at the Togo-Ghana border to assess progress on the Abidjan-Lagos corridor, a key economic artery for the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS). The dialogue aimed to address persistent administrative delays, harassment, and insufficient information that hinder the free movement of people and goods, particularly for women and young traders.

Damtien Tchintchibidja, Vice-President of the ECOWAS Commission, highlighted the corridor's importance in fostering regional trade and embodying the vision of an integrated, borderless West Africa. While protocols like the 1979 free movement agreement and the common external tariff exist, ground-level realities show significant discrepancies between policy and practice.

Discussions involved border agents, customs officials, and merchant associations reviewing tools such as biometric identity cards and joint border posts to improve passage. However, participants noted that current efforts fall short of translating policy into tangible improvements, with the 1979 protocol remaining largely aspirational for many.

Why it mattersTraders along the Abidjan-Lagos corridor are affected by administrative hurdles and lack of information, despite ECOWAS protocols aimed at facilitating free movement.

MMLK Demands Government Action on Soaring Lomé Rents

The MMLK is calling on the Togolese government to urgently address rising housing costs and illegal deposit demands in Lomé, citing widespread distress among residents.

The Mouvement Martin Luther King (MMLK) has formally appealed to the Togolese government to intervene in the escalating rental market of Lomé and its surrounding areas. The organization, acting as a voice for the voiceless, highlighted that securing housing in the capital has become a significant challenge for many citizens. This appeal follows reports of unchecked rent increases and violations of existing laws regarding rental deposits.

Despite legislation capping rental guarantees, the MMLK states that landlords are routinely demanding deposits equivalent to six, eight, or even twelve months' rent, with some cases reaching two years. Rents are reportedly increasing without justification or oversight, making it impossible for ordinary citizens, public servants, students, and families to afford housing. This situation is leading to threats of eviction and widespread precarity.

The MMLK argues that the current situation, where laws are not enforced and rent costs exceed salaries, creates significant hardship, despair, and injustice. They assert that this lack of affordable and accessible housing endangers the peace and dignity of Togolese families. The organization is urging the government to take immediate action to ensure dignified and accessible housing for all populations.

Why it mattersThe MMLK's appeal highlights that rising rents and illegal deposit demands are making housing unaffordable for ordinary citizens, public servants, students, and families in Lomé, threatening their stability and dignity.

Ivorian Police Arrest Togolese Miners

Ivory Coast authorities have intensified operations against illegal gold mining, leading to the arrest of several foreign nationals, including Togolese citizens.

Ivorian security forces have launched an intensified crackdown on illegal gold mining operations across the country since August 22, targeting four key sites. During these operations in Zaranou, the Special Brigade for Surveillance and Intervention (BSSI) apprehended five individuals suspected of clandestine mining, three of whom were Togolese nationals and two from Burkina Faso. All arrested individuals have been transferred to judicial authorities for further proceedings.

The anti-illegal mining offensive has also resulted in the seizure or destruction of hundreds of pieces of equipment. These items include motor pumps, crushers, washing plants, mats, connectors, makeshift shelters, motorcycles, and gas cylinders, all used in the illicit extraction of gold. The authorities aim to curb illegal mining and its associated environmental damage and clandestine activities.

These interventions by Ivorian authorities are part of a broader effort to strengthen the fight against illegal gold mining. The operations target not only the miners but also the infrastructure supporting their activities, aiming to dismantle the networks involved in this illicit trade and mitigate its negative impacts on the environment and local communities.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens involved in illegal gold mining in Ivory Coast face arrest and judicial proceedings, impacting their livelihoods and freedom.
