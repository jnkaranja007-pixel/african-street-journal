Ex-Minister Gnakadé Held for Nearly a Year Awaiting Trial

Former Togolese Minister of the Armed Forces Marguerite Gnakadé faces charges of inciting uprising and remains in detention as her trial date is yet to be set.

Essossimna Marguerite Gnakadé, who served as the former Minister of the Armed Forces under Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé, has been in detention for nearly a year. She was arrested on September 17, 2025, at her Lomé residence following public statements critical of the government. Four days later, she was formally charged and placed under a detention order.

The Togolese justice system is prosecuting Gnakadé for alleged "incitement to uprising against the authority." Despite the charges and her prolonged detention since September 2025, the date for her trial has not yet been scheduled. Information regarding the specific location and conditions of her confinement has not been detailed.

Gnakadé's arrest and ongoing detention follow a period of public opposition to the current administration. Her case highlights a legal process that has yet to reach a trial phase, leaving her status uncertain for almost twelve months since her initial apprehension.

Why it mattersFormer Minister Marguerite Gnakadé is affected by the Togolese justice system, which has charged her with inciting uprising and has not yet set a trial date, leading to her prolonged detention.

Police Arrest Suspect in August Motorcycle Theft Attempt

Togolese police apprehended a 20-year-old man in connection with a failed motorcycle robbery in Lankouvi, while two accomplices remain at large.

A 20-year-old man has been arrested by Togolese national police in connection with a motorcycle theft attempt that occurred on August 26 in Lankouvi. The suspect, identified as D. Sodjiné, a mason residing in Ghana, was apprehended on the same day as the incident. Two other individuals involved in the attempted robbery are currently being sought by authorities.

The incident involved three individuals who, armed with a machete, attempted to steal a BAJAJ 125 BM motorcycle from its owner, A. Kodjo, a carpenter. The attempted theft took place around 3:12 AM. The victim was reportedly saved by the timely arrival of a police patrol unit, which caused the assailants to abandon the motorcycle and flee towards Ghana.

During their escape, the suspects dropped a mobile phone, which was recovered by the police. Analysis of the phone led investigators to Sodjiné. The police have stated their commitment to combating crime and have urged the public to remain vigilant and cooperate with security forces by reporting suspicious activities.

Why it mattersThe arrest of a suspect in a motorcycle theft attempt affects the general public by potentially increasing safety and security in the area.

Guin People Celebrate 363rd Kpesoso Ritual

The annual Kpesoso ritual in Glidji marks the sacred stone-taking ceremony and the Guin New Year, fostering unity among diverse clans.

The Guin people of Glidji, Togo, marked the 363rd anniversary of the Kpesoso ritual on Thursday, September 3, 2026. This sacred ceremony, involving the taking of the Sacred Stone, is a central event in the historical capital of Guinyigban. While historians note a discrepancy between the mythical date of 1663 and the historical date of 1680 for the migration of the Gan people, the ritual's importance in Guin religion remains paramount.

The Kpesoso ritual, unique to the Guin in Togo and absent among the Gan in Ghana, is believed to have originated from the traumatic split of the Gan people following a war with the Akwamu. This division led to the formation of the modern Guin people, comprising various clans including Fanti, Anlo, and Afro-Brazilians, united under new leadership and foundational myths.

The ritual, culminating in an oracle that foretells the new year's events, serves as a political and religious tool for social cohesion. It brings together the population and the Vodou priests of 41 official deities, reinforcing national unity and potentially consolidating power against external threats. The following Sunday, the Guin New Year is celebrated with the sharing of 'yekê yekê', a national couscous dish, symbolizing reconciliation and abundance.

Why it mattersThe Kpesoso ritual affects the Guin people by fostering unity and a shared identity through religious and political ceremonies.

French filmmakers leave Togo after provisional release

Two French documentary filmmakers, arrested in July for alleged false declarations, have been permitted to depart Togo.

French filmmakers Gaël Mocaër and Sebastian Perez Pezzani, who were detained in Togo since July 27 while filming a documentary for France Télévisions, have left the country. They were granted provisional liberty and an authorization to exit Togolese territory. A diplomatic source reported that the filmmakers were expected to arrive back in France on Thursday morning.

The French Ministry of Foreign Affairs confirmed on September 1 that the decision for provisional release was communicated, expressing relief for the families and the ministry's engagement in the matter. The filmmakers, along with their Togolese collaborator Fred Vedomey, were initially arrested in northern Togo. They were subsequently held in Lomé on charges of making false declarations, which they deny, according to their lawyer.

Togolese authorities cited several procedural irregularities, including a lack of necessary permits and the unauthorized use of a drone in a sensitive northern region. This area has experienced jihadist incursions since 2021. Sebastian Perez Pezzani, who had been ill, was previously released in late August for medical treatment, with his passport confiscated at the time.

Why it mattersThe filmmakers were affected by being detained and charged with false declarations, leading to their provisional release and departure from Togo.

Opposition Parties Announce Public Conference on Sociopolitical Situation

The National Framework for Consultation for Change will hold a public conference on September 4 in Lomé to discuss the nation's sociopolitical landscape.

Several opposition parties under the National Framework for Consultation for Change (CNCC) are organizing a public conference on September 4 in Lomé. The meeting, initiated by the National Alliance for Change (ANC), Alliance of Democrats for Integral Development (ADDI), Democratic Forces for the Republic (FDR), and the Socialist Pact for Renewal (PSR), aims to address the country's sociopolitical situation. The Ministry of Territorial Administration, Local Governance and Customary Affairs was notified of the event via a letter submitted on August 31, 2026.

The conference is scheduled to take place at 8:30 AM in the MUGET conference hall in Djidjolé. This gathering follows a recent formalization of synergy between the two main opposition blocs, marked by a joint memorandum on August 25 proposing a way out of the political crisis. The momentum was also boosted by a ruling from the ECOWAS Court of Justice, which rejected the constitutional revision adopted in 2024.

Participants in the CNCC conference intend to use the platform to share their perspectives on current events and to continue mobilizing support around institutional issues that have divided Togolese politicians for months. The opposition parties aim to present their analysis of the political climate and foster broader discussion on these divisive matters.

Why it mattersThe opposition parties are affected by institutional issues that have divided Togolese politicians for months and are using this conference to mobilize support.
