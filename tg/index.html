Togo elites accused of compromising values for power

Intellectuals and civil society members are reportedly gravitating towards the ruling power, sacrificing integrity for status.

In Togo, political engagement and intellectual or citizen commitment are expected to be founded on core values like justice, equity, and the common good. However, the country's political history shows many intellectuals, academics, and civil society figures, once seen as defenders of freedom, are now aligning with President Faure Gnassingbé's circles through gradual compromise.

These individuals are described as embodying venality by subordinating their convictions to gain status, sinecures, or protection. This compromise represents a surrendered conscience, a perverted intelligence, and a deliberate abandonment of integrity, which critics argue is a central mechanism for an oppressive system that stifles dissent and plunders national wealth.

The alignment with the ruling power rarely stems from spontaneous ideological agreement but rather from a process of moral erosion. This is driven by the allure of privileges and the fear of losing status or returning to anonymity, especially in a context of scarce opportunities and widespread unemployment, where proximity to power guarantees material advantages.

Why it mattersIntellectuals and civil society members in Togo are reportedly compromising their values for personal gain, contributing to a system that stifles dissent and economic well-being.

Togo president attends Francophonie summit in Cambodia

Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé joined other leaders in Phnom Penh for the 20th Francophonie Summit, focusing on peace and dialogue.

Togo's President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé attended the 20th Francophonie Summit in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, on Sunday, November 15, 2026. Leaders from the French-speaking world gathered to discuss peace, dialogue, solidarity, and partnership.

The summit's opening ceremony was presided over by the King of Cambodia, Norodom Sihamoni, and included leaders such as French President Emmanuel Macron and Cambodian Prime Minister Hun Manet. A key moment was the official handover of the summit's presidency from France to Cambodia.

Discussions at the summit focused on preventing conflict, strengthening cooperation, promoting cultural diversity, and addressing youth employment. Leaders also held closed-door consultations on the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie's (OIF) activities and strategic orientations.

Why it mattersTogo's president participated in high-level discussions on peace and cooperation within the Francophonie space.

Algeria beat Togo 1-0 in CAN 2027 qualifier in Tizi Ouzou

Ramy Bensebaïni's header in the 76th minute decided the match at the Hocine-Aït-Ahmed stadium on Saturday, 14 November 2026.

Togo's national team, the Sparrowhawks, lost 1-0 to Algeria on Saturday, 14 November 2026, in the third round of qualifying for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. The match was played at the Hocine-Aït-Ahmed stadium in Tizi Ouzou.

The Togolese side held firm for much of the game before Algerian defender Ramy Bensebaïni headed in the only goal in the 76th minute, from a cross by Hadj Moussa. Algeria took the three points in front of their own fans, while the result left Togo's players with a bitter taste, despite a solid organisation that kept the match close for most of the game.

Algeria now lead Group I of the qualifiers, RFI Afrique reported. The Sparrowhawks must turn the page quickly and prepare for the next stage of their qualifying campaign, with their chances of reaching the tournament still at stake.

Why it mattersTogo must now recover from this defeat and prepare for the next qualifying matches, with its chances at the 2027 tournament still at stake.
