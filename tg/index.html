Kara University Trains Staff on Corruption Prevention

The University of Kara hosted a training session aimed at embedding anti-corruption principles and ethical conduct into Togolese university curricula.

The University of Kara recently hosted a training session organized by HAPLUCIA for its teacher-researchers. This initiative focuses on integrating corruption prevention, ethics, deontology, responsibility, and good governance into university programs across Togo.

HAPLUCIA's goal is to empower teacher-researchers to instill values of integrity and transparency in students. The program seeks to position universities as central players in combating corruption by fostering responsible citizenship and a culture of integrity within higher education.

This effort aims to cultivate a generation of students who understand and uphold ethical standards, contributing to a more transparent and accountable society in Togo. The training emphasizes the university's role in shaping future leaders committed to good governance.

Why it mattersTeacher-researchers at the University of Kara are affected by training that integrates corruption prevention and ethical conduct into their teaching programs.

Fire Destroys Lomé Shops, Emergency Response Questioned

Flames engulfed multiple businesses in the Tokoin Ramco neighborhood, prompting a community response amid reports of delayed emergency services.

An intense fire broke out Monday in Lomé's Tokoin Ramco district, directly opposite the Bomaco bakery, destroying several shops and causing significant material damage. Local residents joined firefighters in battling the blaze, but difficulties were reported in reaching emergency services via the 118 emergency number.

Gendarmerie officers were present to secure the area and potentially investigate the fire's origin. The exact cause of the fire and the total value of the losses remain unknown at this time. This incident highlights ongoing challenges in accessing emergency services during disasters.

The fire's origin and the full extent of the financial losses are still under investigation. The event underscores the critical need for reliable and swift emergency response systems in urban areas.

Why it mattersShop owners and local businesses are affected by the destruction of their commercial spaces and inventory.

Togo, China Sign 13 Deals on Debt, Soy, and Trade

Togo and China signed thirteen accords in Beijing to boost cooperation in finance, agriculture, and trade.

Togo and China have signed thirteen agreements and memorandums of understanding aimed at strengthening bilateral cooperation. The signing ceremony in Beijing was presided over by Togolese President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé and Chinese Premier Li Qiang. These new instruments cover multiple sectors including finance, economy, security, trade, agriculture, training, media, and decentralized cooperation, following the elevation of Sino-Togolese relations to a "Community of Shared Future for China and Togo in the New Era."

A significant outcome of the financial cooperation is the partial cancellation of Togo's public debt, which will alleviate the country's financial obligations. In terms of economic and commercial exchanges, the agreements include a protocol on the Authorized Economic Operators (AEO) program between the Togolese Revenue Office (OTR) and China's General Administration of Customs. This aims to streamline trade, enhance trust between customs administrations, and facilitate import and export operations.

Agricultural cooperation will see enhanced access for Togolese soybeans to the Chinese market through a protocol on phytosanitary requirements. This agreement is expected to boost agricultural trade and promote Togolese produce on international markets. Both nations also committed to strengthening collaboration in industrial sectors, supply chains, digital trade, international economic and trade cooperation, and green development, fostering modernization and sustainable practices.

Why it mattersTogo's financial obligations are reduced and its agricultural exports to China are facilitated.

Togolese Opposition Fractures Amidst Personal Agendas

Internal divisions and alleged financial incentives continue to undermine unified opposition efforts against the ruling regime.

The Togolese opposition faces persistent challenges due to internal rivalries, personal ambitions, and alleged financial dealings, hindering unified action against the Gnassingbé regime. A past agreement in May 2010 between Gilchrist Olympio and Faure Gnassingbé, following Eyadema's death, is cited as a betrayal, with claims of secret financial clauses and indemnities for the Olympio family. This event is seen as a significant factor in the ongoing disunity within opposition ranks.

Despite recent efforts to unite, such as a memorandum presented on August 25, 2026, by the Manifeste Génération Togo (MGT) and the Cadre National de Concertation pour le Changement (CNCC), concrete progress remains elusive. Disagreements over leadership and personal egos reportedly obstruct the formation of a common front, preventing joint actions like coordinated demonstrations. The Alliance Nationale pour le Changement (ANC) has also expressed a willingness to participate in elections under the current regime, further complicating unity.

The communication manager for the ANC, Éric Dupuy, indicated that his party might participate in elections, a stance that contrasts with the broader opposition's goal of demanding a return to the 1992 constitution and a political transition. This divergence highlights the deep-seated issues of personal interests and a lack of cohesive strategy that plague the opposition, preventing them from presenting a united front against a long-standing dictatorial rule.

Why it mattersThe Togolese opposition's internal divisions and personal agendas prevent a unified front, impacting the country's political landscape.

Politics · 2026-10-21

Togo Leaders' Inaction Leaves Citizens Unheard

The president travels abroad while an exiled opposition leader publishes extensively, creating a political stalemate.

Togolese citizens face hardship with insufficient salaries, under-equipped hospitals, and educated youth seeking to emigrate. The nation's current leadership is described as detached, with the president reportedly traveling extensively, including to Mongolia, while the country struggles. This perceived indifference is seen as a system that marginalizes the governed.

Meanwhile, an opposition figure based in Switzerland issues daily statements and writings, which are characterized as a substitute for action rather than a catalyst for change. This prolific output of text, including social media activity, is criticized for being disconnected from the practical needs of the population, who desire organization and shared risk over eloquent pronouncements.

This dual inaction, one from the governing elite through detachment and the other from the opposition through excessive rhetoric and distance, leaves the populace without effective government or resistance. The disconnect between these leaders and the daily realities of Togolese citizens, such as the cost of food and healthcare access, is identified as a significant political ailment.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are affected by a lack of effective governance and resistance due to the perceived detachment and inaction of their leaders.
