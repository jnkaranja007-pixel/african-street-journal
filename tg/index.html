Togo party welcomes ECOWAS court letter on constitutional order

The Santé du Peuple party urged a swift return to the 1992 constitution, rejecting academic discussions.

The Santé du Peuple party in Togo, led by Dr Georges WILLIAM-KOUESSAN, has welcomed a letter sent on October 23, 2026, by the ECOWAS Court of Justice to the Togolese Minister of Justice. This letter demands the execution of the court's January 29, 2026 ruling, which declared unconstitutional a constitutional change made two years prior.

In a statement dated October 30, 2026, the party expressed satisfaction with the ECOWAS court's action. They are calling on Togolese authorities for a "rapid and peaceful" return to the 1992 constitutional order. The party also stated that academic discussions cannot resolve the current crisis.

The Santé du Peuple party's reaction follows the ECOWAS court's demand for the enforcement of its January 29, 2026, ruling. The party believes this ruling invalidates the constitutional changes implemented in Togo over the past two years.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are affected by the call for a return to the 1992 constitutional order and the rejection of recent constitutional changes.

Togo journalist's judicial control challenged

The Union of African Free Press Journalists demands an end to restrictions on veteran reporter Apollinaire Mèwènèmesse K.

The Union of African Free Press Journalists (UJPLA) is calling on Togolese judicial authorities to lift the judicial control placed on journalist Apollinaire Mèwènèmesse K. The 73-year-old director of La Dépêche has been subject to these restrictions for over two and a half years following an article published in February 2024.

UJPLA stated that the prolonged measures disproportionately affect the journalist's individual liberty and professional activity. The union emphasized that while journalists are not above the law, judicial proceedings against them must uphold fairness, proportionality, and the presumption of innocence.

The organization urged the courts to re-examine the case and lift the judicial control unless a legal and proportionate necessity justifies its continuation. UJPLA highlighted the importance of protecting press freedom and the public's right to information.

Why it mattersVeteran Togolese journalist Apollinaire Mèwènèmesse K. faces ongoing judicial restrictions impacting his freedom of movement and professional work.

African consumers urge zero added sugar for young children

The African Consumers Organisation (OAC) is pushing for new international standards to remove added sugars from infant foods.

The African Consumers Organisation (OAC) is urging African nations to support a goal of zero added sugar in foods for children aged 6 to 36 months. This call comes as the 45th session of the Codex Committee on Nutrition approaches. The OAC, representing 50 consumer groups across 39 African countries, wants to protect children's health from an early age.

An investigation by Public Eye found that Cerelac infant cereals, popular in Africa, contain nearly six grams of added sugar per serving, unlike European versions. The OAC asked Nestlé on June 25, 2026, to remove added sugar from its African products. Nestlé responded on July 20, 2026, stating its products meet national regulations and Codex Standard CXS 74-1981, and that it now offers versions with and without added sugar.

The current Codex standard allows up to 20% added sugar in infant cereals, equivalent to about 10 grams per portion. The World Health Organization warns that early sugar exposure can lead to a lasting preference for sweet foods and obesity, which is rising alarmingly in Africa. The OAC is advocating for changes at the standards level to ensure child protection.

Why it mattersAfrican children are at risk of developing a preference for sweet foods and facing health issues like obesity and diabetes due to added sugars in infant foods.

Lokossa school denies intimate video rumour

CEG 3 administration states the student named in viral social media posts is not enrolled.

The administration of CEG 3 in Lokossa, Benin, has formally denied rumours circulating on social media about an intimate video allegedly involving a student. The school's management stated that the name mentioned in the viral posts does not appear in their student registration records.

School officials confirmed that no such incident has occurred within the college premises. This denial aims to protect the reputation of the institution, its students, and its staff from unverified information.

The CEG 3 management urged internet users to exercise caution and verify information shared on social media platforms before distributing it.

Why it mattersStudents and staff at CEG 3 in Lokossa, Benin, face reputational damage from unverified social media rumours.

Lycée Adidogomé 1 beats Lycée Zébévi in Génie Imhotep

Adidogomé 1 scored 410 points to defeat Zébévi's 370 in the third season of the 'Génie Imhotep' quiz competition.

Lycée Adidogomé 1 defeated Lycée Zébévi on Saturday, October 31, 2026, in the fourth round of the third season of the 'Génie Imhotep' quiz competition. The match took place at the Prof James studio of SOS Docteur TV in Lomé.

Students from Lycée Zébévi, located in the Lacs prefecture, scored 370 points, while their counterparts from Lycée Adidogomé 1, from the Grand Lomé region, achieved 410 points. This loss follows a narrow victory for Zébévi against Tokoin 2 the previous week.

This third edition of the competition, supported by the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) and promoted by Professor Serge Michel Kodom of Aimes-Afrique, now includes six schools from the Maritime region alongside six from Lomé. The next match will feature Lycée Adidogomé 1 against Lycée Attitogon.

Why it mattersStudents in Togo are participating in an educational quiz competition that tests their knowledge.
