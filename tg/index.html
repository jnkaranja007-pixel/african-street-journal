Wycliffe-Togo Seeks Local Church Support for Bible Translation

The organization faces declining external funding and calls for increased local engagement to continue its mission of translating scriptures into indigenous languages.

Wycliffe-Togo held its ordinary general assembly in Lomé on August 29, 2026, focusing on the challenges of translating the Bible into local languages, mobilizing resources, and strengthening the commitment of the local church. The organization highlighted that this work requires significant linguistic, cultural, technological, human, and financial resources. Sépényon Komla Egou, President of the Board, described Bible translation as a demanding, long-term endeavor.

The importance of using mother tongues for biblical understanding was emphasized by Egou and Samuel Tinnidjotobe Kpagheri, Wycliffe-Togo's Executive Director. They stated that messages are more directly and deeply understood when conveyed in everyday language, fostering a sense of comfort and eliminating the need for dictionaries. Projects are underway for Lama, Kabyè, Ifè, and Moba, with the Akébou New Testament taking 17 years to complete and the Kabyè Old Testament translation ongoing since 2005.

Facing a reduction in funding from external partners, Wycliffe-Togo is appealing for greater involvement from the local church, missionary organizations, and Christians. Missiologist Pr Nouwodé Kwassi Adjé stressed that the church's mission is integral to its purpose and future, warning that neglecting it leads to a loss of vocation. Christians are urged to unite their efforts to sustainably support this missionary work as a believing community.

Why it mattersWycliffe-Togo is seeking increased financial and human resources from the local church and Christians to continue its Bible translation projects due to declining external funding.

Togo Detains Local Producer After Foreign Filmmakers Arrested

Komi Mawufemo Vedomey, a Togolese producer who secured official filming permits, remains in custody while French filmmakers he worked with have received consular support.

Togolese producer Komi Mawufemo Vedomey has been detained for over three weeks, while two French filmmakers he assisted, Sébastien Perez-Pezzani and Gaël Mocaër, have been released under surveillance and received diplomatic attention. The French filmmakers, known for their work on "Les Routes de l’impossible," had their health and identities disclosed, with Paris actively engaging through its consuls and the Quai d’Orsay. Reporters Without Borders has also highlighted their case.

Vedomey, described as a "fixer" who facilitates foreign teams in Togo, lacks the diplomatic backing of an embassy. His detention at the Agoè-Logopé camp raises questions about the specific accusations against him, especially since he obtained official authorization for the documentary project. Documents show Vedomey formally requested filming permission on June 13, detailing the project's scope, locations, and team, including the French director and cameraman.

The Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Arts, through Minister Isaac Tchiakpe, officially granted Vedomey authorization N°363/MTCA/CAB/SG/CNCIA on June 19, permitting the filming of "Les routes de l’impossible" from July 10 to July 30 across various Togolese locations. This authorization, bearing the minister's signature and copies sent to local authorities, indicates a transparent process that contrasts with Vedomey's current detention without clear charges.

Why it mattersTogolese producer Komi Mawufemo Vedomey is detained, raising concerns about the treatment of local professionals involved in international productions.

Togo President Attends Shanghai Cooperation Organization Summit

President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé is in Kyrgyzstan to represent Africa and discuss global governance, security, and economic development.

Togo's President, Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé, is participating in the 26th summit of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan. The summit, themed "25 Years of the SCO: Together for Lasting Peace, Development, and Prosperity," began on August 31, 2026. Gnassingbé joins other leaders, including Egypt's President Abdel Fattah al-Sissi, to voice African perspectives on global challenges.

Discussions at the summit are set to cover counter-terrorism, violent extremism, cybercrime, infrastructure development, and the establishment of an independent financial architecture. President Gnassingbé plans to address the security and humanitarian situation in West Africa and the broader continent, highlighting Togo's contributions to finding lasting solutions. The SCO, founded in 2001, aims to foster peace, security, and stability while promoting a new international political and economic order.

The SCO is also exploring partnerships with the African Union (AU) based on shared values and common interests between Asian and African nations. This initiative seeks to strengthen dialogue and cooperation between the continents. Togo's participation underscores its engagement in international forums aimed at addressing complex global issues and contributing to multilateral efforts.

Why it mattersTogo's President is attending an international summit to discuss global governance, security, and economic development, potentially influencing regional and international policies.
