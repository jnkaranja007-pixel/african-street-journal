Togo signs cooperation deals in Russia

Togo's president met Russian officials in Moscow for the Russia-Africa Forum, signing accords on education and humanitarian action.

Togo's President Faure Gnassingbé attended the third Russia-Africa Economic and Humanitarian Forum in Moscow on Wednesday, October 28, 2026. The meeting focused on shared growth drivers including trade, investment, energy, digital transformation, and food security. At the forum's closing on Thursday, October 29, Gnassingbé reiterated Togo's priorities for cooperation with Russia: energy, agriculture, industrial transformation, and scientific/technical training.

During the forum, Togo signed two cooperation agreements on Tuesday, October 27, in Moscow, covering education, culture, and humanitarian action, alongside a memorandum of understanding on human rights. Gnassingbé stated that partnership strength is measured by what it builds, the freedom it grants for future choices, the peace it preserves, and the prospects it opens for future generations. He emphasized that sovereignty is the foundation of cooperation, requiring African nations to define their own priorities and freely choose partners based on mutual respect.

The report notes that Gnassingbé had previously visited China from October 9 to 16, 2026, with a large delegation. Discussions there reportedly covered trade, infrastructure, agriculture, customs, debt relief, and technological modernization, including energy, transport, and customs with Huawei. The article criticizes these frequent international trips as a waste of public funds, suggesting they do not lead to Togo's development.

Why it mattersTogo's President Faure Gnassingbé signed cooperation agreements in Russia covering education, culture, humanitarian action, and human rights, impacting the nation's international relations and development priorities.

Togo opposition coalition to hold rally in Tsévié

The National Coordination for Change will gather on November 8 to protest the 2024 constitutional reform.

The National Coordination for Change (CNCC) will hold a rally on Sunday, November 8, at the Atikoumé square in Tsévié. The coalition, comprising opposition parties ADDI, ANC, FDR, and PSR, along with civil society groups, received approval from the local mayor for the event.

The municipal authority confirmed in a letter that necessary arrangements are being made to provide the requested space on the specified date. This follows the CNCC's October 22 request to use the site. The rally was initially planned for October 25 but was postponed due to an administrative dispute over the venue's availability.

The CNCC's message for the gathering is "No to permanent coups, yes to the restoration of C92." The group is protesting the 2024 constitutional reform.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens in Tsévié will participate in a rally organized by opposition parties and civil society groups to protest a constitutional reform.

Togo coach faces media demands for apology

The MMLK movement wants coach Patrice Neveu to apologize publicly for his actions towards a journalist.

The Mouvement Martin Luther King (MMLK) has condemned the behaviour of Togolese national football team coach Patrice Neveu towards journalist Espoir Gato of Nana FM. The incident occurred during a press conference on November 3 in Kégué.

The MMLK stated that journalists should not be intimidated for seeking explanations, calling it a matter of respect for media professionals. The organisation issued a statement on November 4 in Lomé, emphasizing that asking questions, even difficult ones, is not an attempt to destabilise.

The movement is demanding public and personal apologies from Neveu within 48 hours to the journalist, the Togolese press, and the Togolese people. The MMLK also urged the Togolese Football Federation (FTF) to ensure media safety at press conferences.

Why it mattersThe MMLK movement seeks public apologies from Patrice Neveu to uphold the dignity of journalists.

Togo's CHU Campus hospital adds new medical services

The Lomé hospital now offers interventional cardiology, MRI, and dialysis with new equipment.

The CHU Campus hospital in Lomé, Togo, has introduced advanced medical services including interventional cardiology, MRI scans, and dialysis since early September 2026. These upgrades aim to improve the quality, accessibility, and safety of healthcare for the population, according to hospital director Dr. Kalao Assima Essodom.

The cardiology department now features a state-of-the-art operating room and intensive care unit, capable of performing angioplasties, coronarographies, and pacemaker insertions. The medical imaging complex includes a 1.5 Tesla MRI, a new scanner, remote-controlled X-ray tables, ultrasounds, and mammography equipment.

A new emergency department and six operating theatres for trauma, surgery, and specialized procedures are now operational. The hospital also opened a pathology service on November 5 and will launch its dialysis unit with 10 generators on November 9. A 100-capacity morgue has been serving the greater Lomé area for two months.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens can now access advanced cardiac procedures, detailed imaging, and kidney dialysis services at the CHU Campus hospital.

Togo merges social security agencies

The government will combine pension and health insurance bodies into a single national institute.

Togo's Council of Ministers met on November 6, 2026, to adopt a new law creating the National Social Security Institute (INSS). This new body will merge the Togo Retirement Fund (CRT) and the National Health Insurance Institute (INAM). The merger aims to streamline social coverage for public servants and their dependents by creating a single point of contact and consolidating management resources.

Currently, public servants deal with two separate agencies for pensions and health insurance, leading to duplicated efforts and administrative costs. The INSS will manage both retirement and health insurance schemes, aiming to improve service quality and ensure the long-term sustainability of the social protection system for active and retired public employees and their families.

The council also reviewed a draft law to amend the 2018 land and property code. This revision seeks to enhance land governance through a national observatory, digitize land management, and secure customary rights. It also aims to promote gender equality in land access, protect agricultural land, and address pastoral and climate concerns.

Why it mattersPublic servants and retirees in Togo will experience a unified system for their pensions and health insurance benefits.
