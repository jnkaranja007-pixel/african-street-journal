Togo power outages linked to gas pipeline maintenance

Electricity cuts across Togo are due to maintenance on the gas pipeline supplying power plants, with normal service expected by month's end.

Several regions in Togo are experiencing electricity disruptions because of maintenance work on the gas pipeline that fuels some power plants. The Compagnie énergie électrique du Togo (CEET) stated that these operations, initially scheduled for October 12 to October 27, 2026, have been postponed multiple times. The CEET's director general, Ilagou Ayeva, indicated that a gradual return to normal power supply is anticipated before the end of October.

Despite efforts to use alternative energy sources to mitigate the impact, further power interruptions are possible during the maintenance period. The CEET is advising customers to unplug their electrical appliances during outages to prevent potential damage. This measure is part of an effort to manage the situation while awaiting improved electricity provision across the affected areas.

The maintenance aims to ensure the continued reliable operation of the gas pipeline, which is crucial for electricity generation. The CEET is urging users to exercise caution and patience as the work progresses. The company expects the situation to stabilize once the maintenance concludes, restoring consistent power supply to all customers.

Why it mattersBusinesses and households in Togo are affected by power outages that disrupt daily operations and could damage electrical equipment.

University of Kara trains teachers on corruption prevention

The University of Kara hosted a training session for its faculty aimed at embedding anti-corruption principles into Togo's university curricula.

The University of Kara recently hosted a training session for its teachers and researchers, organized by HAPLUCIA. The initiative focuses on integrating corruption prevention, ethics, professional conduct, responsibility, and good governance into university programs across Togo.

Through this program, HAPLUCIA aims to empower educators to instill values of integrity and transparency in their students. The goal is to position universities as central players in combating corruption by fostering responsible citizenship and promoting a culture of integrity within higher education.

This effort seeks to cultivate a generation of students who are aware of ethical standards and committed to transparent practices. By equipping educators with these tools, the university hopes to build a foundation for a more accountable future within Togo's academic and public spheres.

Why it mattersTeachers and researchers at the University of Kara are affected by training that integrates anti-corruption and ethics into their teaching.

Journalist sidelined amid French media ban talks

France 24's correspondent in Togo and Benin was suddenly removed from her post in September, as France seeks to restore broadcast access.

Emmanuelle Sodji, France 24's correspondent for Togo and Benin, was abruptly sidelined by her management in September. This dismissal occurred as the French government, represented by its foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, was actively negotiating with Togolese authorities to lift the ban on RFI and France 24 broadcasts. The French media outlets have been prohibited from airing in Togo for over a year.

Minister Barrot had previously visited the West African nation in April, advocating for the reinstatement of the two prominent public broadcasting services. At the time, he expressed optimism, stating to the press that he had pleaded with Togolese officials to end the suspension and was hopeful for a resolution in the near future. An investigation by Off Investigation has sought to uncover the reasons behind Sodji's sudden removal.

The ongoing ban on RFI and France 24 has been in place for more than 12 months, impacting the flow of information and French media presence in Togo. The circumstances surrounding Sodji's departure, coinciding with diplomatic efforts to resolve the broadcast issue, have raised questions about potential internal or external pressures influencing media operations in the country.

Why it mattersJournalists and media organizations are affected by sudden dismissals that coincide with diplomatic efforts to lift broadcast bans.

Togo and China sign 13 cooperation agreements

Agreements cover finance, economy, security, trade, agriculture, media, and decentralized cooperation, elevating bilateral ties.

Togo and China have signed 13 new agreements and memorandums of understanding aimed at strengthening bilateral cooperation. The signing ceremony in Beijing was presided over by Togo's President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé and Chinese Premier Li Qiang. These accords span multiple sectors including finance, economy, security, trade, agriculture, training, media, and decentralized cooperation, following the elevation of Sino-Togolese relations to a "China-Togo community of shared future in the new era" on October 13, 2026.

A significant outcome of the financial cooperation is the partial cancellation of Togo's public debt, which will alleviate the country's financial obligations. Economically, the agreements include a customs cooperation protocol between the Togolese Revenue Office (OTR) and China's General Administration of Customs to facilitate trade and streamline import-export operations. Both nations also committed to enhancing collaboration in industrial sectors, supply chains, digital trade, international economic and commercial exchanges, and green development.

In agriculture, a protocol on phytosanitary requirements for Togolese soybeans destined for export to China was signed. This agreement aims to ensure Togolese agricultural products meet Chinese sanitary and phytosanitary standards, thereby facilitating market access and promoting Togolese produce on international markets. The accords signal a renewed impetus for modernization, technological innovation, economic diversification, and sustainable development practices between the two countries.

Why it mattersTogo's public debt is partially cancelled, and its agricultural exports to China will be facilitated, benefiting the nation's economy and farmers.

Togo mayor postpones CNCC meeting

A planned information rally by the CNCC in Atikoumé has been cancelled due to venue unavailability, according to the local mayor.

A planned information rally by the Cadre national de concertation pour le changement (CNCC) in Atikoumé, Zio 1 commune, has been postponed. The meeting, scheduled for Sunday, October 25, 2026, will no longer take place as the designated venue is unavailable.

Mayor Dr. Atatsi Ayawo of Zio 1 informed the CNCC organizers in a letter dated October 19 that the Atikoumé site is occupied by other activities. He advised them to consider rescheduling the event for a later date.

The CNCC meeting aimed to mobilize supporters around the slogan "No to permanent coups, yes to the restoration of C92." The rally was intended to protest the 2024 constitutional reform and advocate for a return to the 1992 Constitution.

Why it mattersTogo citizens who support the CNCC's call to protest constitutional changes are affected because their planned rally has been postponed.
