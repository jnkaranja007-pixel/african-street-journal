African consumers urge zero added sugar for young children

The African Consumers Organisation (OAC) wants African nations to back a ban on added sugars in baby food.

Source: Icilome · 2026-11-02 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The African Consumers Organisation (OAC) is calling on African countries to support a goal of zero added sugar in foods for children aged 6 to 36 months. This plea comes as the 45th session of the Codex Committee on Nutrition approaches. The OAC represents 50 national consumer groups across 39 African nations.

An investigation by Public Eye found that Cerelac infant cereals, popular in Africa, contain nearly six grams of added sugar per serving, unlike European versions. The OAC asked Nestlé S.A. on June 25, 2026, to remove added sugar from its African baby cereals. Nestlé responded on July 20, 2026, stating its products meet national regulations and Codex Standard CXS 74-1981, and that it now offers versions with and without added sugar.

The current Codex standard CXS 74-1981 allows up to 20% added sugar in infant cereals, which is about 10 grams per portion. The World Health Organization (WHO) warns that early exposure to sweet tastes can lead to a lasting preference for sugary foods and is a major risk factor for obesity. The number of overweight African children under five has nearly doubled since 1990.

Why it mattersChildren in Africa under five are at risk of obesity and related health issues due to added sugars in their food.
