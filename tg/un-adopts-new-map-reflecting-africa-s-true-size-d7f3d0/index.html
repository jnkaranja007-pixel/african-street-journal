UN Adopts New Map Reflecting Africa's True Size

The UN General Assembly voted to replace the traditional Mercator projection map with one that more accurately depicts the size of African nations.

The United Nations General Assembly has voted to adopt a new world map that more accurately reflects the true size of Africa, replacing the widely used Mercator projection. The resolution, championed by Togo and supported by African Union members, passed with 164 nations in favor, including France and the UK, while the US voted against and six nations abstained. This vote, though non-binding, signals a shift in how global institutions will represent continents.

The Mercator map, in use since the 16th century, has been criticized for distorting the sizes of countries, notably showing Africa as roughly the same size as Greenland, despite Africa being 14 times larger. Critics argue this misrepresentation can lead to the minimization of development needs and potential for equatorial nations. The Mercator projection was originally designed by Gerardus Mercator in 1569 to aid European explorers in navigation.

This new map aims to correct the visual inaccuracies that have persisted for centuries. The adoption of a more representative map by the UN could influence cartographic standards globally, potentially impacting how geographical data and proportions are perceived and utilized by various international bodies and educational institutions.

Why it mattersAfrican nations are affected by the UN's adoption of a new map because it corrects historical misrepresentations of their continent's true size, which critics say minimized development needs.
