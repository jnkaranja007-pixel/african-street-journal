Togolese Opposition Fractures Amidst Personal Agendas

Internal divisions and alleged financial incentives continue to undermine unified opposition efforts against the ruling regime.

Source: Icilome · 2026-10-21 · 2 min read

WhatsApp X

The Togolese opposition faces persistent challenges due to internal rivalries, personal ambitions, and alleged financial dealings, hindering unified action against the Gnassingbé regime. A past agreement in May 2010 between Gilchrist Olympio and Faure Gnassingbé, following Eyadema's death, is cited as a betrayal, with claims of secret financial clauses and indemnities for the Olympio family. This event is seen as a significant factor in the ongoing disunity within opposition ranks.

Despite recent efforts to unite, such as a memorandum presented on August 25, 2026, by the Manifeste Génération Togo (MGT) and the Cadre National de Concertation pour le Changement (CNCC), concrete progress remains elusive. Disagreements over leadership and personal egos reportedly obstruct the formation of a common front, preventing joint actions like coordinated demonstrations. The Alliance Nationale pour le Changement (ANC) has also expressed a willingness to participate in elections under the current regime, further complicating unity.

The communication manager for the ANC, Éric Dupuy, indicated that his party might participate in elections, a stance that contrasts with the broader opposition's goal of demanding a return to the 1992 constitution and a political transition. This divergence highlights the deep-seated issues of personal interests and a lack of cohesive strategy that plague the opposition, preventing them from presenting a united front against a long-standing dictatorial rule.

Why it mattersThe Togolese opposition's internal divisions and personal agendas prevent a unified front, impacting the country's political landscape.
