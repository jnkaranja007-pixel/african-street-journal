Togo’s industrial platform targets local processing and jobs

The Adétikopé zone hosts textile, agro-industry, manufacturing and some pharmaceutical activities, with the state holding 35% of its management company.

Source: 24heureinfo · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Togo’s Adétikopé Industrial Platform, near Lomé, is designed to host factories that process raw materials locally, increase exports and create jobs. The special economic zone is part of the government’s industrialisation strategy.

The platform is backed by a partnership between the Togolese state and Arise Integrated Industrial Platforms. Its management company is 35% state-owned and 65% owned by Arise; the site includes industrial space, storage, a dry port and a one-stop administrative service. Textile firms are expected to process and make garments from Togolese cotton, while agro-industry is intended to add value to farm products.

The report says updated figures are needed on companies operating, jobs created, investment made and export volumes. The platform’s results will depend on attracting durable businesses and developing local skills.

Why it mattersPeople seeking factory and related logistics work will depend on firms taking up the site; updated job and investment figures are needed to assess results.
