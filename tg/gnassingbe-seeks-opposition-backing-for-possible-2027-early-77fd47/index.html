Gnassingbé seeks opposition backing for possible 2027 early elections

Togo’s government is trying to unite a divided opposition behind early legislative polls after an ECOWAS court ruling on the country’s republic.

Sources: Jeune Afrique and Icilome · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Togo’s government is seeking support from a divided opposition for possible early legislative elections from 2027, as President Faure Gnassingbé responds to a ruling by the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) court. The court said Togo’s Fifth Republic amounted to an unconstitutional change of government.

The government is trying to build agreement around the principle of holding the polls early, according to Jeune Afrique. The report links Gnassingbé’s effort to the court’s ruling, but gives no election date or details of any opposition response.

The report does not say whether the opposition has accepted the proposal or whether the government has set a timetable for elections.

Why it mattersOpposition parties must decide whether to support the principle of early legislative polls from 2027 after the ECOWAS court ruling.
