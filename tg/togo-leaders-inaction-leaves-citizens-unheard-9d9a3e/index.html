Togo Leaders' Inaction Leaves Citizens Unheard

The president travels abroad while an exiled opposition leader publishes extensively, creating a political stalemate.

Togolese citizens face hardship with insufficient salaries, under-equipped hospitals, and educated youth seeking to emigrate. The nation's current leadership is described as detached, with the president reportedly traveling extensively, including to Mongolia, while the country struggles. This perceived indifference is seen as a system that marginalizes the governed.

Meanwhile, an opposition figure based in Switzerland issues daily statements and writings, which are characterized as a substitute for action rather than a catalyst for change. This prolific output of text, including social media activity, is criticized for being disconnected from the practical needs of the population, who desire organization and shared risk over eloquent pronouncements.

This dual inaction, one from the governing elite through detachment and the other from the opposition through excessive rhetoric and distance, leaves the populace without effective government or resistance. The disconnect between these leaders and the daily realities of Togolese citizens, such as the cost of food and healthcare access, is identified as a significant political ailment.

Why it mattersTogolese citizens are affected by a lack of effective governance and resistance due to the perceived detachment and inaction of their leaders.
