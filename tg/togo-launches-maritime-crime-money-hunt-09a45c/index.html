Togo launches maritime crime money hunt

National workshop aims to identify threats and strengthen coordination against illicit maritime finance.

Source: Icilome · 2026-10-28 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Togo is hosting a national workshop until Friday to track illicit funds linked to maritime crime. The event will assess risks, identify threats, and enhance coordination among institutions. Recommendations will be formulated to combat financial crime at sea, supported by new legislation adopted in March 2026.

The workshop is organized under the auspices of the National Financial Information Processing Unit, with support from GIABA and the African Development Bank. This initiative follows the interception of Togolese-flagged vessels carrying significant cocaine loads, including the Blume and Orion V in 2023, and a fishing boat in the South Pacific in January 2026.

The port of Lomé is considered the economic engine of the nation, but questions remain about the transparency of its revenues, concessions, and contracts. The article suggests that an audit, rather than a workshop, is needed for port administration that lacks clear public accounting of its financial flows.

Why it mattersTogo's government is holding a workshop to combat financial crime related to maritime activities.
