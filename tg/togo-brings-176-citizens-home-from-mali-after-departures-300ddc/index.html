Togo brings 176 citizens home from Mali after departures from Guinea

The government received the returnees at three sites and is still identifying Togolese in Burkina Faso who want to come home.

Sources: 24heureinfo and L-Frii · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Togo’s government brought 176 citizens back from Bamako, Mali, after they left Guinea amid operations against illegal gold mining. The return was organised by a mission sent to Mali and Burkina Faso to identify Togolese, assess their needs and prepare travel home, the Security Ministry said.

The returnees arrived at Dapaong, Sokodé and Logopé, where authorities arranged family reunions and registered them for biometric national identity cards. The ministry said the operation involved several government departments and the National Civil Protection Agency.

At the Bobo-Dioulasso transit centre in Burkina Faso, authorities had counted 983 Togolese among foreign nationals there as of 20 November. Togo is continuing work to identify those who want to return. Guinea’s operations against illegal gold mining began on 2 November, and Togo’s Foreign Ministry urged young people on 17 November to follow migration and mining rules.

Why it mattersTogolese at the Bobo-Dioulasso transit centre are being identified for return, with 983 counted there as of 20 November.
