Benin plans a learning city in each of its six development hubs

Decentralisation Minister Janvier Yahouédéhou presented the plan in Cotonou and cited Porto-Novo, admitted to UNESCO's learning-cities network in 2025, as the model.

Source: Benin Web TV · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Benin's government wants at least one learning city in each of the country's six territorial development hubs, Decentralisation and Local Governance Minister Janvier Yahouédéhou said on Thursday, 26 November, at the Palais des Congrès in Cotonou. He set out the ambition before municipal officials from several African countries.

The principle is to widen access to training beyond the school system. Municipalities can use libraries, community centres, vocational training colleges and workplaces so that residents keep learning at any age. The minister linked infrastructure and economic activity to the skills of residents.

Porto-Novo, the administrative capital, joined UNESCO's global network of learning cities in 2025. Its programmes include literacy, business management and microcredit for women entrepreneurs, and the city's École d'entrepreneuriat offers young people training in commerce and management. The African conference, held with UNESCO's Institute for Lifelong Learning on 26 and 27 November, will present certificates to newly admitted cities.

Why it mattersResidents in each of the six development hubs would gain local access to vocational training, literacy and business courses, since the government plans at least one learning city in every hub.
