British PM open to slavery apology, rejects financial reparations

Andy Burnham proposes debt relief for poor nations instead of direct payments for historical slave trade role.

Source: Benin Web TV · 2026-11-01 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham has stated he is open to discussing official apologies for the UK's role in the transatlantic slave trade. In a statement published on October 30, Burnham proposed dialogue with affected countries and populations but rejected financial reparations.

Burnham clarified that this openness does not constitute an apology from the British state, suggesting it should be part of future discussions. He argued that current Britons cannot be held financially responsible for historical crimes they did not participate in. Instead, he proposed an international initiative to ease the debt burden of poor nations by extending repayment terms and lowering interest rates, with former Prime Minister Gordon Brown assisting.

Caribbean nations, including Jamaica, have presented a 10-point plan through CARICOM that includes apologies, support for health and education, and measures addressing the economic and social consequences of slavery. Jamaica submitted a petition to King Charles III on September 7, seeking referral of legal questions to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council regarding potential UK reparation obligations. These discussions are set to precede the Commonwealth leaders' summit on November 1 in Antigua and Barbuda.

Why it mattersThe British Prime Minister's proposal on debt relief and apologies for the slave trade affects African nations and the diaspora who are seeking redress for historical injustices.
