Wadagni secures initial agreement for eight-country UEMOA summit in 2027

The Beninese president consulted leaders from Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger after months of deadlock over holding a top-level meeting.

Sources: La Nouvelle Tribune and Benin Web TV · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Benin’s President Romuald Wadagni has secured an agreement in principle to bring the leaders of the eight-member West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA) together in early 2027, according to reporting by Africa Intelligence cited by La Nouvelle Tribune. The proposed summit follows several months of deadlock over arranging a leaders’ meeting.

Preparations included discreet consultations with the military authorities in Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger. The three countries remain in UEMOA despite leaving the Economic Community of West African States. The agenda is expected to include reforming the union’s statutes, renewing mandates in its bodies and discussing the common currency and monetary integration.

The reported agreement does not settle all arrangements for the meeting. Benin Web TV also reported that institutional reform is among the subjects under consideration.

Why it mattersWadagni must now move from a principle agreement to arranging a summit for all eight member states in early 2027.
