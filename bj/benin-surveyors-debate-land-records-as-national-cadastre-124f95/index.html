Benin surveyors debate land records as national cadastre project targets 1.5 million plots

More than 300 participants from 18 countries met in Cotonou to discuss parcel registration, land security and public-sector use of artificial intelligence.

Source: Benin Web TV · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Benin's francophone surveyors opened a four-day meeting in Cotonou on Tuesday, 24 November, to discuss parcel registration, land security and the use of artificial intelligence in public decisions. The organisers, the Federation of Francophone Surveyors and Benin's surveyors' association, said more than 300 people from 18 countries were attending.

Jean-David Gnansounou, head of Benin's surveyors' association, cited difficulties accessing reliable land information and gaps in records in some parts of the country. The first day's programme included a presentation on the national cadastre by an official from the National Agency for the Domain and Land (ANDF), as well as experiences from Gambia and Zanzibar.

A second phase of the national cadastre preparation project is planned across 12 communes from 2026 to 2031, the government said; nine, including Cotonou, Porto-Novo and Parakou, are expected to be fully covered. A separate initiative, the Benin land registration drive, aims to register 1.5 million plots.

Why it mattersBenin's land agency and surveyors face unreliable records and gaps in land information as the national project is planned for 12 communes through 2031.
