Benin requests return of 35 looted artifacts from Paris museum

President Romuald Wadagni raised the demand with President Macron during an official visit to France in October.

Source: Africanews · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Benin has formally asked France to return 35 artifacts held by the Musée du Quai Branly in Paris, five years after France returned 26 looted treasures to the West African nation. The objects include a divination tray and several weapons.

At the centre of the request is a 19th-century iron sculpture of the god Gou. Benin's President Romuald Wadagni raised the issue with President Macron during an official visit to France in October. The 2021 returns included royal thrones, altars and statues seized by French troops from the Kingdom of Dahomey in 1892.

France passed a law in May to return cultural property acquired illegally during its colonial empire, whether stolen, looted or taken with violence. France has lagged behind Germany, which has returned more than a thousand objects to African states. Earlier this year Paris returned a sacred drum to Ivory Coast, which French administrators had confiscated in 1916.

Why it mattersThe Musée du Quai Branly must now decide on Benin's request for 35 objects, a decision that would follow the 26 items France returned in 2021.
