Benin forum questions Africa's democratic future

A two-day symposium in Porto-Novo examines democracy and development in French-speaking Africa.

Source: La Nouvelle Tribune · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

The Institute of Artisans of Justice and Peace (Iajp/Co) launched its 29th annual symposium in Porto-Novo on Friday, November 13, 2026. Organized with the Konrad Adenauer Foundation, the event gathers academics, civil society actors, and institutional representatives to discuss "Democracy and Development: French-speaking Africa at a Crossroads."

Bishop Aristide Gonsallo of Porto-Novo stressed that economic growth alone does not ensure progress if its benefits do not reach populations, citing persistent gaps between economic performance and living conditions. Kevin Anvo of the Konrad Adenauer Foundation noted improvements in Benin's governance according to Freedom House and the Ibrahim Index, but highlighted a general decline across 23 African nations between 2016 and 2025.

Afrobarometer data shows 64% of Africans prefer democracy, yet only 36% fully endorse it, reflecting citizens' desire for responsive institutions, especially among youth. The symposium will explore rule of law, political alternation, and development conditions, with discussions on governance models and economic policies' impact on populations.

Why it mattersThe symposium in Porto-Novo will examine governance models and economic policies affecting citizens in French-speaking Africa.
