African court rejects Ajavon relatives' damages claim against Benin

The court said the four family members never tried the domestic remedies open to them in Benin before bringing their complaint.

Sources: La Nouvelle Tribune and Benin Web TV · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

The African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights declared a complaint by four relatives of businessman Sébastien Ajavon against the Benin state inadmissible on 13 November 2026. The family had sought reparations for losses linked to the seizure of their personal bank accounts.

The complaint was filed on 29 November 2019 by Ida Afiavi Goudjo, Ronald K. Steeven Karen Ayité Ajavon, Ludmila Isis Ayélé Ajavon and Evaëlle Jonella Noémie Ayoko Ajavon, who live in Paris as political refugees. They asked for 170 million CFA francs each, or 680 million in total, plus compensation tied to their stakes in the companies COMON and SOCOTRAC.

The court ruled that the applicants had not brought the available cases in Benin. It said civil law allowed action over COMON, and OHADA rules allowed challenges to account seizures. The applicants had neither used nor attempted to use these remedies, so the court did not examine the alleged violations.

Why it mattersThe ruling leaves the family's 680 million CFA franc claim unheard by the court, so any compensation must now be sought through Benin's civil courts or OHADA procedures.
