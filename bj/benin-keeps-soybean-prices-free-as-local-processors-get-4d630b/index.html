Benin keeps soybean prices free as local processors get first supply

The government leaves prices to growers and buyers, while exports of soybeans remain barred until 15 February 2027.

Source: Benin Web TV · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Benin will keep no floor or ceiling price for soybeans during the 2026-2027 season, government spokesperson Wilfried Léandre Houngbédji said on Sunday 22 November. Growers and buyers will negotiate prices directly, while local processing plants have priority over supplies before exports begin.

Houngbédji put national soybean production at about 800 000 tonnes and local processors’ needs at around 450 000 tonnes. Domestic sales began on 6 November under rules set in an interministerial notice dated 4 November, with each sale priced by agreement.

A CFA10-per-kilogram bonus applies to soybeans delivered to local processors after their installed capacity is met; the reference volume for the 2026-2027 season is 450 000 tonnes. The government set the bonus on 3 June 2026, and exports can start on 15 February 2027.

Why it mattersGrowers receive a CFA10-per-kilogram bonus for deliveries to local processors once their installed capacity is met, against a reference volume of 450 000 tonnes.
