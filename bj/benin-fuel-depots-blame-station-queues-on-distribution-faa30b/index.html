Benin fuel depots blame station queues on distribution delays

Depot director Sosthène H. Mesmin Hounsou says stocks remain available, while higher demand strains station staff, trucks and delivery schedules.

Source: La Nouvelle Tribune · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Benin’s petroleum depot director, Sosthène H. Mesmin Hounsou, says fuel stocks are not exhausted despite long queues at several service stations. He attributes the shortages at pumps to distribution constraints, not a lack of products in storage.

Hounsou said Benin continues to receive fuel regularly, but licensed distributors must order products from the state-run depots, buy them and transport them to their own stations. He said increased customer traffic has stretched some stations’ staff and delivery fleets, slowing orders and restocking.

The director also said informal fuel from Nigeria has become scarcer and more expensive since the crisis of April 2026 and cuts to Nigerian subsidies. In some localities, informal-market fuel sells for 1 000 to 1 CFA200 a litre, against CFA750 at official pumps. Depot officials have discussed with distributors delays from ships’ arrival and unloading through order processing.

Why it mattersFuel distributors must resolve order and delivery delays, which depot officials have examined from ships’ arrival through order processing.
