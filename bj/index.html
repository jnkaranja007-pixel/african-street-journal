Benin president attends AICESIS board meeting in Madrid

Abdoulaye Bio Tchané participated in discussions on climate change, technological transformations, and social dialogue.

Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, president of Benin's Economic and Social Council (CES), attended an AICESIS board meeting in Madrid, Spain, on October 29 and 30, 2026. The meeting reviewed the 2025 activity report and outcomes from international forums held in China, Russia, and Zimbabwe. Discussions also covered the organization's financial situation, noting a surplus in treasury despite a budgetary deficit.

The AICESIS board examined reports on international forums focusing on climate change, technological transformations, and social dialogue. Benin's CES president, Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, participated for the first time as a new board member. The meeting also addressed the interest of several councils in joining AICESIS and welcomed new officials from member councils, including Greece.

The participation of Benin's CES aims to strengthen cooperation with peer institutions and contribute to international exchanges on economic and social issues. The discussions provided opportunities for experience sharing and consolidating relationships between represented institutions. Benin's CES reaffirmed its commitment to dialogue and international cooperation for equitable development.

Why it mattersBenin's Economic and Social Council president attended an international meeting to foster cooperation and share experiences on economic and social matters.

Akpakpa Boys and Ace win cotonou beach tour

The cotonou stage of the benin beach tour concluded on october 25, 2026, with Akpakpa Boys and Ace claiming the men's and women's titles respectively.

Akpakpa Boys and Ace were crowned champions of the cotonou stage of the Bénin Beach Tour on Sunday, October 25, 2026, at Erevan beach. The event, which ran from October 23 to 25, showcased the skill and endurance of beninese beach-volleyball players.

In the men's final, Akpakpa Boys defeated Dynamic in a three-set match, winning 21-13, 21-23, and 15-12. The women's final saw Ace, represented by Kona-Francisco, beat Bees, led by Kouyema-Adom, in straight sets, 21-15, 21-15.

Organized by the Fédération béninoise de volleyball (Fbvb) under the supervision of Colonel Oscar Dafia, the tour aims to develop beach-volleyball in Benin. The next stage will be held in Parakou in December 2026.

Why it mattersThe Fédération béninoise de volleyball aims to develop beach-volleyball and reveal new talents through this tour.

Benin ambassador gets guidance before Paris posting

Jean-Michel Abimbola met Constitutional Court President Cossi Dorothé SOSSA on October 29, 2026.

Jean-Michel Abimbola, appointed Benin's ambassador to France on October 7, 2026, met with Constitutional Court President Professor Cossi Dorothé SOSSA on October 29, 2026. The meeting in Cotonou focused on inter-institutional cooperation and gathering constitutional guidance for his mission in Paris, which includes several European states and international organizations.

Abimbola stated that consulting with institutional leaders is a republican tradition for diplomats before international deployment. He highlighted the strategic importance of the Paris embassy for national institutions and discussed cooperation between Benin's Constitutional Court and its European counterparts.

The ambassador expressed respect for the court and sought the president's advice for a flawless mission. Professor SOSSA reiterated his support and encouraged Abimbola to honor the trust placed in him by the Head of State.

Why it mattersBenin's new ambassador to France received guidance from the Constitutional Court president to ensure a successful diplomatic mission.

Beninese student wins fourth global UPU prize

Marie-Ange Chekpo, 15, also took first national prize for her letter on digital age relationships.

15-year-old Marie-Ange Chekpo from Godomey won the fourth global prize in the 55th Universal Postal Union (UPU) international letter-writing competition. She received her award on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, in Cotonou, after also securing the national first prize and an international jury special mention.

Chekpo, a student at Le Bon Berger school in Godomey, wrote about why human relationships remain important in an increasingly digital world. Benin ranked behind China, Portugal, and Iran in this year's competition, which saw 1.48 million participants from 65 countries.

The UPU competition, established in 1971, is open to young people aged 9 to 15. For the 2026 edition, the UPU partnered with the World Health Organization and UNESCO to focus on human connections in the digital era.

Why it mattersA 15-year-old Beninese student has been recognised internationally for her writing on human relationships.

Benin sextortion victims fear judgment

Women and girls make up 69% of sextortion victims in Benin, facing shame and fear of exposure.

Sextortion, where intimate images are used for blackmail, is a hidden crime in Benin. Victims like Houéfa, who was threatened with shared videos in 2016, suffer in silence due to fear of judgment and lack of knowledge about available help. Organizations are now working on prevention and support for those affected by this digital violence.

The Centre national d’investigations numériques (CNIN) reported that 69% of sextortion victims in Benin are women and girls, according to Matin Libre on February 20, 2026. Sociologist Dr. MONTCHO Bruno emphasizes that prevention campaigns must address the social, economic, and digital realities of victims to combat the shame and vulnerability that prevent them from seeking assistance.

Civil society organizations are actively involved in preventing and supporting individuals facing sextortion. Despite legal frameworks allowing prosecution, strengthening victim protection remains a key challenge for Benin.
