Agriculture · 2026-11-16

Benin loses 2% of farmland to urbanisation

Government plans to secure agricultural land and create green reserves in each commune.

Benin's government is taking steps to halt the decline of its arable land, which has fallen from 43% in 2021 to 41% in 2023. This 2% loss over two years is largely attributed to rapid urbanisation, according to Antoine Houedji, technical advisor for land affairs to the Minister of Agriculture. The government aims to secure agricultural land to reverse this trend.

The initiative coincides with advancements in land reform, including the launch of projects TERRA BÉNIN and PPMEC II in Cotonou on November 6, 2026, to enhance the e-Foncier platform. The World Bank's representative, Karine Bachongy, stated the goal is to establish an operational cadastre nationwide, map land, identify rights holders, and permanently register land transactions.

Minister Adin Yéton Bloukounon Goubalan announced that under the Benin Vert 2033 strategy, several hectares of land will be identified and secured in each commune for the "One Commune, One Green Lung" initiative. Prefect Laurent Zomaï stressed the decisive role of local authorities in mobilising and securing agricultural land reserves, while Minister Janvier Yahouedehou highlighted that protecting farmland preserves food sovereignty and territorial development.

Why it mattersFarmers in Benin are losing 2% of cultivable land to urbanisation, impacting food sovereignty and territorial development.

Politics · 2026-11-16

Benin's UP-R party sets first ordinary congress for early 2027

The party's National Council approved preparations for the congress, which will end its transitional period and update its foundational texts in Cotonou.

The Union progressiste le Renouveau (UP-R), a Beninese party led by Joseph Djogbénou, held sessions of its Political Bureau and National Council at the Palais des Congrès in Cotonou on Saturday, November 14, 2026. The members adopted resolutions to prepare the party's first ordinary congress, planned for the first quarter of 2027 in Cotonou.

Djogbénou called the first National Council meeting historic, saying it was the first time the party had gathered in that body. The congress is meant to end the party's transitional period, update its founding texts and strengthen its territorial structures. Nearly 400 women were named village and neighbourhood chiefs during the same meeting.

The National Council endorsed the Political Bureau's recommendations on delegates. The party has 118 sections, and each may name up to three extra delegates to the congress, on top of members with automatic seats. The final number of participants will depend on the funds the party raises.
