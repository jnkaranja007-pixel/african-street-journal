EBRD Triples Risk Sharing Facility with Attijari Bank Tunisia to 70 Million Euros

The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development has increased its risk-sharing mechanism with Attijari Bank Tunisia to seventy million euros, also launching a ten million.

The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD) announced on Friday, August 28, 2026, that it has tripled its risk-sharing facility with Attijari Bank Tunisia, raising it from twenty million to seventy million euros. The European financial institution also introduced a new ten million dollar line, approximately 8.6 million euros, dedicated to environmental, social, and governance (ESG) investments for the Tunisian bank's corporate clients.

This risk-sharing mechanism allows the EBRD to share credit risk with Attijari Bank Tunisia, enabling the bank to extend financing to a broader range of economic sectors and support private investment. The new ESG facility aims to fund projects focused on energy efficiency, renewable energy installations, waste reduction, staff training, improved social and risk management practices, and gender equality initiatives within client companies. Subsidies for this line come from the High Impact Partnership on Climate Action (HIPCA) fund.

Since its inception in 2021, the risk-sharing arrangement has supported twenty-five projects totaling approximately four hundred million Tunisian dinars, or about 118 million euros. The EBRD has invested over 3.1 billion euros in ninety-seven projects in Tunisia, with sixty-six percent directly benefiting the private sector, underscoring its strategy to bolster Tunisian businesses through partnerships like this one with Attijari Bank Tunisia.

Why it mattersInvestors in Tunisia are affected by the increased credit availability and new ESG funding lines that support private sector investment and sustainable business practices.

FSR Prison Torture Kills Over 215 Detainees

The UN documented widespread abuse and extortion at Darkis prison, where over 215 people died in May and June 2026.

More than two hundred fifteen people died in May and June 2026 at Darkis prison, near Nyala in South Darfur, due to torture, lack of care, and epidemics. The Sudanese Doctors Network reported the figures in late June. The prison, controlled by the Rapid Support Forces (FSR), is one of their largest detention centers and has faced accusations of abuses documented by the UN and human rights groups.

The UN's mission of inquiry on Sudan reported in June 2026 that the FSR systematically uses arbitrary detention, citing widespread physical violence and inhumane conditions at Darkis. The report also detailed ransom demands of up to twenty-five million Sudanese pounds, approximately forty thousand dollars, made to families for the release of detainees. A former FSR officer described the situation as catastrophic, with summary executions and mass detentions.

Human rights organizations have documented severe mistreatment at Darkis, including beatings, electric shocks, and cigarette burns, alongside food and water shortages. Some civilians arrested in October 2025 have been transferred to the facility. The Sudanese army accuses the FSR of widespread abuses in prisons they control. Access to Darkis prison for independent verification of conditions has not been granted to humanitarian organizations or UN investigators.

Why it mattersOver 215 detainees died in the FSR-controlled Darkis prison due to torture, lack of care, and epidemics, affecting families seeking release and human rights organizations investigating abuses.

Saddam Haftar Named General Commander of Libyan National Army

The appointment of Khalifa Haftar's son signals a continuation of the succession within the Eastern Libyan military structure.

Marshal Khalifa Haftar, leader of the Libyan National Army (LNA) based in eastern Libya, has appointed his son, Saddam Haftar, as the new general commander of the LNA. The announcement was made on Wednesday, August 27, 2026, via a statement on the LNA's official Facebook page. Saddam Haftar, previously the deputy general commander since the summer of 2025, now assumes the top military role.

This promotion marks a significant step in the ongoing succession plan for Marshal Haftar's leadership of the military apparatus he founded in Benghazi. In August 2025, the eastern Libyan Parliament had already approved changes to the LNA's governing law, facilitating this transition. At that time, Saddam Haftar was promoted to deputy commander, while his brother Khaled Haftar was appointed chief of general staff.

Saddam Haftar, born in 1991, has steadily risen through the ranks, notably leading the Tariq bin Ziyad brigade before becoming the LNA's second-in-command in 2025. He recently hosted foreign diplomats in Benghazi, including the UN Secretary-General's special envoy for Libya, Hanna Tetteh, and met with the Qatari State Minister for Foreign Affairs on August 27. Unconfirmed reports suggest discussions about Saddam Haftar potentially holding a future presidential role in Libya.

Why it mattersThe appointment of Saddam Haftar as general commander of the LNA affects investors by potentially signaling a shift in military leadership and future political stability in Libya.

DRC Government Plans Public Housing Bank

A proposed public housing bank, to be backed by the national housing fund, aims to boost financing for a sector with a significant accommodation deficit.

The Congolese government is considering the creation of a public housing bank, an initiative that would be supported by the national housing fund (FONHAB). This project, discussed on August 28, 2026, aims to strengthen the financing of the housing sector, which is currently facing a substantial shortfall in available units. The plan is still in its early stages, focusing on transforming the existing FONHAB rather than establishing a new, fully operational bank.

Patrick Muyaya stated that this specialized bank is intended to enhance sector financing, secure social housing programs, and promote sustainable growth. The government is linking housing development to long-term credit access and the mobilization of both public and private investments. The FONHAB, already a public entity focused on housing finance, affordable housing, and public-private partnerships, will serve as the institutional foundation for this planned evolution.

The scale of the housing need is considerable. In March 2025, Malick Fall, the IFC representative in the DRC, estimated the country's housing deficit at approximately four million units. He also calculated that resolving this deficit would require an investment of $132 billion over sixteen years, highlighting the significant financial challenge ahead.

Why it mattersThe government is planning a new public housing bank to address a massive deficit in housing units, which will require substantial long-term credit and investment.
