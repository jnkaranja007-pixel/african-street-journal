Benin Updates 27 Northern Agricultural Plans

Agricultural development plans in 27 northern communes are undergoing review and updates to align with national strategies and address climate challenges.

Officials launched a mission to review and update 27 Communal Agricultural Development Plans (PCDA) for northern Benin. The process aims to harmonize agricultural strategies and adapt them to new national orientations, including those of the Benin Vert initiative. This update goes beyond document revision, intending to refresh territorial diagnostics, prioritize agricultural value chains, and ensure PCDA compliance with national guidelines and investment plans.

The ten-week exercise involves local authorities, producer organizations, agricultural development agencies, and the private sector. It emphasizes tailoring plans to the specific agro-economic characteristics of each commune. The updated PCDA, originally developed with Swiss cooperation, will guide annual planning and budgeting, incorporating considerations for climate change, food security, and increasing agricultural value addition.

The Ministry of Agriculture stressed the need to adapt plans to Benin's agricultural transformation ambitions, focusing on sector organization, water access, soil fertility, and producer income. The goal is to make agriculture a more profitable activity that supports local economic development and better meets producers' needs. This initiative seeks to foster a common understanding of the challenges and approaches among all stakeholders involved.

Why it mattersFarmers in northern Benin will see their agricultural development plans updated to better address climate change, food security, and income generation.

Amnesty Benin Urges President to Visit Prisons

The human rights group calls for the president to extend his recent public engagements to include visits to detention facilities and health centers.

Amnesty International Benin is urging President Romuald Wadagni to expand his recent public outreach, which included visits from Nikki to Parakou, to encompass prisons and detention centers. Dieudonné Dagbeto, the executive director, expressed satisfaction with the president's visible engagement but cautioned against it being mere propaganda. He emphasized the need for the head of state to genuinely listen to the concerns of various societal groups he encountered.

Dagbeto specifically recommended that the president increase his visits to health centers and hospitals, and to engage with individuals displaced by development projects. He also called for greater dialogue with civil society organizations. The organization hopes these actions will lead to concrete positive impacts on human rights, as many concerns remain unaddressed.

Amnesty International and its partners have released a human rights manifesto for the 2026-2033 period. The organization advocates for the president to demonstrate a real commitment to human rights by actively seeking out and listening to the experiences of those in detention and affected by national development initiatives.

Why it mattersAmnesty Benin wants the president to visit prisons and detention centers to address human rights concerns for detainees.

UBA Benin Seeks Legal Assistant for Compliance and Contracts

The bank is hiring a legal assistant to ensure its operations align with group standards and local laws.

UBA Benin has announced a vacancy for a Legal Assistant tasked with managing litigation, contracts, and legal advice. The role reports to the Director of Legal Affairs and is responsible for adapting the subsidiary's legal policies to group standards while ensuring compliance with all local Beninese legal requirements.

Key duties include managing and evaluating legal partners such as lawyers and bailiffs, ensuring the bank's legal commitments to third parties are met, and vice versa. The assistant will also draft legal documents, oversee litigation involving the bank, and represent UBA Benin in various proceedings before administrative bodies.

The position requires advising the General Directorate and other departments on legal matters, managing relationships with the Board of Directors and shareholders, and coordinating meetings and general assemblies. Additionally, the role involves identifying and managing legal and regulatory risks impacting the subsidiary and reporting on legal developments.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the bank is hiring to ensure compliance and manage legal risks, which impacts operational stability.

Sudan Rapid Support Forces See Cadre Defections Multiply

A unit led by Brigadier Moussa Khamsine joined the Sudanese army, continuing a trend of departures from the paramilitary group.

A unit of approximately three hundred eighteen men, including four officers and fifty vehicles, defected from the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) to the Sudanese army on August nineteen. Brigadier Moussa Khamsine led the group, which arrived via Al-Dabbah and was welcomed in Omdurman. This defection follows a series of departures from the paramilitary group that began in spring twenty twenty-six.

Previous defections include Al-Nour al-Qubba in April, Ali Rizq Allah in May, and Fares al-Nur in June. Ali Rizq Allah, also known as Al-Savannah, claimed on May twenty that around five thousand RSF members had defected with him, and that Hemedti had stated the war had escaped his control. Dayfallah Adam also reported on June twenty-four that RSF fighters had transited through Chad and Ethiopia before engaging near Kurmuk.

Human Rights Watch on June ten called for RSF commanders who joined the army to be held accountable for alleged serious crimes, particularly in Darfur, warning against de facto impunity. Despite these departures, the RSF retains operational capacity, announcing on August thirteen the recapture of Kurmuk in Blue Nile State, a city the Sudanese army had retaken on July eight.

Why it mattersThe ongoing defections from the Rapid Support Forces and the calls for accountability for alleged crimes affect the stability and leadership within the paramilitary group.
