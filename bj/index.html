Benin Beach Tour hosts 23 teams in Cotonou

The second stage of the 2026 Benin Beach Tour will feature 11 women's and 12 men's teams competing from October 23 to 25.

The Benin Beach Tour (BBT) is organizing its second stage of the 2026 edition from October 23 to 25 on Erevan beach in Cotonou. 23 teams, including 11 women's and 12 men's formations, will compete over three days. This competition is an important passage towards the season's grand finale.

Soulé Arouna, the deputy national technical director for beach volleyball, explained the project's philosophy to the press on Wednesday, October 21, at the Benin Volleyball Federation headquarters. The BBT aims to provide regular competition and establish a ranking system to track team performance. The federation, supported by its president Ali Yaro, is backing this initiative.

The Benin Volleyball Federation secretary general, Cokou Romain Ahlinvi, stated the goal is to gain international recognition for the BBT and enable athletes to participate in ranking competitions. Foreign teams may participate pending necessary approvals. The event includes a technical meeting on Friday, matches starting Saturday, and finals on Sunday.

Why it matters23 beach volleyball teams from Benin will compete in Cotonou for the second stage of the 2026 Benin Beach Tour.

Benin nominated for four Africa Tourism Awards

The country, its cultural heritage, Benin Tourisme, and a tourism campaign are finalists for the 2026 awards.

Benin has received four nominations at the 2026 Africa Tourism Awards, recognizing its cultural heritage and tourism appeal. The country is a finalist for national destination of the year and cultural destination of the year. Benin Tourisme is competing for best tourism office, and the "Explore Benin, A World of Wonders" campaign is nominated for best tourism video.

The public vote, which closes on October 29, accounts for 20% of the final score. Professional juries and the Balearica Awards College each hold 40% of the vote. Winners will be announced on November 1 in London.

The nominations cover the destination, its cultural heritage, promotion structure, and international communication. These are not yet awards, as final scores will determine the winners across more than 30 categories.

Why it mattersBenin's cultural heritage and tourism promotion are being recognized on a continental stage.

Benin ranks 94th in 2026 rule of law index

The country's overall score improved slightly, with gains in civil justice but a decline in freedoms.

Benin placed 94th out of 143 countries in the World Justice Project's 2026 Rule of Law Index, released on October 21. The nation's overall score reached 0.47 out of 1, a less than 1% increase from the previous year. Benin ranked 10th among 34 sub-Saharan African nations evaluated.

The country achieved its highest global rank of 79th for constraints on government powers, fundamental rights, and criminal justice. Benin was ranked 89th for regulatory enforcement, 91st for order and security, and 92nd for absence of corruption. However, civil justice and open government were its least favorable areas, at 108th and 115th globally.

The WJP report noted improvements in access to and affordability of civil justice, alongside a lack of discrimination in this area. Conversely, freedom of opinion, expression, assembly, and association declined. These results contrast with the regional trend where 23 of 34 evaluated countries saw their scores decrease over the past year.

Why it mattersBenin's ranking in the rule of law index affects its citizens' access to justice and their freedoms.

Benin handball team appoints French coach

Euphémie Dartron-Lairy will lead the senior women's national team for the 2026 CAN tournament.

Euphémie Dartron-Lairy, a French coach of Guadeloupean origin, has been appointed technical coordinator for Benin's senior women's handball team. She will lead the team, known as the Amazons, in the 2026 Women's Senior CAN tournament scheduled for December 2-12 in Tunisia. Dartron-Lairy aims to develop Benin's women's handball by focusing on human connection, team cohesion, and performance.

Dartron-Lairy, trained by the French Handball Federation, describes herself as a "facilitator of potential." Her career includes coaching and management roles, notably with ASUP men's team where they won 13 titles in 16 competitions and were French National 3 runners-up in 2022. She moved to mainland France in 2021 to further her coaching development and has been coaching Palente Besançon Handball in the second division since July 2025.

Beyond the sport, Dartron-Lairy sees her role with the Benin selection as a personal project. As someone of African descent, she stated her motivation extends beyond handball itself. Her experience includes developing young talent and coaching at various levels in France and Guadeloupe.

Why it mattersBenin's senior women's handball team will benefit from the technical expertise of a new coach for the 2026 CAN tournament.

Benin proposes electric buses for commuters

The government is considering a new public transport system to reduce travel costs for citizens.

Millions of Beninese citizens face high daily travel expenses due to the lack of a structured public transport system. The current informal sector, relying on motorcycles and modified minibuses, has led to increased road accidents and pollution. President WADAGNI's government is exploring a political choice to implement a new public transport solution.

The proposal focuses on a network of electric buses, starting with the densely populated corridor between Cotonou and Abomey-Calavi. This initiative aims to provide a more affordable and efficient mobility option, drawing lessons from successful public transport models in Dakar and Abidjan. These cities demonstrate that state support is essential for public transport viability.

The government views subsidizing public transport as a social investment, treating mobility as a right rather than a privilege. This move acknowledges the economic reality that public transport systems, like those in Paris and London, often require partial state funding to operate effectively and offer economies of scale.

Why it mattersBeninese citizens with modest incomes will benefit from reduced daily travel costs.
