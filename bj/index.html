Benin loses 2% of farmland in two years

Government aims to halt the decline of cultivable land, which has dropped from 43% to 41% since 2021.

Benin's government is working to stop the shrinking of its agricultural land, which has decreased by 2% in just two years. Antoine Houedji, a land affairs advisor to the agriculture minister, stated on Friday, November 13, 2026, that cultivable land fell from 43% in 2021 to 41% in 2023. Rapid urbanisation is the main cause of this loss.

This warning comes as land reform progresses, with the TERRA BÉNIN and PPMEC II projects launching on November 6, 2026, in Cotonou to improve the e-Foncier land registry platform. The World Bank's representative, Karine Bachongy, outlined the goal: to have an operational cadastre nationwide, map land, identify rights holders, and permanently register land transactions. The ministries of Agriculture and Decentralisation are leading these efforts, gathering prefects to discuss protecting rural agricultural land.

Minister Adin Yéton Bloukounon Goubalan announced that under the Benin Vert 2033 strategy, each commune will have a secured land reserve for the "One Commune, One Green Lung" initiative. The prefect of Zou, Laurent Zomaï, stressed the decisive role of local leaders in securing agricultural land reserves. Minister of Decentralisation Janvier Yahouedehou added that protecting farmland preserves food sovereignty and territorial development.

Why it mattersFarmers in Benin are losing 2% of cultivable land due to urbanisation, impacting food sovereignty and development.

African court rejects Ajavon relatives' damages claim against Benin

The court said the four family members never tried the domestic remedies open to them in Benin before bringing their complaint.

The African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights declared a complaint by four relatives of businessman Sébastien Ajavon against the Benin state inadmissible on 13 November 2026. The family had sought reparations for losses linked to the seizure of their personal bank accounts.

The complaint was filed on 29 November 2019 by Ida Afiavi Goudjo, Ronald K. Steeven Karen Ayité Ajavon, Ludmila Isis Ayélé Ajavon and Evaëlle Jonella Noémie Ayoko Ajavon, who live in Paris as political refugees. They asked for 170 million CFA francs each, or 680 million in total, plus compensation tied to their stakes in the companies COMON and SOCOTRAC.

The court ruled that the applicants had not brought the available cases in Benin. It said civil law allowed action over COMON, and OHADA rules allowed challenges to account seizures. The applicants had neither used nor attempted to use these remedies, so the court did not examine the alleged violations.

Why it mattersThe ruling leaves the family's 680 million CFA franc claim unheard by the court, so any compensation must now be sought through Benin's civil courts or OHADA procedures.

Benin forum questions Africa's democratic future

A two-day symposium in Porto-Novo examines democracy and development in French-speaking Africa.

The Institute of Artisans of Justice and Peace (Iajp/Co) launched its 29th annual symposium in Porto-Novo on Friday, November 13, 2026. Organized with the Konrad Adenauer Foundation, the event gathers academics, civil society actors, and institutional representatives to discuss "Democracy and Development: French-speaking Africa at a Crossroads."

Bishop Aristide Gonsallo of Porto-Novo stressed that economic growth alone does not ensure progress if its benefits do not reach populations, citing persistent gaps between economic performance and living conditions. Kevin Anvo of the Konrad Adenauer Foundation noted improvements in Benin's governance according to Freedom House and the Ibrahim Index, but highlighted a general decline across 23 African nations between 2016 and 2025.

Afrobarometer data shows 64% of Africans prefer democracy, yet only 36% fully endorse it, reflecting citizens' desire for responsive institutions, especially among youth. The symposium will explore rule of law, political alternation, and development conditions, with discussions on governance models and economic policies' impact on populations.

Why it mattersThe symposium in Porto-Novo will examine governance models and economic policies affecting citizens in French-speaking Africa.

Benin's UP-R party sets first ordinary congress for early 2027

The party's National Council approved preparations for the congress, which will end its transitional period and update its foundational texts in Cotonou.

The Union progressiste le Renouveau (UP-R), a Beninese party led by Joseph Djogbénou, held sessions of its Political Bureau and National Council at the Palais des Congrès in Cotonou on Saturday, November 14, 2026. The members adopted resolutions to prepare the party's first ordinary congress, planned for the first quarter of 2027 in Cotonou.

Djogbénou called the first National Council meeting historic, saying it was the first time the party had gathered in that body. The congress is meant to end the party's transitional period, update its founding texts and strengthen its territorial structures. Nearly 400 women were named village and neighbourhood chiefs during the same meeting.

The National Council endorsed the Political Bureau's recommendations on delegates. The party has 118 sections, and each may name up to three extra delegates to the congress, on top of members with automatic seats. The final number of participants will depend on the funds the party raises.
