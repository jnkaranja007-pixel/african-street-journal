Benin Justice Minister Sets New Performance Standards for Courts

Minister Yvon Detchenou mandates faster decision signing and structured court sessions for the 2026-2027 judicial year.

The Beninese judicial system is set for a performance overhaul following a judicial year opening conference in Cotonou. Minister of Justice Yvon Detchenou announced new directives for the 2026-2027 judicial year, emphasizing a shift from intuition-based management to data-driven performance. Key changes include setting clear objectives, regular service quality evaluations, and the digitalization of services.

Under the new framework, courts will operate with defined performance indicators. Specific mandates include setting court dockets with a maximum of 50 cases per session, limiting judges' working sessions to four hours for health preservation, publishing court schedules 48 hours in advance, and requiring decisions to be signed within eight days. Professionalism and punctuality are also stressed across all national courts.

Annual awards will recognize outstanding performance in the judicial sector, based on performance dashboards and reports from the Inspectorate General of Justice. These awards will honor the best-reasoned decision, the most diligent case processing, and the highest-performing court. Judicial leaders, including court presidents and chief clerks, are tasked with implementing and monitoring these new standards.

Why it mattersJudicial officials and staff are directly affected by new performance metrics and deadlines for case processing and decision signing.

SBEE Mobilizes National Production Amidst Nigerian Power Disruptions

Benin's electricity company is tapping domestic sources to counter voltage drops from Nigeria, causing localized outages.

The Société béninoise d’énergie électrique (SBEE) announced on Monday, October 19, that it is activating national production sources to supply the country. This action follows a voltage reduction originating from Nigeria, according to a company communiqué.

The SBEE stated that this mobilization will lead to announced disruptions in some areas. The technical directorate has not specified which localities are affected nor provided an estimated time for full restoration. The company did note that efforts are underway to normalize power supply across the entire territory.

Customers seeking more information can contact the SBEE customer relations center at 7302. The communiqué did not specify the duration of these disruptions or the exact scale of the national production being mobilized.

Why it mattersLocalised power outages affect businesses and households across Benin.

Cavaliers FC Leads Celtiis Ligue 1 After Four Matchdays

Cavaliers FC secured first place with ten points after a strong start to the 2026-2027 season.

Cavaliers FC has surged to the top of the Celtiis Ligue 1 standings after the fourth matchday of the 2026-2027 season. The team, led by Jean Luc Duho, has achieved three victories and one draw, accumulating ten points and a +10 goal difference. This performance contrasts sharply with defending champions Sobemap FC, who are still seeking their first win and have recorded four consecutive draws, including a goalless tie against Panthère FC at home.

The league has seen eighteen new goals added in the latest round of fixtures, bringing the total to seventy-eight goals across the first four matchdays. While Cavaliers FC celebrates its success, other teams are struggling. Dragons FC and Dynamo FC have had particularly difficult starts, with Dragons FC managing only one draw and three losses, while Dynamo FC remains winless with four defeats.

Other notable results include Buffles FC dropping their first points of the season in a 1-1 draw with Espoirs FC, and Hodio FC suffering their inaugural loss, falling 0-1 to ASPAC FC. The league continues to evolve, with teams like Sobemap FC facing early pressure to improve their performance.

Africa Digital Expo Renames to One Africa Digital Forum

Cotonou hosts 300 participants from 17 nations to discuss digital transformation and production capacity.

The 15th Africa Digital Expo (ADEX) in Cotonou, Benin, has rebranded to the One Africa Digital Forum, signaling a shift in focus from digital adoption to production and innovation. The event, held October 19-21, 2026, gathered over 300 participants from 17 countries to explore digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, AI, and public services. The forum aims to accelerate the continent's digital transformation by fostering synergies and innovation.

Syntyche Le Boedec, Director of Cabinet for Benin's Minister of Digital Transformation and Innovation, emphasized the nation's commitment to a digital ecosystem and 5G infrastructure. She stated that while connecting Africa was a past ambition, the current challenge is transforming digital adoption into production and value creation. The forum's inaugural panel addressed critical infrastructure and national strategies for 5G deployment across the continent.

Hind Sidqui, Director General of One Africa Digital Forum, announced the name change, asserting that digital technology is no longer a separate sector but the economy itself. This rebranding reflects the ambition to strengthen collaboration and drive digital advancement. The forum's discussions over two days included B2B meetings and reflections on how digital advancements can spur continental development.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by discussions on digital infrastructure, 5G deployment, and the shift towards digital production and innovation in Africa.

World Athletics Demands South African President's Removal

Athletics South Africa must remove its suspended president and revise its statutes within six months or face further sanctions.

World Athletics has instructed Athletics South Africa (ASA) to immediately remove its suspended president, James Moloi, and to revise the federation's constitution within six months. Failure to comply could result in the matter being escalated to the global body for potential sanctions. A letter dated October 15, signed by World Athletics CEO Jon Ridgeon, also requires ASA to provide a detailed reform timeline within one month.

This directive follows an October 6 disciplinary decision by an independent panel that found Moloi guilty of two charges related to the misuse of federation funds, recommending his removal from the presidency and executive committee. World Athletics and the Confederation of African Athletics have appointed Zakhele Dlamini to oversee ASA's reform process, which includes adopting new statutes and holding fresh elections within three months.

A September mission led by Dlamini identified structural governance weaknesses within ASA, including executive committee divisions, decision-making difficulties, and conflicts of interest from dual national and provincial roles. World Athletics seeks improved separation of duties, independent election and disciplinary mechanisms, and clearer rules on mandates, athlete representation, gender balance, and financial oversight.

Why it mattersAthletics South Africa faces potential sanctions from World Athletics due to governance issues and financial misconduct.
