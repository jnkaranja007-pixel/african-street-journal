Benin finance minister forecasts 300 billion CFA franc surplus

The minister of finance announced the projected surplus for public companies and offices in 2025 at a Cotonou accounting congress.

Benin's Minister of Finance announced a projected surplus of nearly 300 billion CFA francs for public companies and offices in 2025. The announcement was made at the 8th Congress of Accountants of the West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA), which began on October 21, 2026, in Cotonou. The congress, also marking the 20th anniversary of Benin's Order of Chartered Accountants, focuses on financial transparency and sovereignty within the UEMOA zone.

The event, held at the Cotonou Congress Center until October 22, 2026, gathered over 350 participants from more than 18 countries. Discussions centered on the theme 'The Accountant, Guarantor of Transparency and Financial Sovereignty in the UEMOA Space.' Experts, public officials, and economic actors shared knowledge on the role of accountants in state and business governance.

The Minister of Finance officially launched the proceedings, emphasizing the importance of reliable accounts in attracting investment, mobilizing savings, improving financing conditions, and strengthening decision-making capacity. The congress aims to generate operational recommendations to enhance financial governance and regional economic integration.

Why it mattersInvestors can benefit from the projected surplus and improved financial governance, which may attract investment and enhance financing conditions.

Benin lawmakers to review new road traffic code

Parliamentarians will consider proposed measures including legal alcohol limits and child restraint systems during the current session.

Benin's National Assembly will examine a draft road traffic code during its second ordinary session, which began on October 21, 2026. The announcement was made by the assembly's president, Joseph Djogbénou, at the session's opening. Lawmakers participated in a preparatory seminar in Grand-Popo on October 20, engaging with government officials and road safety experts to ready the text for review.

Key proposals within the draft code include establishing a legal blood alcohol content limit, cracking down on driving under the influence of drugs, and addressing excessive speeding. The code also aims to introduce child restraint systems, enhance road safety education in schools, and impose stricter penalties for hit-and-run offenses and failure to obey orders.

The preparatory discussions also covered control mechanisms and sanction scales. The draft legislation will undergo committee review before a plenary session debate. These measures are currently part of a proposed bill and have not yet been enacted.

Why it mattersRoad users in Benin will be directly affected by new rules on alcohol limits, child restraints, and penalties for traffic violations.

Cotonou closes city hall access for road work

Road construction on streets 12 007 and 12 048 will block access to Cotonou's city hall for three months starting October 26.

Access routes to Cotonou's city hall will be closed for three months beginning Monday, October 26, 2026, due to ongoing road construction. The closures affect access points from Boulevard de la Marina and Avenue Jean-Paul II. The announcement comes from the Société des infrastructures routières et de l’aménagement du territoire (SIRAT) in a statement dated October 20.

The road closures are a direct result of the commencement of work on streets 12 007 and 12 048, which are located around the city hall currently under construction. These infrastructure improvements are part of the Asphaltage 1 B project. SIRAT has stated that measures will be put in place to ensure residents can still reach their homes and places of business.

SIRAT has outlined specific arrangements to maintain access for residents and businesses during the construction period. The public is urged to adhere to safety regulations, observe speed limits, and follow the directions provided by traffic agents and the Republican Police.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses near Cotonou's city hall will face temporary access restrictions due to road construction.

Ligue Pro du Bénin names media commission

The Benin Professional Football League has appointed a new ad hoc media commission to improve relations with clubs and journalists.

The Benin Professional Football League (LFPB) has established a new ad hoc media commission, with its members appointed by LFPB President Joël Debally on October 20, 2026, in Cotonou. The commission's primary objective is to enhance the relationship between the LFPB, its clubs, and media professionals. It will also focus on improving the conditions under which media cover league competitions, including streamlining accreditation processes, stadium access, and match-day coordination.

To ensure information reaches all parts of the country, the commission will work with six regional correspondents. The appointed members include Eddy Rabin as president, Hamadou Issa as LFPB communication representative, Salomon Singbo for Ligue 1 clubs, Bienvenu Azoma for Ligue 2 clubs, Pascal Hounkpatin representing the media, Maurice Zounon overseeing accreditations and press relations, and Claude Diogninou responsible for match security.

The regional correspondents appointed are Nouhoum A. Biaou for Atacora-Donga, Bruno Agassounon for Atlantique-Littoral, Aliou Toundé for Borgou-Alibori, Adrien Amoussou for Mono-Couffo, Edmond Houssikinde for Ouémé-Plateau, and Élisée Hounkpatin for Zou-Collines. This structure aims to create a more organized and accessible media environment for the league's events.

Why it mattersJournalists and media professionals will benefit from improved access and coordination for covering football matches.
