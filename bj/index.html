Benin court postpones trial over victim's dreadlocks

A judge in Abomey-Calavi delayed a theft and assault case because the victim wore dreadlocks, citing judicial decorum.

A court in Abomey-Calavi, Benin, postponed a trial on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, because the victim's dreadlocks were deemed inappropriate by the presiding judge. The judge stated that such hairstyles were unacceptable in his court and that the case would be rescheduled to December 30, 2026, to allow the victim to change their appearance.

The case involved alleged theft and voluntary assault. The victim, a young man in his twenties, appeared in court with dreadlocks, prompting the magistrate to halt proceedings. The judge emphasized the need for proper attire and appearance in judicial settings to set a good example for attendees.

This postponement means the accused, who denies the charges and is currently detained, will have to wait longer for their case to be heard. The court's decision highlights a strict stance on judicial appearance in Abomey-Calavi.

Why it mattersThe accused, who denies charges of theft and assault, will remain in detention longer due to the trial's postponement to December 30, 2026.

Benin launches 7.15 billion FCFA climate resilience project

The project aims to protect farmers in nine vulnerable communes from drought, floods, and erratic rainfall.

In Sèmè-Podji on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, Benin's Minister of Agriculture, Livestock, and Fisheries, Adin Yéton Bloukounon Goubalan, launched the Integrated Project to Strengthen Climate Resilience of Vulnerable and Border Communes (Pirrcf). The project, with a budget of 7.15 billion FCFA, is supported by the UNDP, Belgium, and Switzerland and will operate in nine communes.

The initiative seeks to equip rural communities to better withstand droughts, floods, and irregular rainfall through climate-adapted agriculture, hydraulic infrastructure construction, renewable energy promotion, digitalization, and the use of local building materials. Minister Goubalan highlighted the severe impact of climate events on maize and peanut fields, stressing the need for sustainable solutions to protect producers and their incomes.

Gabrielle Spirli, head of Swiss cooperation, advocated for compressed earth bricks and local materials to reduce carbon footprints and create economic opportunities for youth and women. Belgian Ambassador Sandrine Plateau noted the success of a previous program funded with 4 million euros from March 2023 to October 2026 in four communes, emphasizing rigorous, inclusive, and transparent implementation. Dr. El Hadji Fall, the UNDP resident representative in Benin, stated that beneficiary communities must be central to the project, integrating local knowledge, technological innovation, and territorial resources.

Why it mattersThe Pirrcf project will implement climate-adapted agriculture and build hydraulic infrastructure in nine communes to protect farmers' incomes from extreme weather.

Adjohoun adopts first budget under Mayor Hounkanrin

The Benin commune of Adjohoun will operate with a 1.92 billion FCFA budget for 2027.

The commune of Adjohoun has adopted its first budget under Mayor Félicien Hounkanrin, setting a preliminary budget of 1.92 billion FCFA for the 2027 fiscal year. This budget, balanced in revenue and expenditure, was approved during the communal council's fourth ordinary session of the year.

The councilors also approved the 2027 Annual Work Plan (PTA) and the 2027 Annual Investment Plan (PAI). The PTA will guide activity programming and performance monitoring, while the PAI outlines infrastructure projects aimed at meeting population needs and improving living conditions.

The supervision council presented its third-quarter 2026 activity report during the session. The adoption of these financial and planning documents signifies the new elected team's commitment to enhancing administrative efficiency and local public action in Adjohoun.

Why it mattersThe commune of Adjohoun will operate with a 1.92 billion FCFA budget for 2027, impacting local development and public services.

Benin signs AI deal with China

The country becomes the first pilot nation for an AI initiative supported by UNESCO.

Benin's Minister of Higher Education and Scientific Research, Sèdami Medegan Fagla, signed a memorandum of understanding with the Sino-North Africa Education Innovation Association (SNAEIA) in Wuhan on November 11, 2026.

The agreement aims to foster cooperation between Benin and Chinese institutions in artificial intelligence, technological innovation, and vocational training.

As part of the initiative, Benin was designated the first pilot country for an artificial intelligence network program, which will offer training and capacity-building for its youth, with support from the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO).

Why it mattersBenin will become the first pilot country for an artificial intelligence initiative offering training and digital skills development for its youth.

Benin beats Central African Republic 4-1 to top Group F

Benin moved to the top of Group F in the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers after a 4-1 win in Abidjan on Thursday, November 12, 2026.

Benin's national team, Les Guépards, beat the Central African Republic 4-1 on Thursday, November 12, 2026, at the Félix Houphouët-Boigny Stadium in Abidjan, in the third round of qualifiers for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. Imourane Hassane opened the scoring in the 7th minute. Louis Mafouta equalised for the Central African side in the 19th minute, but Rodolfo Aloko and Tosin Aiyegun scored before halftime. Aiyegun added a second goal in the 56th minute.

Coach Gernot Rohr kept the same starting line-up used in Benin's first two matches. Benin drew 1-1 away to Burkina Faso in September, then beat Mauritania 2-1, with Aloko scoring twice in that match. With seven points, Benin now sits alone at the top of Group F, three points ahead of Burkina Faso, who play Mauritania on Friday.

The Central African Republic is still without a point after three defeats and stays in last place. The two sides meet again on Monday, November 16, at 17:00 for the return fixture, the fourth round of the group.

Why it mattersBenin is now first in Group F with seven points, and its next match against the Central African Republic on Monday, November 16, at 17:00 decides who leads the group after the fourth round.
