Benin president replaces military high command

General Abou Issa appointed as the new head of the military on November 4, 2026.

Benin's President Romuald Wadagni replaced the entire military high command on November 4, 2026. General Abou Issa, an officer who previously survived an attempted coup, was appointed to lead the new military leadership.

This extensive reshuffling of the military leadership was confirmed in a Council of Ministers meeting. The move follows months of speculation within the Beninese army about a potential reorganization.

The government also appointed special envoys to the prefects of Benin's 12 departments during the November 4 Council of Ministers. Separately, Haiti's army chief visited Benin to explore military training cooperation.

Why it mattersBenin's military leadership was entirely replaced by President Romuald Wadagni on November 4, 2026.

Tchègnon Amoussou heads SBEE board

Benin's Council of Ministers appointed Tchègnon Amoussou as chairman of the Benin Electricity Company board on November 4, 2026.

Tchègnon Amoussou has been appointed the new president of the board of directors for the Société béninoise d’énergie électrique (SBEE). The government announced the decision on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, as part of broader changes within the Ministry of Energy, Water, and Mines.

This leadership role is distinct from the chief executive position, which manages the company's daily operations. The Council of Ministers also named Alidé Pedro as the director general for Energy Planning and Rural Electrification, and Koffi Germain Tchokpon as technical advisor for Mines.

Gédéon Jesugo Gbedonou was appointed technical advisor for artificial intelligence within the same ministry. These appointments signal shifts in the management and strategic direction of Benin's energy sector.

Why it mattersBenin's electricity company has a new board president, affecting energy supply management.

Cotonou mayor orders cemetery upkeep

Luc Gnacadja inspects PK 14, Zogbo, and Akpakpa cemeteries after three months of maintenance work.

Cotonou Mayor Luc Gnacadja inspected several city cemeteries on October 21, 2026, including PK 14, Zogbo, and Akpakpa. The inspection followed his instructions from three months prior for maintenance work. The visit aimed to assess the cleanliness and improvements at these burial sites.

The mayor emphasized controlling service providers, standardizing grave preparation, and enhancing landscaping. He also recommended beautification efforts like tree planting. Gnacadja laid a wreath on a nameless grave on November 1, Toussaint, to symbolically honor the deceased.

The state of the Danto cemetery, with its overgrowth and lack of maintenance, was noted as particularly concerning. The report suggests that municipal authorities should regularly attend to cemeteries, not just before Toussaint, to ensure they remain clean and accessible.

Why it mattersBenin's citizens visiting cemeteries like Danto are affected by the neglected state of these burial grounds.

Benin prefect tours N’Dali and Pèrèrè communes

Prefect Abdoul-Chakour Naro Assouma met local leaders to discuss security and infrastructure.

Borgou prefect Abdoul-Chakour Naro Assouma began his first official tour on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, in the communes of N’Dali and Pèrèrè. He met with local authorities, religious leaders, and civil society members to discuss security, economic development, and infrastructure needs.

Accompanied by a delegation including members of the Departmental Administrative Conference and the economic and social council, Assouma engaged in consultations in N’Dali in the morning and Pèrèrè in the afternoon. The discussions aimed to assess local governance and gather concerns.

The tour, which included the substitute prosecutor for minors, focused on monitoring communal actions. Each member of the Departmental Administrative Conference presented projects relevant to their sectors during the meetings.

Why it mattersLocal leaders in N’Dali and Pèrèrè raised concerns about security, economic development, and infrastructure to the Borgou prefect.
