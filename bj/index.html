Cotonou hosts Next Fintech Forum 2026, targeting economic growth

The eighth Next Fintech Forum will convene in Cotonou on November twenty-sixth and twenty-seventh, 2026, focusing on industrializing fintech to accelerate economic growth in the.

Cotonou will host the Next Fintech Forum (NFF) in 2026, shifting its focus from technology adoption to creating measurable wealth for states and economies within the UEMOA zone. The event, organized by the Alliance Fintech UEMOA, aims to transform fintech into a structured industry.

The forum, held at the Sofitel Cotonou Marina Hotel & Spa, will bring together public decision-makers, regulators, banks, fintech companies, investors, and international partners. It builds on recommendations from the 2025 Dakar edition and ongoing work on a regional fintech development strategy.

Key innovations for the 2026 edition include the Africa Blockchain & Crypto Conference, launching the UEMOA Crypto Initiative, and dedicated workshops on AI in finance, with the introduction of the Africa AI Center for Finance. These initiatives aim to produce concrete commitments formalized in the “Déclaration de Cotonou.” Alex Sea, President of Alliance Fintech UEMOA, stated that the challenge for 2026 is to transform fintech adoption into economic growth, business financing, employment, productivity, and sustainable prosperity.

Why it mattersThe forum directly impacts businesses and individuals in the UEMOA zone by aiming to create jobs, finance enterprises, and integrate previously marginalized populations into the economic system through fintech.

Sabourou Karim receives Parakou King's blessing for Eloquentia World competition

Sabourou Karim, Benin's national Eloquentia champion, secured the blessing of the King of Parakou before traveling to France for the international finals.

Sabourou Karim, who won the national Eloquentia competition, visited the Royal Palace of Parakou on Thursday, August twenty-first, two thousand twenty-six, to receive blessings from His Majesty the King of Parakou. This symbolic act occurred days before Karim's departure to France for the international Eloquentia competition, where he will represent Benin and Parakou.

A local delegation, including municipal councilor Faïçal Lafia and the Mayor of Youth of Parakou, Farouk Dine MAMA SANNI, accompanied Karim. This support highlights a community effort to back local talent as Karim prepares for the international stage. Lafia stated that a son of Parakou should not travel to represent Benin abroad without blessings from his homeland.

Karim will compete against candidates from various countries in the Eloquentia World competition, which focuses on public speaking, defending ideas, and conveying messages effectively. Over one hundred ten candidates participated in the national competition, with Karim emerging as the winner to represent Benin internationally.

Why it mattersThis event affects young Beninese orators by providing a platform for international recognition and cultural representation.

Sudanese Army Claims Ethiopian Drone Shot Down Near Kurmuk

Sudan's Ministry of Defense announced on Sunday, August twenty-third, that it shot down a drone near Kurmuk, claiming it originated from Ethiopian territory.

The Sudanese Ministry of Defense stated on Sunday that its forces shot down a drone in the Blue Nile State near Kurmuk, identifying it as hostile and asserting it came from Ethiopia. This area is a border region already experiencing conflict between the army and the Rapid Support Forces.

Anadolu reported that Ethiopian authorities had not issued an immediate public response to the claim. The Sudanese authorities' assertion about the drone's Ethiopian origin remains unconfirmed by Ethiopia.

In a conflicting account, the Founding Forces coalition, allied with the Rapid Support Forces, claimed on Monday, August twenty-fourth, that they shot down a Turkish Akinci drone belonging to the Sudanese army in Al-Kurmuk. The governor of Kurmuk, Abdel Ati Mohamed Al-Faki, also stated on Monday that two drones targeting Sudanese army positions were shot down in the border area over two days.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects Sudanese and Ethiopian citizens living near the border due to increased military activity and potential for escalation.
