Bio Tchané hails science "torchbearers" at Djougou camp closing

Seventy secondary students completed a three-week science and math immersion program, culminating in an awards ceremony.

The Abdoulaye Bio Tchané Cultural Center in Djougou hosted the closing ceremony for the second edition of the Summer Excellence Camp and the third edition of the General Competition for Mathematics and Physical Sciences. Initiated by the Agency for the Development of Technical Education (ADET) under the Ministry of Secondary Education, the event celebrated 70 top secondary science students from across Benin. These students spent three weeks at the center, focusing on the theme, "Choosing a scientific field is choosing to understand the world to transform it."

The program concluded with a rigorous competition to identify and reward outstanding students from both the second and first year secondary classes. Awards were presented to the laureates amidst applause from peers and notable political and administrative figures, including the Mayor of Djougou, the Director General of ADET, the Prefect of the Donga department, and national assembly deputies. President Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, President of the Economic and Social Council, was the guest of honor.

Bio Tchané, an advocate for scientific development, emphasized the crucial role of science in national progress, stating that scientific disciplines drive innovation and provide solutions for societal challenges in health, agriculture, and the environment. He thanked the executive, particularly President Romuald Wadagni, for their vision in promoting science education for Benin's youth.

Why it mattersSeventy secondary students were recognized for their achievements in science and mathematics, highlighting the importance of scientific education for national development.

Ten Afro-descendants receive Benin nationality

Ten individuals, primarily from Guadeloupe, officially received their Benin nationality certificates on Saturday, August 29, 2026.

Ten people of Afro-descendant origin, mainly from Guadeloupe, were officially granted Benin nationality on Saturday, August 29, 2026, at the Judicial Complex in Cotonou. The ceremony, presided over by the Keeper of the Seals, Minister of Justice and Legislation Yvon Detchenou, marked a symbolic and legal return for descendants of victims of the transatlantic slave trade.

Recipients like Lavaury Gutemberg Théodore and Kiavué Francelise expressed pride in setting foot on their ancestors' land and solidifying this connection. They hailed the initiative as a historic victory for their deported families. This event follows legal reforms aimed at simplifying the process for granting Benin nationality to individuals of African descent impacted by the slave trade.

The official ceremony included the national anthem and was led by the minister's technical advisor, Marie Rose Sossa. This formal recognition underscores Benin's ongoing efforts to reconnect with its diaspora and acknowledge historical injustices through legal and symbolic gestures.

Why it mattersTen Afro-descendants from Guadeloupe received their Benin nationality, solidifying a legal and symbolic connection to their ancestral land.

Hoovi book launched, becomes pan-African festival

A 70-page compilation of twin culture research and events is now a festival aiming for institutional recognition.

The "Hoovi, un patrimoine vivant" book was officially launched on August 28, 2026, at the Azalaï Hôtel in Cotonou. This event also marked the inauguration of the 6th edition of the Hoovi Week, now elevated to a pan-African festival celebrating twinhood, arts, and living heritage. Initiated in 2021 by Bola Daniel Babayédjou, the project is the first in West Africa to scientifically document twin cultures.

The 70-page book serves as a collective memory, compiling research from the first five editions (2021-2025), including project details, reports, panelist interventions, and recommendations. It aims to provide a scientific, cultural, and social understanding of twinhood and advocate for the lasting institutional recognition of this living heritage in Benin and across Africa. The founder emphasized the book is a tool for transmission and advocacy, not an end in itself.

Looking ahead, the festival plans several innovations for December 2026, including an international artistic residency, a heritage documentary, a culinary village, and social actions. The founder also shared a vision for 2030, proposing a "Cité des Hoovi" with a museum, documentary site, and artisan village. The President of the National Assembly attended, calling for state collaboration to preserve and explain the cultural and environmental knowledge associated with the Hoovi phenomenon.

Why it mattersThe launch of the "Hoovi, un patrimoine vivant" book and its elevation to a pan-African festival aims to achieve lasting institutional recognition for Benin's twin heritage.

Kouandé Youth Summit Calls for Unity Behind Romuald Wadagni

Director General Kassoumou Nassirou urged young people to overcome divisions and support the national development agenda.

The first Kouandé Youth Summit, held from August 26 to 28, transformed the Maison des jeunes into a hub for discussion and commitment. Discussions focused on employment, training, financing, and the commune's future. Kassoumou Nassirou, Director General of the Observatory of Family, Women, and Children (OFFE), delivered a direct message: the youth must move beyond divisions and rally behind the state's development efforts led by Romuald Wadagni.

Organized by the Kouandé Youth Mayor's Office, the summit aimed to empower youth to drive local development rather than just be recipients of public policy. The motto, "United for excellence and innovation, Building the future together," reflected the participants' spirit. Key concerns raised included access to jobs, vocational training, funding for initiatives, entrepreneurship, agriculture, innovation, and youth participation in local governance.

A central theme that emerged was the importance of youth unity and collective influence on Kouandé's future. Nassirou's intervention emphasized generational responsibility, urging young people to build positive leverage for their commune's interests. He called for unity beyond political affiliations and inherited disputes, encouraging support for President Romuald Wadagni's vision to make Kouandé a pioneer in territorial development.

Why it mattersYoung people in Kouandé are affected by calls for unity and support for the national development agenda, which could influence local opportunities and governance.
