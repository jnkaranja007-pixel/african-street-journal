Benin bishops announce African traditional religion conference

The conference, scheduled for November 19-20, 2026, aims to foster mutual understanding between faiths.

The Episcopal Conference of Benin (CEB) will host an international conference on traditional African religion in Cotonou on November 19 and 20, 2026. The event will take place at the Saint-Charbel hall in Akpakpa.

This announcement was made in the final communique from the CEB's 79th ordinary plenary session, held in Natitingou from October 25 to 28. Organizers stated the goal is to deepen mutual knowledge between confessions and promote interreligious dialogue, linking it to African cultures and the continent's integral development.

The conference commemorates two past events involving Pope John Paul II: his 1986 meeting on peace in Assisi, Italy, and his 1993 visit to Cotonou where he met with leaders of indigenous religions and Vodun cults.

Why it mattersBeninese citizens who practice traditional African religions and those involved in interreligious dialogue will participate in the conference.

Politics · 2026-11-01

British PM open to slavery apology, rejects financial reparations

Andy Burnham proposes debt relief for poor nations instead of direct payments for historical slave trade role.

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham has stated he is open to discussing official apologies for the UK's role in the transatlantic slave trade. In a statement published on October 30, Burnham proposed dialogue with affected countries and populations but rejected financial reparations.

Burnham clarified that this openness does not constitute an apology from the British state, suggesting it should be part of future discussions. He argued that current Britons cannot be held financially responsible for historical crimes they did not participate in. Instead, he proposed an international initiative to ease the debt burden of poor nations by extending repayment terms and lowering interest rates, with former Prime Minister Gordon Brown assisting.

Caribbean nations, including Jamaica, have presented a 10-point plan through CARICOM that includes apologies, support for health and education, and measures addressing the economic and social consequences of slavery. Jamaica submitted a petition to King Charles III on September 7, seeking referral of legal questions to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council regarding potential UK reparation obligations. These discussions are set to precede the Commonwealth leaders' summit on November 1 in Antigua and Barbuda.

Why it mattersThe British Prime Minister's proposal on debt relief and apologies for the slave trade affects African nations and the diaspora who are seeking redress for historical injustices.

Sport · 2026-11-01

Benin calls up Parfait Faton for CAN 2027 qualifiers

The Guépards will face the Central African Republic on November 12 and November 16 in Abidjan and Douala.

Benin's national football team, the Guépards, will play the Central African Republic on November 12 in Abidjan and November 16 in Douala for the third and fourth rounds of the CAN 2027 qualifiers. Midfielder Parfait Faton received his first call-up to the senior team, named among 25 players by coach Gernot Rohr on Saturday, October 31, 2026.

The squad includes experienced players like goalkeepers Marcel Dandjinou and Saturnin Allagbé, midfielders Sessi d’Almeida and Junior Olaïtan, and forwards Jodel Dossou, Aiyegun Tosin, Rodolfo Aloko, and Yamirou Ouorou. Dodo Dokou is also included in the group.

Benin currently has four points from their first two matches in Group F, having drawn 1-1 with Burkina Faso on September 25 and beaten Mauritania 2-1 on September 29 in Abidjan.

Why it mattersBenin's national football team will play two matches for CAN 2027 qualification.
