Beninese Citizens Want Traditional Chiefs Politically Neutral

A new Afrobarometer survey reveals that seventy-five percent of citizens believe traditional leaders should remain outside of partisan politics.

Seventy-five percent of Beninese citizens want traditional chiefs to remain politically neutral, according to an Afrobarometer survey. The study, conducted by IREG, found that while citizens value the social and community role of traditional authorities, they wish for them to stay out of elections. This sentiment reflects a commitment to democratic principles and independent voting, especially given the continued influence of traditional leaders within communities.

Despite the desire for political neutrality, a majority of respondents acknowledge the positive contributions of traditional chiefs to local governance. Sixty percent of those surveyed want to see their influence in community and local affairs strengthened. Furthermore, fifty-four percent believe traditional authorities help reinforce democracy in Benin, indicating trust in these figures for social mediation and conflict prevention.

The survey results emerge following the adoption of a law in April 2025 that legally recognizes ninety traditional chiefdoms nationwide. While this law limits their roles to symbolic, cultural, and advisory functions, it acknowledges their importance in promoting social cohesion and dialogue. Afrobarometer highlights a public aspiration to maintain the moral authority of chiefs while ensuring their impartiality in political matters.

Why it mattersSeventy-five percent of Beninese citizens are affected by the desire for traditional chiefs to remain politically neutral, impacting their voting choices and the governance of their communities.

Wadagni Adopts Presidential Style Amidst Talon's Shadow

One hundred days into his presidency, Romuald Wadagni is asserting his leadership with a focus on social measures and diplomatic engagement.

Romuald Wadagni, now president, is making his mark on Benin's governance just over three months after his inauguration. His approach is characterized by a sober style, the implementation of social measures, and active diplomatic efforts. This period follows the election of Patrice Talon to the new Senate on August 6, an event that has reignited concerns about Wadagni's autonomy in leading the country.

Political observers in Benin are questioning the extent of Wadagni's independent decision-making power. These suspicions echo sentiments from the final months of Talon's previous presidential term, particularly after the selection of Wadagni as his successor. The transition has brought renewed scrutiny to the dynamics of power within the nation's leadership.

Wadagni's official visit to Nigeria in June 2026, as documented by the Benin Presidency's Facebook page, highlights his diplomatic activities. Despite these engagements, the presence of Patrice Talon, now serving as president of the Senate, continues to cast a significant shadow over the presidential palace, leading to ongoing debate among political analysts.

Why it mattersBenin's political observers are questioning President Romuald Wadagni's autonomy in governing the country, with Patrice Talon now serving as Senate president.

Benin's Culture Sector Transforms Under New Leadership

Ousmane Alédji highlights significant reforms and state interest in arts and tourism since 2016, calling it a revolution.

The cultural and arts sector in Benin has seen a profound transformation since 2016, marked by numerous reforms under the current administration. Ousmane Alédji, speaking in an interview, described this period as a "revolution" driven by increased engagement from high-level government officials, including the President. This direct state interest has revitalized the sector, fostering a new dynamic that Alédji believes is unprecedented.

Alédji pointed to the development of modern museums as a key indicator of this progress. Projects include the construction of international-standard museums in Abomey, Porto Novo, and Ouidah, with more planned for Allada. These initiatives represent a significant shift from previous efforts, which primarily focused on renovating existing royal palaces. The Vodun Days festival is cited as a visible, though small, component of these broader cultural development policies.

The "revolution" in Benin's cultural landscape is characterized by a renewed focus on arts and tourism, supported by substantial government investment in infrastructure and policy. Alédji emphasized that the presence of quality interlocutors within the government, starting with the Head of State, has been crucial in driving these changes. This sustained attention has led to a general awakening and repositioning of the cultural sector.

Why it mattersThe transformation of Benin's cultural sector and the development of new museums and festivals directly affect artists and cultural professionals by creating new platforms and opportunities.

Eskom net profit more than doubles to 30.3 billion rand

South Africa's state-owned power utility reported a second consecutive profitable year, driven by tariff increases and cost savings.

South African public group Eskom announced on August 31, 2026, a net profit of 30.3 billion rand for the fiscal year ending March 31, 2026. This figure, more than double the 14 billion rand from the previous year, marks the second consecutive profitable year for the country's primary electricity provider, which has long struggled with financial crises and frequent power outages.

Revenue increased by 4.1% to 354.7 billion rand, while operating profit (EBITDA) rose by 10.9% to 108.6 billion rand, despite a 6.2% decrease in electricity sales to 178 terawatt-hours. These gains were largely attributed to a 12.74% average tariff increase granted by the National Energy Regulator of South Africa (Nersa), internal cost-saving programs amounting to 22.4 billion rand, and a 10.6 billion rand reduction in fuel costs for backup gas power plants.

Eskom's financial recovery remains fragile due to a 17.9% increase in municipal non-payment, reaching 111.6 billion rand, a debt projected to climb to 358 billion rand by 2031 without intervention. Industrial demand also fell by 22.5%, partly due to increased solar self-generation by large companies. The company plans to invest 343 billion rand over five years to modernize its production and transmission infrastructure.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by Eskom's improved financial performance and future investment plans, which signal potential stability and growth in the energy sector.

Teachers demand career review after delayed integration

Contract teachers from the 2014 cohort are calling for administrative decisions to be re-examined due to a delayed professional integration date.

A collective of contract teachers, including ACDPE, EPA, and EPC from the 2014 promotion, held a press conference on Monday, August 31, 2026, in Cotonou. After twelve years of service, they are seeking a review of administrative decisions impacting their professional advancement. The group has appealed to the President and several government ministers, including those responsible for secondary education, higher education, budget, and public function.

Central to their demands is the date of integration into the Professor Adjoint and Professor Certifié corps. An interministerial order from June 27, 2025, designated the 2018-2019 and 2020-2021 school years as professional training periods. However, the teachers argue that based on a November 21, 2015 decree, their two-year training should have concluded by 2022, allowing integration from January 1, 2023. Instead, training began in December 2022 and ended in September 2024, with integration set for January 1, 2025.

The collective disputes the administration's rationale for the delayed integration, arguing that the beneficiaries should not be penalized for administrative delays. They also raised concerns about seniority, noting that an addendum from May 13, 2026, integrated them into new corps with one step advancement, a measure the administration justified based on unspecified factors.

Why it mattersTeachers are affected by a delayed professional integration and a dispute over seniority, impacting their career progression and salary.
