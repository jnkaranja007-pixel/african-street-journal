Health · 2026-10-28

Benin trains first drug quality control specialists

12 graduates from the University of Abomey-Calavi are now equipped to ensure the safety of medicines.

Benin has trained its first 12 specialists in drug quality control, graduating on Monday, October 26, 2026. This Master's program, the first of its kind in Benin, aims to protect citizens from risks associated with unsafe medicines and health products. The graduates, with backgrounds in biology, chemistry, and pharmacy, will verify the quality of products to prevent deaths from substandard items.

Professor Archille YEMOA, the Master's Coordinator, stated that the program was created due to a recognized need for quality control specialists in Benin and the wider sub-region. The graduates are prepared to work in pharmaceutical, agri-food, and cosmetic industries, as well as regulatory agencies. The University of Abomey-Calavi's Faculty of Health Sciences developed the program to strengthen national capacity.

The Minister of Health, Professor Benjamin HOUNKPATIN, who was the patron of the graduating class, urged the new specialists to uphold ethics, rigor, and empathy in their work. The Rector of the University, Professor Charlemagne IGUE, reminded them of their duty to serve humanity and protect public health. This initiative enhances Benin's ability to ensure product quality and safeguard its population.

Why it mattersBenin's population will be better protected from unsafe medicines and health products.

Benin launches sixth phase of commune development fund

The FADeC VI program will support investments in all 77 communes, with a resilience component for 27 northern communes.

Benin launched the sixth phase of its Communal Development Support Fund (FADeC VI) on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, in Cotonou. The program, organized by the Local Investment Fund (FIC), aims to finance communal investments across all 77 communes and bolster resilience in 27 northern communes facing violent extremism. An initial two-day workshop is planned to refine implementation measures.

FADeC VI includes a general investment window for all communes and a Sahel Window targeting 27 northern communes for resilience and social cohesion projects chosen with community input. Financial partners, including German, European Union, and Swiss cooperation, have reaffirmed their support for decentralization and improving access to basic infrastructure. Christiane Kuadjo, representing the Minister of Budget, urged for better project quality and FIC support in diversifying commune financing.

The FIC's Director General, Koumagbeafide Réginal, stated the fund aims to be a reference partner for communes, offering financial resources and technical support to diversify funding sources and consolidate investment capacity. The program benefits from contributions from KfW, the European Union, and Swiss Cooperation, particularly for the Sahel Window. Stéphanie Küraten, Director of KfW, affirmed continued commitment to the partnership.

Why it mattersBenin's FADeC VI program will support investments in all 77 communes, with a specific resilience component for 27 northern communes.

Business · 2026-10-28

Benin official urges better business access to capital

Abdoulaye Bio Tchané called for more guarantees and financial transparency to help companies secure funding.

Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, president of Benin's Economic and Social Council, called for improved access to capital markets for private businesses. He made this plea on Monday, October 26, 2026, at the Capital Reach Forum in Cotonou. Bio Tchané highlighted that the regional stock market's progress alone is insufficient to meet business needs, noting a concentration of listed companies in a few sectors and the dominance of state bonds.

Bio Tchané advocates for guarantee and risk-sharing mechanisms to facilitate private sector financing. He also stressed the need to mobilize more savings from institutional investors, individuals, and the diaspora. Financial transparency must strengthen the confidence needed for this mobilization, according to him. The forum, organized by SGI AGI, aimed to connect companies with investors and financial intermediaries.

The Minister of Economy and Finance, Aristide Médénou, announced at the forum that the SME guarantee fund, established in July 2024 with an initial endowment of 5 billion FCFA, has received central bank approval and will soon be operational. This fund is designed to share risks with banks and microfinance institutions, thereby easing business access to credit.

Why it mattersBeninese businesses and investors are affected by the call for better access to capital markets and the upcoming operationalization of the SME guarantee fund.

Benin fashion show unveils 16 ocean-inspired indigo patterns

Couleur Indigo designer Nadia Adanlé presented the "Apusomɛ" collection on the shores of Lake Toho.

On October 24, 2026, the Couleur Indigo brand launched its 19th collection, "Apusomɛ," featuring 16 new indigo patterns inspired by the seabed. Designer Nadia Adanlé presented the creations on the shores of Lake Toho in Ouidah, Benin.

The "Apusomɛ" collection combines textile art with historical memory, aiming to preserve and transmit knowledge of indigo dyeing. Adanlé stated the goal is to promote indigo fabric and enhance its image, noting motifs like fish, seashells, and corals were drawn from the Atlantic Ocean.

One pattern, "Filimi," meaning "Remember Me," was created to honor those lost at sea. Adanlé explained this motif serves as a fabric memorial for those who perished in the Atlantic without graves.

Why it mattersBeninese artisans and designer Nadia Adanlé are promoting cultural heritage through the "Apusomɛ" fashion collection.

Sport · 2026-10-28

CAF Women's Champions League schedule released

The tournament begins November 1 in Ismailia, Egypt, with eight clubs competing for the African title.

The Confederation of African Football (CAF) has unveiled the full match schedule for the 2026 Women's Champions League, set to kick off on November 1 in Ismailia, Egypt. Eight clubs will compete in the tournament, which concludes with the final on November 15. The competition offers the winner a spot in the 2027 FIFA Women's Champions League qualification round.

The eight participating teams are divided into two groups of four. Group A includes FC Masar (Egypt), Mamelodi Sundowns (South Africa), FUS Rabat (Morocco), and Lekié Football Filles (Cameroon). Group B features AS FAR (Morocco), Kawempe Muslim Ladies (Uganda), USFAS Bamako (Mali), and an as-yet-undetermined representative from the UFOA B zone.

The group stage commences on November 1 with FUS Rabat facing Lekié Football Filles, followed by FC Masar against Mamelodi Sundowns. The semi-finals are scheduled for November 11, with the third-place match on November 14 and the final on November 15 at the Suez Canal Stadium.

Why it mattersUSFAS Bamako from Mali is participating in the tournament.
