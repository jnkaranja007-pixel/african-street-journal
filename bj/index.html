UBA Benin Seeks Legal Assistant for Compliance and Contracts

The bank is hiring a legal assistant to manage litigation, contracts, and provide counsel, ensuring local compliance and adherence to group standards.

UBA Benin has announced a recruitment drive for a Legal Assistant tasked with overseeing contentious matters, contracts, and legal advice. This role is crucial for aligning the subsidiary's legal policies and procedures with group standards while ensuring full compliance with all local Beninese legal requirements.

The successful candidate will be responsible for appointing, managing, and evaluating legal partners, including lawyers and bailiffs, assigned by the bank. They will also ensure the execution and respect of the bank's legal commitments to third parties and vice versa, alongside providing specialized support on intellectual property matters.

Key duties include drafting legal documents such as service and maintenance contracts, monitoring litigation involving the bank, and representing UBA Benin in various procedures and before administrative bodies like the Labor Inspectorate. The role also involves advising management on legal implications of operational actions and training staff on fundamental legal issues.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the need for a legal assistant to ensure compliance and manage contractual obligations, which impacts the bank's operational risk and regulatory standing.

ORANA launches industrial fruit concentrate production in Benin

The new agro-industrial plant in Za-Kpota began operations on August 15, 2026, transforming local oranges and pineapples into aseptic concentrates for the food and beverage.

The ORANA factory in Za-Kpota, Zou department, officially started its industrial operations on August 15, 2026. This agro-industrial unit specializes in transforming locally produced oranges and pineapples into aseptic concentrates. These products are intended for professionals in the beverage and agri-food sectors.

The plant utilizes a fully automated FENCO production line, handling fruit reception, extraction, juice concentration at low temperatures using vacuum evaporators, pulp treatment, and aseptic packaging. ORANA emphasizes the traceability of its raw materials, sourced locally, and its capacity to deliver directly to industrial clients.

This development marks a significant step in local fruit processing in Benin. The factory aims to transform a larger volume of the country's fruit production, offering concentrates for beverages, cocktails, pastries, and the broader agri-food industry. Technical skills are also being transferred to local teams.

Why it mattersFarmers can sell more locally grown oranges and pineapples to the ORANA factory for processing into concentrates, potentially increasing their income.

Benin Schools Introduce Free Tuition for Girls, New Agricultural Courses

Starting with the 2026-2027 school year, Benin's public secondary schools will offer free tuition to all girls, alongside expanded agricultural and vocational training programs.

Benin's education system is set to undergo significant changes with the upcoming 2026-2027 school year, beginning September 14. A key reform is the introduction of free tuition for all girls enrolled in public general and technical secondary education nationwide. This measure, approved by the Council of Ministers in June 2026, aims to promote equal opportunities and improve female student retention.

In addition to the tuition policy, technical agricultural high schools will launch new training programs leading to a Technician Diploma in various agricultural trades. These include poultry, rabbit, and unconventional livestock farming; cattle and small ruminant husbandry; pig farming; pisciculture and aquaculture; food and ornamental horticulture; cereal and legume production; root and tuber cultivation; fiber and textile plant production; fruit, forest, and non-timber forest product cultivation; and oil palm and coconut farming.

Vocational training is also being enhanced, with inscriptions opening at 13 public Vocational and Apprenticeship Training Centers (CFPA) across locations like Abomey, Djougou, and Nikki. These centers will offer training for Certificates of Qualification in Trades (CQM) and Professional Qualification (CQP). Inscriptions opened August 17 and close September 25, 2026, with the apprenticeship term commencing September 28, 2026. The government intends these changes to better align education and training with youth employment needs.

Why it mattersFarmers can access new agricultural training programs and potentially benefit from increased food production due to more skilled workers, while investors may see opportunities in the expanding agricultural sector and vocational training.

Official Tours Benin Craft Fair, Hears Artisan Concerns

Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, president of the Economic and Social Council, visited the regional craft fair in Nikki to engage with 75 exhibitors and understand their working conditions.

Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, president of Benin's Economic and Social Council (CES), visited the third regional craft fair in Nikki, which runs from August 21 to 30. Organized by the Ministry of Small and Medium Enterprises and Employment Promotion, the event features 75 exhibitors showcasing local craftsmanship. Tchané's visit aimed to listen to and dialogue with artisans about their skills and work environments.

Accompanied by CES officials, Tchané toured the fair, examining the diverse artisanal products, including textiles, sculptures, and pottery. He inquired about the artisans' production methods, their working conditions, and the difficulties they face. Biaou François ATCHADE, a CES councilor, noted that artisans appreciated the president's interest and the advice offered, expressing a desire for continued support and the fair's expansion.

The fair, held in conjunction with the Gaani 2026 festivities, serves as a platform to promote the North's artisanal expertise and boost the visibility and sales of local creations. This visit aligns with the CES's renewed mission to engage directly with citizens and understand on-the-ground realities, allowing Tchané to gather artisans' concerns and expectations firsthand.

Why it mattersThe visit of the Economic and Social Council president to the regional craft fair directly impacts artisans by providing a platform for their concerns to be heard and potentially addressed, influencing their business conditions.

African Officials Complete Arms Control Training in Benin

Twenty-two diplomats and security personnel from 16 African nations concluded a five-day intensive training program focused on light arms control.

The second African edition of the UNREC training on the control of light and small arms concluded Friday in Ouidah, Benin. Twenty-two fellows from 16 countries received certificates and enhanced skills to bolster security within Africa. The intensive five-day program, held at the Centre for Post-Conflict Actions Improvement (CPADD), complemented theoretical phases conducted in Togo.

Participants, primarily diplomats and law enforcement officials, covered international frameworks, arms and ammunition management, marking, registration, tracing, stock management, destruction, border security, transfers, national commissions, and national action plans. They also explored gender, youth, and armed violence prevention dimensions. Organizers noted that the training was a two-way exchange, with participants also sharing their insights.

The training culminated in practical exercises and field visits, with participants developing project proposals to address light arms control challenges in their respective countries. One hundred percent of the fellows passed a final test, with a project by a Moroccan participant receiving first prize. The UNREC plans to monitor these projects and offer financial support for some implementation efforts.

Why it mattersAfrican diplomats and security personnel are better equipped to manage and control light arms within their countries following this training.
