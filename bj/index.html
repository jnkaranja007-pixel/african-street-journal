Benin Beach Tour hosts 23 teams in Cotonou

The second stage of the 2026 Benin Beach Tour will feature 11 women's and 12 men's teams competing from October 23 to 25.

The Benin Beach Tour (BBT) is organizing its second stage of the 2026 edition from October 23 to 25 on Erevan beach in Cotonou. 23 teams, including 11 women's and 12 men's formations, will compete over three days. This competition is an important passage towards the season's grand finale.

Soulé Arouna, the deputy national technical director for beach volleyball, explained the project's philosophy to the press on Wednesday, October 21, at the Benin Volleyball Federation headquarters. The BBT aims to provide regular competition and establish a ranking system to track team performance. The federation, supported by its president Ali Yaro, is backing this initiative.

The Benin Volleyball Federation secretary general, Cokou Romain Ahlinvi, stated the goal is to gain international recognition for the BBT and enable athletes to participate in ranking competitions. Foreign teams may participate pending necessary approvals. The event includes a technical meeting on Friday, matches starting Saturday, and finals on Sunday.

Why it matters23 beach volleyball teams from Benin will compete in Cotonou for the second stage of the 2026 Benin Beach Tour.

Cotonou port to open weekends for cargo pickup

Goods can be collected on Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays starting October 24, 2026.

The Port Autonomous de Cotonou will operate weekend services for cargo pickup starting Saturday, October 24, 2026. This measure, announced by the port's general directorate, aims to speed up the exit of goods from the port.

The new schedule involves customs, stevedores, consignees, port services, livestock and fisheries departments, Benin Control, and banks. The port authority seeks to reduce delays from administrative procedures and prevent cargo accumulation within the port.

This operational change follows infrastructure upgrades at the port, including the construction of 400 meters of quay completed in the first phase of extension work.

Why it mattersBusinesses and individuals relying on goods passing through the Port of Cotonou will experience faster clearance.

Benin launches seventh 'consume local' month

75 local producers are exhibiting at PK3 market in Cotonou until October 24.

Benin's Ministry of Commerce launched the seventh "Consume Local" month on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, at the PK3 market in Cotonou. The event, running from October 20 to 24, features 75 local producers showcasing products made in Benin. The theme for this edition is "Consume local for a deep economic benefit."

Cotonou's third deputy mayor, Irène Françoise Béhanzin, stated that economic resilience is built through local consumption habits. Ferdinand Gomez, departmental director of internal trade, emphasized that national sovereignty is produced in Benin's workshops and fields. He noted that buying local supports the UEMOA economic bloc.

Gilbert Déou Malè, Prefect of Littoral, encouraged producers as the foundation of socio-economic development and urged Cotonou residents to support them. Benjamin Alanmènou, technical advisor to the Ministry of Commerce, called buying Beninese an act of economic patriotism that supports jobs.

Why it mattersBeninese citizens are encouraged to buy local products to support domestic producers and create jobs.

Benin lawmakers to review new road code

The proposed legislation includes legal alcohol limits, child restraints, and stricter penalties for hit-and-runs.

Benin’s parliament will examine a new road traffic code during its ordinary session, which began on October 21, 2026. The proposed law aims to modernize regulations dating back to 1956 and adapt them to current traffic conditions and accident reduction goals. Lawmakers attended a preparatory seminar in Grand-Popo on October 20 to discuss the draft with government officials and road safety experts.

Key measures in the draft code include establishing a legal blood alcohol content limit, cracking down on driving under the influence of narcotics, and addressing excessive speeding. The legislation also proposes child restraint systems, road safety education in schools, and tougher sanctions for drivers who flee accident scenes or refuse to comply with authorities.

The reform package, presented by transport sector officials, will undergo a commission review before a plenary session debate. The proposed changes are part of an effort to update the country's traffic laws and improve road safety.

Why it mattersBenin's parliament will review a new road traffic code that includes measures for road safety.

Benin handball team appoints French coach

Euphémie Dartron-Lairy will lead the senior women's national team for the 2026 CAN tournament.

French coach Euphémie Dartron-Lairy has been appointed technical coordinator for Benin's senior women's handball team. She will lead the "Amazones" in the CAN Seniors Dames 2026 tournament, scheduled for December 2-12 in Tunisia. Dartron-Lairy aims to develop Benin's women's handball by focusing on human connection, team cohesion, and performance.

Dartron-Lairy, who has a background in coaching and sports management in France and Guadeloupe, described herself as a "facilitator of potential." Her previous coaching experience includes leading a men's team to 13 titles in 16 competitions and achieving a French national runner-up position in 2022. She also directs the Palente Besançon Handball team in the second division.

Beyond the sporting challenge, Dartron-Lairy sees her role with the Benin selection as a personal project. As someone of African descent, she stated that her motivation for coaching the team runs deeper than just handball.

Why it mattersBenin's senior women's handball team will compete in the 2026 CAN tournament under new leadership.
