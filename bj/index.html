Benin launches national review of coastal state stability mechanism

The interior minister opened a Cotonou workshop to assess three years of the Coastal State Stability Mechanism and plan its future.

Benin's Minister of Interior and Public Security, Djibril MAMA CISSÉ, launched a national workshop in Cotonou on October 27, 2026, to review the Coastal State Stability Mechanism (MSEC). The three-year-old program, run by the International Organization for Migration (IOM) and funded by the UK, Germany, and the Netherlands, aims to counter violent extremism in border communes.

The workshop brought together government officials, including the Minister of Maternal and Primary Education Armand Kuyema NATTA, ambassadors from partner countries, and the head of the IOM office. Participants will evaluate MSEC's results, identify challenges, and define strategic orientations for the next three years, focusing on local ownership, youth inclusion, and strengthening state presence.

German Ambassador Stefan BUCHWALD emphasized that MSEC targets areas with acute security challenges to rebuild trust between populations and public institutions. He expressed hope that the workshop would preserve MSEC's flexible approach, tangible state presence, and focus on vulnerable communes.

Why it mattersBenin's government is working to improve stability in border areas exposed to violent extremism through a program involving international partners and local communities.

Dadjè FC removed from Benin's Ligue 2

The Benin Professional Football League cited invalid federal licenses and unqualified players for the decision.

Dadjè FC will no longer be scheduled in the Celtiis Ligue 2 from the fifth matchday of the 2026-2027 season. The Benin Professional Football League's Management Council issued this notification on October 28, 2026, after reviewing the club's administrative status.

The league cited the absence of valid federal licenses and the use of unqualified players in early matches. The council determined that Dadjè FC does not currently meet the necessary regulatory conditions to continue in the championship.

This administrative measure does not preclude further sporting or disciplinary actions from competent bodies. These licensing issues had previously prevented the club from playing its first match of the season, resulting in a 3-0 loss on the green carpet against Abeilles FC.

Why it mattersDadjè FC, a club that won the Benin championship in June 2025, has been removed from the second division league due to administrative and player eligibility issues.

Benin's Bio Tchané attends AICESIS meeting in Madrid

Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, president of Benin's Economic and Social Council, met in Madrid to discuss social justice and cooperation.

Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, president of Benin's Economic and Social Council (CES), arrived in Madrid on Wednesday, October 28. He is attending the statutory meeting of the International Association of Economic and Social Councils and Similar Institutions (AICESIS) on October 29 and 30.

The meeting agenda includes reviewing the association's activity and financial reports, and discussing its partnership with the International Labour Organization. A key topic is the just transition, focusing on social support for economic changes. A joint session with the Ibero-American Network of Economic and Social Councils is planned for October 30 to explore cooperation.

This Madrid meeting follows Bio Tchané's participation in international discussions on social dialogue. From September 28 to 30, he represented the CES at a forum on French-speaking social dialogue and the General Assembly of the Union of Councils of Economic and Social Institutions of Francophone States and Governments in Bucharest, where crisis prevention and social cohesion were discussed.

Why it mattersBenin's representative, Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, is engaged in international discussions on social justice and economic cooperation.
