Benin President Mediates Celestial Church Dispute

Former President Patrice Talon, now Senate President, is working to ease tensions within the Celestial Church of Christ and announced upcoming legislation on religious.

Hundreds gathered on August 29th in Saint-Ouen, near Paris, for an information meeting organized by the France diocese of the Church of Celestial Christianity. The unexpected guest at this religious forum was Patrice Talon, the former head of state of Benin and current President of the Senate. His presence signals a high-level engagement in resolving internal disputes within the church.

Talon's intervention comes after a significant step towards reconciliation within the Church of Celestial Christ. On April 30, 2026, he received the final report from the Superior Council of Transition at the Sofitel Cotonou Marina, a process aimed at healing divisions. This involvement suggests a continued effort by the former president to influence the political landscape through religious mediation.

The former president's actions indicate a desire to maintain a role in national affairs by addressing sensitive internal church matters. The announcement of a forthcoming law on the organization of worship in Benin, made during his address in the Paris region, is seen as a strategic move to manage religious affairs and potentially consolidate his influence.

Why it mattersThe former president's mediation efforts and the announcement of new legislation on religious organization directly impact the governance and internal affairs of the Celestial Church of Christ in Benin.

Benin U17 Amazons Draw Burkina Faso, Ivory Coast in UFOA-B Tournament

The Benin U17 women's football team will compete in Group A against Burkina Faso and Ivory Coast in the upcoming UFOA-B tournament.

The Benin U17 women's national football team, known as the Amazons, has learned its opponents for the group stage of the UFOA-B U17 Tournament. Following a draw held in Ouagadougou on Thursday, September 3, 2026, the young Benin players were placed in Group A. They will face counterparts from Burkina Faso and Ivory Coast in this phase of the competition.

Coach Idah Azonsou's squad aims to secure a qualifying spot for the 2027 U17 Africa Cup of Nations finals. The tournament is scheduled to take place from September 15 to September 27, 2026, in Yamoussoukro, Ivory Coast. This event marks a significant opportunity for the young athletes to showcase their talent on a continental stage.

The draw places Benin in a challenging group, requiring them to perform well against regional rivals to advance. Success in this tournament is crucial for the development of women's football in Benin and for the aspirations of these young players to reach higher levels of competition.

Why it mattersYoung female athletes from Benin will compete in a regional football tournament, with the potential to qualify for a continental championship.

Wadagni Accelerates Benin's Economic Path

Benin's new head of state, Romuald Wadagni, continues established economic policies while emphasizing social spending and growth distribution.

Benin's first one hundred days under President Romuald Wadagni signal an acceleration rather than a break in economic strategy. The former Minister of Economy and Finance, now head of state, is focused on maintaining the country's financial stability, which is well-regarded by markets. This approach aims to preserve existing economic equilibria while seeking to translate them into tangible benefits across different regions.

Wadagni's administration is prioritizing social expenditures and a more equitable distribution of economic growth among the nation's territories. This focus aims to ensure that the benefits of the country's economic performance are felt more broadly, addressing potential disparities and fostering inclusive development. The strategy seeks to build upon the foundations laid by the previous administration.

The economic direction under Wadagni appears to be a continuation of the trajectory set under Patrice Talon. The emphasis remains on prudent financial management and fostering an environment attractive to investors. The new leadership's commitment to social spending and territorial growth distribution suggests an effort to balance fiscal responsibility with social equity.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the continuation of stable economic policies and a focus on growth distribution, which may influence investment decisions.

African Russian cultural centers face private management amid sanctions

State agency Rossotrudnichestvo's assets are frozen by the EU, leading to externalized management of Russian cultural centers in Africa.

The European Union has frozen the financial assets of Rossotrudnichestvo, the Russian state agency overseeing cultural centers worldwide, since July 2022. This measure aimed to disrupt Moscow's public diplomacy tools abroad. Despite these sanctions, some centers, like the one in Paris, continue their operations, offering language courses and cultural events.

Several European governments have unilaterally terminated agreements with local Russian cultural centers. Romania closed its center in February 2023, followed by Denmark and Moldova, which is set to close its Chisinau branch in July 2026. Germany formally ended bilateral agreements due to suspected intelligence activities.

Investigations reveal that some Russian cultural centers in Africa are now managed by private entities. These centers are reportedly used as geopolitical influence tools and recruitment channels, operating outside the European Union's direct financial oversight.

Why it mattersAfrican Russian cultural centers are being managed by private entities, potentially serving as geopolitical influence and recruitment channels.

Congo Teachers Demand Back Pay, Threaten School Year Start

Twelve thousand community teachers in Congo-Brazzaville are demanding ten months of unpaid allowances and threaten a general strike if not paid.

Approximately twelve thousand community teachers in Congo-Brazzaville are demanding payment of their outstanding allowances. These educators, who support the national education system without civil servant status, insist on receiving their back pay before the upcoming school year begins. The teachers have accumulated a deficit of ten months of unpaid allowances from the Congolese state.

To ensure a peaceful school reopening and avoid disruptions, the protesting teachers have set a condition: at least four months of their owed sums must be settled before they return to classrooms. They organized a protest in the capital, Brazzaville, in front of the National Institute for Pedagogical Research and Action, under the banner of the Coalition of Community Teacher Structures of Congo (CSECC).

Beyond financial demands, the protesters criticize the authorities' proposed conditions for permanent employment, rejecting the requirement for a competitive exam for public service integration. The CSECC argues that their years of experience justify direct integration. If their demands regarding allowances and recruitment are not met, the community teachers plan to initiate a general strike at the start of October 2026.

Why it mattersTwelve thousand community teachers are affected by the non-payment of ten months of allowances, threatening the start of the school year.
