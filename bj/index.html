Wadagni and To Lam target rice, cashew and cotton trade

The Benin and Vietnam presidents discussed agricultural cooperation, port links and conditions for Vietnamese investment at a meeting in Phnom Penh.

Benin’s President Romuald Wadagni and Vietnam’s President Tô Lâm discussed expanding trade in rice, cashew and cotton at a meeting in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, on 15 November 2026. They also identified freshwater aquaculture as a possible area for cooperation.

The talks covered putting an agricultural cooperation memorandum into effect, with both leaders seeking practical work between businesses in the two countries. They also discussed links between their ports to facilitate commercial exchanges.

Tô Lâm asked Benin’s authorities to create favourable conditions for Vietnamese companies to establish themselves and carry out economic projects in Benin. The presidents also discussed political ties and coordination in international organisations.

Why it mattersBeninese authorities are being asked to create conditions for Vietnamese firms to establish businesses and carry out projects in the country.

Benin’s IAJP urges leaders to put living conditions at the centre of policy

The institute’s four recommendations call for fairer public resource management, stronger essential services and job-focused education.

Benin’s Institute of Artisans of Justice and Peace (IAJP/CO) issued four main recommendations after its 29th annual symposium, held with the Konrad Adenauer Foundation on 13 and 14 November 2026. The institute urged political leaders, economic actors and citizens to make human dignity central to development policy.

In a public appeal released on 14 November, IAJP director Father Arnaud Éric Aguénounon said economic growth and infrastructure projects do not necessarily improve living conditions, particularly in rural areas and urban peripheries. The institute called for greater attention to purchasing power, social justice and access to drinking water, electricity, food and basic health care.

The recommendations also urge more funding for health, education and social protection, fairer distribution of public resources and transparent communication on public works, mining contracts and job opportunities. IAJP called for education reform, vocational training and greater recognition of technical education, manual work and crafts.

Why it mattersRural and peripheral communities are the focus of calls to improve access to water, electricity, food and basic health care.

Benin qualifies for 2027 Africa Cup of Nations after Burkina Faso win

Burkina Faso beat Mauritania 3-1 in Nouakchott on Tuesday, securing the Guépards a place without playing.

Benin qualified for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations on Tuesday, 17 November, without playing, after Burkina Faso beat Mauritania 3-1 in Nouakchott. The result also sent Burkina Faso through to the finals, which will be held in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda from 19 June to 17 July 2027.

Benin have 10 points after four rounds, with three wins and one draw. They drew 1-1 with Burkina Faso, beat Mauritania 2-1 and defeated Central African Republic 4-1 in Abidjan on 12 November. Central African Republic lost 1-0 on Monday, with Joseph Ede Oloko scoring in the 93rd minute.

Benin have two matches remaining in March 2027 and are targeting an unbeaten qualifying campaign. It will be the country’s sixth appearance at the tournament and its second consecutive qualification, following the 2008 and 2010 editions. Coach Gernot Rohr’s contract expires on 31 December; he said he had held no discussions about his future.

Why it mattersBenin’s players have two qualifying matches in March 2027, with the team targeting a campaign without defeat.

Benin receives 81 million CFA francs in court equipment

The United States funded the equipment, while the United Nations Development Programme provided technical support for the justice project.

Benin’s Ministry of Justice and Legislation received information technology and audiovisual equipment worth 81 million F Cfa at the Tour administrative on Friday 6 November 2026. The donation was made through the Project to Strengthen Access to Justice, backed financially by the United States and technically by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP).

The equipment includes 130 digital cameras, 23 laptops, four all-in-one computers, five 65-inch display screens, two 85-inch monitoring screens and a colour multifunction photocopier. The project has total funding of 2.3 million US dollars, Ambassador Peter W. Lord said. It is intended to modernise courts in northern Benin and speed up the handling of cases.

Justice Minister Yvon Détchénou said the equipment would help secure data, preserve evidence, improve services for court users and strengthen internal controls. The delivery completes the project’s planned equipment donations, following vehicles handed over in July 2025; ministry officials were told to use the new items carefully.

Why it mattersCourt users and people held in pretrial detention are among those the project targets through faster case handling, with equipment deliveries now complete.

FeRCAB opens Parakou production studio for 56 community radios

The shared facility, delivered with Fondation Hirondelle and European Union funding, is intended to help members produce radio and digital content.

Benin’s Federation of Community and Related Radios (FeRCAB) opened a production studio at its headquarters in Parakou on 12 November 2026 for its 56 member stations. Named for federation pioneer Dr Lazare Maurice Sèhouéto, the facility strengthens FeRCAB’s training and co-production centre.

FeRCAB president Dr Inoussa Guinin Asso said the studio would be shared by member stations. He pledged to maintain it, set fair access rules and generate federation income through production services. Fondation Hirondelle partnered on the project, with European Union funding through CFI-Sawara.

The studio is designed to support higher-quality radio and digital productions, including verified and inclusive content. Fondation Hirondelle representative Olympe Dossa said the project would improve community media production conditions; the media regulator, HAAC, called for greater quality in information.

Why it mattersFeRCAB’s 56 member radios are to receive shared production access under rules the federation president pledged to define.
