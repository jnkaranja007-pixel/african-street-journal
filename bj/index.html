Benin's Supreme Court opens judicial year

Vice President Mariam Chabi Talata officially launched the new judicial year on November 5, 2026.

The President of the Economic and Social Council (CES), Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, attended the solemn opening of the Supreme Court's judicial year on Thursday, November 5, 2026, in Porto-Novo. The event, themed "The Electoral Judge and the Stakes of Local Democracy," highlighted justice's role in upholding the rule of law and managing elections. Vice President Mariam Chabi Talata, representing the Head of State, officially opened the new judicial year.

Supreme Court President Victor Dassi Adoussou presided over the ceremony, acknowledging the presence of institutional leaders, including Bio Tchané. Bio Tchané's attendance demonstrated the CES's commitment to inter-institutional synergy, emphasizing dialogue and cooperation for national development. This participation signals the CES's dedication to strengthening democratic institutions.

The Supreme Court's new judicial year, 2026-2027, focuses on the electoral judge's role in local democracy. The ceremony underscored the importance of justice in maintaining democratic balance and the rule of law. The CES reaffirmed its commitment to inter-institutional collaboration for Benin's progress.

Why it mattersThe Vice President and the President of the Economic and Social Council attended the Supreme Court's judicial year opening, showing institutional engagement.

Benin book celebrates 41 inspiring figures

The "Those Who Inspire Benin" collection aims to guide young people through the experiences of successful individuals.

The Novotel in Cotonou hosted the launch of "Those Who Inspire Benin" on Wednesday, November 4, 2026. The book features 41 portraits of Beninese women and men recognized for their professional and civic achievements. Canal+ sponsored the event, which brought together institutional figures, business leaders, and young people.

Marlène Plomik, founder of the international "Those Who Inspire" movement, stated that this 17th edition, focused on Benin, aims to provide concrete guidance for students and young professionals. The book will be distributed for free in public universities, accompanied by direct exchanges between featured individuals and students during "Inspiring Days."

Local project coordinator Izar Hyacinthe explained that the book's creation involved a full year of research, interviews, and writing. Several individuals featured in the book, including scientist Moumouni Adamou, entrepreneur Lionel Talon, and public health specialist Ghislaine Loko Djidjoho, shared their career paths and societal contributions during the launch event.

Why it mattersYoung Beninese students and professionals will receive free copies of the book and engage with featured individuals to gain insights into career paths.

Benin president replaces military high command

General Abou Issa, who survived a coup attempt, now leads the military after a complete reshuffle on November 4.

Benin's entire military high command was replaced on November 4, with General Abou Issa appointed to lead the new structure. Issa is an experienced officer who previously survived an attempted coup on December 7, 2025. The reshuffle was approved by the Council of Ministers and announced by the Minister of Defence.

Speculation about a military reorganization had circulated for months within the Benin army. President Romuald Wadagni, who was inaugurated on May 24, has now acted on these rumors. The previous high command was removed following this decision.

The announcement was made on November 4, with a modification to the report published the following day. The military changes come after the celebration of Benin's 66th anniversary of national and international sovereignty on August 1, 2026.

Why it mattersBenin's military leadership has been completely changed, affecting national security and the chain of command.

Politics · 2026-11-06

Benin's economic council role questioned

Former head of Benin's economic council discusses its limited power against the government.

Conrad Gbaguidi, formerly head of Benin's Economic and Social Council (CES) in Cotonou, has questioned the body's effectiveness. He stated the CES is neither aligned with the ruling party nor the opposition, suggesting a neutral but potentially powerless position.

Gbaguidi shared his experience leading the CES, highlighting its limited capacity to address issues like the high cost of living. He spoke about the council's constrained room for manoeuvre when dealing with the government.

His reflections are featured in a new book titled 'La troisième voix' published by L’Harmattan, where he discusses the council's purpose and influence.

Why it mattersBenin's citizens are affected by the limited power of the Economic and Social Council to address the high cost of living.
