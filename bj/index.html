Benin women's handball team draws Egypt for Africa Cup

The Amazones will face Egypt, Congo, and Cape Verde in Group A of the 2026 tournament in Tunisia.

Benin's women's national handball team, the Amazones, will compete against Egypt, Congo, and Cape Verde in Group A of the 2026 Africa Women's Handball Cup of Nations. The draw for the tournament, scheduled for December 2-12 in Tunisia, took place on Thursday, October 29, in Abidjan.

The 27th edition of the Cup of Nations will feature 16 teams. Egypt finished fourth and Congo sixth in the previous edition. Benin qualified for the tournament by winning the first IHF Women's Trophy for Africa Zone III in Ouagadougou in September, defeating Ivory Coast 29-28 in the final.

To prepare for the Tunisian event, the Benin Handball Federation appointed Euphémie Dartron-Lairy as technical coordinator for the selection. The team is currently training in Morocco at the Smara International Tournament, which also includes the host nation, the United States, and Canada.

Sport · 2026-10-31

Akpakpa Boys and Ace win cotonou beach tour

The cotonou stage of the benin beach tour concluded on october 25, 2026, with Akpakpa Boys and Ace claiming the men's and women's titles respectively.

Akpakpa Boys and Ace were crowned champions of the cotonou stage of the Bénin Beach Tour on Sunday, October 25, 2026, at Erevan beach. The event, which ran from October 23 to 25, showcased the skill and endurance of beninese beach-volleyball players.

In the men's final, Akpakpa Boys defeated Dynamic in a three-set match, winning 21-13, 21-23, and 15-12. The women's final saw Ace, represented by Kona-Francisco, beat Bees, led by Kouyema-Adom, in straight sets, 21-15, 21-15.

Organized by the Fédération béninoise de volleyball (Fbvb) under the supervision of Colonel Oscar Dafia, the tour aims to develop beach-volleyball in Benin. The next stage will be held in Parakou in December 2026.

Why it mattersThe Fédération béninoise de volleyball aims to develop beach-volleyball and reveal new talents through this tour.

Benin sextortion victims fear judgment

Women and girls make up 69% of sextortion victims in Benin, facing shame and silence.

Sextortion, a form of blackmail using intimate images, is a hidden crime in Benin where victims are often silenced by fear of judgment and lack of knowledge about available help. Organizations are working on prevention and support for those affected by this digital violence.

Houéfa, a victim from 2016, shared her experience of being threatened with intimate video diffusion by a former partner on Facebook if she refused to resume their relationship. Her fear of her parents' and community's reactions initially prevented her from seeking help, illustrating the shame that keeps victims silent.

Matin Libre reported on February 20, 2026, citing the Centre national d’investigations numériques (CNIN), that 69% of sextortion victims in Benin are women and girls. Sociologist Dr. MONTCHO Bruno emphasizes that prevention campaigns must consider the social, economic, and digital realities of those targeted.

Why it mattersVictims of sextortion in Benin, predominantly women and girls, suffer psychological distress and social stigma due to blackmail involving intimate images.

Benin Supreme Court to address electoral judge role

The court will hold its solemn judicial opening for 2026-2027 on November 5 in Porto-Novo.

The Supreme Court of Benin will hold its solemn judicial opening for the 2026-2027 year on Thursday, November 5, in Porto-Novo. Discussions will focus on the role of the electoral judge and the stakes of local democracy.

This ceremony follows disputes arising from the renewal of local authorities in 2026, including 211 cases related to communal elections and appeals concerning the distribution of village and town chief seats. President Romuald Wadagni will preside over the event, marking the first opening under his leadership.

The agenda includes remarks from the bâtonnier Zachari Baba Body and the prosecutor general Saturnin Afaton, before Supreme Court President Victor Dassi Adossou addresses the theme. The President of the Republic, or a representative, will officially launch the judicial year.

Why it mattersThe Supreme Court's focus on the electoral judge and local democracy affects citizens involved in disputes over local authority renewals and seat allocations.

Benin bishops announce African traditional religion conference

The conference, scheduled for November 19-20, 2026, aims to foster mutual understanding between faiths.

The Episcopal Conference of Benin (CEB) will host an international conference on traditional African religion in Cotonou on November 19 and 20, 2026. The event will take place at the Saint-Charbel hall in Akpakpa.

This announcement came from the final statement of the CEB's 79th ordinary plenary session, held in Natitingou from October 25 to 28. Organizers aim to deepen mutual knowledge between confessions and promote interreligious dialogue, linking it to African cultures and the continent's development.

The conference commemorates two meetings involving Pope John Paul II: one in Assisi, Italy, on October 27, 1986, and another in Cotonou on February 4, 1993, where he met with leaders of indigenous religions and Vodun cults.

Why it mattersBeninese religious leaders and followers of traditional African religions will engage in dialogue with Christian leaders.
