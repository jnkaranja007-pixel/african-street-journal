Benin journalists trained on maternal health reporting

The Cerrhud and health ministry aim to improve media coverage of reproductive health issues.

Journalists in Benin received training on maternal and neonatal health issues on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, at the Cerrhud site in Gbégamey, Cotonou. The Centre for Research in Human Reproduction and Demography (Cerrhud) organized the event with the Association of Journalists for Sexual and Reproductive Health to enhance media's role in public awareness.

Dr. Jean-Paul Dossou, Cerrhud's executive director, emphasized the media's vital link between health programs and communities, stating that closer collaboration between researchers and journalists makes health information more accessible. Dr. Romuald Bothon from the National Agency for Primary Health Care highlighted the need for information tailored to community needs and the ministry's efforts to reduce maternal and neonatal mortality by addressing delays in seeking care, accessing facilities, and receiving treatment.

Data presented revealed significant challenges in West Africa, with nearly half of women experiencing unintended pregnancies. The figures also showed that 37% of women marry before 18, 48% lack prenatal consultations, and 42% face domestic violence, underscoring the need for improved prevention and protection measures.

Why it mattersBenin's maternal and neonatal health is a major public health concern, with specific data highlighting issues like unintended pregnancies and domestic violence affecting women.

Benin adopts new road safety law

New rules require certified helmets, registration, and safety equipment, impacting household budgets.

Benin has enacted a new road safety code aimed at reducing the country's high rate of traffic fatalities, particularly among motorcyclists. The updated legislation introduces stricter requirements for vehicle registration, mandatory safety equipment, and enhanced insurance and technical inspection protocols. These changes are expected to significantly alter daily life for road users across the nation.

Key provisions include the mandatory use of certified helmets, replacing older styles, and the general registration of all motorcycles with secure, reflective license plates issued by ANaTT. Additionally, the law strengthens insurance requirements and technical inspections, with penalties for non-compliance transitioning to an electronic contravention system. The cost of required safety gear like reflective vests and fire extinguishers, when combined with other new demands, could amount to a significant portion of a low-income worker's salary.

The government is urged to implement a year-long public awareness campaign before enforcing penalties to avoid social unrest and increased corruption. Officials are also considering subsidizing safety equipment and negotiating bulk prices with importers to make compliance more affordable. Benin experiences a high number of road deaths annually, with motorcyclists accounting for 70% of fatalities, and road accidents costing the state nearly 3% of its GDP.

Why it mattersThe new road code imposes costs on citizens for certified helmets, registration, and safety equipment, potentially straining household finances.

Business · 2026-11-02

Benin minister opens African electricity finance summit

Think tanks debated regional electricity markets, consumer protection, and debt constraints on October 29.

Benin's Minister of Economy and Finance, Aristide Médenou, officially opened the 12th Summit of African Think Tanks in Cotonou on October 29, 2026. The summit focused on financing reforms in the electricity sector, with discussions covering governance, tariffs, and financial viability. Participants also examined regional electricity markets and consumer protection.

The think tanks debated financing projects amidst significant debt limitations, exploring combinations of funding sources and national resource mobilization. They also discussed mechanisms for risk reduction, including currency and state-related financial risks, which are obstacles to attracting capital. The summit aimed to link electricity access to industrial needs and regional value chains, particularly within the African Continental Free Trade Area.

Preliminary research findings on energy, economic transformation, and governance were presented by research consortiums for public and expert feedback. Other sessions addressed clean cooking, women's roles in energy transition, and decentralized solutions.

Why it mattersAfrican think tanks and Benin's finance minister met in Cotonou to discuss how to fund electricity sector reforms and attract capital amid debt concerns.

Business · 2026-11-02

Benin concludes local consumption month with awards

The seventh edition of the 'Consommons Local' month ended with prizes for creative content and product promotion.

Benin celebrated the end of its seventh annual 'Consommons Local' month on Friday, October 30, 2026, at the Benin Chamber of Commerce and Industry. The government initiative, led by the Ministry of Commerce, aims to promote Beninese products, national know-how, and local production.

Flora Akoutey, technical advisor to the Minister of Commerce, stated that consuming local products is a major economic driver, supporting industries, skills, and job creation. She urged consumers to maintain this habit beyond the celebration period.

The closing ceremony also recognized winners of the 'Indigo créatif' and 'Made in Benin' content creation contests. Each of the top contestants received 500,000 FCFA and a trophy, highlighting the event's focus on creativity and local economic transformation.

Why it mattersBeninese consumers and businesses are encouraged to prioritize local products to stimulate economic growth and job creation within the country.

Culture · 2026-11-02

Benin priest dies during All Saints' Day mass

Father Éric Ahissou, 48, died on November 1, 2026, while leading a service in Cotonou.

Father Éric Ahissou, the parish priest of Notre-Dame de la Visitation de Gbèdagba in Cotonou, died on Sunday, November 1, 2026, while celebrating the All Saints' Day mass.

The 48-year-old priest, who was ordained in August 2010, reportedly experienced a possible indisposition during the service. The exact circumstances of his death were not specified by Sunday evening.

Ahissou had previously served in the diocese of Montauban, France, where he also studied canon law. On September 26, 2021, he was installed as administrator for the parish of Lavilledieu-du-Temple and seven other nearby communes.

Why it mattersThe death of Father Éric Ahissou affects the Catholic community in Cotonou.
