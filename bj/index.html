Ecobank posts $737 million profit before tax

The pan-African banking group's profit before tax rose 12% to $737 million in the first nine months of 2026.

Ecobank Group reported a profit before tax of $737 million for the first nine months of 2026, a 12% increase compared to the same period in 2025. The pan-African banking group's net banking income reached $2 billion, also up 12% year-on-year. These results were published on October 27, 2026, in Lomé.

Customer deposits grew by 14% to $27.5 billion, strengthening the group's ability to manage margin pressures from falling interest rates. The operational cost-to-income ratio stood at 47.8%, below the target range of 48-50% for 2026. Ecobank's return on tangible equity was 24.9%, with non-performing loans stable at 7.5%.

Group CEO Jeremy Awori stated that the results reflect the effectiveness of the implemented strategy and the strength of the pan-African business model. Ecobank also opened new representative offices in Beijing and signed a memorandum of understanding with China's cross-border interbank payment system (CIPS) to facilitate trade between China and Africa.

Why it mattersEcobank's financial performance and strategic international expansion directly impact investors and the broader African business environment.

Benin student wins international writing prize

Chekpo Marie-Ange S. D., 15, received a special mention at the 55th Universal Postal Union competition.

Chekpo Marie-Ange S. D., a 15-year-old student from Benin, won the national first prize and the fourth global prize in the 55th International Letter Writing Competition. The Universal Postal Union (UPU) organized the 2026 competition for 1,480,000 candidates from 65 countries.

Marie-Ange, a student at CS Le Bon Berger in Godomey, wrote about the importance of human connection and empathy in the digital age. Her work earned her a special mention from the international jury, placing Benin among the top seven countries, including China, Portugal, and Iran.

Benin was the only African nation to be ranked in the 2026 competition. The UPU competition aims to promote young writing talents worldwide.

Why it mattersBenin's young writers are recognized internationally for their creative work.

Dogo-bis programme recruits eight for Benin unit

The Benin Republic's Presidency seeks staff for a $150 million World Bank-funded project.

The Dogo-bis programme is hiring eight people for its management unit, which reports to the Benin Republic's Presidency. Applications are due by noon on November 18, 2026, in Cotonou.

The roles include administrative and financial management, monitoring and evaluation, executive secretarial duties, and accounting. Three specialist positions cover environmental and social safeguards, social development and gender, and procurement. Most positions will be based in Cotonou or nearby towns like Abomey-Calavi, with frequent travel to other locations.

Successful candidates will receive two-year renewable contracts, starting January 1, 2027. The Dogo-bis programme, funded by a $150 million World Bank loan for its first phase, aims to build a multifunctional dam and related infrastructure in the Ouémé River basin by 2033.

Benin ranks 12th in Africa governance index

The country scores 60.1 points, exceeding continental and West African averages despite political participation decline.

Benin placed 12th out of 54 African nations in the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, achieving 60.1 points out of 100. The Mo Ibrahim Foundation's assessment, covering 2016-2025, noted a 2.6-point overall increase since 2016.

The nation's score surpasses the African average of 50.1 points and West Africa's average of 52.9 points. While economic opportunities, business environment, infrastructure, and public administration showed significant improvements between 2016 and 2025, political participation indicators declined.

Specifically, the participation sub-category dropped 18.6 points, with democratic elections falling 36.3 points and anti-corruption measures decreasing by 27.7 points. These declines occurred despite improvements in measured corruption levels.

Why it mattersBenin's citizens are affected by a decline in democratic participation and anti-corruption measures, despite overall governance improvements.

Man killed with machete in Ouénou

A man in his fifties died after a machete attack by his neighbor on November 1, 2026.

A man in his fifties died after a violent machete attack in Ouénou, Nikki commune, Borgou department, on Sunday, November 1, 2026. The victim was returning from his soybean field when his neighbor, identified as a cattle herder, allegedly attacked him. The suspect fled the scene after the incident.

The victim reportedly tried to resist his attacker but was knocked down. The assailant then inflicted a fatal blow to the man's throat before escaping. The Republic Police from the Tchikandou station visited the scene and have opened an investigation to find the suspect and determine the exact circumstances of the fatal assault.

This man leaves behind a wife and one child. The exact reasons for the attack remain unclear as the investigation continues, leaving the community seeking answers.

Why it mattersA farmer was killed by his neighbor while returning from his soybean harvest, leaving his family bereaved.
