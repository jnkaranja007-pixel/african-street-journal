Northern Benin agricultural plans updated for national goals

Twenty-seven communal agricultural development plans in northern Benin are undergoing review and updates to align with national agricultural strategies and address current.

An official workshop launched the mission to review and update Benin's Communal Agricultural Development Plans (PCDA) in Parakou on August 27, 2026. The initiative aims to harmonize efforts and revise 27 PCDAs from northern Benin to better reflect new national agricultural directives. This process goes beyond simple document revision, intending to update territorial diagnostics, prioritize agricultural value chains, and ensure PCDAs comply with national methodological guidelines.

The updated plans will align with the Strategic Plan for Agricultural Sector Development (PSDSA), the National Plan for Agricultural and Food and Nutritional Security Investments (PNIASAN), and the Benin Vert initiative. A support plan will also be developed to aid effective implementation at the communal level. The ten-week exercise involves communal authorities, producer organizations, agricultural development agencies, prefectures, private sector, and other stakeholders, emphasizing differentiated territorialization to account for each commune's agro-economic specificities.

The update comes amid challenges like climate change, food security, and the need to increase agricultural production value. Officials stressed adapting plans to Benin's agricultural transformation ambitions, focusing on value chains, water access, soil fertility, fodder production, and producer income. The goal is to make agriculture a more profitable activity that enhances local economic development and producer livelihoods.

Why it mattersFarmers in northern Benin will see their local agricultural plans updated to better address climate change, food security, and income generation, aligning with national development strategies.

UBA Benin Seeks Legal Assistant for Compliance and Contracts

The bank is hiring a legal assistant to manage litigation, contracts, and provide advice, ensuring compliance with group standards and local laws.

UBA Benin has announced a vacancy for a Legal Assistant specializing in litigation, contracts, and legal counsel. The role involves adapting the subsidiary's legal policies and procedures to group standards while ensuring adherence to all local Beninese legal requirements.

Key responsibilities include managing and evaluating legal partners such as lawyers and bailiffs, ensuring the bank's legal commitments to third parties are met, and vice versa. The assistant will also draft legal documents, including service and maintenance contracts, and oversee litigation involving the bank and various administrations.

The position requires advising the General Directorate and other departments on legal matters, managing relationships with the Board of Directors and shareholders, and reporting on legal affairs to management and regulatory bodies. The role also entails identifying and managing legal and regulatory risks impacting the subsidiary.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the bank seeks to ensure compliance and manage legal risks, which can impact financial operations and regulatory standing.

WAPCo Delays Nigerian Gas Flow Halt to Benin, Togo Until September 7

The West African Gas Pipeline Company has postponed maintenance that will suspend Nigerian natural gas deliveries to Togo and Benin, originally scheduled to begin August 28.

The West African Gas Pipeline Company (WAPCo) has rescheduled the suspension of Nigerian natural gas transport to Togo and Benin to September 7, 2026. This follows an earlier announcement of a partial shutdown of the WAGP system initially planned for August 28. The scope of the maintenance on the West African gas pipeline remains unchanged.

WAPCo's August 21 statement detailed planned maintenance, asset integrity checks, and modernization work across its network in Togo, Benin, and Nigeria. In Togo, the program includes replacing an underwater valve on the Lomé lateral and pipeline inspection. Benin's work involves an internal inspection of the Cotonou lateral and the WAPCo-BGTS interconnection project.

Previously, WAPCo had indicated the supply suspension would run from August 28 to September 1, 2026, with Ghana's supply expected to continue via the western flow. The West African Gas Pipeline, spanning 678 kilometers, connects Nigeria, Benin, Togo, and Ghana, according to the West African Gas Pipeline Authority.

Why it mattersThe delay affects the supply of Nigerian natural gas to Benin and Togo, impacting energy infrastructure and maintenance schedules for these countries.

Presidential Visit Sparks Calls for Prison and Health Facility Access

Amnesty Benin urged the president to extend his recent public engagement to include visits to prisons, health centers, and areas affected by development projects.

Presidential proximity to citizens was noted during recent travels from Nikki to Parakou, with images circulating of his engagement at the Gaani 2026 festival. Amnesty Benin expressed hope that this interaction is not merely propaganda, but a genuine effort to address public concerns.

The organization specifically called for the head of state to visit prisons and detention centers, as well as health facilities and hospitals. They also requested that he engage with individuals displaced by development projects and listen to civil society organizations.

Amnesty Benin hopes these actions will positively impact human rights, noting that numerous concerns remain. The organization's human rights manifesto for 2026-2033 is available for consultation.

Why it mattersThe president's engagement with the public could lead to a greater focus on human rights issues, including conditions in prisons and health facilities, affecting all citizens.

Société Générale Benin Sale Stalls Amid Regulatory Hurdles

The French banking group's planned sale of its Benin subsidiary to the state is delayed, with the entity still classified as 'under divestment' as of June 30, 2026.

The planned sale of Société Générale Benin to the Benin state is experiencing significant delays, with the subsidiary and its branch still listed as 'divestments in progress' by the French banking group as of June 30, 2026. This situation is part of a broader trend across West and Central Africa where the divestment of several French group subsidiaries is faltering due to regulatory blockages and nationalistic interests.

Société Générale had intended to transfer 93.43% of its Benin subsidiary to the Benin state. However, the process is moving slowly, contributing to the overall slowdown in the group's African divestment strategy. This is occurring even as Société Générale has fallen to third place among banking groups in the West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA), overtaken by Coris, according to the Banking Commission.

Between 2023 and 2025, nine other African subsidiaries of the banking group changed ownership. The prolonged nature of these sales, including the one in Benin, suggests ongoing challenges in concluding these transactions across the continent. The reasons cited include regulatory obstacles and a desire for state control over financial assets.

Why it mattersInvestors face uncertainty regarding the ownership and operational status of Société Générale Benin, impacting potential future investments and financial sector stability.
