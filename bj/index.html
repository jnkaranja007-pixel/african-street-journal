Abdoulaye Bio Tchané launches departmental CES activities in Borgou

The Economic and Social Council (CES) president began a national tour in Parakou to operationalize departmental CES branches and open their second ordinary session.

Abdoulaye Bio Tchané, president of the Economic and Social Council (CES), officially launched the activities of departmental CES branches on Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, 2026, in Parakou. This event, coupled with the opening of their second ordinary session, marks a new stage in the CES's transformation, following the start of its eighth mandate. The ceremony took place at the Parakou prefecture, attended by the Borgou prefect, departmental councilors, National Assembly deputies, and local mayors.

The Borgou prefect stated that the operationalization of the CES at the territorial level is a significant advancement that brings the institution closer to the population and their daily realities. President Bio Tchané emphasized four pillars for the CES's new dynamic: utility, citizen proximity, inter-institutional dialogue, and international outreach. He highlighted the adoption of internal regulations and the launch of the 2027-2031 strategic plan as decisive steps already taken.

The event in Parakou is the first stop on a tour that will take the presidential delegation to all twelve departmental capitals of Benin. Bio Tchané stated that the departmental councilors will serve as a link, explaining the CES to citizens and ensuring the population's voice is heard, thereby fostering local democracy.

Why it mattersCitizens across Benin's twelve departments will experience a new level of local governance as the CES aims to bring its services and dialogue closer to them.

Mathurin De Chacus concludes eight-year tenure as Benin football president

Mathurin De Chacus officially handed over leadership of Beninese football to his successor, Marcellin Bocovè, after two terms marked by significant development in the sport.

Mathurin De Chacus concluded his eight-year presidency of the Beninese Football Federation (FBF) on Tuesday, August 25, 2026, at the Elective General Assembly held at the Missérété Football Excellence Center. He transferred authority to Marcellin Bocovè, who acknowledged De Chacus's contributions to the sport.

During his tenure, De Chacus oversaw the construction of stadiums, the establishment of excellence centers, and the training of coaches. Marcellin Bocovè noted that statutory competitions were organized, youth opportunities expanded, women's football gained prominence, and clubs received support.

Bocovè also highlighted that De Chacus's leadership ensured the federation operated without crises, a point of pride for the outgoing president, who expressed happiness in setting an example for a smooth transition of power in Beninese football.

Why it mattersThis event affects football players, coaches, and fans in Benin by changing the leadership responsible for the sport's development and governance.

Madagascar civil society fears Russian election interference

Civil society groups in Madagascar express concern over Russia's offer of technical support for the 2027 presidential election, citing past actions in the Central African Republic.

Concerns are rising among some civil society members in Madagascar regarding Russia's proposal to provide technical assistance for the 2027 Malagasy presidential election. Critics view this offer as part of an influence strategy previously observed in the Central African Republic, where increased Russian presence reshaped political and security dynamics.

In the Central African Republic, Moscow's "technical support" for the National Election Agency reportedly led to systematic ballot stuffing, direct threats against opponents of Faustin Archange Touadera, and targeted intimidation of the ruling party's adversaries. Russian forces, identified as Wagner, secured polling stations in strategic areas, controlled voter flows, and ensured official results aligned with Kremlin interests, leading to a presidential re-election deemed incredible by most independent observers.

The current rapprochement between Madagascar's transitional authorities and the Kremlin fuels debate on the risks of foreign interference in a process intended to restore constitutional order. Critics suggest the Russian offer is a maneuver to meddle in Madagascar's internal affairs and manipulate election results in favor of a chosen candidate, rather than supporting democratic transition.

Why it mattersMalagasy citizens face potential manipulation of their electoral process and the undermining of democratic institutions if foreign interference occurs.

Guinean authorities investigate six bodies found in Sabakhouré pit

An investigation has commenced in Forécariah, Guinea, following the discovery of six unidentified male bodies in a pit in Sabakhouré on August twenty-fourth, 2026.

Six unidentified male bodies were found in a pit in Sabakhouré, within the sub-prefecture of Allassoyah, prompting local authorities and a prosecutor to visit the scene. The discovery led to an immediate alert from residents in the Forécariah area.

The initial local reports have not established the identities of the victims or the exact circumstances of their deaths. This lack of information prevents attributing a motive or identifying perpetrators in connection with the discovery.

The bodies have been transferred to the Forécariah prefectural hospital. This transfer is a necessary step for the judicial inquiry and the identification process to proceed, according to local reports from Benin Web TV.

Why it mattersResidents of Sabakhouré and Allassoyah are directly affected by the discovery of six bodies, creating local concern and initiating a judicial investigation in their community.

Business · 08/26/2026

Owner of Avakpa fuel cargo identified after August 6 fire

The owner of the adulterated fuel cargo involved in the August 6 fire in Avakpa, Porto-Novo, has been identified and questioned, according to local media reports.

Following the August 6 to 7, 2026, fire in Avakpa, Porto-Novo, caused by Kpayo fuel, the owner of the cargo has been identified and interviewed. The incident, which resulted in significant material damage, involved a truck carrying barrels of adulterated fuel that reportedly struck an electrical pole before igniting.

Local media reports indicate that the owner's identification was possible due to customs records. Any such cargo crossing Beninese borders must be registered with customs services, making it difficult to conceal ownership or involvement in the incident. This traceability is expected to help determine responsibility.

Despite public speculation about potential interference in the investigation to downplay the incident or secure leniency for the cargo owners, authorities state that such attempts would be impossible. No deaths were reported in the fire, which involved an explosion of the fuel barrels.

Why it mattersThe identification of the cargo owner affects individuals involved in the illegal fuel trade by holding them accountable for incidents like the Avakpa fire.
