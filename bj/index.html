Talon Mediates Celestial Church Dispute, Promises New Law

Benin's former president and current Senate leader is working to ease tensions within the Protestant church and plans to introduce legislation governing religious organizations.

Former Beninese President Patrice Talon, now the head of the Senate, is actively involved in mediating a dispute within the Church of Celestial Christianity. Talon attended a meeting organized by the church's France diocese in Saint-Ouen, near Paris, on August 29, where he announced that a law on the organization of worship in Benin will soon be voted on. This intervention aims to de-escalate internal church conflicts and potentially signal Talon's continued influence on the national political landscape.

The meeting, attended by hundreds of church members, also saw the presentation of a final report from the Superior Council of Transition, a significant step in the reconciliation process for the Church of Celestial Christianity. This report was formally received by Talon and Romuald Wadagni on April 30, 2026, at the Sofitel Cotonou Marina, indicating a structured approach to resolving the church's internal divisions.

Talon's dual role as a political leader and a mediator in religious affairs highlights the intersection of state and religious matters in Benin. The upcoming legislation is expected to provide a framework for religious organizations, addressing the governance and operational aspects of worship across the country. The church's internal strife has drawn the attention of the highest political office, underscoring its significance within the nation.

Why it mattersBenin's former president is mediating a dispute within the Church of Celestial Christianity and plans to introduce legislation governing religious organizations, affecting church members and potentially the broader religious landscape.

Benin U17 Amazons Draw Burkina Faso, Ivory Coast for UFOA-B Tournament

The Benin U17 women's football team will face Burkina Faso and Ivory Coast in Group A of the UFOA-B U17 Tournament.

The Benin U17 Amazons have learned their opponents for the upcoming UFOA-B U17 Tournament. Following a draw held on Thursday, September 3, 2026, in Ouagadougou, the young Beninese players were placed in Group A. Their matches will be against the national teams of Burkina Faso and Ivory Coast.

Coach Idah Azonsou's squad is aiming to secure a qualifying spot for the 2027 U17 Africa Cup of Nations. The tournament itself is scheduled to take place from September 15 to September 27, 2026, in Yamoussoukro, Ivory Coast.

This draw sets the stage for the Amazons' campaign in the regional competition, where they will compete against neighboring nations for a chance to advance to the continental finals.

Why it mattersYoung female athletes from Benin will compete in a regional football tournament with aspirations of reaching the U17 Africa Cup of Nations.

Ebola Kills 45 Health Workers in Congo Outbreak

At least 158 health professionals have been infected, with a nearly 30% fatality rate among them.

At least 45 health workers have died from Ebola in the Democratic Republic of Congo since the epidemic began in May 2026. A total of 158 health professionals have been contaminated, resulting in a fatality rate of nearly 30% among infected caregivers. This toll includes doctors, nurses, midwives, aides, laboratory technicians, maintenance staff, and drivers involved in patient transport.

The alarming situation is exacerbated by persistent weaknesses in hospital hygiene. Infection prevention and control measures remain critical in general care centers, with average scores of 29% in six audited facilities in North Kivu and 21.3% at Ganga General Hospital in Bas-Uélé. The World Health Organization compiled the figures, which encompass all health structure agents involved in the response.

The current epidemic is caused by the Bundibugyo species of the Ebola virus, raising questions about the effectiveness of the Ervebo vaccine, which was developed for the Zaire strain. This immunological uncertainty has led to ongoing clinical trials and priority research protocols aimed at protecting frontline personnel.

Why it mattersHealth workers in the Democratic Republic of Congo are at high risk of contracting and dying from Ebola due to inadequate hygiene and an uncertain vaccine efficacy.

Wadagni Accelerates Benin's Economic Trajectory

Benin's new head of state, Romuald Wadagni, continues the economic path set by his predecessor, focusing on social spending and growth distribution.

Benin's first one hundred days under President Romuald Wadagni show an acceleration rather than a break in economic policy. The former Minister of Economy and Finance, who took office on April 12, is committed to maintaining the country's financial stability, which is well-regarded by markets. His administration aims to prioritize social expenditures and ensure that economic growth is shared more equitably across different regions.

This approach seeks to preserve the established economic equilibrium while also addressing social needs and regional disparities. The focus remains on continuity, building upon the foundations laid during the previous administration. The government's strategy involves careful management of public finances to support social programs and foster a more inclusive distribution of the nation's expanding economy.

The economic direction under Wadagni emphasizes a balanced approach, combining fiscal prudence with increased investment in social welfare and territorial development. This strategy is designed to reinforce Benin's reputation among international investors and financial institutions, while also aiming to improve the living standards of its citizens through better allocation of resources.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the continuation of sound economic policies that maintain the country's reputation with markets.
