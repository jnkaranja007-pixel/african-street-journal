Benin expects gas supply to stabilise within two weeks

A ship has docked at Cotonou to unload a shipment, but distributors must complete restocking before affected shops have bottles.

Sources: La Nouvelle Tribune and Benin Web TV · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Benin’s government expects domestic gas supplies to stabilise within about two weeks, government spokesman Wilfried Léandre Houngbédji said in a BÉNIN TV broadcast on 22 November. Six-kilogram gas bottles have been difficult to find since the start of November, with shortages reported in Cotonou and Abomey-Calavi from 6 November.

Houngbédji said stocks had not met demand, encouraging price increases. Replenishment orders have arrived since midweek, and a ship has docked at the port of Cotonou to unload a cargo. Distributors must complete the required steps and restock affected outlets; Trade Minister Shadiya Alimantou Assouma discussed those steps with them. The president has asked for faster distribution across the country.

Houngbédji blamed people taking advantage of the shortage for raising prices. He also said ships were no longer moving normally on the oceans, without giving further details.

Why it mattersHouseholds looking for 6 kg bottles must wait for distributors to restock affected outlets, with the government forecasting stabilisation within about two weeks.
