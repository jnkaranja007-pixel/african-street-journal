Benin loses 2% of farmland in two years

Government aims to halt the decline of cultivable land, which has dropped from 43% to 41% since 2021.

Source: La Nouvelle Tribune · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Benin's government is working to stop the shrinking of its agricultural land, which has decreased by 2% in just two years. Antoine Houedji, a land affairs advisor to the agriculture minister, stated on Friday, November 13, 2026, that cultivable land fell from 43% in 2021 to 41% in 2023. Rapid urbanisation is the main cause of this loss.

This warning comes as land reform progresses, with the TERRA BÉNIN and PPMEC II projects launching on November 6, 2026, in Cotonou to improve the e-Foncier land registry platform. The World Bank's representative, Karine Bachongy, outlined the goal: to have an operational cadastre nationwide, map land, identify rights holders, and permanently register land transactions. The ministries of Agriculture and Decentralisation are leading these efforts, gathering prefects to discuss protecting rural agricultural land.

Minister Adin Yéton Bloukounon Goubalan announced that under the Benin Vert 2033 strategy, each commune will have a secured land reserve for the "One Commune, One Green Lung" initiative. The prefect of Zou, Laurent Zomaï, stressed the decisive role of local leaders in securing agricultural land reserves. Minister of Decentralisation Janvier Yahouedehou added that protecting farmland preserves food sovereignty and territorial development.

Why it mattersFarmers in Benin are losing 2% of cultivable land due to urbanisation, impacting food sovereignty and development.
