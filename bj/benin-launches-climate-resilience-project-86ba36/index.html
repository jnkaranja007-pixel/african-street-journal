Benin launches climate resilience project

The seven-billion CFA franc project will support rural populations in nine vulnerable communes facing ecological threats.

Source: Matin Libre · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

The integrated project to strengthen the climate resilience of vulnerable and border communes (Pirrcf) has officially launched in Sèmè-Podji, Benin. The seven billion 150 million CFA franc program aims to provide sustainable solutions for rural populations severely impacted by climate change. Nine communes across the national territory are directly involved, addressing recurrent ecological threats that endanger food security and impoverish local producers.

The plan includes developing protective structures, supporting adapted agricultural practices, and reinforcing water resources to stabilize living conditions. The project's implementation relies on collaboration between the state, local authorities, and international organizations, with financial support from the United Nations Development Programme. Minister of Agriculture, Livestock, and Fisheries Adin Yéton Bloukounon Goubalan presided over the launch, emphasizing the need for effective field implementation and urging communal authorities to actively monitor construction sites.

Field interventions are set to begin in the coming weeks to implement initial commitments to the affected communities. Experts highlighted that the vulnerability of rural and border areas requires sustained attention and investment, stressing that shared responsibility between ministries and mayoralties will ensure the durability of the initiated actions.

Why it mattersThe Pirrcf project will implement protective structures and support adapted agricultural practices in nine communes, starting in the coming weeks.
