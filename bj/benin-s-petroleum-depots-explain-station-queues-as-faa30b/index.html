Benin's petroleum depots explain station queues as distribution lags

Director Sosthène H. Mesmin Hounsou says depot stocks remain available, while higher station demand and delivery constraints slow supplies.

Sources: La Nouvelle Tribune and Benin Web TV · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Benin’s petroleum depots have stocks available, but queues at several service stations persist as distribution lags, the depots’ director general, Sosthène H. Mesmin Hounsou, said in a social-media video. The state-run depot operator receives orders from licensed distributors, which buy fuel and transport it to their own stations.

Hounsou said more customers are using stations that were designed to serve a smaller share of the population. Higher sales have put pressure on staffing and delivery fleets, and ordering and transport delays can slow replenishment even when fuel remains in storage.

He also linked the shift to reduced Nigerian subsidies after the April 2026 crisis: informal-market fuel has become scarcer and more expensive. In some localities, it reaches 1 000 to 1 CFA200 a litre, against CFA750 at the pump. Depot officials are discussing supply obstacles with distributors, including delays from ship arrivals through unloading and order processing.
