Benin sees three local administrators leave posts

The executive secretary of Cotonou resigned, while those in Glazoué and Djougou were removed.

Source: Matin Libre · 2026-11-07 · 1 min read

Less than nine months after new municipal teams took office, Benin's decentralization is showing instability. The executive secretary of Cotonou resigned, and the secretaries for Glazoué and Djougou were revoked. These departures create an image of an unstable administration struggling to establish itself.

Citizens observe these high-ranking officials leaving within a short period, which projects a troubling image of local governance. The tension between mayors, holding political power, and executive secretaries, managing technical aspects, appears to be a recurring issue. While holding officials accountable for results can demonstrate rigor, arbitrary decisions without clear explanations risk being seen as personal vendettas.

The repeated premature departures weaken the image of the mayoralties. For the decentralization reform to succeed, the new mandate must demonstrate effective human resource management alongside political ambitions. The administration needs to show it can manage its staff seriously to ensure local governance functions effectively amidst this uncertainty.

Why it mattersBenin's citizens are concerned about the instability in local governance, which affects the perceived effectiveness and reliability of their municipal administrations.
