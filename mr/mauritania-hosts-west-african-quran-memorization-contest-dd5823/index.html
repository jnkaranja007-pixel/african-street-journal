Mauritania hosts West African Quran memorization contest

Over 160 participants from 16 West African nations are competing in Nouakchott in the second edition of a Saudi-sponsored competition focused on the Quran and Sunna.

Source: Sahara Medias · 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

Nouakchott, Mauritania, hosted the opening of the second edition of the King Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud Guardian of the Two Holy Mosques competition on Sunday morning. The event, organized by Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Islamic Affairs, Da’wah and Guidance in collaboration with Mauritania's Ministry of Islamic Affairs and Original Education, aims to test the memorization of the Quran and Sunna among participants from West African countries.

The competition features 160 participants representing 16 West African nations, divided into two main categories: Quran memorization and Sunna memorization. Each category includes five disciplines, covering memorization, recitation (tajwid), exegesis (tafsir), and understanding of hadiths. Mauritanian Minister of Islamic Affairs Sid El-Haj Ould Dah stated that the event embodies the pooling of efforts and resources for the Quran and Sunna, strengthening scientific and intellectual exchanges between Islamic, Arab, and African countries.

Saudi Ambassador to Mauritania Abdelaziz ben Abdullah Al-Raqabi noted that holding the competition in Mauritania for the second time reflects the deep fraternal relations and historical ties between the two countries. He added that it signifies Mauritania's role in serving Islamic causes and enhancing regional cooperation. The competition's focus on religious scholarship highlights Mauritania's scientific standing and the contributions of its scholars and madrasas.
