Mauritania's parliament approves two International Development Association financing deals

The loans fund a social protection overhaul and an extension of high-speed digital infrastructure, and must be repaid over 20 years.

Source: Sahara Medias · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Mauritania's parliament approved two financing agreements with the International Development Association (IDA), the World Bank's concessional lending arm, on Monday. The texts cover the strengthening of resilience and productive social safety nets, and an additional loan for the digital transformation project under the first phase of the regional West African digital integration programme.

Economic Development Minister Mohamedou Ahmedou M'Haimid told deputies that the first agreement, signed on 21 August 2026, will fund technical assistance to review the social registry, its payment system and its complaints procedures. The second, signed on 16 September 2026, is worth €25.8 million and will extend high-speed digital infrastructure, develop digital services and integrate digital markets across West Africa.

The loan is repayable over 20 years, with a five-year grace period, annual interest of 1.5% and a 0.5% annual commitment fee on amounts not yet disbursed.

Why it mattersRepayment of the €25.8 million loan begins only after five years, so the state's debt service is spread over two decades.
