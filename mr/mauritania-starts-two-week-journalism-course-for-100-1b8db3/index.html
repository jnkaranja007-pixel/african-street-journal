Mauritania starts two-week journalism course for 100 reporters

The Nouakchott programme covers digital reporting, artificial intelligence, data journalism and methods to verify information and fight disinformation.

Source: Sahara Medias · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Mauritania began a two-week journalism course for 100 journalists in Nouakchott on Thursday, covering professional reporting and adaptation to digital change. The programme includes digital journalism, content creation, data journalism and applications of artificial intelligence in reporters’ work.

The course also addresses journalistic ethics, social responsibility, professional law, investigative reporting and digital verification of information. Culture Minister Mohamed Ould Babana said investment in media starts with training journalists and strengthening their skills and ability to access, process and present information responsibly.

The launch was led by Ould Babana, Mohamed Yahya Ould Saïd, director general of the National School of Administration, Journalism and Justice, and Mohamed Abdallah Lehbib, president of the High Authority for the Press and Audiovisual Media. The course also covers combating disinformation.

Why it mattersThe 100 journalists enrolled in Nouakchott spend two weeks on training that includes digital verification, data journalism and artificial intelligence.
