Mauritania adopts 2027 budget with 152 billion ouguiya revenue

The government's finance bill projects a 6.2 billion ouguiya surplus and 0.5% economic growth amid global economic risks.

Mauritania's government has drafted its 2027 finance bill, projecting total revenues and grants at approximately 152.11 billion Mauritanian ouguiyas (MRU) and a global budget surplus of 6.2 billion MRU. Expenditures and net borrowing are estimated at 145.91 billion MRU. The plan anticipates real economic growth of 0.5% for 2027, with an inflation rate of 3.3%. The government aims for an average economic growth of 4.3% between 2027 and 2029, guided by a new fiscal rule limiting the non-extractive primary balance to 3.5% of nominal GDP.

The budget allocates significant funds to development programs, including 11.59 billion MRU for the second phase of the Nouakchott development program and 26.4 billion MRU for an emergency program to expand access to essential services by 2027. The Dakhlet Nouadhibou region's development plan for 2026-2029 includes an estimated 39.6 billion MRU for infrastructure supporting productive sectors, water, sanitation, transport, and urban modernization.

Economic forecasts highlight risks to budget execution, such as ongoing Middle East tensions, rising energy prices, potential drops in gold prices or gas production disruptions, and reduced preferential financing. Delays in inflation reduction could also increase public aid burdens. The government stated that expenditure ceilings, a liquidity reserve, and contributions to the national oil revenue fund are in place to mitigate these risks.

Why it mattersInvestors face potential budget instability due to global economic risks like energy price hikes and commodity price drops.

Opposition party protests potential third presidential term

The Tewassoul party organized simultaneous rallies across Mauritania to reject a third presidential term and protest corruption and rising prices.

The opposition party Tewassoul organized simultaneous protest rallies on Wednesday in Nouakchott and other wilaya capitals. The demonstrations aimed to reject a potential third presidential term for the current leader and to denounce what the party described as corruption, rising prices, and restrictions on public freedoms.

According to a party statement, these rallies are part of a broader action plan to express the party's stance on the political and economic situation. Tewassoul is demanding action against corruption, improvements in living conditions, and the preservation of public liberties. The party had previously announced a schedule of actions including federation and section rallies from October 23 to 25.

Further actions planned by Tewassoul include internal travel and festivals in wilaya capitals between November 1 and 15, followed by regional festivals from November 15 to 30. These events are being coordinated with the party's parliamentary group as part of their ongoing campaign.

Why it mattersThe opposition party Tewassoul is protesting against a potential third presidential term and economic issues, affecting the political landscape.

President Ghazouani orders opening of 28 health centers

Mauritanian President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani has directed the government to expedite the launch of 28 newly built health facilities in Nouakchott within days.

President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani has instructed the Mauritanian government to swiftly operationalize 28 recently constructed health centers in Nouakchott in the coming days. The directive, issued via a presidential communiqué, follows a meeting between the president, the prime minister, relevant ministers, and the governors of Nouakchott's three regions. The meeting aimed to assess construction progress and finalize preparations for the facilities' launch.

The presidential orders emphasize the need to complete medical teams, equip the centers with necessary medical supplies and materials, and ensure the buildings are maintained and clean. A digital system for managing patients, consultations, and medical stock is to be implemented, with clear responsibilities and timelines established. Field monitoring will guarantee the execution of these measures.

Further directives include reinforcing professional discipline among health teams, improving center management, rationalizing resource use, and monitoring service quality. The president also called for organizing specialized consultations and strengthening coordination among the involved sectors to ensure the efficient functioning of these new health facilities.

Why it mattersResidents of Nouakchott will gain access to 28 new health centers offering improved medical services and management.

Mauritania approves decree for local drug manufacturing

A new government decree aims to regulate pharmaceutical factories, boost domestic supply, and reduce reliance on imported medicines.

Mauritania's Council of Ministers has approved a draft decree to establish procedures for opening pharmaceutical manufacturing facilities. The decree outlines technical standards and operational conditions for these establishments, addressing a regulatory gap in local drug production.

The new regulation seeks to develop a domestic pharmaceutical industry capable of ensuring a stable supply of medicines. By reducing dependence on imports, the government aims to enhance Mauritania's health sovereignty. The decree also details application processes for manufacturing licenses and establishes a commission to review applications.

Furthermore, the legislation lays the groundwork for good manufacturing practices for human-use drugs. It also introduces a system to coordinate local production with medicine imports, aiming for a more integrated national health supply chain.

Why it mattersThe decree affects Mauritanians by aiming to ensure a more stable and potentially affordable supply of medicines through local production.

Politics · 2026-10-22

Mauritanian official reassigned after power station fires

Akhrambali Ould Mohamed Lehbib moves to hydrocarbons company one week after dismissal from electricity firm following an investigation into recent fires.

Mauritania's Council of Ministers appointed Akhrambali Ould Mohamed Lehbib as the new director general of the Mauritanian Hydrocarbons Company. This appointment comes approximately one week after his dismissal from the helm of the Mauritanian Electricity Company, Somelec.

Lehbib's removal followed the release of an investigation report into two fires at Nouakchott's electrical substations in August. The report cited irregularities in awarding contracts for electrical cables and in overseeing their installation, including selecting a vendor lacking qualifications and altering cable specifications without proving equivalence.

The investigation also noted that cables were activated before technical reservations were cleared and that protection requirements were unmet. The fires caused significant power outages in the capital. President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani ordered the government to implement the report's recommendations and make the findings public.

Why it mattersThe public is affected by the power outages caused by the fires and the subsequent investigation into contract irregularities.
