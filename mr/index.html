Mauritania reclassifies bloggers' case as terrorism

What began as accusations of financial blackmail has shifted three times in seven weeks, now landing with anti-terrorism investigators.

Mauritanian authorities have reclassified the case of several bloggers, known as the "bloggers from abroad," as a terrorism investigation. This marks the third qualification change for the affair in less than seven weeks. The case began on September 30 with a government announcement of a "financial blackmail network," then shifted on October 7 to "financial corruption and moral depravity." On November 13, the investigation was assigned to the unit handling terrorism crimes.

The investigation now involves seven individuals detained, nine under judicial supervision, and arrest warrants for seven others, many of whom are abroad. The case originated from Facebook pages managed by Mauritanian bloggers, primarily residing in the United States, who have been publishing critical content since 2018. Prominent figures like Sidi Ould Kmache, Al-Hafiz Ould Loubad, and Diaa Al-Haq Ould Abih are among those sought by the authorities.

The prosecution has framed the legal basis for the case around three axes: criticism of political figures, intrusion into the private lives of officials including the president and his family, and the publication of sensitive military information. These allegations, which evolved from online posts and payment app transfers, have led to the case being transferred from the State Security Directorate to the anti-terrorism investigation unit.

Why it mattersSeven people are detained and seven more are sought by authorities in an investigation that has shifted from blackmail to terrorism charges.

UK pledges to implement export credit deal with Mauritania

British ambassador Guy Harrison said the agreement will widen financing for infrastructure projects and investment between the two countries.

Guy Harrison, the United Kingdom's ambassador to Mauritania, said his country is committed to implementing a memorandum of understanding signed with Mauritania on export credit. He made the remarks at a reception the embassy held in Nouakchott on Friday to mark the British sovereign's birthday.

Harrison said the deal will expand bilateral cooperation in financing infrastructure projects and open investment and partnership opportunities for economic actors in both countries. He said the embassy in Nouakchott continues to work on strengthening trade and investment ties.

Harrison praised British firms operating in Mauritania, naming BP, Chariot Energy and Ora Energy. He also reaffirmed the UK's commitment to cooperation on climate resilience, food security and protecting livelihoods, which he called essential to the country's stability.

Mauritania media authority lists professional outlets

The High Authority for Press and Audiovisual releases a preliminary list of national media outlets meeting professional standards.

The High Authority for Press and Audiovisual (HAPA) of Mauritania published a preliminary list on Friday of national media outlets that satisfactorily meet the professional and institutional standards required by law. Several audiovisual outlets, including Al-Sahel, Sahara 24, Al-Mourabitoun, Al-Medina, and Chinguetti, were identified as compliant.

For online media, HAPA identified the press agency Sahara Média, the Al-Sakhra Centre, Takadoumi, Mauritanie Aujourd’hui, Belwar Média, Tawater, Al-Murasiloun, Madar, the Mauritanian press agency, Cridem, Nouakchott Direct, Libjawi, Kader, Al-Tayyar, Info Plus, Mauritanie Aujourd’hui, Al-Fikr, Al-Chourouk Media, Mauritania News, Tahaad, and Al-Muraqib. The written press list included Elilam Media Groupe, Al-Amal, La Voix des Ouvriers, Le Rénovateur, the Al-Sada group for media, Authentique, Le Quotidien de Nouakchott, Éveil Hebdo, and FOOYRE BAMTAAARE.

HAPA stated that this decision was based on a technical evaluation from an exhaustive investigation into the national journalistic landscape in 2026, along with reliability audit reports and performance evaluations of national audiovisual institutions from 2025-2026. Further technical evaluations of internet site content and a report from the professional journalists' registry also informed the list.

Why it mattersThe High Authority for Press and Audiovisual has identified national media outlets that meet professional standards, affecting how citizens access information.

Burkina Faso beats Mauritania 1-0 in last seconds of CAN qualifier

Mauritania's national football team, the Mourabitounes, lost 1-0 in Ouagadougou and now has three points in Group 6 after two games.

Mauritania's national football team, the Mourabitounes, lost 1-0 to Burkina Faso on Friday evening in Ouagadougou. The decisive goal came in the final seconds of the match, in the third matchday of Group 6 of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers.

The Mourabitounes have three points after beating the Central African Republic in the first matchday and losing to Benin in the second. Having lost twice, they must now win all their remaining matches to secure a place in the next round.

The two sides meet again next Tuesday in Nouakchott, in the fourth matchday of the same qualifiers.

Why it mattersMauritania must win every remaining qualifier to reach the next round of the Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers, starting with the return match against Burkina Faso in Nouakchott next Tuesday.
