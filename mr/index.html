Mauritania opposition condemns student protest crackdown

The democratic opposition coalition in Mauritania has condemned the violent intervention of security forces against students protesting for public transport.

The leader of Mauritania's democratic opposition coalition condemned the violent intervention of security forces against students protesting for public transport on Monday. The coalition issued a statement on Tuesday, calling for an end to restrictions on public freedoms and for serious dialogue with student representatives. The leader stated that using force against students violates the rule of law and fundamental rights.

The opposition coalition reaffirmed that peaceful protest and freedom of expression are constitutional rights. They demanded that students' legal rights and their right to protest without threat or intimidation be guaranteed. The coalition called for concrete solutions to the students' demands, rather than a security-focused approach, and expressed solidarity with those affected by the events.

Student protests occurred on Monday around the University of Nouakchott, with demonstrators demanding the implementation of public transportation. Police intervened to disperse the protesters. The student unions called for dialogue and respect for the right to peaceful expression.

Mauritanian coast guard intercepts boat with 204 migrants

The intercepted vessel, originating from The Gambia, carried individuals from four different West African nations.

Mauritania's coast guard intercepted a boat carrying 204 clandestine migrants on Tuesday. The vessel, which departed from Barra, The Gambia, was stopped at 11 a.m. within Mauritanian territorial waters during routine surveillance and rescue missions.

The passengers comprised 143 Senegalese, 48 Gambians, 7 Guineans, and 6 Ivorians. Among the Senegalese migrants were 7 women and 20 minors, while the Gambian group included 10 women and 8 minors. The Guinean and Ivorian groups each had one woman.

This marks the second such interception by the Mauritanian coast guard in a week, following the capture of a boat with 191 migrants from Barny, Senegal, a few days prior.

Why it mattersThe Mauritanian coast guard intercepted a boat carrying 204 migrants from Senegal, The Gambia, Guinea, and Ivory Coast.

Mauritanian parliament hosts German delegation

Discussions focused on strengthening parliamentary ties and promoting investments between Mauritania and Germany.

The Speaker of Mauritania's National Assembly, Mohamed Ould M’Barek, met with German Parliamentary State Secretary Annette KRAMME in Nouakchott on Tuesday. KRAMME, representing the German Federal Minister of Justice, was accompanied by a delegation from the German Foundation for International Legal Cooperation. The delegation is visiting Mauritania from October 25 to 28, 2026.

Discussions covered the strong friendship and cooperation between Mauritania and Germany, particularly in the parliamentary sphere. Both sides explored avenues for enhancing exchanges and encouraging investments. The Speaker praised the visit as a reflection of the strong relations and emphasized the importance of continued bilateral cooperation.

KRAMME expressed satisfaction with the reception and the promising prospects for cooperation. A working meeting also took place between the German delegation and the Mauritanian-German Parliamentary Friendship Group, chaired by Vice-Speaker Ahmedou Ould Mohamed Mahfoudh Ould M’Baleh. The German delegation also visited the Parliament's television channel and the general assembly hall.

Why it mattersThe meeting between Mauritanian and German officials aims to strengthen parliamentary relations and promote investments between the two countries.

Mauritania plans 10-year rice development strategy

The agriculture ministry aims for food self-sufficiency by boosting productivity and reducing costs.

Mauritania has begun developing a 10-year national strategy for rice cultivation and food sovereignty. The initiative, a collaboration between the Ministry of Agriculture and International Cooperation Agency (JICA) and the Coalition for African Rice Development (CARD), seeks to achieve rice self-sufficiency. The plan focuses on increasing productivity, improving quality, and lowering production costs in the Trarza, Brakna, Gorgol, and Guidimakha regions.

Ahmed Salem Ould Al-Arabi, secretary-general of the Ministry of Agriculture, stated that rice farming is a significant economic and food sector, creating jobs and reducing reliance on imports. However, challenges such as water management, infrastructure maintenance, seed and input supply, financing, mechanization, and climate change hinder development.

The strategy will be implemented in two phases over the next decade. Officials aim to enhance the competitiveness of national rice production. The development plan addresses issues from harvest and storage to climate impacts.

Why it mattersMauritania's agriculture sector is developing a 10-year strategy to boost rice production and achieve food self-sufficiency.

Mauritania approves 360 education sector hires and promotions

Minister Houda Babah announced the government's approval for new recruitment and advancement of 360 education professionals.

The Mauritanian government has approved the recruitment and promotion of 360 education sector agents, announced Minister of Education and Reform Houda Babah. The approved measures include hiring 30 fundamental education inspectors and promoting 30 others to chief inspector. Additionally, 100 first-cycle teachers will be promoted to secondary education, and 200 course instructors will become first-cycle secondary education professors.

Minister Babah made the announcement during a meeting with union representatives to discuss sector issues. The meeting also covered criteria for the President of the Republic Award for Excellence and decisions on appointing directors for newly constructed and currently building schools.

The approved promotions and recruitments aim to bolster the education system. The government also reviewed procedures for regional appointments of school directors.

Why it matters360 education sector agents in Mauritania will be recruited or promoted.
