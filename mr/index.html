Mauritania and World Bank Discuss Reform Project

Government officials and World Bank representatives met to review proposed reforms for the Prevention and Resilience project.

Mauritania's Ministry of Economic Affairs and Development initiated meetings this week to discuss reform proposals with the World Bank. These discussions are crucial for meeting the conditions required to access funding for the "Prevention and Resilience" (PRA) project. The goal is to strengthen the partnership between the Mauritanian government and the World Bank by examining priority reforms with technical and financial partners to create an integrated and achievable reform matrix.

Yacoub Ould Ahmed Aïcha, Secretary-General of the Ministry, stated that securing resources for this mechanism depends on achieving expected results. He urged ministerial sectors and relevant institutions to take responsibility, monitor reform implementation, and adhere to agreed-upon deadlines. Aïcha highlighted that the national strategy for peace and social cohesion (2025-2030) emphasizes decentralization, territorial integration, economic empowerment for youth and women, natural resource governance, and extremism prevention.

Ibou Diouf, the World Bank's Resident Representative in Mauritania, stressed the importance of enhanced cooperation. He noted the meeting as an opportunity to discuss the proposed reforms for the "Prevention and Resilience" project, underscoring the significance of collaboration in peace and security given regional challenges.

Why it mattersFarmers may be affected by reforms related to natural resource governance and economic empowerment initiatives.

Mauritania Aviation Agency Joins Global Security Forum

National Civil Aviation Agency officials attended the ICAO Security Week in Astana to share expertise and prepare for an upcoming international audit.

The Mauritanian National Civil Aviation Agency participated in the International Civil Aviation Organization's (ICAO) Security Week 2026 in Astana, Kazakhstan. The agency's director general, Baba Ahmed Mohamed Baba Ahmed, and the director of security and facilities, Oumar Mokhtar, represented Mauritania at the forum. The agency stated on Facebook that the event provided a crucial platform for exchanging knowledge and experiences among states and relevant bodies in civil aviation security.

The forum focused on current developments and challenges in air transport security, exploring methods to enhance aviation security systems and improve protection procedures and facilities in line with international standards. This participation is particularly significant as Mauritania prepares for its national civil aviation security system to undergo an ICAO security audit in 2027, assessing its compliance with global norms.

This engagement aims to bolster Mauritania's preparedness for future international aviation security engagements. By participating in discussions on the latest security trends and best practices, the agency seeks to strengthen its own systems and procedures ahead of the critical 2027 audit.

Why it mattersMauritania's aviation sector is preparing for an international audit, making participation in global security forums directly relevant to its operational standards.

Chinguitel Upgrades Rosso and Kaédi Networks

The telecommunications company has modernized its infrastructure to enhance service quality and network stability for subscribers.

Chinguitel has announced the completion of network upgrades in the cities of Rosso and Kaédi. The company stated the modernization aims to improve the quality of telecommunications and internet services, while also strengthening network stability and efficiency.

The initiative involved developing technical infrastructure and equipment, which Chinguitel believes will boost network performance and the reliability of services offered to customers. This comes amid a growing demand for telecommunications and internet services across the region.

This project is part of Chinguitel's broader program to modernize its infrastructure and expand services throughout Mauritania. The company is investing in modern networks and technologies to support the nation's digital transition and meet customer expectations.

Mauritania Ministry Inspects Meat Quality in Nouakchott Butcheries

Government teams are collecting meat samples for laboratory analysis to ensure food safety and detect dangerous substances.

The Mauritanian Ministry of Commerce and Tourism has launched an inspection campaign across Nouakchott's butcheries to verify the safety and wholesomeness of red meat. Teams from the consumer protection and anti-fraud directorate, alongside the Mauritanian Food Safety Agency, are conducting the checks.

These teams are collecting samples of meat on sale for laboratory analysis to detect any potential traces of dangerous substances. The intensified controls are part of the ministry's routine activities aimed at protecting food safety and public health.

The ministry plans to extend these quality control measures to other food products available in markets and shops. This initiative seeks to safeguard consumers and ensure the integrity of the food supply chain within the capital's three wilayas.

Why it mattersConsumers in Nouakchott are affected by potential risks to their health from unsafe meat products.

Retired soldiers to mentor Mauritanian youth

Mauritanian authorities are enlisting retired military personnel to guide young students in technical and vocational training centers.

Mauritanian officials are preparing to involve retired army personnel in mentoring young people at technical and vocational training institutions. This program focuses on civic and moral education, with an initial phase in Sélibabi and a subsequent expansion to Néma.

A delegation from the Ministry of Defense, headed by retired Colonel Sidi El Bou, has begun on-site preparations. They are coordinating with training centers to form teams of retirees who will oversee students, leveraging their career experience.

The initiative aims to instill values of citizenship, discipline, and responsibility in young students. This effort seeks to ensure that experienced individuals continue to contribute to training and guidance even after completing their military service.

Why it mattersRetired soldiers will mentor youth in technical and vocational schools, impacting educational development.
