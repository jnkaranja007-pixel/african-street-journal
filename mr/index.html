Mauritania central bank raises interest rate to 7.25%

The central bank also increased the mandatory reserve rate for banks from 8% to 15% to combat rising inflation.

The Mauritanian central bank increased its key interest rate by 50 basis points, from 6.75% to 7.25%, on January 11, 2026. The bank also raised the mandatory reserve rate for commercial banks from 8% to 15%. These measures aim to curb inflation, which reached 9.3% in September 2026.

According to a statement from the Monetary Policy Council, these decisions are intended to control inflationary pressures, anchor price stability expectations, and absorb excess banking liquidity. The council noted that the real interest rate, adjusted for inflation, remains negative. The increase in mandatory reserves is expected to immobilize an additional 10.19 billion ouguiyas in banks, bringing the total to approximately 21.83 billion ouguiyas.

The Monetary Policy Council cited accelerating inflation, which surpassed its previous forecast of 8.2% for September, and signs of price increases spreading beyond fresh produce and energy as reasons for tightening monetary policy. The council warned of rising inflation risks due to potential increases in imported energy and food costs, noting that previous forecasts assumed falling international prices and stable fuel costs.

Why it mattersMauritanian banks will have approximately 10.19 billion more ouguiyas immobilized due to increased reserve requirements.

Somelec blames technicians for Nouakchott power pole fires

Mauritania's electricity company cites unqualified climbers and illegal connections for recurring fires in the capital.

The Mauritanian electricity company Somelec stated that unqualified external technicians, poor quality cables, and illegal connections are causing recurring fires on electric poles. These practices lead to short circuits when network load and pressure increase, exceeding the capacity of poles and transformers.

Somelec announced a field campaign to remove illegal connections and regularize hookups on poles and the electrical network in the capital, Nouakchott. The company urged citizens to help protect the electrical network and warned of risks to lives, property, and neighborhood safety.

Nouakchott has recently experienced repeated fires in electrical installations, including distribution posts and technical rooms. Somelec attributed these incidents to electrical overload and rising temperatures.

Why it mattersRecurring fires in Nouakchott's electrical installations threaten the safety of residents and their property.

Mauritanian navy saves 134 migrants near Nouadhibou

The migrants, including women and children, were en route to the Canary Islands when intercepted.

Mauritania's national navy rescued 134 migrants from a boat in the Nouadhibou port channel on Saturday. The migrants, of various nationalities, were attempting to reach the Canary Islands when the navy intercepted their vessel.

The rescued group comprised 118 men, 14 women, and two children, according to an army announcement. This operation was part of the navy's efforts to save lives at sea and combat illegal immigration and trafficking networks.

The navy also aims to enhance maritime security and safety. The army stated these actions are taken to save lives at sea.

Mauritania interior minister reviews capital's sanitation

Minister Mokhtar Ould Ahmed Bousseif met with officials to assess the performance of the contracted cleaning company, Arma.

Mauritania's Interior Minister Mokhtar Ould Ahmed Bousseif chaired a meeting in Nouakchott on Sunday to evaluate the city's sanitation services and the performance of the contracted company, Arma. The minister reviewed the company's contract, which began in July 2025, and noted ongoing issues with waste management in the capital's neighborhoods and streets.

The minister stated that Arma's contractual obligations have not been met to the required standard, giving the company until the end of December to correct deficiencies. He also stressed the need to activate legal frameworks for sanitation and enforce penalties for violations, including construction sites improperly disposing of debris. The governors of Nouakchott's three wilayas also reported a decline in Arma's performance.

The government is considering alternative solutions to ensure the capital's cleanliness, as the current company may not wish to continue the contract beyond the December deadline. The minister emphasized that the priority is achieving tangible results in cleaning Nouakchott, whether through the current contractor or other options.

Why it mattersThe sanitation services in the capital city of Nouakchott are being reviewed due to the underperformance of the contracted company, Arma.

Mauritanian minister orders Zouerate hospital work accelerated

Mamoudou Mamadou Niang inspected ongoing construction at the regional hospital and schools in Zouerate.

Mamoudou Mamadou Niang, Mauritania's minister of state property and land reform, ordered work on Zouerate's regional hospital to speed up on Saturday. He visited ongoing construction sites as part of an emergency program to expand access to essential services.

The minister inspected school construction at "Tarhil 2" and "deputy Saïdou Wane" schools, where work was completed. At Hadrami Ould Moma school, he reviewed progress on four classrooms and received explanations on remaining steps.

Niang's visit aimed to assess the progress of construction and the readiness of completed facilities, ensuring adherence to technical specifications and required standards for the hospital and schools.

Why it mattersResidents of Zouerate will benefit from the accelerated completion of the regional hospital and new school classrooms.
