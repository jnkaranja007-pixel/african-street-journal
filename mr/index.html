Mauritanian diplomat elected OIC Secretary General

The International Network of Arab and African Journalists congratulated Ismail Ould Cheikh Ahmed on his unanimous election, citing his extensive experience.

Ismail Ould Cheikh Ahmed, a Mauritanian diplomat, has been elected Secretary General of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation (OIC). The International Network of Arab and African Journalists welcomed the appointment, sending congratulations and wishing him success in his new role. The network highlighted Ahmed's decades of political and professional experience, both domestically and within the United Nations, as qualifications for bringing qualitative change to the organization's operations and addressing its challenges.

The network praised the Mauritanian government for officially nominating Ould Cheikh Ahmed, stating that this action enhances the diplomatic presence of Mauritanian talent and confirms international confidence in their leadership capabilities for regional and international bodies. The organization noted Mauritania's history of producing competent individuals who have held significant positions in international and regional organizations such as the UN, Arab League, and African Union.

Ould Cheikh Ahmed's election was reportedly unanimous. His background suggests he is well-equipped to lead the OIC and navigate the complexities of common Islamic action. The network's statement underscores the significance of this appointment for Mauritanian diplomacy and its broader implications for international cooperation.

Mauritania Approves New Drug Law Expanding Criminal Offenses

The government has enacted a new bill that broadens criminal penalties to encompass the production, trafficking, and financing of narcotics and psychotropic substances.

Mauritania's government approved a new draft law on Thursday that expands criminal offenses related to narcotics and psychotropic substances. The legislation targets production, manufacturing, processing, possession, cultivation, and trafficking, as well as criminal groups involved in these activities. Officials stated the bill aims to modernize the national drug control system and enhance prevention, detection, and deterrence measures in line with international standards.

The new law also addresses acts involving precursors and equipment used for illicit production. It includes specific provisions for the creation, management, direction, financing, membership, or support of criminal organizations. This legislative action follows recent successes by security services, which have dismantled ten active drug trafficking networks since early July.

These operations have led to the arrest of at least 40 individuals and the seizure of over 20 kilograms of various narcotics, more than ten thousand psychotropic tablets, and hallucinogenic pills. Seized items included industrial cannabis resin known as 'black stone,' Indian cannabis, high-purity cocaine, synthetic drugs like 'kouch,' ecstasy, crystal, amphetamines, and psychotropic medications such as pregabalin.

Why it mattersThe new law affects farmers by potentially impacting the cultivation of cannabis and other illicit crops, and influences investors by signaling increased regulatory scrutiny and enforcement in the drug trade sector.

SOMELEC Restores Power to Eastern and Northern Transformation Stations

Mauritania's electricity company announced the full restoration of service at two key transformation stations after repair work, ensuring electricity supply to several districts.

The Mauritanian Electricity Company (SOMELEC) has announced the return to normal operations at the Eastern and Northern electrical transformation stations, located in Toujounine and Tevrag Zeina respectively. The company's general director, Kharambale Lehbib, stated that repair work has successfully brought both stations back to 100% operational capacity. This restoration means that all connected neighborhoods, including Toujounine, Dar Naïm, Tarhil, and Mellah, are now receiving electricity.

Lehbib explained that the repairs involved bringing the 15 kV cells back into service after their refurbishment and recommissioning. The company also utilized its existing backup equipment and imported additional components from abroad to complete the restoration. The primary challenge moving forward, according to Lehbib, is ensuring the continuous and stable functioning of this equipment in the coming days and weeks.

The successful repair and recommissioning of the transformation stations mark a significant step in stabilizing the electricity supply for the affected areas. The efforts included not only the repair of critical components but also the strategic use of internal resources and international procurement of necessary parts to overcome the technical issues.

Why it mattersBusinesses and residents in Toujounine, Dar Naïm, Tarhil, and Mellah will benefit from the restored and stable electricity supply, impacting daily operations and services.

Rainfall Drenches Five Mauritanian States

Scattered showers were recorded across five regions in the last 24 hours, according to the Ministry of Interior.

Rainfall has been recorded in scattered areas across five Mauritanian states over the past 24 hours. The Administrative Communications Department of the Ministry of Interior, Decentralization and Local Development reported the precipitation amounts.

The ministry's report did not specify the exact quantities of rain that fell in each area or the specific locations within the five states. This information is based on readings from rain gauges across the country.

The affected states are spread across the nation, though no further details were provided regarding the impact or distribution of the rainfall. The report was issued by the ministry's communication service.

Why it mattersFarmers in five states may benefit from the rainfall, potentially aiding crop growth and water availability.
