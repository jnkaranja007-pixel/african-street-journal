Mauritanian doctors protest exclusion from Algerian scholarships

Graduates from Egyptian universities say their names were removed from a list for specialization courses in Algeria.

Dozens of Mauritanian doctors who graduated from Egyptian universities are protesting their exclusion from specialization scholarships in Algeria. Their names, initially listed online for 41 students, were removed from the final list of 21 recipients. The affected doctors claim they submitted all required documents and have filed appeals that have gone unanswered.

The protesting doctors stated they were surprised by the exclusion, especially after seeing their names on an initial online platform. They have appealed to Prime Minister Mokhtar Ould Diay, submitting two lists of beneficiaries. They reported being told by the scholarship administration that the matter was not within their purview, and the IT administration denied responsibility.

The Minister of Higher Education, Yacoub Ould Meïne, expressed surprise at their exclusion and directed them to the IT administration. The ministry cited a technical opinion from a cultural advisor regarding Egyptian medical studies and training procedures as justification for the exclusion. The doctors maintain they provided all requested documents and are prepared to supply a training completion certificate, which was not initially required.

Why it mattersMauritanian medical graduates are unable to pursue specialized studies in Algeria due to their exclusion from scholarship lists.

Adrar governor launches cleaning campaign in Atar

Governor Abdellahi Ould Mohamed Mahmoud initiated a sanitation drive in Atar's sector 5, targeting Adabaye neighborhood and the western administrative area.

Adrar Governor Abdellahi Ould Mohamed Mahmoud launched a cleaning campaign in sector 5 of Atar city on Saturday. The initiative is part of a regional sanitation drive, following directives from the President.

The governor visited the area, which includes the Adabaye neighborhood and the western administrative zone, to observe the sanitation work. He praised the efforts of the Tourism and Hajj Federation section and urged residents to cooperate in eliminating waste and random dumps.

The head of the Tourism and Hajj Federation section in Adrar, Aslamou Ould Cherki, stated the campaign aims to improve the urban landscape and public health. He called on citizens to actively participate in the cleaning efforts.

Why it mattersThe governor of Adrar and local officials launched a cleaning campaign in Atar city, affecting residents and urban areas.

Mauritania sends 9 students to Algeria for medical studies

The move contradicts a 2024 policy to halt overseas scholarships for high school graduates.

Mauritania's Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research has sent nine high school graduates to Algeria to study general medicine. This is part of a 50-place medical contingent for the 2026-2027 academic year. The decision raises questions about a government policy announced in August 2024 to stop scholarships abroad for baccalaureate holders.

A document obtained by Sahara Media shows the list includes nine students for general medicine and 41 for specialized medical studies, with a total of 241 students being sent abroad. Sidi Mohamed Ahmed, interim director of orientation, scholarships, and international cooperation, signed the document. The National Commission for Orientation and Scholarships announced in September 2024 the suspension of state-funded scholarships abroad for baccalaureate holders, limiting them to master's and doctoral levels.

The ministry has not revealed the selection criteria or mechanisms for the 50 students going to Algeria for medical studies. The commission stated the new policy aims to strengthen national sovereignty at the undergraduate level and redirect funds to master's and doctoral scholarships and university infrastructure. A supplementary list is expected to fill the remaining 350 available places.

Why it mattersNine Mauritanian high school graduates are sent to Algeria to study general medicine, contradicting a recent government policy.
