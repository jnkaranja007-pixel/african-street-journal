Germany funds 15 million euros for Mauritanian jobs

Nouakchott and Berlin sign financing agreement to support small and medium enterprises.

Mauritania's Minister of Economic Affairs and Development, Mouhamedou Ahmedou Mhaimid, signed a 15 million euro financing agreement in Nouakchott on Tuesday. The deal with Sonja Haus, regional advisor for the German development bank KfW, aims to support employment in Mauritania by financing small and medium enterprises (SMEs). The funds, equivalent to approximately 675 million Mauritanian Ouguiya (MRU), will provide financial resources to foster value creation, growth, and job creation within these businesses.

Minister Mhaimid stated that Mauritania and Germany share a strong partnership across economic and social sectors, highlighting employment as a key area of cooperation. He noted that mobilizing resources remains a challenge for SMEs. The German Ambassador stated the agreement strengthens bilateral relations and reflects a mutual desire for continued partnership based on trust and open dialogue, expressing hope for improved living conditions for young Mauritanians.

The financing agreement is intended to provide SMEs with the financial resources they need. This initiative is expected to contribute to the improvement of living conditions for young Mauritanians. The partnership between Mauritania and Germany is described as solid and fruitful in multiple economic and social areas.

Why it mattersSmall and medium enterprises in Mauritania will receive financing to create jobs and foster growth.

Three Koumé family members face Nouakchott court

Family members arrested during a forced eviction on October 21 are to appear before the prosecutor on November 5.

Three young members of the Koumé family will appear before the Nouakchott west prosecutor on Thursday, November 5, 2026, following their arrest during a controversial eviction from their home on Îlot L.

The family denounces the October 21 forced eviction, which involved nine police vehicles, and disputes the land documents used for the operation. They state that women and children are still living in the street outside their former home and report death threats on November 3.

A peaceful sit-in is announced for Thursday at 10 a.m. in front of the Nouakchott west justice palace if the three are not released. Organizers are calling for their return to their home, an independent investigation into the eviction and arrests, and accountability.

Why it mattersThe Koumé family, including women and children, are displaced and living in the street following a forced eviction and subsequent arrests.

Mauritania affirms positive neutrality in regional affairs

Culture Minister Mohamed Ould Babana stated the nation is part of the solution, not the problem.

Mauritania is closely monitoring developments in its regional environment and is committed to positive neutrality, according to Culture Minister Mohamed Ould Babana. He stated the country sees itself as part of the solution, not the problem. The minister made these remarks on Wednesday evening while commenting on cabinet meeting results.

Responding to a question about private media, Ould Babana confirmed the culture sector is open to private media institutions. He also discussed efforts to develop public media and enhance partnerships between authorities and press organizations. The ministry is consulting with media stakeholders on support mechanisms and fund outcomes.

The cabinet also approved draft decrees and statements concerning economic affairs, finance, and higher education. Minister of Economic Affairs and Development Ould M'Haimid highlighted reforms to the public procurement system. He stated a new decree modernizes public tenders, improves efficiency, rationalizes spending, and strengthens transparency and governance.

Why it mattersThe Minister of Economic Affairs and Development stated reforms to the public procurement system will modernize public tenders, improve efficiency, rationalize spending, and strengthen transparency.
