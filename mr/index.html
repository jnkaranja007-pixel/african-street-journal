Mauritania's parliament approves two International Development Association financing deals

The loans fund a social protection overhaul and an extension of high-speed digital infrastructure, and must be repaid over 20 years.

Mauritania's parliament approved two financing agreements with the International Development Association (IDA), the World Bank's concessional lending arm, on Monday. The texts cover the strengthening of resilience and productive social safety nets, and an additional loan for the digital transformation project under the first phase of the regional West African digital integration programme.

Economic Development Minister Mohamedou Ahmedou M'Haimid told deputies that the first agreement, signed on 21 August 2026, will fund technical assistance to review the social registry, its payment system and its complaints procedures. The second, signed on 16 September 2026, is worth €25.8 million and will extend high-speed digital infrastructure, develop digital services and integrate digital markets across West Africa.

The loan is repayable over 20 years, with a five-year grace period, annual interest of 1.5% and a 0.5% annual commitment fee on amounts not yet disbursed.

Why it mattersRepayment of the €25.8 million loan begins only after five years, so the state's debt service is spread over two decades.

Mauritania weighs border risks after UN ends MINURSO mandate

The Security Council declined to renew the Western Sahara mission on 30 October, removing a mechanism that monitored incidents and helped contain escalation.

Mauritania is assessing security risks along its northern border after the United Nations Security Council declined to renew the mandate of MINURSO, the UN mission in Western Sahara, on 30 October 2026. The mission’s monitoring role is now ending, removing a mechanism that documented violations, checked incidents and helped contain escalation.

A prospective risk assessment cited by Le Calame rates the chance of armed incidents spilling into Mauritanian territory as low to medium, but their potential impact as very high. It rates risks from weapons and armed groups, and cross-border trafficking, as medium to high in probability and high in impact. The report says northern desert areas could face accidental incursions, gunfire or movements of armed groups.

The report describes continued low-intensity fighting as the more likely of four possible paths, with intermittent clashes and greater uncertainty in Mauritania’s north. A wider escalation could increase pressure on security and civil-protection services; intensified fighting could also displace people toward areas seen as safer, requiring reception, registration, protection and assistance.

Why it mattersMauritanian security and civil-protection services face added pressure in the escalation scenario, while displaced people would need reception, registration, protection and assistance.

Morocco Week brings more than 50 Moroccan companies to Nouakchott

The third edition of the economic and cultural event runs until 22 November, with bilateral meetings between businesspeople and institutions from both countries.

The third edition of Morocco Week, an economic and cultural event, opened in Nouakchott on Monday with more than 50 Moroccan companies operating in different sectors taking part. Organised by Morocco's embassy in Mauritania and the Forum du Sud association, with several Mauritanian government departments and economic institutions, it runs until 22 November.

Mauritania's Trade and Tourism Minister, Zeinebou Ahmednah, said the companies' participation showed business interest in investment and trade between the two countries. The programme includes bilateral meetings between businesspeople, companies and institutions, and conferences on joint projects.

Morocco's minister delegate for foreign trade, Omar Hejira, said economic ties were moving towards lasting integration. Morocco's ambassador to Mauritania, Hamid Chabar, called for wider cooperation in agriculture, sea fishing, tourism, trade and vocational training.

Why it mattersMauritanian and Moroccan firms are holding talks through 22 November on investment and trade in sectors including fishing and agriculture.
