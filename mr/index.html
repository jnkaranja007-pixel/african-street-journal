Mauritania Well Exceeds Target Depth for Deep Water Exploration

The Ministry of Hydraulics and Sanitation announced that the Tintane experimental deep well has reached 518 meters, surpassing its 500-meter target.

The Ministry of Hydraulics and Sanitation announced that the first experimental deep well in Tintane has reached a depth of 518 meters, exceeding the targeted 500 meters. This achievement is part of an experimental deep drilling program aimed at exploring the nation's deep groundwater potential.

Officials stated that reaching this depth represents an unprecedented advancement in the exploration of deep groundwater resources within the country. The ministry highlighted that the initial results from the drilling are encouraging and that the program will continue at other identified sites to further assess and potentially expand water resources.

This initiative seeks to identify new water sources that could significantly contribute to strengthening Mauritania's overall water reserves. The program's continuation at additional locations underscores a commitment to a comprehensive exploration of the country's underground water potential.

Why it mattersThe deep well exceeding its target depth offers potential for new water sources, which could benefit farmers by increasing water availability for irrigation.

Mauritania Envoy Attends Arab League Meeting

Ambassador Al-Hussein Sidi Abdallah Die participated in the 166th ordinary session of the Arab League Council in Cairo.

Mauritania's Permanent Representative to the Arab League, Ambassador Al-Hussein Sidi Abdallah Die, attended a meeting of the council at the level of permanent delegates in Cairo. The session, the 166th ordinary session, took place at the headquarters of the General Secretariat.

The delegates discussed several agenda items, including the Palestinian issue, the Arab-Israeli conflict, and Arab national security. Legal and administrative matters related to the Arab working process were also on the table. Recommendations from this meeting will be presented to the ministerial-level council.

Ambassador Die was joined by First Counselor Mohamed Mahboubi and Second Counselor Dah Bab. The ministerial meeting is scheduled for Monday, September 7. The discussions covered a range of critical regional issues.

Why it mattersThe meeting addressed regional security and political issues that could affect Mauritania's foreign relations and stability.

Ministry Denies Sponsoring Off-Brand Tourism Activities

Mauritania's Commerce and Tourism Ministry refutes claims of backing travel and media content that violates societal values.

The Ministry of Commerce and Tourism has denied any responsibility for travel, activities, or media content circulated and associated with its national domestic tourism promotion program, 'My Country... My Destination.' The ministry stated it is implementing a national program to showcase the country's tourist and cultural assets and encourage citizens to vacation domestically.

The ministry clarified that launched tourist seasons, including 'Al-Gaitna' in Tagant, 'Culture of the River' in Guidimakha, and 'Autumn' in Assaba, are organized under the supervision of competent administrative authorities and in compliance with legal and institutional provisions, respecting religious principles and moral values.

The ministry emphasized its program focuses on national heritage, including religious and cultural sites, with official media content distributed through approved digital platforms or public and private media. It explicitly stated it neither sponsored, supported, directed, nor authorized any travel, activity, or media content outside the official program framework or containing practices contrary to societal values.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Commerce and Tourism is clarifying its role in domestic tourism promotion to ensure its program aligns with national values and legal frameworks.

Workshop Approves Emergency Cash Response Procedures

Mauritanian Red Crescent and British Red Cross collaborate on a new mechanism to aid 100 disaster-affected families.

A workshop in Kiffa concluded Saturday, reviewing and approving simplified operational procedures for emergency cash responses. The initiative aims to establish a rapid and effective mechanism to support 100 families impacted by disasters, funded by the Disaster Fund implemented by the Mauritanian Red Crescent in partnership with the British Red Cross.

Officials emphasized the importance of a ready financial mechanism and flexible indicators for swift disaster response. Success hinges on clear roles, rapid information exchange, transparent beneficiary selection, and close coordination with local authorities and stakeholders who possess direct knowledge of field realities and population needs.

The project is an experimental initiative to enhance the Mauritanian Red Crescent's capacity for rapid response to urgent needs of populations affected by limited-scale disasters. It focuses on monitoring, anticipation, and pre-positioning financial resources to quickly activate emergency cash aid once predefined criteria are met.

Why it mattersOne hundred families affected by disasters will receive rapid financial assistance through a new emergency cash response mechanism.

Ghazouani receives OIC Secretary-General Ismaël Ould Cheikh Ahmed

The Mauritanian President met the newly elected OIC leader at his holiday residence in Assaba, acknowledging his unanimous election.

President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani met with the newly elected Secretary-General of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, Ismaël Ould Cheikh Ahmed, at his vacation site near Kiffa. The presidency stated that Ould Cheikh Ahmed expressed gratitude to President Ghazouani for his nomination and the efforts that led to his unanimous election.

During the meeting, Ould Cheikh Ahmed presented President Ghazouani with two awards: one in the name of the "Sira" (biography of the Prophet) and another on behalf of the OIC. President Ghazouani congratulated the Secretary-General, wishing him success in his new role.

Ismaïl Ould Cheikh Ahmed, a Mauritanian diplomat, succeeds Houssein Ibrahim Taha of Chad. His career includes roles as Minister of Foreign Affairs and Chief of Staff for President Mohamed Ould Cheikh Ghazouani, alongside diplomatic and UN positions.

Why it mattersThe meeting between the Mauritanian President and the newly elected OIC Secretary-General highlights diplomatic relations and the country's role in international organizations.
