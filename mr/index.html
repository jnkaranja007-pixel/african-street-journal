Mauritanian navy joins Gulf of Guinea exercises

Naval forces from Mauritania participated in the annual 'Grand African NEMO 2026' drills from October 29 to November 6.

Mauritania's navy participated in regional naval exercises in the Gulf of Guinea during the annual 'Grand African NEMO 2026' drills. The exercise, which ran from October 29 to November 6, involved naval forces from multiple countries aiming to combat illegal immigration, drug trafficking, and piracy.

The Mauritanian army announced the participation via its official Facebook page, stating the navy deployed the vessel 'Timbédra' and a special forces team. The drills also included search and rescue, medical evacuation, and efforts against illegal fishing, with Mauritanian liaison officers present at regional maritime coordination centers.

The stated goals of Mauritania's participation were to enhance maritime military cooperation and coordination, share experiences, and develop the operational capabilities of its navy. These exercises are designed to improve maritime security in the region.

Why it mattersMauritania's navy participated in maritime security exercises to enhance its operational capabilities and cooperation with other nations.

Mauritanian opposition denies rejecting UFP seat

The opposition coalition spokesperson stated that no decision has been made regarding the UFP's representation in the national dialogue commission.

The leader of Mauritania's democratic opposition coalition denied reports that the group refused to include the Union of Progressive Forces Party (UFP) in its representation for the national dialogue commission. Sidi Ould El Kori, spokesperson for the Alliance, stated on Sunday that the coalition has not yet discussed designating its representatives for this commission.

Sahara Media had reported that the opposition bloc rejected a proposal to give the UFP a seat within its quota for the commission, despite efforts by dialogue commission president Moussa Fall. Ould El Kori called these reports inaccurate and unfounded, emphasizing that any official stance on representation must come through authorized channels.

The Alliance is continuing consultations with the Democratic Opposition Institution to reach an agreement on appointing opposition representatives to the national dialogue supervision commission. Ould El Kori stressed that only official channels can attribute positions regarding the coalition's representation and participation in dialogue bodies.

Mauritania signs hajj agreement in Saudi Arabia

The deal covers the 1448 H pilgrimage season, with ministers from both nations meeting in Jeddah.

Mauritania and Saudi Arabia signed an agreement in Jeddah on Monday for the upcoming hajj pilgrimage season, 1448 Hijri. Mauritania's Minister of Islamic Affairs and Original Education, Sidi El-Haj El-Dah Ebe Aïnina, met with Saudi Minister of Hajj and Umrah, Tawfiq ben Fawzan Al-Rabiah, to finalise the deal.

The Saudi minister expressed pleasure at the agreement, highlighting Mauritania's status as a land of knowledge and Quranic preservation. He also recalled the historical ties between the Chenaquitas and the Kingdom. The Mauritanian minister praised the strong relationship between the two countries, particularly under the leadership of Mauritanian President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El-Ghazouani and Saudi King Selmane ben Abdelaziz.

The agreement signifies the current exceptional period in bilateral relations, according to the Mauritanian ministry. Saudi Arabia was acknowledged for its efforts in developing services for pilgrims undertaking hajj and umrah.

Why it mattersMauritanian citizens wishing to perform the hajj pilgrimage will be affected by the terms of this agreement.

National Assembly approves government policy statement

Lawmakers voted 145 to 21 to adopt the government's general policy statement presented by the prime minister.

Mauritania's National Assembly approved the government's general policy statement on Saturday. Prime Minister Mokhtar Ould Djaay presented the statement to the lawmakers.

National Assembly President Mohamed Bamba Makt announced the adoption of the statement with 145 votes in favour and 21 against. One deputy abstained from the vote.

The vote took place during a public session of the assembly. The government's policy statement outlines its agenda and plans for the country.

Why it mattersLawmakers in Mauritania have approved the government's policy statement, indicating legislative support for the administration's plans.

Mauritania public projects incur 34 billion MRO in late penalties

72 percent of 113 major public projects have exceeded deadlines, costing the state dearly.

Parliamentary group leader Ahmedou Ould M’Balla stated that late penalties for public projects in Mauritania have reached approximately 34 billion old ouguiyas. He reported on Monday that 81 out of 113 public projects, each exceeding 800 million old ouguiyas in funding, have passed their execution deadlines.

This delay affects about 72% of all such projects, according to Ould M’Balla. He explained that these delays not only harm citizens and hinder development but also impose extra financial burdens on the state through penalties and related costs.

Ould M’Balla suggested these funds could have built schools, health centers, or improved water and electricity supplies. He also noted that the delays could have funded job creation and improved living conditions for citizens.
