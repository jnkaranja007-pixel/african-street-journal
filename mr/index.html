Mauritania reaffirms commitment to combat desertification at UN conference

Mauritania's delegation, led by Moulay Ibrahim Moulay Idris, Secretary General of the Ministry of Environment, participated in COP17 in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia.

Mauritania reaffirmed its commitment to implementing the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification yesterday, Monday, during its participation in the 17th session of the Conference of the Parties (COP17) in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. The delegation aims to showcase national efforts in combating desertification and restoring degraded lands.

The Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development stated that the participation seeks to present Mauritania's experience in protecting ecosystems and promoting sustainable natural resource management. This conference provides an opportunity to exchange experiences and discover innovative practices and solutions in these areas.

The delegation, which includes experts from the Ministry and representatives from bodies responsible for combating desertification, also aims to strengthen international cooperation and partnerships to support national priorities in environmental protection and land restoration.

Why it mattersMauritanians, especially those in rural areas, are directly affected by the government's efforts to combat desertification and restore degraded lands.

Nouakchott Hospitals Maintain Services Amidst Power Outages

The two main hospitals in Nouakchott, the National Hospital Center and Cheikh Zayed Hospital, continued operations without interruption during recent electricity cuts.

Nouakchott's primary hospitals report uninterrupted service despite recent power outages affecting the capital. Both the National Hospital Center and Cheikh Zayed Hospital confirmed their medical services remained fully operational, utilizing backup power sources to ensure continuity. This comes after two fires at transformer stations caused significant disruptions across several neighborhoods.

The National Hospital Center stated it deployed seven generators, including a main 950 kVA unit and seven 250 kVA units, which have been running continuously since the crisis began. A dedicated emergency committee was established to manage fuel and logistics, reinforcing critical departments like resuscitation, emergency, and dialysis. No deaths were reported, and medical equipment functioned without interruption.

Similarly, Cheikh Zayed Hospital implemented measures to maintain power to all departments, ensuring patient care, emergencies, consultations, and surgeries proceeded normally. Technical and administrative teams continuously monitored the situation. The Mauritanian Electricity Company (Somelec) announced the gradual restoration of one transformer station and the installation of a replacement unit to cover 60% of the former station's load.

Why it mattersResidents of Nouakchott who require medical attention, especially for critical conditions, directly benefit from the hospitals' ability to maintain services during widespread power outages.

SOMELEC restores power to parts of Sebkha and Tevrag Zeina

Mauritania's electricity company, SOMELEC, announced the gradual return of power to several districts in Nouakchott after technical interventions on substations.

Electricity has been restored in parts of Sebkha and is progressively returning to large areas of Tevrag Zeina, according to a statement released by SOMELEC on Tuesday morning. This follows successful technical interventions to reactivate the northern substation, which operates at 33/15 kilovolts. Service was also re-established in Sebkha zones like Al-Koufa, Al-Basra, and Neteg, which were affected by technical failures on supply lines linked to the western substation.

The gradual restoration in Tevrag Zeina includes areas around the national cardiology center, neighborhoods along the Nouadhibou road, the Saudi ambassador's residence, the university district, Diar Tata, and the Exterior district towards the statistics administration. SOMELEC teams continue to work on restoring service to other affected neighborhoods progressively and securely, based on available load.

Work is also ongoing at the eastern substation, north of Nouakchott, to bring it to full capacity and extend power restoration to remaining affected areas. This follows the installation of a replacement 33 kV transformer station, which covers approximately 60% of the old station's load. These efforts address significant power outages in the capital caused by two fires at transformer stations within two days.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in Sebkha and Tevrag Zeina are directly affected by the return of electricity, improving daily life and economic activity.

Mauritania Ministry Sets October Date for Diaspora Forum

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, African Cooperation, and Mauritanians Abroad announced the first edition of the Mauritanian diaspora forum will convene in Nouakchott from.

The forum, held under the patronage of Mauritanian President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani, follows extensive consultations with Mauritanian communities in 22 countries across four continents. These discussions involved representatives from associations, federations, scientific and professional experts, business people, and individuals interested in diaspora issues.

A reflection day on June 30, organized by the ministry, debated the consultation results with government sectors, parliamentarians, and community organization representatives. This process identified key demands and concerns from the communities, which have been forwarded to relevant bodies for study and action.

The ministry stated the forum will serve as a national platform for dialogue between public authorities and Mauritanian communities worldwide. Its agenda includes developing policies and institutional frameworks for community affairs, strengthening social and legal protection for Mauritanians abroad, and leveraging diaspora skills for knowledge transfer and development support.

Why it mattersMauritanians living abroad will have a direct opportunity to voice their concerns and aspirations to their government, potentially influencing policies that affect their lives.

Mauritania appoints new National Human Rights Commission members

The Mauritanian Presidency announced the appointment of nine individuals to the National Human Rights Commission through a presidential decree issued today.

The Mauritanian government has named new members to its National Human Rights Commission, according to a presidential decree issued today, Tuesday. This move follows an announcement from the Presidency of the Republic regarding the appointments.

The newly appointed members include Amo Jagana, Tabara Ambedj, Moussa Ould Gaoui, El Najim Ould Abham, Mamadou Denba Ba, Eloulou Ahmed Zarroug, El Tah Mohamed Abnou, Nafissa El Mokhtar El Mesned, and Sidi Abdallahi Hammadi.

These individuals will now serve on the commission, which is responsible for addressing human rights issues within the country. The appointments were made public by the Mauritanian News Agency.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects Mauritanian citizens by establishing the individuals responsible for overseeing and protecting their human rights.
