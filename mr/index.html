Prix Chinguetti names 2026 laureates

Dr Moustapha ben Hamouch and Professor Oumar ben Ishaq win awards for Islamic studies and arts.

The Conseil du Prix de Chinguetti announced the 2026 winners on Thursday for Islamic studies, science and technology, and letters and arts. The number of candidates increased by 118% compared to the previous edition, with 30 nationalities represented.

Dr Moustapha ben Hamouch received the Islamic studies prize for his book "Les repères de l’urbanisme islamique". The Faculty of Science and Technology at the University of Nouakchott won the science and technology award for quantum physics research. Professor Oumar ben Ishaq was awarded the letters and arts prize for his critical edition of "Manar al-Ha’ir fi Tartib al-Nawadir".

The Conseil reviewed reports from specialized scientific committees on October 15 before announcing the results. This year marked the first time five national and foreign scientific institutions applied for the prize.

Why it mattersMauritanian scholars and institutions were recognised for their contributions to Islamic studies, science and technology, and arts.

Mauritania minister's niece withdraws from Morocco study place amid controversy

Zahra Al-Tayeb Amin renounces a doctoral enrollment in Morocco after a classmate questioned selection criteria.

Zahra Al-Tayeb Amin, niece of the Minister of Higher Education Yacoub Ould Meïne, has withdrawn her enrollment for doctoral studies in Morocco. The decision follows a public dispute with classmate Belkis Abdallahi El Adel, who claimed she was more qualified for the opportunity. The controversy centers on the selection process for a study place in Morocco, with El Adel asserting she was first in their master's promotion and Amin second.

Amin stated that the faculty had not published an official ranking among the three eligible students and that the opportunity was an enrollment place, not a scholarship as initially claimed. She also noted administrative hurdles due to her new training role within the Ministry of Social Action, Childhood, and Family. Amin announced she would pursue a scholarship offer from Utah State University in the United States instead.

El Adel rejected Amin's explanations, insisting her objection was about selection criteria, not a personal dispute. She pointed to an official university list ranking candidates by merit, contradicting Amin's claims. El Adel also criticized the lack of a public call for applications and clear selection criteria, noting that other students had not received similar opportunities in recent years.

Why it mattersStudents seeking doctoral study opportunities in Morocco are affected by opaque selection processes.

Mauritania gendarmerie seizes 4,500 litres of fuel

Nema-based gendarmes intercepted gasoline intended for smuggling as part of anti-trafficking efforts.

Mauritanian gendarmes in Nema, the capital of Hodh Chargui wilaya, seized 4,500 litres of gasoline destined for smuggling. The operation was conducted by a company of the gendarmerie based in the city.

The gendarmerie announced the seizure as part of its ongoing fight against fuel smuggling and illicit trafficking. The statement did not provide details on the circumstances of the seizure or the identity of the owner.

The operation targets illegal fuel trade, a common practice in border regions. No further information was provided on the next steps in the investigation or potential charges.

Why it mattersFuel smuggling affects the availability and price of gasoline for ordinary Mauritanians.

Mauritania and EU discuss fishing deal renewal

Fisheries Minister Aissata Lam met EU Commissioner Kostas Kadis to negotiate a new sustainable fishing partnership agreement.

Mauritania's Minister of Fisheries, Aissata Lam, held a video conference on Thursday with European Commissioner for Fisheries and Oceans, Kostas Kadis. The meeting aimed to continue discussions on the main points of the ongoing negotiations between Mauritania and the European Union regarding fishing agreements.

Both sides exchanged views on agenda items and explored ways to align their positions and enhance mutual understanding of the fishing partnership. The discussions also emphasized the importance of continued dialogue and coordination to achieve balanced outcomes that consider shared interests and promote sustainable exploitation of fish resources.

Intensive negotiations are underway to renew the sustainable fishing partnership protocol before the current agreement expires on November 14. The goal is to reach a new agreement that strengthens cooperation in the fisheries sector.

Why it mattersMauritania and the European Union are negotiating to renew a fishing agreement that affects their shared interests in fish resources.

Mauritanian party denounces dialogue committee selection

Al-Messar party criticizes unilateral decision-making process for representatives in national dialogue supervision commission.

The Party for Centre Action and Progress (Al-Messar) in Mauritania has expressed surprise and reservations regarding the selection of representatives for the majority parties' coordination committee overseeing the national dialogue. The party stated it was not consulted on the chosen method for selecting eight individuals, many of whom are party leaders, for the supervision commission.

Al-Messar communicated its objections in a letter to the Coordination of Majority Parties, calling for a review of the "unilateral methodology and adopted criteria" to ensure more inclusive and equitable representation. The party noted that the names were finalized and sent to the dialogue coordinator without addressing its concerns or demands.

The Front for Citizenship and Justice (JAM) also suspended relations with the Coordination of Majority Parties, citing the exclusion of its request to join the supervision commission and claiming some appointees hinder dialogue. Al-Messar urged the adoption of clear, participatory decision-making mechanisms within the coordination to strengthen partnership and serve national interests.

Why it mattersThe selection process for representatives in Mauritania's national dialogue supervision commission has caused internal party disputes.
