Mauritania orders EU fishing vessels to leave its waters

Mauritania's coast guard has told European Union fishing boats to head to the port of Nouadhibou before the fishing protocol expires at midnight.

Mauritania's coast guard has ordered all European Union fishing vessels working in its waters to stop fishing and head to the port of Nouadhibou before midnight, when the current fishing protocol with the EU expires. The coast guard said in a circular published on Saturday, November 14, that any vessel that ignores the order will be punished under current law.

The vessels must complete the administrative and maritime formalities needed to leave Mauritania's exclusive economic zone. The fishing agreement between Mauritania and the EU dates back to 1987, and the protocol now ending was never replaced, even after several rounds of talks in recent months.

Negotiators have not agreed on a new protocol, so European fleets cannot fish in Mauritanian waters until a replacement is signed.

Why it mattersEuropean fleets must leave Mauritanian waters and cannot return until a new protocol is signed, and any boat that stays faces sanctions.

Morocco Week to explore trade and investment in Nouakchott

The event, from November 16 to 22, aims to boost economic ties between Mauritania and Morocco.

Nouakchott will host the third "Morocco Week" from November 16 to 22, showcasing trade and investment opportunities between Mauritania and Morocco. Government institutions and economic bodies from both nations are participating in the event, organized in collaboration with the Moroccan embassy and the "South Forum" association.

The program includes bilateral meetings for businesspeople and companies, as well as roundtables focused on economic cooperation and joint projects. The event is supported by the Mauritanian Ministry of Commerce and Tourism and the Moroccan Ministry of Industry, Investment, and Digital Economy.

Discussions will cover opportunities in industry, construction, public works, energy, agri-food, agriculture, banking, and telecommunications. The week also features presentations on Moroccan culture, crafts, gastronomy, and local products, alongside artistic and musical evenings.

Why it mattersBusinesses and investors can explore trade and investment opportunities in sectors including industry, energy, and agriculture between November 16 and 22.

Mauritania hosts West African Quran memorization contest

Over 160 participants from 16 West African nations are competing in Nouakchott in the second edition of a Saudi-sponsored competition focused on the Quran and Sunna.

Nouakchott, Mauritania, hosted the opening of the second edition of the King Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud Guardian of the Two Holy Mosques competition on Sunday morning. The event, organized by Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Islamic Affairs, Da’wah and Guidance in collaboration with Mauritania's Ministry of Islamic Affairs and Original Education, aims to test the memorization of the Quran and Sunna among participants from West African countries.

The competition features 160 participants representing 16 West African nations, divided into two main categories: Quran memorization and Sunna memorization. Each category includes five disciplines, covering memorization, recitation (tajwid), exegesis (tafsir), and understanding of hadiths. Mauritanian Minister of Islamic Affairs Sid El-Haj Ould Dah stated that the event embodies the pooling of efforts and resources for the Quran and Sunna, strengthening scientific and intellectual exchanges between Islamic, Arab, and African countries.

Saudi Ambassador to Mauritania Abdelaziz ben Abdullah Al-Raqabi noted that holding the competition in Mauritania for the second time reflects the deep fraternal relations and historical ties between the two countries. He added that it signifies Mauritania's role in serving Islamic causes and enhancing regional cooperation. The competition's focus on religious scholarship highlights Mauritania's scientific standing and the contributions of its scholars and madrasas.

Mauritania reaffirms Francophonie commitment

Foreign Minister Mohamed Salem Ould Merzoug attended the 48th ministerial conference in Phnom Penh.

Mauritania is committed to continuing its contribution to the Francophonie movement, stated Foreign Minister Mohamed Salem Ould Merzoug on Saturday. He spoke at the opening of the 48th ministerial session of the International Organisation of La Francophonie in Phnom Penh, Cambodia. The minister aims for summit decisions to result in concrete actions for the French language, cultural diversity, and Francophone peoples.

Ould Merzoug praised France's report on implementing commitments from the 19th summit, viewing it as a foundation for a more unified and effective Francophone space. He emphasized that collective mobilization, regular dialogue, and solidarity among member states are fundamental to strengthening common commitments. The conference prepares for the 20th Francophonie Summit.

Mauritania is a candidate for the position of Secretary-General of the Organisation, with Coumba Ba's candidacy competing against three others. The new Secretary-General will be announced on Monday during the 20th Francophonie Summit in Phnom Penh.

Why it mattersMauritania's Foreign Minister attended a meeting in Cambodia to discuss the future of the Francophonie, where a new Secretary-General will be announced on Monday.
