Mauritania adopts 2027 budget with 152 billion ouguiya revenue

The government plans a 6.2 billion ouguiya surplus, projecting 0.5% economic growth amid global risks.

Mauritania's government has adopted its 2027 budget, projecting total revenues and subsidies at 152.11 billion ouguiyas. The budget anticipates expenditures and net borrowing of 145.91 billion ouguiyas, aiming for a global surplus of 6.2 billion ouguiyas. This financial plan forecasts a real economic growth of 0.5% for 2027, with an inflation rate of 3.3%.

The budget operates under a new fiscal rule limiting the non-extractive primary balance, including subsidies, to 3.5% of nominal GDP. This links spending ceilings to anticipated revenues, excluding extractive industry income. Under this rule, the non-extractive primary balance is set at a deficit of 21.03 billion ouguiyas for 2027, a reduction from the projected 22.18 billion ouguiyas deficit in 2026. Significant investments are allocated, including 11.59 billion ouguiyas for Nouakchott's development and 26.4 billion for a program to expand essential services by 2027.

Economic forecasts highlight risks to budget execution, such as Middle East tensions, rising energy prices, falling gold prices, or gas production disruptions. Decreased subsidies and preferential financing, alongside delayed inflation reduction, could increase public aid burdens. The government stated that the budget framework includes spending caps, a liquidity reserve at the central bank, and contributions to the national oil revenue fund to mitigate these risks.

Why it mattersMauritanian citizens will be affected by the government's budget allocations for essential services and local development programs.

Prix Chinguetti names 2026 laureates

Dr Moustapha ben Hamouch and Professor Oumar ben Ishaq win awards, alongside the University of Nouakchott.

The Prix Chinguetti announced its 2026 winners on Thursday, recognizing achievements in Islamic studies, science and technology, and letters and arts. The number of candidates increased by 118% compared to the previous year, with 30 nationalities represented.

Dr. Moustapha ben Hamouch received the Islamic studies prize for his work on Islamic urbanism. The University of Nouakchott's Faculty of Science and Technology won the science and technology award for research in quantum physics. Professor Oumar ben Ishaq was honored for his critical edition of a historical manuscript in the letters and arts category.

The selection followed a review of scientific committee reports and candidate work during an October 15 meeting. This edition marked the first time five national and foreign scientific institutions submitted applications for the award.

Why it mattersThe Prix Chinguetti awarded laureates in Mauritania for their contributions to Islamic studies, science, and arts.

Mauritania official distributes 2,500 school uniforms

The deputy of the Naama district provided the uniforms to support public schools and ease financial burdens on families.

The assistant governor of the Naama district, Al-Naha ولد المصطفى, distributed 2,500 school uniforms on Thursday in Naama city. The uniforms were provided by the district's deputy, Mohamed Al-Ghaith ولد الشيخ الحضرامي, for educational institutions in the district's municipalities.

This initiative aims to support public education, reduce financial burdens on families, and decrease social disparities in schools, promoting equal opportunities for students. The assistant governor emphasized the importance of such initiatives in developing public education and reinforcing the goals of the public school system.

The deputy's representative, Al-Sheikh Al-Tirad ولد آبي, stated the contribution supports educational reforms and families, aligning with the deputy's development, cultural, and social interventions. The inspector of education for Naama district, Sidi Mohamed ولد آباتي, praised the initiative for supporting students and improving learning conditions.

Why it matters2,500 school uniforms were distributed to students in Mauritania's Naama district to support public education and ease financial burdens on families.

Mauritania ruling parties face internal dissent over dialogue committee

Al-Messar party criticizes unilateral selection of eight representatives for national dialogue supervision commission.

The Party for Centre, Action and Progress (Al-Messar) in Mauritania has expressed surprise and reservations regarding the selection process for representatives to the national dialogue supervision commission. The party stated it was not consulted on the chosen mechanism, despite participating in prior discussions.

Al-Messar communicated its concerns in a statement, calling the methodology and selection criteria unilateral. The party addressed a letter to the Coordination of Majority Parties, urging a review to ensure more inclusive and equitable representation from its various components. Eight individuals were appointed to the committee.

The Front for Citizenship and Justice (JAM) also reacted by suspending relations with the Coordination of Majority Parties, citing the rejection of its request to join the supervision commission. JAM claimed that some appointed figures could hinder the dialogue process.

Why it mattersThe selection of representatives for a national dialogue commission in Mauritania has caused internal dissent among ruling coalition parties.

Mauritania minister inspects Tasiast mine

The minister of mines visited the Tasiast mine to ensure compliance with laws and boost investor confidence.

Mauritania's Minister of Mines, Adi Ould Zein, began a two-day visit to the Tasiast Mauritania mine on Thursday. He was accompanied by the governor of Inchiri region, Moulay Idris Ould Sass Ould Ajeik. The visit aims to inspect mine operations, strengthen government-investor trust, and stress strict adherence to the law.

The minister and his delegation received presentations on Kinross Tasiast's history, development, social programs, and safety and health measures for workers. They toured recreational areas, water tanks, administrative offices, housing, civil protection centers, and the company clinic.

Inchiri region chief, Sheikh Ma'alainin Obeidi El Gharabi, highlighted the company's role in creating jobs for Mauritanians and urged local development programs. The mayor of Bennichab municipality, Mohamed Abdallahi Ould Mohamedou, noted that company interventions, including building a market and slaughterhouse in Bennichab and classrooms in Akjoujt, are still below expectations. Tasiast's vice president, Bab Ould Salihy, stated that Mauritanians constitute 98% of the company's workforce and that the company is committed to continuing social programs for sustainable development.

Why it mattersThe minister's visit to the Tasiast mine affects Mauritania's economy and employment.
