Mauritania reaffirms commitment to combat desertification at UN conference

Mauritania's delegation, led by the Secretary General of the Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development, participated in the 17th session of the Conference of Parties.

Mauritania reaffirmed its commitment to implementing the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification yesterday, Monday, during its participation in the 17th session of the Conference of Parties (COP17) in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. The delegation, headed by Moulay Ibrahim Moulay Idris, Secretary General of the Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development, included ministry officials and experts, alongside representatives from anti-desertification and land restoration bodies.

The Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development stated in a communiqué that its participation aimed to showcase national efforts in combating desertification, restoring degraded lands, protecting ecosystems, and promoting sustainable natural resource management. This engagement highlights Mauritania's proactive stance on environmental challenges.

The conference provided an opportunity for Mauritania to exchange experiences and discover innovative practices and solutions in these areas. It also served to strengthen international cooperation and partnerships, aligning with national priorities for environmental protection and sustainable development.

Why it mattersThe Mauritanian population is affected by the government's commitment to combat desertification and restore degraded lands, which directly impacts their environment and livelihoods.

Nouakchott Hospitals Maintain Services Amidst Power Crisis

The two main hospitals in Nouakchott, the National Hospital Center and Cheikh Zayed Hospital, confirmed uninterrupted medical services despite recent electricity outages in the.

The National Hospital Center and Cheikh Zayed Hospital in Nouakchott reported that their medical services continued without interruption during the recent power crisis affecting the capital. Both institutions activated backup energy sources to ensure the continuous operation of medical services and equipment, following two fires at transformer stations that caused significant outages across several districts.

The National Hospital Center stated it has seven generators, including one main 950 kVA unit and seven 250 kVA units, which have been running 24 hours a day under specialized technical supervision. An emergency committee was established to secure fuel and logistical supplies, and staffing was increased in critical areas like resuscitation, emergencies, and dialysis. No deaths were recorded, and surgical, resuscitation, and dialysis services proceeded as usual.

Cheikh Zayed Hospital also confirmed that it implemented measures to maintain power supply across all departments, ensuring that inpatient care, emergencies, consultations, and surgeries were not disrupted. Technical and administrative teams continuously monitored the situation, with necessary energy sources and equipment readily available to support ongoing operations.

Why it mattersResidents of Nouakchott are directly affected as the continued operation of critical medical services ensures their access to healthcare during a city-wide power crisis.

Mauritania schedules first diaspora forum for late October

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, African Cooperation, and Mauritanians Abroad announced the inaugural forum will take place in Nouakchott from October twenty-ninth to.

The Mauritanian government will host its first Forum of Mauritanian Communities Abroad in Nouakchott from October twenty-ninth to thirty-first, 2026. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, African Cooperation, and Mauritanians Abroad stated that the event will be held under the patronage of President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani.

This forum follows extensive consultations, including direct meetings with Mauritanian communities in twenty-two countries across four continents. These discussions involved representatives from associations, federations, scientific and professional experts, businessmen, and individuals interested in issues concerning Mauritanians abroad. The ministry also organized a reflection day on June thirtieth to debate the results with government sectors, parliamentarians, and community organization representatives.

The forum aims to create a national platform for dialogue between public authorities and Mauritanian communities worldwide. Its agenda includes developing policies and institutional frameworks for community affairs, strengthening social and legal protection for Mauritanians abroad, and leveraging their skills and expertise for knowledge transfer and development support.

Why it mattersThe forum directly affects Mauritanians living abroad by providing a platform for their concerns and aspirations to be discussed with government officials.

Foreign Minister Decorates WHO Representative in Nouakchott

Mauritania's Minister of Foreign Affairs, Mohamed Salem Ould Merzoug, awarded the Order of Officer of National Merit to Professor Ngai Fatou Charlotte, WHO Representative, as her.

Mohamed Salem Ould Merzoug, the Minister of Foreign Affairs, African Cooperation, and Mauritanians Abroad, on behalf of President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani, decorated Professor Ngai Fatou Charlotte, the World Health Organization's representative in Mauritania, with the Order of Officer of National Merit. Ambassador Mohamed El Amine El Beidh, Director of the Arab World at the Ministry's General Directorate of Bilateral Cooperation, attended the meeting, underscoring the diplomatic significance of the event.

SOMELEC restores power to parts of Sebkha and Tevrag Zeina

The Mauritanian Electricity Company (SOMELEC) announced the progressive return of electricity to several neighborhoods in Sebkha and Tevrag Zeina after technical interventions.

Electricity has been restored in parts of Sebkha and is gradually returning to large areas of Tevrag Zeina, according to SOMELEC. This follows successful technical interventions to reactivate the northern substation, which operates at 33/15 kilovolts. Service was also re-established in Sebkha zones affected by technical failures on supply lines connected to the western substation, including Al-Koufa, Al-Basra, and Neteg.

The progressive restoration in Tevrag Zeina includes areas around the national cardiology center, neighborhoods along the Nouadhibou road, the Saudi ambassador's residence area, the university district, Diar Tata, and the Exterior district towards the statistics administration. SOMELEC stated that its technical teams are continuing to restore service in other affected neighborhoods gradually and securely, based on available load.

Work is ongoing at the eastern substation, north of Nouakchott, to bring it to full capacity and extend electricity restoration to remaining affected areas. These efforts follow two fires that damaged two substations in Nouakchott within two days, causing significant power outages across the capital.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in Sebkha and Tevrag Zeina are directly affected by the return of electricity, improving daily life and economic activity after recent outages.
