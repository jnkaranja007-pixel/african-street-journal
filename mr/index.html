African Union Calls for Peace Mechanism Reform Amid Complex Security Challenges

The African Union Commission president urged accelerated reforms to address escalating terrorism, violent extremism, and interstate tensions across the continent.

African Union Commission President Mahmoud Ali Youssef called for an urgent reform of the continent's peace and security mechanisms, citing increasingly complex security and political challenges. Speaking at an extraordinary session in Luanda, Angola, he highlighted rising terrorism, violent extremism, internal crises, and external shocks as threats requiring a re-evaluation of current strategies and resources. Youssef questioned the adequacy of existing frameworks to confront these evolving threats, emphasizing the need for Africa to adapt.

The commission president stressed that current mechanisms are insufficient to handle new challenges, necessitating swift reforms. He advocated for strengthened early warning and prevention efforts, improved coordination among African institutions, and a comprehensive approach to combating terrorism and violent extremism. While maintaining a firm stance against unconstitutional changes of government, Youssef also underscored the importance of dialogue and urged increased African funding for peacekeeping operations to achieve the goal of silencing guns by 2030.

The meeting, focused on enhancing conflict prevention and resolution, also heard from Burundi's Foreign Minister Édouard Bizimana, who acknowledged progress in collective security mechanisms but warned of a "worrying" escalation of crises. These include political instability, cross-border crime, climate-related insecurity, and emerging technological threats, all demanding a more robust and adaptable African response to ensure lasting peace and stability.

Why it mattersAfrican nations and their leaders are affected by the growing complexity of security threats and the need to reform existing peace and security mechanisms to effectively address them.

Mauritanian Scholar Appointed OIC Secretary General's Chief of Staff

Dr. Ahmed Saïd Ould Bah will lead the cabinet for the newly elected Secretary General of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation.

Ismaïl Ould Cheikh Ahmed, the newly elected Mauritanian Secretary General of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC), has appointed Dr. Ahmed Saïd Ould Bah as his chief of staff. This appointment is part of the team-building process following Ould Cheikh Ahmed's election to lead the international organization. Dr. Ould Bah, a Mauritanian academic, holds a doctorate and previously headed the English department at the University of Nouakchott. He also possesses a master's degree in social sciences from Paris.

Dr. Ould Bah brings extensive experience from his prior roles within the Islamic Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (ISESCO). He began his career there in the mid-1990s, serving in various capacities including director of the Director-General's cabinet and director of external relations and cooperation. More recently, he acted as an advisor to the Director-General on international partnerships and cooperation. Currently, he presides over the Modern University of Chinguetti, succeeding his father, Dr. Mohamed Mokhtar Ould Bah.

The new Secretary General, Ismaïl Ould Cheikh Ahmed, succeeds Houssein Ibrahim Taha of Chad. Dr. Ould Bah's appointment to lead the cabinet signifies a key role in the OIC's upcoming operations under its new leadership. His academic background and international organizational experience are expected to inform his contributions to the OIC's strategic direction.

Why it mattersMauritanian professionals are appointed to lead key international organizations, impacting national representation and diplomatic influence.

Morocco, Mauritania Electricity Project Studies to Finish by December 2026

Ministers from both nations discussed the project and recent power outages in Nouakchott during a phone call.

Studies for the planned electricity interconnection between Morocco and Mauritania are on schedule and expected to conclude before December 2026, according to the Moroccan Ministry of Energy. This project is a key element of energy cooperation between the two countries. The announcement followed a phone conversation between Mauritanian Minister of Energy and Petroleum Mohamed Ould Khaled and Moroccan Minister of Energy Transition and Sustainable Development Leïla Benali.

The ministers also addressed the recent electrical crisis in Nouakchott, which saw two transformer stations damaged by fires earlier this month. These incidents caused widespread and prolonged power cuts in the capital. The Mauritanian Electricity Company has since reported a gradual restoration of services and the implementation of temporary solutions to manage the affected load.

Morocco, through its National Office of Electricity and Potable Water, provided support to Mauritania following the fires. The interconnection project aims to enhance energy cooperation as Mauritania faces challenges in meeting its electricity demand and ensuring supply stability.

Why it mattersThe project will affect investors by advancing a significant energy infrastructure link between two countries, potentially improving regional energy stability and trade.

Rainfall Covers Six Mauritanian States

The Ministry of Interior reported significant rainfall across scattered areas of the country over the past twenty-four hours.

Significant rainfall has fallen on scattered areas of Mauritania over the last twenty-four hours, affecting six states. The Ministry of Interior, Decentralization and Local Development's Administrative Communications Department announced the precipitation event.

The ministry's announcement detailed the affected regions, though specific quantities or locations within the states were not provided. The report covers a twenty-four hour period ending on the date of the announcement.

This widespread rainfall event comes as a notable weather development across the nation. The ministry's statement provided a general overview of the areas that received precipitation without further specifics on the intensity or duration.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by this widespread rainfall event as it directly impacts agricultural conditions and water availability across six states.

Islamic Economics Graduates Demand Judicial Recruitment Access

Graduates excluded from a judicial recruitment competition are citing a Supreme Court ruling to assert their eligibility for the role.

Graduates from the Islamic Economics section of Mauritania's Higher Institute for Islamic Studies and Research have petitioned the Justice Minister and the Supreme Court President to be included in the provisional list for the upcoming judge recruitment competition. They were previously excluded from the process, prompting their formal complaint.

In a letter of grievance, the graduates stated that their section falls under the Sharia faculty and argued that their exclusion contradicts existing legal requirements for candidacy. They referenced a Supreme Court administrative chamber decision from April 13, which addressed a similar appeal by students from the Foundations of Religion section against the rejection of their applications.

The complainants interpret this decision to apply to all Sharia-affiliated fields, including Islamic Economics, as the law does not explicitly restrict specialization. They highlighted that their curriculum covers transaction law and contemporary economic issues, providing relevant legal knowledge for judicial functions. Furthermore, they noted that graduates from this section have previously served as judges, underscoring the lack of basis for their current exclusion.

Why it mattersGraduates of Islamic Economics are affected because they are being denied access to a judicial recruitment competition despite believing their qualifications meet legal standards.
