Mauritanian Diplomat Elected OIC Secretary General

Ismail Ould Cheikh Ahmed's election to lead the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation was welcomed by Arab and African journalists.

The International Network of Arab and African Journalists congratulated Mauritanian diplomat Ismail Ould Cheikh Ahmed on his election as Secretary General of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation. The network expressed confidence that his decades of political and professional experience, both domestically and with the United Nations, will enable him to bring about significant changes in the organization's operations and address key challenges facing joint Islamic action.

The network specifically praised the Mauritanian government's decision to officially nominate Ould Cheikh Ahmed for the position. This move, they stated, enhances the diplomatic presence of Mauritanian talent and underscores international trust in their ability to lead regional and international bodies. The organization highlighted Mauritania's history of producing competent individuals who have held high-level positions in various international and regional organizations.

Ould Cheikh Ahmed's election was reportedly unanimous. His extensive career includes service within his home country and at the United Nations, providing a strong foundation for his new role. The network anticipates his leadership will foster qualitative change within the OIC and help overcome current obstacles.

Why it mattersArab and African journalists are affected by the election of Ismail Ould Cheikh Ahmed as he is seen as capable of bringing qualitative change to the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation.

Somelec Cuts Power on Aleg-Boutilimit Line for Maintenance

The national electricity company will temporarily shut down the 33 kV line Saturday morning to service equipment at the Aleg substation.

The Mauritanian Electricity Company (Somelec) announced a planned power outage on Saturday between 7 a.m. and 10 a.m. The interruption will affect the 33 kV electrical line connecting the towns of Aleg and Boutilimit. This measure is necessary for periodic maintenance work at the Aleg high-voltage substation.

The operation involves de-energizing transformer number 2 at the substation, a 90/33 kV unit with a capacity of 5 MVA, to ensure safety during the maintenance procedures. The outage will impact all settlements and localities situated along the affected power line, as well as the town of Boutilimit itself, which relies on this line for its electricity supply.

Somelec has issued an advance apology to customers in the affected areas for the temporary disruption. The company stated that this maintenance is part of its regular schedule and is essential for the safe operation of its electrical infrastructure. The work is being conducted to meet safety requirements.

Why it mattersBusinesses and residents in Aleg and Boutilimit will experience a temporary loss of electricity, impacting operations and daily life.

Mauritanian Defense Minister Departs for Angola Summit

Minister Hanena Ould Sidi travels to Luanda to represent President Ghazouani at an African Union summit on conflict resolution.

Mauritanian Minister of Defense, Veterans Affairs and Martyrs' Children, Hanena Ould Sidi, left Nouakchott early Friday for Angola. He is set to represent President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani at an extraordinary African Union summit. The summit is scheduled to take place on August 29 and 30, 2026, in the Angolan capital, Luanda.

The summit's agenda includes discussions on strengthening mechanisms for conflict prevention and resolution across the African continent. Mauritania's participation underscores its commitment to contributing to African efforts aimed at consolidating peace, security, and stability. The nation seeks to bolster the AU's capabilities in crisis prevention and peaceful settlement.

This engagement reflects Mauritania's dedication to addressing the security and political challenges facing Africa through peaceful means. The country aims to enhance the African Union's capacity in managing and resolving crises, thereby fostering a more stable continent.

Why it mattersThe Minister of Defense's attendance at the AU summit directly impacts Mauritania's engagement in continental security discussions and its role in conflict resolution efforts.

Mauritanian Party Demands Independent Power Plant Fire Inquiry

The El Messar party is calling for an independent investigation into two recent power plant fires that caused significant electricity outages.

The Mauritanian party El Messar has demanded an independent, transparent, and impartial investigation into two fires that occurred at Nouakchott's power plants. The party stated that the commission should comprise individuals with no past or present ties to the institution or the matters under investigation. They also called for the publication of results, the establishment of responsibility, and the application of legal sanctions.

In a statement released Friday, El Messar highlighted that the consecutive fires over two days caused substantial power disruptions, impacting citizens, families, and economic activities. Specific concerns were raised regarding essential goods like food and medicine, as well as services related to electricity, telecommunications, and the internet. The party expressed solidarity with those affected and acknowledged the president's visit and directives to address the crisis.

El Messar described the events as more than a mere technical incident, suggesting a need to review current crisis management programs and plans, and to develop medium and long-term strategies for essential services. The party noted that the crisis's repercussions, which led to seeking aid from neighboring countries, underscore the necessity for strengthening national capacity to handle crises independently.

Why it mattersThe El Messar party is affected by the power plant fires through disruptions to essential services and economic activities, leading them to demand accountability and improved crisis management.

Mauritania Expands Drug Crime Laws

A new bill broadens criminal offenses to cover production, trafficking, and organized crime linked to narcotics.

Mauritania's government approved a new draft law on Thursday to combat narcotics and psychotropic substances. The legislation expands criminal offenses to include the production, manufacturing, processing, possession, cultivation, and trafficking of these substances. It also targets criminal groups and organizations involved in this activity, aiming to modernize the national system and strengthen prevention, detection, and deterrence measures in line with international standards.

The new law also addresses acts related to precursors and equipment used for illicit production. It includes specific provisions for the creation, management, direction, financing, membership, or support of criminal groups. This legislative push follows recent successes by security services, which have dismantled ten active drug trafficking networks since early July.

These operations resulted in the arrest of at least forty individuals and the seizure of over twenty kilograms of various narcotics. Additionally, more than ten thousand psychotropic tablets and hallucinogenic pills were confiscated. Seized items included industrial cannabis resin known as "black stone," Indian cannabis, high-purity cocaine, synthetic drugs like "kouch," ecstasy, crystal, amphetamines, and psychotropic medications such as pregabalin.

Why it mattersThe new law affects citizens by expanding the scope of criminal offenses related to drug production, trafficking, and organized crime.
