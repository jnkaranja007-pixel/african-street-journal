Excavation Damages Mauritania Power Grid

A private company's excavation work in Arafat severed a key Somelec cable, causing widespread power outages across several neighborhoods.

Several neighborhoods in Arafat are without electricity after a private construction company damaged a vital Somelec power cable during excavation work. The incident occurred in the "Carrefour" area and has disrupted power supply across large sections of the district. Somelec's technical teams are currently working to repair the damage and restore electricity as quickly as possible.

The damage was caused by excavation work carried out by an independent contracting firm on behalf of the National Sanitation Office. This action has led to significant disruptions to the electrical network. Somelec has stated that its teams are actively engaged in repairs to resume service promptly.

The Mauritanian Electricity Company (Somelec) is urging all public works companies to coordinate with them before commencing any urban excavation projects. This call aims to prevent future damage to the electrical grid and mitigate the resulting economic and social losses. The company seeks to avoid similar incidents that impact service delivery.

Why it mattersBusinesses in Arafat are affected by power outages that disrupt operations and cause economic losses.

Mauritania Recruits 1,132 Teachers to Bolster Education System

A nationwide competition has launched to hire 1,132 educators, including 241 middle school professors and 891 teachers, to strengthen school teams across the country.

Mauritania has initiated a recruitment drive for 1,132 new teachers and professors to enhance educational staff and support schools in various regions. The competition aims to bolster pedagogical teams and address staffing needs. The Secretary General of the Ministry of Education, Sadvi Sidi Mohamed, emphasized the importance of adhering to established procedures, responsibility, and discipline throughout the process.

During a visit to several examination centers, the Secretary General observed organizational and technical operations, as well as candidate reception and testing conditions. Officials provided explanations on measures taken to ensure the smooth running of the competition and equal opportunities for all applicants. The initiative is part of broader efforts to reform the educational system.

The Secretary General urged candidates to recognize the significant responsibility of the teaching profession and its crucial role in serving students. He highlighted the profession's contribution to the ongoing reforms within the educational system, stressing the commitment to fair and transparent selection procedures to guarantee equitable chances for all participants.

Why it mattersThe recruitment of 1,132 teachers and professors directly affects the education sector by aiming to improve teaching quality and support for students across Mauritania.

Health Ministry Bans Reassignments Without Prior Approval

Health facilities are prohibited from returning transferred staff to the ministry without explicit authorization, citing operational disruptions.

The Ministry of Health has issued a directive prohibiting health sector facilities from reassigning staff back to the ministry without prior authorization. This ban applies to transfers initiated by either the facility or the employee. The ministry stated that such practices disrupt the smooth operation of health facilities, negatively impact human resource planning, and undermine staff commitment.

The circular emphasizes that this prohibition extends to all entities under the ministry, including regional directorates and public health establishments. It clarifies that unilateral reintegration of staff into the central administration is not permitted. Any request for reintegration must be submitted in writing by administrative hierarchy, clearly stating the reasons, and will only be considered after review and endorsement by the Minister.

Regional directors and directors of public health facilities are tasked with ensuring compliance with this directive and disseminating it to their subordinates. The ministry aims to maintain stability in staff assignments and ensure their dedication to their roles within healthcare institutions.

Why it mattersHealth sector employees are affected by new rules that restrict their ability to be reassigned without explicit ministerial approval, impacting their job stability and career progression.

Migrant Boat Drifts 27 Days, Killing Over 80

A vessel carrying 128 people from Gambia to the Canary Islands was found with 45 survivors and five bodies still aboard.

More than 80 people died after a boat carrying migrants from Gambia to the Canary Islands drifted for approximately 27 days at sea. The platform "Sur les traces des migrants" (SLTM) reported that the vessel, which departed in early August, was found carrying 128 people. Only 45 passengers, including one woman, survived the perilous journey.

A surveillance plane located the boat about 120 kilometers south of Gran Canaria on Wednesday. The rescue ship "Guardamar Urania" then intervened to assist the survivors. Those rescued were transported to the Arguineguín dock, where they received aid from the Red Cross and authorities. Five bodies remained on board as the sea conditions prevented their recovery.

Spanish Civil Guard confirmed markings on the boat hull matched a departure from Gambia in early August. Survivors told rescue teams that some companions had died during the 27-day drift. The SLTM platform stated that 45 passengers survived, 44 men and one woman, with five bodies still on board upon rescue.

Agriculture · 2026-09-04

Minister Reviews Agricultural Projects and Job Creation Program

The Minister of Agriculture and Food Sovereignty assessed progress on the Al-Rakba agricultural project and the national program to create ten thousand jobs.

Minister Ahmedou Ould Mouhamedou Ould Mhaimid reviewed preparations for the Al-Rakba agricultural project in Brakna province and the first phase of a national program aimed at creating ten thousand jobs in agriculture. The meeting focused on the advancement of the job creation initiative, which targets ten thousand direct agricultural positions over twenty-four months across five regions. Fifty percent of these roles are designated for women, with a priority for young people aged eighteen to thirty-five.

The evaluation also considered youth employment experiences from R’Kiz and Bakh Jik in Trarza province as part of the program's first phase review. The Al-Rukba project, located in Dar El Baraka, will cover one thousand five hundred seventy-five hectares, divided into three sections. Its irrigation system will draw water from the Senegal River over a distance of twenty-five kilometers.

The Al-Rukba project's total cost is estimated to exceed six billion Mauritanian Ouguiya. Funding for this agricultural transformation initiative is being jointly provided by the Mauritanian government and the Islamic Development Bank. The program aims to boost employment and agricultural output within the designated regions.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by the development of the Al-Rakba agricultural project and the national program to create ten thousand jobs in the agricultural sector.
