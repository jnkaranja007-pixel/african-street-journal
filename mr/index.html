Global Transparency Finds Faulty Cables in Nouakchott Power Station Fires

An investigation by Global Transparency points to non-compliant cables and a company linked to a parliamentarian as potential causes for recent electrical fires.

An investigation by the organization Global Transparency has identified technical suspicions and a potential conflict of interest in the recent fires at two Nouakchott electrical substations. The organization focused on cables supplied and installed by CTM Bâtiment, a company reportedly owned by a local parliamentarian, examining their conformity to project specifications and the circumstances of their installation. Technical sources indicated that newly installed cables showed problems and were disconnected as a precaution before the fires occurred.

The report suggests that non-compliant cables or installation errors could be contributing factors, citing video evidence reviewed by a Somelec official. This evidence indicated that smoke originated from recently connected new cables. Global Transparency dismissed sabotage and grid overload as primary causes, noting that one of the burned substations was not connected to a line implicated in overload theories.

The organization also questioned CTM Bâtiment's experience, as the tender required prior completion of two medium-voltage substation projects. While CTM Bâtiment listed a solar power project in Zoueirat on its website as a completed EPC project, a SNIM report indicated CTM Bâtiment handled only construction, with BGL Energy managing technical installations.

Why it mattersThe investigation into faulty electrical infrastructure and potential conflicts of interest affects businesses and investors by raising questions about project oversight and the reliability of power supply.

Health Ministry Prepares 28 New Health Centers for Nouakchott

Minister Thiam Tidjani chaired a meeting to finalize preparations for 28 new health facilities in the capital, assessing needs for staff and equipment.

The Mauritanian Ministry of Health is in the final stages of preparing 28 new health centers for service in Nouakchott. These facilities are part of an emergency program aimed at developing the capital city. Minister Thiam Tidjani recently led a meeting with regional health directors and chief physicians from Nouakchott's three wilayas to review the readiness of these centers.

The meeting focused on identifying the specific requirements for each center, including the necessary number of doctors, nurses, and other health personnel. Discussions also covered the installation of equipment and the establishment of a system for distributing human resources to ensure a smooth launch of services. The minister stressed the importance of coordination between central and regional administrations.

The health component of Nouakchott's emergency development program includes 28 health establishments across the capital's nine districts. These involve new constructions, equipment procurement, and rehabilitation of existing facilities. The goal is to ensure these centers are fully operational and ready to serve citizens upon opening.

Why it mattersResidents of Nouakchott will gain access to 28 new health centers, improving local healthcare services.

Opposition Party Denounces Rights Violations, Warns of Consequences

The Union of Progress Forces party criticizes the use of courts to suppress free speech and opposes constitutional changes for a third presidential term.

The opposition party Union of Progress Forces has warned of a "climate of violation of liberties," stating that using the justice system to stifle free expression and deter those who report abuses could severely impact public freedoms. This declaration followed a regular session of the party's executive bureau, where national, regional, and international situations, along with political and organizational matters, were discussed.

The party also condemned calls to amend the constitution to allow a third presidential term. They view this as a threat to stability and an attempt to negatively influence upcoming dialogue. The executive bureau approved plans to hold a party congress after the upcoming Ramadan, with an extraordinary session scheduled for late December to prepare for it.

The Union of Progress Forces' statement highlights concerns over the erosion of democratic space and potential political instability stemming from attempts to extend presidential terms. The party's leadership is preparing for internal organizational changes, including a congress to be held after Ramadan.

Why it mattersThe Union of Progress Forces party is affected by a perceived climate of rights violations and the stifling of free expression, which they believe could harm public liberties.

Mauritania Caps Financial Transfer Tax at 200 Ouguiyas

The tax on financial transfers will not exceed 200 new ouguiyas, regardless of the transfer amount, effective September 1.

Mauritania's General Tax Administration has set a new maximum of 200 new ouguiyas for taxes on financial transfers. This measure, which applies to all banks operating in the country, aims to cap the tax amount irrespective of the sum being transferred. The new regulation is scheduled to take effect no later than September 1.

The tax will be calculated at 0.1% of the transfer's value, with the 200 new ouguiyas ceiling in place. Mokhtar Salem El-Mouna, the Director General of Taxes, met with bank representatives to instruct them to implement the necessary technical and administrative adjustments to their systems to ensure uniform application of the new rules.

Bank representatives have committed to adopting the required measures and beginning the application of the capped tax on financial transfers by the September 1 deadline. The General Tax Administration confirmed this commitment, signaling a shift in how financial transactions will be taxed across the nation.

Why it mattersThis new tax cap affects businesses and individuals by limiting the cost of sending money within Mauritania.

Mauritanian Minister Links Peace to Structural Causes of Conflict

Minister Hanena Ould Sidi stated that addressing poverty, unemployment, and weak governance is essential for lasting peace in Africa.

Mauritanian Defense Minister Hanena Ould Sidi asserted that achieving peace in Africa requires tackling the root causes of conflict, including institutional fragility, poverty, unemployment, poor governance, social inequality, and limited opportunities for youth. Speaking at the African Union summit in Luanda, he emphasized that establishing the rule of law, strengthening justice, and fostering national cohesion are crucial for continental security.

Ould Sidi highlighted Mauritania's "integrated security approach," which posits that sustainable development is impossible without security, and vice versa, especially when institutional, economic, and social fragility prevails. The summit focused on enhancing conflict prevention and resolution mechanisms, developing AU political and legal tools, and supporting mediation efforts.

Discussions also centered on promoting "African solutions to African problems" and mobilizing resources for stability and development. The Mauritanian delegation included Ambassador Mohamed Ould Mekhalla and Ambassador El-Houssein Ould Naji, among other officials.

Why it mattersThe minister's statements on structural causes of conflict and the need for development and security directly affect how African nations approach governance and economic stability.
