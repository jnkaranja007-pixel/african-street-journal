Mauritania refers 24 to terrorism investigation team

Prosecutors in Nouakchott West have accused 24 individuals of crimes including conspiracy against state security and money laundering.

The public prosecutor in Nouakchott West, Abdellahi Ould Indjily, announced on Thursday evening that 24 people have been referred to an investigation team for terrorism-related crimes. The prosecutor stated that the case file was transferred to the public prosecution on November 11, following the completion of preliminary investigation procedures.

The accused face charges including conspiracy against state security, incitement to hatred and discrimination, disclosure of military information, spreading false news, and money laundering. The prosecution has requested arrest warrants for seven of the accused and detention orders for 15 others, with two individuals accused of money laundering to be placed under strict judicial supervision.

The charges stem from alleged involvement in forming an organized gang to threaten internal and external state security, attempting to abolish the constitutional order, and harming state dignity and national unity. The accused also allegedly disclosed military information to harm national defense and morale, spread false news, and engaged in money laundering to conceal illicit financial activities.

Why it matters24 individuals face charges related to terrorism and conspiracy against state security, with legal proceedings including arrest warrants and detention orders underway.

Mauritanian prosecutors seek charges against 28 in 'bloggers from abroad' case

Charges are sought against 28 people, with arrest warrants requested for seven, over alleged criminal organisation, false information and terrorism financing.

The public prosecutor in Nouakchott has asked for charges to be brought against 28 people in the case of the "bloggers from abroad". It also requested arrest warrants for seven of them, provisional detention for 15 others, and reinforced judicial supervision for two people accused of money laundering.

In a statement issued after a press conference, the prosecutor said the investigation had uncovered offences linked to forming a criminal organisation, incitement to hatred and discrimination, and spreading false information. Suspicions also cover terrorism financing, money laundering and threats to national security. The prosecutor said it had completed preliminary investigation procedures and referred the suspects to its services.

More than 15 people held in custody appeared before the prosecutor-general after about six weeks in detention, following arrests by state security services. The arrests began in late September and included journalists, money exchange employees and real estate agents, among them relatives of bloggers living abroad. The government had earlier described the case as a "financial blackmail network" that fabricated and spread false information.

Why it matters28 people face possible charges, and seven of them now face arrest warrants, with 15 more facing provisional detention.

Mauritania's national team arrives in Ouagadougou for Africa Cup of Nations qualifier

The Mourabitounes landed early on Thursday and face Burkina Faso in the third round of qualifying for the 2027 tournament, with a return match in Nouakchott next Tuesday.

Mauritania's national football team, known as the Mourabitounes, arrived in Ouagadougou early on Thursday ahead of its third-round qualifier for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations against Burkina Faso, scheduled for Friday evening.

Burkina Faso's team is coached by Amir Abdo, a former coach of the Mauritanian national side, who will face his old team in the match. The two sides meet again in Nouakchott next Tuesday in the fourth round.

Mauritania opened its qualifying campaign with a win over the Central African Republic, then lost to Benin in the second round.

Why it mattersA win in Ouagadougou would lift Mauritania's chances in its group, while a loss would leave it needing results in the return match in Nouakchott.
