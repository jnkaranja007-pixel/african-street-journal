Mauritania plans new diaspora policy

Foreign Minister Ould Merzoug announced direct consultations with Mauritanians abroad to shape national policy.

Mauritania's government is developing a new national policy for its diaspora, based on direct consultations with communities abroad. Foreign Minister Mohamed Salem Ould Merzoug stated that these discussions, held with representatives in 23 countries, covered consular services, civil status, integration, and social and legal protection.

The consultations identified the economic, scientific, and professional potential of Mauritanians living overseas, which the government aims to leverage for national development. The minister noted that the ongoing three-day forum facilitates exchanges between diaspora talent, investors, and institutions to foster partnerships and projects.

The forum's discussions focus on improving consular services, enhancing social and legal protection, mobilizing skills, promoting investment, and understanding the role of remittances in development. The government expects the forum to produce actionable recommendations for a national policy tailored to the diaspora's needs.

Why it mattersMauritanians living abroad will benefit from improved consular services, social and legal protection, and opportunities for investment and development.

Mauritania ministries sign deal to aid farmers

The environment and agriculture ministries agreed to protect farmlands in Oualata under the 'Mbedra' project.

Mauritania's ministries of Environment and Sustainable Development, and Agriculture and Food Sovereignty signed a cooperation agreement in Nouakchott on Friday. The deal, part of the 'Mbedra' project, aims to support farmers in the Oualata municipality, protect agricultural depressions, and boost local production to improve living conditions.

The agreement was signed by the secretaries-general of both ministries, Moulay Brahim Ould Moulay Idriss for Environment and Ahmed Salem Ould Larbi for Agriculture. The Environment secretary explained the accord supports efforts to manage the Klab Risha protected area, balancing resource protection with local livelihoods. This initiative responds to demands from Oualata residents and farmers following the President's visit on September 21, 2026, especially regarding the protection and use of agricultural depressions after recent rains.

The Agriculture secretary stated the agreement aligns with the President's 'Ambitious for the Nation' program, focusing on improving rural life and food security. Oualata's mayor, Mohamed Elamine Ould Bab, identified three key needs for protecting the agricultural depressions, locally known as 'Lkrayer': 14 kilometers of additional metal fencing, 10 guards for four months, and 10 flexible tanks for drinking water for farm workers. These needs arise as farmers prepare to cultivate the depressions, which are now filled with rainwater, facing challenges like stray animals and the need for crop security and drinking water.

Why it mattersFarmers in Oualata will receive support to protect their agricultural depressions and enhance local production.

European Union gives Mauritania 20 million euros for surveillance

The funds will provide non-military equipment to boost the army's territorial monitoring capabilities.

The European Union has increased its military aid to Mauritania by 20 million euros, bringing the total to 75 million euros. This new funding will provide non-military equipment to enhance the army's territorial surveillance capacity. The EU Council stated this fourth program under the European Peace Facility will supply additional ground surveillance equipment to the Mauritanian armed forces.

This aid aims to support the Mauritanian army's ability to monitor situations across the country. The EU Council said the assistance contributes to regional stability in the Sahel and reduces destabilization risks. It also seeks to protect Mauritania's sovereignty, territorial integrity, and civilian population security.

Previous European aid programs included military equipment and capabilities for land and maritime operations. These focused on surveillance and deterrence.

Why it mattersMauritania's army will receive new equipment to monitor its territory, aiming to improve stability and security for its population.

Mauritania digitizes over 8,000 Nouadhibou port vessels

Fisheries minister Aissata Lam inspected the ongoing registration and modernization of the Bay of Rest port facilities.

More than 8,000 vessels operating in Nouadhibou's Bay of Rest port are being digitized and registered. This operation aims to update the database of ships and improve the organization and tracking of their activities.

Fisheries, Maritime and Port Infrastructure Minister Aissata Lam visited the Bay of Rest port to observe the vessel census and data digitization. She also reviewed the progress of the port's modernization and the rehabilitation of its old market.

The initiative seeks to enhance the management of maritime activities within the port.

Why it mattersOver 8,000 vessels in Nouadhibou's Bay of Rest port are undergoing digitization and registration, impacting maritime operations.
