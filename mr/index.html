Mauritania Approves New Law Expanding Drug Crime Penalties

The government has passed legislation to modernize its anti-narcotics system and combat criminal organizations involved in drug trafficking.

Mauritania's government approved a new draft law on Thursday that broadens criminal offenses related to narcotics and psychotropic substances. The legislation targets the production, manufacture, processing, possession, cultivation, and trafficking of these substances, as well as associated criminal groups. Officials stated the law aims to update the national system and enhance prevention, detection, and deterrence measures in line with international standards.

The new law also addresses acts involving precursors and equipment for illicit production, and includes specific provisions for the creation, management, direction, financing, membership, or support of criminal organizations. This legislative update follows recent successes by security services, which have dismantled ten drug trafficking networks since early July, arresting at least forty individuals and seizing over twenty kilograms of various narcotics.

Seizures included industrial cannabis resin known as "black stone," Indian cannabis, high-purity cocaine, synthetic drugs like "kouch," ecstasy, crystal, amphetamines, and psychotropic medications such as pregabalin. In a significant operation on August 3rd in Tiris Zemour, authorities dismantled a drug trafficking network, arresting seven people and confiscating 7.5 kg of "black stone" and 1.349 kg of cocaine, alongside three thousand two hundred psychotropic tablets.

Why it mattersThe new law affects citizens by expanding criminal penalties for drug-related activities and targeting organized crime, potentially leading to increased enforcement and prosecution.

Somelec Cuts Power on Aleg-Boutilimit Line for Maintenance

The national electricity company will temporarily shut down the 33 kV line Saturday morning to service equipment at the Aleg substation.

The Mauritanian Electricity Company (Somelec) announced a planned power outage on Saturday between 7 a.m. and 10 a.m. The interruption will affect the 33 kV electrical line connecting the towns of Aleg and Boutilimit. This measure is necessary for periodic maintenance work at the Aleg high-voltage substation.

The operation involves de-energizing transformer number 2 at the substation, a 90/33 kV unit with a capacity of 5 MVA, to ensure safety during the maintenance procedures. The outage will impact all settlements and localities situated along the affected power line, as well as the town of Boutilimit itself, which relies on this line for its electricity supply.

Somelec has issued an advance apology to customers in the affected areas for the temporary disruption. The company stated that this maintenance is part of its regular schedule and is essential for the safe operation of its electrical infrastructure. The work is being conducted to meet safety requirements.

Why it mattersBusinesses and residents in Aleg and Boutilimit will experience a temporary loss of electricity, impacting operations and daily life.

Rain gauges recorded precipitation across various parts of the country over the past 24 hours, according to the Ministry of Interior.

The ministry's report detailed the specific locations where precipitation was observed. No further details regarding the intensity or duration of the rainfall were provided in the initial announcement.

Mauritanian Party Demands Independent Power Plant Fire Inquiry

The El Messar party is calling for an independent investigation into two recent power plant fires that caused significant electricity outages.

The Mauritanian party El Messar has demanded an independent, transparent, and impartial investigation into two fires that occurred at Nouakchott's power plants. The party stated that the commission should comprise individuals with no past or present ties to the institution or the matters under investigation. They also called for the publication of results, the establishment of responsibility, and the application of legal sanctions.

In a statement released Friday, El Messar highlighted that the consecutive fires over two days caused substantial power disruptions, impacting citizens, families, and economic activities. Specific concerns were raised regarding essential goods like food and medicine, as well as services related to electricity, telecommunications, and the internet. The party expressed solidarity with those affected and acknowledged the president's visit and directives to address the crisis.

El Messar described the events as more than a mere technical incident, suggesting a need to review current crisis management programs and plans, and to develop medium and long-term strategies for essential services. The party noted that the crisis's repercussions, which led to seeking aid from neighboring countries, underscore the necessity for strengthening national capacity to handle crises independently.

Why it mattersThe El Messar party is affected by the power plant fires through disruptions to essential services and economic activities, leading them to demand accountability and improved crisis management.

Foreign Minister Returns to Nouakchott After OIC Meeting

Minister of Foreign Affairs Mohamed Salem Ould Merzouk arrived back in Nouakchott after attending an emergency OIC meeting in Jeddah.

Minister of Foreign Affairs, Cooperation, and Mauritanians Abroad, Mohamed Salem Ould Merzouk, returned to Nouakchott on Friday evening. He arrived from Saudi Arabia following his participation in the emergency conference of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) foreign ministers.

The OIC meeting was held in Jeddah on the twenty-seventh of this month. The minister’s return signifies the conclusion of his official engagements abroad related to this specific conference.

His presence in Jeddah indicates Mauritania's engagement with regional diplomatic efforts concerning the issues discussed at the OIC gathering. The specific outcomes of the conference were not detailed in the provided information.

Why it mattersThe Minister of Foreign Affairs' participation in an international conference affects Mauritania's diplomatic standing and foreign policy decisions.
