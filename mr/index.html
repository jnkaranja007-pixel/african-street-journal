Climate · 2026-10-30

Mauritania seeks biodiversity funding in Armenia

Environment Minister Massouda Baham Mohamed Lagdaf met UN officials to discuss financing for conservation projects.

Mauritania presented its biodiversity protection efforts at the 17th Conference of the Parties to the Convention on Biological Diversity in Yerevan, Armenia. Minister of Environment and Sustainable Development Massouda Baham Mohamed Lagdaf highlighted national work on restoring degraded lands and preserving ecosystems.

During her visit, Minister Lagdaf discussed cooperation with Astrid Kozian, head of the UN Capital Development Fund mission. They explored funding for biodiversity projects, coastal ecosystem protection, and support for local communities' livelihoods.

The discussions also covered developing innovative financing tools, including bankable investment projects and risk-sharing. This aims to mobilize public and private resources for biodiversity preservation initiatives.

Why it mattersMauritania is seeking international partnerships and innovative financing to support its biodiversity protection and land restoration efforts.

Somelec cuts power in Nouakchott for maintenance

Temporary electricity outages will affect five Nouakchott districts between October 30 and November 1, 2026.

The Mauritanian Electricity Company (Somelec) announced temporary power cuts in five Nouakchott districts from Friday, October 30 to Sunday, November 1, 2026. These maintenance works on electrical installations will last up to four hours daily in affected areas. The cuts are scheduled for Tevrag Zeina and Ksar on October 30, Teyarett and Dar al-Naïm on October 31, and Carrefour (Arafat) on November 1.

Somelec stated the work will occur at the main 33/15 kV central substation between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. The company apologized to customers for the inconvenience, explaining the outages are necessary for network maintenance and operational safety. These repairs aim to ensure the continued reliability of the electricity supply.

The maintenance operations are part of periodic upkeep for the electrical infrastructure. Somelec aims to guarantee the safe functioning of its network through these scheduled interventions. Customers are advised to prepare for potential disruptions during the specified dates.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in five districts of Nouakchott will experience temporary electricity outages for up to four hours daily.

Mauritania finds new study options for excluded medical students

The ministry is offering alternative countries for Mauritanian students denied specialization spots in Algeria.

Mauritania's Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research has found alternative study options for students who could not enroll in medical specialization programs in Algeria. The ministry will allow these students to study in other countries after limited capacity in Algeria prevented their admission. This action follows complaints from Mauritanian doctors excluded from scholarship lists for Algeria, despite completing all application steps.

Sidi Mohamed Ahmed, director of information systems at the ministry, told Sahara 24 that Algerian authorities could not register the students due to insufficient places. He stated the ministry proposed alternatives for students to complete their studies elsewhere. Some doctors, including Egyptian university graduates, were surprised not to find their names on final scholarship lists after submitting all required documents; 12 doctors were initially listed on the electronic platform but later removed.

The excluded doctors have filed appeals with the scholarship allocation department and contacted relevant ministry services without receiving a response. The director did not specify which countries were offered or the total number of students affected by these alternative solutions. It remains unclear if these arrangements cover all doctors who contested their exclusion from Algerian specialization scholarships.

Why it mattersMauritanian medical students are affected by being unable to secure specialization spots in Algeria and are now being offered alternative study countries.

Agriculture · 2026-10-30

Mauritania launches local development program consultations

Minister of Agriculture reviews phase two of an emergency local development plan in the Brakna region.

Minister of Agriculture and Food Sovereignty, Ahmed Babah Ould Aali, launched consultations for the second phase of an emergency program aimed at universal access to basic services for local development in the Brakna region. The consultations, held in Aleg, will identify development priorities based on citizen needs through a participatory approach. This initiative follows directives from President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani to assess the first phase and address ongoing projects.

The minister reviewed the progress of the emergency program in Brakna, which includes 309 interventions across vital sectors. Water and sanitation projects show 100% completion, while electricity stands at 96%, education at 78.3%, and rural access at 72.5%. Livestock development has reached 67% completion, agriculture 57.5%, and youth and sports 42%. The next phase will focus on accelerating project completion and resolving identified issues.

The program's participatory approach, adopted in 2025, involved direct consultation with administrative authorities, elected officials, and local stakeholders to set development priorities. The minister emphasized the citizen's central role in the development process and urged local officials to ensure all districts and municipalities benefit from the planned interventions. Future efforts will concentrate on speeding up project execution and rectifying shortcomings.

Why it mattersThe emergency local development program in Mauritania's Brakna region includes interventions in agriculture and livestock, directly impacting rural livelihoods.

Health · 2026-10-30

Mauritania health ministry prepares new clinics

Health Minister Naha Hamdi Mouknass reviewed preparations for opening new health centers in Nouakchott.

Mauritania's Ministry of Health is preparing to open new health centers in Nouakchott, focusing on supply needs and team readiness. Minister Naha Hamdi Mouknass led a meeting with ministry officials, regional health directors, and representatives from the medical supply agency and digital transformation sector. The minister directed the medical supply agency to ensure all new centers are stocked, with a review of the supply system planned three months after launch.

A daily monitoring mechanism has been established, assigning a ministry official to each center to track progress and report to a supervision committee. This system aims to address any shortcomings before the centers become operational. Human resources are also being addressed, with training for new center staff set to begin on Thursday, combining theoretical and practical instruction to prepare them for their roles.

The training will equip staff with the skills needed for the new facilities. The supply system will be evaluated for effectiveness and necessary improvements. Daily reports will help identify and fix issues before the centers open.

Why it mattersNew health centers are being prepared for opening in Nouakchott, which will affect the local population's access to healthcare.
