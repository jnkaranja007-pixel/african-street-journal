Mauritanian Prime minister vows judicial reform

Mokhtar Ould Diay also pledged to fight corruption and boost private sector investment.

Mauritanian Prime Minister Mokhtar Ould Diay pledged to reform the judicial system, intensify the fight against corruption, and develop public administration. He also aims to implement economic reforms to stimulate investment and expand the private sector's role in wealth and job creation. Ould Diay announced these commitments on Saturday while presenting the government's general policy to the National Assembly.

The Prime Minister stated that his government's action plan aligns with President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani's program, "My Ambitions for the Homeland." The plan focuses on five key areas, including building an institutional state, strengthening the economy, and developing human capital. Ould Diay emphasized the government's direction towards developing the democratic system, reforming the judiciary, and enhancing human rights and freedom of expression.

Economically, the government intends to undertake structural reforms to liberalize the economy and encourage investment, supporting the private sector's growth contribution. Plans also include developing infrastructure programs and mobilizing necessary funding. The government will also focus on developing sectors with economic potential, considering environmental factors in projects. Human capital development, prioritizing youth qualification in education, training, employment, health, culture, and sports, is considered essential for economic and social policy success.

Why it mattersMauritanian citizens will experience reforms in the justice system, increased efforts against corruption, and potential economic growth through private sector investment.

Nouakchott hosts Maghreb radiology congress

Doctors and experts from eight countries gather for three days of medical imaging discussions.

The 21st Maghreb Congress of Radiology and Medical Imaging began on Friday in Nouakchott, Mauritania. Specialists and experts from Mauritania, Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia, Senegal, Mali, Ivory Coast, and France are attending the three-day event. The conference aims to share experiences and discuss scientific advancements in radiology and medical imaging.

Professor Mohamed Beddi, president of the Mauritanian Association of Radiology and Medical Imaging, stated that Mauritania's hosting of the congress aligns with its traditions of organizing scientific meetings. He highlighted that the gathering facilitates the exchange of knowledge among professionals from the Maghreb, West Africa, and France, strengthening professional cooperation. Beddi emphasized that radiology and medical imaging are essential for diagnosis and patient treatment, requiring continuous training and awareness of technical progress.

The congress program includes discussions on gynecological diseases, digestive system imaging, emergencies, artificial intelligence, and interventional radiology. These topics were chosen to address the evolving demands in the field. The association president thanked the country's highest authorities and public and private partners for their support and contributions to the event's success.

Why it mattersMedical professionals and patients in Mauritania and surrounding regions benefit from the sharing of knowledge and advancements in diagnostic imaging techniques.

Ghazouani launches Nouakchott development phase

Mauritania's president announced intensive efforts to improve basic services and infrastructure in the capital.

President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani announced on Thursday evening that Nouakchott will receive intensive efforts to improve basic services and infrastructure. These improvements will focus on water supply, healthcare access, and road networks, alongside school facilities.

During a meeting with officials in Nouakchott-Sud, Ghazouani stated that efforts aim to reduce suffering from thirst and extend healthcare services closer to citizens. The road network will be improved to serve areas outside the city, and hospitals will be reinforced with specialized medical staff.

Ghazouani launched the second phase of Nouakchott's emergency development program, costing 115 billion old ouguiyas, bringing the total program cost to 184 billion old ouguiyas. This phase includes building health and school facilities, expanding utility networks, and rehabilitating public spaces.

Mauritanian military delegation visits Moroccan naval facilities

The visit to Casablanca aimed to strengthen military cooperation and exchange expertise in training.

A Mauritanian military delegation visited Moroccan Royal Navy facilities in Casablanca from Monday to Thursday. The delegation, led by Colonel Mohand Baba Hamdi, commander of the Naval Academy, toured the Royal Naval School and the Royal Navy Training Center. This exchange is part of the implementation of the mixed military commission program between Mauritania and Morocco.

The Mauritanian army announced on Friday that the visit focused on enhancing military cooperation and sharing expertise in skills development. The program included presentations and site tours of several naval installations and specialized centers. Discussions and experience-sharing sessions were held between the two naval forces.

The reciprocal visit also saw a delegation from the Moroccan Royal Navy visit the Mauritanian Navy's staff headquarters during the same period. This initiative seeks to foster mutual learning and improve naval training capabilities.

Mauritania minister discusses infrastructure with World Bank

Mouhamedou Ahmedou Mhaimid and a World Bank delegation reviewed transport and basic services projects.

Mauritania's Minister of Economic Affairs, Mouhamedou Ahmedou Mhaimid, met with a World Bank delegation on Friday to discuss cooperation and strengthen their partnership, focusing on infrastructure development.

The discussions covered the progress of World Bank-funded projects in Mauritania, ways to speed up their completion, and improve their effectiveness. Priorities for infrastructure, including transport and basic services in various regions, were reviewed.

The World Bank delegation included regional director Frans Dries Gross and resident representative Ibou Diouf.

Why it mattersMauritania's economic development and infrastructure projects are being discussed with the World Bank.
