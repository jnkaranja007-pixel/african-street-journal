Artisanal gold miners report cross-border shelling near Sahara sand wall

Artisanal gold miners in a buffer zone near the Sahara sand wall reported hearing artillery and rocket fire from both sides of the border on Sunday night.

Artisanal gold miners in the Sahara's buffer zone reported that heavy artillery and rocket fire erupted on both sides of the sand wall during the night of Saturday to Sunday. The miners stated that the Polisario Front targeted areas near the sand wall, while the Moroccan army responded by shelling areas outside the wall. One miner present in Akleibat al-Fouleh said about 15 shells fell west of the prospecting sites without causing casualties.

The Moroccan army and the Polisario Front have not yet commented on the incident, and Sahara Media could not independently verify the information. Akleibat al-Fouleh, an artisanal mining area frequented by Mauritanian gold prospectors operating illegally, is located a few kilometers from the Mauritanian border. Mauritanian authorities have repeatedly warned prospectors against prospecting outside national borders, especially in this dangerous zone.

This development follows the end of the United Nations Mission for the Sahara (MINURSO) activities in early November after 35 years. Morocco welcomed the non-extension of the mission's mandate, viewing it as progress towards resolving the Sahara issue. Mauritania has reaffirmed its position of "total positive neutrality" regarding the conflict and has asked citizens in areas near the northern border to exercise caution and stay away from the border line or disputed areas.

Why it mattersArtisanal gold miners in areas near the northern border were warned by Mauritanian authorities to exercise caution and stay away from disputed zones.

Culture · 2026-11-17

Nouakchott hosts second Saudi Quran and Sunna memorisation contest for West Africa

The contest brings 160 participants from 16 West African countries to compete in two sections, one on memorising the Quran and one on the Sunna.

The second edition of a contest on memorising the Quran and the Sunna, the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad, opened on Sunday morning in Nouakchott. The competition is named after King Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud, Guardian of the Two Holy Mosques, and is organised by Saudi Arabia's ministry of Islamic affairs, Da'wa and Guidance.

Some 160 participants from 16 West African countries are taking part, divided into two main sections, one on the Quran and one on the Sunna. Each section has five disciplines, ranging from memorisation and recitation (tajwid) to exegesis (tafsir) and understanding of hadiths. Mauritania's minister of Islamic affairs and original education, Sid El-Haj Ould Dah, said the event reflected cooperation between the two countries' ministries and recognition of Mauritania's scholars and madrasas.

Saudi Ambassador to Mauritania Abdelaziz bin Abdullah Al-Raqabi said that holding the contest in Mauritania for a second time reflected the close fraternal and historical ties between the two countries.

Why it mattersParticipants from 16 West African countries are competing in Nouakchott, so families and students in those countries are directly involved in the contest.
