Mauritanian gendarmerie dismantles land fraud network

Several suspects arrested for illegally acquiring and selling land using forged documents.

Mauritanian gendarmes announced on Tuesday they dismantled a criminal network that illegally acquired land parcels and sold them to individuals using forged property documents.

The operation, conducted by the gendarmerie unit responsible for housing and urban planning, resulted in the arrest of several suspects who have been transferred to judicial authorities.

This action is part of national efforts to combat crime, protect citizens' property, and fight fraud in the real estate sector.

Why it mattersCitizens in Nouakchott are affected by the illegal acquisition and sale of land using forged documents.

BII and IFC sign $10 million guarantee to support Mauritanian trade

The guarantee agreement, signed under IFC's Global Trade Finance Program, will help BII back its corporate clients in import and export deals with foreign banks.

The Banque Internationale d’Investissement (BII) and the International Finance Corporation (IFC), a member of the World Bank Group, have signed a $10 million guarantee agreement. The deal is meant to make it easier for Mauritanian companies to trade with partners abroad.

The agreement was concluded under IFC's Global Trade Finance Program. It will let BII better support its corporate clients in import and export operations through a guarantee mechanism that eases their dealings with international banks, while securing commercial operations.

BII said the partnership with IFC reaffirms its commitment to Mauritania's economic actors and its aim of supporting the long-term growth of international trade in the country.

Why it mattersMauritanian companies importing and exporting will be able to trade through a guarantee mechanism that eases their dealings with international banks.

Mauritanian Quranic school chief jailed over student's death

Abou Bakr Ould Sidi Ahmed Babah received a two-year sentence, with one year suspended, for negligence.

The head of the Al-Mabrouk Quranic school, Abou Bakr Ould Sidi Ahmed Babah, known as ‘Bekrin’, was sentenced to two years in prison, one year suspended, by the Rosso court. He was found guilty of negligence leading to the death of a student in February 2026.

The verdict followed the death of young Sidi Mohamed Ould Mahfoud at the Mahadra in Al-Mouyessar, Boutilimit. An investigation into the circumstances of the child's death led to the arrest of the school chief and another official, with the case then proceeding to trial.

The investigating judge had ordered the detention of the school chief, while others involved were placed under judicial supervision as the inquiry continued into the student's death.

Why it mattersThe sentencing of the Mahadra chief affects students and their families who rely on these religious schools for education.

Business · 2026-11-12

Mauritanian entrepreneur joins UAE royal advisor role

Yacoub Sidiya, born in Nouakchott in 1978, will advise on international affairs and investments.

Mauritanian entrepreneur Yacoub Sidiya has been appointed special advisor to Prince Abdulaziz bin Humaid Al Nuaimi for international affairs and investments. Sidiya, born in Nouakchott in 1978, has built a career across private security, precious metals, aviation, and health sectors.

Sidiya studied business administration at Northern Kentucky University in the United States before entering the telecommunications sector. He founded MSS Security in 2005, which expanded across West Africa, and later established Phoenix Precious Metals in 2015 to deal in gold.

In 2015, he also launched Global Aviation for humanitarian flights and strategic air logistics. In 2024, Sidiya added MSS Clinic in Nouakchott to his portfolio, focusing on healthcare services.

Why it mattersThe appointment places a Mauritanian entrepreneur in a role influencing international economic relations and investment opportunities.

Mauritania president meets Congolese counterpart

Mohamed Ould Cheikh Ghazouani met Denis Sassou Nguesso at the Antalya Leaders Summit.

Mauritanian President Mohamed Ould Cheikh Ghazouani met with Congolese President Denis Sassou Nguesso on Wednesday. The meeting took place on the sidelines of the Antalya Leaders Summit.

The summit is being held as part of the proceedings for the 31st Conference of the Parties (COP31). The two leaders held discussions during the event.

The COP31 conference is a significant international gathering focused on climate issues. The meeting between the two presidents occurred within this context.

Why it mattersThe presidents of Mauritania and the Republic of the Congo met at the COP31 summit.
