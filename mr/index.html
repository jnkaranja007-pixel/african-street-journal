France lends Mauritania 21 million euros for training

The loan will support vocational training and bolster the civil protection agency's capabilities.

France has provided Mauritania with a 21 million euro loan, approximately 944 million Mauritanian Ouguiyas, to boost vocational training and strengthen the civil protection force. The funds will support a project to enhance the resilience and capabilities of the civil protection agency, aiming to improve public safety against various risks. An additional 4 million euro agreement will finance a project supporting the industrial technical and vocational training school in Nouakchott.

The financing agreements were signed in Nouakchott by Mauritania's Minister of Economic Affairs and Development, Mouhamedou Ahmedou Mhaimid, alongside the French Ambassador to Mauritania, Emmanuel Besnier, and the director of the French Development Agency. Minister Mhaimid stated that these agreements represent progress in cooperation between Mauritania and France in civil protection and technical and vocational education.

The French Development Agency's support extends to national development programs in education, vocational training, decentralization, food security, health, and debt relief. The minister highlighted the strong cooperation and support from the agency for these sectors.

Why it mattersMauritanian citizens will benefit from improved civil protection services and enhanced vocational training opportunities.

Mauritania closes 19 bakeries for bread price gouging

The Ministry of Commerce and Tourism shut down bakeries across Nouakchott for speculation and unjustified price hikes.

Mauritania's Ministry of Commerce and Tourism has closed 19 bakeries in Nouakchott for illegal practices related to bread. The closures followed inspections that found bakeries engaged in monopoly, speculation, and unjustified price increases.

Teams from the ministry inspected bakeries across the capital, leading to the shutdown of 12 in Nouakchott nord, 4 in Nouakchott sud, and 3 in Nouakchott ouest. These operations were conducted with the Ministry of Mines and Industry and the Federation of Bakeries, which has pledged to adhere to set prices.

The ministry stated that inspections will continue around the clock to regulate prices and combat fraud, speculation, and monopolies. These controls aim to ensure fair pricing for bread across the capital's three districts.

Why it matters19 bakeries in Nouakchott were closed by the Ministry of Commerce for speculating on bread prices and increasing them without justification.

Mauritania minister warns Nouakchott cleaning firm

Minister Mokhtar Ould Ahmed Bouceif says ARMA's services are below expectations and must improve by December.

Nouakchott's Minister of Interior, Mokhtar Ould Ahmed Bouceif, stated on November 2, 2026, that the cleaning company ARMA is not meeting expectations for the city's cleanliness. He noted persistent waste in many areas, indicating contract failures despite government and partner cleanup campaigns.

The minister explained that the ministry must ensure the capital's cleanliness, either by improving ARMA's performance or finding alternative solutions. He gave ARMA until the end of December to fix issues and ordered preparations for alternative providers to avoid service delays.

Measures include creating a plan for accumulated waste, using all available dumpsites, and assessing the Tifirit landfill. Logistics and equipment from ARMA will be available to Nouakchott's three governors starting November 2 to boost coordination. An evaluation meeting is set for November 8, 2026.

Why it mattersResidents of Nouakchott are directly affected by the poor waste management and the potential for alternative solutions.

Politics · 2026-11-03

Mauritania parliament approves new national documents law

The new legislation replaces a 1968 text and aims to unify collection, preservation, and access to national records.

Mauritania's National Assembly approved a new law governing national documents on Monday. The legislation, presented by the Minister of the General Secretariat of the Government, Mokhtar Abdallahi, aims to create a unified legal framework for managing national documents in all formats.

Minister Abdallahi stated the new law addresses the obsolescence of the 1968 archives text and aligns legal practices with international standards. He explained it defines national document types, confirms public and heritage status for public archives, and strengthens the National Archives' role in conservation and guidance.

The law also regulates private documents of national interest and sets conditions for their accessibility. It covers electronic and audiovisual documents, and includes incentives and sanctions to ensure proper management and protection of public records, modernizing administration and preserving national memory.

Why it mattersThe new law aims to modernize administration, preserve national memory, enhance transparency, protect citizen rights, and support scientific and historical research in Mauritania.

Mauritanian journalists host regional press meeting

Noukchott will host 21 press leaders from the Arab world and Middle East to discuss journalist challenges.

The Mauritanian Journalists Union is hosting a regional meeting for the International Federation of Journalists in Nouakchott from January 11 to 13, 2027. The event, held in cooperation with the IFJ, will bring together 21 leaders of press organizations from across the Arab world and the Middle East.

Attendees include the IFJ president, secretary-general, and administrative team members. They will discuss key challenges facing journalists and their unions, professional solidarity, and press freedom. The meeting will also review the regional work program and its priorities.

Participants will also address the impact of artificial intelligence and technological shifts on journalism and media jobs. They will explore ways to launch regional initiatives and campaigns to respond to current and emerging challenges.

Why it mattersJournalists and press organizations in Mauritania and the wider Arab world and Middle East will discuss challenges and future strategies for their profession.
