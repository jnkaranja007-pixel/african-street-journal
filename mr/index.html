Mauritania joins Gulf of Guinea naval drills

The Mauritanian navy participated in the annual Grand African NEMO 2026 exercise from October 29 to November 6.

Mauritania's navy participated in regional naval drills in the Gulf of Guinea as part of the annual "Grand African NEMO 2026" exercise. The drills, which ran from October 29 to November 6, involved naval forces from multiple countries and focused on combating illegal immigration, drug trafficking, and piracy. The exercise also included search and rescue operations, medical evacuations, and efforts against illegal fishing.

The Mauritanian army announced its participation via its official Facebook page, stating the navy deployed the vessel "Timbédra" and a special forces team. Liaison officers also attended the West Africa Maritime Coordination Centre (CRESMAO) and the Maritime Coordination Centre (MMCC) in Praia. This involvement aimed to enhance maritime military cooperation, share experiences, and build the Mauritanian navy's operational capabilities.

The exercise scenarios were designed to address maritime security threats in the Gulf of Guinea. The participation of the "Timbédra" and its special forces team highlights Mauritania's commitment to regional maritime security initiatives and inter-state military collaboration.

Why it mattersMauritania's participation in the naval exercise aims to strengthen its maritime security capabilities and cooperation with other nations.

Mauritanian opposition denies refusing UFP seat on dialogue commission

The opposition coalition's spokesman says it has taken no decision against the UFP joining its representation on the national dialogue commission.

The leader of Mauritania's democratic opposition coalition denied reports that the coalition had refused to include the Union of Progressive Forces (UFP) among its representatives on the commission supervising the national dialogue. He said the question of naming the coalition's members to the commission had not yet been addressed.

The reports, published by Sahara Medias, said the coalition had rejected a proposal to give the UFP a seat within its quota, despite efforts by the dialogue commission's head, Moussa Fall, to persuade it to accept. Sidi Ould El Kori, the coalition's spokesman, told Sahara Media on Sunday that the information was inaccurate and not based on decisions by the coalition's competent bodies.

El Kori said the coalition is still consulting the Democratic Opposition Institution to agree on who will represent the opposition on the commission. He added that positions on the coalition's representation and its participation in dialogue bodies can only be attributed to it through its official channels.

Why it mattersThe opposition coalition's choice of representatives for the national dialogue commission determines which parties, including the UFP, sit on the body overseeing the talks.

Mauritania signs Hajj agreement with Saudi Arabia

The deal covers the pilgrimage season for the year 1448 Hijri.

Mauritania's Minister of Islamic Affairs and Original Education, Sidi El-Haj El-Dah Ebe Aïnina, signed an agreement in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, on Monday for the Hajj pilgrimage season 1448 Hijri. He signed the accord with Saudi Minister of Hajj and Umrah, Tawfiq bin Fawzan Al-Rabiah.

The Saudi minister expressed pleasure at the agreement, highlighting Mauritania's status as a nation of knowledge and a guardian of the Quran. He also noted the historical ties between the Chenaquitas and the Kingdom. The Mauritanian minister praised the strong relationship between the two countries, acknowledging Saudi Arabia as the guardian of the Two Holy Mosques and its efforts to improve Hajj and Umrah services.

Both ministers acknowledged the exceptional period in bilateral relations, citing the leadership of Mauritanian President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El-Ghazouani, Saudi King Salman bin Abdulaziz, and Crown Prince Mohamed bin Salman.

Why it mattersMauritanian pilgrims will be able to perform the Hajj this season following the agreement.

Mauritania announces start of Jumada al-Ula on Wednesday

The National Moon Sighting Committee said Wednesday, November 11, 2026, is the first day of the Islamic month of Jumada al-Ula, year 1448.

The National Moon Sighting Committee announced that Wednesday, November 11, 2026, will be the first day of Jumada al-Ula, the fifth month of the Islamic Hijri year 1448. The declaration followed the confirmed sighting of the crescent moon on Tuesday evening.

The committee said in a statement issued after its meeting at the Ministry of Islamic Affairs and Original Education that it carried out the necessary communications and investigations before reaching its conclusion.

The announcement sets the start of the new Islamic month for religious observances in the country.

Why it mattersMuslims in Mauritania will mark religious observances according to the new Islamic month beginning November 11, 2026.

Mauritania's national dialogue debates restoring the Senate

Participants are weighing whether to bring back the upper house that Mohamed Ould Abdel Aziz abolished after it refused to back his plans.

Mauritania's national dialogue is debating institutional changes, including the possible restoration of the Senate. The upper chamber was abolished under President Mohamed Ould Abdel Aziz, who ended it after it refused to validate his political and economic projects. The suppression was confirmed by the 2017 referendum, but a return is not an announced decision and remains open for discussion.

The strongest argument for a second chamber, according to the commentary, is a gap in representation: since 2018 elected regional councils have had no voice in drafting national laws they must then apply. A Senate representing territories rather than only citizens could close that gap. The article says the balance of power would depend on the chamber's exact powers, since one too weak would be useless and one too strong could paralyse the executive.

The dialogue also reopens the debate on term limits. The constitution sets a five-year mandate renewable once, and some currents want that limit revised so a president judged competent could continue beyond 10 years. A move to seven-year terms is part of public debate, but the sources do not report a precise project for it, so it is not presented as a likely outcome.

Why it mattersElected regional councils have had no role in drafting national laws since 2018, and a restored Senate would give territories a voice in that process.
