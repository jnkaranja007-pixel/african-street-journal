Nouakchott hosts Maghreb radiology congress

Doctors and specialists from eight countries gathered for three days to share knowledge and discuss advancements.

The 21st Maghreb Congress of Radiology and Medical Imaging began on Friday in Nouakchott. Physicians, specialists, and experts from Mauritania, Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia, Senegal, Mali, Ivory Coast, and France are attending the three-day event. The congress aims to foster the exchange of experiences and expertise among specialists and to discuss scientific and technical progress in radiology and medical imaging.

Professor Mohamed Beddi, president of the Mauritanian Association of Radiology and Medical Imaging, stated that Mauritania's hosting of the congress aligns with its Arab and African traditions of organizing scientific meetings. He highlighted that the gathering of specialists from the Maghreb, West Africa, and France facilitates knowledge sharing and strengthens professional cooperation. Beddi emphasized that radiology and medical imaging are essential for diagnosis and patient treatment, requiring continuous training and awareness of technical advancements.

The congress program includes discussions on gynecological diseases, digestive system imaging, emergencies, artificial intelligence, and interventional radiology. These themes were chosen to address evolving demands in the field. The association president thanked the country's highest authorities and public and private sector partners for their support and contributions to the event's success.

Why it mattersDoctors and patients in Mauritania and surrounding regions will benefit from the shared knowledge and discussions on new diagnostic and treatment techniques in radiology.

Ghazouani launches Nouakchott development phase two

Mauritania's president announced intensive efforts to improve basic services and infrastructure in the capital city.

Mauritanian President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani announced that Nouakchott will receive intensive efforts to improve basic services and infrastructure. These improvements will focus on water supply, healthcare access, and road and school upgrades. Ghazouani made these remarks on Thursday evening during a meeting with officials in Nouakchott-Sud.

The president stated that efforts will target reducing the population's suffering from thirst, expanding healthcare facilities, and improving road networks to reach areas outside the city. Hospitals will be reinforced with specialized medical staff, and urban beautification will be undertaken. Ghazouani emphasized that serving citizens is an absolute priority, with progress being gradual and continuous.

On Thursday, Ghazouani launched the second phase of Nouakchott's emergency development program, costing 115 billion old ouguiyas. This brings the total cost for all phases to 184 billion old ouguiyas. Projects include new health and school facilities, expanded water and electricity networks, roads, sports facilities, and public space rehabilitation.

Why it mattersResidents of Nouakchott will experience improvements in essential services like water, healthcare, and infrastructure.

16 teams compete in Mauritania's Hodh El Gharbi governorate cup

The third edition of the cup, themed 'Cleanliness is Faith', kicked off with a ceremony attended by local officials.

The governor of Hodh El Gharbi, Mohamed Ould Ahmed Mouloud, launched the third edition of the Hodh El Gharbi governorate cup on Friday evening at El Arquoub stadium in Ayoun. 16 teams representing most of the wilaya's districts are participating in the tournament.

Governor Mouloud thanked the organizers for adopting the slogan 'Cleanliness is Faith' and stressed the importance of community participation in cleaning campaigns. The head of the Hodh El Gharbi region, Jamal Ould Mohamed, highlighted the role of sports in development and awareness, calling on youth to contribute to city cleaning and public hygiene education.

The opening ceremony was attended by the assistant governor of Ayoun district, the mayor, security and military leaders of the wilaya, and several elected officials. The tournament aims to foster sportsmanship and community engagement.

Why it mattersYoung people in Mauritania's Hodh El Gharbi region are encouraged to participate in sports and community cleaning initiatives.

Mauritanian parliament approves government programme timeline

The Conference of Presidents spent over seven hours reviewing the government's agenda before setting dates for its presentation and vote.

Mauritania's National Assembly Conference of Presidents spent seven hours and 20 minutes reviewing the government's programme. The conference approved the timeline by a majority vote. The programme will be presented on November 7 and debated and voted on November 9.

The parliamentary body also scheduled the examination and vote for the 2027 finance bill for December 28. This review follows the bill's referral to the finance commission. The meeting was chaired by first vice-president Sidney Sokhona, with Minister of Culture Mohamed Ould Babana representing the government.

The Conference of Presidents is a body within the National Assembly. Its decisions on the government's programme and finance bill directly impact national policy and budget allocation for the coming year.

Why it mattersThe government's programme and the 2027 finance bill will be debated and voted on by the Mauritanian National Assembly, affecting national policy and budget.

Business · 2026-11-07

Mauritania minister discusses infrastructure with World Bank

Mouhamedou Ahmedou Mhaimid and a World Bank delegation reviewed transport and basic services projects.

Mauritania's Minister of Economic Affairs, Mouhamedou Ahmedou Mhaimid, met with a World Bank delegation on Friday to discuss cooperation and strengthen their partnership, focusing on infrastructure development.

The discussions covered the progress of World Bank-funded projects in Mauritania, ways to speed up their completion, and improve their effectiveness. Priorities for infrastructure, including transport and basic services in various regions, were reviewed.

The World Bank delegation included regional director Frans Dries Gross and resident representative Ibou Diouf.

Why it mattersMauritania's economic development and infrastructure projects are being discussed with the World Bank.
