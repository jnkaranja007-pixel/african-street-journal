African Union Chief Urges Peace Mechanism Reform Amid Complex Security Challenges

Mahmoud Ali Youssef called for accelerating reforms to address escalating terrorism, violent extremism, and interstate tensions across the continent.

The African Union Commission President, Mahmoud Ali Youssef, has urged a swift reform of the continent's peace and security mechanisms, citing increasingly complex challenges. Speaking at an extraordinary session in Luanda, he highlighted the rise in terrorism, violent extremism, internal crises, and external shocks as threats that current strategies may not adequately address. Youssef questioned the readiness of existing mechanisms and resources to confront these evolving dangers.

Youssef emphasized the need for enhanced prevention and early warning efforts, improved coordination among African institutions, and a comprehensive approach to combating terrorism. He stressed that while firmness against unconstitutional changes of government remains crucial, it must be coupled with dialogue. The President also underscored the importance of increasing African funding for peacekeeping operations, stating that the goal to silence arms by 2030 cannot rely primarily on external resources.

The meeting, focused on strengthening conflict prevention and resolution, also heard from Burundi's Foreign Minister and acting Chair of the AU Executive Council, Édouard Bizimana. Bizimana acknowledged progress in collective security mechanisms but warned of a "worrying" escalation of political and security crises, including terrorism, cross-border crime, and climate-related insecurity. He noted that political will is the cornerstone of collective efforts, requiring a focus on prevention, coordination, sustainable financing, and accountability.

Why it mattersAfrican Union leaders are affected by the need to reform peace and security mechanisms due to escalating threats like terrorism and unconstitutional government changes.

Mauritanian Minister Links Peace to Addressing Root Causes of Conflict

Minister Hanena Ould Sidi stated that institutional fragility, poverty, and unemployment are key drivers of African instability.

Mauritanian Defense Minister Hanena Ould Sidi asserted that achieving peace in Africa requires tackling the fundamental causes of conflict. He identified institutional weakness, poverty, unemployment, poor governance, growing social inequalities, and limited opportunities for youth as primary issues. Sidi spoke at the 21st extraordinary session of the African Union heads of state and government conference in Luanda, Angola.

The minister emphasized that establishing the rule of law, strengthening justice and national cohesion, and expanding future prospects for young people are crucial for consolidating security and peace across the continent. He highlighted Mauritania's 'integrated security approach,' which posits that sustainable development cannot exist without security, and vice versa, especially where institutional, economic, and social fragility prevails.

The summit focused on enhancing conflict prevention and resolution mechanisms, developing the African Union's political, legal, and financial tools to manage crises, and supporting mediation efforts. Discussions also covered consolidating the principle of 'African solutions to African problems' and mobilizing resources for stability and sustainable development.

Why it mattersThe minister's statements on institutional fragility, poverty, and unemployment directly affect the stability and development prospects for African populations.

Health Ministry Prepares 28 New Health Centers for Service

Officials reviewed staffing and equipment needs for 28 new health facilities in Nouakchott as part of an urgent development program.

The Ministry of Health is finalizing preparations to launch 28 new health centers across Nouakchott. These facilities are part of an urgent development program aimed at improving the capital's infrastructure. Minister of Health Thiam Tidjani recently chaired a meeting with regional health directors and chief physicians to assess the readiness of these centers, focusing on their requirements for personnel and equipment.

The meeting specifically addressed the exact needs for doctors, nurses, and other health staff at each location. Discussions also covered the installation of necessary equipment and the establishment of a system for distributing human resources to ensure a smooth service launch. The minister emphasized the importance of coordination between central administration and regional services for the successful opening of these new health establishments.

These 28 health facilities are distributed across Nouakchott's nine districts and include new constructions, equipment upgrades, and rehabilitations. The initiative aims to enhance healthcare access for citizens by ensuring the centers are fully operational and well-equipped to provide services upon their opening.

Why it mattersCitizens in Nouakchott will gain access to new health services once the 28 centers are operational.

Mauritanian Academic Appointed OIC Secretary-General's Cabinet Chief

Dr. Ahmed Saïd Ould Bah, president of the Modern University of Chinguetti, will lead the new OIC chief's office.

Ismaïl Ould Cheikh Ahmed, the newly elected Secretary-General of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC), has appointed Dr. Ahmed Saïd Ould Bah to head his cabinet. This appointment is part of the team-building efforts following Ahmed's election to lead the international organization. Dr. Ould Bah, a Mauritanian academic holding a doctorate, previously led the English department at the University of Nouakchott and earned a master's degree in social sciences from Sorbonne Nouvelle in Paris.

Ould Bah's career includes significant roles within the Islamic Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (ISESCO) starting in the mid-1990s. He served in various capacities, including director of the Director-General's cabinet and director of external relations and cooperation. He also acted as an advisor to the Director-General on international partnerships and cooperation. Currently, he presides over the Modern University of Chinguetti, succeeding his father, Dr. Mohamed Mokhtar Ould Bah.

Ismaïl Ould Cheikh Ahmed, who was elected Secretary-General of the OIC, succeeds Houssein Ibrahim Taha of Chad. The appointment of Ould Bah signals a key step in establishing the leadership structure for the OIC under its new Mauritanian head.

Why it mattersMauritanian academic Dr. Ahmed Saïd Ould Bah has been appointed to a senior leadership role within the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, affecting the organization's administrative structure.

Mauritania Caps Financial Transfer Tax at 200 Ouguiyas

New regulations from the General Tax Administration will limit the financial transfer tax to 200 new ouguiyas, regardless of the transaction amount, effective September 1.

Mauritania's General Tax Administration has mandated that banks operating within the country implement a new cap on the financial transfer tax. This measure ensures that the tax due will not exceed 200 new ouguiyas, irrespective of the transfer's total value. The directive is set to take effect no later than September 1.

The updated rules stipulate a tax rate of 0.1% of the transfer's value, with the aforementioned 200 new ouguiyas ceiling. Mokhtar Salem El-Mouna, the Director General of Taxes, met with bank representatives to emphasize the need for necessary technical and administrative adjustments to ensure uniform application of these provisions.

Bank representatives have affirmed their commitment to complying with the new requirements and implementing the tax cap by the September 1 deadline. The General Tax Administration relayed this confirmation, indicating that banks will adapt their systems to meet the new regulatory demands.

Why it mattersBusinesses and individuals conducting financial transfers in Mauritania will pay a maximum of 200 new ouguiyas in tax, regardless of the transfer amount.
