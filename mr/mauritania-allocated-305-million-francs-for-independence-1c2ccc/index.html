Mauritania allocated 305 million francs for independence celebrations

A 1960 budget law drew on French aid and cancelled credits for government departments to finance the celebrations.

Source: Sahara Medias · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Mauritania’s 1960 budget law allocated 305 million francs to celebrations of the proclamation of independence, according to a law published in the official gazette on 7 December 1960. The measure opened 323 million francs in additional operating credits, with more than 94% assigned to a single celebrations budget line.

The law, approved by the National Assembly and promulgated by Prime Minister Mokhtar Ould Daddah, said 125 million francs came from a French grant. The government raised the rest from its own resources, including 129 million francs in cancelled credits from departments such as agriculture, justice, police and the presidency.

The remaining additional credits included 15 million francs for shared material costs, 11 million for air transport and 4 million for building rentals. The document does not break down the celebrations allocation or state how much was ultimately spent.

Why it mattersGovernment departments, including agriculture, justice and the police, lost budget credits as 129 million francs was redirected to fund the celebrations.
