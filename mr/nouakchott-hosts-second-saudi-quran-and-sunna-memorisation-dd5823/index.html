Nouakchott hosts second Saudi Quran and Sunna memorisation contest for West Africa

The contest brings 160 participants from 16 West African countries to compete in two sections, one on memorising the Quran and one on the Sunna.

Source: Sahara Medias · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

The second edition of a contest on memorising the Quran and the Sunna, the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad, opened on Sunday morning in Nouakchott. The competition is named after King Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud, Guardian of the Two Holy Mosques, and is organised by Saudi Arabia's ministry of Islamic affairs, Da'wa and Guidance.

Some 160 participants from 16 West African countries are taking part, divided into two main sections, one on the Quran and one on the Sunna. Each section has five disciplines, ranging from memorisation and recitation (tajwid) to exegesis (tafsir) and understanding of hadiths. Mauritania's minister of Islamic affairs and original education, Sid El-Haj Ould Dah, said the event reflected cooperation between the two countries' ministries and recognition of Mauritania's scholars and madrasas.

Saudi Ambassador to Mauritania Abdelaziz bin Abdullah Al-Raqabi said that holding the contest in Mauritania for a second time reflected the close fraternal and historical ties between the two countries.

Why it mattersParticipants from 16 West African countries are competing in Nouakchott, so families and students in those countries are directly involved in the contest.
