Abdelaziz calls for 10-year plan to reform Libya's education sector

He says more than 700,000 staff and teachers serve 1.3 million students, and argues staffing reform must protect employees' livelihoods.

Source: Libya Update · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

A teachers' strike is disrupting the academic year in Libya, and Mahmoud Abdelaziz, a university professor who has trained teachers, is calling for a 10-year reform plan for the sector. He criticised the protests as having gone beyond acceptable limits and rejected the teachers' union proposal to make up missed classes on Fridays and Saturdays.

Education employs more than 700,000 staff and teachers for 1.3 million students, Abdelaziz said, adding that Libya needs no more than 150,000 teachers, based on scientific data. Any cut in numbers, he said, must not leave employees without income, and simply raising the education budget would not fix staffing imbalances. He praised schools and teachers that have resumed classes.

He said weekends are a right and that make-up plans must account for students' psychological and social needs. Paying all outstanding wages at once, he said, is unrealistic, and he offered to take part in implementing the reform plan.

Why it mattersTeachers who have not been paid and families whose children are out of class depend on the 10-year plan for any change to pay and class schedules.
