Benghazi book fair session explains Marrakesh Treaty access rules

Speakers at the fifth Benghazi International Book Fair said the treaty lets books be converted into accessible formats without prior permission from authors.

Source: Libya Update · 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

An informational session at the fifth Benghazi International Book Fair examined how the Marrakesh Treaty can improve access to books and literary works for people with disabilities. Abdul Basit Abbas Al-Aqouri, a faculty member in the Arabic Language Department at the University of Benghazi and chairman of the Libyan Organization for the Rights of the Visually Impaired, said books in suitable formats let beneficiaries keep up with new publications.

Dr. Ibtisam Abhih, a professor of private law at the University of Benghazi, gave a legal paper at the session on the treaty, adopted in 2013 under the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO). She said it covers people who are blind, visually impaired, or have physical disabilities that stop them turning pages, and allows works to be converted into Braille, digital and audio versions without the author's prior permission.

Abhih said the treaty balances authors' rights, which Law No. 9 of 1968 governs locally, against the fundamental right of persons with disabilities to access knowledge. She also linked it to Articles 9 and 30 of the International Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, and noted that Law No. 5 of 1987 did not explicitly address cultural rights for those persons.

Why it mattersBlind, visually impaired and physically disabled readers in Libya gain a legal route to Braille, digital and audio editions of books without waiting for each author's permission.
