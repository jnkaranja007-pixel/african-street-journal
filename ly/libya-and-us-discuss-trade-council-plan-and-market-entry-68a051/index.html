Libya and US discuss trade council plan and market-entry obstacles

Officials reviewed the Trade and Investment Framework Agreement and ways to ease American companies’ entry into Libya’s market.

Source: Libya Herald · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

A Libyan government delegation met US trade officials in Washington on Friday to discuss the work plan of the Libyan-US Trade and Investment Council and follow up on the countries’ Trade and Investment Framework Agreement (TIFA).

The talks covered regulatory and customs obstacles to American companies entering Libya, alongside investment opportunities in energy, mining, infrastructure and services. Libya’s delegation included Economy and Trade Minister Suhail Abu Shiha and National Oil Corporation chairman Masoud Suleiman; the US delegation included officials from the Office of the US Trade Representative.

The sides also discussed priorities for cooperation in trade, investment and digital services, and coordination between their institutions. The meeting’s stated aim was to turn the agreement’s areas of cooperation into practical measures and specific opportunities.

Why it mattersLibyan officials must identify priorities and address customs and regulatory obstacles as they seek practical trade and investment measures with the United States.
