Mountain municipalities give Libyan government one week over stalled funding

Local leaders from the mountain regions reject recent cabinet financial allocations and accuse Prime Minister Abdul Hamid Dbeibah's government of unfair treatment.

Representatives of Libya's mountain municipalities have given the government one week to address delays in funding development projects in their regions. The group, which includes mayors, local leaders and community representatives, met in an emergency meeting in Yefren and called on Prime Minister Abdul Hamid Dbeibah and the Government of National Unity to release payments for agreed projects.

The representatives said projects approved two or three years ago have not moved forward because funds have not been disbursed, and blamed both the government and the Ministry of Local Government for failing to defend municipal rights. They said the delays have harmed residents, especially in infrastructure, water and health. They also rejected the latest Cabinet decisions on financial allocations and called for a review so that the municipalities receive what they consider their fair share.

The representatives said they are tired of repeated meetings, promises and unfulfilled commitments, and that their rights are not open to compromise or negotiation. While they said the statement was not a threat, they insisted they would no longer stay silent or accept further delays.

Why it mattersResidents in the mountain regions, who the representatives say already face hardship, will go without the stalled water, health and infrastructure work until the funding is released.

Libya's central bank governor meets payment firms over e-payment expansion

Governor Fawzi Abu Khazam discussed performance indicators and technical problems that are holding back electronic payment services.

Fawzi Abu Khazam, Governor of the Central Bank of Libya (CBL), met at his Tripoli office with several electronic payment service providers, the state-owned Moamalat switch company and directors of relevant CBL departments. The meeting reviewed the latest progress in expanding e-payment services and the technical challenges slowing their delivery.

Abu Khazam stressed the need for stronger infrastructure, better technical integration and connectivity among payment providers, so that transactions are executed quickly and easily. He also called for stronger cybersecurity, business continuity and operational risk management, in line with international standards, to build user confidence.

He said the services should be raised to a higher standard and extended across regions and sectors, supporting financial inclusion and reducing reliance on cash. He ended by calling for continued coordination between the CBL and sector companies, with performance indicators monitored regularly.

Why it mattersCitizens and institutions across Libya will depend on whether the providers fix the technical gaps and extend electronic payments beyond current users.

Libya plans 50-vessel fishing fleet and aquaculture

Two Libyan companies will sign a deal to finance and develop the nation's marine resources sector.

Libya's Al-Tijari Al-Watani Leasing Company and Libyan Distinguished Group Holding are preparing to sign a memorandum of intent to finance a national project for fishing fleet modernization and aquaculture development.

The partnership aims to build 50 modern fishing vessels and rehabilitate existing ones, alongside developing tuna fattening projects and local maritime industries. A representative stated the initiative seeks to transform maritime potential into productive ventures, creating jobs for Libyan youth and developing national talent.

The project requires scientific and economic studies, adherence to governance principles, and leveraging international best practices. The cooperation framework also aims to establish sustainable financing models with local and international banks.

Why it mattersThe initiative aims to build 50 modern fishing vessels and develop aquaculture projects to boost the national economy.

Business · 2026-11-13

Libya opens two-day Invest in Libya forum in Tripoli

Ministers, the Deputy Prime Minister and foreign diplomats gathered at a Tripoli hotel to discuss how to bring domestic and foreign capital into the economy.

The Permanent Libyan Economic Forum opened in Tripoli on Wednesday, 11 November, under the banner "Invest in Libya". The two-day event runs to 12 November at a leading hotel in the capital and is overseen by the National Economic and Social Development Board (NESDB), with the General Union of Chambers of Commerce as organiser.

The opening ceremony was attended by the Deputy Prime Minister, the ministers of economy and trade, housing and construction, tourism and artificial intelligence, members of the House of Representatives and the High State Council, the NESDB's director-general, ambassadors and business leaders. The sessions will review the investment and commercial environment, the economic policies needed to attract domestic and international capital, and ways to strengthen the private sector.

The forum presented the "Namaa" (Growth) plan drafted by the NESDB, which sets out promising sectors meant to diversify income sources. Two dialogue sessions were held on the first day: one on geopolitical changes and their effect on the local and international economy, and one on national visions and prospects for Libya's economic development.

Why it mattersInvestors weighing Libya's business climate now have the NESDB's Namaa plan, which names the sectors the government wants to grow, to test against their own plans.
