Libya, Pakistan Discuss Benghazi Consulate Opening

Libya's Foreign Ministry and Pakistan's ambassador reviewed steps to establish a Pakistani consulate in the eastern city of Benghazi.

Libya's Foreign Ministry is preparing to open a consulate in Benghazi with Pakistan. Saad Hassan Bakkar, Director of the Asia and Australia Affairs Department, met Sunday with Pakistan's Ambassador to Libya, Mumtaz Hussain Sakr, and his delegation. The officials discussed strengthening bilateral ties and the specific preparations needed to establish the Pakistani consulate in the eastern Libyan city.

The meeting, held in Benghazi, focused on the logistical and diplomatic steps required for the consulate's establishment. This move signals an intent to deepen economic and consular relations between the two nations. The Pakistani delegation's presence indicates a commitment to advancing this diplomatic initiative.

The opening of a Pakistani consulate in Benghazi is expected to facilitate consular services for citizens and streamline business interactions. This development is part of ongoing efforts to enhance diplomatic representation and cooperation between Libya and Pakistan, particularly in the eastern region.

Why it mattersInvestors may benefit from improved trade relations and easier access to consular services for business dealings in Benghazi.

Libyan Mayor Tackles Bread Shortage Amid Fuel Crisis

Qatroun's mayor convened a meeting to discuss the impact of diesel shortages on local bakeries and food availability.

Mohammad Younes Ramadan, the mayor of Qatroun, met Monday with municipal council members and economic officials to address a severe bread shortage affecting the city. The crisis stems from several bakeries ceasing operations or significantly cutting production due to an insufficient supply of diesel fuel. The discussions focused on the primary challenges hindering the local economy and food supply chain.

The meeting, held at the mayor's office, reviewed the critical issues impacting the municipality's ability to provide essential goods. The lack of diesel fuel has directly led to the closure of bakeries, disrupting the availability of bread, a staple food for the community. Officials are seeking solutions to ensure a consistent supply of fuel to these vital businesses.

The economic overseer and the mayor’s office director were present to provide insights into the logistical and financial hurdles. The situation highlights the vulnerability of local food production to energy supply disruptions. The municipality is exploring immediate measures to alleviate the shortage and prevent further economic fallout.

Why it mattersResidents of Qatroun are facing a bread shortage because bakeries lack diesel fuel to operate.
