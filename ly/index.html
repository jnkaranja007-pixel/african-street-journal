Libya's central bank starts notifying banks of approved import credits

Food, medicine and animal feed get priority as the Central Bank of Libya releases documentary credits to commercial banks ahead of Ramadan.

The Central Bank of Libya (CBL) has begun notifying commercial banks that it has approved applications for documentary credits, the letters of credit that pay for imports. The applications being notified had already cleared the documentary credit system.

Priority is going to applications for food products, medicines and animal feed, following coordination with the Ministry of Economy. The CBL said the aim is a steady supply of goods to the market as the holy month of Ramadan approaches.

The CBL said it will keep notifying banks of approvals for all types of goods every week until the end of the year. The measure follows an 11 November agreement between the CBL and the Ministry of Economy and Trade that credits will be issued on the basis of national priority, to control how much hard currency is released and save reserves.

Gas tankers Derna and Al-Tahadi boost Libyan supplies

Gas deliveries have resumed to restock company depots as congestion begins to ease, according to Brega Petroleum Marketing Company.

Two gas tankers, the Derna and the Al-Tahadi, have boosted supply levels at depots run by Brega Petroleum Marketing Company. The Derna docked at Tripoli port, while the Al-Tahaddi arrived at the Jalyana port in Benghazi.

Ahmed Al-Meslati, the company's media director, said the arrivals have resumed gas deliveries to support stocks and immediate filling systems. The deliveries follow a period of congestion in supply.

The company has not said how much gas each vessel carried or how long the stocks will last. Further updates on supply levels are expected from Brega.

Why it mattersHouseholds and businesses that rely on gas filling stations and depots should see supply become more regular as the stock shortage eases.

Libya's Health Ministry reports girl's death from toxic wild mushrooms

17-year-old Maryam Jassim Saeed died after eating poisonous wild mushrooms, the ministry said, with several other patients treated at Al-Bayda Medical Center.

The Ministry of Health announced the death of Maryam Jassim Saeed, 17, who died from poisoning after eating toxic wild mushrooms. She was among several patients admitted to Al-Bayda Medical Center.

The ministry said Maryam suffered acute liver failure caused by the poisoning. Despite receiving medical care and treatment from a specialised team, she did not survive.

The report did not say whether the other patients admitted with her had recovered or were still in care, or where the mushrooms were collected.

Why it mattersFamilies who gather wild mushrooms in the Al-Bayda area now face a fatal example of how quickly poisoning can cause liver failure.

Libya launches electronic system for foreign firms

The Ministry of Economy and Trade is piloting a new digital platform to streamline foreign company branch registrations.

Libya's Ministry of Economy and Trade has launched a pilot electronic registration system for branches of foreign companies. Imad Ashour, Media Officer at the ministry, announced the system, developed by the Economic Information and Documentation Centre, aims to simplify procedures and increase transparency. The digital platform allows for electronic submission and tracking of registration applications.

The pilot phase will test the system's operational efficiency and gather feedback before full implementation. Ashour stated this initiative is part of the ministry's strategy to modernize economic administration and improve the business environment for foreign investors. The goal is to streamline processes for companies operating in the Libyan market.

This move is intended to enhance services provided to companies and investors by promoting digital transformation within the ministry. The system's development precedes this pilot launch, focusing on improving administrative efficiency and transparency in foreign company branch registrations.

Why it mattersForeign companies seeking to operate in Libya can now submit and track registration applications electronically, simplifying administrative procedures.

Business · 2026-11-16

Emmaus expands in Libya through drug distribution deal

The American biopharmaceutical company has signed an exclusive distribution agreement with a Libyan pharmaceutical importer to grow its presence in the country.

Emmaus, an American biopharmaceutical company, has entered into an exclusive distribution agreement with the National International Company for Pharmaceuticals and Medical Supplies to strengthen its commercial presence in Libya. The US website Stock Titan reported the partnership, saying it aims to expand Emmaus's international footprint and boost its position in the market.

The agreement makes the Libyan company the exclusive distributor for Emmaus in Libya, so Emmaus products will reach the market through a single local partner. The report gives no details on which products are covered or when distribution will begin.

The deal adds Libya to the list of markets where Emmaus is seeking a larger share, according to the report, which does not describe the size of the Libyan market or the terms of the agreement.

Why it mattersEmmaus medicines in Libya will now move through one exclusive local distributor, which determines who supplies them to pharmacies and hospitals.
