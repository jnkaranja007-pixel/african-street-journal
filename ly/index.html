GECOL Chairman Promises End to Libya Power Cuts in Two Weeks

Newly appointed GECOL Chairman Bashir Al-Marash stated that new units are entering the national grid and the South Tripoli power station is nearing commissioning, aiming.

Libya's electricity crisis, marked by frequent and extensive power cuts since mid-June, is expected to end within 10 to 15 days, according to Bashir Al-Marash, the new Chairman of the General Electricity Company of Libya (GECOL). Speaking at a meeting with the Municipality of Sug Il Juma, Al-Marash expressed confidence that the situation will improve for the entire country, citing the addition of new units to the national grid and a 90 percent chance of improvement. He also noted that fair load-shedding requires a computerized system.

Officials from GECOL also confirmed to the Municipality of Tripoli Centre that the South Tripoli power station is nearing its commissioning phase. This facility is anticipated to bolster the national grid and enhance the stability of electricity supply, further contributing to the expected gradual improvement in power availability. GECOL has not yet provided a specific date for when the new power station will become operational.

The current electricity crisis has led to significant public unrest, including demonstrations with road blockades involving fires and the barricading of state buildings. The assurances from GECOL leadership come as the nation grapples with the consequences of prolonged blackouts throughout the summer.

Why it mattersThe promise of ending power cuts within two weeks directly impacts businesses by potentially restoring stable operations and reducing losses from electricity disruptions.

Libyan Chamber Seeks Spanish Support for Energy Forum and Housing Visit

The General Union of Chambers of Commerce in Tripoli met with the Spanish Ambassador to discuss enhancing bilateral trade and securing Spanish participation in an upcoming energy.

Libya's General Union of Chambers of Commerce, represented by its head Mohamed Raied, hosted the Spanish Ambassador at its Tripoli headquarters. The meeting focused on strengthening economic and trade ties between Libya and Spain, exploring avenues for enhanced cooperation and mutual benefit. Discussions also centered on the upcoming third Libyan-European Oil, Gas, and Energy Forum, scheduled for December 2-3, 2026, in Madrid.

Raied requested the Ambassador's assistance in securing support from Spanish authorities, including the Spanish Trade Office and Repsol, for the energy forum. The aim is to ensure the event's success by facilitating the participation of Spanish companies and institutions. The Ambassador expressed his commitment to supporting the forum, highlighting its importance in fostering economic connections and opening new avenues for private sector collaboration between the two nations.

Additionally, the Chamber sought Spanish support for a planned visit by Libya's Minister of Housing and Utilities to Spain. This visit aims to engage with Spanish specialists in construction and infrastructure to leverage their expertise. The Ambassador welcomed this initiative and pledged to provide necessary coordination and support to achieve the visit's objectives, underscoring a shared commitment to advancing Libyan-Spanish economic, trade, and investment relations.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the potential for increased participation in Libya's energy sector and infrastructure development through the planned forum and ministerial visit.

Libyan Experts Forum Proposes Economic Reforms

Recommendations include expediting digital projects, improving financial governance, and redirecting bank financing towards productive sectors and SMEs.

The first Libyan Experts Forum, held in Tripoli from August 23 to 25, concluded with a series of wide-ranging recommendations aimed at practical implementation. Organized by the Ministry of Labor and Rehabilitation in cooperation with the Libyan Authority for Scientific Research, the forum brought together Libyan professionals from within and outside the country for three days of dialogue on national issues.

Key proposals include expediting the implementation of a "Digital Addressing Platform" project and completing the digitization of real estate ownership documents to reduce disputes and stimulate investment. The forum also recommended establishing a national office to monitor strategic projects using standard indicators for progress tracking.

Further recommendations focus on financial reforms, such as redirecting bank financing towards productive sectors and small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), and developing public financial governance and banking supervision. These initiatives are intended to support state institutions, enhance development, and improve service levels.

Why it mattersThe recommendations from the Libyan Experts Forum could impact investors by proposing measures to reduce disputes, stimulate investment, and redirect financing towards productive sectors and SMEs.

Power Restored to Sirte After 17-Hour Blackout

Electricity has gradually returned to Sirte and surrounding areas following a prolonged outage caused by technical failures at the Gulf Steam Power Station and transmission line.

Electricity has returned to Sirte and its surrounding areas after a complete power outage lasting over seventeen hours. The disruption was attributed to technical malfunctions at the Gulf Steam Power Station and the failure of multiple transmission lines, according to reports from the Libyan News Agency.

The restoration of power was a gradual process, with service slowly being reinstated following the resolution of the technical issues. The outage significantly impacted daily life and commercial activities within the affected regions, highlighting the fragility of the local power infrastructure.

The incident underscores ongoing challenges with Libya's electricity grid. The complete shutdown of a major power station and the failure of transmission lines demonstrate critical vulnerabilities that require immediate attention to ensure consistent service delivery.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in Sirte and surrounding areas were affected by a 17-hour power outage due to technical failures, disrupting daily life and economic activities.

Libya, Germany Discuss Reopening Consular Services and Direct Flights

Libyan Minister of State Mohamed Ben Ghalboun met with German Ambassador Arvid Enders to explore strengthening bilateral ties, including consular services and new air routes.

Libya and Germany are in discussions to reopen the German consular section in Tripoli and establish new direct flights connecting Tripoli with Berlin and Frankfurt. The talks occurred yesterday during a meeting between the Libyan Minister of State for Prime Ministerial and Cabinet Affairs, Mohamed Ben Ghalboun, and the new German Ambassador to Libya, Arvid Enders. The meeting, also attended by Abubaker Al-Tawil, Director of the European Affairs Department, focused on enhancing cooperation for mutual benefit.

Ambassador Enders expressed his appreciation for the support received since his arrival and reiterated his commitment to deepening cooperation across various sectors. Both sides acknowledged the importance of expediting the consular section's reopening and initiating the new flight routes. These discussions aim to solidify the Libyan-German partnership through continued consultations on shared interests.

The potential resumption of consular services could facilitate official processes for citizens and businesses. The introduction of direct flights between Tripoli and major German cities like Berlin and Frankfurt would significantly improve travel connectivity and could boost trade and tourism. These developments signal a move towards strengthening diplomatic and economic ties between the two nations.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the potential reopening of the German consular section and the establishment of new direct flights, which could facilitate business travel, trade, and investment between Libya and Germany.
