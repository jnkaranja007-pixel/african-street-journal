Libya's House of Representatives speaker meets planning council head

Salehin Abdel Nabi and Ahmed Khalifa Breidan discussed an emergency economic plan to reduce public spending.

Speaker of the House of Representatives Salehin Abdel Nabi met Ahmed Khalifa Breidan, head of the National Planning Council, in Benghazi on Thursday. They discussed a new emergency economic plan for Libya aimed at reducing public spending and protecting public funds.

The officials also considered revising the exchange rate of the US dollar against the Libyan dinar. An experts' meeting on the economic plan is scheduled within 10 days.

The House of Representatives also rejected a unilateral appointment by the High State Council for a Deputy Head of the Audit Bureau.

Why it mattersLibyans will be affected by potential changes in public spending and the exchange rate.

Libyan and Indonesian businesses sign trade deal

A cooperation agreement signed in Semarang aims to boost direct trade and commercial ties between the two nations.

Libyan and Indonesian business leaders signed a cooperation agreement on October 20 in Semarang, Indonesia, to facilitate trade between their countries. The deal aims to foster direct cooperation between private-sector companies from Libya and Indonesia.

The agreement follows a Libyan Business Forum held on October 16 in Jakarta, where 12 letters of intent for commercial deals worth US$1.8 million were signed. Bilateral B2B meetings were also held to explore export and import opportunities.

The Libyan Business Council is now working with Indonesian economic entities to develop trade exchange opportunities and convert the letters of intent into actionable commercial agreements. The Central Java region, where Semarang is located, is an industrial and export hub.

Why it mattersLibyan businesses will benefit from new trade opportunities and commercial agreements with Indonesian companies.

Benghazi thesis on air pollution approved

Manal Kamel Mohamed Al-Iskandarani’s master’s thesis studied cement factory emissions and climate factors.

Manal Kamel Mohamed Al-Iskandarani’s master’s thesis on air pollution around Benghazi’s cement plant was approved without changes on Wednesday by the Libyan Academy’s Benghazi branch. The study, titled “The Impact of Air Pollution on Climate Change – A Case Study of the Cement Factory in Benghazi,” was submitted to the Department of Environmental Engineering.

The research examined pollutants like nitrogen oxides, sulfur oxides, and particulate matter, alongside climate factors such as temperature and wind speed, between June 2024 and May 2025. Al-Iskandarani, who is also Director of the Office of the Minister of State for Women’s Affairs, presented findings that showed monthly variations in pollutant levels and highlighted the role of wind patterns in dispersion.

The study’s results did not solely establish a direct causal link between factory emissions and climate change, but indicated that pollutant concentrations are influenced by weather conditions and various pollution sources. Members of the House of Representatives, Youssef Kamoud Al-Fakhri and Ibrahim Al-Zagheid, attended the thesis defense session.

Why it mattersThe research examines the impact of air pollution from a cement factory on climate change in Benghazi.
