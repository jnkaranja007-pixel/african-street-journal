Zawia Power Station Unit Returns 150 MW to Grid After GE Vernova Maintenance

The third unit at Zawia Power Station has reconnected to Libya's national grid, adding 150 MW after delayed repairs by GE Vernova.

The General Electricity Company of Libya (GECOL) announced that the Third Unit at the Zawia Combined-Cycle Power Plant is now connected to the national grid, contributing 150 MW of power. This follows the completion of significant maintenance work by US company GE Vernova, the maintenance division of GE, in partnership with GECOL engineers.

GE Vernova had previously suspended work in August due to an armed drone attack on the site and for nine days in May because of nearby militia clashes, both incidents contributing to the delay. The original maintenance completion date was July 6, 2026, but GE Vernova also cited technical difficulties for further delays.

To expedite the process, GE Vernova deployed additional engineering expertise and technical resources, both on-site and remotely. Libya has experienced a very hot summer with extended power cuts since the end of June, coinciding with demonstrations and civil disobedience aimed at dislodging the Tripoli government.

Why it mattersLibya's national grid has gained 150 MW of power, potentially alleviating extended summer power cuts affecting the population.

Former Libyan Goalkeeper Ali Hussein Abu Oud Dies

A celebrated figure in Libyan football, Abu Oud was known for his long career and a remarkable return to play after a nine-year retirement.

Former Libyan national football team goalkeeper Ali Hussein Abu Oud died on Thursday in Benghazi. Abu Oud, a prominent figure in Libyan sports during the 1960s and early 1970s, was recognized for his significant contributions both on and off the field. He played for Al-Ahli Benghazi and the national team, earning over 50 caps and participating in the 1964 Arab Cup where Libya finished as runners-up.

Born in 1943, Abu Oud began his football journey in 1956 and transitioned to goalkeeping due to his unique abilities. He was the national team's first-choice goalkeeper from 1960 to 1974. His career included appearances in Olympic qualifiers and the 1967 Mediterranean Games. He also played in continental fixtures for Al-Ahli Benghazi against teams like Esperance and Ismaily.

Abu Oud is remembered for an extraordinary comeback in the 1982-1983 season, returning to Al-Ahli Benghazi at over 40 years old to address a goalkeeping crisis. He played two matches, including a 2-0 victory in his first game back, earning praise from fans for his loyalty and sacrifice. This return is considered a rare feat in Libyan football history.

Why it mattersLibyan sports fans and the football community are affected by the death of a respected former national team goalkeeper.

Libya, South Korea Plan Economic Forum

Officials met to finalize arrangements for a September 2026 forum aimed at boosting bilateral trade and investment opportunities.

Libya and the Republic of Korea are preparing to host an Economic Cooperation Forum on September 28, 2026, in Libya. A meeting between Ambassador Ashraf Mohamed Al-Taib of Libya's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Cho Woo-seok, the South Korean Chargé d'affaires, discussed the necessary technical, organizational, and protocol arrangements. The Libyan side expressed readiness to host the event and committed to coordinating with Korean officials and relevant Libyan authorities to ensure its success.

The forum aims to foster practical and tangible outcomes by facilitating bilateral meetings between Libyan and Korean institutions and companies. Discussions also covered opening broader prospects for economic and investment cooperation between the two nations. Both sides affirmed their commitment to continued coordination and consultations to complete all arrangements and achieve the forum's objectives.

On the sidelines of the meeting, a proposal for reciprocal visa exemption for holders of diplomatic and special passports was raised. This measure is intended to facilitate official visits and enhance communication and cooperation. The Director of the International Cooperation Department stressed the importance of following up on this proposal through official channels.

Why it mattersThe forum aims to open broader prospects for economic and investment cooperation between Libya and South Korea.

Libya, Japan Sign Health Cooperation Memorandum

The agreement aims to enhance training, expertise exchange, and the adoption of Japanese medical technologies to bolster Libya's healthcare system.

Libya and Japan have formalized their first health sector cooperation agreement through a signed memorandum of understanding. Libyan Minister of Health, Mohamed Al-Ghouj, finalized the deal with his Japanese counterpart, Kenichiro Ueno, during an official visit to Japan.

This collaboration is designed to foster training programs, professional development, and the exchange of expertise between the two nations. It also seeks to improve health institutions and services by integrating Japanese technologies and experience to strengthen Libya's healthcare capabilities.

During the visit, Minister Al-Ghouj met with Japanese officials from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA), and the Diet to explore further cooperation avenues and support for health initiatives. A tour of the Ariake Cancer Institute Hospital showcased advanced diagnostic and treatment technologies for tumors.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens will benefit from improved healthcare services and access to advanced medical technologies through this cooperation.

Libya Deports 50 Sudanese Migrants with Infectious Diseases

The Anti-Illegal Migration Agency is relocating 50 Sudanese nationals from Benghazi to Al-Kufra as part of their removal from Libya.

Fifty Sudanese migrants diagnosed with infectious diseases are being deported from the Greater Benghazi detention center to Al-Kufra. The Anti-Illegal Migration Agency announced the operation on Saturday, stating it is a preliminary step toward their complete removal from Libyan territory. The agency confirmed the action followed instructions from Major General Salah Mahmoud Al-Khafifi.

The migrants, all of Sudanese origin, were transferred to the city of Al-Kufra, located in southeastern Libya. This move is part of a broader effort by Libyan authorities to manage and deport undocumented migrants, particularly those identified as having health concerns that could pose a risk within detention facilities or upon potential return to their home countries.

The agency's announcement did not specify the exact nature of the infectious diseases or the intended final destination for the migrants after their relocation to Al-Kufra. The operation highlights the ongoing challenges Libya faces in managing migration flows and detention center conditions, especially concerning the health and well-being of detained individuals.

Why it mattersFifty Sudanese migrants with infectious diseases are being moved from a detention center in Benghazi to Al-Kufra as part of their deportation process from Libya.
