Libya official meets Tebu tribal sultan

Mahmoud Al-Ferjani and Sheikh Zalawi Mina Saleh discussed strengthening state ties with social groups.

Mahmoud Al-Ferjani, Director General of the National Development Agency, met with Sheikh Zalawi Mina Saleh, Sultan of the Tebu tribes, in Tripoli. The meeting aimed to improve communication and cooperation between state institutions and social and tribal groups.

Both Al-Ferjani and Saleh stressed the importance of unifying national efforts. They also highlighted the need to enhance connections between government bodies and the country's various social components.

The discussions focused on strengthening communication and cooperation with social and tribal groups across Libya.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens, specifically those belonging to Tebu tribes and other social groups, are affected by efforts to improve communication and cooperation with state institutions.

Libyan publishers face rising book costs

Fluctuating exchange rates and import expenses drive up production costs for local books.

Libyan publishers are struggling with increased book production costs due to reliance on imported paper and fluctuating foreign currency exchange rates. These economic challenges are pushing up retail prices for books across the country.

Ali Jaber, manager of Al-Jaber Publishing, stated that printing costs are similar across Libya, Egypt, and Tunisia because all presses use imported paper. He explained that books are often priced using the US dollar on the parallel market, as official letters of credit for imports are unavailable, making production costs dependent on unofficial exchange rates. In 2010, producing a book cost about $5, equivalent to 7.5 dinars, but today the same dollar amount is nearly 50 dinars.

While Libyan titles typically range from 30 to 50 dinars, imported books can cost 100 to 1000 dinars. Shipping books from Cairo to Benghazi can be cheaper than between some Libyan cities, costing about 100 dinars per parcel, though delays of up to 45 days are common. Printing in Egypt offers Libyan publishers more flexibility for participating in international book fairs.

Why it mattersLibyan readers face higher book prices due to increased production costs driven by exchange rates and imported materials.

Libya seizes 21 trucks carrying subsidised goods

15 trucks were found heading towards border regions, suspected of being used for smuggling.

Libyan authorities intercepted 21 trucks and their drivers on Monday, November 2, for allegedly transporting subsidised goods. The Anti-Financial Crimes, Money Laundering, and Terrorist Financing (AFCMLTF) reported that six trucks were released after initial checks, but 15 were found to be heading towards border areas.

These 15 trucks, along with their drivers, were referred to the AFCMLTF for legal proceedings. Investigators suspect these domestic transport operations are a front for smuggling goods out of the country. The case file was sent to the Advocate General in Tripoli for further action.

The operation follows directives from the Prime Minister to control rising prices of essential commodities. Measures include increased oversight on transport, distribution, and storage to ensure goods remain in local markets and prices are stable.

Why it mattersCitizens are affected by rising prices of essential commodities and potential exploitation of goods meant for local markets.

Malta firm discusses marine safety centre in Tripoli

Camilleri Marine director visited the Libyan Ports Company to advance plans for a new maintenance facility.

Kenneth Camilleri, director of Malta-based Camilleri Marine, visited the Libyan Ports Company's branch at the Port of Tripoli yesterday. The visit aimed to establish a specialized centre for maintaining marine vessels' safety equipment.

Camilleri toured the "Oea Marine Centre," slated to open soon, and discussed operational mechanisms and a memorandum of understanding with Libyan Ports Company officials. The centre, designed to international standards, will enhance technical services for marine safety equipment.

An agreement was reached on executive steps to accelerate preparations for the "Oea Marine Centre," Libya's first facility of its kind. This centre will begin operations after technical procedures and equipment installation are complete.

Why it mattersLibyan ports will soon have a new facility for maintaining marine safety equipment, improving services for vessels operating there.

Libya cuts customs dollar rate to LD 2.12

The Ministry of Finance reduced the rate used for import duties from LD 4.80 to LD 2.12 per US dollar.

Libya's Ministry of Finance issued a directive last Tuesday, November 3, to lower the Customs Dollar exchange rate for imported goods. The rate has been set at 2.12 Libyan dinars per US dollar, a significant decrease from the previous rate of 4.80 Libyan dinars.

This temporary measure, announced via official directive, aims to adjust the calculation of duties on imported goods. The Ministry of Finance stated this change is pending the resolution of existing issues related to the rate.

The directive suspends Resolution No. (160) of 2026, which previously governed the Customs Dollar exchange rate. This adjustment impacts the cost of importing goods into Libya.

Why it mattersThe reduction in the Customs Dollar rate will lower the cost of imported goods for businesses and consumers in Libya.
