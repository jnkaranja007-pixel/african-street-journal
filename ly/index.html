Libya and US discuss visa challenges

Officials from Libya's Foreign Ministry and the U.S. Embassy met to improve consular cooperation and address obstacles for citizens applying for visas.

Libya's Foreign Ministry reported that officials met with Janet Robert, Head of Consular Affairs at the U.S. Embassy in Libya, to discuss strengthening consular communication. The meeting, which included representatives from both sides, focused on enhancing cooperation and facilitating matters of mutual interest. Both parties emphasized the importance of maintaining institutional dialogue and coordination channels.

A key topic of discussion was the challenges and obstacles faced by citizens of both Libya and the United States in their visa application processes. Views were exchanged on existing difficulties, and potential approaches were explored to overcome these hurdles. The aim is to further streamline procedures while ensuring compliance with the national legislation and legal requirements of both countries.

The discussions also covered several other issues of mutual interest, with an emphasis on continued consultation and coordination. These efforts are intended to develop practical solutions to the challenges discussed and to support the further development of bilateral relations between Libya and the United States, serving the interests of citizens from both nations.

Why it mattersLibyan and American citizens may face fewer difficulties when applying for visas to travel between the two countries.

Economy minister bans foreign truck drivers

Suhail Abu Shiha's decree prohibits non-Libyan drivers from transporting goods and commodities within the country.

Libya's Minister of Economy and Trade, Suhail Abu Shiha, issued a decree yesterday prohibiting the employment of foreign drivers for transporting goods and commodities within the country. Decree No. (532) of 2026 aligns with existing labor and road transport legislation.

The ban applies to trucks and vehicles used for transporting goods and commodities, whether for third parties or directly for employers, provided the operation falls under Libyan law. The Ministry stated the decision aims to regulate road transport, enforce labor market rules, and ensure legal compliance.

This measure is intended to strengthen adherence to Libyan regulations within the road transport sector. The Ministry emphasized that all such operations must now be conducted in accordance with the legal requirements currently in force across the nation.

Why it mattersLibyan businesses that rely on road transport for goods and commodities will be affected by the ban on foreign drivers.

Libya signs food security deal with Islamic organization

The agriculture minister and the Islamic Organization for Food Security agreed to a framework for cooperation on livestock and crop production.

Libya's Minister of Agriculture and Livestock, Abdel Latif Al-Tahir, signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the Islamic Organization for Food Security (IOFS) in Astana, Kazakhstan. The agreement establishes an institutional framework to support Libya's national priorities in agriculture, livestock, and food security, aligning with the IOFS 2031 vision.

The MoU aims to enhance technical cooperation and develop partnerships in the agricultural and livestock sectors. The IOFS expressed readiness to support joint projects, exchange expertise, and develop value chains for grains and livestock, as well as advance fisheries. Discussions also covered plant and animal genetic resources and food security initiatives.

A joint working group will be formed to create an action plan for 2026-2028, outlining cooperation priorities and projects. This initiative is part of Libya's efforts to strengthen partnerships with regional and international organizations to boost sustainable agricultural development and enhance the efficiency of its agricultural and livestock sectors.

Why it mattersLibyan farmers and livestock producers will benefit from enhanced technical cooperation and the development of value chains for grains and livestock.

Libya hosts oil forum to boost investment

The Libya Oil Industry Localisation and Investment Forum will bring together 200 government and private sector representatives in Tripoli to foster partnerships.

The Libyan Council for Oil and Gas and Renewable Energy (LCOGRE) is organising the Libya Oil Industry Localisation and Investment Forum in Tripoli from October 25 to 26. The event is set to take place at a leading hotel in the capital.

LCOGRE Chairman Khaled Ben Othman stated that the forum aims to strengthen strategic partnerships and support the localisation of oil sector industries and services. He expects approximately 200 participants from both government and private sectors to attend.

The broader objectives of the forum include developing the Libyan economy, opening investment horizons, and facilitating the transfer of experiences and technologies to the country. This initiative is led by the private sector lobby association, LCOGRE.

Why it mattersThe forum aims to develop the Libyan economy and open investment horizons, potentially impacting businesses and employment.

Libya parliament ratifies Turkey maritime deal

The House of Representatives voted to approve a memorandum of understanding with Turkey on maritime jurisdiction areas in the Mediterranean Sea.

Libya's House of Representatives has ratified a memorandum of understanding with Turkey concerning the delimitation of maritime jurisdiction areas in the Mediterranean Sea. The decision was made during a session in Benghazi chaired by Speaker Al-Salheen Abdel-Nabi. The vote followed a briefing from a committee established to address Libya's land and maritime borders.

In addition to ratifying the Turkey deal, the parliament unanimously revoked two previous resolutions. Resolution No. (1) of 2020 had rejected the ratification of similar memorandums between the Tripoli-based Government of National Accord and Turkey, while Resolution No. (6) of 2020 had called for severing political ties with Türkiye. The House also voted to form a committee to engage with the Supreme Judicial Council.

Discussions on establishing a Libyan authority for emergency management were postponed. The House also considered amendments to the Cybercrime Law, referring the proposal back to the Legislative Committee for redrafting. A draft law on restrictions for possessing Libyan currency was also sent to the Finance Committee for input before further legislative review.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are affected by the parliament's decision to ratify a maritime deal with Turkey, which redefines their country's maritime jurisdiction areas.
