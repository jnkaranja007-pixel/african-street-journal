National Mining Corporation Explores Sustainable Energy Transition

Libya's National Mining Corporation engaged in discussions on integrating clean energy sources into mining operations.

Representatives from the National Mining Corporation (NMC) attended a national workshop yesterday focused on transforming Libya's energy sector towards sustainable systems. The event, organized by the Renewable Energy Authority of Libya (REAoL) and supported by the United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Western Asia (ESCWA), brought together various national and international entities to explore sustainable energy integration across different industries.

The NMC's delegation included directors from its Investment and Companies Department and Mining Department. They participated in discussions about sustainable energy trends and their impact on economic and productive sectors, particularly the mining industry. The corporation stated its participation underscores a commitment to strengthening partnerships and aligning with global energy transitions.

Through this engagement, the NMC aims to advance the mining sector's role in Libya's sustainable development and clean energy initiatives. The corporation also seeks to foster a sustainable investment environment and utilize clean energy sources in its ongoing and future mining activities and projects.

Why it mattersThe National Mining Corporation is exploring sustainable energy to develop the mining sector and create a cleaner investment environment.

Libya Official Urges Implementation of '4+4' Agreement

Ashraf Buduwara, a member of the Structured Dialogue, called for a new phase of political responsibility to implement the recently signed '4+4' agreement.

Ashraf Buduwara, a member of Libya's Structured Dialogue, has called for the nation to enter a new phase of political responsibility by implementing the "4+4" agreement. He stressed the importance of creating an environment conducive to the agreement's execution, aiming to bridge the existing political divide and unify state institutions.

Buduwara's remarks, made in a post on his social media, highlight a push for practical steps following the agreement's signing. The focus is on moving beyond past divisions and working towards a cohesive governmental structure that can effectively serve the country. This initiative seeks to foster stability and progress through unified action.

The call for implementation signals a desire to consolidate political efforts and move forward from prolonged periods of division. By focusing on the "4+4" agreement, Libyan officials hope to establish a clear path for institutional reform and national reconciliation, addressing the immediate needs for governance and stability.

Court convicts ten in diesel and food smuggling ring

Zawia Criminal Court sentenced a Libyan national and nine expatriates to lengthy prison terms for economic sabotage.

A Libyan national and nine expatriates have been convicted by the Zawia Criminal Court for smuggling diesel fuel and essential food commodities out of the country. The Attorney General’s Office reported that the group operated from the vicinity of Zawia, engaging in activities deemed detrimental to the national economy.

The Public Prosecution charged the ten individuals with forming a criminal group to manage the illicit trade. In its final ruling, the court imposed sentences ranging from fifteen to eighteen years in prison for the defendants.

This conviction highlights ongoing efforts to combat economic crimes and smuggling operations within Libya. The sentences aim to deter future illicit activities that impact national resources and trade.

Why it mattersTen individuals, including a Libyan national and nine expatriates, were convicted for smuggling diesel and food, impacting the national economy.

Power cut cancels children's heart surgeries at Tripoli hospital

Open heart surgeries and cardiac catheterization for children were cancelled today due to power cuts and insufficient generator fuel at Tripoli University Hospital.

Open heart surgeries and cardiac catheterization for children were cancelled today at Tripoli University Hospital due to power cuts. The hospital administration could not guarantee a stable power supply, and generators lacked adequate diesel for uninterrupted operation. The hospital's bypass machine also has no batteries, further complicating emergency procedures.

A visiting US medical team, the Novick Cardiac Alliance, was left frustrated by the cancellations. The team, which has been working with Libyan medical staff for two weeks performing these complex procedures, had planned to conduct two more operations today after successfully completing eighteen previously. The cancellations have caused significant stress for parents awaiting these critical surgeries.

The Novick Cardiac Alliance, led by William M Novick and Paul Nemir Jr. from the University of Tennessee Health Science Center, has been collaborating with Libyan teams in the Department of Paediatric Cardiology and Paediatric Cardiac Surgery. Their work aims to provide life-saving interventions for children with cardiac conditions.

Why it mattersChildren requiring open heart surgeries and cardiac catheterization are affected by the cancellation of these procedures due to power outages and equipment limitations.

Sirte Oil Restores Gas Turbine Unit After Gearbox Replacement

The Second Gas Turbine unit at the Petrochemicals Power Plant is back online, ensuring continuous electricity supply for industrial operations.

Sirte Oil and Gas Production and Processing Company announced the successful return to service of the Second Gas Turbine unit at its Petrochemicals Power Plant. Maintenance teams replaced the unit's gearbox, a key step in ongoing efforts to enhance equipment readiness and ensure the continuous operation of the industrial complex's power system. This restoration is expected to provide the necessary electricity to support ongoing operations and production with improved efficiency and reliability.

The company stated that this maintenance achievement highlights the technical teams' capabilities in performing complex refurbishment work with high precision. This success bolsters the readiness of Sirte Oil's units and facilities, directly supporting the company's strategic plans to maintain stable operational and production activities across its complex.

The Petrochemicals Power Plant's operational status is crucial for the industrial complex's output. The successful maintenance of the gas turbine unit ensures a stable power supply, which is fundamental for uninterrupted production processes and the overall efficiency of the facility.
