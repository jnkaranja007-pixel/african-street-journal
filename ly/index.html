Libyan Factions Sign Electoral Framework Deal

The UN-facilitated '4+4' Mini-dialogue Committee agreement sets a two-year timeline for national elections and establishes a framework for presidential and legislative polls.

Libyan parties have signed an agreement on the country's electoral framework, a development welcomed by the United Nations Support Mission in Libya (UNSMIL). The deal, reached through the UN-facilitated Smaller Convening, addresses key milestones for national elections, including the reconstitution of the High National Elections Commission's Board of Commissioners and the resolution of constitutional and legal issues.

The agreement outlines arrangements for presidential and legislative elections to be held under a single executive authority. It commits signatories to working towards holding these elections within a maximum of 24 months. Furthermore, the framework includes provisions for national endorsement by the House of Representatives and the High Council of State within one month, alongside a follow-up mechanism to ensure its implementation.

Walid Al-Lafi, head of the Government of National Unity’s delegation to the ‘4+4’ Committee, described the signed agreement as a significant breakthrough after more than a decade of political deadlock. He stated that the deal provides a crucial legal and organizational structure to overcome obstacles hindering the path to national elections.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are affected by this agreement as it provides a legal and organizational framework to address key obstacles to national elections, potentially ending a decade of deadlock.

Libya Receives Fuel Tankers, Easing Supply Crisis

Twelve of sixteen August fuel tankers have arrived, with the remaining four discharging at Libyan ports, signaling an end to the fuel shortage.

Libya's fuel crisis is easing as the Brega Petroleum Marketing Company reports significant improvements in supply. Ahmed Al-Masallati, the company's spokesperson, stated that twelve out of sixteen fuel tankers scheduled for August have been received. The remaining four tankers are currently at Libyan ports and are discharging their cargo. Congestion at petrol stations is expected to decrease starting tonight, with supply operations anticipated to return to normal by Monday.

Al-Masallati attributed the recent fuel shortages to a combination of global shipping disruptions, including those in the Strait of Hormuz, and local issues. He cited reduced production at the Zawia refinery, caused by power outages and security conditions, as a contributing factor to the temporary drop in available fuel quantities. The company's operations and emergency teams are working around the clock to manage supplies and address any sudden shortages.

The overall fuel situation is now described as reassuring, a marked improvement compared to the recent past. The Brega Petroleum Marketing Company is actively working to ensure rapid alternatives and maintain distribution stability. This influx of fuel is expected to alleviate the pressure on consumers and restore normalcy to the energy supply chain across the country.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are directly affected by the easing fuel crisis, which is expected to reduce congestion at petrol stations and normalize supply.

Bank Employees Detained for Alleged Embezzlement of 9.9 Million Dinars

Libya's Public Prosecution ordered the detention of two Jumhouria Bank employees accused of stealing approximately 9.9 million dinars.

Two employees from Jumhouria Bank's Mizran and Mellitah branches are in detention following accusations of embezzling around 9.9 million dinars. The Public Prosecution's media office stated that investigators are examining transactions conducted at both locations.

The detention order comes after an investigation into financial activities at the bank branches. The Public Prosecution has not yet released further details regarding the specific transactions or the timeline of the alleged embezzlement.

This incident raises concerns about financial security within the banking sector. The Public Prosecution's ongoing investigation aims to determine the full extent of the alleged fraud and bring those responsible to justice.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the alleged embezzlement of 9.9 million dinars from Jumhouria Bank could impact confidence in the financial sector and potentially lead to increased regulatory scrutiny.

Libya Petroleum Company Continues 24-Hour Fuel Distribution

Brega Petroleum Marketing Company reports continuous fuel operations across Libya, aiming to alleviate summer shortages and power cut impacts.

Brega Petroleum Marketing Company announced that fuel receipt and distribution operations are ongoing around the clock at various sites throughout Libya. This effort aims to address the country's summer fuel crisis, which has been exacerbated by power outages. While some cities like Tripoli are experiencing improvements, other areas in both western and eastern Libya continue to face long queues at petrol stations.

Over the past 24 hours, distribution companies drew 11.955 million litres of petrol from Tripoli Seaport. The Zawia Oil Depot saw withdrawals of approximately 1.11 million litres of petrol and 773,400 litres of diesel. Additionally, 940,000 litres of petrol and 240,000 litres of diesel were supplied to 42 stations in the West Coast and Mountain regions, with the Southern Region receiving 750,000 litres.

In Misrata, the depot plans to distribute 5 million litres of petrol and 2 million litres of diesel on Friday, alongside 500,000 litres of diesel for the Misrata Power Plant, as part of its continuous 24-hour program. Meanwhile, the tanker MT SARIYAH arrived at Tobruk's Al-Hariga Port with 32,000 metric tons of petrol for the Eastern Region, supporting ongoing deliveries to Derna.

Why it mattersLibyan residents are affected by the ongoing fuel distribution efforts which aim to reduce long petrol queues and address shortages compounded by power cuts.

Egyptian and Libyan Chambers of Commerce Plan Permanent Expo

Officials from Egypt and Libya met to discuss establishing a permanent Egyptian product exhibition in Benghazi, aiming to boost trade and investment.

The Federation of Egyptian Industries and the Libyan Union of Chambers of Commerce in Benghazi have discussed plans to create a permanent exhibition for Egyptian goods in Libya. This initiative includes the development of warehouses and logistical support centers to enhance industrial, commercial, and investment cooperation between the two nations. The agreement also outlines an annual Egyptian-Libyan Industrial Integration Exhibition in Benghazi, with the first session tentatively scheduled for April 2027.

Hisham El-Gazzar, Head of the Exhibitions and Small Projects Committee of the Federation of Egyptian Industries, stated the agreement's goal is to foster industrial integration, facilitate technology transfer, and encourage joint projects and value chains. This aims to increase trade and investment flows and improve product competitiveness in both Libyan and Egyptian markets. Targeted sectors include building materials, food, chemicals, engineering, plastics, wood, furniture, clothing, textiles, leather, and shoes.

Saleh Al-Obaidi, Chairman of the Management Committee of the Libyan General Union of Chambers of Commerce in Benghazi, highlighted the vision of combining Egypt's industrial capacity with Libya's strategic location to create an economic hub for African markets. Libya is working to strengthen its role as a transit trade center for countries like Chad, Niger, Sudan, and Mali by improving storage, logistics, and utilizing free zones to facilitate product movement.

Why it mattersEgyptian and Libyan business groups are planning a permanent expo and annual exhibition to boost trade and investment, targeting sectors like manufacturing and consumer goods.
