Lawmaker Welcomes 4+4 Agreement, Hopes for Unified Institutions

Member of Parliament Essam Al-Jehani expressed optimism that the recently signed "4+4" committee agreement will alleviate suffering and lead to the unification of state.

Member of Parliament Essam Al-Jehani has welcomed the signing of the "4+4" committee agreement, expressing hope that outstanding obligations will be met. Al-Jehani stated on his official Facebook page that he desires to see the unification of state institutions, emphasizing the importance of bringing them together.

The agreement, referred to as the "4+4" committee agreement, was recently signed, and Al-Jehani's comments signal a positive reception from within the political sphere. His remarks highlight a focus on the potential for this agreement to address the suffering of the Libyan people.

Al-Jehani's statement underscores a broader aspiration for institutional cohesion in Libya. The unification of state institutions is presented as a key objective, with the "4+4" agreement seen as a potential step in that direction.

Power cuts cancel children's heart surgeries at Tripoli hospital

Visiting US medical teams and parents face frustration and stress as essential pediatric cardiac procedures are halted due to a lack of reliable electricity and generator fuel.

Open heart surgeries and cardiac catheterization for children were unexpectedly cancelled today at Tripoli University Hospital due to power cuts. Hospital administration could not guarantee a stable power supply, and generators lacked sufficient diesel fuel for uninterrupted operation. The hospital's bypass machine also lacks batteries, further complicating emergency procedures.

The cancellations have left a visiting US medical team, the Novick Cardiac Alliance, frustrated after successfully conducting 18 operations over the past two weeks. The team, from the University of Tennessee Health Science Center's Global Surgical Institute, was scheduled to perform two more critical procedures today alongside Libyan medical staff.

The situation has caused significant distress for parents whose children were awaiting these vital operations. The hospital's inability to maintain consistent power, even with backup generators, highlights critical infrastructure challenges impacting essential medical services for vulnerable young patients.

Why it mattersChildren requiring open heart surgeries and cardiac catheterization are directly affected by the cancellation of these procedures due to power outages.

NOC Discusses Training Enhancements with US Universities

The National Oil Corporation held a virtual meeting with US universities and the US Embassy to improve trainee competence and streamline deployment.

The National Oil Corporation (NOC) held an extensive virtual meeting with representatives from several prestigious US universities and a delegate from the US Embassy. The meeting focused on enhancing trainee competence and ensuring high standards in approved training programs. Discussions also covered arrangements to facilitate US visa procedures for targeted personnel.

Participants reviewed academic components, technical skill development, and operational steps for streamlining travel and enrollment. The NOC stated the meeting aligns with its strategy to develop its workforce and improve operational and technical performance in the oil and gas sector through academic partnerships.

The committee tasked with coordinating cooperation with US universities, led by the NOC’s General Manager of Human Resources Development, attended the meeting. The goal is to strengthen academic partnerships with top-tier global universities and educational institutions.

Why it mattersThe NOC is working to improve its workforce and operational performance by partnering with US universities for training and development.

Dabbashi: '4+4' Agreement is Best Outcome Amid Division

Former UN envoy Ibrahim Dabbashi stated the '4+4 Agreement' is a significant breakthrough for elections, despite divisions among Libyans.

Libya’s former ambassador to the UN, Ibrahim Dabbashi, has described the ‘4+4 Agreement’ as the best possible outcome for overcoming obstacles to elections. He stated on Facebook that the deal, brokered by the UN Support Mission in Libya, represents important progress in the country’s political crisis. Dabbashi noted that while some Libyans oppose the agreement, many citizens are focused on how the country is governed and ending daily hardships rather than specific political figures.

Dabbashi explained that opposition stems from fears of exclusion by certain figures or concerns that the deal extends the power of corrupt officials and delays progress. He characterized the agreement as a compromise that cannot fully satisfy every party, but a major breakthrough in overcoming barriers to presidential and parliamentary elections. He believes the deal is the best the UN could achieve given deep stakeholder divisions and foreign interference.

The former ambassador warned that the current situation could persist for years or lead to war, emphasizing that no alternative exists to change the ground reality or compel parties to relinquish control. Dabbashi urged societal unity and peaceful pressure for the agreement's implementation, advocating for unified authorities composed of capable, honest, and professional figures to build accountable institutions and restore state dignity.

Why it mattersLibyans are affected by the '4+4 Agreement' as it is presented as the best path forward to end daily hardships and establish unified, accountable governance.

Libya Electricity Grid Suffers Partial Blackout

Western Libya experienced a partial power outage in the early hours of this morning, despite recent assurances from the General Electricity Company of Libya.

The General Electricity Company of Libya (GECOL) announced a partial blackout affecting the western section of the electrical grid early today. This incident follows recent assurances from GECOL management that the worst of the country's power cuts were over, with a new power station nearing operation and peak summer demand expected to subside.

GECOL stated the outage occurred after a disturbance caused two major transmission circuits, Misrata/Al-Khoms 400kV and Bir Al-Ghanam/Al-Ruwais, to trip. This led to a significant frequency drop from 50 Hz to 47.5 Hz, indicating a loss of balance between electricity generation and demand. The company is investigating whether the cause was technical faults or deliberate sabotage.

A thorough technical investigation into the protection system logs is underway to determine the exact cause of the circuit trips. GECOL has pledged to restore system stability and will transparently disclose the findings to pursue legal measures against any interference with these vital national security assets.

Why it mattersWestern Libya's residents are affected by a partial power outage, disrupting daily life and economic activities.
