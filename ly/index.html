Libyan inflation hits 16 percent

Purchasing power of the Libyan dinar and people's savings have declined by the same rate.

Libyan inflation reached an average of 16 percent in September 2026 compared to the previous year, according to data from the Bureau of Statistics and Census. This means the purchasing power of the Libyan dinar and people's wealth have declined by 16 percent.

Dr. Mohamed Al-Manfi, Advisor to the Libyan Business Council (Eastern Region Branch), reported the figures on the council's social media. He questioned whether external or internal factors are driving this inflation, noting that Libya imports a large portion of its goods.

The House of Representatives has begun debating Libya's economic crisis, aiming to implement reforms. Inflation has been a significant concern for Libyans with fixed and low incomes.

Why it mattersLibyans' savings and the value of their currency have decreased by 16 percent.

Libyan government proposes lifting fuel subsidies

Parliament member Ali Al-Tikbali stated the proposal came from the government, not the House of Representatives.

Libya's government has put forward a proposal to remove fuel subsidies, a move that has not yet been debated by the House of Representatives. Member of Parliament Ali Al-Tikbali clarified that the initiative originated within the executive branch.

Al-Tikbali told Sputnik that Libya is currently facing a budget shortfall and financial difficulties. These challenges include problems in securing allocations for public sector salaries.

The parliament member's statement indicates a lack of discussion on this economic measure between the government and the legislative body.

Why it mattersLibyans may face higher fuel costs due to a government proposal to lift subsidies, impacting household budgets.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Libya central bank governor handover completed

Fawzi Abu Khazam officially received duties from former Governor Naji Issa at the CBL.

The Central Bank of Libya (CBL) announced on Tuesday that formal handover procedures for the Governor's office have been completed. Current Governor Fawzi Abu Khazam officially received the duties from former Governor Naji Issa. The ceremony included the signing of an official handover protocol to ensure the continuity of the bank's functions.

The proceedings were attended by members of the Board of Directors and department heads. Photographs showed the two governors shaking hands and the outgoing governor signing documents. The former governor was honoured for his contributions during his tenure.

The CBL stated the transition underscores a commitment to institutional governance and administrative stability. However, the installation of the new governor on October 22 was described as a forceful, coup-like action, with institutional niceties performed afterwards.

Why it mattersThe handover of the Central Bank of Libya's governorship affects the country's administrative stability and institutional governance.

Libyan ministry hosts seminar on Palestinian cause

A cultural seminar at the Benghazi Book Fair focused on documenting the Palestinian struggle and historical ties.

Libya's Ministry of Culture and Arts organized a cultural seminar titled “Palestine: A Never-Ending Story” on Saturday evening at the National Library in Benghazi. The event, part of the fifth Benghazi International Book Fair, was attended by members of the Palestinian community.

Journalist Mohamed Abdelhaq moderated discussions by Palestinian and Libyan activists and academics on documenting the Palestinian cause and its historical dimensions. Speakers noted Libyan support for the cause and emphasized fraternal ties between the two peoples.

Ministry representative Khaled Al-Omami said such seminars revive Arab causes and strengthen cultural bonds. The Palestinian consulate in Benghazi presented an honorary shield to Minister of Culture and Arts Dr. Salha Al-Drouqi for supporting cultural activities, and honored Omar Jumaa Haiba for his support of writers and Arab causes.

Why it mattersThe event was organized by the Libyan Ministry of Culture and Arts and involved Libyan activists and academics.

Business · 2026-11-09

Libya ministry discusses water plant with Hungary's Rotary

The Ministry of Industry and Minerals met with Rotary International Ltd. to follow up on establishing a modern water desalination plant.

Mustafa Al-Samu, Undersecretary of the Ministry of Industry and Minerals, met a delegation from Hungary’s Rotary International Ltd. at the ministry’s headquarters. The meeting followed previous discussions in Misrata and Tripoli regarding a proposal for a modern, eco-friendly water desalination plant in Libya. The discussions covered project updates, cooperation prospects, and the plant's importance for industrial water needs, infrastructure, and sustainable development.

The Undersecretary stated the Ministry is committed to serious investment, international partnerships, and technology transfer to develop Libya's industrial sector. On November 26, the Ministry signed a Memorandum of Understanding with Rotary International for joint infrastructure projects, including water desalination and purification.

Further meetings with Rotary International occurred on September 22 to study the company's proposal for the water desalination plant.

Why it mattersLibya's industrial sector and infrastructure development will be supported by a new water desalination plant.
