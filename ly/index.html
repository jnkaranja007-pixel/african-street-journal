Chevron secures Libya oil exploration deal in Sirte Basin

Libya's National Oil Corporation (NOC) and US energy major Chevron signed a production-sharing agreement for exploration acreage in the Sirte Basin, advancing a key award from.

The National Oil Corporation (NOC) and Chevron signed a production-sharing agreement on Monday, August 24, for exploration in Area 106, also known as S4, within the Sirte Basin. This agreement follows Chevron's selection in February as the successful bidder for this onshore area during Libya's 2025 exploration bid round, marking Chevron's entry into Libya's upstream sector.

NOC Chairman Masoud Suleman stated the agreement is an important step for hydrocarbon resource exploration and development, strengthening cooperation with major international energy companies. The deal provides a contractual framework for Chevron to begin exploration activities in the 7,437-square-kilometer area, which is Libya's most prolific oil-producing region.

NOC data from the bidding round identified a gas discovery within Area 106, with estimated 2P reserves of about 100 million barrels of oil equivalent, alongside other exploration leads. This agreement builds on previous cooperation, including a January memorandum of understanding to assess onshore opportunities and a March agreement for a technical assessment of offshore Block NC146.

Why it mattersThis agreement directly impacts Libya's energy sector by bringing a major international company to explore and potentially develop new oil and gas resources, which could affect national revenue and employment.

GECOL investigates power station sabotage, warns of total blackout risk

Libya's General Electricity Company (GECOL) has launched an investigation into suspected sabotage at multiple power substations, including an explosion at substation 16 in Sug Il.

GECOL is investigating suspected sabotage at several power stations, including an explosion at substation 16 in Sug Il Juma. The company stated that tampering with the grid could lead to a total blackout. This follows incidents where on-duty personnel were forcibly removed and stations shut down, causing automatic line disconnections that required security intervention to restore.

The company emphasized that any interference with its infrastructure directly threatens grid stability and citizen safety. GECOL warned that attempts to restore power independently could cause equipment fires, units and lines to go offline, and ultimately lead to a complete grid collapse. The transformer on Route 16 caught fire, which GECOL cited as an example of the dangers of uncoordinated power restoration.

GECOL clarified that restoring electricity is a precise technical process, not a simple switch-flipping operation, designed to protect the grid and equipment. The company works around the clock to maintain grid stability and urges the public to avoid electrical installations and refrain from interfering with power disconnection and restoration efforts.

Why it mattersThe stability of the national power grid directly affects all citizens and businesses that rely on electricity for daily life and operations.

Libyan Attorney General uncovers 63,986 forged national ID numbers

Libyan authorities have identified tens of thousands of forged national identification numbers, with an additional 88,819 incorrect numbers also under scrutiny, according to.

The Attorney General's Office reported 63,986 forged national numbers lacking legitimate civil registry basis, alongside 88,819 incorrect national numbers with other irregularities. These figures were announced by Attorney General Al-Siddiq Al-Sour on August 17, 2022, as part of ongoing investigations into widespread identity fraud that intensified from 2017.

Investigations revealed that a single dormant family record, a deceased person’s name, or a bribed civil servant could facilitate the issuance of national ID numbers, passports, jobs, grants, and voting rights. One case involved a foreign national paying 10,000 dinars to a deceased man's father to assume his identity, leading to marriage and four children in 2014.

Further audits, including one by the Social Solidarity Fund announced August 11, 2026, found 6,954 forged national IDs used to withdraw more than three million dinars in pensions over five years. These financial reviews may overlap with other cases and are not simply added to the official total of forged numbers.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are affected by the potential for identity theft to infiltrate families, create legal uncertainties for marriages and children, and compromise the integrity of public services and financial benefits.

Libyan Experts Forum proposes national strategy for maritime transport

The first Libyan Experts Forum, held in Tripoli, concluded with a proposal to develop a national strategy for maritime transport and logistics, aiming to transform Libya into a.

The Ministry of Labour and Rehabilitation organized the first Libyan Experts Forum in Tripoli from August twenty-third to twenty-fifth. The event gathered Libyan experts and professionals from within the country and twenty-six other nations to discuss leveraging international knowledge for national development.

Abdalla Wanis Al-Tarhouni, an expert in maritime transport economics, stated that Libya's strategic location, long coastline, and capable ports offer significant opportunities for regional leadership in maritime transport and logistics. He explained that developing these assets could transform Libya from a transit country into an active logistics hub connecting Europe, Africa, and Mediterranean markets.

Al-Tarhouni emphasized the need for a long-term national strategy, developed with government, private sector, and expert participation. This strategy would shift from viewing ports as mere entry points to building an integrated logistics system, including port development, transportation networks, free zones, digital services, and human capital development.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects Libyan businesses and the national economy by proposing a strategy to enhance the country's role in regional trade and logistics.

Central Bank Governor Urges Policy Integration for Economic Stability

Central Bank of Libya Governor Nagy Issa emphasized the need for monetary and fiscal policy integration to support economic and financial stability, calling for stronger.

Nagy Issa, Governor of the Central Bank of Libya, recently highlighted the critical importance of integrating monetary and fiscal policies to bolster the nation's economic and financial stability. He stressed that this integration is essential for a robust economic environment.

Issa called for enhanced coordination among various state institutions to achieve this goal. He explained that effective integration requires close monitoring of both government revenue and public spending, ensuring that financial flows are managed efficiently.

The Governor also noted that implementing fiscal policy measures and prioritizing key initiatives are vital components of this integrated approach. These steps are intended to create a more stable and predictable economic landscape for Libya, according to Issa.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are affected as the integration of monetary and fiscal policies aims to improve the overall economic and financial stability of the country, which can impact their daily lives and financial well-being.
