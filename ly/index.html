UN extends Libya mission until October 2027

Security Council resolution backs efforts to unify state institutions and hold elections within two years.

The United Nations Security Council has extended its political mission in Libya until October 31, 2027. The resolution supports efforts to unify state institutions and establish a single executive authority. It also urges Libyan parties to commit to holding national elections within 24 months.

Resolution 2835, adopted on Friday, October 30, renews support for Hanna Tetteh, the UN secretary-general’s special representative. Britain led negotiations on the text, which supports the “4+4” process agreement reached on August 30. This agreement provides for changes to the electoral framework and leadership of the High National Elections Commission.

The Council calls for a broader UN presence across Libya, including in Benghazi, Sabha, and Sirte, with greater engagement in the east and south. A dedicated economic section will address fuel smuggling, diversion of public funds, and illicit exploitation of state resources, stressing that petroleum resources belong to all Libyans.

Libya and Italy sign economic cooperation deal

Tripoli Chamber of Commerce and the Joint Italian-Arab Chamber of Commerce agreed to boost trade and investment.

The Tripoli Chamber of Commerce signed a memorandum of understanding with the Joint Italian-Arab Chamber of Commerce on October 29 in Milan, Italy. The agreement aims to strengthen economic and commercial cooperation between Libya and Italy. Anwar Abu Sitta, Chairman of the Tripoli Chamber, and Pietro Rompino, President of the JIACC, signed the deal.

The memorandum seeks to foster private sector economic relations, encourage joint ventures, and facilitate communication between Libyan and Italian companies. Cooperation will cover sectors including energy, infrastructure, agriculture, pharmaceuticals, and digital services. The agreement also focuses on developing young entrepreneurs through training and expertise exchange.

Future cooperation includes organizing a Libyan-Italian Business Forum alternately in each country, facilitating business-to-business and business-to-government meetings, and encouraging participation in trade exhibitions. This initiative was part of the 5th Arab-Italian Economic Forum.

Why it mattersLibyan businesses and entrepreneurs will benefit from expanded partnership opportunities, investment, and trade with Italian companies.

Libyan PM discusses market instability with traders

Abdel Hamid Aldabaiba met with traders and chambers of commerce in Misrata to address commodity prices and import regulations.

Libyan Prime Minister Abdel Hamid Aldabaiba met with traders and representatives from chambers of commerce and industry today in the Misrata Free Zone. The meeting focused on rising commodity prices, mechanisms for opening documentary credits, the market dollar exchange rate, and the customs dollar.

Discussions also covered the Minister of Economy and Trade's decision to limit import operations to banking channels and an evaluation of economic decisions regulating imports. Aldabaiba stressed the need for balanced measures to ensure goods availability, control price increases, and maintain citizens' purchasing power.

The meeting was attended by several ministers, including the Minister of Economy and Trade, Suhail Abu Shiha, and the Minister of Transport, Mohamed Al-Shahoubi. Aldabaiba called for enhanced coordination between government agencies and the private sector to address market imbalances.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are affected by rising commodity prices and the availability of goods due to import regulations.

Libya limits insulin distribution to electronic system

New insulin shipments have arrived, but distribution will now only reach registered patients.

Libya's National Diabetes Authority will now only distribute insulin to patients registered in its electronic system. Awad Al-Quweri, the authority's head, announced this change, stating that insulin will not be dispensed outside this organized framework. Al-Quweri confirmed that three new shipments of insulin have arrived in the country.

Al-Quweri explained the new distribution policy to Al-Massar TV, emphasizing the move towards an electronic system for dispensing the vital medication. This measure aims to streamline access for those already enrolled in the digital registry. The arrival of three new batches offers a supply update for the nation's diabetic population.

The authority's announcement means that individuals not yet registered in the electronic system will face challenges in obtaining insulin. The specific quantity of insulin in the new shipments was not detailed. Further updates on the electronic system's capacity and registration process are expected.

Business · 2026-11-01

Armed group closes Libya oil pipeline valve

National Oil Corporation reports 15,000 barrels per day of crude oil supply to Zawia refinery disrupted.

An armed group closed a valve on the main Al-Hamada crude oil pipeline in the Al-Rayayna area today, disrupting supplies to the Zawia refinery.

The National Oil Corporation (NOC) stated that the forced closure of the 18-inch diameter pipeline caused a pressure spike, halting pumping operations by 12:30 AM last night to protect infrastructure.

The NOC warned that the continued closure will result in a loss of approximately 15,000 barrels per day of crude oil and called on the Petroleum Facilities Guard to reopen the valve.

Why it mattersLibya's national economy faces negative repercussions due to the disruption of crude oil supplies and potential threats to oil infrastructure.
