Libyan dialogue group seeks judicial unity for elections

London talks focus on resolving institutional divisions to enable credible presidential and parliamentary votes.

Libya's UN-facilitated 4+4 dialogue group met in London and agreed to consult more widely with the judiciary to resolve institutional divisions. Participants stated these divisions threaten the prospects for credible national elections.

The group, meeting under UN Special Representative Hanna Tetteh, identified judicial unity and independence as essential for holding presidential and parliamentary votes that would be accepted across political divides. Members committed to seeking political support for the electoral agreement signed in Tripoli on August 30 and will meet again in two weeks.

The August agreement addresses reconstituting the High National Elections Commission board and settling disputes over the constitutional and legal framework for polls. It envisages elections under a single executive authority within 24 months, though this remains a political commitment. The High Council of State has rejected the agreement, citing concerns about bypassing established rules.

Why it mattersLibyans face continued uncertainty regarding the timing and legitimacy of national elections due to ongoing institutional divisions.

Libya restarts Brega gas unit

Sirte Oil Company recovers 60 million cubic feet of gas daily that would otherwise be flared.

Sirte Oil Company has restarted a natural gas processing unit at Brega after years out of service, enabling Libya to recover gas that would otherwise be burnt off.

The National Oil Corporation announced the restart on October 25, stating the unit would redirect 60 million cubic feet of gas into the supply network. This action aims to reduce flaring and make more of the resource available for use, citing environmental protection as a benefit.

The announcement did not specify if the 60 million cubic feet figure represented a daily volume.

Why it mattersLibya's National Oil Corporation is recovering gas that would otherwise be flared, improving resource use.

Libyan professor links wage reform to spending control

Ali Al-Sharif stated that managing oil revenues transparently is key to closing wage gaps.

An economics professor from the University of Benghazi, Ali Al-Sharif, stated that reforming Libya's salaries requires controlling public spending and improving the governance of oil revenues. Al-Sharif told Al-Araby Al-Jadeed newspaper that rationalizing expenses and ensuring transparent collection and management of oil money are necessary steps. He said these measures are needed to close wage gaps in a sustainable manner.

Al-Sharif, who teaches at the University of Benghazi, emphasized the need for transparency in how oil revenues are managed. He indicated that controlling spending is a prerequisite for any successful wage reform. The professor's comments were made in an interview with Al-Araby Al-Jadeed newspaper.

The professor's remarks focus on the financial and governance aspects of salary imbalances. He did not provide specific figures for wage gaps or spending targets. The next step would involve the government implementing policies based on such economic advice.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens' salaries and public spending are directly affected by the proposed economic reforms.

France warns citizens against travel to most of Libya

French authorities advise against travel to Tripoli due to volatile security, armed clashes, and crime risks.

French authorities have issued a travel advisory against visiting most of Libya, including the capital, Tripoli. Travel to Misrata, Sirte, and Benghazi is only recommended for absolutely essential purposes.

The updated French travel guidance highlights volatile security conditions, ongoing armed clashes, and a high risk of kidnapping and general crime. French citizens in Libya are urged to adopt strict security measures.

Consular services for French citizens in Libya will continue to be managed from Tunisia, even though the French Embassy in Tripoli reopened on March 29.

LAICO approves changes to protect overseas assets

Libyan African Investment Company's general assembly reviewed business plans and adopted new regulations on October 29.

The Libyan African Investment Company (LAICO) approved changes to its structure and regulations on Thursday, October 29, to safeguard overseas assets. The general assembly met at the Libya Africa Investment Portfolio’s (LAIP) headquarters in Tripoli to review LAICO’s business plan and future strategy.

LAICO members adopted a revised organisational structure and amendments to administrative, financial, and investment regulations. They also ratified financial statements for 2014–2020 and reviewed those for 2021–2025, endorsing an external audit firm and its fees.

The assembly assessed progress on resolutions from 2019 and 2021 meetings. LAICO’s business plan was considered within LAIP’s objectives to improve management efficiency and support sustained financial performance.

Why it mattersLibyan investors will see changes to how overseas assets are managed and protected.
