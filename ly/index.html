Libyan publishers face rising book costs

Imported paper and fluctuating exchange rates drive up production expenses for local bookmakers.

Libyan publishers are grappling with increased book production costs due to reliance on imported paper and fluctuating foreign currency exchange rates. These economic pressures are affecting both the local publishing industry and the affordability of books for Libyan readers.

Ali Jaber, manager of Al-Jaber Publishing, stated that printing costs are similar across Libya, Egypt, and Tunisia because all presses use imported paper. He explained that publishers often price books based on the US dollar's parallel market rate, as they cannot open official letters of credit for imports. Producing a book that cost $5 (7.5 dinars) in 2010 now costs nearly 50 dinars.

While Libyan titles are generally priced lower than imported Arab books, ranging from 30 to 50 dinars, rising costs have reduced publisher revenues. Shipping books from Cairo to Benghazi can be cheaper than between some Libyan cities, costing about 100 dinars per parcel, though cross-border shipments can take up to 45 days.

Why it mattersLibyan readers face higher prices for books due to increased production costs driven by imported materials and exchange rate volatility.

Libya central bank governor takes legal oath

Fawzi Abu Khazam swore his oath before the House of Representatives to assume duties.

Fawzi Abu Khazam, the new Governor of the Central Bank of Libya, took his legal oath before the Speaker of the House of Representatives and several members today. This action fulfills legal requirements for him to begin his official duties, following his appointment by a House resolution on November 2, 2026.

The Central Bank of Libya stated this step is for strengthening monetary and financial stability and consolidating confidence in the banking sector. The bank aims to develop monetary policy instruments and enhance cooperation with state institutions to support the country's economic stability.

The CBL reaffirmed its commitment to carrying out its mandated functions, working to enhance the efficiency of the banking sector, and developing financial and banking services in compliance with governing laws and regulations.

Why it mattersThe new governor's oath-taking is intended to strengthen Libya's monetary and financial stability and support the country's economic stability.

Libya allows imports using parallel market currency

The Ministry of Economy and Trade issued a decree permitting temporary use of foreign currency from the parallel market for goods importation until March 31, 2027.

Libya's Ministry of Economy and Trade announced on April 11, 2026, that goods can be imported using foreign currency from the parallel market. Minister Suhail Abu Shiha issued Decree No. (555) of 2026, allowing this on a temporary basis until March 31, 2027, subject to legal regulations.

The ministry stated this U-turn aims to regulate import operations and oversee trade financing, excluding certain essential goods and medicines that require prior approval. This decision follows previous attempts to restrict imports through bank payments, which were previously reversed.

The current system of documentary letters of credit is seen as unequal, dominated by a few large businesses that obtain most hard currency. These businesses are perceived to be in collusion with bank officials, leaving small and medium-sized enterprises unable to secure funding through official channels and forcing them to use the parallel market.

Why it mattersSmall and medium-sized businesses in Libya are affected because they are forced to use the parallel market for import funding.

Libyan candidate warns against canceling school year

Fadhel Al-Ameen accuses authorities of negligence in education and healthcare, risking the nation's future.

Presidential candidate Fadhel Al-Ameen has warned against canceling Libya's school year, criticizing what he calls neglect in education and healthcare sectors. He stated on Facebook that this disregard puts the country's future at risk.

Al-Ameen questioned if Libyans would allow children to lose an academic year, attributing the crisis to a lack of responsible leadership rather than funds. He pointed to teachers' strikes and healthcare worker protests as evidence of authorities failing basic duties despite having resources.

He described neglecting education and healthcare as an "unforgivable crime and a grave betrayal." Al-Ameen called for accountability for officials controlling public funds and depriving citizens of essential services, stating those unable to govern should step down.

Why it mattersLibyan children and grandchildren risk losing an academic year due to official negligence in education.

Business · 2026-11-05

German ambassador discusses trade with Misrata Chamber

Libyan businesspeople could see easier visa access as Germany offers expertise.

The German Ambassador to Libya, Arvid Anders, met with the Misrata Chamber of Commerce today at its Misrata headquarters. He was accompanied by Dominik Lauf-Wilhelm, First Secretary for Political Affairs. The Chamber's Chairman, Fathi Al-Amin Al-Turki, and Yousef Abu Shaiba, Director of the Chamber’s Office of External Relations, welcomed the delegation.

Discussions focused on strengthening economic and commercial cooperation between Libya and Germany. The parties explored ways to use German expertise in various economic and industrial sectors. They also addressed facilitating visa procedures for Libyan businesspeople and resolving challenges faced by Chamber members.

Al-Turki highlighted the importance of increasing cooperation and opening new channels for communication and partnership between the business communities of both nations. The meeting aimed to foster broader avenues for collaboration.

Why it mattersLibyan businesspeople may face fewer obstacles when seeking to travel to Germany for commercial purposes.
