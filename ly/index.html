Libya and Indonesia sign trade deal

A cooperation agreement aims to boost private sector trade and direct commercial ties between the two nations.

Libya and Indonesia signed a cooperation agreement on October 20 in Semarang, Indonesia, to facilitate trade between their nations. The deal aims to foster direct cooperation between private-sector companies from both countries.

Business leaders from Libya and Indonesia attended the signing ceremony, which was preceded by bilateral B2B meetings. The Central Java region, where Semarang is located, is a key industrial and export hub for products like textiles, footwear, and food items. This agreement follows the Libyan Business Forum held on October 16th in Jakarta.

At the Jakarta forum, over 12 letters of intent for commercial deals valued at US$1.8 million were signed. The Libyan Business Council is now working with Indonesian economic entities to develop trade exchange opportunities and convert these letters into actionable agreements.

Why it mattersLibyan and Indonesian businesses will benefit from facilitated trade and direct commercial ties.

Libya's NOC reviews Southern Refinery project progress

The National Oil Corporation met with Zallaf Libya to track completion stages and discuss challenges for 2027 targets.

Libya's National Oil Corporation (NOC) and Zallaf Libya Oil & Gas Co. met yesterday in Sebha to review the Southern Refinery project's progress. Officials discussed completion stages, technical challenges, and ways to accelerate work towards 2027 targets.

The meeting, held at Zallaf Libya's headquarters, assessed the project's current status, implementation phases, and financial requirements. Discussions also covered fuel storage and distribution systems to integrate the refinery into the national oil sector.

The project aims to stabilize domestic fuel supplies, reduce import reliance, and bolster energy security. Representatives from the NOC's General Directorate for Oil Industries and Zallaf Libya's board attended the review.

Why it mattersLibya's energy security and domestic fuel supply are directly affected by the Southern Refinery project.

Libya speaker discusses economic plan

Salehin Abdel Nabi met with the head of the National Planning Council to discuss reducing public spending.

Speaker of the House of Representatives Salehin Abdel Nabi met Thursday in Benghazi with Ahmed Khalifa Breidan, head of the National Planning Council. They discussed an emergency economic plan for Libya intended to reduce public spending and safeguard public funds.

The officials also considered revising the exchange rate of the US dollar against the Libyan dinar. This meeting focused on protecting public funds through spending cuts.

The National Planning Council will hold a meeting of experts within 10 days to further discuss the economic plan.

Why it mattersLibyan officials are discussing an economic plan to reduce public spending and adjust the exchange rate.

UNSMIL warns against forceful CBL leadership change

The UN mission stresses operational independence and integrity of the Central Bank of Libya are essential for economic stability.

The United Nations Support Mission in Libya (UNSMIL) stated today that it notes developments regarding the House of Representatives Presidency's decision on the Central Bank of Libya (CBL) leadership. This follows the forceful installation yesterday of Fawzi Abu Khazam as a new interim CBL Governor.

UNSMIL emphasized that the CBL's operational independence and integrity are essential to protect public resources, maintain economic stability, and ensure credibility with international financial partners. Any leadership changes must be driven by a commitment to improved governance and oversight to maintain confidence in the management of monetary policy and reserves.

The mission warned that continued pressure on the CBL to finance escalating public expenditure is unsustainable and could intensify exchange rate pressures, fuel inflation, and deplete reserves, potentially inflicting increased economic hardship and social unrest. UNSMIL urged all stakeholders to follow Libyan legal procedures to protect the CBL's credibility.

Why it mattersUNSMIL warns that forceful changes at the Central Bank of Libya could lead to economic hardship and social unrest for the Libyan people.

Fawzi Abu Khazem takes charge as Libya's acting central bank governor

The House of Representatives appointed Fawzi Abu Khazem, with endorsement from the High Council of State.

Fawzi Abu Khazem officially became the acting governor of the Central Bank of Libya on Thursday. The House of Representatives made the appointment, which received endorsement from the High Council of State. A handover ceremony occurred with members from both legislative bodies present.

The Central Bank of Libya assured citizens and international partners of its continued services and commitment fulfillment. This assurance follows the official assumption of duties by the new acting governor.

The appointment marks a change in leadership at the central bank. The bank stated its intention to maintain operational continuity and uphold its financial obligations.

Why it mattersLibyans and international partners rely on the Central Bank of Libya for continued services and fulfillment of commitments.
