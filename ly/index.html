Libyan Officials Welcome 4+4 Agreement, Seek Unified Institutions

Prime Minister Aldabaiba and MP Al-Jehani expressed optimism for the agreement's implementation and the unification of state institutions.

Tripoli-based Prime Minister Abdel Hamid Aldabaiba has welcomed the signing of an agreement by the "4+4" Mini-Dialogue Committee, brokered by UNSMIL. Member of Parliament Essam Al-Jehani also endorsed the accord, expressing hope that remaining obligations will be met. Al-Jehani, writing on his official Facebook page, voiced a desire to see the unification of state institutions and emphasized the importance of bringing them together.

The "4+4" agreement signifies a step toward alleviating the suffering of the Libyan people, according to Al-Jehani. He expressed his desire for the fulfillment of outstanding commitments in the upcoming phase, signaling a focus on continued progress and implementation of the accord's provisions.

The agreement, facilitated by the United Nations Support Mission in Libya (UNSMIL), marks a development in the ongoing efforts to stabilize the country. The focus now shifts to the practical execution of the agreed-upon terms and the subsequent unification of governmental bodies.

Why it mattersThe agreement and subsequent calls for unified institutions may impact the stability and governance of Libya, affecting all citizens.

Libya Power Grid Suffers Partial Blackout After Transmission Circuit Trips

Western Libya experienced a partial power outage in the early hours of the morning following the tripping of two major transmission circuits.

The General Electricity Company of Libya (GECOL) announced a partial blackout affecting the western section of the electrical grid early this morning. This incident follows recent assurances from GECOL management that the worst of the country's power cuts was over, with a new power station nearing operation and peak summer demand expected to subside with the arrival of September.

GECOL stated the sudden outage was caused by a disturbance that led to the tripping of two major transmission circuits, the Misrata/Al-Khoms 400kV and Bir Al-Ghanam/Al-Ruwais. This resulted in a significant frequency drop from 50 Hz to 47.5 Hz, indicating a loss of balance between electricity generation and demand on the grid.

A technical investigation is underway to determine if the circuit trips were due to technical faults or deliberate sabotage. GECOL has pledged to restore system stability and will disclose the investigation findings to the public to pursue legal action against any perpetrators. The company emphasized the critical nature of these facilities for national security.

Why it mattersWestern Libya's residents are affected by a partial power outage, disrupting daily life and economic activities.

Libyan Express Launches Three New European Flights

The privately owned carrier will begin direct service to Malta, Rome, and Milan on September 6, expanding travel options for Libyan passengers.

Libyan Express, a private airline, announced it will launch three new direct flights to European destinations: Malta, Rome, and Milan, starting September 6. The airline described the launch as a significant step, opening broader horizons for Libyan travelers. This expansion is part of a strategic partnership with Aeroitalia and 4Airways, aiming to provide customers with enhanced comfort and a wider selection of travel choices.

These new routes stem from cooperation agreements signed earlier this year. In May, Libyan Express finalized a partnership in Italy with Horizon Technic, alongside Italian entities ALBA Technic and 4Airways. The collaboration focuses on strengthening air transport and aircraft maintenance in line with European standards.

The agreements encompass two key areas: a joint air route between Libya and Europe operated by Libyan Express and 4 Airways, and cooperation between Horizon Technic and ALBA Technic for EASA-certified aircraft maintenance services at Misrata Airport. These initiatives underscore a shared vision for international expansion and bolstering Libyan companies' presence in the aviation sector.

Why it mattersLibyan travelers will have new direct flight options to Malta, Rome, and Milan, increasing their access to European destinations.

Rosgeologia Explores Sahara for Underground Water in Libya

Russia's state geological company is deploying advanced techniques to locate deep aquifers, a move that could stabilize the nation's water infrastructure.

Russia's state geological company, Rosgeologia, is actively searching for underground water sources within Libya's Sahara Desert, according to Russian Natural Resources Minister Aleksandr Kozlov. This initiative represents a significant development for Libya's water sector, which is critically stressed due to the country's lack of permanent rivers and heavy reliance on deep desert aquifers.

The exploration involves advanced geological mapping and exploration techniques aimed at identifying untapped, deep aquifers beneath the desert. This effort is seen as a crucial alternative or supplementary strategy for stabilizing Libya's water infrastructure, especially considering that over 70% of the population depends on non-renewable fossil water. A previous project in 2011, which involved concrete pipe production for a river near Brega, was disrupted by a NATO airstrike.

This Russian-led exploration aligns with broader regional resource management efforts, including Libya's recent signing of the Tripoli Declaration with Algeria and Tunisia. The declaration aims for the equitable sharing of the North-Western Sahara Aquifer System, underscoring the growing international focus on securing vital water resources in the arid region.

Why it mattersThis exploration directly impacts Libya's water sector by seeking new groundwater reserves to supplement the nation's water supply.

Al-Brega Oil Company Pumps Fuel from Sebha Depot

The company announced continued fuel pumping and delivery operations from the Sebha depot to southern Libya following new shipments.

Al-Brega Oil Marketing Company is continuing to load and deliver fuel from the Sebha depot to distribution stations across southern Libya. The company's board of directors issued instructions for these ongoing operations, which are supported by the recent arrival of new fuel shipments.

The company's media office confirmed that fuel is being supplied to various cities and regions in the southern part of the country. This action aims to ensure the availability of fuel supplies in these areas.

The specific volume of fuel being pumped and delivered was not detailed in the announcement, but the operations are described as continuous. The company stated that these efforts are in line with directives from its leadership.

Why it mattersSouthern Libyan regions are receiving fuel supplies, which could impact local transportation and economic activities.
