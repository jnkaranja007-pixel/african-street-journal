National Oil Corporation Condemns Drone Attack, Empties Fuel Tanks

The National Oil Corporation has raised emergency readiness and begun emptying fuel tanks after a drone attack on its Brega Airport Road Fuel Depot.

The National Oil Corporation (NOC) condemned a drone attack on its Brega Airport Road Fuel Depot this morning. In response, the corporation has raised the readiness of all emergency teams and begun emptying fuel from tanks as a precautionary measure. The NOC called for intensified efforts from security and military authorities to protect vital oil facilities.

The NOC stated that the attempt to target fuel tanks with a remotely operated drone caused no significant damage. However, the corporation stressed that such sabotage operations against oil facilities are a dangerous indicator and pose a real threat to citizens' lives and national wealth.

The corporation emphasized that targeting oil facilities anywhere in Libya directly threatens the supply system for power plants, health facilities, and daily citizen needs. It urged respect for the safety and sanctity of oil facilities and their workers to ensure continuity of fuel supply and energy security.

Why it mattersThe attack on the fuel depot and subsequent precautionary measures by the National Oil Corporation could disrupt fuel supply, affecting energy production and daily needs for citizens.

Libya, Japan Sign Health Cooperation Pact

A new memorandum of cooperation in the health sector will support training, expertise exchange, and the adoption of Japanese technologies for Libya.

Libyan Minister of Health, Mohamed Al-Ghouj, signed the first memorandum of cooperation in the health sector between Libya and Japan yesterday with his Japanese counterpart, Kenichiro Ueno. The agreement aims to bolster training, professional development, and the exchange of expertise. It also seeks to develop health institutions and services by leveraging Japanese technologies and experience to enhance Libya's healthcare capabilities.

During an official visit to Japan, Minister Al-Ghouj also engaged in discussions with various Japanese officials, including representatives from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA), and the Japanese parliament. These meetings focused on broadening areas of cooperation and securing support for priority health initiatives within Libya.

The visit also included a tour of the Ariake Cancer Institute Hospital, providing insights into the medical and specialized services offered, as well as the advanced technologies employed in diagnosing and treating tumors. This exposure is intended to inform the development of Libya's own health infrastructure and treatment protocols.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens will benefit from improved health services through training, expertise exchange, and the adoption of advanced Japanese medical technologies.

Libya Authorities Tackle LC Fraud Impacting Exchange Rates

A committee discussed oversight reports on documentary credit fraud and its effect on currency exchange rates, adopting measures to protect the economy.

Representatives from the Central Bank of Libya, Customs, and the Municipal Guard met to review reports detailing documentary credit (LC) fraud and its influence on exchange rates. The committee also noted low tax collection on imports, which did not exceed 3 billion dinars, and identified unregistered companies or those failing to settle their status.

To counter these issues, the committee resolved to implement firm measures, including identifying and blacklisting non-compliant companies, and expediting a unified electronic system to track goods and financial coverage. They will also coordinate with the Ministries of Economy and Industry to set reference prices for commodities and increase market inspections.

LC fraud has significantly increased since 2011, with companies exploiting the official exchange rate by importing lower-valued goods or never importing anything at all, then repatriating or selling the difference. Companies are required to present customs proof of import when opening an LC.

Why it mattersThe national economy is affected by LC fraud, which leads to the loss of foreign currency and impacts exchange rates.

Interior Ministry Closes 490 Petrol Stations for Fuel Smuggling

The Ministry of Interior has shut down 490 petrol stations accused of exploiting fuel for smuggling operations as part of a security plan.

Major General Imad Trabelsi, Minister of Interior and Chairman of the Fuel and Petrol Crisis Committee, announced the closure of 490 petrol stations involved in fuel smuggling. This action is a component of a coordinated security plan aimed at ensuring fuel availability for citizens. The announcement came during a press conference attended by committee members, oil company representatives, and various security and supervisory authorities.

Trabelsi stated that the ongoing fuel crisis necessitates a unified effort from the government, Ministry of Interior, oil sector, companies, security agencies, regulatory bodies, and citizens. In addition to the closures, approximately 365 petrol stations have been approved for operation based on actual needs, with an emphasis on allowing technical decisions to be made by specialists without external interference.

The Minister indicated that urgent and clear solutions to the crisis are being developed, with practical measures and actionable decisions expected soon. He also noted that work is underway to address the root causes of the crisis within days, acknowledging that challenges faced by the National Oil Corporation and other oil companies have contributed to the worsening fuel situation.

Why it mattersThe closure of 490 petrol stations and approval of 365 others directly impacts fuel availability and distribution mechanisms for citizens.

Libyan E-Payments Reach LD 765 Billion

Transactions between January and August show a significant increase in digital banking and electronic payment methods across the country.

Electronic payment transactions in Libya processed between January 1 and August 31, 2026, totaled approximately 764.9 billion Libyan dinars. This figure indicates a growing adoption of digital banking services and electronic payment methods within the nation.

The Central Bank of Libya (CBL) released these figures during a meeting in Tripoli with commercial bank representatives and the state company Moamalat. The meeting, chaired by CBL Governor Naji Issa, also addressed cash liquidity, foreign currency provision, and the enhancement of electronic payment systems.

Discussions included measures to ensure cash availability at commercial banks, facilitate access to foreign currency according to regulations, and improve the readiness of bank infrastructure for electronic payments. The Governor stressed adherence to CBL instructions and enhancing service quality for citizens.

Why it mattersThe growth in e-payment transactions affects citizens by reflecting the expansion of digital banking services and potentially improving access to financial services.
