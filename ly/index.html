Chevron secures Libya exploration deal for Sirte Basin acreage

Libya's National Oil Corporation (NOC) and US energy major Chevron signed a production-sharing agreement for exploration in the Sirte Basin, advancing a key award from the 2025.

Chevron and Libya's National Oil Corporation (NOC) finalized a production-sharing agreement on Monday, August 24, for exploration acreage in the Sirte Basin. This deal follows Chevron's selection in February as the successful bidder for onshore Area 106, also known as S4, during Libya’s 2025 exploration bid round. The agreement provides a contractual framework for Chevron to begin exploration activities in the 7,437-square-kilometer area, which is located in Libya's most productive oil region.

NOC Chairman Masoud Suleman stated that the agreement represents an important step toward developing Libya’s hydrocarbon resources and strengthening cooperation with major international energy companies. The bidding round data identified a gas discovery within Area 106 with estimated 2P reserves of about 100 million barrels of oil equivalent, along with several additional exploration leads.

This agreement marks Chevron's entry into Libya's upstream sector, following a memorandum of understanding signed in January to assess onshore exploration opportunities. The two entities have since expanded their collaboration, including a technical assessment of offshore Block NC146 in March and a joint study of unconventional shale oil and gas potential across the Sirte, Murzuq, and Ghadames basins in April.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are affected as this agreement could lead to increased hydrocarbon production, impacting national revenue and energy sector development.

GECOL investigates substation sabotage after Sug Il Juma explosion

The General Electricity Company of Libya (GECOL) launched an investigation into suspected sabotage at several power substations, including an explosion at substation 16 in Sug Il.

GECOL confirmed it is investigating suspected acts of sabotage targeting multiple power substations, with the most recent incident being an explosion at substation 16 in Sug Il Juma. The company stated that tampering with the grid could lead to a total blackout and poses a direct threat to grid stability and citizen safety.

The incident at Sug Il Juma involved the forced removal of on-duty personnel and the shutdown of the station, causing automatic line disconnections. Restoration was only possible after security forces intervened, allowing personnel to return and reconnect the lines. A transformer on Route 16 also caught fire, which GECOL cited as an example of the dangers of independent power restoration attempts.

GECOL emphasized that restoring electricity is a precise technical process, not a simple switch-flipping operation, designed to protect the grid and equipment. The company works around the clock to maintain grid stability and urged the public to avoid electrical installations and refrain from interfering with power operations.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are affected by the risk of total blackouts and disruptions to essential services due to suspected sabotage of power infrastructure.

Libya Central Bank Governor urges fiscal, monetary policy integration

Central Bank of Libya Governor Nagy Issa emphasized the need for integrating monetary and fiscal policies to bolster economic and financial stability in the country.

Governor Nagy Issa of the Central Bank of Libya has called for stronger coordination among state institutions to integrate monetary and fiscal policies. This integration is crucial for supporting the nation's economic and financial stability, according to Issa.

Issa explained that achieving this integration requires close monitoring of both revenue and public spending. He also highlighted the importance of implementing specific fiscal policy measures and prioritizing key areas within the national budget.

The Governor's remarks underscore a push for a more unified approach to economic management, aiming to create a stable financial environment through coordinated efforts between the central bank and other government bodies responsible for fiscal matters.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens and businesses are affected by the call for integrated policies, which could lead to more stable economic conditions and predictable financial planning.

Libya hosts first Schools of the Future Exhibition in Tripoli

The inaugural Schools of the Future Exhibition will showcase new technologies and engineering solutions for educational facilities, aiming to establish 500 modern schools.

The Schools of the Future Exhibition, a new component of the 9th Libya Construction Exhibition 2026, will debut from November 8-11 at the Tripoli Fairgrounds. This event, sponsored by the state Organisation for the Development Administrative Centres (ODAC), focuses on advancing the education sector by bringing together companies specializing in school development and equipping them with modern technologies and innovative engineering solutions.

Organizers ADD Events state the exhibition highlights ODAC's achievements and projects under the Tripoli government's "Return to Life" plan. The Schools of the Future project aims to establish 500 modern schools built to international standards across Libya. In 2025, ODAC delivered 46 new schools, accommodating approximately 23,500 students.

Currently, more than 431 school projects are underway, alongside numerous health and public facility initiatives. The exhibition will be held for the first time in Hall 3, providing a platform for showcasing progress and future plans in educational infrastructure development.

Why it mattersThe exhibition directly affects Libyan students and communities by showcasing efforts to build 500 modern schools and improve educational infrastructure.

Libyan Attorney General uncovers 63,986 forged national ID numbers

Investigations by the Attorney General's Office since 2017 have revealed widespread identity fraud, impacting government services and financial systems.

Libyan authorities have identified 63,986 forged national identification numbers that lack legitimate basis in the civil registry, according to Attorney General Al-Siddiq Al-Sour on August 17, 2022. An additional 88,819 national numbers were cited as incorrect, indicating further irregularities beyond proven forgeries. These findings are part of intensified investigations into broad identity fraud that began in 2017.

The fraud has allowed individuals to obtain passports, public employment, government grants, and even voting rights. One case involved a foreign national who paid 10,000 dinars to a deceased man's father to use the stolen identity, leading to a marriage and four children whose legal status is now based on this crime. The Attorney General's office did not accuse the wife or children of collaboration.

Further audits have uncovered financial implications, with the Social Solidarity Fund reporting 6,954 forged national IDs used to withdraw over three million dinars in pensions across five years, as announced on August 11, 2026. These financial reviews may overlap with other cases, so their numbers cannot be directly added to the official total of forged IDs.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are affected by identity theft that can infiltrate families and create legal uncertainties for individuals, including children, whose status is built on fraudulent records.
