Two Bank Employees Detained for Alleged Embezzlement of 9.9 Million Dinars

Libya's Public Prosecution ordered the detention of two Jumhouria Bank employees accused of stealing approximately 9.9 million dinars.

The Public Prosecution in Libya has ordered the detention of two employees from Jumhouria Bank's Mizran and Mellitah branches. They are accused of embezzling around 9.9 million Libyan dinars from the bank's funds. Investigators are examining transactions conducted at both locations.

A statement from the Public Prosecution’s media office confirmed the detention order. The investigation focuses on the legitimacy of transactions carried out at the Mizran and Mellitah branches. The exact period under review for these transactions was not specified.

This action follows an examination of financial activities at the two bank branches. The Public Prosecution is pursuing charges related to the alleged misappropriation of significant bank funds. Further details on the investigation's progress are expected.

Why it mattersTwo bank employees are detained on charges of embezzling approximately 9.9 million dinars, impacting the financial sector.

Brega Petroleum Distributes Fuel Around Clock

The company is working continuously to supply petrol and diesel across Libya amidst ongoing summer fuel shortages.

Brega Petroleum Marketing Company reported that fuel receipt and distribution operations are continuing around the clock across various sites in Libya. This effort aims to alleviate a summer fuel crisis that has been exacerbated by power cuts, leading to long queues in some western and eastern areas, though Tripoli is experiencing fewer issues.

Over the past 24 hours, distribution companies drew 11.955 million litres of petrol from Tripoli Seaport. The Zawia Oil Depot supplied approximately 1.11 million litres of petrol and 773,400 litres of diesel. Additionally, 940,000 litres of petrol and 240,000 litres of diesel were sent to 42 stations in the West Coast and Mountain regions, with the Southern Region receiving 750,000 litres.

In Misrata, the depot plans to distribute 5 million litres of petrol and 2 million litres of diesel on Friday, alongside 500,000 litres of diesel for the Misrata Power Plant. Meanwhile, the tanker MT SARIYAH docked at Tobruk’s Al-Hariga Port with 32,000 metric tons of petrol for the Eastern Region, supporting deliveries to Derna.

Why it mattersLibya's fuel distribution companies and power plants are directly affected by the continuous supply of petrol and diesel from Brega Petroleum.

Libya Trucks Receive 7,000 Diesel Loads in Four Days

Security forces supplied nearly 7,000 trucks with diesel between August 21 and 24, significantly easing a nationwide trucking crisis.

The Tripoli-based Security Directorates Support Unit reported that fuel stations under its supervision dispensed diesel to almost 7,000 trucks over a four-day period, from August 21 to 24. This intervention, coupled with 24-hour supervision at five stations and mobile filling points established by Brega Petroleum Marketing Company, has reportedly led to the disappearance of long truck queues.

Brega Petroleum Marketing Company, a subsidiary of the National Oil Corporation responsible for fuel imports and distribution, continues to report the arrival of fuel tankers at various Libyan posts. These efforts are intended to alleviate the ongoing fuel crisis and are hoped to bring an end to the widespread shortages.

The easing of the diesel supply problem is expected to positively impact households, businesses, and industries that rely on generators for electricity. The crisis had driven the black-market price of diesel, typically around LD 1.80-2.00 per litre, to as high as LD 8.00 per litre, a significant increase from the subsidized LD 0.15 per litre.

Why it mattersBusinesses and households relying on diesel for electricity generators are directly affected by the easing of the diesel supply crisis.

US Medical Team Completes 16 Heart Surgeries in Libya

A visiting US medical team successfully performed 16 open-heart surgeries and cardiac catheterizations for children at Tripoli University Hospital over ten days.

Libyan and visiting US medical teams have concluded a ten-day surgical mission at Tripoli University Hospital, completing 16 successful open-heart surgeries and paediatric cardiac catheterizations. The initiative, part of a medical cooperation program, aimed to support the localization of paediatric treatment within Libya.

The Novick Cardiac Alliance, led by William M Novick and Paul Nemir Jr. from the University of Tennessee Health Science Center's Global Surgical Institute, collaborated with Libyan medical professionals. This partnership involved cardiologists, surgeons, anaesthetists, nurses, and laboratory staff, demonstrating integrated medical and technical efforts.

The operations are part of a larger plan to conduct 40 procedures during the visit, with ongoing evaluations for targeted cases. The Ministry of Health reported the successful completion of all surgeries performed so far, highlighting the collaborative spirit and shared goal of enhancing local paediatric cardiac care capabilities.

Why it mattersChildren in Libya requiring complex cardiac procedures benefit from the transfer of advanced medical skills and direct surgical intervention.
