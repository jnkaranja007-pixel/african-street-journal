Libya launches electronic system for foreign firms

The Ministry of Economy and Trade is piloting a new digital platform to streamline foreign company branch registrations.

Libya's Ministry of Economy and Trade has launched a pilot electronic registration system for branches of foreign companies. Imad Ashour, Media Officer at the ministry, announced the system, developed by the Economic Information and Documentation Centre, aims to simplify procedures and increase transparency. The digital platform allows for electronic submission and tracking of registration applications.

The pilot phase will test the system's operational efficiency and gather feedback before full implementation. Ashour stated this initiative is part of the ministry's strategy to modernize economic administration and improve the business environment for foreign investors. The goal is to streamline processes for companies operating in the Libyan market.

This move is intended to enhance services provided to companies and investors by promoting digital transformation within the ministry. The system's development precedes this pilot launch, focusing on improving administrative efficiency and transparency in foreign company branch registrations.

Why it mattersForeign companies seeking to operate in Libya can now submit and track registration applications electronically, simplifying administrative procedures.

Libya discusses project finance with Islamic Development Bank

Delegates met at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, to review funding needs for environmental, agricultural, and water sectors.

A Libyan government delegation met with the Director General of the Islamic Development Bank in Antalya, Turkey, to discuss financing and implementing projects in the environmental, agricultural, and water resource sectors. The discussions, held during the climate conference (COP31), aimed to bolster food and water security in Libya.

The meeting included Libya's Ministers of Agriculture and Water Resources, an advisor from the Ministry of Environment, and the Director General of the Government Communication Centre. They reviewed the priorities and funding requirements for these Libyan sectors, as well as the technical support needed for proposed projects.

Participants also addressed strengthening partnerships with international institutions, accelerating national strategies for the environment and climate, and supporting sustainable development projects.

Why it mattersLibya's ministers discussed funding and technical support for agricultural and water projects to bolster food and water security.

Benghazi book fair session explains Marrakesh Treaty access rules

Speakers at the fifth Benghazi International Book Fair said the treaty lets books be converted into accessible formats without prior permission from authors.

An informational session at the fifth Benghazi International Book Fair examined how the Marrakesh Treaty can improve access to books and literary works for people with disabilities. Abdul Basit Abbas Al-Aqouri, a faculty member in the Arabic Language Department at the University of Benghazi and chairman of the Libyan Organization for the Rights of the Visually Impaired, said books in suitable formats let beneficiaries keep up with new publications.

Dr. Ibtisam Abhih, a professor of private law at the University of Benghazi, gave a legal paper at the session on the treaty, adopted in 2013 under the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO). She said it covers people who are blind, visually impaired, or have physical disabilities that stop them turning pages, and allows works to be converted into Braille, digital and audio versions without the author's prior permission.

Abhih said the treaty balances authors' rights, which Law No. 9 of 1968 governs locally, against the fundamental right of persons with disabilities to access knowledge. She also linked it to Articles 9 and 30 of the International Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, and noted that Law No. 5 of 1987 did not explicitly address cultural rights for those persons.

Why it mattersBlind, visually impaired and physically disabled readers in Libya gain a legal route to Braille, digital and audio editions of books without waiting for each author's permission.
