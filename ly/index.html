Libya expects cooking gas relief as third tanker nears

Brega Petroleum Marketing Company blames irregular supplies and illegal sales for shortages in the Mountain region.

The Mountain region of Libya is experiencing a temporary shortage of cooking gas due to irregular tanker arrivals and disruptions in supply, according to Ahmed Al-Masallati, spokesman for Brega Petroleum Marketing Company. He stated that the company distributed approximately 1,200 cylinders in Zintan and 600 in Nalut, but operational restrictions and safety concerns are impacting regular deliveries.

Al-Masallati accused some distributors of selling their allocated gas on the parallel market, exacerbating the crisis and depriving citizens. He urged security and oversight authorities to increase monitoring of distribution points and organize supply operations better. Filling points remain open in Zintan and Nalut, with plans for a new one in Jadu.

While Al-Masallati could not provide an exact end date for the shortage, he assured residents that the situation is temporary and expects availability to improve with the arrival of a third tanker in the coming days. Increased winter consumption has contributed to the current demand.

Why it mattersResidents in Libya's Mountain region face difficulties obtaining cooking gas due to supply disruptions and illegal sales by distributors.

Libyan mountain municipalities give Dbeibah one week deadline

Local leaders demand funding for stalled development projects and reject recent cabinet financial allocations.

Representatives from Libya's mountain municipalities have given Prime Minister Abdul Hamid Dbeibah one week to release funding for development projects. The group, including mayors and local leaders, met in Yefren to express frustration over alleged unfair treatment and marginalization.

The statement specifically targeted the Government of National Unity and the Ministry of Local Government for delays in payments for projects approved two to three years ago. These delays have negatively impacted infrastructure, water, and health sectors, worsening hardships for residents.

The representatives rejected recent cabinet decisions on financial allocations and called for a review to ensure their regions receive a fair share. They declared their rights are not negotiable and warned that further neglect could affect national stability.

Why it mattersResidents of mountain municipalities face worsening hardships due to stalled infrastructure, water, and health projects.

Libya central bank governor meets economy minister

Fawzi Abu Khazam and Suhail Abu Shiha discussed preparing an import budget and boosting institutional coordination.

Central Bank of Libya Governor Fawzi Abu Khazam met with Economy and Trade Minister Suhail Abu Shiha yesterday. The meeting focused on preparing an import budget using scientific and realistic criteria. This aims to meet local market needs and rationalize foreign currency spending.

Both officials explored ways to enhance cooperation between their institutions. They agreed to exchange economic data and statistics to improve decision-making accuracy. The Central Bank of Libya stated this meeting is part of an effort to foster integration and unify authorities' actions.

This initiative seeks to contribute to economic stability and better resource management within Libya. The preparation of the import budget will ensure Libyan citizens' needs are met.

Why it mattersThe meeting aims to ensure the needs of Libyan citizens are met through a prepared import budget.

Libyan firms explore farm tech at Italy expo

Companies from Libya attended Bologna's EIMA 2026 to see innovations in machinery and smart irrigation.

Libyan companies specializing in date cultivation, agricultural machinery, and modern irrigation systems attended the EIMA 2026 exhibition in Bologna, Italy, from November 10-14. The international event featured approximately 1,600 companies showcasing agricultural technology and equipment.

Mohamed Shalouf, owner of a Libyan agricultural machinery firm, stated that the exhibition is a significant opportunity to explore the latest developments and engage directly with global manufacturers. He noted the potential for adopting smart irrigation systems and electronic controls to develop Libya's agricultural sector.

Libyan representatives viewed participation as a chance to explore technological shifts and consider localizing solutions. This engagement aims to modernize agricultural production, improve water conservation, and enhance product quality, potentially opening new avenues for Libyan dates in global markets.

Why it mattersLibyan agricultural companies are seeking new technologies to modernize their operations and improve product quality.

Business · 2026-11-12

Libyan businesswomen seek French trade deals

Dr. Amal Abdullah Al-Tarhouni is in France to build partnerships in health, banking, and food sectors.

Dr. Amal Abdullah Al-Tarhouni, head of the Libyan Businesswomen’s Organisation, is visiting France for five days to foster economic and trade ties. The visit, co-organised by the French Council of Investors in Africa and others, aims to connect the 200-member organisation with French counterparts in health, banking, and food industries.

Dr. Al-Tarhouni stated the visit aims to strengthen trade and economic relations between Libya and France, building communication and partnership bridges between women entrepreneurs and companies. She also seeks to introduce French companies to Libyan market opportunities and invite them to upcoming international events in Libya.

A key priority is attracting French manufacturers of advanced medical technologies for the International Strategic Forum for Advanced Medical Technologies, scheduled for January 16-18, 2027. This forum is expected to be a major event for Libya's medical sector, bringing together global manufacturers and offering opportunities for cooperation and knowledge exchange.

Why it mattersLibyan businesswomen are seeking new economic and trade partnerships with French companies.
