GECOL Chairman Marash vows to end Libya power cuts in 10-15 days

Bashir Al-Marash, the new head of the General Electricity Company of Libya (GECOL), stated that the nation's electricity crisis will conclude within two weeks.

GECOL Chairman Bashir Al-Marash announced that Libya's acute electricity crisis, ongoing since mid-June, will end within 10 to 15 days. Speaking during a meeting with the Municipality of Sug Il Juma, Al-Marash cited new units entering the national grid, giving a 90 percent chance of improvement for the entire country. He emphasized that fair load-shedding requires a computerized system, as manual implementation is impossible.

The South Tripoli power station is nearing commissioning, according to GECOL officials who met with the Municipality of Tripoli Centre. This new station is expected to support the grid and enhance electricity supply stability, strengthening hopes for gradual improvement. However, GECOL did not provide a precise date for when the power station will become operational.

Libya has experienced extensive and frequent power cuts and blackouts since mid-June, leading to public unrest. These demonstrations have included road blockades with burning rubbish and tires, and the barricading of state buildings. The promised resolution aims to alleviate these disruptions across all regions of Libya.

Why it mattersLibyan residents and businesses are directly affected by the promise of an end to power cuts, which have caused significant daily disruption and public demonstrations.

Libyan Chambers Union requests Spanish support for Madrid energy forum

The General Union of Chambers of Commerce head, Mohamed Raied, met with the Spanish Ambassador to discuss the upcoming Libyan-European Oil, Gas, and Energy Forum in Madrid.

Mohamed Raied, head of Libya’s General Union of Chambers of Commerce, hosted the Spanish Ambassador at the Chamber’s Tripoli headquarters yesterday to discuss economic and trade relations between Libya and Spain. The meeting focused on developing and enhancing these ties to broader levels, serving mutual interests and opening new avenues for cooperation between the two countries.

A key topic was the third Libyan-European Oil, Gas, and Energy Forum, scheduled for December 2 to 3, 2026, in Madrid. Raied requested the Ambassador's support for the event from Spanish authorities, including the Spanish Trade Office and Repsol, to ensure its success and boost participation from Spanish companies and institutions.

The Ambassador expressed readiness to contribute to the forum's success, highlighting its importance in strengthening economic ties and opening new channels for cooperation between the private sectors of both nations. Raied also sought assistance in coordinating a visit by Libya's Minister of Housing and Utilities to Spain to meet with officials and companies specializing in construction and infrastructure.

Why it mattersLibyan businesses and government ministries are seeking Spanish expertise and investment in key sectors like energy, construction, and infrastructure.

Libyan Experts Forum recommends digital reforms, redirects bank financing

A forum of Libyan experts concluded in Tripoli with proposals to digitize real estate, monitor strategic projects, and shift bank funding to productive sectors.

The first Libyan Experts Forum, organized by the Ministry of Labor and Rehabilitation and the Libyan Authority for Scientific Research, concluded its three-day deliberations in Tripoli yesterday. The forum produced several recommendations aimed at transforming presented visions into practical programs and projects to support state institutions, enhance development, and improve services.

Key proposals include expediting the implementation of a "Digital Addressing Platform" project and completing the digitized transfer and registration of valid real estate ownership documents. These measures are intended to reduce disputes and stimulate investment, according to the forum's findings.

Further recommendations involve establishing a national office to monitor strategic projects, adopting standard indicators for progress tracking, redirecting bank financing towards productive sectors and small and medium-sized enterprises, and developing public financial governance and banking supervision.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens and businesses are affected by these recommendations, which aim to streamline property ownership, improve public services, and potentially shift financial support towards local enterprises.

Sirte residents regain power after 17-hour outage

Electricity has gradually returned to Sirte and its surrounding areas following a complete blackout caused by technical malfunctions and transmission line failures.

Power has been restored to Sirte and its surrounding areas after a complete electricity outage that lasted more than 17 hours. The blackout, which began yesterday, affected all residents and businesses in the region.

The Libyan News Agency’s correspondent in Sirte reported that the disruption was caused by technical malfunctions and a shutdown of the Gulf Steam Power Station. Additionally, several transmission lines failed, contributing to the widespread power loss.

The gradual return of electricity marks the end of a significant period without power for the affected communities, allowing daily activities to resume after the prolonged interruption.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in Sirte and surrounding areas were directly affected by the 17-hour power outage, disrupting their daily lives and operations.

Libya and Germany discuss reopening consular section and new direct flights

Libyan and German officials met to advance plans for reopening the German consular section in Tripoli and establishing direct air routes between Tripoli, Berlin, and Frankfurt.

Mohamed Ben Ghalboun, the Minister of State for Prime Ministerial and Cabinet Affairs, met with German Ambassador Arvid Enders to discuss strengthening cooperation between Libya and Germany. The meeting focused on mutual interests and enhancing the partnership between the two nations.

A key point of discussion was the importance of expediting the reopening of the German consular section in Tripoli. Both sides also addressed working towards establishing new direct air routes connecting Tripoli with Berlin and Frankfurt, aiming to improve travel and trade links.

Ambassador Enders expressed gratitude for the support received since his arrival in Libya and affirmed Germany's commitment to enhancing cooperation across various sectors. Both officials agreed to continue consultations and coordination on issues of common interest to further strengthen the Libyan-German partnership.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens and German nationals will benefit from easier travel and consular services, while businesses could see improved trade and investment opportunities.
