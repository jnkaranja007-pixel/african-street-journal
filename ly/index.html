Libyan government warns it will reorganise regional administration after Zintan session...

The government says it will act if obstruction of the House of Representatives continues, giving regions more authority over their administration, finances and public resources.

Libya's government warned on Monday, November 16, that it will take urgent administrative and financial measures in areas under its control after the House of Representatives failed to hold a formal session in Zintan. The measures could give regions greater autonomy over administration, finances and public resources.

In Statement No. 17 for 2026, issued in Benghazi, the government said threats and attempts to block lawmakers from meeting prevented them from carrying out their constitutional duties. It called the developments dangerous and unacceptable, and said interference with constitutional institutions threatens national unity and stability.

The government thanked the people, elders and leaders of Zintan for preparing to host the session. It said it remains committed to ending national division, but that the responsibility cannot be one-sided, and held all parties disrupting state institutions accountable for any consequences.

Why it mattersResidents in areas under the government's control would see local administration and public-resource management handed to regional authorities if the obstruction continues.

Libya's central bank governor urges a unified national budget

Fawzi Abu Khazam told Finance Minister Rashid Abu Ghaffa that a single budget would consolidate public spending and strengthen fiscal discipline.

Central Bank of Libya Governor Fawzi Abu Khazam urged adoption of a unified national budget in a meeting with Finance Minister Rashid Abu Ghaffa in Tripoli. The two discussed joint technical arrangements and ways to strengthen cooperation between the central bank and the ministry.

Abu Khazam said a single budget was needed to consolidate public spending and improve fiscal discipline. He also stressed the bank's independence and said it would coordinate with state institutions within existing laws.

The governor called for a firm commitment to combating money laundering, terrorist financing and corruption, and for essential economic reforms. The bank and the Ministry of Economy and Trade had agreed on 11 November that documentary letters of credit would be issued to applicants on the basis of national priorities identified by the ministry, to ration hard-currency releases and save reserves.

Why it mattersImporters seeking documentary letters of credit will be ranked by the Ministry of Economy and Trade's national priorities, so businesses needing hard currency for imports face a tighter allocation as the central bank works to protect its reserves.

Libya's Medical Supply Organisation invites institutions to Tripoli forum

The state medical purchasing body wants participants to examine the system's problems and propose changes at a two-day gathering in December.

Libya's state medical purchasing entity, the Medical Supply Organisation (MSO), has invited relevant entities and institutions to a two-day Medical Supply Forum for Dialogue and Development in Tripoli on 21 and 22 December.

The forum will be held at a leading hotel in the capital. The MSO said it aims to review the current state of the medical supply system, address its key challenges and explore development opportunities, while presenting proposals to improve operational mechanisms and strengthen partnerships among stakeholders.

The MSO said the gathering is part of its continuing efforts to develop the medical supply system and raise its operational efficiency. It described the forum as a step toward constructive dialogue and a more efficient and sustainable system.

Why it mattersHospitals, suppliers and other bodies that take part can put their proposals on how medicines are bought and distributed directly to the body that runs the system.

Education · 2026-11-18

Tripoli University opens job fair with more than 50 participating entities

The fair runs from 16 to 19 November, with university and government bodies presenting services for job seekers and graduates.

Tripoli University opened its 2026 Job Fair at the Faculty of Science theatre, with more than 50 entities taking part. The event runs from 16 to 19 November and is in its second consecutive year.

The university’s Alumni Training Office organised the fair. A representative of the Labour and Rehabilitation Ministry said cooperation among education and training institutions, employers and government bodies can help graduates enter professional careers, and described the private sector as a partner in hiring national talent.

The ministry’s pavilion included the Financial Facilities Fund, the Human Resources Development Fund, the Information and Documentation Centre, and the National Centre for Quality Assurance and Vocational Standardization. Their representatives outlined programmes and services for job seekers and initiatives to build professional skills.

Why it mattersJob seekers can hear about services and skills programmes from ministry-affiliated bodies at the fair, which runs through 19 November.
