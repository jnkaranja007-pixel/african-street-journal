Libyan Fuel Stations Supply Diesel to 7,000 Trucks in Four Days

The Security Directorates Support Unit reported that diesel was supplied to nearly 7,000 trucks between August 21 and 24, significantly easing a trucking crisis.

Nearly 7,000 trucks received diesel from fuel stations supervised by the Tripoli-based Security Directorates Support Unit between August 21 and 24. The unit reported that continuous 24-hour supervision at five stations, including mobile filling points set up by Brega Petroleum Marketing Company, helped alleviate the long truck queues that had characterized the recent crisis.

Brega Petroleum Marketing Company, a subsidiary of the National Oil Corporation responsible for fuel imports and distribution, continues to report the arrival of fuel tankers at various Libyan posts. This influx is expected to further ease the diesel shortage, with hopes that these measures will bring an end to the ongoing fuel crisis.

The improved diesel supply is anticipated to have a positive impact on households, businesses, and industries reliant on diesel for electricity generators. Previously, the black-market price for a litre of diesel, nominally priced at LD 0.15 from Brega, had surged to as high as LD 8.00, from its usual LD 1.80-2.00 range.

Why it mattersBusinesses and industries relying on diesel for electricity generators are affected by the easing of the fuel crisis, which had driven up black-market prices.

Diesel Prices Surge, Burdening Libyan Families

Record inflation driven by high transportation costs and diesel shortages is impacting household budgets ahead of the school year.

Libyan families are facing significant financial strain as the price of diesel fuel reaches between 8 and 10 dinars per liter on the parallel market. This surge in fuel costs is a primary driver behind a broader wave of inflation that is affecting numerous goods across the country. The head of the Libyan Consumer Protection Associations Union, Ahmed Al-Kurdi, highlighted these rising prices as a major concern for consumers.

The escalating cost of diesel is directly contributing to increased transportation expenses, which in turn inflates the prices of everyday items. This situation is particularly acute as the new school year approaches, with school supplies also experiencing record price hikes. Al-Kurdi pointed to the dual pressures of high transport costs and a scarcity of diesel as the root causes of this inflationary trend.

The impact of these price increases is being felt most acutely by households, who are bearing the brunt of the rising costs for essential goods and services. The shortage of diesel fuel, coupled with its elevated price on the parallel market, creates a ripple effect throughout the economy, making it more expensive for businesses to operate and for consumers to purchase necessities.

Why it mattersLibyan families are affected by inflation that increases the cost of goods due to high transportation expenses and diesel shortages.

Tripoli Hospital Performs Advanced Laparoscopic Surgery

A patient left Tripoli University Hospital within 23 hours after a successful laparoscopic repair of a congenital anterior diaphragmatic hernia.

Tripoli University Hospital has successfully performed an advanced laparoscopic surgery to repair a congenital anterior diaphragmatic hernia, a procedure that allowed the patient to be discharged within 23 hours. The minimally invasive keyhole surgery, conducted by Professor Ali Al-Gallai's team in the Oncology, Liver, and Pancreas Surgery Department, eliminated the need for open surgery and demonstrated the effectiveness of the hospital's specialized services.

This achievement highlights the speed of patient recovery following advanced surgical intervention, adhering to high standards of medical care and post-operative follow-up. The hospital stated this success is part of its ongoing efforts to provide advanced surgical services, promote modern laparoscopic techniques, and train a new generation of surgeons to enhance expertise in specialized fields.

The operation underscores the importance of integrated medical teams, with cooperation between surgical, anesthesia, and nursing staff contributing to comprehensive patient care. This initiative aligns with the Tripoli government's policy to localize healthcare provision, saving privately funded patients the cost and inconvenience of seeking treatment abroad and reducing state subsidies for overseas medical care.

Why it mattersThe successful localization of advanced surgical procedures at Tripoli University Hospital means patients no longer need to travel abroad for complex operations, saving them personal expense and the state money on subsidies.

South Tripoli Power Station Unit Begins Operation

The General Electricity Company of Libya has started operating the first unit at its new South Tripoli Power station, aiming to alleviate the nation's energy crisis.

The General Electricity Company of Libya (GECOL) announced the commencement of operations for the first unit at the new South Tripoli Power station. This activation is a preparatory step before the unit is connected to the public electricity grid, as part of ongoing efforts to complete and equip the station's generating units. GECOL has not yet specified a timeline for when the newly generated power will be available to the public, though Libyan media reports suggest it could be within days.

This development comes as GECOL Chairman Bashir Al-Marash previously assured that Libya's current electricity crisis would be resolved within two weeks. The South Tripoli station has a total projected capacity of approximately 1,300 megawatts, and both GECOL and the Tripoli-based Abdel Hamid Aldabaiba government are placing significant reliance on this station to end the country's persistent electricity shortages.

Libya has been experiencing a severe electricity crisis since mid-June, marked by frequent and extensive power outages that have triggered public demonstrations, road blockades, and the occupation of state buildings. The power cuts have had widespread consequences, including disruptions to telecommunications, increased demand and prices for diesel used in generators, bread shortages, industrial production losses, and the closure of health clinics.

Why it mattersThe activation of a new power station unit is intended to address Libya's electricity crisis, which has caused widespread disruptions and shortages affecting daily life and economic activity.
