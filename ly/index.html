Politics · 2026-11-17

Libyan parliament calls emergency session in Benghazi

Members of the House of Representatives will meet on Wednesday after the government in Tripoli blocked their planned session in Zintan.

The Speaker of the House of Representatives (HoR), Al-Salheen Abdel-Nabi, has called an emergency session for Wednesday, 18 November, at the HoR headquarters in Benghazi.

The move follows the Tripoli-based government's decision to stop the HoR from holding a session in Zintan. Officials first withheld landing permits, then used military vehicles to stop aircraft carrying HoR members from landing at Zintan airport.

The HoR has condemned the closure of the airport. The United Nations Support Mission in Libya (UNSMIL) said only that it was concerned about the blocked session.

Why it mattersHoR members who were stopped from reaching Zintan must now travel to Benghazi to take part in the emergency session.

Business · 2026-11-17

Libya tightens import controls to protect economy

Customs and Economy Ministry officials met in Tripoli to discuss regulating goods and safeguarding national markets.

Libyan customs officials met with the Minister of Economy and Trade in Tripoli on Monday to discuss strengthening import controls and protecting the national economy. The meeting focused on enhancing oversight of goods entering through customs ports, particularly food and pharmaceuticals.

Discussions also covered verifying shipment routes for essential goods financed by letters of credit, ensuring they comply with regulations. Officials explored tightening controls over transit goods to prevent violations as they move across Libyan territory.

This effort is part of a wider initiative to rationalize the use of Libya's hard currency deposits, following an agreement on November 12 between the Economy Minister and the Central Bank of Libya governor to prioritize import documentary credits.

Why it mattersThe Economy Ministry and Customs Authority are enhancing import oversight and regulating goods to safeguard the national economy and rationalize hard currency use.

Education · 2026-11-17

Libyan experts propose unified national framework for medical training

A Benghazi symposium recommended continuous evaluation of trainees and a single national certification for specialists.

A symposium in Benghazi, held as part of the Eighth Conference of the Libyan Society of Otolaryngology, ended with recommendations to improve medical education and training in Libya. Experts from Libya, Egypt, Oman, Canada, Qatar, Jordan and Sudan took part.

Dr. Hussein Al-Khamri, head of the cochlear implant and rehabilitation department at the Center for Specialized Surgery, said the proposals call for a unified national framework for professional competencies. Trainees' progress would be tied to their actual competence through continuous evaluation of clinical and professional performance, with methods covering knowledge, skills and professional behaviour.

The recommendations also urge closer links between undergraduate medical education and specialised training, and better coordination between medical schools and training institutions. They include policies for the safe use of artificial intelligence in medical education, electronic portfolios for trainees, and a unified national certification for specialists.
