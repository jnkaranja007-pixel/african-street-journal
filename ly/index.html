Libya restarts Brega gas unit

Sirte Oil Company recovers 60 million cubic feet of gas for the supply network.

Sirte Oil Company restarted a natural gas processing unit at Brega on October 25, ending years of disuse and allowing Libya to capture gas that would have been burned off.

The National Oil Corporation stated the unit will redirect 60 million cubic feet of gas into the supply network, reducing flaring and making more resources available. The corporation cited environmental protection and improved use of Libya’s gas resources as the expected benefits.

The announcement did not specify if the 60 million cubic feet figure represented a daily volume.

Why it mattersLibya will recover 60 million cubic feet of gas for its supply network, reducing flaring and improving resource use.

UN extends Libya mission to October 2027

Security Council resolution backs efforts to unify state institutions and hold elections within two years.

The United Nations Security Council has extended its political mission in Libya until October 31, 2027. The resolution supports efforts to unify state institutions and establish a single executive authority. It also urges Libyan parties to honour commitments towards presidential and parliamentary elections within 24 months.

Resolution 2835, adopted on Friday, October 30, renews support for Hanna Tetteh, the UN secretary-general’s special representative. Britain led negotiations on the text, which supports the formation of a single executive authority as part of efforts to end the country’s prolonged political transition.

The political effort centres on an agreement reached on August 30 through UN-facilitated talks known as the “4+4” process. The Council stresses that petroleum resources belong to all Libyans and calls for the National Oil Corporation to operate free from political interference.

Why it mattersLibyans are directly affected by the extension of the UN mission which supports efforts to unify state institutions and hold national elections.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Tripoli court affirms judicial independence

The Tripoli Court of Appeals rejected attempts to fragment the judiciary and influence its rulings.

The Tripoli Court of Appeals affirmed its commitment to judicial independence and the separation of powers on Tuesday, October 27. The court stressed that judicial independence is a constitutional immunity and rejected any interference in its work or rulings.

In its General Assembly, the court also emphasized the binding authority of the Supreme Court's Constitutional Chamber. It rejected amendments to judiciary laws made outside the constitutional process, warning against exceptional measures that could harm judicial independence.

The court declared its adherence to the unity of the judicial body, stating that its strength lies in cohesion. It aims to remain separate from political and regional tensions, serving as a safe haven.

Why it mattersThe Tripoli Court of Appeals' actions directly affect the judiciary and its ability to operate independently of political influence.

Tripoli drainage stations halt, 130 buildings at risk

Rainfall preparations continue amid warnings of infrastructure decay and building collapse in Libya's capital.

Several sewage lift stations in Tripoli's Central Municipality are out of service, causing stagnant rainwater in low-lying areas. Sami Hammadi, a member of the Central Municipal Council, warned of the deteriorating infrastructure after repeated appeals to authorities for repairs have gone unanswered.

The municipality is coordinating with the Tripoli Security Directorate, the Water and Sanitation Company, and other agencies for current preparations, which include deploying drainage trucks. Recent rainfall measured between 1 and 5 millimeters, significantly less than previous levels that reached 92 millimeters.

Additionally, 130 buildings within the municipality are at risk of collapse, with the Tripoli City Development Authority responsible for repairs or redevelopment. Plans are in place to relocate residents of dangerous buildings to hotels if necessary, coordinated with the National Safety Authority and the municipality.

Why it mattersResidents in Tripoli face potential flooding and the danger of collapsing buildings due to neglected infrastructure.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Libyan dialogue group seeks judicial unity for elections

London talks focus on resolving institutional divisions to enable credible national votes.

Libya’s 4+4 dialogue group met in London and agreed to consult further with the judiciary to resolve institutional divisions. Participants stated these divisions threaten the prospects for credible national elections. The group identified judicial unity and independence as essential for holding presidential and parliamentary votes that would be accepted across political divides.

Under UN Special Representative Hanna Tetteh, the group committed to wider consultations within the judicial community. They aim to find solutions while respecting the judiciary's neutrality and independence. The UN Support Mission in Libya reported that members also agreed to continue seeking political support for the electoral agreement signed in Tripoli on August 30.

The August agreement addresses obstacles to elections, including reconstituting the High National Elections Commission board and settling disputes over the electoral legal framework. It envisages elections under a single executive authority within 24 months, though this remains a political commitment. The High Council of State has rejected the agreement, citing concerns about bypassing constitutional rules.

Why it mattersLibyans face continued institutional divisions that hinder the possibility of holding national elections.
