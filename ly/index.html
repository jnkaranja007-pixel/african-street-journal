Libya parliament to debate unified public sector pay scale

Teachers demand action as lawmakers review salary legislation that has exposed divisions among public employees.

Libya's House of Representatives will consider a proposed unified public sector salary scale on Monday in Sabha. Parliamentary finance committees finished technical discussions on the bill, which aims for consistent pay across government institutions while managing state expenditure.

The final consultations in Tripoli on Sunday included members of parliament, Central Bank Governor Fawzi Abu Khuzam, Finance Minister Rashid Abu Ghaffa, and ministry representatives. They focused on the financial implications, existing salary differences, and the cost of a unified framework.

Teachers on strike are pressing for immediate approval of the legislation, linking it to outstanding salary entitlements. The Tripoli Court of Appeal has also cautioned parliament against changing judicial pay without consulting the Supreme Judicial Council.

Why it mattersPublic sector employees, including teachers, university academics, and the judiciary, are directly affected by the proposed unified salary scale.

Libya economy minister meets farmers on production challenges

Tripoli meeting addressed rising input costs, feed shortages, and market price stability for local produce.

Libya's Ministry of Economy and Trade held a meeting in Tripoli on Thursday, October 29, to discuss challenges facing farmers and livestock breeders.

The discussions focused on supporting local production and enhancing food security, addressing issues like rising input costs, difficulties in procuring animal feed and veterinary medicines, and price stability for local products.

The ministry aims to build a system aligning import policies with local production needs to support farmers and breeders and transition towards a productive economy.

Why it mattersLibyan farmers and livestock breeders face rising costs for essential production inputs and struggle with market price stability.

Business · 2026-11-02

Tripoli Chamber signs economic deal with Italian counterpart

An agreement aims to boost trade, joint ventures, and investment between Libya and Italy.

The Tripoli Chamber of Commerce signed a memorandum of understanding with the Joint Italian-Arab Chamber of Commerce on October 29 in Milan, Italy. The agreement, signed on the sidelines of the 5th Arab-Italian Economic Forum, aims to strengthen economic and commercial cooperation and expand partnership opportunities for business owners in both nations.

Anwar Abu Sitta, Chairman of the Tripoli Chamber of Commerce, and Pietro Rompino, President of the Joint Italian-Arab Chamber of Commerce, signed the MoU. The memorandum seeks to foster economic relations within the private sector, encourage joint ventures, and promote investment and trade exchange through forums, gatherings, and trade missions.

Cooperation areas include energy, infrastructure, agriculture, pharmaceuticals, transport, and digital services. The agreement also focuses on supporting young entrepreneurs through training and expertise exchange, and will facilitate reciprocal Libyan-Italian Business Forums and B2B/B2G meetings.

Why it mattersLibyan and Italian businesses will benefit from expanded partnership opportunities, joint ventures, and facilitated trade and investment.

Business · 2026-11-02

Libya launches engineering project management conference in Tripoli

The two-day event focuses on sustainable development and reconstruction in key sectors.

The First International Engineering Project Management Conference began yesterday in Tripoli, aiming to foster an integrative vision for sustainable development and reconstruction. The conference brings together officials, experts, and engineers from Libya and abroad to discuss the oil, gas, construction, and services sectors.

Organized by the College of Engineering Project Management and sponsored by the Libyan Iron and Steel Company (LISCO), the event features participation from various ministries and government institutions. LISCO Chairman Mohamed Abd al-Malik al-Faqih highlighted the company's progress in engineering project management, noting its expertise in the field.

Three research papers from LISCO engineers on managing iron and steel projects, green industry shifts, and resource sustainability were presented. The conference will continue for two days, including further research presentations to exchange experiences and propose recommendations for development projects.

Why it mattersLibyan businesses and government bodies are involved in discussions about reconstruction and development projects.

Education · 2026-11-02

Libyan MP warns of danger to poor children's education

Jayallah Al-Shaibani says suspending classes denies poor children access to schooling while wealthy families find alternatives.

Member of Parliament Jayallah Al-Shaibani warned on Facebook that suspending classes and denying education to children from poor families is extremely dangerous. He noted that wealthier families can afford alternative educational opportunities for their children, creating inequality.

Al-Shaibani expressed support for teachers and their right to a decent living, but stressed the negative impact of class suspensions on vulnerable students. He stated that this situation threatens the future of children from impoverished households.

The MP's comments highlight a growing concern over educational access for disadvantaged children in Libya. The ongoing disruption to schooling raises questions about the long-term consequences for these students and the nation.

Why it mattersChildren from poor families in Libya are at risk of losing their education due to class suspensions, while wealthier children can access alternative schooling.
