Health · 2026-11-08

Libya monitors Ebola outbreak as cases rise in Congo

The National Center for Disease Control has raised alert levels for travelers despite no confirmed cases in Libya.

Libya's National Center for Disease Control announced it is on alert for the Bundibugyo Ebola virus, though no cases have been recorded in the country. Surveillance and rapid response teams are heightened to detect and respond to any suspected cases. The center classifies Ebola as a dangerous viral hemorrhagic fever with high death rates without immediate intervention.

The outbreak is expanding in Africa, with the Democratic Republic of Congo reporting the largest outbreak of this strain and the second largest Ebola outbreak ever. The World Health Organization reports 9,668 confirmed cases and 4,649 deaths in Congo. The disease has spread to Kenya, with 9 cases, and Nigeria, with 1 case.

More than 31,000 contacts are under monitoring across eight affected provinces in Congo, with Ituri being the hardest hit. Healthcare workers have also been affected, with 301 infections and 48 deaths reported among medical staff. The situation is complicated by insecurity and displacement.

Why it mattersLibyan travelers may be exposed to the virus as it spreads across Africa and crosses borders.

Business · 2026-11-08

Libya's NOC moves to capture 34 million cubic feet of flared gas daily

The National Oil Corporation and SLB agreed to proceed with a project to reduce gas flaring at Messla and Sarir fields.

Libya's National Oil Corporation (NOC) is moving forward with a project to capture 34 million cubic feet of gas daily that is currently being flared at the Messla and Sarir oil fields. The NOC's "2030 Initiative" Committee met with SLB representatives on November 4 and 5 to discuss the project's first phase outcomes. This initiative aims to turn flared gas into an economic resource.

The project, operated by the Arabian Gulf Oil Company (AGOCO), will involve a detailed technical study to be completed by the first quarter of 2027. NOC Chairman stressed the importance of reducing gas flaring and maximizing national resource utilization. Participants agreed to enhance cooperation with SLB for its technical expertise.

The NOC stated the initiative aligns with efforts to achieve sustainable economic and environmental benefits. The next phase of the project will produce a comprehensive, implementation-ready plan. This follows the initial engineering study conducted by SLB.

Why it mattersThe initiative aims to transform associated gas into a source of economic value and energy, delivering sustainable economic and environmental benefits.

Culture · 2026-11-08

Benghazi book fair discusses Libyan novel writing

Young writers share experiences and discuss the construction of the Libyan novel at the fifth Benghazi International Book Fair.

The fifth Benghazi International Book Fair is hosting cultural evenings and programs focused on writing experiences and the development of the Libyan novel. Organized under the theme “Generations Meet and Visions Renew,” the events feature writers, poets, and intellectuals discussing poetry, literature, and creative writing.

A seminar on youth writing in Libyan novels on Thursday explored the contributions of young authors. Writer Awatif Al-Shehibi moderated the session, where young writer Mawadda Amqada’ Al-Tarhouni discussed early writing experiences and influences. Qusai Balamaskin, born in 1996, highlighted his novel “Aqab,” published by Dar Al-Nahda Al-Arabiya and supported by the Libyan Novel Workshop.

The seminar aimed to introduce new literary experiences and highlight young writers' contributions to Libyan literature. Accompanying cultural events, including poetry evenings and panel discussions, will continue until November 12, featuring writers from across Libya.

Why it mattersYoung Libyan writers are discussing their experiences and the future of novel writing in their country at a cultural event.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Libya's HoR studies cash fuel subsidies

The House of Representatives is considering a shift to cash-based fuel subsidies, aiming to reassure citizens.

Libya's House of Representatives issued an official statement confirming that a study on changing from commodity-based fuel subsidies to a cash-based system is being considered to address economic imbalances. The House emphasized that this approach does not mean immediate abolition or replacement of subsidies.

The statement clarified that any decision to replace or reform the subsidy system will only be made after all necessary studies are completed and essential safeguards are in place. These measures are designed to protect citizens' rights, ensure fair access, safeguard living standards, and promote the nation's economic stability.

Libyan citizens fear they will pay for economic mismanagement since 2011, distrusting the state's ability to deliver promised cash subsidies due to past failures with public goods like salary payments and cash availability at banks.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens fear they will pay the price for economic mismanagement and distrust the state's ability to deliver promised cash subsidies.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Libyan professor backs separate salary budget item

Attia Al-Fitouri, an economics professor, supports separating salaries into an independent budget item for transparency.

Attia Al-Fitouri, an economics professor at the University of Benghazi, has endorsed a proposal to create a distinct budget category for employee salaries. This initiative, supported by the Speaker of the House of Representatives, aims to enhance transparency in government spending.

Al-Fitouri stated that separating salaries would allow them to be distinguished from other financial allocations currently grouped under a broader budget section. This move is intended to provide a clearer picture of how public funds designated for wages are managed.

The proposal seeks to increase accountability within the government's financial operations. Further details on the implementation timeline or the specific impact on different government departments were not provided in the initial report.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens' salaries will be managed with greater transparency.
