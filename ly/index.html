Politics · 2026-11-03

Libya parliament to debate unified public sector pay scale

Teachers demand action as lawmakers review salary legislation that has exposed divisions among public employees.

Libya's House of Representatives will consider a proposed unified public sector salary scale on Monday in Sabha. Parliamentary finance committees finished technical discussions on the bill, which aims for consistent pay across government institutions while managing state expenditure.

The final consultations in Tripoli on Sunday included members of parliament, Central Bank Governor Fawzi Abu Khuzam, Finance Minister Rashid Abu Ghaffa, and ministry representatives. They focused on the financial implications, existing salary differences, and the cost of a unified framework.

Teachers on strike are pressing for immediate approval of the legislation, linking it to outstanding salary entitlements. The Tripoli Court of Appeal has also cautioned parliament against changing judicial pay without consulting the Supreme Judicial Council.

Why it mattersPublic sector employees, including teachers, university academics, and the judiciary, are directly affected by the proposed unified salary scale.

Climate · 2026-11-03

Libya braces for Mediterranean storm Agnetta

The storm is centered south of Italy and moving east with active winds and low pressure.

Libya is preparing for the impact of Mediterranean storm Agnetta, which is currently affecting central and eastern regions of the Mediterranean. The storm is located south of Italy and is characterized by low atmospheric pressure and strong winds around its core.

Fares Fathi, Director of the Geographic and Environmental Systems, stated that the storm is gradually moving eastward. The unstable weather conditions are developing across the region.

The storm's progression indicates potential weather changes for Libya as it moves closer.

Why it mattersLibya is bracing for the impact of storm Agnetta, which is bringing unstable weather and strong winds.

Education · 2026-11-03

Libya council discusses delayed school year

National Planning Council head Ahmed Ibreidan met with MPs and teachers' unions to resolve employment and living demands.

Ahmed Ibreidan, head of Libya's National Planning Council, met with members of parliament and representatives from teachers' unions and faculty members in Tripoli. The meeting focused on finding solutions for the delayed start of the academic year and addressing the employment and living demands of education sector staff.

The discussions aimed to tackle the immediate issues affecting the education sector, including the unresolved employment status and living conditions of teachers. The council and parliamentary members engaged with union representatives to understand the full scope of the educators' concerns.

This meeting signifies an official step towards resolving the ongoing disruptions in Libya's education system. The National Planning Council is expected to propose actionable steps following these deliberations to ensure the resumption of studies and improve conditions for educators.

Why it mattersTeachers and faculty members in Libya face unresolved employment and living demands, impacting the start of the academic year.
