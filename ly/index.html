Libya and Turkey aim for $5 billion trade target

Ministers met in Istanbul to discuss expanding economic ties and joint investment opportunities.

Libya's Minister of Economy and Trade, Suhail Aboushiha, met with Turkey's Minister of Trade, Ã–mer Bolat, in Istanbul to boost economic and commercial relations. The ministers discussed increasing trade volume between the two nations to US$ 5 billion. This goal is to be achieved by revitalizing trade exchanges and broadening private sector participation.

The meeting, held during the 42nd COMCEC Ministerial Session, also focused on holding the first Libyan-Turkish Joint Economic and Trade Commission meeting and a business forum. These events aim to bring business leaders together to explore investment opportunities in priority economic sectors. Italy sees Misrata as logistics hub

Ambassador Alberini stated that two-thirds of goods between Libya, Italy, and Europe pass through the port.

Italy's Ambassador to Libya, Gianluca Alberini, said Misrata and its Free Zone have the potential to become a transcontinental logistics hub. He spoke at the Italian-Libyan Business and Investment Forum held in Misrata Free Zone on Sunday, November 1.

Alberini highlighted the participation of approximately 60 Italian companies and 250 Libyan firms as proof of mutual interest in new collaborations. He noted that Annalisa Tardino, President of the Port System Authority of the Western Sicilian Sea, attended, signaling new prospects for cooperation between Italian and Libyan ports.

Italy remains Libya's primary commercial partner, serving as the main destination for its exports and its leading supplier. Two-thirds of goods moving between Libya, Italy, and Europe transit through Misrata, which Alberini described as a vibrant commercial and industrial hub.

Why it mattersLibyan businesses and the national economy are affected by the potential for Misrata to become a logistics hub and the strengthening of economic relations with Italy.

Mediterranean storm Agneta approaches Libya

Storm Agneta is centered south of Italy and moving east, bringing unstable weather to the Mediterranean.

Mediterranean storm Agneta is bringing unstable weather to the central and eastern Mediterranean. The storm is currently south of Italy with active winds around its core. Fares Fathi, Director of the Geographic and Environmental Systems, stated that the storm continues its gradual eastward movement.

Libya is bracing for the impact of storm Agneta, which is expected to affect the country's central and eastern regions. The storm's development is characterized by low atmospheric pressure and strong winds.

The storm's path indicates a continued eastward trajectory across the Mediterranean Sea.

Why it mattersLibya's central and eastern regions are bracing for unstable weather conditions due to storm Agneta.

German ambassador discusses trade with Misrata Chamber

Libyan businesspeople aim for easier visa access and German expertise to boost economic ties.

The German Ambassador to Libya, Arvid Anders, met with the Misrata Chamber of Commerce today at their Misrata headquarters. He was accompanied by Dominik Lauf-Wilhelm, First Secretary for Political Affairs, Communications, Migration, and Human Rights.

Chamber Chairman Fathi Al-Amin Al-Turki and Yousef Abu Shaiba, Director of the Chamber’s Office of External Relations, discussed strengthening economic and commercial cooperation. They also explored leveraging German expertise and facilitating visa procedures for Libyan businesspeople.

The Chamber Chairman emphasized the importance of enhancing cooperation and opening broader avenues for communication and partnership between the business communities of Libya and Germany.

Why it mattersLibyan businesspeople face challenges with visa procedures and seek German expertise to improve economic and commercial cooperation.

Libya justice minister vows to improve justice system

Minister Khaled Al-Modeer met ministry branch heads in Sirte to discuss workflow challenges and solutions.

Libyan Minister of Justice Khaled Al-Modeer pledged to enhance the justice system and address operational hurdles during a meeting in Sirte on Tuesday. The session brought together heads of ministry branches from across the country to identify and explore solutions for workflow difficulties.

Al-Modeer stated the meeting's objective was to pinpoint major challenges faced by ministry branches and devise appropriate strategies for smoother operations. He emphasized the dedication of branch heads to improving the justice system and the necessity of cooperation for judicial bodies to fulfill their duties and support national stability.

The minister also acknowledged the support of the General Command of the Armed Forces and Lieutenant General Saddam Haftar for development projects in Sirte, noting significant advancements in the city. He thanked Sirte residents and supporting entities like the Libyan Development and Reconstruction Fund for their contributions.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens will be affected by improvements to the justice system and the stability it supports.
