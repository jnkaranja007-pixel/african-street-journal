Libya's Abdelaziz urges 10-year school reform amid teachers' strike

He argues the sector employs far more people than it needs, pointing to 700,000 staff and teachers against 1.3 million students.

Source: Libya Update · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Mahmoud Abdelaziz has criticised the teachers' strike now under way in Libya, saying it is disrupting the academic year and has gone beyond acceptable limits. He also rejected a union proposal to make up missed lessons on Fridays and Saturdays.

Abdelaziz put the number of education staff and teachers at more than 700,000, against 1.3 million students, and said Libya needs no more than 150,000 teachers, citing scientific data. He called for a 10-year plan to reform the sector, insisting that any cut should not leave employees without income, and said a bigger education budget alone would not fix the imbalance.

He said hundreds of thousands were hired with salaries of 150 to 160 dinars, and that many appointments made in and before 2011 should never have been made. He added that paying all outstanding wages at once is unrealistic, and that weekends should stay free for students.

Why it mattersTeachers and education staff face a possible reduction in numbers, with incomes to be protected under the 10-year plan he proposes.
