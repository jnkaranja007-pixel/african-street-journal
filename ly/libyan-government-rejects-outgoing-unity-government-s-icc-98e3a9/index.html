Libyan government rejects outgoing unity government’s ICC declaration

It says the May 2025 acceptance was made without legislative approval and does not represent the state’s position on the court’s jurisdiction.

Source: Libya Update · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Libya’s government has rejected a May 2025 declaration by the outgoing Government of National Unity accepting the International Criminal Court’s jurisdiction, saying it does not express the state’s will. The declaration concerns national judicial sovereignty and was issued without approval from the competent legislative authority, the government said.

In a statement Wednesday, the government said the prosecutor’s briefing to the United Nations Security Council did not reflect Libya’s official position. The House of Representatives opposed the declaration, and the Council of Ministers sent letters to the Security Council and the court. A South Benghazi court suspended its implementation on July 28, 2025; a Benghazi appeals court later halted it and granted its ruling executory status.

The government called on the prosecutor to verify the position of Libya’s institutions and record the objections and court rulings in the file on Libya. It also said it intends to pursue legal action against those responsible for issuing or passing the declaration without constitutional or legal authority.

Why it mattersLibya’s institutions must have their objections and court rulings recorded in the ICC file, and the government says it intends to pursue legal action over the declaration.
