Rehabilitated University of Benghazi Reopens After War Damage

The Libya Development and Reconstruction Fund completed a comprehensive rehabilitation project for the university, which had suffered extensive damage during past conflicts.

The University of Benghazi officially reopened yesterday following a significant rehabilitation and development project. The Libya Development and Reconstruction Fund oversaw the initiative, aiming to restore the institution after it sustained extensive damage during the city's wars. The reopening ceremony was marked by an extravagant event.

Attendees at the ceremony included prominent political and military figures, such as the Speaker of the House of Representatives, Ageela Saleh, the First Deputy Speaker, Fawzi Al-Nuwairi, the Second Deputy Misbah Douma, Prime Minister Osama Hammad, and Field Marshal Khalifa Haftar. Lieutenant General Saddam Haftar described the university as a beacon of knowledge and a symbol of Libyan resilience.

This project is part of the Fund's broader strategy to enhance higher education infrastructure and create an environment suitable for the university's academic and national standing. The rehabilitation aims to provide an integrated university experience for students and faculty, reflecting the institution's historical importance.

Why it mattersThe reopening of the University of Benghazi affects students and faculty by providing a restored and functional educational environment after wartime damage.
