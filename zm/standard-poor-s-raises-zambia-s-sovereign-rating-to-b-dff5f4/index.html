Standard & Poor's raises Zambia's sovereign rating to B-

The upgrade from CCC+/C carries a stable outlook, with the ratings agency citing debt-restructuring progress and sustained fiscal reforms.

Source: Lusaka Times · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

S&P Global Ratings, the New York-based credit rating agency, raised Zambia's sovereign credit rating from CCC+/C to B-/B on November 20, 2026, and assigned a stable outlook. The move comes as the country's debt restructuring nears completion.

S&P cited improved economic performance, progress in restructuring debt and sustained fiscal reforms as reasons for the upgrade. Situmbeko Musokotwane, Zambia's finance and national planning minister, described it as an important milestone in the country's economic recovery.

The revised rating replaces the previous CCC+/C assessment; the announcement gave no further details on the restructuring's completion.

Why it mattersZambia's government and sovereign debt holders now face the B-/B rating, with debt restructuring nearing completion.
