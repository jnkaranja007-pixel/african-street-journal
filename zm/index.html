Freddie Mwila Snr dies aged 80

The former Zambia captain and coach died at Maina Soko Medical Centre in Lusaka on Monday.

Former Zambia national team captain and coach Freddie Mwila Snr has died at the age of 80. Family spokesperson Lillian Kalikeka confirmed his death to FAZ Media.

Mwila passed away at Maina Soko Medical Centre in Lusaka around 01:00 hours on Monday following a stroke. He is remembered as one of Zambia’s most decorated football figures.

Mwila was part of the first generation of Zambian footballers to achieve significant recognition.

Why it mattersThe death of football icon Freddie Mwila Snr, aged 80, leaves Zambian football without one of its decorated figures.

Mweetwa reaffirms loyalty to President Hichilema

Choma Central MP Cornelius Mweetwa states he is a "disciple" of the President after not being appointed to Cabinet.

Choma Central Member of Parliament Cornelius Mweetwa declared his unwavering loyalty to President Hakainde Hichilema and the UPND party on February 11, 2026, following his exclusion from recent ministerial appointments. Mweetwa, a former Minister of Information and Media, stated he has no entitlement to a cabinet position and respects the President's decision.

Speaking at a media briefing, Mweetwa dismissed social media claims linking his non-appointment to opposition ties, asserting his allegiance remains with the UPND and President Hichilema. He described himself as a "disciple" of the President, having shared a long political journey and struggle, and emphasized his commitment to advancing the Grow Zambia Agenda.

Mweetwa urged UPND supporters to resist propaganda designed to create divisions within the ruling party. In a separate statement, Nalolo MP Imanga Wamunyima proposed legislation to penalize parties whose candidates withdraw from elections after ballot papers are printed, citing wasted taxpayer money.

Sport · 2026-11-02

FAZ fines Power Dynamos and Nkana K100,000 each

Football Association of Zambia penalizes clubs for political chants at matches.

The Football Association of Zambia (FAZ) has fined Power Dynamos and Nkana Football Club K100,000 each for allowing political slogans at match venues. The disciplinary committee released separate rulings on Saturday, sanctioning the clubs for breaching FAZ's constitution.

Power Dynamos was fined for supporters chanting political slogans during their MTN Super League match against Nchanga Rangers FC at Arthur Davies Stadium on October 11, 2026. Nkana faced similar charges for political chants by supporters during their match against Makeni All Stars FC at Nkana Stadium on October 10, 2026. FAZ stated this conduct is inconsistent with sportsmanship and neutrality.

Both clubs were asked to show cause for their supporters' actions. Power Dynamos acknowledged responsibility for supporter behaviour, describing it as an isolated incident. Nkana expressed regret and outlined remedial measures in their response to FAZ.

Why it mattersFootball clubs and their supporters in Zambia face financial penalties and stadium bans for political chants during matches.

ZCSA intensifies mobile money scam awareness

Scammers use advanced social engineering tactics to bypass security measures and trap users.

The Zambia Cyber Security Agency (ZCSA) is increasing public awareness campaigns to combat sophisticated mobile money scams and account hacking. Scammers are employing advanced social engineering tactics that bypass existing safety measures and breach security systems.

ZCSA Director of Research, Education and Awareness, Edgar Mlauzi, stated that these tactics are making it increasingly difficult to protect users. The agency is working to educate the public on these evolving threats.

The ZCSA's efforts aim to equip citizens with knowledge to identify and avoid these scams. Further details on specific scam methods and protective measures are expected from the agency.

Why it mattersZambians using mobile money services are at risk of losing funds due to advanced scam tactics.

Business · 2026-11-02

Konkola Copper Mines employs 14,500 in Zambia

The mining company's workforce has grown 30 percent since Vedanta's reinstatement, with salaries also increasing.

Konkola Copper Mines (KCM) is now Zambia’s largest private sector employer, with over 14,500 people working at its operations in Chingola, Chililabombwe, Kitwe, and Nampundwe. This represents a 30 percent increase from approximately 11,500 employees when Vedanta was reinstated as owner.

KCM employees support multiple dependants, meaning the number of Zambians relying on KCM salaries for basic needs is significantly higher than the payroll number. Salaries have risen by about 35 percent since July 2024, with a further 13 percent increase in January 2026.

The company has invested $20 million through its Community Trust, reaching about 100,000 people with malaria control, scholarships, and skills training. KCM and its employees also generated approximately $180 million for the government through taxes and royalties.

Why it mattersKonkola Copper Mines' expansion and salary increases directly benefit thousands of Zambian workers and their families, impacting their ability to afford food, rent, school fees, and medicine.
