Nkana and Power Dynamos fined K100,000 each

The Football Association of Zambia sanctioned two clubs for political slogans at matches.

Nkana Football Club and Power Dynamos have each been fined K100,000 by the Football Association of Zambia (FAZ) Disciplinary Committee. The sanctions follow breaches of FAZ's constitution, which prohibits political slogans at match venues. The Football Association of Zambia released separate rulings on Saturday, October 31, 2026.

The Football Association of Zambia issued the ruling after a complaint from the Zambian Premier League (ZPL) concerning political chants during a MTN Super League fixture. Nkana Football Club stated on October 31, 2026, that it respects the committee's decision.

The charges against both clubs relate to games played in the MTN Super League. The FAZ Disciplinary Committee's decisions were made on October 30, 2026.

Health · 2026-11-01

UTH water project reaches 70 percent completion

The K69.7 million project aims to ensure reliable water supply and prevent infections at the hospital.

The University Teaching Hospitals (UTH) water supply improvement project in Lusaka is 70 percent complete, with remaining works scheduled for December. Minister of Health Roma Chilengi stated that reliable water is vital in health facilities to avoid disrupting services and prevent infections like cholera.

Professor Chilengi emphasized the government's commitment to resolving UTH's water challenges for a safe and hygienic patient environment. He noted that water is a fundamental human right and essential for patient care, sanitation, and cleaning in hospitals.

The project was initiated after the 2023 cholera outbreak highlighted the need for better water and sanitation infrastructure. The Ministry of Water Development and Sanitation engaged Lusaka Water Supply and Sanitation Company in late 2023 to assess and recommend improvements for UTH's water system.

Why it mattersPatients at the University Teaching Hospitals will receive care in a safer and more hygienic environment due to improved water supply.

Agriculture · 2026-11-01

Zambia launches presidential irrigation pilot

Five schools in Kaoma District will each cultivate one hectare of maize under the new programme.

The Presidential Irrigation Initiative Programme has been launched as a pilot in Kaoma District, Western Province. This initiative is a key part of the Grow Zambia Agenda, designed to improve the nation's food security. The launch event took place at Kaoma Secondary School.

Under this programme, five schools within Kaoma district will each cultivate one hectare of maize. This pilot phase aims to test the effectiveness of the irrigation initiative before wider implementation.

The programme's success will be evaluated based on maize yields and its contribution to food security. Further details on the programme's timeline and expansion plans were not immediately available.

Why it mattersFarmers in Kaoma District will benefit from the pilot irrigation programme aimed at enhancing maize production.

Business · 2026-11-01

Zambia raises fuel prices for November

Petrol, diesel, and kerosene costs increase due to global oil prices and Kwacha depreciation.

The Energy Regulation Board (ERB) has increased fuel prices for November, raising petrol by K1.60 per litre, diesel by K1.18, and kerosene by K2.32.

Petrol now costs K33.06 per litre, diesel K34.45, and kerosene K32.08, according to ERB Board Chairperson James Banda, who cited rising international oil prices and Kwacha depreciation as reasons for the adjustments.

The ERB noted that international oil prices have risen due to Middle East tensions, with petrol increasing from US$121.58 to US$125.84 per barrel, though diesel prices saw a slight decrease.

Why it mattersZambians will pay more for petrol, diesel, and kerosene starting in November.

Politics · 2026-11-01

Zambia plans orderly urban growth

Vice-President Nalumango says urban planning must improve services without displacing people.

Vice-President Mutale Nalumango stated that urban planning in Zambia must address community needs by enhancing access to basic services while avoiding unnecessary displacement of people and their livelihoods. She announced the government's commitment to fostering orderly human settlements, upgrading infrastructure, and expanding economic prospects as urban areas grow. Minister of Local Government and Rural Development Gift Sialubalo delivered the speech on her behalf at the National Urban Conference in Lusaka.

Mrs Nalumango emphasized that urban development should be inclusive and practical, ensuring planning leads to better living conditions for citizens. She noted that challenges like unplanned settlements, poor drainage, sanitation, housing congestion, and insecurity require coordinated planning and infrastructure investment. Land allocation and investment decisions should be guided by spatial planning and supported by essential infrastructure such as roads, water, schools, and health facilities.

The government is also promoting decentralisation to move decision-making closer to communities, with local authorities tasked to translate development priorities into actionable plans and projects. Integrated development plans are important in guiding local investments, and national institutions are urged to align their support with local authority priorities. Chief Chamuka called for stronger collaboration between traditional leaders, local authorities, and policymakers to manage urban expansion, especially in customary areas facing high land demand.

Why it mattersUrban planning decisions by the government and local authorities will affect where citizens live and access services.
