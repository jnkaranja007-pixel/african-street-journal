Opposition Leaders Mundubile, Zulu Detained on Treason Charges

Brian Mundubile, the leading opposition presidential candidate, and his running mate Makebi Zulu were detained at Lusaka Central Police after hours of interrogation regarding.

Opposition figures Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu are being held at Lusaka Central Police Station after police issued them a Warn and Caution Statement. The charges relate to treason, following extensive questioning by authorities. Mundubile had reportedly been in hiding after disputing the results of the recent presidential election.

The detention follows a period of police interrogation that lasted for hours. The exact nature of the treason allegations remains unclear, but the development marks a significant escalation in the political climate following the election. Both individuals are prominent figures within the opposition.

The standoff ended with their arrival at the police station, where they are now detained. This action comes after Mundubile had been in hiding, a move reportedly linked to his dissatisfaction with the presidential election outcome. Further details on the charges and their implications are expected.

Zambian Courts Reopen After Election Petition Dispute

Courts in Zambia resumed operations Thursday after a four-day closure due to a security threat on the deadline for challenging presidential election results.

Zambian courts reopened on Thursday, August 27, following a shutdown that began on Monday, the deadline for filing petitions against the presidential election results. Chief Administrator Yvonne N. Nalomba announced the staff return in a memo dated August 26, stating that security agencies had advised the threat had diminished. Police presence will remain visible at all court buildings to ensure continued security.

The closure occurred on the final day to challenge the August 13 presidential election results, which saw President Hakainde Hichilema declared the winner on August 18. The Judiciary reported that no petition was officially filed in the Constitutional Court Registry by the 18:00 deadline. However, Chief Justice Dr. Mumba Malila received an electronic copy of a document termed the “people’s presidential petition” via private email, which the Judiciary deemed a highly irregular delivery.

Despite the irregular delivery of the electronic petition, Chief Justice Malila referred the document to the Constitutional Court for a ruling on its proper filing. The court closure had drawn criticism from some opposition figures who questioned its timing. State House denied President Hichilema ordered the closure, asserting that security assessments were conducted across multiple government ministries.

Why it mattersThe reopening of courts allows for the formal processing of election challenges, impacting the stability of the electoral process and governance in Zambia.

Bus-Truck Crash Kills Four on Great East Road

A head-on collision between a bus and a truck in Rufunsa District on Thursday resulted in four fatalities and 36 injuries, according to police.

Four people died and 36 were injured when a bus carrying 57 passengers collided with a truck on the Great East Road in Rufunsa District. The crash occurred around 11 a.m. Thursday. Police identified the bus as a Zhongtong public service vehicle operated by Power Tools Company and the truck as a Volvo.

Preliminary police investigations suggest the truck driver swerved into the opposite lane to avoid a stationary vehicle, leading to the head-on collision with the bus. The bus was traveling toward Chipata, while the truck was headed for Lusaka.

The bus driver and his conductor, who was also his son, were pronounced dead at Mpanshya Mission Hospital. Thirty-five bus passengers and the truck driver sustained injuries and were admitted to the same hospital.

Why it mattersThe accident directly impacts travelers and their families, potentially disrupting transportation services and causing grief.

Zambia Inflation Drops to 6.2% on Lower Food and Fuel Prices

Annual inflation in Zambia decreased to 6.2% in August, down from 6.5% in July, as reported by the Zambia Statistics Agency, driven by falling prices for essential food items.

Zambia's annual inflation rate fell to 6.2% in August, a decrease from the 6.5% recorded in July, according to the Zambia Statistics Agency. Statistician General Sheila Mudenda stated that this rate signifies a 6.2% increase in the average prices of goods and services over the twelve months leading up to August 2026.

The decline in overall inflation was significantly influenced by a slowdown in food prices, which eased to 6.0% in August from 6.4% in July. Mudenda attributed this easing to reduced costs for cereals such as breakfast mealie meal and roller mealie meal, maize grain, local rice, wheat flour, sugar, cooking oil, and dried beans.

Non-food inflation also experienced a slight deceleration, moving from 6.7% in July to 6.6% in August. This reduction was linked to price changes in fuels and lubricants, including diesel, petrol, and paraffin, as well as engine oil, brake fluid, and the purchase of vehicles.

Why it mattersThis event affects farmers through lower prices for cereals, sugar, cooking oil, and beans, and impacts investors through reduced fuel costs for transport and machinery.
