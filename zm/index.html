ZRA says fake export claims drain treasury

The Zambia Revenue Authority paid K25 billion in VAT refunds last year, with fraudulent claims costing the treasury.

The Zambia Revenue Authority (ZRA) paid over K25 billion in Value Added Tax (VAT) refunds to businesses last year. Commissioner General Dingani Banda stated that the treasury is losing substantial revenue due to fraudulent refund claims. One firm's gold exports yielded only K64 million, highlighting the scale of the issue.

Banda explained that these fake export claims are draining the treasury, impacting national finances. The ZRA is investigating these fraudulent activities to recover lost funds and prevent future losses. The exact number of firms involved in these fraudulent claims was not immediately available.

The ZRA's investigation into these claims is ongoing. The authority is implementing stricter measures to verify export documentation and prevent further revenue leakage. This crackdown aims to ensure that legitimate businesses receive their refunds while curbing fraudulent activities.

Why it mattersThe Zambia Revenue Authority is investigating fraudulent export claims that are draining the treasury, impacting national finances.

Kalumbila district commissioner and driver killed in road crash

Their council vehicle crashed into a truck early Sunday on the Mutanda-Mwinilunga Road, killing both occupants.

Kalumbila District Commissioner Brenda Sankisa and her driver were killed early on Sunday when their Kalumbila District Council vehicle crashed into a truck on the Mutanda-Mwinilunga Road.

The deaths came on the day Zambia joined the rest of the world in remembering people lost to road traffic accidents. The report does not give the cause of the collision.

Ms Sankisa was the district's top government official, a post appointed by central government to oversee administration and development in the district.

Why it mattersThe council loses its district commissioner, leaving the district's administration to be led in her absence.

Opposition NDC says Zambia's Grow Zambia Agenda is failing in practice

National Democratic Congress president Saboi Imboela says the 2027 national budget does not show the agenda delivering results, and the government has asked the opposition.

National Democratic Congress (NDC) president Saboi Imboela has accused the United Party for National Development (UPND) administration of failing to turn the Grow Zambia Agenda into tangible results. She said the 2027 national budget does not reflect the agenda in practice.

The government has told the opposition to write in if it wants talks with President Hichilema, according to the report. The NDC has framed the agenda as good on paper but weak in delivery, a complaint the report attributes to Imboela.

The report does not give further budget figures or a timetable for the talks.

Why it mattersThe NDC's dispute with the government over the agenda's delivery sets up whether the opposition gets a formal meeting with the president.

Chitambala penalties send Zambia U-23 into next round

Zambia overturned a 1-0 first-leg defeat to Burkina Faso, winning the shootout 3-2 after a 1-1 aggregate draw in Lusaka.

Zambia have advanced to the final qualifying round of the TotalEnergies CAF U-23 Africa Cup of Nations Egypt 2027 after beating Burkina Faso. The Junior Chipolopolo overturned a 1-0 first-leg defeat in Lusaka on Sunday, winning 3-2 on penalties after the tie finished 1-1 on aggregate.

Substitute Caleb Musonda scored a late goal to take the second leg to extra time and penalties. Goalkeeper Chitambala saved decisively in the shootout, and the win keeps Zambia's Under-23 side in the continental qualifiers.

The report from News Diggers, published on Monday, names Chitambala as the player whose penalty saves decided the tie. Zambia's next opponent and match date were not given in the reports.

Why it mattersZambia's Under-23 side now plays in the final qualifying round for the continental tournament, which qualifies a team for Egypt 2027.

Business · 2026-11-16

Zambia proposes joint food showcase with Japan

The proposal aims to link chefs, producers, and investors to boost trade and tourism.

Zambia's Trade and Exports Minister Caroline Kaunda Katotobwe proposed a joint culinary and export showcase with Japan to foster business ties. The event would connect chefs, producers, exporters, and investors through demonstrations, exhibitions, and business meetings.

Katotobwe put forward the idea at a reception in Lusaka, stating that food can preserve identity and drive economic development, citing Japan's UNESCO-recognized washoku cuisine as an example. She believes culinary diplomacy could open the Japanese market to Zambian horticultural products, fruits, honey, and processed foods, encouraging value addition.

The proposed showcase would involve the Embassy of Japan, relevant Zambian ministries, and the private sector. Katotobwe also suggested pairing Zambian food experiences with attractions like Victoria Falls to strengthen destination marketing and attract more tourists.
