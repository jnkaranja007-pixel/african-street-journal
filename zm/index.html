Zambian minister declines to say whether Hichilema backs ending term limit

Rival petitions on Article 106(3) of the Constitution are now before the National Assembly, and one quotes the president's own "third term" remarks.

Justice Minister Jack Mwiimbu declined to say whether President Hakainde Hichilema would support scrapping the presidential term limit if Parliament approved it. Rival petitions on Article 106(3) of the Constitution are now before the National Assembly.

One of the petitions quotes the president's own past words on a "third term". Mwiimbu's refusal to take a position leaves open where the government stands on the amendment before lawmakers.

The petitions will be considered by the National Assembly, which will decide whether the constitutional provision is changed.

Why it mattersIf lawmakers approve the change, the question of whether the president would back it will shape how Zambians judge the amendment.

Agriculture · 2026-11-15

Zambia reservoir boosts smallholder farming

A new water reservoir established by the WASA project will support 50 households in Chief Mulendema’s area, improving water access and enabling year-round cultivation.

A new communal reservoir capable of holding 1.62 million litres of water is transforming farming for smallholder farmers in Chief Mulendema’s area, Zambia. The Water and Soil Accelerator (WASA) project established the reservoir to combat erratic rainfall and dry conditions, aiming to provide a consistent water source for agricultural production during dry periods.

The Mini Water Reservoir is designed to support 50 households, benefiting 250 people by improving water access, reducing production costs, and increasing crop yields. Solidaridad Southern Africa, with support from the International Water Management Institute (IWMI) and local partners including the Ministry of Agriculture, is implementing the project. Chief Mulendema stated that the reservoir offers the community a chance to plan beyond immediate rainfall and emphasized fair benefit sharing.

The project combines water storage with practical support for farmers, including training in sustainable farming and responsible water management. Plans are underway to establish a vegetable garden for practical learning on efficient water use. Solidaridad's Country Manager for Zambia, Sheila Garakara, noted that the intervention expands choices for farmers, particularly women, to strengthen household incomes.

Why it mattersFarmers in Chief Mulendema’s area can now plan planting and crop maintenance through dry spells, with a new reservoir providing water for up to 250 people.

Lusaka police and council detain 54 over 'Chi Cup' drinking

Police and council officers swept the area near two Lusaka colleges, catching buyers and drinkers of the illicit spirit known as 'Chi Cup'.

54 people were arrested in Lusaka for buying and drinking alcohol popularly known as “Chi Cup”, in an operation near the National Institute of Public Administration and Evelyn Hone College. The Lusaka City Council said the Zambia Police Service joined it in the NIPA area at about 10:20 p.m. on Friday.

20 of those detained were found drinking the spirit, the council said. Its stated aim was to curb consumption of such drinks in public spaces, particularly close to learning institutions.

The council did not say what action would follow against those arrested, but said it would keep enforcing public order rules.

Why it mattersAnyone buying or selling Chi Cup near Lusaka's colleges now faces the same arrests the council has begun enforcing.

Business · 2026-11-15

Zambia proposes joint food showcase with Japan

The proposal aims to link chefs, producers, and investors to boost trade and tourism.

Zambia's Trade and Exports Minister Caroline Kaunda Katotobwe proposed a joint culinary and export showcase with Japan to foster business ties. The event would connect chefs, producers, exporters, and investors through demonstrations, exhibitions, and business meetings.

Katotobwe put forward the idea at a reception in Lusaka, stating that food can preserve identity and drive economic development, citing Japan's UNESCO-recognized washoku cuisine as an example. She believes culinary diplomacy could open the Japanese market to Zambian horticultural products, fruits, honey, and processed foods, encouraging value addition.

The proposed showcase would involve the Embassy of Japan, relevant Zambian ministries, and the private sector. Katotobwe also suggested pairing Zambian food experiences with attractions like Victoria Falls to strengthen destination marketing and attract more tourists.
