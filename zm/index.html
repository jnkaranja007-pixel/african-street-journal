Hichilema Sworn In Amidst Rival's Treason Charge

President-elect Hakainde Hichilema begins his second term as rival Brian Mundubile faces treason charges and detention.

President-elect Hakainde Hichilema is set to be sworn in for a second term today at Heroes Stadium in Lusaka. This inauguration follows closely on the heels of his main rival, Brian Mundubile, being moved to Mukobeko Maximum Security Prison on a treason charge. The proximity of these events, occurring within 48 hours of each other, highlights the deeply divided political climate surrounding Zambia's transition.

Chief Justice Dr Mumba Malila will administer the presidential oath, with Vice-President Mutale Nalumango also taking hers. Foreign dignitaries, including Botswana President Duma Boko and China's special envoy Xian Hui, are confirmed attendees. Mundubile and his running mate were transferred to Mukobeko on Sunday morning after their treason charge on Saturday, with their continued detention drawing criticism from opposition groups who allege political persecution.

Political analyst Dr Lawrence Mwelwa urged the ruling UPND to cease its election petitions against opposition winners, citing a need for consistency after declaring the presidential poll fair. Approximately 30 parliamentary election petitions are pending in the Lusaka High Court. Socialist Party president Dr Fred M’membe warned that Zambia's international standing in UN peacekeeping missions could be at risk due to actions perceived as blurring the lines between the army and party politics during the election period.

Why it mattersThe inauguration of President Hichilema and the detention of his rival Brian Mundubile on treason charges create political uncertainty for the nation's leadership and legal processes.

Losing Candidates File Thirty Election Petitions in Zambia

Former ministers and other candidates are challenging parliamentary election results in Lusaka High Court, citing alleged malpractice and non-compliance with electoral laws.

Approximately thirty parliamentary election petitions have been lodged with the Lusaka High Court by losing candidates, including former ministers Paul Kabuswe and Sylvia Masebo. These candidates are seeking to nullify election results due to alleged malpractice and failures to adhere to the Electoral Process Act. The Electoral Commission of Zambia is named as a respondent in all cases, accused of breaching various provisions of the Act, which petitioners claim impacted the outcomes in their respective constituencies.

Allegations range from character assassination and vote-buying to voter intimidation and the obstruction of voters. For instance, Paul Kabuswe claims voters were offered money and that vehicles transporting voters were blocked. Sylvia Masebo alleges defamatory statements were circulated against her. Other petitions cite violence, corruption, and procedural irregularities, with some candidates seeking full election re-runs or recounts.

The challenges span multiple constituencies, including Chililabombwe, Chongwe East, Kanchibiya, Milanzi, Chitambo, Nyimba South, Mwandi, Ndola Central, Chingola Central, and Kabushi. Petitioners are contesting the victories of candidates from various parties and independent candidates, with accusations including vote-buying, bribery, and the concealment of cash within items distributed to voters.

Why it mattersLosing parliamentary candidates are challenging election results, alleging malpractice and non-compliance with electoral laws, which could lead to recounts or re-runs.

Fire Destroys Lusaka Market Property, Suspects Arrested

A blaze at Buseko Market late on August 30th consumed merchandise and structures, with two youths detained for alleged theft.

A fire swept through Buseko Market in Lusaka's Matero area on the night of August 30, 2026, destroying property belonging to multiple traders. The blaze, first observed between 9:30 p.m. and 10:30 p.m., was reported to Matero Police Station by the public around 12:30 a.m. the following morning. Preliminary police findings indicate the fire originated in a shop selling wooden poles and planks on the market's eastern side, subsequently spreading to adjacent premises.

While investigations into the exact cause are ongoing, an unattended charcoal brazier is suspected of contributing to the outbreak. The total value of destroyed property, including planks and merchandise, remains unestablished as many traders have yet to report their losses. Firefighting efforts were undertaken by the Lusaka City Council Fire Brigade, Zambia National Service, Zambia Air Force, and TAZAMA personnel, who successfully extinguished the fire.

During the incident, two male suspects, aged 18 and 17, were apprehended on suspicion of theft after allegedly being found removing Bonanza gaming machines from an affected shop. Three gaming machines were recovered. Defense and security personnel have been deployed to the market to prevent further theft, vandalism, and other criminal activities.

Why it mattersTraders and business owners at Buseko Market are affected by the destruction of their property and merchandise, while two youths face charges for alleged theft.

ERB Maintains Fuel Prices for September 2026

The Energy Regulation Board will keep pump prices unchanged for September 2026, utilizing mitigation measures despite global price pressures.

The Energy Regulation Board (ERB) has announced that fuel pump prices for September 2026 will remain the same as the previous month. This decision comes despite upward pressure on international oil prices for petrol, diesel, kerosene, and Jet A-1, attributed to geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and fluctuating exchange rates.

ERB Board Chairperson James Banda stated in a release on Monday, August 31, 2026, that the board maintained current prices through the continued application of mitigation strategies. These include the suspension of excise duty, zero-rating of value-added tax, and the use of a regulatory price smoothing mechanism.

The national uniform pump prices per litre will therefore remain in effect until the next scheduled review. This stability in fuel costs is intended to provide a predictable economic environment for consumers and businesses across the country.

Why it mattersThis decision affects businesses and consumers by maintaining current fuel costs, impacting transportation and operational expenses.

ZESCO Ndola Girls Coach Vows Return After COSAFA Final Loss

The Zambian champions conceded a late penalty to Mamelodi Sundowns, marking their second consecutive year as finalists.

ZESCO Ndola Girls coach Florence Mwila stated that Saturday's 2-1 defeat to Mamelodi Sundowns in the CAF Women’s Champions League COSAFA Qualifier final was unexpected. The Zambian champions were unable to secure the title, losing in the final for the second year in a row after a controversial late penalty in Botswana. Mwila acknowledged a slow start but praised her team's fighting spirit and commitment throughout the tournament. ZESCO had previously topped Group A with three wins and defeated Silver Strikers 2-1 to reach the final match.

Despite the disappointment, Mwila highlighted the positives gained from the competition, emphasizing the players' dedication. She noted that conceding the penalty under pressure was a significant setback. Mamelodi Sundowns will now represent the COSAFA region at the continental finals of the CAF Women’s Champions League. Mwila expressed her team's determination to defend their championship back home and improve on their weaknesses.

The coach vowed that the team will return stronger and better prepared for future competitions. "We have a task to defend the championship back home. We will improve in the grey areas as a team so that when we come back we will come back for the title," Mwila stated. The team's focus now shifts to domestic competitions and preparing for another attempt at the regional title next year.
