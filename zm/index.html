Health · 2026-10-31

UTH water project 70 percent complete

The K69.7 million project aims to ensure reliable water supply for patient care and sanitation at the hospital.

The K69.7 million University Teaching Hospitals (UTH) water supply improvement project in Lusaka is 70 percent complete, with remaining works scheduled for completion by December this year. Health Minister Roma Chilengi stated that reliable water is essential in health facilities, where shortages can disrupt service delivery and expose patients to infections, including cholera. The government is committed to resolving UTH's long-standing water issues for a safe and hygienic environment.

Professor Chilengi emphasized water's non-negotiable role in hospitals for patient care, sanitation, and medical services, comparing a lack of water to closing the facility. He urged authorities to prevent wastage due to leaks, especially given the country's energy challenges. The improved system is also intended to benefit the Women and Newborn Hospital and the Cancer Diseases Hospital on the UTH premises.

The project was initiated after the 2023 cholera outbreak highlighted the need for better water and sanitation infrastructure in health facilities. The Ministry of Water Development and Sanitation, through Lusaka Water Supply and Sanitation Company, assessed UTH's water infrastructure in late 2023 to identify necessary improvements.

Why it mattersPatients at the University Teaching Hospitals face risks of infection and disrupted care due to unreliable water supply.

Zambia president orders end to plot allocation without infrastructure

President Hichilema directs housing minister to address 1.5 million deficit and prevent unplanned settlements.

President Hakainde Hichilema has ordered Housing Minister Christopher Kalila to stop local authorities from allocating plots without basic infrastructure. The president wants lasting solutions to unplanned settlements and strengthened urban planning standards. He also tasked Transport and Logistics Minister Charles Milupi with accelerating goods movement along economic corridors.

Speaking at a swearing-in ceremony, President Hichilema stressed that land for housing must have plans and essential services like roads. He urged Dr. Kalila to tackle Zambia's 1.5 million housing deficit while ensuring councils adhere to planning rules. The president warned that inadequate services could lead to public health issues like cholera.

The president also called for more multi-storey buildings to maximize land use and for affordable housing initiatives with effective mortgage systems. He emphasized clearing blocked roads for emergency vehicles. For the transport sector, Mr. Hichilema wants road and railway infrastructure to support faster goods transportation, identifying economic corridors as key development drivers.

Why it mattersPresident Hichilema's directive impacts citizens seeking housing by demanding that plots come with essential services, aiming to prevent the growth of unplanned settlements and associated health risks.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Zambia candidate withdraws from by-election citing threats

Forum for Democracy and Development candidate Daniel Chisenga quits Mandevu race, leaving three contenders.

Forum for Democracy and Development (FDD) parliamentary candidate Daniel Chisenga withdrew from the November 5 Mandevu by-election, citing life threats. Mr Chisenga stated yesterday that unknown individuals and political players threatened him, leading to his decision to quit the race. He has informed the Electoral Commission of Zambia (ECZ) of his withdrawal.

Chisenga's exit reduces the number of candidates for the Mandevu seat to three, with the constituency having over 123,000 registered voters. The remaining candidates are Adrian Banda of the UPND, Ntazana Musukuma of the Leadership Movement, and independent Daniel Daka. The ECZ is training 730 poll staff in Lusaka to ensure professionalism and impartiality.

ECZ vice-chairperson Vincent Mukanda urged poll staff to be exemplary and familiarise themselves with the Electoral Code of Conduct. Acting chief electoral officer Collins Kachaka also stressed the importance of accurate record-keeping and control at polling stations. The Tonse Pamodzi Alliance has asked the ECZ to cancel the by-election, alleging intimidation and offers of incentives to candidates.

Why it mattersThe Mandevu by-election in Zambia is affected by candidate withdrawals due to threats, impacting the electoral process and voter choice.

Climate · 2026-10-31

Kalusha Bwalya drainage project nears completion

Lusaka City Council says the 7.2-kilometre project will improve stormwater management and prevent flooding.

Works on the 7.2-kilometre Kalusha Bwalya drainage project in Lusaka are nearing completion. The Lusaka City Council is undertaking the project to improve stormwater management and prevent flooding in flood-prone areas.

The project is among several drainage clearing activities the council is undertaking across the city. These efforts aim to ensure drainages are free from blockages.

The council stated that the Kalusha Bwalya drainage project is part of efforts to improve stormwater management.

Why it mattersResidents in flood-prone areas of Lusaka will experience improved stormwater management and reduced flooding.

Man denies paternity of child heard through rumours

A 23-year-old man told the Kabushi Local Court he cannot accept responsibility for a child he only heard about from neighbours.

Rafael Mwamba, 23, of Main Masala, told the Kabushi Local Court on October 31, 2026, that he cannot accept responsibility for his ex-girlfriend’s baby. Mwamba stated he only learned about the pregnancy and birth through rumours from neighbours and friends.

He insisted that he cannot accept a child he only heard about through rumours, maintaining that his ex-girlfriend never personally informed him of her pregnancy. This statement was made during a court hearing concerning the child's paternity.

The court heard testimony from Mwamba regarding his lack of direct knowledge of the pregnancy. The case proceeded with arguments about the evidence of paternity presented.

Why it mattersA man is denying paternity of a child because he claims he only heard about the pregnancy and birth through rumours.
