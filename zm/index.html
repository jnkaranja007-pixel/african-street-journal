Zambians urged to uphold integrity on Remembrance Day

President Hichilema led commemorations at the national cenotaph, laying wreaths for fallen soldiers.

President Hakainde Hichilema led service chiefs, diplomats, war veterans, and government officials in commemorating Remembrance Day at the national cenotaph in Lusaka. Zambians were urged to uphold integrity, discipline, patriotism, and responsibility while reflecting on the legacy they wish to leave behind.

During the commemorative service, Zambia Police Service head of chaplaincy Kondwelani Sakala reflected on the sacrifices made by men and women in uniform. Bishop Sakala challenged present-day leaders and citizens to consider if their conduct would make those who sacrificed their lives proud, emphasizing that service means accepting responsibility for the welfare of others.

Bishop Sakala stated that society needs to preserve fundamental values such as integrity, discipline, and patriotism, despite rapid technological advancement and emerging security threats. He added that soldiers, police officers, public servants, teachers, and parents all have a responsibility to make a positive difference in the lives of those around them.

Why it mattersZambians are urged to reflect on their personal conduct and contributions to society.

Activist Demands clarity on detentions

Brebner Changala asks if Zambia is under a state of emergency due to continued detentions.

Civil rights activist Brebner Changala has asked the government to state clearly if Zambia is under a state of emergency. He said the ongoing detention of citizens without court appearances raises questions about constitutional rights.

Mr Changala appealed to the Human Rights Commission to investigate the detentions. He stated that the continued holding of individuals without trial is a violation of their fundamental freedoms.

The activist's demand comes amid concerns over the state of civil liberties in the country. The government has not yet responded to Mr Changala's questions regarding the detentions.

Why it mattersZambian citizens are affected by potential violations of their constitutional rights due to ongoing detentions.

Africa's LPG market needs $2 billion annual investment

The continent's fastest-growing petroleum product requires infrastructure upgrades to meet rising demand for clean cooking.

Africa needs about $2 billion annually by 2040 to achieve universal clean cooking access, a fraction of global energy investment. Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) is the continent's defining downstream opportunity for the next two decades, with demand growing rapidly. Nearly one billion Africans still cook with firewood and charcoal, leading to significant household air pollution deaths.

Despite a 12% demand growth last year, sub-Saharan Africa's LPG supply chain is underdeveloped. The region consumed 6.4 million tonnes in 2025, far below North Africa's consumption, and forecasts predict demand will nearly triple to 17 million tonnes by 2045. Investment is urgently needed in processing, import terminals, bottling plants, and logistics to connect supply to demand.

The current infrastructure cannot handle large import vessels, and storage facilities are limited, often covering only 10 days of demand. This leads to reliance on expensive spot market purchases and price volatility, forcing households back to charcoal when fuel costs double. Experts state capital is available, but bankable structures for the entire value chain are lacking.

Why it mattersAfrican households seeking cleaner cooking methods and businesses involved in the LPG supply chain are directly affected by the need for infrastructure investment.

Zambia proposes K284.8 billion budget for 2027

Domestic revenue will fund 82.1% of the budget, a significant increase from previous years.

Zambia has proposed a K284.8 billion national budget for 2027, with domestic revenue expected to finance K233.7 billion, representing 82.1% of the total expenditure. This marks a substantial increase in self-reliance compared to 58.7% in 2021 and 76% in 2024.

Of the K90.5 billion allocated to general public services, K67.6 billion will be directed towards debt servicing, including K50.8 billion domestically and K16.8 billion externally. An additional K4.7 billion is set aside to clear arrears for suppliers and contractors, potentially enabling businesses to resume operations.

Economic affairs will receive K75.9 billion, with K22.7 billion specifically for agriculture, fisheries, and livestock. This includes K9.1 billion for farmer input support and K100 million for beef compartments to boost exports, alongside a planned agricultural loan scheme starting in 2027.

Why it mattersZambians may see improvements in food availability, job opportunities, affordable transport, medicines, and education due to the budget's focus on domestic revenue and key economic sectors.

LAZ postpones hearing of complaint against minister

The Law Association of Zambia adjourned a hearing for a complaint against Foreign Affairs Minister Mulambo Haimbe.

The Law Association of Zambia (LAZ) has postponed a hearing for a complaint filed by UPND member Charles Longwe against Foreign Affairs Minister Mulambo Haimbe. The matter was scheduled for hearing but has been adjourned to a date that will be communicated later.

Mr Longwe stated that the postponement was due to the unavailability of both parties, as they were both out of the country when the hearing was due. The complaint concerns the minister's conduct.

LAZ will advise on a new date for the hearing once both Mr Longwe and Minister Haimbe are available. The specific nature of the complaint against Minister Haimbe has not been detailed.
