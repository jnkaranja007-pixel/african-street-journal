Zambia Upgrades Anti-Money Laundering Framework

Three key areas of Zambia's financial crime defenses now meet international standards, improving compliance with global anti-money laundering recommendations.

Zambia has advanced its efforts against financial crimes, with three components of its anti-money laundering framework now upgraded to meet Financial Action Task Force (FATF) standards. Attorney-General Mulilo Kabesha announced the progress, stating that Zambia is now compliant or largely compliant with 38 out of 40 FATF Recommendations.

The improvements cover transparency and beneficial ownership of legal entities, as well as the regulation and supervision of both financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses and professions. These upgrades were achieved during recent meetings of the Eastern and Southern Africa Anti-Money Laundering Group (ESAAMLG) in Kigali, Rwanda.

Mr. Kabesha, who also chairs the Anti-Money Laundering Authority, credited the advancements to collaborative efforts between public and private sectors. He urged Zambian institutions to focus on the practical effectiveness of anti-financial crime measures, moving beyond mere compliance with requirements.

Why it mattersInvestors may see improved confidence in Zambia's financial system due to strengthened anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing measures.

Military Takes Tally Centers, Courts Close as Zambia Election Results Challenged

Allegations of tampering and a nationwide court closure prevented an opposition challenge to President Hichilema's victory.

Zambians elected a new president on August 13, 2026, in an election that began calmly but became troubled after polls closed. The Electoral Commission of Zambia suspended vote counting for several hours without explanation as President Hakainde Hichilema was reportedly trailing. During this period, the military took control of tallying centers, with some observers alleging they were removed, raising transparency concerns. When counting resumed, Hichilema took the lead, and claims of tampered seals and altered figures emerged.

Opposition leader Brian Mundubile announced his intention to challenge the results, after which his home was raided by the military, resulting in the death of former Minister of Transport Mutotwe Kafwaya. Mundubile and his running mate were charged with treason, prompting a UN call for their release. On the final day for filing election petitions, courts nationwide were closed, preventing Mundubile from submitting his challenge.

Hakainde Hichilema was inaugurated on September 1, 2026. Supporters view this as a move for the country to progress, citing congratulatory messages from global partners. However, critics point to the military's involvement and the court closures as evidence of interference, warning that this sets a dangerous precedent for future elections in the copper-rich nation.

Why it mattersThe military's actions and the closure of courts have created uncertainty around the electoral process and the legitimacy of the presidency, potentially impacting stability and governance.

Nakonde schools receive irrigation funding

Four schools in Zambia's Muchinga Province will receive funding for irrigation projects to boost food security and school feeding programs.

Four schools in Nakonde district have been funded under the Presidential Irrigation Initiative Programme (PIIP) to promote food security and sustain school feeding programs. Ministry of Education District Planning Officer John Zimba stated that the initiative targets schools with active production units for sustainability. The program is being implemented in three secondary schools and one primary school.

Beneficiary schools include Nakonde Secondary, Kaombwe Secondary, Ntolondo Secondary, and Mukumbe Primary School. Mr. Zimba explained that schools were selected based on existing active production unit programs. These units have used previous grants to facilitate payments for volunteer teachers and assist vulnerable pupils with essential items like shoes and uniforms.

Three schools, Mukumbe, Kaombwe, and Ntolondo, each received K298,000, while Nakonde Secondary was allocated K214,000 due to its existing borehole. The funding will cover drilling new boreholes to a minimum depth of 80 meters, installing 5,000-liter water tanks, and establishing complete irrigation systems.

Zone Fam Reunites, Releases First Music in 12 Years

The Zambian hip-hop group Zone Fam is back with a new single and music video after a decade-long hiatus, aiming to reconnect with fans and attract new listeners.

Zambian hip-hop group Zone Fam has reunited to release new music for the first time in over a decade, ending a 12-year wait for fans. The quartet will release their new single, ‘One Fight,’ on September 4, 2026, under Headphone Music. The track is produced by member Jay Rox and features a music video directed by Lanzee Cooper. This marks their first release since ‘Two Things’ in 2014.

All four members—Jay Rox, Dope G, Yung Verbal, and Tim—shared teasers on social media this week, generating excitement. During their 12-year break, members pursued individual careers; Sam Sakala (Dope G) and Jackson Ng’ambi (Jay Rox) established themselves in television, while Reginald Lube (Yung Verbal) became a media personality and podcast host.

In a joint statement, the group stated that the release signifies a new chapter, offering a chance to reconnect with long-time supporters and introduce their music and legacy to a new generation. The ‘Chikali’ hitmakers declared their return with a defiant message: “Twelve years later, Zone Fam are back. One group. One legacy. One Fight.”

Why it mattersThe return of the hip-hop group Zone Fam with new music after a 12-year hiatus affects fans by offering them new material and a chance to reconnect with the group's legacy.
