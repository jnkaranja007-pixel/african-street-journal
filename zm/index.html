Moody's upgrades Zambia's credit rating to Caa1

The rating agency cited progress in debt restructuring and fiscal consolidation following the country's 2020 default.

Moody's Ratings upgraded Zambia's sovereign credit rating from Caa2 to Caa1 on October 30, 2026, reflecting sustained improvements in the country's debt trajectory since its 2020 default.

The Ministry of Finance and National Planning stated the upgrade demonstrates progress in debt restructuring, fiscal consolidation, and macro-economic stabilisation, following a staff-level agreement with the IMF for a proposed US$1.472 billion Extended Credit Facility.

The improved credit rating could strengthen investor confidence, potentially lower borrowing costs for government and businesses, attract foreign direct investment, and support exchange-rate stability, aligning with President Hichilema's Grow Zambia Agenda.

Why it mattersZambia's improved credit rating could strengthen investor confidence and attract foreign direct investment.

EU election mission gives Zambia 13 reforms

Government challenges report findings, stating they lack sufficient historical and institutional context.

The European Union Election Observation Mission (EU EOM) presented Zambia with a 13-point reform agenda following the August 13, 2026 general elections. Chief Observer Michael McNamara delivered the final report in Lusaka on Tuesday, setting up a fresh test of political will.

Government spokesperson Elvis Nkandu stated that some findings in the EU EOM report lack sufficient historical, institutional, and security context. He argued that without this context, the report risks presenting an incomplete picture of Zambia’s democratic journey.

The EU EOM report raised concerns about security, media freedom, and transparency in the electoral process. Nkandu noted that previous administrations also enacted reforms during election years, citing examples from the eras of Presidents Kaunda, Chiluba, and Lungu.

Why it mattersZambian citizens are affected by the government's response to the EU's recommendations for future elections.

Sialubalo demands fair market stall allocation

Minister Sialubalo says Lusaka has over 14,000 market stalls available but only 405 are allocated.

Minister of Local Government and Rural Development Gift Sialubalo has directed local authorities to ensure market stall and trading space allocations are transparent, fair, and accountable. Sialubalo stated yesterday in Parliament that allegations of unfair allocation, multiple stall occupation, unauthorised subletting, and unofficial payments undermine public confidence in market management. He emphasised that all trading must comply with the law, aiming to promote lawful trading, protect infrastructure, improve cleanliness and safety, and ensure orderly markets.

Sialubalo instructed local authorities to conduct enforcement operations lawfully, professionally, consistently, and humanely, sensitising traders on requirements and providing clear information on designated areas. He stressed that enforcement must include practical arrangements for traders to access suitable spaces, noting that removing vendors from undesignated locations without providing alternatives may not solve street vending. The minister expressed concern over traders occupying roads, drainage channels, and walkways, which causes congestion, obstructs movement, and compromises sanitation.

Regarding Lusaka, Sialubalo reported that the city has 34 markets with over 14,000 trading spaces ready for allocation, yet only 405 have been assigned. Approximately 8,000 registered street vendors in Lusaka could be accommodated in these available spaces. His ministry continues to guide local authorities on enforcing regulations for markets, public health, sanitation, waste management, and public space usage.

Why it mattersMinister Sialubalo is addressing concerns about unfair market stall allocations and the impact of street vending on urban order, affecting thousands of registered vendors in Lusaka.

Zambia elected to lead global green growth bodies

The nation will hold vice-president roles in the assembly and council of the Global Green Growth Institute for 2027-2028.

Zambia has secured two leadership roles within the Global Green Growth Institute (GGGI), becoming vice-president of the assembly and vice-chairperson of the council for the 2027–2028 term. These appointments elevate Zambia's influence in guiding the intergovernmental organisation, which aids developing nations in transitioning to sustainable, low-carbon economies. Minister of Green Economy and Environment Gilbert Siame stated that these positions will enhance Zambia's voice in the governance of the 55-member organisation.

The elections took place last Friday in Seoul, South Korea, during the 15th session of the GGGI Assembly and the 19th session of the council. Minister Siame explained that in these capacities, Zambia will assist the President of the assembly in directing the supreme governing body and support the Council chairperson in overseeing strategy, budgets, and performance. He noted that these roles offer a platform to advance international cooperation on sustainable development, climate action, and green investment.

Minister Siame outlined three priority areas for cooperation: investment in clean energy, electric mobility, and battery value chains. He urged partners with technology and capital to invest in Zambia's green economy and called for continued GGGI support in developing bankable projects, particularly in electric mobility, climate-resilient agriculture, and carbon credit generation. Zambia intends to use these positions to promote access to climate finance, technology transfer, and renewable energy investment.

Why it mattersZambia's new leadership roles in the Global Green Growth Institute will allow it to promote international cooperation on sustainable development, climate action, and green investment.
