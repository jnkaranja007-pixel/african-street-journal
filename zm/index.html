Zambia to introduce Advanced Level education from February 2027

Secondary schools will move to Forms 5 and 6 leading to an Advanced Level qualification, with learners registering for up to four subjects.

Zambia's government has announced the introduction of Advanced Level education under the 2023 Zambia Education Curriculum Framework. Education Minister Harry Kamboni made the announcement at a media briefing, saying the reform is meant to prepare learners for a modern, knowledge-driven economy. The first A-level intake begins in February 2027.

Under the new structure, Forms 1 to 4 lead to the Ordinary Level qualification, and Forms 5 and 6 lead to the Advanced Level qualification. Learners may register for a maximum of four A-level subjects drawn from approved combinations. Permanent Secretaries Noriana Muneku and Kelvin Mambwe attended the briefing alongside Examinations Council of Zambia Executive Director Michael Chilala.

Pathways on offer include natural sciences, agriculture science, computer science, business studies, social sciences, performing and creative arts, and physical education and sports. Other options are hospitality management, travel and tourism, textile design and technology, and nutrition and food technology.

Why it mattersLearners entering Forms 5 and 6 from February 2027 will choose from approved subject combinations, with a limit of four A-level subjects.

Zambia's proposed K22.7 billion farm budget wins praise from Kasama group

Impanga Emayo Business Hub says the 2027 National Budget proposal backs a sector the Grow Zambia Agenda treats as a key economic pillar.

A Kasama-based agricultural organisation has praised the government's proposal to set aside K22.7 billion for agriculture, fisheries and livestock development in the 2027 National Budget.

Brian Changwe, director of the Impanga Emayo Business Hub, said the figure shows the government's commitment to strengthening farming, which he described as a key economic pillar under the Grow Zambia Agenda.

The proposal was reported by the Lusaka Times on 12 November 2026. The packet gives no further details of the allocation or its timing.

Why it mattersFarmers in agriculture, fisheries and livestock are the sectors that the proposed K22.7 billion allocation in the 2027 National Budget is designed to support.

Zesco targets 11,630MW by 2030

The state utility aims to exceed the government's 10,000MW goal by 1,630MW, a year ahead of schedule.

Zesco Limited plans to reach 11,630 megawatts of installed generation capacity by 2030. This target surpasses the Government's 10,000MW objective under the Grow Zambia Agenda by 1,630MW. The company aims to achieve this a year before the 2031 deadline.

Managing director Eng. Justin Loongo presented the plan during a visit by Minister of Electricity Mr Peter Kapala. The details of the visit and the specific location were not provided in the report.

The Grow Zambia Agenda sets national energy targets to bolster the country's power supply. Zesco's accelerated goal indicates a strategic push to expand electricity generation capacity.

Why it mattersZambians will benefit from increased electricity generation capacity by 2030.

Zambian court jails two men five years for possessing live pangolin

Mongu Subordinate Court convicted Anthony Musole Musole and Oscar Nyundu after an officer posing as a buyer set up their arrest.

Two men, Anthony Musole Musole and Oscar Nyundu, were sentenced to five years in prison with hard labor after pleading guilty to unlawful possession of a live pangolin in Zambia, prosecutors said.

The pair pleaded guilty in the Mongu Subordinate Court to unlawful possession of a protected animal under Section 130(1) of the Zambia Wildlife Act No. 14 of 2015, the National Prosecution Authority's Mongu Office said. Officers from the Department of National Parks and Wildlife acted on a tip from members of the public, and an officer posing as a buyer arranged a meeting.

On October 28, the two were arrested at the meeting point in Mongu after a sack carried by one of them was found to contain the animal. Neither man could produce a certificate authorizing its possession, and an examination found the pangolin weighed 12 kilograms and was valued at more than 23,438 kwacha.

Why it mattersAnyone in Zambia who keeps or trades a protected animal without a permit now faces a five-year prison term with hard labor.

Zambian mother seeks word on son detained in Mozambique

Grace Nampokolwe Banda has had no contact with her son Emmanuel, known as Jay Jay, since he was taken in Mozambique on October 14.

Grace Nampokolwe Banda is waiting in Lusaka for a document that would tell her where her son stands. Emmanuel Banda, known to Zambians as Jay Jay, was taken in Mozambique on October 14. She has not seen him since.

The Lusaka Times reports that Banda is seeking information on her son's whereabouts and legal status. The report gives no detail of the grounds for his detention or of any charge against him.

The outlet frames the case as one that Zambia must not become used to. The report names no official body that has responded to the family's requests.

Why it mattersEmmanuel Banda's family has no confirmed information on where he is held or what legal process he faces in Mozambique.
