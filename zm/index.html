Inauguration Venue Ready, Police Warn Against Protests

Secretary to the Cabinet confirms Heroes Stadium is prepared for Tuesday's ceremony, while police issue warnings about planned demonstrations.

The venue for President-elect Hakainde Hichilema's inauguration ceremony on Tuesday, September 1st, 2026, is ready, according to Secretary to the Cabinet Patrick Kangwa. Kangwa expressed satisfaction with the preparations at Heroes Stadium during a recent tour, stating that all stakeholders have completed their work. The confirmation comes amidst opposition calls for the African Union to intervene in the disputed election, with concerns that the inauguration will make scrutiny of the vote moot.

Meanwhile, the Inspector General of Police, Graphel Musamba, has warned against planned protests ahead of the inauguration. Musamba cited intelligence indicating efforts to incite unlawful gatherings and mobilize people to protest the September 1st event and the detention of suspects. Police have received credible information about these planned actions by certain groups and individuals.

The upcoming inauguration is set to proceed on schedule despite the ongoing dispute over the election results. Opposition parties have voiced their concerns, suggesting that the inauguration will preempt any meaningful review of the vote. The police are taking preventative measures to ensure public order and security during the transition period.

Why it mattersThe inauguration of President-elect Hakainde Hichilema on September 1st, 2026, is proceeding despite election disputes, with police warning against protests.

Zambian Opposition Leader Mundubile Detained on Treason Charges

Opposition leader Brian Mundubile and his running mate Makebi Zulu are in police custody following allegations of treason after Mundubile disputed recent election results.

Zambian opposition leader Brian Mundubile and his presidential running mate Makebi Zulu have been taken into custody over allegations of treason, police confirmed Thursday. Mundubile, who was runner-up in the recent presidential election, reportedly surrendered to police after two weeks in hiding following a summons related to an alleged national security threat.

The charges stem from an alleged violation of Section 43(1) of the Penal Code, Chapter 87 of the Laws, according to police spokesperson Godfrey Chilabi. Mundubile, a 55-year-old lawyer, had vowed to challenge the election results which saw incumbent Hakainde Hichilema re-elected with 60 percent of the vote. Mundubile secured 38 percent.

The detention follows the closure of Zambia's judiciary on Monday, the deadline for filing election objections, which the government cited as being for "security reasons." International NGO Human Rights Watch called the closure "a blow to the administration of law, transparency, and due process in Zambia" without providing a credible explanation.

Why it mattersThe detention of a prominent opposition leader and the closure of the judiciary raise concerns about the rule of law and political stability in Zambia.

Zambia Government to Boost Cyber Security Capacity

The Ministry of Technology and Science is committed to enhancing the nation's digital security infrastructure to support its ongoing digital transformation agenda.

The Zambian government plans to significantly strengthen the country's cyber security capabilities, according to Brilliant Habeenzu, Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Technology and Science. Habeenzu stated that a secure digital environment is essential for the successful implementation of Zambia's digital transformation agenda. These remarks were made in Lusaka during the Fortinet Tech Seminar.

The Ministry's commitment underscores a recognition of the growing importance of digital infrastructure and the need to protect it from emerging threats. This initiative aims to create a more robust and trustworthy digital space for citizens and businesses alike as the nation progresses with its technological advancements.

While the Ministry of Technology and Science focuses on cyber security, the Ministry of Transport has also expressed support for the upcoming 16th Lusaka Motor Show, scheduled for August 28th to 30th, 2026. This indicates a broad governmental focus on developing various sectors of the economy and public life.

Why it mattersZambian citizens and businesses are affected by the government's efforts to strengthen cyber security, which is fundamental to the country's digital transformation.

New Heritage Party Seeks Election Petition Filing Extension

The opposition party claims court closures unfairly hindered their ability to submit a presidential election petition before the deadline.

The New Heritage Party (NHP) is demanding a two-day extension to file its presidential election petition, citing a nationwide court closure that occurred just before the filing deadline. NHP president Chishala Kateka stated that this closure unfairly disadvantaged the opposition, preventing them from submitting their case within the allotted time.

The Law Association of Zambia (LAZ) has also called on the Judiciary to provide guidance regarding cases and applications that could not be filed due to the three-day court closure. LAZ president Arnold Kaluba emphasized the need for clear direction on how such matters should be handled following the disruption.

The NHP's demand highlights concerns about procedural fairness in the electoral process. The party argues that the judiciary's actions created an uneven playing field for opposition parties seeking to challenge election results through legal channels.

Why it mattersThe New Heritage Party is directly affected as they are seeking an extension to file a presidential election petition due to court closures.

ECZ Orders Fresh Mandevu Election After Attack Destroys Materials

The Electoral Commission of Zambia will hold a new parliamentary election in Mandevu Constituency following an attack that destroyed election materials and prevented.

A fresh parliamentary election will be held in Mandevu Constituency, the Electoral Commission of Zambia announced, after an attack on a results totaling center destroyed essential election materials. Chief Electoral Officer Brown Kasaro stated the decision followed a security incident during the aggregation of National Assembly results from the August 13 general elections.

According to an official report from the Lusaka District Electoral Officer dated August 18, unknown individuals attacked the totaling center in Mandevu. They destroyed result transmission kits and took all documentation, leaving the returning officer unable to declare the constituency's results. Kasaro confirmed the commission received this report.

The Electoral Commission of Zambia has not yet announced a specific date for the fresh election. The incident has disrupted the electoral process in Mandevu, preventing the finalization of the National Assembly election results for the constituency. Further details regarding the investigation into the attack were not provided.

Why it mattersThe fresh election in Mandevu Constituency directly affects residents by requiring them to vote again, and it delays the finalization of representation in the National Assembly.
