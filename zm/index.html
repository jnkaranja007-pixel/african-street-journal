LAZ vows legal action as youths plan march to scrap term limits

Over 60,000 young people from 10 provinces plan to march in Lusaka on November 25 to demand the removal of presidential term limits.

The Law Association of Zambia, a body of legal professionals, has restated its support for presidential term limits and warned it may take legal action to defend Article 106(3) of the Constitution. Its statement, dated November 16, comes while petitions to remove the two-term limit are before the National Assembly.

Boas Kanyama, vice chairperson of the Zambia Youth Organisation Consortium, said 63,151 youths from all 10 provinces have confirmed they will march in Lusaka on November 25. The march will start at Northmead Bus Station and end at Parliament Buildings.

The Association welcomed an assurance on the constitutional provisions governing the presidency, according to its statement.

Why it mattersPetitions to change the two-term limit now before lawmakers are the target, and Parliament Buildings is where the youths will end their march.

Court sets December 16 to resume Esther Lungu theft trial

The Lusaka Magistrate's Court has barred any further adjournments, so the case must move forward on that date.

The Lusaka Magistrate's Court has fixed December 16, 2026, for the resumption of the theft trial of former First Lady Esther Lungu and four others. Lusaka Resident Magistrate Mbuyanwa Sinvula set the date in chambers.

The magistrate ruled that there will be no further mentions or adjournments in the matter. She said the case must now proceed to hearing on the scheduled date.

The trial was due to resume on the set date, with the court refusing any further delays to the proceedings.

Why it mattersLungu and her four co-accused must be ready to appear in court on December 16, 2026, as the court has refused any further postponements.

Zambia performs first-ever cochlear implant activation at UTH

A 20-year-old patient with profound hearing loss received the country's first cochlear implant activation.

A 20-year-old woman with profound hearing loss has started a new hearing journey following the successful activation of a cochlear implant at the University Teaching Hospitals Adult Hospital. The activation took place on Monday, November 16, following the facility's first-ever cochlear implant surgery performed on October 13.

Ear, nose and throat specialists at the hospital conducted the procedure alongside a visiting medical team from the Czech Republic operating under the Czech government's MEDEVAC programme. Cochlear implants differ from conventional hearing aids because they bypass damaged parts of the inner ear to deliver electrical signals directly to the auditory nerve.

The patient now enters hearing rehabilitation involving device adjustments and auditory training to help her brain interpret the new sounds. The hospital stated that the procedure demonstrates progress in strengthening local capacity for specialized care through international partnerships.

Why it mattersThe 20-year-old patient begins rehabilitation involving device adjustments and auditory training following the activation on Monday, November 16.

Business · 2026-11-17

Lusaka construction worker wins K6,000,000 on betPawa

Davis, a construction worker, collected Zambia's largest recorded JetX win after four bets each paid the K1,500,000 maximum in one flight.

Davis, a construction worker from Lusaka, won K6,000,000 playing JetX on the betting platform betPawa. Four separate bets placed in one JetX flight each paid the K1,500,000 maximum, which added up to the total.

The Lusaka Times reports that the win is the largest recorded for the game in Zambia. Davis placed his four bets and stepped away from the screen while the round played out.

JetX is a crash game in which players watch a multiplier rise and decide when to cash out. The report does not give further details of the win.

Why it mattersAnyone staking on JetX in Zambia now has a published benchmark for the maximum payout a single bet can return.

Business · 2026-11-17

Mutale-Nalumango urges private investment in refugee areas

Zambia's vice-president calls for business support to transform refugee settlements into economic hubs.

Vice-President Mrs W.K. Mutale-Nalumango has urged commercial enterprises to invest in regions hosting refugees. According to the Lusaka Times, she delivered the call during an address focused on regional development.

The vice-president stated that these areas present commercial opportunities capable of driving shared prosperity and national growth. She specifically pointed to financial services, enterprise development, agriculture, and value chains as sectors needing capital.

The official push aims to foster self-reliance among displaced populations by integrating them into the broader economy through targeted business participation.

Why it mattersPrivate companies are asked to direct capital into agriculture, financial services, and enterprise development in refugee-hosting regions.
