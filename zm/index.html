Mundubile and Zulu Face Treason Charges, Detained at Lusaka Central

Leading opposition presidential candidate Brian Mundubile and his running mate Makebi Zulu received a Warn and Caution Statement on treason charges after police interrogation.

Brian Mundubile, the leading opposition presidential candidate, and his running mate, Makebi Zulu, have been detained at Lusaka Central Police. This follows hours of police interrogation where the pair were issued a Warn and Caution Statement regarding treason charges, according to the Lusaka Times.

The detention concludes a standoff that began after Mundubile had been in hiding. State media, reported by BBC Africa, indicated that Mundubile was questioned by police after disputing the outcome of the recent presidential election.

The development signals a significant escalation in political tensions following the disputed election results. The specific nature of the treason charges has not been detailed beyond the issuance of the Warn and Caution Statement.

Why it mattersThe detention of the leading opposition presidential candidate and his running mate on treason charges directly impacts the political landscape and stability for Zambian citizens.

Zambian Courts Reopen After Security Threat, Election Petition Dispute Continues

Zambia's courts resumed operations Thursday following a four-day closure due to security concerns during the presidential election petition deadline, with police remaining on site.

Zambia's courts reopened on Thursday, four days after a security threat prompted their closure on the final day for challenging presidential election results. Chief Administrator Yvonne N. Nalomba announced the reopening in a memo on August 26, stating that security agencies advised the threat had de-escalated, though police will maintain a visible presence at all court buildings.

The closure occurred on Monday, the last day to file a petition against the August 13 presidential election results, which saw President Hakainde Hichilema declared the winner on August 18. The shutdown drew criticism from some opposition figures, though State House denied President Hichilema ordered the closure, citing ongoing security assessments.

The Judiciary confirmed no petition was filed in the Constitutional Court Registry by 18:00 on August 24. However, Chief Justice Dr. Mumba Malila received an electronic copy of a document called the “people’s presidential petition” via private email, which the Judiciary described as “highly irregular” but referred to the Constitutional Court for a ruling on proper filing procedures.

Why it mattersThe reopening of courts and the ongoing dispute over election results directly affect Zambian citizens by influencing the stability of the country's legal and political systems.

Bus-truck collision kills four, injures 36 in Rufunsa

A head-on collision between a Power Tools Company bus and a Volvo truck on the Great East Road in Rufunsa District has resulted in four fatalities and 36 injuries.

Four people died and 36 others were injured on Thursday when a bus and a truck collided on the Great East Road in the Lukwipa area of Rufunsa District, according to the Zambia Police Service. The crash occurred around eleven a.m. and involved a Zhongtong public service bus operated by Power Tools Company, traveling toward Chipata with 57 passengers, and a Volvo truck and trailer heading toward Lusaka.

Police identified the deceased as Ask Mulobela, the bus driver, and Innocent Mulobela, the bus conductor and son of the driver. Both were pronounced dead at Mpanshya Mission Hospital. Thirty-five bus passengers sustained injuries and were admitted to the same hospital. The truck driver, Paul Banda, 25, also suffered serious injuries and is hospitalized.

Preliminary investigations by police indicate that the truck driver moved into the opposite lane to avoid a stationary vehicle, leading to the head-on collision with the bus. The incident highlights the dangers on the Great East Road, affecting numerous travelers and transport operations in the region.

Why it mattersThis incident directly impacts the safety and travel plans of individuals using the Great East Road, including those who rely on public transport for daily commutes or longer journeys.

Zambia Women's Volleyball Team Advances at African Championships

The Zambia Women National Volleyball team secured a 3-1 victory over Mali, moving them into the next round of the Women’s African Volleyball Championship.

The Zambia Women National Volleyball team has advanced to the next round of the Women’s African Volleyball Championship after defeating Mali 3-1. This win follows a previous 3-0 loss to Kenya, who are the defending champions in the tournament.

Zambia's annual inflation rate falls to 6.2% in August

The Zambia Statistics Agency reported a decline in the annual inflation rate to 6.2% in August, down from 6.5% in July, indicating a slowdown in price increases for goods.

Zambia's annual inflation rate decreased to 6.2% in August, according to the Zambia Statistics Agency. This figure is a drop from the 6.5% recorded in July, meaning that prices for goods and services rose by 6.2% between August 2025 and August 2026, as stated by Statistician General Sheila Mudenda.

Food inflation also eased, moving from 6.4% in July to 6.0% in August. This decline was primarily due to lower prices for cereals, including breakfast mealie meal, roller mealie meal, maize grain, local rice, and plain household wheat flour. Additionally, sugar, cooking oil, and dried beans contributed to the reduction in food prices.

Non-food inflation similarly saw a slight decrease, falling to 6.6% from 6.7% in July. This change was attributed to price movements in fuels and lubricants, such as diesel, petrol, paraffin, engine oil, and brake fluid, alongside a reduction in the cost of purchasing vehicles.

Why it mattersZambian households will find some relief as the cost of essential goods like mealie meal, cooking oil, and fuel has increased at a slower rate.
