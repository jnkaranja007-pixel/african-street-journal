Electoral Commission Orders Fresh Mandevu By-Election After Security Breach

The Electoral Commission of Zambia has ordered a new parliamentary election in Mandevu following disruptions to result management during the August 13 general election.

The Electoral Commission of Zambia (ECZ) has decided to hold a fresh parliamentary election in Lusaka's Mandevu constituency. This decision follows a significant security breach that disrupted the management of election results after the August 13 general election. The ECZ announced its resolution after concluding its review of the situation, a move that has garnered support from various civic and electoral oversight stakeholders.

The disruption occurred during the process of managing results from the August 13 general election. Details of the security breach itself were not provided, but the ECZ's decision indicates it was severe enough to compromise the integrity of the initial count. The commission's conclusion led to the order for a new election in the affected constituency.

This development comes as opposition parties urge the African Union to intervene in Zambia's disputed election, with concerns that the September 1 inauguration of President-elect Hakainde Hichilema could render scrutiny of the vote moot. The ECZ's decision to order a fresh election in Mandevu addresses a specific electoral management issue within the broader context of the general election.

Why it mattersThe fresh by-election in Mandevu constituency directly affects voters in that area who will need to participate in another election, and potentially impacts the overall political landscape of Zambia.

Zambian opposition leader detained on treason charges

Brian Mundubile, who disputed the presidential election results, surrendered to police after two weeks in hiding.

Zambian opposition leader Brian Mundubile and his running mate Makebi Zulu have been taken into custody on treason allegations, police announced Thursday. Mundubile, a 55-year-old lawyer, surrendered after two weeks in hiding following a summons related to an alleged national security threat. He was the runner-up in the recent presidential election, receiving 38 percent of the vote compared to incumbent Hakainde Hichilema's 60 percent.

Mundubile had vowed to challenge the election outcome, but Zambia's judiciary was closed on Monday, the final day for filing objections, citing unspecified security reasons. International NGO Human Rights Watch called the closure a blow to due process and transparency. The Constitutional Court stated it had not received any election petitions, paving the way for Hichilema's inauguration, though the Chief Justice noted an irregular petition from a private citizen was forwarded for consideration.

Eight church mother bodies have denied negotiating a political reconciliation deal between the government and the detained opposition leaders. They stated their role was limited to facilitating Mundubile and Zulu's safe departure from United Nations premises to their lawyers. The leaders' detention follows their dispute of the election results, with Mundubile having been in hiding prior to his surrender.

Why it mattersOpposition leader Brian Mundubile and his running mate Makebi Zulu are detained on treason charges, impacting political stability and due process.

Zambia Pursues Deeper Trade Ties With Brazil

Government implements reforms to lower business costs and attract Brazilian investment, seeking enhanced cooperation.

Zambia is actively pursuing enhanced trade and investment cooperation with Brazil, according to government officials. The Ministry of Commerce, Trade and Industry is implementing reforms designed to reduce the cost of doing business within the country. These measures aim to position Zambia as a more attractive investment destination for Brazilian companies and other international investors.

Permanent Secretary for Investment and Industrialisation, Crusivia Hichikumba, stated that the government's focus is on deepening these economic partnerships. The reforms are intended to streamline processes and create a more favorable environment for foreign direct investment. This initiative signals a strategic effort to boost bilateral trade relations and stimulate economic growth through increased collaboration.

The government's commitment to these reforms underscores its objective of making Zambia a competitive hub for international business. By addressing the cost of doing business, Zambia hopes to unlock new opportunities for trade and investment, particularly with key partners like Brazil. This proactive approach is expected to yield significant economic benefits and strengthen Zambia's position in the global marketplace.

Why it mattersZambian government reforms aim to reduce the cost of doing business, making the country more attractive for Brazilian investors.

UTH Surgeons Complete 30 Operations in Kasama

A team of pediatric surgeons from the University Teaching Hospital performed thirty procedures during a four-day surgical camp at Kasama General Hospital.

Paediatric surgeons from the University Teaching Hospital (UTH) have concluded a four-day surgical camp at Kasama General Hospital, successfully performing thirty operations. The team, led by UTH Paediatric Surgeon Amon Ngongola, focused on providing critical surgical care to children in the region. Seven of the procedures were classified as major surgeries, addressing complex medical conditions in young patients.

Dr. Ngongola reported the successful completion of the surgical camp, highlighting the team's efforts to extend specialized medical services beyond the capital. The initiative aimed to alleviate the burden on local healthcare facilities and provide access to advanced surgical interventions for children who might otherwise face significant delays or lack of access to such care.

The operations conducted at Kasama General Hospital represent a significant effort to decentralize specialized medical services. This surgical camp provided essential treatment for children with various conditions, underscoring the importance of outreach programs in improving healthcare outcomes in underserved areas of Zambia.

Why it mattersChildren with complex medical conditions in Kasama received specialized surgical care, improving their health outcomes.

Bus Truck Collision Kills Four on Great East Road

A head-on collision between a bus and a truck on Thursday morning in Rufunsa District resulted in four fatalities and 36 injuries.

Four people died and 36 were injured Thursday when a bus carrying 57 passengers collided with a truck on the Great East Road in Rufunsa District. The crash occurred around 11 a.m. in the Lukwipa area, according to the Zambia Police Service. The bus, a Zhongtong public service vehicle operated by Power Tools Company, was traveling toward Chipata. The truck, a Volvo with a trailer, was heading toward Lusaka.

Police identified the deceased as the bus driver, Ask Mulobela, and the bus conductor, Innocent Mulobela, who was also the driver's son. Both were pronounced dead at Mpanshya Mission Hospital. Thirty-five bus passengers were injured and admitted to the same hospital, along with the truck driver, Paul Banda, 25, who sustained serious injuries.

Preliminary investigations suggest the truck driver swerved into the opposite lane to avoid a stationary vehicle, leading to the head-on collision with the bus. The bus was registered as ADE 5711, while the truck and trailer were registered as AJE 4462 and ALF 7035T, respectively. The truck driver is from Sinda District.

Why it mattersFour people were killed and 36 others were injured in a bus-truck crash, impacting families and local emergency services.
