Zambian president directs halt to plot allocations without infrastructure

President Hichilema tasks Housing Minister Christopher Kalila to address the 1.5 million housing deficit and enforce planning standards.

President Hakainde Hichilema has ordered Housing Minister Christopher Kalila to stop local councils from allocating plots without basic infrastructure, aiming to curb unplanned settlements. The President also directed Transport and Logistics Minister Charles Milupi to speed up the movement of goods along economic corridors to boost investment and economic growth.

Speaking at a swearing-in ceremony, President Hichilema stressed the need for strengthened urban planning, stating that all housing land must include essential services like roads to prevent public health issues. He urged Minister Kalila to tackle Zambia's estimated 1.5 million housing deficit while ensuring local authorities adhere to proper planning requirements.

The President also called for more multi-storey buildings to maximize land use and for affordable housing initiatives supported by effective mortgage systems. Minister Milupi was tasked with ensuring road and railway infrastructure supports faster and safer transportation of goods, as economic corridors are vital drivers of development.

Why it mattersPresident Hichilema's directive impacts citizens seeking housing and the development of urban areas by enforcing infrastructure standards for plot allocations.

Court nullifies UPND Mapatizya seat

High Court Judge Kazimbe Chenda ruled the election of Emelda Munashabantu premature due to tallying irregularities.

The Lusaka High Court has nullified the election of UPND’s Emelda Munashabantu as Mapatizya member of parliament. High Court Judge Kazimbe Chenda delivered the ruling in Livingstone, stating the declaration of Munashabantu as winner was premature. The election results were based on data from 64 of the constituency’s 75 polling stations.

The court heard that Munashabantu had polled 14,812 votes before the full tally was complete. The nullification followed a successful election petition citing irregularities in the tallying and declaration process for the August 13, 2026 parliamentary elections. Mapatizya Constituency in Zimba District is now without a Member of Parliament.

Judge Chenda ruled that serious irregularities in the post-voting electoral process affected the outcome of the August 13, 2026 parliamentary election. The UPND party has lost its Mapatizya seat following this court decision.

Why it mattersMapatizya Constituency in Zimba District is now without a Member of Parliament following the court's decision.

Mandevu by-election candidate withdraws citing threats

Forum for Democracy and Development candidate Daniel Chisenga pulled out of the race for the Mandevu seat.

Forum for Democracy and Development (FDD) candidate Daniel Chisenga withdrew from the November 5 Mandevu by-election, citing threats to his life from unknown individuals and political players. His withdrawal leaves UPND candidate Adrian Banda as the sole contender for the seat, which has over 123,000 registered voters.

Mr Chisenga stated he has informed the Electoral Commission of Zambia (ECZ) of his decision. The remaining candidates for the Mandevu seat are Adrian Banda of the UPND, Ntazana Musukuma of the Leadership Movement, and independent Daniel Daka. The ECZ is training 730 poll staff in Lusaka to ensure professionalism and impartiality.

ECZ vice-chairperson Vincent Mukanda urged poll staff to uphold integrity and fairness during the by-election. Acting chief electoral officer Collins Kachaka also stressed the importance of accurate record-keeping and control at polling stations. The by-election is scheduled for next Thursday.

Why it mattersZambians in the Mandevu constituency will have only one candidate, Adrian Banda, to vote for in the upcoming by-election.

First Lady receives 200 hospital beds

Donation from Chinese Chamber of Commerce aims to improve maternal health delivery in Zambia.

First Lady Mutinta Hichilema received 200 hospital beds and 200 sewing machines in Lusaka today. The Chinese Chamber of Commerce in Zambia (CCCZ) donated the beds to improve maternal health delivery. The sewing machines are intended to support women through skills empowerment.

Mrs. Hichilema stated that the donation was a gesture aimed at improving healthcare services. The CCCZ's contribution directly supports the government's efforts to enhance the quality of care for mothers across the country.

The hospital beds will be distributed to facilities across Zambia. The sewing machines will be used in skills development programs for women, fostering economic independence.

Why it mattersZambian women will benefit from improved maternal healthcare and skills empowerment initiatives.

Health · 2026-10-30

Zambia starts cholera vaccine plant construction next month

The facility at Lusaka South Multi Facility Economic Zone will produce 20 million doses annually.

Construction of Africa’s first oral cholera vaccine manufacturing plant will begin next month at the Lusaka South Multi Facility Economic Zone. President Hakainde Hichilema announced the project, which is a collaboration between Zambia and Chinese company International Medical Technology. The plant is expected to produce 20 million doses of the vaccine each year.

President Hichilema stated the plant demonstrates Zambia’s commitment to cholera prevention and continental efforts to eliminate the disease by 2030. He also announced that the government released K13 million for the emergency supply of safe water amid a cholera outbreak. This outbreak has reached 1,622 cases and 30 deaths since July 28, affecting 20 districts across six provinces.

As of October 28, 104 patients remained admitted to hospitals, with 1,488 having recovered. The Ministry of Health reported 74 new cases in the latest 24-hour period, with no new fatalities. Minister of Health Roma Chilengi urged prompt delivery of safe water to affected communities with proper accountability for the K13 million funding.

Why it mattersZambians are directly affected by the cholera outbreak and the government's response through vaccine production and water provision.
