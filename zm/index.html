Zambia, Switzerland trade reaches $20 billion

President Hichilema urges Swiss businesses to invest beyond minerals and support the Grow Zambia Agenda.

Trade and investment between Zambia and Switzerland has reached over 20 billion United States Dollars. President Hakainde Hichilema met with Switzerland’s State Secretary Helene Artieda and a business delegation at State House yesterday. The President called for a comprehensive economic partnership to ease investor cooperation and support the Grow Zambia Agenda.

Mr Hichilema urged Switzerland to increase investment and trade beyond traditional minerals like copper. He wants stronger economic partnerships to accelerate growth, create jobs, and promote local value addition. Switzerland’s State Secretariat for Economic Affairs supports the Grow Zambia Agenda and is ready to encourage Swiss businesses to participate.

The President expressed gratitude for Switzerland’s support in Zambia’s debt restructuring process. He stated that his administration’s focus in the second term is to drive economic growth through investment and trade. Switzerland’s delegation expressed interest in exploring opportunities in emerging sectors.

Why it mattersZambian businesses and the national economy are affected by the call for increased trade and investment with Switzerland.

Youths petition parliament to scrap term limits

A consortium of youth organisations wants presidential term limits and the Grade 12 certificate requirement for MPs abolished.

A consortium of youth organisations has petitioned Zambia's Parliament to abolish presidential term limits and remove the Grade 12 certificate requirement for aspiring MPs and councillors. The Zambia Youth Organisation Consortium (ZYOC) submitted the petition to nominated MP Francis Kope for presentation to the National Assembly. The consortium includes Youth for Dialogue, Youth Development Network, Ambia Young Women, Different Abled Youths Association, and Arise and Soar Zambia.

ZYOC chairperson Jacob Longwe stated that presidential term limits restrict citizens' freedom to choose leaders and that the Grade 12 certificate requirement excludes capable individuals lacking formal education. He confirmed that consultations were held nationwide before the petition was prepared. Nominated MP Francis Kope assured the organisations that he would initiate the process of presenting the petition to the National Assembly.

Mr Kope, accompanied by MPs Twaambo Mutinta and Charles Tembo, received the petition as an expression of young people's views on national issues. Mr Mutinta added that presenting the proposed motion would allow for parliamentary debate on the matters raised. The ZYOC believes these changes reflect concerns about political participation and eligibility for elected office among young people.

Why it mattersYoung people in Zambia are directly affected by proposed changes to presidential term limits and candidate eligibility requirements for parliamentary and council seats.

Zambia parliament approves ministry changes

President Hichilema's plan to merge and dissolve ministries passed with majority support in the National Assembly.

Zambia's Parliament approved President Hakainde Hichilema's proposal to restructure government ministries yesterday in Lusaka. The changes aim to improve efficiency and accelerate the Grow Zambia Agenda. Vice President Mutale Nalumango moved the motion, which was supported by most lawmakers.

The approved changes include dissolving the ministries of Energy; Commerce, Trade and Industry; and Youth, Sport and Arts. New ministries will focus on areas like Trade and Export, Industry, Electricity, and Culture and Arts. Several ministries, including Defence and Finance, will retain their existing structures.

Mrs Nalumango stated that dedicated ministries for Electricity and Petroleum are important for economic growth and responding to energy demands. She also highlighted the economic potential of the Culture and Arts sector for tourism and job creation. Monze East MP Jack Mwiimbu supported the motion, calling it non-controversial.

Why it mattersZambian citizens will experience changes in government service delivery and the focus of national development priorities.

Zambia Railways train intercepted with 25 cholera patients

Health Ministry officials evacuated patients from a train travelling to Livingstone after a cholera outbreak was reported.

Health Ministry officials evacuated 25 cholera patients from a Zambia Railways passenger train on October 27, 2026. The train was travelling towards Livingstone when it was stopped in the Mapepe–Kachenjela area of Chilanga District following a cholera outbreak notification.

Ministry of Health Permanent Secretary Kennedy Lishimpi confirmed the evacuation to Chilanga Urban Clinic. The affected passengers were removed from the train en route to Livingstone.

The Ministry of Health has not yet announced further steps or the total number of individuals affected by the outbreak on the train.

Why it matters25 passengers on a Zambia Railways train were evacuated due to a cholera outbreak.

Zambia government to pay K14.7 billion to maize farmers

Treasury releases funds to settle outstanding payments for maize and rice delivered to the Food Reserve Agency.

The Zambian government will progressively pay farmers who supplied maize to the Food Reserve Agency (FRA), with K14.7 billion released by the Treasury for outstanding payments. Acting Minister of Agriculture Situmbeko Musokotwane assured Parliament that this funding is sufficient to cover debts for maize and rice delivered to the agency. The FRA purchased 2.7 million tonnes of maize and 1,304.41 tonnes of rice, totaling approximately K18.7 billion, with K4.1 billion already paid.

Dr. Musokotwane stated that the FRA has an outstanding balance of about K14.6 billion to farmers. The agency's current maize purchases bring the total FRA stock to 4.1 million metric tons, including carry-over from previous seasons. Government remains committed to settling all payments as funds become available and appreciates farmers' patience.

To manage stock and generate revenue, the FRA is seeking regional markets for its surplus maize, having secured sales contracts for 1.7 million metric tons for export and domestic markets. Revenue from these sales will support the national strategic food reserve and future procurement. Zambia's food security is assured until the next harvest, with sufficient maize stocks.

Why it mattersFarmers who supplied maize and rice to the Food Reserve Agency will receive outstanding payments totaling K14.6 billion.
