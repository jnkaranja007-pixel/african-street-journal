Zambia’s ECZ starts Mwinilunga review meetings on 2026 election

The Electoral Commission of Zambia began sessions with district structures to assess activities from the August 13 general election.

The Electoral Commission of Zambia (ECZ) has started meetings with its district structures in Mwinilunga, North-Western Province, to review activities from the 2026 general election held on August 13.

The first session brought together the District Voter Education Committee, voter education facilitators and voter education trainers, according to the commission’s account. The meetings form a series focused on the election’s activities in the district.

The review is taking place in Mwinilunga district. The account provided no further details on the meetings’ schedule or conclusions.

Why it mattersThe district voter education committee, facilitators and trainers are reviewing their election activities through a series of meetings.

Zambia police reject clearance for two Lusaka term-limit marches

The planned processions, one for and one against removing presidential term limits, were set for November 25, 2026.

Zambia Police Service declined to clear two planned processions and public assemblies in Lusaka over presidential term limits and proposed constitutional changes. The groups had scheduled them for November 25, 2026.

Police said in a press statement that they received notifications from two consortiums of organisations intending to hold peaceful processions. The service said its decision followed a review of the notifications.

The proposed marches take opposing positions: one supports removing presidential term limits, while the other opposes the proposed constitutional changes. Zambia Youth Organisation Consortium Vice Chairperson Boas Kanyama said 63,151 youths from all 10 [the report’s statement is incomplete].

Why it mattersOrganisers of both Lusaka gatherings cannot proceed with their planned public processions on November 25 after police declined clearance.

Nzovu links Lusaka-Ndola road progress to public-private partnerships

Zambia’s infrastructure minister made the case for private-sector partnerships while inspecting work on the 321-kilometre dual carriageway.

Zambia’s Infrastructure and Urban Development Minister Collins Nzovu said public-private partnerships are important to the country’s infrastructure plans while inspecting construction on the 321-kilometre Lusaka-Ndola dual carriageway.

Nzovu said progress on the road showed the results of cooperation between the government and private partners. The report did not give a completion date or a measure of work finished.

The Lusaka Times report did not provide further details on the project’s cost, financing or next construction milestone.

Why it mattersPrivate partners working on the 321-kilometre road are part of the cooperation Nzovu cited as making progress on the project.

Sport · 2026-11-18

Fashion Sakala retires from Zambia’s international team

The Chipolopolo captain said representing Zambia and leading its national side were among the proudest moments of his life.

Fashion Sakala, captain of Zambia’s Chipolopolo national team, has retired from international football, ending his career with the side. He announced the decision after what he described as much reflection.

In a farewell message titled “God Bless, My Farewell to the Zambian National Team,” Sakala said representing Zambia and captaining the Chipolopolo were among the proudest moments of his life.

Sakala also reflected on his journey from playing barefoot in Eastern Province as a young boy. His announcement concerns his international career; the reports provide no further details about his next step.

Why it mattersSakala’s departure ends his international career with the Chipolopolo, the team he captained for Zambia.
