President Hichilema Pledges Increased Investment in Key Sectors

Following his inauguration for a second term, President Hichilema outlined plans to expand investments in education, health, and economic empowerment programs.

President Hakainde Hichilema has pledged to scale up investments in education, skills development, and health sectors under his renewed mandate. He highlighted that reforms in the education sector during his previous term, including free education, led to 2.6 million learners returning to school. The President assured that his administration will continue building on these successes, emphasizing that education is a crucial investment for equal opportunities and a lasting inheritance for children.

The President also committed to increased economic empowerment for women and young people to foster job creation and economic growth. He stated that empowering youth will reduce reliance on handouts and enable greater contributions to the economy, while social protection programs aim to uplift vulnerable citizens. Furthermore, the administration will expedite decentralization efforts, including the increase of Constituency Development Funds from 1.6 to 40 million kwacha, to ensure development reaches all parts of the country.

Hichilema was sworn in for his second term amidst political developments where his main rival, who was the runner-up in the recent elections, is now in custody on treason charges. Despite this, the President's focus remains on governance and development initiatives outlined in his 'Go Zambia Agenda'.

Why it mattersZambians will benefit from expanded access to education and health services, alongside increased economic empowerment opportunities for women and youth.

Zambian Official Dismisses Election Dispute Claims

An official stated that Zambians have spoken and that claims of crisis are unfounded, attributing them to diaspora-based critics.

An official has dismissed claims of an election crisis or dispute in Zambia, characterizing such narratives as unfounded and originating from individuals operating from abroad. The official stated that Zambian people have made their choice and that while their verdict may be disliked or disputed through lawful channels, attempts to incite citizens towards revolt are misguided.

The official contrasted this with the experiences of those who have faced narrow electoral defeats, citing margins of approximately 27,000 votes in 2015 and 100,000 in 2016. These moments, though difficult, were met with acceptance and moving forward, rather than calls for confrontation from a distance. The official recalled a personal response to a narrow loss involving a family gathering and sharing a meal with farm workers.

Furthermore, the official criticized individuals who urge confrontation from the safety of foreign countries, labeling such actions as cowardly. The statement emphasized that in past narrow losses, humiliations, arrests, and torment, the choice was never to seek refuge abroad and incite citizens. This approach, the official argued, is not statesmanship or bravery but rather an easy prescription for turmoil when others are expected to bear the consequences.

Why it mattersThis statement is relevant to diaspora readers who may be critical of Zambian politics from abroad and to investors who are concerned about political stability.

Simon Mwila Loses Cars in Mayoral Campaign

Losing mayoral candidate Simon Mwila revealed personal sacrifices, including the loss of vehicles, during his 2026 election bid.

LUSAKA mayoral candidate Simon Mwila has spoken out after the 2026 general election, detailing the personal costs of his campaign.

Mwila stated he is coping well but is currently reflecting and praying. He ran his campaign without the support of party machinery or significant financial backers, leading to considerable personal sacrifice.

The candidate mentioned that he lost cars to what he described as 'Kaloba,' a term often associated with loan sharks or predatory lending, indicating financial strain from his political efforts.

Why it mattersSimon Mwila, a mayoral candidate, is affected by losing vehicles due to financial pressures from his campaign.

Kasempa Administration Cites Rise in Teen Pregnancies

District officials are urging traditional leaders to help address limited access to reproductive health information and rising adolescent pregnancies.

Kasempa District Administration has expressed concern over an increase in teenage pregnancies and inadequate access to reproductive health information among young people. District Administrative Officer Francis Makanga stated that traditional leaders are crucial partners in tackling sexual and reproductive health issues, including sexually transmitted infections and limited access to vital information within communities.

Makanga made these remarks during a meeting with traditional leaders in Kasempa District, North-Western Province, focused on integrated sexual and reproductive health services. He emphasized that sexual and reproductive health is a fundamental human right and a cornerstone of sustainable development, empowering individuals and communities to make informed life choices. The integrated approach combines family planning, maternal and child health, HIV and STI prevention, adolescent health, and gender-based violence response for comprehensive care.

The district is calling for the creation of a supportive environment where adolescents can obtain age-appropriate information and services without facing significant obstacles. This initiative aims to ensure that integrated sexual and reproductive health services are delivered in a manner that is convenient, effective, and cost-efficient for the population.

Why it mattersAdolescents in Kasempa District face challenges with teenage pregnancies and limited access to essential reproductive health information and services.

Zambia Police Warn Against Inciting Social Media Content

Police spokesperson Godfrey Chilabi stated that creating and sharing online content intended to incite violence is a criminal offense.

Zambian police have issued a stern warning to the public against the creation and dissemination of social media content that incites violence or threatens public order. The service expressed concern over the circulation of offensive and disrespectful remarks online, including those targeting President Hakainde Hichilema.

Police spokesperson Godfrey Chilabi emphasized that such actions are considered criminal offenses. The warning comes as authorities aim to maintain peace and public order by monitoring online activities and prosecuting individuals found to be violating these regulations.

The police service is committed to ensuring that online platforms are not used to spread hate speech or undermine the stability of the nation. This proactive stance seeks to deter potential offenders and uphold the integrity of public discourse.

Why it mattersThe public is affected by police warnings against creating and sharing social media content that incites violence or threatens public order.
