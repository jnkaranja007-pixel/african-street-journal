Zambian parliament approves ministry changes

President Hichilema's plan to merge and dissolve ministries passes National Assembly vote.

Zambia's Parliament has approved President Hakainde Hichilema's proposal to restructure government ministries. The changes, aimed at boosting efficiency and accountability, involve dissolving the ministries of Energy; Infrastructure, Housing and Urban Development; Commerce, Trade and Industry; Lands and Natural Resources; and Youth, Sport and Arts. New ministries will focus on areas like Trade and Export, Industry, Electricity, Petroleum, and Culture and Arts.

Vice President Mutale Nalumango moved the motion, stating that dedicated ministries for Electricity and Petroleum are important in responding to changing energy demands and supporting economic growth. She also described the Culture and Arts sector as an economic opportunity capable of attracting tourists and creating jobs. The motion passed with a majority of lawmakers supporting the proposal, which was put to the House by Speaker Nelly Mutti.

Functions will be transferred between ministries to align government responsibilities with national development priorities. Charles Milupi was sworn in as Minister of Transport and Logistics and Christopher Kalila as Minister of Housing following the approved changes. President Hichilema stressed that ministries should not be viewed as mere offices but as critical institutions for service delivery, facilitating investment and trade.

Why it mattersZambian citizens will experience changes in government service delivery and focus as ministries are restructured.

Zambia poll staff trained for Mandevu by-election

Electoral Commission of Zambia urges staff to know the Electoral Code of Conduct before November 5 vote.

Poll staff in Zambia's Mandevu Constituency are undergoing training for the upcoming parliamentary by-election scheduled for November 5, 2026. The Electoral Commission of Zambia (ECZ) is conducting the training to prepare officials for their duties.

ECZ Vice Chairperson Major General Vincent Mukanda officially opened the training session. He specifically implored the poll staff to familiarise themselves with the Electoral Code of Conduct during the proceedings.

The by-election in Mandevu Constituency will fill a vacant parliamentary seat.

Zambia releases K14.7 billion for farmer payments

The Treasury has released funds to settle outstanding payments for maize and rice supplied to the Food Reserve Agency.

The Zambian government will progressively pay farmers who supplied maize to the Food Reserve Agency (FRA), with most outstanding payments expected next month. The Treasury has released K14.7 billion to settle these dues. Acting Minister of Agriculture Situmbeko Musokotwane stated that this funding will cover payments for maize and rice delivered to the agency.

The FRA purchased 2.7 million tonnes of maize from 1,850 depots nationwide for K18.738 billion, with K4.1 billion already paid. This leaves an outstanding balance of K14.6 billion. The agency also bought 1,304.41 tonnes of rice valued at K9.7 million. Government is committed to settling all payments as funds become available.

To improve stock turnover, FRA is seeking regional markets for surplus maize, having secured sales contracts for 1.7 million metric tons for export and domestic markets. Revenue from these sales will support future procurement and maintain national food reserves, which are sufficient until the next harvest.

Why it mattersFarmers who supplied maize and rice to the Food Reserve Agency will receive their outstanding payments following the release of K14.7 billion.

Zambia energy conference explores future technologies

The 2nd Power & Elec Zambia International Exhibition & Conference opened in Lusaka with stakeholders discussing energy solutions.

The 2nd Power & Elec Zambia International Exhibition & Conference began today at the New Government Complex in Lusaka. The event gathers stakeholders from Zambia’s power and energy sector to discuss technologies, opportunities, and solutions for the nation's energy future.

Mr. Mafayo Zuba, Director of Energy, represented the Ministry of Energy at the opening of the conference. Attendees are exploring innovations and strategies that will shape the country's energy landscape.

The conference is open to everyone, with no account or email required for entry. Discussions will focus on advancing Zambia's energy sector.

Two Zambians jailed five years for ivory

Lusaka Magistrate Court sentenced Bydon Ndolo, 30, and West Ng’andu, 48, to five years each for unlawful possession of elephant ivory.

Lusaka Magistrate Court has sentenced two men to five years imprisonment each for unlawfully possessing elephant ivory. Magistrate Anna Holland found Bydon Ndolo, 30, and West Ng’andu, 48, guilty of possessing a prescribed trophy under the Zambia Wildlife Act.

The convictions relate to unlawful possession of elephant ivory, which is a prescribed trophy. The Zambia Wildlife Act governs penalties for such offenses.

The sentencing took place on October 29, 2026. The court also fined two other individuals for illegal timber possession.

Why it mattersBydon Ndolo and West Ng’andu will serve five years in prison for possessing elephant ivory.
