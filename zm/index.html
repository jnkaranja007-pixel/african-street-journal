Yango Zambia and LOLC Finance Launch Vehicle Ownership Program

The Drive to Own initiative allows eligible drivers to finance vehicles with a low initial contribution.

Yango Zambia, a subsidiary of Yango Group, has partnered with LOLC Finance to introduce the Drive to Own initiative. This program aims to provide vehicle financing solutions for eligible partner drivers in Zambia. The initiative was launched on October 19, 2026, in Lusaka.

Under the Drive to Own program, drivers can access vehicle financing by making a minimal initial contribution of 10%. This scheme is designed to make vehicle ownership more accessible to individuals working with Yango.

More than 200 vehicles have already been financed through this new program since its inception. The partnership between Yango Zambia and LOLC Finance seeks to expand opportunities for drivers within the Yango ecosystem.

Why it mattersThe initiative affects drivers by providing a pathway to vehicle ownership through financing.

Zambia U-17 Women Seek Recovery Against Venezuela

The Zambian Under-17 Women's National Team faces Venezuela in a crucial World Cup match after an opening defeat.

The Zambia Under-17 Women’s National Team aims for recovery in their second Group D match at the FIFA U-17 Women’s World Cup in Morocco, facing Venezuela on Wednesday. The team seeks a turnaround after a 5-0 loss to Japan in their opening game. Venezuela also lost their first match, falling 2-0 to France, placing both teams under pressure to secure a win and keep their last-16 hopes alive.

Despite the significant defeat to Japan, coach Carol Kanyemba expressed confidence in her team's morale, stating that the setback should serve as a turning point. Kanyemba emphasized the importance of the right attitude for the upcoming match, viewing it as an opportunity to advance from the group stage. She anticipates a physical contest, noting Venezuela's playing style is similar to African teams, and believes maximum points are achievable.

Zambia's progression in the tournament follows a Round of 16 appearance last year, a step up from their first-round elimination in 2024. Coach Kanyemba, who has guided the team to three consecutive World Cups, aims to continue this upward trend in performance and development.

Why it mattersThe Zambia U-17 Women's National Team needs a win against Venezuela to advance in the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup.

Banda Apprehension Defended by Citizens and State

Zambians and the government reject criticism of the arrest of former MP Emmanuel Jay Banda, stating he must face justice.

Zambian citizens and the State have defended the apprehension of former Petauke Member of Parliament Emmanuel Jay Banda, dismissing criticism from some civil society organizations and insisting the fugitive must face justice. Members of the public stated that no one is above the law and that a suspect who publicly called for violence should answer before a competent court. Citizens argued that Banda’s alleged broadcasts, which included calls for the assassination of a democratically elected head of state and the annihilation of an ethnic group, cannot be defended as free expression.

The citizenry's position aligns with that of the State. In a statement, State House Communications Specialist Clayson Hamasaka assured the nation that Banda will be afforded medical care, access to legal counsel and his family, and all rights guaranteed by the Constitution. "Justice will be administered in accordance with the law," Hamasaka said. He was responding to a joint statement by a group of local and regional civil society organizations that questioned the apprehension.

The civil society groups, while not declaring the apprehension unlawful, called on the international community to condemn "these acts of lawlessness." Many citizens argued that describing such incitement as merely “vulgar” minimizes its gravity and that turning a fugitive into a victim undermines genuine human rights advocacy.

Why it mattersFormer MP Emmanuel Jay Banda's apprehension is defended by citizens and the state, who insist he must face justice for alleged incitement.

Chikosa School Faces Classroom Shortage Amid Enrollment Surge

Head teacher appeals for more classrooms and teacher housing due to increased attendance following the implementation of free education.

Chikosa Primary School in Mporokoso is experiencing a critical shortage of classroom space as a direct result of the government's free education policy. Head teacher Yolanda Chirwa stated that the policy has successfully encouraged former dropouts to return to school, creating a positive but challenging surge in pupil numbers. This influx necessitates immediate expansion of school facilities to accommodate the growing student body.

In addition to the urgent need for more classrooms, Ms. Chirwa also highlighted a pressing requirement for additional teacher housing. The Chikosa Parents Teachers Committee Chairperson, Kasanda Muswewa, acknowledged the government's efforts in improving learner comfort, but the school's infrastructure is struggling to keep pace with the increased enrollment.

The free education law has provided a second chance for many children to access schooling, a development welcomed by the school community. However, the success of this initiative has outstripped the school's current capacity, underscoring the need for investment in both educational and residential facilities to support the expanded student and staff population.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected because increased enrollment at Chikosa Primary School may divert resources and attention from agricultural education programs.

Man dies after sister pours hot water on him

A 36-year-old man died from severe burns after his younger sister allegedly poured boiling water on him during a domestic dispute.

A 36-year-old man in Mazabuka District has died following an incident where his 15-year-old sister allegedly poured hot water on him. The victim sustained severe burns and was admitted to Mazabuka General Hospital on October 11, 2026. He remained hospitalized until October 17, 2026, when he was pronounced dead by a medical officer.

According to Southern Province Police Commanding Officer Moono Namalongo, the altercation began when the deceased, reportedly in a drunken state, arrived home and insulted his sister and attempted to assault their mother. The juvenile suspect, who was preparing to cook, allegedly reacted by pouring boiling water from a brazier onto her brother.

Police were informed of the circumstances surrounding the death after the incident. The 15-year-old suspect has been detained by police. The body is currently at Mazabuka General Hospital mortuary, awaiting a postmortem examination to confirm the exact cause of death.

Why it mattersA 36-year-old man died from injuries sustained in a domestic dispute.
