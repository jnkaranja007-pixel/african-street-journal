Education · 2026-11-14

Hichilema orders urban teachers moved to rural schools

Public workers who fail to return to their original posts could lose their government salaries, the president has warned.

President Hakainde Hichilema has ordered that surplus teachers in towns and cities be moved at once to rural schools that are short of staff. The same order covers nurses under the Ministry of Health and agricultural extension officers who have left their assigned posts.

Those who do not go back to the stations they were first posted to could be struck off the government payroll, the president said. The aim is to fill gaps in rural areas where staff are missing.

The report gives no timetable for the moves and does not say when the affected workers must report to their new postings.

Why it mattersTeachers, nurses and extension officers who have strayed from their posts face losing their salaries if they stay where they are.

Security · 2026-11-14

Zambia police to go digital

Government plans to deploy surveillance cameras to improve crime prevention and detection.

The Zambian government plans to digitalise police operations to enhance crime prevention, detection, and national security. Minister of Home Affairs and Internal Security Clement Andeleki announced the initiative, which will involve installing surveillance cameras in public areas. These cameras are intended to enable the police to respond more effectively to criminal activities across the country.

Dr. Andeleki stated that the deployment of these cameras is a strategic move to strengthen the Zambia Police Service's capabilities. The aim is to create a more robust system for monitoring public spaces and gathering evidence. This digital transformation is expected to improve the overall efficiency of law enforcement in combating crime.

The initiative is part of a broader effort to modernise the police force and adapt to new technological approaches in security. The government believes this digital upgrade will significantly contribute to maintaining public order and safety throughout Zambia.

Why it mattersSurveillance cameras will be deployed in public places to improve police response to crime.

Agriculture · 2026-11-14

Mulendema communal reservoir gives Zambian farmers water beyond the rainy season

A communal reservoir in Chief Mulendema's area, Mumbwa district, is designed to hold about 1.62 million litres of water for smallholder farmers.

A communal reservoir built through the Water and Soil Accelerator (WASA) project is giving smallholder farmers in Chief Mulendema's area of Mumbwa district, Central Province, a way to farm through dry spells. The reservoir can provide an estimated 1.62 million litres of water every five to six weeks with its current pump installation.

The Mini Water Reservoir supports 50 households, or 250 end-users, in the Nalusanga GMA. Solidaridad Southern Africa, backed by the International Water Management Institute (IWMI), is running the project with GreenCare Eco Solutions, Zambia's Ministry of Agriculture, the Water Resources Management Authority (WARMA) and traditional leaders. Sheila Garakara, Solidaridad's Country Manager for Zambia, said the reservoir widens farmers' choices, particularly for women growing food.

Community members, particularly women and young people, have received compost and fertiliser, and a vegetable garden is planned for later this year. IWMI's gender researcher, Dr Ojong Enokenwa Baa, said women and young people must help set the rules for managing the reservoir, and that a women's club provides an important foundation, though more support is needed.

Why it mattersHouseholds in the Nalusanga GMA gain water for growing crops through dry periods, and women and young people are to help set the rules for using it.
