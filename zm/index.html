ZDA signs $122m copper processing deal

Rong Xing Investments will boost copper cathode production to 10,000 tonnes annually.

The Zambia Development Agency (ZDA) signed an investment promotion and protection agreement with Rong Xing Investments for a US$122 million copper processing project in Kalulushi, Copperbelt Province. The agreement, signed yesterday, aims to foster direct foreign investment in Zambia.

Rong Xing Investments, a Zambian subsidiary of Jin Xun operating in the Sabina area for 10 years, has already invested US$42 million and is currently investing US$30 million in plant III construction. The project will process copper ore into high-grade copper cathodes and concentrates using modern hydrometallurgical technologies.

The expansion will increase copper cathode production capacity from 5,500 tonnes to 10,000 tonnes per annum, supporting the government's goal of three million tonnes of copper annually. The investment has already created 506 jobs and will add 194 more, bringing the total workforce to 700.

Why it mattersThe investment will create 700 jobs and boost copper production.

Bank of Zambia donates K1.5 million laundry equipment to UTH

The donation includes industrial pressing machines and digital scales to improve patient care and infection control.

The Bank of Zambia (BoZ) has donated industrial laundry equipment valued at K1.5 Million to the University Teaching Hospital (UTH) Adult Hospital. The donation aims to strengthen infection prevention and improve patient care at the facility.

The donated items include two sheet ironers, two hydro extractors, three industrial pressing machines, and two industrial digital scales. Ministry of Health Permanent Secretary George Sinyangwe stated that the equipment is essential for controlling infections and promoting patient safety.

Rekha Mhango, Bank of Zambia Deputy Governor-Administration, noted that a clean hospital environment reduces healthcare-associated infections and supports patient recovery. UTH management has been urged to ensure the equipment is properly maintained.

Why it mattersThe University Teaching Hospital Adult Hospital in Lusaka received laundry equipment to improve patient care.

Zambia reaffirms pension system reform commitment

Government aims to expand coverage, strengthen oversight, and improve retirement income security for informal sector workers.

Zambia's government has recommitted to reforming the national pension system to broaden its reach, enhance regulatory control, and secure retirement incomes, especially for informal sector employees. Ministry of Labour and Social Security Secretary Zachariah Luhanga stated at the 2026 Annual Pension Conference that these reforms are vital for protecting member contributions, increasing benefits, and enabling pension funds to support economic development.

Mr. Luhanga emphasized that a sustainable pension system must safeguard members' savings, provide adequate retirement income, and assist families in old age, describing pensions as recognition of workers' service and sacrifice. He also noted that pension funds can invest capital in productive sectors like infrastructure, housing, and capital markets, contributing to national economic growth.

The Permanent Secretary highlighted ongoing legislative efforts to improve the pension system and identified digital technology as a key tool for modernizing administration, cutting costs, and improving information access. He stated that success is measured by the system's ability to protect members, deliver adequate benefits, and reach those most in need.

Why it mattersZambian workers, particularly those in the informal sector, will benefit from improved pension coverage and security.

Hichilema urges COMESA to boost trade

Zambian President calls for ambitious economic growth targets and increased intra-regional trade.

President Hakainde Hichilema challenged the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA) to move beyond commitments and adopt ambitious economic growth targets. Speaking at the 25th COMESA Heads of State and Government Summit, he urged member states to increase intra-regional trade and create jobs for their growing population. The President stressed the need for practical steps to strengthen trade, mobilize domestic capital, and invest collectively in production and value addition.

Mr Hichilema, who is the new COMESA vice chairperson, stated that Africa does the least business with itself and expressed concern over a "phobia of doing business with each other." He argued that sustainable development requires countries to support their own producers and businesses, emphasizing that "nobody will do it for us." The summit's theme was “One Market, One Future: Advancing Inclusive Industrialisation, Investment and Regional Integration in COMESA.”

The President called for the removal of trade barriers, faster movement of goods, one-stop border posts, and harmonisation of policies to reduce the cost of doing business. COMESA has a population of almost 700 million people and significant opportunities to expand trade in minerals and agricultural produce while promoting industrialisation and local value addition.

Why it mattersZambian President Hakainde Hichilema urged COMESA member states to increase intra-regional trade and create jobs.

stakeholders urge cso objectivity on jay banda

democracy advocate Fabian Mutale and UPND youth chair Gilbert Liswaniso question civil society groups' focus.

Stakeholders have urged Zambian civil society organisations (CSOs) to be objective when commenting on national matters, particularly the apprehension of former Petauke Central MP Emmanuel Jay Banda. They questioned the approach of some CSOs in discussing Mr Banda’s transfer from Mozambique to Zambia, arguing that human rights advocacy must respect the rule of law and the rights of all affected individuals.

Democracy advocate Fabian Mutale stated on Crown TV that some CSOs are hypocritical for questioning Mr Banda’s apprehension without considering his alleged escape from lawful custody. He said stakeholders should ensure Mr Banda answers to allegations through the judicial process, stating that while his life and right to appear before courts must be protected, the law should also apply to alleged offences. Mr Mutale added that Mr Banda should face charges for escaping lawful custody.

United Party for National Development (UPND) national youth chairperson Gilbert Liswaniso also called on CSOs to remain truthful and objective, with positions informed by facts. He questioned why some organisations demand government explanations on Mr Banda’s apprehension but not on his alleged escape from custody, urging consistent standards of accountability regardless of the individual.

Why it mattersZambian citizens, represented by stakeholders and political party officials, are concerned about the objectivity of civil society organisations' commentary on the apprehension and legal proceedings of former MP Emmanuel Jay Banda.
