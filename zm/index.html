Zambian president assures Mandevu of development

President Hakainde Hichilema pledged to address water, roads, and security challenges in Mandevu Constituency.

President Hakainde Hichilema thanked Zambians for re-electing him and the United Party for National Development (UPND) on August 13. Speaking at a rally in Mandevu Constituency, Lusaka, he assured residents that the government will tackle the area's challenges, focusing on water supply, road infrastructure, and security. He also introduced Adrian Banda, the new Member of Parliament for Mandevu.

The President stated that his administration's post-election focus is delivering development nationwide, with water, roads, and security identified as key concerns for Mandevu residents. He emphasized water as a basic necessity requiring urgent attention and highlighted how improved roads would boost mobility and economic activity. President Hichilema also directed security forces to address insecurity caused by individuals referred to as 'junkies'.

He suggested that youths involved in criminal activities should be taken through skills training programmes to become productive citizens, noting that the enhanced Constituency Development Fund (CDF) offers opportunities for vocational training. The President urged residents to abandon violence and embrace development, promoting freedom of movement without fear of attack.

Why it mattersResidents of Mandevu Constituency will see improvements in water supply, road infrastructure, and security, and may benefit from skills training programs.

Zambian cooperative backs fish in school meals

ZACOSO sees new markets for fish producers and entrepreneurship for students.

The Zambian Aquaculture Cooperative Society Limited (ZACOSO) supports the government's plan to add fish to school meals. ZACOSO believes this will create new markets for local fish producers.

The cooperative also sees the integration as a way to promote entrepreneurship among students. The government has called for the inclusion of nutrient-dense aquatic foods to improve learner nutrition.

ZACOSO expressed optimism about the planned integration of fish into the school feeding programme.

Zambia budget revenue covers 80.2% of spending

Domestic revenue increased by 76% across four budgets while expenditure rose 25.5%.

Zambia's government managed successive budgets through debt restructuring and drought, expanding community funding and social services. The 2027 Budget aims to connect economic stabilization to the Grow Zambia Agenda, focusing on stronger businesses, increased production, jobs, and household incomes.

Original budget figures show improving domestic financing, with domestic revenue covering 57.1% of the 2022 budget and reaching 80.2% by 2025. In 2024, domestic revenue reached K141.1 billion against an expenditure of K177.9 billion, with a deficit target of 4.8%.

Across the 2022 to 2025 budgets, planned domestic revenue grew by approximately 76%, while expenditure increased by 25.5%. These nominal increases reflect a stronger domestic financing position for the government.

Why it mattersZambians are set to experience stronger businesses, increased production, jobs, and household incomes as the government connects economic stabilization to the Grow Zambia Agenda.

Zambia labour minister demands monthly performance targets

Misheck Nyambose warns that poor performance and lack of accountability will no longer be tolerated in the ministry.

Minister of Labour and Social Security Misheck Nyambose has ordered management to implement monthly performance targets for ministry staff in Lusaka. He stated that the Grow Zambia Agenda targets are non-negotiable and that poor performance and lack of accountability will not be tolerated.

Speaking to ministry staff on Tuesday, Nyambose called for a mindset shift towards a results-oriented work culture. He instructed department heads and provincial officials to create clear targets and frequently report on activities to allow management to assess performance and identify improvement areas.

The minister also announced he will conduct unannounced visits to ministry offices and workplaces to check if officers are meeting their duties. Nyambose warned officers against corruption, stating he will not tolerate the vice and urged employees to uphold integrity, transparency, and professionalism.

Why it mattersZambian public servants in the Ministry of Labour and Social Security will face new monthly performance targets and unannounced inspections.
