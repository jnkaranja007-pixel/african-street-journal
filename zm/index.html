Court rejects State bid to block Mundubile and Zulu bail hearing

The Lusaka High Court ruled the bail application of opposition leaders Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu and 16 others must now be heard on its merits.

The Lusaka High Court has dismissed the State's attempt to block the constitutional bail application of opposition leaders Brian Mundubile and Makebi Zulu, along with 16 others. The court found the matter is properly before it and must now be heard on its merits.

The ruling removes a procedural barrier that had stood in the way of the application. The bail bid is being brought by two senior opposition figures and their co-accused.

The hearing can now proceed, with the court set to consider the substance of the constitutional bail application.

Zambia's 2027 budget leans on taxes to finance services

Domestic revenue would cover 82.1% of spending in the proposed K284.8 billion budget, up from 58.7% in 2021, according to the Budget Speech.

Zambia's proposed K284.8 billion budget for 2027 expects K233.7 billion in domestic revenue, made up of K178.1 billion in taxes and K55.6 billion in non-tax revenue. Domestic revenue would finance 82.1% of spending, compared with 58.7% in 2021 and 76% in 2024.

Professor Lubinda Haabazoka, writing in Mwebantu, acknowledged that taxes cut workers' disposable income and can squeeze business margins, but argued they fund schools, hospitals, roads and electricity. The budget sets aside K38.2 billion for education, K30.3 billion for health and K10.4 billion for Social Cash Transfers, which reach more than 1.6 million households.

He said the government still plans K41.9 billion in borrowing and K9.2 billion in grants. Rejecting revenue measures without credible alternatives, he wrote, would mean more borrowing, unpaid obligations or fewer services.

Why it mattersWorkers and businesses face higher taxes to fund the budget, which would reduce disposable income and squeeze margins, as the column acknowledges.

FAZ clarifies U-15 World Cup winning allowances

The Football Association of Zambia explained payment details for the U-15 team's victory in Baku, Azerbaijan.

The Football Association of Zambia (FAZ) has addressed questions about the winning allowances for its Under-15 national team. This team recently became champions at the Tier-B World Cup held in Baku, Azerbaijan.

FAZ media Manager, Godfrey Chikumbi, spoke to reporters at Football House in Lusaka. He stated that no funds for these allowances originated from the International Federation of Association Football.

Chikumbi did not specify the exact amount of the allowances or who would be responsible for their payment. The clarification comes after the team's success in the international tournament.

Why it mattersZambian football fans and players are affected by the clarification on tournament winnings.

Two men jailed for five years over live pangolin in Zambia

Mongu court convicts Anthony Musole Musole and Oscar Nyundu after police found the protected animal in a sack at a meeting point.

Anthony Musole Musole and Oscar Nyundu were each sentenced to five years in prison with hard labor by the Mongu Subordinate Court after pleading guilty to unlawfully possessing a live pangolin. The National Prosecution Authority's Mongu Office said the charge was brought under Section 130(1) of the Zambia Wildlife Act No. 14 of 2015.

Officers from the Department of National Parks and Wildlife acted on a tip from members of the public, and an officer posing as a buyer arranged a meeting with the pair. On October 28 the two were apprehended at the meeting point in Mongu, where a sack carried by one of them was found to hold the live animal. Neither man could produce a certificate authorising possession.

Prosecutors said the pangolin weighed 12 kilograms and had an assessed value exceeding 23,438 kwacha. Both men admitted the facts presented to the court before the sentence was handed down.

Why it mattersThe two men face five years behind bars and a criminal record for carrying a protected animal without a permit.

Zambia: 1,558 teachers idle as rural schools lack staff

Copperbelt Province Minister Emmanuel Kasambo revealed that 1,247 teachers in Kitwe alone are not assigned to classes.

Approximately 1,558 teachers in Kitwe and Ndola are reportedly receiving government salaries without assigned duties, while rural schools face severe teacher shortages. Copperbelt Province Minister Emmanuel Kasambo stated that Kitwe District has an excess of 1,247 teachers, including 548 secondary and 699 primary school teachers, who are not currently assigned to any classes.

Kasambo disclosed this situation on November 11, 2026, highlighting the incongruity of idle teachers in urban areas while remote schools struggle with staffing. The minister indicated that the government plans to address this imbalance by reassigning teachers to areas with critical needs.

The provincial administration is set to implement measures to ensure all teachers are deployed to schools where their services are required. This move aims to improve educational access and quality in underserved rural districts across the province.

Why it mattersRural schools in Zambia face teacher shortages, impacting student education.
