Moody's upgrades Zambia's credit rating to Caa1

The rating agency cited progress in debt restructuring and fiscal consolidation following the country's 2020 default.

Moody's Ratings upgraded Zambia's sovereign credit rating from Caa2 to Caa1 on October 30, 2026, reflecting sustained improvements in the country's debt trajectory since its 2020 default.

The Ministry of Finance and National Planning stated the upgrade demonstrates progress in debt restructuring, fiscal consolidation, and macro-economic stabilisation, following a staff-level agreement with the IMF for a US$1.472 billion Extended Credit Facility.

The improved credit rating could strengthen investor confidence, potentially lower borrowing costs for Government and businesses, attract foreign direct investment, and support exchange-rate stability under President Hichilema's Grow Zambia Agenda.

Why it mattersThe improved credit rating could strengthen investor confidence, potentially lower borrowing costs for businesses, and attract foreign direct investment.

Chief urges embrace of Grow Zambia Agenda

Traditional leader Chief Chisunka says the initiative aims to improve livelihoods and create economic opportunities, not seize land.

Chief Chisunka of Mansa District urged Zambians on Saturday to support President Hakainde Hichilema’s Grow Zambia Agenda. The traditional leader described the initiative as a program to improve livelihoods and create economic opportunities. He spoke during the 20th anniversary celebration of Chief Kasoma Lwela’s reign in Chembe.

The chief dismissed claims that the Grow Zambia Agenda intends to seize traditional land, assuring communities that their land rights remain intact. He stated the initiative encourages productive land use rather than ownership surrender. Chief Chisunka advised citizens to form cooperatives, increase agricultural production, and use Constituency Development Fund resources for irrigation.

Member of Parliament Francis Kope stated that traditional leaders are important partners in national unity and economic development. He emphasized that the Grow Zambia Agenda requires participation from traditional leaders and communities countrywide. Mr. Kope called on chiefs to partner with the government in improving community livelihoods.

Why it mattersThe Grow Zambia Agenda aims to improve livelihoods and create economic opportunities for Zambians through increased agricultural production and community development initiatives.

Zambia wins global green leadership roles

The nation secured vice-president roles for the Global Green Growth Institute's assembly and council for 2027-2028.

Zambia has been elected vice-president of the Global Green Growth Institute (GGGI) assembly and vice-chairperson of its council for the 2027–2028 term. These appointments provide Zambia with a greater influence in guiding the treaty-based organisation, which supports developing economies in their transition to sustainable low-carbon growth. The elections took place during the 15th session of the GGGI Assembly and 19th session of the council in Seoul, South Korea.

Minister of Green Economy and Environment Gilbert Siame stated that these roles will strengthen Zambia’s influence within the 55-member organisation. He explained that in these capacities, Zambia will assist the President of the assembly and the chairperson of the council in overseeing institutional strategy, work programmes, budgets, and performance. Dr. Siame noted that these positions offer a platform for Zambia to advance international cooperation on sustainable development and climate action.

Zambia plans to use its leadership positions to promote access to climate finance, technology transfer, and renewable energy investment. Minister Siame highlighted three priority areas for cooperation: investment in clean energy, electric mobility, and battery value chains. He also called for partners with capital and technology to invest in Zambia's green economy and requested continued GGGI support for developing bankable projects in areas like electric mobility and carbon credit generation.

Why it mattersZambia's leadership roles in the Global Green Growth Institute will help shape international cooperation on climate action and attract investment in its green economy.

Konkola Copper Mines employs 14,500 in Zambia

The mining giant's workforce has grown by 30 percent since Vedanta's reinstatement, with salaries also increasing.

Konkola Copper Mines (KCM) is now Zambia's largest private sector employer, with 14,500 people working at its operations in Chingola, Chililabombwe, Kitwe, and Nampundwe. This represents a 30 percent increase in staff numbers since Vedanta was reinstated as owner.

The company has also increased salaries, first by 20 percent in July 2024 and then by another 13 percent in January 2026, totaling approximately a 35 percent rise. KCM has invested in safety improvements, including a raise bore machine for ventilation and a new emulsion explosive, leading to the highest monthly mine development in a decade.

Beyond direct employment, KCM supports around 100,000 people through its Community Trust with $20 million invested in health, education, and infrastructure. An additional 1,200 people are employed through business partners, and KCM generated about $180 million for the government through taxes.

Why it mattersKonkola Copper Mines' expansion directly impacts over 14,500 employees and their families through jobs, salaries, and community investments.

Heavy rains blow roof off zambian teacher's house

Buyaka Primary School in Kawambwa District faces infrastructure concerns ahead of El Nino.

A teacher's house at Buyaka Primary School in Kawambwa District, Luapula Province, had its roof torn off by heavy rains on March 11, 2026.

Deputy Head Teacher Cornelius Chalwe stated that the incident raises concerns about the condition of school buildings as the country anticipates El Nino weather conditions.

Mr. Chalwe has appealed to the government for assistance to repair the damaged structure and address ongoing infrastructure issues at the school.

Why it mattersA teacher's home was damaged by weather, and the school faces infrastructure issues due to potential El Nino conditions.
