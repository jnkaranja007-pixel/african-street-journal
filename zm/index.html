International Bodies Seek Resolution to Mundubile Treason Case

The UN, SADC, and church groups are mediating discussions between the government and former opposition leader Brian Mundubile and his co-accused.

International organizations and religious leaders are actively engaging the Zambian government to resolve the treason charges against former opposition leader Brian Mundubile, his running mate Makebi Zulu, and sixteen others. Lawyer Milner Katolo revealed that the United Nations, the Southern African Development Community, and church mother bodies have initiated efforts to mediate the case. The accused are currently held at Mukobeko Maximum Security Prison in Kabwe, awaiting their court appearance.

Katolo emphasized that the current situation presents an opportunity for national healing rather than further confrontation, especially after recent elections. He stated that stakeholders are involved in finding an amicable resolution, drawing a parallel to a 2017 treason case involving President Hakainde Hichilema, which was resolved with the Commonwealth's intervention. The lawyer noted that the current case is at an earlier stage, before presentation to court, which he believes allows for more open dialogue.

The lawyer highlighted the potential for the UN, SADC, African Union, and church bodies to facilitate discussions between the involved parties. He believes these entities can help unite the nation, given that the charges stem from the electoral process. Katolo pointed out that the eighteen accused face charges but have not yet appeared in court, creating a favorable environment for mutual discussions for the benefit of Zambia.

Why it mattersFormer opposition leader Brian Mundubile, his running mate, and sixteen others are affected by treason charges and are currently in custody awaiting court proceedings.

Putin Congratulates Hichilema on Re-election

Russian President Vladimir Putin sent a congratulatory message to Zambian President Hakainde Hichilema following his re-election.

Russian President Vladimir Putin has congratulated Hakainde Hichilema on his re-election as President of the Republic of Zambia. Putin expressed confidence that Hichilema’s leadership will further strengthen the friendly relations between Russia and Zambia. The message was delivered in a press statement to journalists in Lusaka.

President Hichilema took the oath of office for a new term on Tuesday, following his victory in the August 13 general elections. He pledged to build on the economic turnaround achieved during his first term. The elections were described as tense.

The congratulatory message from President Putin highlights ongoing diplomatic ties between the two nations. President Hichilema secured a renewed mandate to continue developing the country across key economic sectors.

Why it mattersInvestors may see continued diplomatic engagement as a positive sign for future trade and economic cooperation between Zambia and Russia.

BETUZ Distributes 90,000 Shirts to Teachers

The union aims to celebrate educators and reinforce professional identity ahead of World Teachers’ Day.

The Basic Education Teachers Union of Zambia (BETUZ) has begun distributing 90,000 shirts to its members. This initiative is timed to coincide with celebrations for World Teachers’ Day on October 5, 2026. BETUZ General Secretary Henry Sinkala stated the shirts symbolize unity, solidarity, and professional pride among teachers.

During a launch event at BETUZ Headquarters in Lusaka, Deputy General Secretary Christopher Simukonda read a speech on behalf of Dr. Sinkala. The shirts are described as more than just clothing, representing the collective strength and dignity of teachers nationwide. This procurement underscores the union's valuation of teachers' contributions to national development.

Dr. Sinkala urged teachers to wear the shirts with pride, viewing them as a visible commitment to the union and the teaching profession. Distribution to all provinces will commence immediately, ensuring every BETUZ member across Zambia receives a shirt. Teachers are recognized as architects of the nation's human capital.

Why it mattersTeachers are affected by receiving shirts that symbolize their professional identity and the union's recognition of their contributions.

UN Rapporteur Demands Release Of Zambia’s Political Detainees

A UN Special Rapporteur has urged Zambia to end its post-election crackdown, citing arbitrary arrests and violations of human rights.

The United Nations Special Rapporteur on freedom of assembly, Gina Romero, stated that Zambia's human rights and democratic environment has worsened since the general election. Romero called on the government to uphold democratic principles, protect civic space, and immediately cease the criminalization of political opposition. Her office highlighted the dangerous precedent set by treason charges against opposition leaders and reported the intimidation and arrest of hundreds of opposition supporters, many held without formal charges beyond legal time limits.

The statement also detailed voter intimidation, assaults, abductions, and an escalation of violence resulting in deaths, including a former cabinet minister and a polling agent. Romero noted that these concerns about attacks on political opponents were previously raised with the government. A separate UN expert had also flagged similar issues in a 2025 report, indicating a pattern of shrinking civic space characterized by arbitrary arrests and disproportionate charges.

The crackdown is further evidenced by the de facto cancellation of RightsCon, a major human rights conference, in Lusaka due to administrative decisions and alleged foreign political pressure. The UN expert asserted that arbitrary detentions, charges like sedition and espionage, and restrictions on peaceful assembly violate international human rights standards. Romero emphasized that a healthy democracy requires freedom of expression for all voices and demanded the release of all political detainees.

Why it mattersZambian political detainees are affected by arbitrary arrests and disproportionate charges that violate international human rights standards.

Couple burnt to death in house fire

Unknown assailants allegedly set fire to a grass-thatched house in Chienge district, killing an elderly couple.

An elderly couple, identified as 70-year-old John Kaweme and his 65-year-old wife Chilombo Idah, died after their home was set ablaze in Kalembwe Kabobo village, Chief Puta’s area. Luapula Province Police Commissioner Lucky Munkhondya confirmed the incident, stating it occurred around 1:00 AM on August 31, 2026. Police officers found the bodies burnt beyond recognition upon arrival at the scene.

The report was made to police around 2:00 AM by Peter Musonda, a nephew to the deceased. Mr. Musonda, 35, informed authorities that his uncle and aunt had perished in the fire. Police have since retrieved the bodies, opened a docket, and initiated investigations to determine the circumstances and identify the perpetrators.

The incident took place in Chienge district, Luapula Province. The house was grass-thatched, and the fire was allegedly started by unknown individuals. Investigations are ongoing to establish the motive and apprehend those responsible for the deaths of the couple.

Why it mattersAn elderly couple in Chienge district died when their house was allegedly set on fire by unknown people.
