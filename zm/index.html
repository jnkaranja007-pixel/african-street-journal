Zambian President Hichilema Pledges Growth After Re-election

President Hakainde Hichilema aims to transition Zambia from economic stabilization to accelerated growth in his second term.

President Hakainde Hichilema has been sworn in for a second term, pledging to build on his first term's economic achievements. His inauguration speech outlined a clear economic direction for the nation, focusing on moving from macroeconomic stabilization to accelerated growth, expansion, opportunity, and prosperity. During his initial term, the government prioritized restructuring external debt and rebuilding international reserves.

The international community has continued to congratulate President Hichilema on his re-election. French President Emmanuel Macron extended his felicitations, noting the deepening relations between France and Zambia through dialogues on debt financing, health, and sustainable resource management. Macron highlighted the promising nature of ongoing and upcoming bilateral projects, particularly for the youth of both nations.

The Organisation of African, Caribbean and Pacific States (OACPS) also congratulated President Hichilema. OACPS Secretary General Moussa Batraki commended the Zambian people and institutions for conducting a peaceful and orderly electoral process. These acknowledgments from international bodies underscore the global attention on Zambia's political and economic trajectory.

Why it mattersZambian citizens are affected by President Hichilema's stated goal to move the economy from stabilization to accelerated growth and prosperity.

Zambia Invests in Cancer Treatment Infrastructure and Decentralization

The Ministry of Health is strengthening oncology services through new equipment, technology, and regional treatment centers.

Zambia has reaffirmed its commitment to enhancing cancer treatment and oncology services across the region. Ministry of Health Permanent Secretary Kennedy Lishimpi stated that this commitment is evident in the nation's investments in specialized cancer treatment infrastructure, equipment, and technology. These efforts were highlighted during the opening of a two-day oncology symposium in Lusaka.

Recent advancements include the commissioning of an upgraded radiotherapy and imaging section by President Hakainde Hichilema, featuring modern technology. The government is also actively decentralizing cancer services. The Ndola Cancer Treatment Centre is nearing 85 percent completion, and a similar facility is planned for Livingstone. These initiatives aim to create a coordinated cancer-care system.

Dr. Lishimpi called for increased collaboration among oncology experts and partners to elevate the quality and safety of cancer treatment. The objective is to ensure patients can access appropriate services closer to their communities, with specialized centers handling advanced and complex cases. This approach seeks to establish a more robust and accessible cancer care network.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Health's investment in specialized cancer treatment infrastructure and decentralization directly affects citizens by improving access to and quality of cancer care services.

Heads of State Inauguration Highlights Zambia's Infrastructure Opportunities

The presence of five African leaders at President Hichilema's inauguration spotlights billions in infrastructure and trade opportunities linked to Zambia's location.

Zambia's geographical position is drawing attention to significant infrastructure, energy, trade, and investment opportunities, according to Ministry of Information and Media Director Spokesperson Henry Kapata. The attendance of leaders from Kenya, Tanzania, Zimbabwe, Namibia, and Botswana at President Hakainde Hichilema’s inauguration on September 1 has highlighted the nation's growing network of potential economic links.

Kapata noted that these neighboring countries are part of an increasingly connected economic network, with railways, highways, border posts, and trade corridors physically linking Zambia to markets from the Indian Ocean to the Atlantic. Namibia's President Nandi-Ndaitwah expressed a desire to strengthen infrastructure for smoother trade, emphasizing the Walvis Bay route and Lobito Corridor connection to Atlantic markets.

Further east, Tanzania is central to the revival of the Tanzania-Zambia Railway Authority (TAZARA), with Zambia, Tanzania, and China signing a US$1.4 billion agreement to revitalize the line connecting Zambia's Copperbelt to the Port of Dar es Salaam. China's involvement signifies Zambia's regional infrastructure agenda attracting international partnerships.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the highlighted infrastructure projects and trade corridors that promise to enhance connectivity and market access for businesses operating in or looking to invest in Zambia.

Hichilema begins second term amid Zambia's political divisions

Zambian President Hakainde Hichilema faces the challenge of uniting a nation after a contested election and concerns over political freedoms.

Zambian President Hakainde Hichilema was sworn in for his second five-year term on Tuesday, following an election that highlighted deep political divisions within the country. This marks a more complex beginning to his presidency compared to his first term, which had reinforced Zambia's reputation for peaceful power transitions.

The election's outcome has been disputed, with Hichilema's main challenger facing treason charges and opposition supporters questioning the vote's credibility. The closure of courts during the period for election petitions has also drawn controversy, complicating the political landscape as the president begins his new term.

Political analyst Ngobola Cengelo Muyembe stated that reconciliation requires acknowledging the significant portion of the electorate that supported an alternative candidate. He noted that while President Hichilema spoke of unity and reconciliation in his inaugural address, the reality on the ground must reflect these sentiments for the message to be effective.

Why it mattersPresident Hichilema's challenge to unite Zambia affects citizens by requiring him to address political freedoms and ensure that his promises of reconciliation are reflected in tangible actions and political space for opponents.

Zambia Ministry Rejects UN Report on Treason Law and Detainees

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs disputes the UN Special Rapporteur's characterization of treason as a capital offense and the use of terms like "political detainees."

Zambia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation has formally rejected a recent news release from a United Nations Special Rapporteur. The release, issued on September 2, 2026, addressed post-election developments and contained what the Ministry termed a factual error, prejudicial language, and claims outside the Rapporteur's mandate. The Ministry stated that the Special Rapporteur's office and the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights should issue a public correction.

The primary point of contention is the Special Rapporteur's description of treason as a "capital offence" under Zambian law. The Ministry asserts this is false, citing the abolition of the death penalty in December 2022 and subsequent legislative actions, including accession to the Second Optional Protocol to the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, which entered into force for Zambia on March 19, 2025. No offense in Zambia currently carries the death penalty.

Furthermore, the Ministry disputes the use of terms such as "political detainees" and "post-election crackdown," arguing these present contested claims as fact and prejudge ongoing legal matters. The government contrasted this approach with that of the UN Secretary-General's Special Envoy, who was present and facilitated the handover of opposition figures. The Ministry reaffirmed Zambia's commitment to constitutional democracy and the rule of law.

Why it mattersThe Zambian government is affected by the UN Special Rapporteur's report which it claims contains factual errors and prejudicial language regarding its laws and the treatment of individuals facing charges.
