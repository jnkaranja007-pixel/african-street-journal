State House denies Hichilema ordered court closure

Zambia's State House has refuted claims that President Hakainde Hichilema directed the closure of courts to prevent opposition election petition filings.

State House, through Chief Communications Specialist Clayson Hamasaka, denied allegations on August 25 that President Hakainde Hichilema ordered the closure of Zambian courts. Hamasaka stated that the claim was "inconsistent with both the constitutional order and the available facts," emphasizing that the president lacks the constitutional authority to issue such a directive.

The denial comes as preparations for President Hichilema's second inauguration are reportedly continuing. The allegations surfaced amid reports of Zambian courts being sealed off as the deadline for election petitions approached.

The controversy centers on whether the court closures were an attempt to block opposition parties from challenging election results, a claim State House firmly rejects.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects Zambian citizens and political parties by impacting the perceived fairness and accessibility of the legal system for electoral challenges.

Zambia sets three million tonne copper production target by 2031

Zambia aims to produce three million tonnes of copper annually by 2031, a goal that could boost exports and fiscal revenues, according to News Diggers.

Zambia has announced an ambitious National Three Million Tonnes Copper Production Strategy, targeting an annual output of three million tonnes by 2031. This objective, if met, is expected to strengthen the country's exports and expand its fiscal revenues.

The strategy also seeks to deepen local supply chains and support greater value addition within the copper industry. News Diggers reported on August 25, 2026, that this production target is considered bold and economically important for the nation.

However, the publication also noted that a production target is not a forecast, implying that achieving this goal will require significant effort and strategic implementation to realize the projected economic benefits.

Why it mattersThe copper production target directly affects the Zambian economy through potential increases in exports and government revenue, impacting all citizens.

Mwense Hospital commissions 25-bed children's ward

The new facility, constructed under capital projects infrastructure development, aims to improve the quality of health services for children in Mwense District.

Mwense District Hospital has opened a new 25-bed children’s ward, enhancing pediatric care capacity. The ward was commissioned by District Commissioner Happystone Mwape, who stated it is part of the government’s commitment to improving health services.

The new ward was constructed as part of capital projects infrastructure development. Mr. Mwape emphasized the government's determination to improve healthcare quality, indicating a focus on expanding medical facilities in the region.

This development directly addresses the need for specialized pediatric care, providing dedicated beds for children. The initiative reflects a broader government strategy to upgrade healthcare infrastructure across the district.

Why it mattersChildren in Mwense District will benefit from dedicated healthcare facilities and increased bed capacity, improving their access to specialized medical attention.

Zambia courts close, blocking election challenge deadline

Zambia's top courts were closed on Monday for security reasons, preventing the opposition from filing a challenge to President Hakainde Hichilema's election victory on the final.

On Monday, Zambia's Supreme Court and Constitutional Court in Lusaka were closed, with police present inside the gates, according to Africanews. A judiciary memorandum advised staff not to report for work due to ongoing security assessments. This closure occurred on the deadline for filing an election petition, drawing criticism from opposition figures, lawyers, and civil society groups.

Opposition leader Brian Mundubile had stated his intention to challenge the August 13 election results, where President Hakainde Hichilema won with about 60% of the vote. Hichilema has denied allegations of electoral fraud, though independent observers have raised concerns about the poll's conduct. Mundubile was questioned by police over alleged national security offenses, which he claims were fabricated, and several members of his camp were arrested.

The Law Association of Zambia and civil society groups warned that the court closure risks undermining the constitutional right to challenge election results. Opposition allies reported they were still attempting to file a petition before the deadline. This event has renewed concerns about shrinking space for dissent in Zambia, previously considered a stable democracy in Africa.

Why it mattersThe closure of courts on the election petition deadline directly affects opposition figures and civil society groups by impeding their constitutional right to challenge election results.

Driver dies in Kazungula Bridge accident

A 32-year-old driver for City Frontiers in Lusaka was killed yesterday after being run over by an unknown truck at the Kazungula Bridge Point.

Andrew Edwin Simwimba, a 32-year-old man from Kabanana Site and Service, Lusaka, died yesterday in Kazungula. He was a driver for City Frontiers in Lusaka when he was run over by an unknown truck.

The incident occurred at the Kazungula Bridge Point, between four o'clock in the morning and seven o'clock in the morning. The accident claimed the life of the victim.

The Kazungula Bridge is a key transit point for commercial traffic, and this incident highlights the dangers faced by drivers operating in the area.

Why it mattersThe incident directly affects commercial drivers and their employers due to the loss of life and potential operational disruptions at a major trade route.
