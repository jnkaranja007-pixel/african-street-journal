Zambia's growth hinges on private investment, says finance minister

Minister Situmbeko Musokotwane stated that government spending alone cannot achieve development ambitions.

Zambia's economic growth over the next five years will depend on private sector investment, policy reforms, and increased exports, according to Minister of Finance and National Planning Situmbeko Musokotwane. He stated that government spending alone cannot achieve the country’s development ambitions. Dr. Musokotwane made these remarks during a symposium on the 2027 National Budget held at the Mulungushi International Conference Centre.

The minister emphasized that the private sector's role is crucial for driving the nation's progress. He highlighted the importance of policy reforms and boosting exports as key strategies to complement government efforts. The symposium focused on the upcoming national budget and its implications for economic development.

The discussions at the symposium centered on strategies to foster economic expansion and meet national development goals. Minister Musokotwane's statements outline the government's perspective on the primary drivers for Zambia's economic future over the coming half-decade.

Why it mattersZambians' economic future and development ambitions are directly linked to the success of private sector investment, policy reforms, and export growth.

Zambian child diagnosed with Type 1 diabetes

Diagnosis provided answers and initiated a new learning process for the family.

A Zambian child was diagnosed with Type 1 diabetes at the age of five. This diagnosis provided the family with an explanation for the condition and marked the start of a new phase of learning.

Understanding insulin, monitoring blood glucose levels, adjusting diet, and recognizing warning signs became essential for the family. The report emphasizes that naming the condition was a crucial first step, but only the beginning of managing it.

The approach to managing Type 1 diabetes involves continuous learning and adaptation. This diagnosis is particularly relevant as the world approaches World Diabetes Day.

Why it mattersA child in Zambia was diagnosed with Type 1 diabetes, requiring significant family adjustments to manage the condition.

Zambia boxers secure third place in Kinshasa

Team Zambia won three men's titles at the IBA Africa Elite Championships with five boxers.

Former boxer Kennedy Kanyanta praised Zambia's performance at the IBA Africa Elite Men’s and Women’s Boxing Championships in Kinshasa, DR Congo. Zambia's team finished third in the tournament standings. The team entered only five boxers but achieved one of the competition's standout results.

Team Zambia secured all three available titles in the men's competition. Boxer Mwengo was among those who claimed a championship. Kanyanta's comments followed the conclusion of the event in Kinshasa.

The IBA Africa Elite Championships brought together boxers from across the continent. Zambia's success highlights the potential of their boxing contingent on the international stage.

Zambians urged to uphold integrity

President Hichilema led Remembrance Day commemorations at the national cenotaph.

President Hakainde Hichilema yesterday led service chiefs, diplomats, war veterans, and government officials in commemorating Remembrance Day. Zambians were urged to uphold integrity, discipline, patriotism, and responsibility while reflecting on the legacy they wish to leave behind. The President arrived at the national cenotaph at about 10:50 hours.

During the service, Zambia Police Service head of chaplaincy Kondwelani Sakala reflected on the sacrifices of fallen soldiers and urged leaders and citizens to consider if their conduct would make those who died proud. Bishop Sakala stated that society must preserve fundamental values like integrity and discipline despite technological advancements and emerging security threats.

Bishop Sakala emphasised that service is not about positions or privileges but about accepting responsibility for the lives and welfare of others. He added that soldiers, police officers, public servants, teachers, parents, and national leaders all have a responsibility to make a positive difference.

Why it mattersZambians are being called upon to reflect on their personal conduct and contributions to society.

Zambian student leaves village for university

Samuel Mundia began his first journey to university with a few kwacha tied in a cloth.

Samuel Mundia left his village for the first time to attend university. His mother, Boma Mundia, prepared a few kwacha for his journey, tying the money into a chitenge cloth.

The young student woke before dawn for his departure, feeling the significance of the day. This marked his first time leaving his home to pursue higher education.

Samuel's mother, Boma Mundia, provided him with a small amount of money for his travel and initial expenses.
