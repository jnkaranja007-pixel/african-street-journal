Zambia prioritises digital infrastructure for growth

Minister Felix Mutati announced government focus on expanding digital infrastructure to support the Grow Zambia Agenda.

The Zambian government is making digital infrastructure a priority to expand the Information and Communication Technology (ICT) sector and drive the nation's digital transformation.

Technology and Science Minister Felix Mutati stated this at the Zambia Development Agency's Zambia-Germany ICT Business Forum in Lusaka, where Airtel Zambia formally committed support to the Grow Zambia Agenda.

Zambia targets 10,000 megawatts power by 2031

The nation is diversifying its energy sources to reduce reliance on hydropower.

Zambia has started an energy diversification programme to lessen its reliance on hydroelectricity and boost power generation to 10,000 megawatts by 2031. The Ministry of Electricity announced the initiative, which aims to increase the country’s power capacity.

Principal Public Relations Officer Bob Sianjalika stated that the programme follows lessons learned from past energy challenges. He spoke to the media today about the government's plan to expand power generation.

The diversification aims to secure a more stable energy supply for the nation.

Why it mattersZambians will benefit from a more stable and expanded electricity supply.

Sialubalo demands fair market stall allocation

Minister Sialubalo says Lusaka has over 14,000 market spaces but only 405 are allocated.

Minister of Local Government and Rural Development Gift Sialubalo instructed local authorities to ensure market stall and trading space allocations are transparent, fair, and accountable. He stated yesterday in Parliament that allegations of unfair allocation, multiple stall occupation, unauthorised subletting, and unofficial payments erode public trust in market management.

Mr Sialubalo emphasised that all trading must follow the law, aiming to promote lawful commerce and urban order rather than deprive people of livelihoods. He directed local authorities to conduct enforcement operations lawfully, professionally, consistently, and humanely, ensuring traders are informed about designated areas and treated with dignity.

The minister noted that removing traders from undesignated areas without providing suitable alternative facilities might not solve street vending. He expressed concern over traders occupying roads and pedestrian walkways, which causes congestion and sanitation issues. Lusaka has over 14,000 market spaces available, with only 405 allocated so far, despite approximately 8,000 registered street vendors needing accommodation.

Why it mattersZambian traders face potential unfair allocation of market stalls and trading spaces.

Hichilema visits Mandevu, election cancelled

President Hichilema will meet Mandevu residents today as all opposition candidates withdraw from the by-election.

President Hakainde Hichilema is expected in Lusaka’s Mandevu Constituency today to interact with residents and discuss community issues. State House announced the President invited residents to Mutambe Community grounds to raise questions on important matters. The announcement received over 13,000 social media comments within three hours, with many users welcoming the visit and calling for increased development.

The Mandevu parliamentary by-election, scheduled for today, will not proceed after all three opposition candidates withdrew. The Electoral Commission of Zambia (ECZ) stated that UPND candidate Adrian Banda is now the sole nominated candidate. Ntazana Musukuma Mutungwa, Daniel Chisenga, and independent Daniel Daka were the candidates who withdrew.

ECZ chief electoral officer Brown Kasaro said Mr Banda will be declared duly elected without a poll, although the commission is yet to formally announce his election. This leaves Mr Banda as the only validly nominated candidate for the Mandevu seat.

Why it mattersPresident Hichilema is directly engaging with Mandevu residents, while the local parliamentary election has been cancelled due to candidate withdrawals.

Lusaka youths walk 480km for Grow Zambia agenda

Five young people are walking for 12 days to raise awareness about national development.

Five young people from Lusaka, three males and two females, have started a 480 km journey called the “Youth Walk.” They are championing the Grow Zambia Agenda and aim to raise awareness about the importance of citizen involvement in national development.

The walk is expected to last 12 days. The youths are walking to encourage everyone to participate in the country's development.

This initiative highlights young people's engagement with the Grow Zambia Agenda.
