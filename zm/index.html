Zambia targets 10 million tonnes of maize annually

President Hichilema's Grow Zambia plan aims to boost food security and export earnings by 2031.

Zambia aims to produce 10 million tonnes of maize annually by 2031 as part of President Hakainde Hichilema's Grow Zambia initiative. This target, unveiled on September 1, 2026, aims to transform agriculture from poverty management to wealth creation. The plan includes seven other production targets for 2031, such as 10,000MW of power generation and a US$1 billion beef industry.

The increased maize production is intended to ensure food security for Zambia's growing population, which is now over 20 million people. With a target of 10 million tonnes, the country could maintain a strategic reserve of over 3 million tonnes after domestic consumption and feed requirements. This would prevent the price spikes and costly imports experienced during the 2024 drought.

Beyond domestic needs, Zambia plans to export 3-4 million tonnes of maize annually, potentially earning US$840 million to US$1.12 billion. This export strategy, managed under the new Ministry of Trade and Exports, targets markets in Eastern Africa, including Kenya, Rwanda, South Sudan, and the DRC. The initiative also emphasizes value addition and downstream processing to create jobs and industrial capacity.

Why it mattersZambians will have greater food security and potentially lower mealie meal prices due to increased domestic maize production and strategic reserves.

Zambia proposes K284.8 billion budget for growth

The 2027 national budget targets increased electricity generation, agricultural output, mining, and tourism.

Zambia's proposed K284.8 billion budget for 2027 prioritises production and infrastructure growth, shifting focus from economic stabilisation to accelerated expansion. Households receiving Social Cash Transfers are set to benefit directly, with funding for the programme rising to K10.4 billion, extending coverage to over 1.6 million households. Other Zambians' gains will depend on public services, market access, and taxes paid.

The budget allocates K68.5 billion to education and health, representing 24 percent of proposed expenditure. School feeding programmes receive an increase from K523 million to K1 billion. Health services will see K6.4 billion earmarked for drugs and medical supplies, aiming to reduce out-of-pocket expenses for patients. A K5.2 billion allocation is also intended to expedite pension payments for retired public servants.

Agriculture, fisheries, and livestock are allocated K22.7 billion, including K9.1 billion for the Farmer Input Support Programme to aid smallholders. The K18.1 billion road allocation aims to improve feeder and intra-district routes, enhancing market access for farmers. Selected manufacturers will benefit from increased customs duty on ceramic tiles and exemptions on raw materials for sanitary product production. Tourism operators stand to benefit from proposed duty suspensions on safari vehicles and hospitality equipment.

Why it mattersThe budget aims to boost economic growth through increased production and infrastructure development, directly benefiting households receiving cash transfers and potentially others through public services and targeted sector support.

Business · 2026-11-07

Zambia and US deepen private sector investment in green economy

Minister Gilbert Siame invites US investors to partner in sustainable businesses for mutual benefit.

Zambia and the United States have agreed to boost private sector investment in Zambia’s green economy. The collaboration will focus on wildlife conservation, electric mobility, and sustainable agri-food systems.

Minister of Green Economy and Environment Gilbert Siame invited US investors to partner with the government. He stated these partnerships would develop sustainable businesses that benefit both investors and local communities.

The agreement aims to foster economic growth through environmentally sound practices. Further details on specific projects and investment frameworks are expected to be announced.

Why it mattersZambian communities and US investors stand to benefit from new sustainable businesses in conservation, electric mobility, and agri-food systems.

Health · 2026-11-07

President launches free spectacles for 100,000 Zambians

The HH Vision 100,000 programme aims to improve eye care access for vulnerable citizens, including learners and the elderly.

President Hakainde Hichilema launched HH Vision 100,000, a new programme that will provide free spectacles to 100,000 vulnerable Zambians. The initiative targets learners, elderly people, military personnel, and individuals living with albinism. President Hichilema announced the programme virtually during the Zambia Ophthalmological Society’s annual conference.

The President stated that the programme is an intervention rooted in social justice, noting that over six million Zambians require spectacles due to poor vision. He explained that the initiative aims to restore dignity and create opportunities for those whose vision problems affect their education and livelihoods. Pilot programmes have already been conducted in schools like New Jack Secondary and Kabanana Secondary, as well as at the Mikango military base.

President Hichilema highlighted that one in five children struggles to see the blackboard and nearly one in three adults has poor vision. He observed that vision-related challenges have long constrained educational opportunities and national development. The pilot programmes demonstrated that improved vision enhances learning and work performance.
