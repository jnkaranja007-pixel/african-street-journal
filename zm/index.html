Zambia to take over K1.7 billion NHIMA debt owed to hospitals

Health Minister Roma Chilengi announced Thursday that Cabinet has also approved a package of reforms to overhaul how the National Health Insurance Management Authority is funded.

The Zambian Government will take over the K1.7 billion that the National Health Insurance Management Authority (NHIMA) owes hospitals and health suppliers, Health Minister Roma Chilengi announced Thursday. Speaking on ZNBC TV1 Main News, Chilengi said the bailout is meant to ease NHIMA's financial strain.

Chilengi said the move is intended to improve service delivery at health facilities that have been waiting to be paid. Cabinet has approved a package of reforms that also covers NHIMA's contributions, the minister said.

The reform details were not immediately available in the report, and the government has not given a date for when the debt transfer will be completed.

Why it mattersHospitals and health suppliers owed money by NHIMA are the people directly affected, since the government is taking over the K1.7 billion that the authority owes them.

Police in Nchelenge arrest fisherman over Lake Mweru killing

Police say the 36-year-old suspect allegedly killed his 58-year-old fellow fisherman, Abraham Kanyembo, on Lake Mweru in Luapula Province.

Police in Nchelenge District, in Zambia's Luapula Province, have arrested a 36-year-old fisherman for allegedly murdering his 58-year-old fellow fisherman on Lake Mweru.

Luapula Province Police Commanding Officer Lucky Mukondya confirmed the incident and identified the deceased as Abraham Kanyembo of Kalesa Village in Chief Kambwali's area. Ms Mukondya said the incident is alleged to have taken place on the lake, where both men worked as fishers.

The suspect is in police custody while investigations continue, and the case is expected to proceed through the courts once police complete their file.

Why it mattersThe suspect is in police custody, and the case now moves toward the courts, with Kanyembo's family and Kalesa Village awaiting the outcome.

Musokotwane names mining, farming, tourism and energy as jobs engines

Zambia's finance minister says growth in four key sectors is needed before ordinary households can afford home loans.

Situmbeko Musokotwane, Zambia's Minister of Finance and National Planning, has named mining, agriculture, tourism and energy as the sectors that must grow to create jobs and business opportunities.

Such growth, he said, would give citizens the income they need to qualify for housing loans. Private-sector investment and higher household incomes are the route he set out for tackling that gap.

The minister framed the four sectors as the government's priorities for economic development, though the report gives no timetable or targets for the growth he described.

Why it mattersHouseholds hoping to qualify for housing loans depend on jobs and incomes that the minister ties to growth in these four sectors.

Agriculture · 2026-11-13

Zambia launches youth agriculture program

The Pro-Agro Youth Programme aims to boost agricultural productivity and rural economies.

The Zambian government has launched the Pro-Agro Youth Programme in Lusaka to create jobs and entrepreneurship in agriculture. The initiative seeks to strengthen the sector and transform rural economies.

Minister of Labour and Social Security, Misheck Nyambose, stated that the program reaffirms the government's commitment to expanding decent employment opportunities. His speech was read on his behalf during the launch event.

The Pro-Agro Youth Programme is designed to enhance agricultural productivity across the country. This initiative is a key component of the Grow Zambia Agenda, focusing on rural economic development.

Why it mattersYoung people in rural areas will gain employment and entrepreneurship opportunities through the Pro-Agro Youth Programme.
