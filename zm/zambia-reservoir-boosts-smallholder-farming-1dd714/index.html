Zambia reservoir boosts smallholder farming

A new water reservoir established by the WASA project will support 50 households in Chief Mulendema’s area, improving water access and enabling year-round cultivation.

Source: Farmers Review Africa · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

A new communal reservoir capable of holding 1.62 million litres of water is transforming farming for smallholder farmers in Chief Mulendema’s area, Zambia. The Water and Soil Accelerator (WASA) project established the reservoir to combat erratic rainfall and dry conditions, aiming to provide a consistent water source for agricultural production during dry periods.

The Mini Water Reservoir is designed to support 50 households, benefiting 250 people by improving water access, reducing production costs, and increasing crop yields. Solidaridad Southern Africa, with support from the International Water Management Institute (IWMI) and local partners including the Ministry of Agriculture, is implementing the project. Chief Mulendema stated that the reservoir offers the community a chance to plan beyond immediate rainfall and emphasized fair benefit sharing.

The project combines water storage with practical support for farmers, including training in sustainable farming and responsible water management. Plans are underway to establish a vegetable garden for practical learning on efficient water use. Solidaridad's Country Manager for Zambia, Sheila Garakara, noted that the intervention expands choices for farmers, particularly women, to strengthen household incomes.

Why it mattersFarmers in Chief Mulendema’s area can now plan planting and crop maintenance through dry spells, with a new reservoir providing water for up to 250 people.
