Algerian physicist says tensions drove his move from France to the US

Noureddine Melikechi says he left for the United States after fearing the social climate in France would not suit his family.

Sources: TSA Algerie and Algerie360 · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Noureddine Melikechi, an Algerian physicist who later worked at the United States National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), says he left France for the United States after deciding the social climate was unsafe for raising his children. He told his story in a biography written by Rym Khelil. Melikechi had studied in Algeria and the United Kingdom before taking academic posts in France.

Melikechi passed the lecturer competition in 1994 and became a university professor the following year. He joined the University of Toulouse in summer 1996, where he said he learned from a research team and worked with advanced equipment. But he recalled continuing tensions between Algeria and France, and said Algerian colleagues in French universities were also considering moves to the United States. Some told him they felt they were not treated like French-born colleagues despite their qualifications and professional skills.

Melikechi recalled that a childhood friend with a new doctorate in computer science was considering leaving France in 1995 amid the far right's electoral rise. The University of Massachusetts recently named one of its research programmes after Melikechi, and President Abdelmadjid Tebboune congratulated him.

Why it mattersAlgerian researchers in French universities told Melikechi they were considering moving to the United States, while some felt their qualifications did not bring equal treatment.
