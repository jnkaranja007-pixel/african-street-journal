Parties Open Candidacy for Local Elections Amidst Exclusion Fears

Political parties have officially opened candidate nominations for upcoming local elections, emphasizing competence and experience to avoid past disqualifications.

Political parties in Algeria have begun accepting applications for the upcoming local elections, scheduled for November 28. This marks the start of the process for individuals seeking to join municipal and provincial popular councils. Several parties are urging their members and potential candidates to submit applications, stressing the importance of competence, experience, integrity, and popularity. This focus aims to prevent a repeat of disqualifications that affected candidates in recent legislative elections.

The National Liberation Front (FLN) has set a deadline of September 10 for submitting applications, with internal committee deliberations scheduled for September 11 and 12. Other parties, including the Movement of Society for Peace (MSP), the Future Front, and the National Democratic Rally (RND), are also reviewing candidates based on criteria beyond mere party affiliation. They are prioritizing skills, honesty, and the ability to lead, recognizing that local elections heavily rely on voter familiarity with candidates.

These party preparations are influenced by recent legislative election experiences, where numerous candidacies were rejected due to legal or electoral status issues. Parties are now more cautious in vetting candidates to avoid disruptions to their lists and to strengthen their competitive chances. Some potential candidates remain hesitant due to concerns about their applications being accepted or legal/party status complications, especially with new regulations limiting political movement between parties.

Why it mattersPolitical parties and potential candidates are affected by the nomination process and eligibility criteria for local elections.
