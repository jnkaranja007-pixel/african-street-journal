Bank ABC Algérie offers halal car loans up to 4.5 million DA

The Islamic finance product, Al Buraq Carousty, allows financing up to 90% of locally made or assembled vehicles.

Bank ABC Algérie has launched a halal car loan product, Al Buraq Carousty, through its Islamic finance window, Buraq. The financing can reach up to 4.5 million Algerian dinars for vehicles manufactured or assembled within Algeria.

This Mourabaha transaction, compliant with Islamic law, involves the bank purchasing the vehicle and reselling it to the client at a marked-up price, payable in installments over a maximum of five years. The loan covers up to 90% of the vehicle's cost and is available to salaried employees and retirees.

Bank ABC Algérie also offers other Islamic finance products, including "Moumkine" for furniture and "Alburaq Soukna Acquisition-Ijara" for real estate. These services are validated by a Sharia control committee composed of Islamic and economic specialists.

Why it mattersAlgerian salaried employees and retirees can now access car financing compliant with Islamic principles.

Algerian investor harvests apples 18 months after planting

Amine Derrar's 28-hectare farm in Tiaret yielded a good crop due to technical processes and irrigation.

Investor Amine Derrar has achieved a successful apple harvest on his 28-hectare farm in Tiaret, Algeria, just 18 months after planting began in June 2025. Derrar attributes the good yield to technical expertise, a drip irrigation system, and continuous learning about agricultural advancements. The farm's location at 1,000 meters altitude provides over 800 hours of cold annually, essential for apple tree dormancy and flowering, alongside ample sunshine.

Minister of Agriculture Sid Ali Khaldi visited Derrar's operation, which is connected to electricity and irrigation networks serving over 1,300 farms in the wilaya. This investment in Tiaret follows the success of apple cultivation in the Aurès region, inspiring other operators. Algeria produced 591,700 tonnes of apples in 2024 across nearly 32,000 hectares, with an average yield of 18.6 tonnes per hectare.

Algeria's apple production has grown by 27% between 2014 and 2024, according to FAOStat. High-quality apples can fetch up to 1,000 dinars per kilogram in Algiers. The favorable climate and increasing investor interest are driving this expansion in the fruit sector.

Why it mattersAlgerian farmers are seeing increased investment and production in apple farming, boosting local supply and income.

Algerian study finds link between eye floaters and retinal tears

A study of 896 people found 9.9% with posterior vitreous detachment also had retinal tears.

A study of 896 people in Algeria found that 89 individuals, or 9.9%, experienced retinal tears alongside posterior vitreous detachment.

Researchers also recorded 8 cases of retinal detachment and observed new tears in 3% of patients within two months who had no tears initially, according to pmc.

The findings highlight the importance of a retinal examination when new floaters appear, especially if accompanied by flashes of light or blurred vision.

Why it mattersAlgerians experiencing new floaters, flashes of light, or blurred vision should seek a retinal examination to detect potential tears or detachments.

Algerian author Yasmina Khadra calls Palestinian situation abominable

The acclaimed writer, participating in the Algiers International Book Fair, spoke about his new novel and his experiences.

Algerian author Yasmina Khadra described the situation of the Palestinian people as "abominable" and "unacceptable" during an interview at the 29th Algiers International Book Fair (SILA 2026). Khadra was present for the release of his new novel, "Le prieur de Bethléem," published by Casbah Editions.

The writer explained that his novel explores the dehumanization caused by violence and fanaticism, stating that fanaticism, not violence itself, strips individuals of their souls. He also mentioned being excluded by French literary institutions and juries since 2008, attributing this to unseen networks or lobbies.

Khadra encouraged young Algerian writers in Tamazight, Arabic, and French to persist in their vocation despite indifference and rejection. He advised them to write without expecting anything from others, as Algerian writers and artists often lack encouragement and face misunderstanding or censorship.

Why it mattersAlgerian author Yasmina Khadra expressed strong condemnation of the events affecting Palestinians and shared his personal experiences of exclusion.
