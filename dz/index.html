algeria launches probe into food waste

videos show produce dumped outside cities to prevent price drops, prompting judicial action.

Algeria's judicial system is investigating the deliberate destruction of food, particularly tomatoes, to artificially keep prices high. The public prosecutor's office at the Court of Algiers ordered immediate and exhaustive inquiries on Thursday, October 22, following the circulation of videos on social media. These videos depict individuals discarding edible food and vegetables outside urban areas from trucks.

The investigations aim to identify all parties involved in these actions, including those who incited, financed, or profited from them, regardless of their position. The judicial authorities also seek to uncover the networks and distribution channels behind these practices. Those found guilty will face prosecution with the full force of current legislation.

This action follows President Abdelmadjid Tebboune's recent order to the Prime Minister to investigate fraudulent practices and speculation in the marketing of imported meats. The president had previously directed investigations into similar issues surrounding imported meat sales during a Council of Ministers meeting.

Why it mattersAlgerian authorities are investigating the deliberate destruction of agricultural products to manipulate market prices, affecting food availability and potentially farmer incomes.

Algeria sets deadline for Mostaganem port specialization

Mostaganem port will exclusively handle public sector import operations starting January 1, 2027.

Algeria has set a timeline for the specialization of the Port of Mostaganem, designating it solely for public company import operations. The Port of Mostaganem company informed private economic operators that all non-public sector rolling traffic must be rerouted by October 31. A transition period until December 31 is available for ships with pre-scheduled rotations and existing commercial commitments.

Starting January 1, 2027, the port will no longer accept vessels not destined for public enterprises or organizations. The activity of self-entrepreneurs in maritime passenger transport is also being redirected to other ports by October 31. These measures are part of a national effort to reorganize port activities and transfer traffic to other locations.

This specialization is a component of Algeria's broader port modernization initiative aimed at reducing vessel waiting times and speeding up customs clearance and cargo unloading. The decision to reserve the port for public entities follows a directive from President Abdelmadjid Tebboune.

Why it mattersThe Port of Mostaganem will change its operations, affecting private businesses and self-entrepreneurs involved in maritime transport.

Algeria extends deadline for HR plan approvals

The General Directorate of Public Function and Administrative Reform has set November 10 as the final date for approving annual HR management plans for 2026.

The General Directorate of Public Function and Administrative Reform in Algeria has extended the deadline for approving annual human resources management plans for the 2026 fiscal year to November 10. This exceptional and final extension is due to delays in resolving legal reservations on some plans and new financial position data emerging in the fourth quarter of 2026.

In a dispatch dated October 18, 2026, the directorate informed ministry secretaries-general, governors, and heads of public function inspectorates of the new deadline. This decision follows an assessment of the progress in reviewing and approving these plans, which revealed outstanding legal reservations that require additional time for resolution to ensure proper HR management.

This measure follows a previous extension from March 15 to September 30, 2026. The current extension specifically covers the approval of plans already submitted within the initial deadlines and does not reopen submissions for new plans. The directorate stressed the exceptional nature of this final extension.

Why it mattersAlgerian government administrative departments must submit their 2026 human resources management plans by November 10.

Algeria and Tunisia sign fisheries cooperation deal

Algerian and Tunisian fishing schools will exchange expertise and develop joint training programs.

Algeria and Tunisia have signed a framework agreement to enhance cooperation in fisheries, building on a long-standing tradition between fishermen from Algeria's El Kala and Tunisia's Tabarka. The deal, signed in Annaba, aims to exchange professional expertise in fishing techniques for "Dorade Coryphene" using fish aggregation devices.

The agreement was signed by Miloud Triaa, Director General of Fisheries and Aquaculture in Algeria's Ministry of Agriculture, and Ashraf Ben Soliman, Director General of the Tunisian Professional Group for Fisheries Products. Triaa stated the accord will strengthen bilateral cooperation and develop partnerships between specialized training institutions to improve professional skills and joint practical training.

The workshop concluded with recommendations for a joint project at Algeria's National Center for Research and Development in Fisheries and Aquaculture, in coordination with Tunisia's National Institute of Marine Sciences and Technologies. This project will involve practical field trips and testing of fish aggregation devices to advance "Dorade Coryphene" fishing techniques and knowledge.

Why it mattersAlgerian and Tunisian fishing professionals will benefit from shared knowledge and training opportunities.
