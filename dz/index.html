Algeria opens two import control laboratories

Prime Minister Sifi Ghrieb inaugurated facilities at Algiers airport and port on Saturday, November 7.

Algeria launched two new laboratories at the Algiers airport and port on Saturday, November 7, to control the quality and health standards of imported goods. Prime Minister Sifi Ghrieb inaugurated the facilities, which were built in a record 40 and 60 days respectively by the Ministry of National Defence.

The laboratories aim to strengthen border control capabilities and combat fraud by analyzing imported products before they reach consumers. President Abdelmadjid Tebboune ordered the creation of these labs in all ports and airports to ensure product safety and protect citizens' health, according to a statement from the Prime Minister's services.

The initiative is part of a global approach to strengthen control throughout the supply chain and combat fraudulent practices through precise laboratory analysis. Prime Minister Ghrieb urged strict vigilance and rigor in these missions to verify imported products meet quality and safety standards.

Why it mattersAlgerian citizens will be protected from fraudulent imported products that may affect their health.

Algerian physicist Noureddine Melikechi honoured by US university programme

A US university has named a permanent scientific innovation programme after Algerian physicist Noureddine Melikechi.

Algerian President Abdelmadjid Tebboune congratulated physicist Noureddine Melikechi on Saturday for having a research programme at the University of Massachusetts in the United States named after him permanently. The programme is endowed with an initial investment of $2.5 million.

Melikechi, a researcher at NASA and dean of the Kennedy College of Sciences, stated that he was deeply honoured by the decision. President Tebboune expressed Algeria's pride in Melikechi's achievement, calling him one of the most eminent scientists of the era.

The University of Massachusetts Lowell (UMass Lowell) created the permanent scientific innovation programme bearing Melikechi's name. This honour acknowledges his contributions and impact in the field of science.

Why it mattersAlgerian scientist Noureddine Melikechi has been permanently honoured by a US university programme, reflecting national pride in his scientific achievements.

Algerian MP proposes fee for consular card

Djamai Nedjah suggests a 25 euro charge every five years for expatriates to access customs benefits.

Algerian expatriates have debated a proposal to make the consular registration card fee-based. The idea, reportedly put forward by emigration deputy Djamai Nedjah during discussions on the 2027 Finance Bill, sparked controversy online. Nedjah has since clarified his suggestion, stating the proposed fee is symbolic and intended to offer benefits.

Nedjah explained that he proposed a 25 euro charge every five years, not annually, for the consular card. This fee would allow holders to benefit from customs advantages related to the certificate of change of residence (CCR) every 10 years, instead of just once in a lifetime. He stated this amendment to the customs code is meant to be beneficial for the national community abroad.

The proposal has drawn criticism on social media, raising questions about diaspora contributions to consular services and national development. The packet notes that while the Algerian diaspora is large, it transfers less foreign currency through official channels compared to other Maghreb countries. Expatriates in Algeria reportedly benefit from free healthcare, subsidized fuel, and food products.

Why it mattersAlgerian citizens living abroad may face a new fee for their consular card, impacting their access to customs benefits.

Algeria's Bachir Belloumi ruled out of Togo matches

Hull City coach confirms the winger has a muscle problem and will miss key November games.

Algeria will be without winger Bachir Belloumi for their upcoming matches against Togo on November 14 and 17. His club, Hull City, confirmed on Saturday that the 24-year-old is suffering from a muscle issue.

Hull City coach Sergej Jakirović announced Belloumi's unavailability before his team's Premier League match against Arsenal. The decision was made jointly by the club and the player to prevent the injury from worsening, with Jakirović stating, "He has muscle problems, and we have decided not to include him in the group."

Belloumi will still join the Algerian national team camp for assessment by their medical staff, though he cannot play. Hull City aims to have him fit for their match against Brighton on November 22, expecting him to be sidelined for approximately three weeks.

Why it mattersAlgerian national team player Bachir Belloumi will miss two important matches due to a muscle injury.

Algerian-born MP quits French Socialist Party

Fatiha Keloua Hachi cited the party's political direction and a 15€ primary fee as reasons for her departure.

Fatiha Keloua Hachi, a French Member of Parliament of Algerian origin, announced on Friday, November 6, that she is leaving the Socialist Party. She stated her decision was driven by a lack of alignment with the party's current political direction after nearly 20 years of membership.

Hachi explained her departure was due to a "profound sadness, but without bitterness," as she no longer shared the party's political orientation. The victory of Raphaël Glucksmann in the Socialist primary for the 2027 presidential election, and the 15€ fee for participation, were key factors in her decision.

The MP, who joined the party in 2007 to fight racism and inequality, believes the Socialist Party has failed to represent the anger of the working classes. She aims to maintain a clear position for the people she represents, advocating for a left that protects the less fortunate and reduces inequality.

Why it mattersAn Algerian-born French politician has left her party, impacting the political representation of those with similar backgrounds.
