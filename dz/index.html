Algeria, Mauritania Discuss OIC Support

Algerian Foreign Minister Ahmed Attaf met with his Mauritanian counterpart Mohamed Salem Ould Merzoug to affirm support for the newly elected Secretary-General.

Algerian Minister of State for Foreign Affairs Ahmed Attaf held discussions with Mauritanian Foreign Minister Mohamed Salem Ould Merzoug on Thursday, August 27. The meeting, which took place in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, on the sidelines of the 23rd extraordinary session of the Council of Foreign Ministers of the OIC member states, included the newly elected Secretary-General, Ismail Ould Cheikh Ahmed.

During the talks, Ould Merzoug expressed gratitude to Algeria for its backing of Mauritania's candidate for the OIC Secretary-General position. He highlighted the competence and experience of Ould Cheikh Ahmed, stating it would enable him to effectively carry out his duties and enhance the organization's work and objectives.

Minister Attaf congratulated Mauritania on its candidate's election and reaffirmed Algeria's commitment to collaborating with the new Secretary-General. He emphasized that this cooperation would serve the organization's goals and principles, strengthening its role in advocating for the causes and interests of the Islamic world and its peoples.

Why it mattersAlgeria's diplomatic engagement with Mauritania and its support for the OIC Secretary-General impacts international relations and cooperation among Islamic nations.

Algeria Declares 3-Day National Mourning After Fires Kill 12

The nation observes a period of mourning following tragic fires that swept across several provinces, claiming lives and prompting condolences from the U.S. Embassy.

Algeria has entered a three-day national mourning period following devastating fires that resulted in the deaths of twelve people across multiple provinces. President Abdelmadjid Tebboune declared the period of mourning to honor the victims. The U.S. Embassy in Algeria expressed its condolences to the families of those affected by the tragic events.

The extent of the casualties was confirmed by the Minister of Interior, Local Assemblies and Transport, Kamel Beldjoud, who announced the death toll of twelve individuals. The minister made the announcement from Bejaia, one of the provinces impacted by the widespread fires. The government has initiated efforts to address the aftermath of the fires.

The U.S. Embassy conveyed its "sincere sympathy and solidarity" to the families of the victims and wished a speedy and full recovery to all those injured. The statement also included wishes for the complete recovery of those who sustained injuries during the incidents. The nation now focuses on recovery and support for those affected.

Why it mattersThe national mourning and reported deaths directly impact the livelihoods and safety of Algerians, including those in the diaspora concerned about family.

Algeria witnesses deep partial lunar eclipse

The phenomenon, visible from Algeria on August 28, 2026, will see nearly all of the moon pass through Earth's shadow.

Algerians will have a chance to observe a deep partial lunar eclipse on Friday, August 28, 2026, according to the Research Center for Astronomy, Astrophysics and Geophysics. The event will not be a total eclipse, but approximately ninety-six percent of the moon's disk will enter Earth's shadow at the peak of the celestial display.

The moon's entry into Earth's penumbra is scheduled to begin at two twenty-three AM local time, followed by its entrance into the umbra around three thirty-three AM. The eclipse will reach its maximum at five thirteen AM, when the moon will be very low on the horizon. The moon is expected to set around six twenty-two AM, before the partial phase concludes at approximately six fifty-one AM.

The center noted that this eclipse is a distinct astronomical event suitable for observation and photography, provided there is an unobstructed western horizon. The public can safely view the lunar eclipse with the naked eye, without any risk to their vision or the need for special glasses.

Why it mattersThis event is a visual astronomical phenomenon that can be observed by the general public in Algeria.

Fires Close Algerian Highways, Divert Traffic

Multiple road closures are in effect across Skikda, Annaba, and El Taref provinces due to ongoing fires, forcing traffic onto national and local routes.

Algerian National Gendarmerie Traffic Information and Coordination Center announced the closure of several roads on Thursday, August 27, due to fires. In Skikda province, the East-West highway was closed at the Ain Charchar interchange towards Annaba, with traffic redirected to National Road 44. Similar closures occurred at the El Ghadir interchange, diverting traffic to National Road 44A.

Annaba province also experienced highway disruptions, with the East-West highway shut down at the Ain El Bardah interchange heading towards Skikda and El Taref. Traffic was rerouted to National Roads 21 and 84. Additionally, Provincial Road 107 between the municipalities of El Triaat and Chétabi, National Road 84 between National Road 44 and 21 near El Allma, and Provincial Road 167 between Berhal and Oued El Enneb were closed.

In El Taref province, fires led to the closure of the East-West highway at the Edh-Dhraan interchange towards Annaba, with traffic diverted to National Road 84. National Road 82, connecting El Taref and El Zaytouna, was also closed and traffic rerouted to Provincial Road 105 towards El Asfour and Bouhadjar.

Why it mattersRoad closures due to fires disrupt transportation and logistics for people and businesses in the affected provinces.

Niger Prime Minister Departs Algeria After Official Visit

Ali Mahamane Lamine Zeine concluded his visit to Algeria, departing from Houari Boumediene International Airport on Thursday evening.

Niger's Prime Minister, Ali Mahamane Lamine Zeine, left Algeria on Thursday evening, concluding an official visit at the head of a significant delegation. His departure from Houari Boumediene International Airport marked the end of discussions and engagements held during his stay.

He was seen off by Algerian Prime Minister Aïmène Benabderrahmane, alongside several key ministers including the Minister of Energy and Mines, the Minister of Finance, and the Minister of Hydrocarbons. Officials from the Ministry of National Defence and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs were also present.

The delegation included high-ranking officials such as the Minister of Post and Telecommunications, the Minister of Public Works, and a Secretary of State for Mines. The Algerian Ambassador to Niger was also part of the send-off ceremony.

Why it mattersThe visit of Niger's Prime Minister to Algeria involves high-level government officials, indicating potential discussions on bilateral relations and economic cooperation impacting both nations.
