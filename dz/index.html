Algerian police recover 23 billion centimes from criminal network

Authorities arrested 25 individuals involved in counterfeiting, fraud, and money laundering across multiple regions.

Algerian police dismantled a criminal network involved in counterfeiting, fraud, and money laundering on October 29. The operation, led by the central service for combating organized crime, recovered 23 billion centimes from the group's illicit activities. The network operated across several regions of Algeria.

The Directorate General of National Security (DGSN) reported that the criminals used over 400 fictitious business registrations for fraudulent transactions to move money outside legal channels. This activity caused an estimated loss of 9200 billion centimes to the public treasury. Police seized trucks, cars, seals, and administrative documents during the operation.

25 members of the network were arrested and presented to the national economic and financial crimes division. Investigations supervised by the competent prosecutor's office revealed the methods used to deceive control services.

Why it matters25 individuals face charges for their involvement in a criminal network that defrauded the public treasury of 9200 billion centimes.

Algeria limits cattle import ban to breeding stock

The agriculture ministry clarified that live cattle for slaughter and imported meat remain unaffected by the sanitary suspension.

Algeria's Ministry of Agriculture has clarified a recent suspension on cattle imports, specifying it applies only to animals intended for breeding and fattening. This decision, issued on October 22, was made for sanitary reasons and remains in effect until further notice.

The veterinary services department issued a note on October 28 to economic operators, stating that live cattle destined for slaughter are not included in the temporary ban. Imports of various types of meat also continue to be permitted, according to the ministry.

The clarification follows reports of foot-and-mouth disease outbreaks in eastern provinces like Jijel, Béjaïa, and Sétif, which led local authorities to close livestock markets as a preventive measure.

Why it mattersAlgerian farmers and importers of cattle for breeding and fattening face restrictions, while those importing for slaughter and meat products can continue their trade.

Algerian businesses explore trade opportunities in China

Linyi city hosts international buyers conference to foster economic and investment cooperation.

Algerian businesses are exploring new avenues for trade and partnership at the fourth International Buyers Conference in Linyi, China. The event, running from October 28-30, aims to boost commercial exchange and economic cooperation, aligning with the Belt and Road Initiative. Officials and business representatives from China and other nations are attending to discover commercial, industrial, and logistical potentials.

The conference provides a platform to expand international partnerships and develop cross-border e-commerce, connecting global markets with Chinese production centers. Yu Shengtao, Deputy Secretary of the CPC Linyi Municipal Committee and Mayor, highlighted the city's historical legacy, economic capacity, and integrated logistics services as key strengths for wholesale trade and distribution. He encouraged foreign investors to leverage these opportunities for mutual benefit.

Chen Baiwei, Director-General of the Foreign Affairs Office of the CPC Shandong Provincial Committee, noted that previous conferences have successfully fostered economic and trade exchanges, attracting international buyers. He expressed hope that this event will lead to new partnerships between Chinese and foreign businesspeople, supporting international trade development and economic communication.

Why it mattersAlgerian businesses can find new opportunities for trade and investment through partnerships with Chinese companies at the Linyi International Buyers Conference.

Mini electric car arrives in Algeria for 249 million centimes

The Livan electric car, designed for city use, has sparked debate among Algerian car enthusiasts.

A new mini electric car, the Livan, has entered the Algerian market, imported by individuals and appearing in private showrooms for approximately 249 million centimes. This price has generated significant online discussion, ranging from mockery to genuine curiosity.

The Livan is a four-seater, compact city car with an advertised range of about 120 kilometers per charge, making it suitable for short urban trips like commuting or errands. Its unusual design has divided opinions, with some finding it futuristic and others comparing it to vehicles from old science fiction films.

The car's 100% electric nature and zero fuel consumption are appealing arguments for potential buyers. It is marketed towards students, new drivers, or households seeking a second, economical vehicle for daily urban use.

Why it mattersAlgerians interested in purchasing a new, affordable, and electric vehicle for urban commuting are directly affected by the arrival and pricing of the Livan car.

Algeria launches major non-hydrocarbon export drive

Companies like Condor and Faderco will ship hundreds of containers of goods to multiple countries on November 7.

Algeria is preparing an unprecedented operation to export non-hydrocarbon products, involving companies from various regions. The Ministry of the Interior and Local Authorities is coordinating this initiative to boost non-oil and gas exports and showcase the country's industrial and artisanal potential. This event is scheduled for Saturday, November 7.

Major companies are participating, including Condor and Faderco. Condor's CEO, Mohamed Daas, announced the export of 400 containers of home appliances and metal structures to Tunisia, Egypt, Jordan, Libya, and Mauritania. Faderco's CEO, Amor Habes, stated that his company will export 50 containers of hygiene products and raw materials to the United States, the United Kingdom, Tunisia, and Ivory Coast.

This operation follows a similar export drive on April 11, which sent non-hydrocarbon products to 19 countries. Companies from 13 wilayas participated in that campaign. Algeria aims to reach $5 billion in non-hydrocarbon exports for 2026, having already exported $4 billion in the first eight months of 2026, according to Zohir Moussaoui of the Ministry of Foreign Trade on October 18.

Why it mattersAlgerian companies are expanding their international reach by exporting a variety of goods, aiming to increase national revenue beyond oil and gas.
