Air Algérie opens bookings for summer 2027

Flights from Algiers to Paris start at 22,929 Algerian dinars for July, while the return from Paris to Algiers is priced from 426 euros.

Air Algérie has opened reservations for its 2027 summer season, with international seats and flights available on the airline's official online platform. The bookings opened in the first half of November, several months before the season starts in June and peaks in July and August.

Prices differ sharply by route. A round trip from Paris Charles de Gaulle to Algiers costs 403.68 euros in June, rising to 546.68 euros in July and 630.68 euros in August, according to the specialist site Voyages & Diaspora. The most expensive France-Algeria ticket is a Lille-Algiers flight in August at 657.34 euros, while Algiers-Paris is priced at 22,929 dinars in July, or 151.18 euros at the official exchange rate and about 83 euros at the parallel rate. Air Algérie made no official announcement of the opening.

Bookings for flights to and from Canada are also open. Demand is expected to rise as the large Algerian community in France travels home for the summer, and the airline opened sales several months early to help families plan their leave.

Why it mattersAlgerians living in France and Canada can now book summer 2027 flights, and fares from France are far higher than the return fares from Algiers.

Algeria tightens CV data protection rules

The national data protection authority issued new rules on handling personal information in job applications.

Algeria's National Authority for the Protection of Personal Data (ANPDP) officially adopted deliberation n° 05 on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. This new text establishes strict guidelines for processing personal data found in CVs, whether submitted on paper or digitally, to employers or recruitment platforms. The measure applies to all public and private organizations across the country.

The deliberation reinforces the June 10, 2018 law on protecting individuals in personal data processing. It specifically prohibits the collection or use of sensitive information such as racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, union membership, or genetic data, unless there is a direct legal justification. Employers can no longer demand such details without a valid reason.

Recruiters and employers must now delete or mask unnecessary information from CVs and ensure optimal security and confidentiality during their storage period. The ANPDP's directive aims to safeguard personal data and prevent its misuse in the hiring process.

Why it mattersEmployers must now adhere to new rules for collecting and storing CV data, with strict prohibitions on sensitive personal information.

Algeria warns of climate change impact at COP31

President Tebboune highlighted desertification, drought, and wildfires as major threats to Algeria's water and food security.

Algeria's President Abdelmadjid Tebboune warned on Thursday, November 12, that climate change is a present reality impacting the nation's water and food security. Speaking at the COP31 Summit in Antalya, Turkey, Tebboune stated that Algeria faces three major climate phenomena: desertification, recurring droughts, and expanding forest fires.

The president outlined Algeria's commitment to reducing emissions despite its small contribution, submitting a National Determined Contribution (NDC) aiming for a 14% reduction by 2035, financed nationally. Tebboune noted that this target could increase to 31% with international support. Algeria is also investing in 15,000 megawatts of renewable energy by 2035 and developing a national hydrogen strategy.

To adapt, Algeria is prioritizing desalination and water reuse, alongside reviving the Green Dam project to combat desertification and develop rural areas. Tebboune emphasized that the scale of these adaptation needs exceeds national capabilities, highlighting the need for international cooperation.

Why it mattersAlgeria is implementing national projects for renewable energy and water security, with a conditional emissions reduction target of 31% by 2035.

Algeria's army warns of disinformation, sabotage and drug trafficking plots

The army's El Djeich magazine says hostile campaigns aim to sow doubt about the country's recent achievements and weaken national unity.

In the editorial of its November 2026 issue, the journal of the National Popular Army (ANP), El Djeich, warned that attempts to harm Algeria's security, stability and unity are continuing. It named three forms these attempts take: disinformation, propaganda and manipulation campaigns; sabotage; and organised crime, notably drug trafficking.

According to the editorial, the aim of those behind them is to spread confusion and cast doubt on the large projects Algeria has completed in recent years. The magazine said the plots had failed, because the people remained united and attached to the homeland and its constants.

The editorial said the army would keep strengthening its capabilities for deterrence and defence, citing the regional and international situation and the interdependent nature of the threats the country faces.
