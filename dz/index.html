Algeria's ruling FLN wins 90 of 407 seats as turnout hits record-low 21%

Algeria's ruling National Liberation Front won the most seats in the country's parliamentary elections, the election board said Monday, securing 90 out of 407 in the July 2 vote. The vote saw a record-low turnout, with just 21 percent of the 25-million electorate casting ballots, and was marred by public apathy and controversy over the government's disqualification of roughly a third of would-be candidates. The National Democratic Rally and the Future Front followed as the next largest blocs in the new assembly.

Why it mattersRecord-low participation signals eroding public confidence in the political system even as the establishment retains full legislative control.

Business · 2026-08-05

Sonatrach signs new Germany gas-supply deal, cementing Berlin energy pivot

Algeria is strengthening its role as one of Europe's most important alternative energy suppliers after state-owned energy company Sonatrach signed a new agreement with German energy group VNG to increase pipeline natural gas deliveries to Germany from January 2027. The deal came during President Abdelmadjid Tebboune's state visit to Berlin, days after Sonatrach completed its first-ever LNG shipment to Germany on July 2. An Algerian-German economic forum on the sidelines of the visit produced more than 30 agreements covering hydrocarbons, renewable energy, pharmaceuticals and advanced technologies.

Why it mattersDeeper pipeline and LNG ties with Europe's largest gas consumer lock in export revenue and diplomatic leverage for Algeria's hydrocarbon-dependent economy.

Bank of Algeria holds rate near 2.75% as fiscal deficit widens to 11.5% of GDP

In August 2025 the Bank of Algeria lowered its key interest rate by 25 basis points to 2.75% and cut the reserve requirement ratio to 2% to support bank lending and non-hydrocarbon growth. Despite elevated gas revenue, Algeria's fiscal balance stood at -11.5% of GDP in 2025, the widest gap of any oil exporter in the MENA region, and is expected to widen toward -12% in 2026. The debt-to-GDP ratio rose an estimated 12% year-on-year in 2025 and is projected to reach 80% of GDP by 2030.

Why it mattersA widening budget gap despite strong gas exports raises pressure for tax or subsidy reform down the line.

Algeria's inflation seen rising to 3.5% in 2026 on fuel and wage increases

Inflation is projected to rise to 3.5% in 2026, driven in part by an increase in retail fuel prices introduced in January 2026, although those prices remain relatively low by regional standards. A minimum-wage increase in 2026 is also likely to add upward price pressure. The Algerian dinar weakened against the U.S. dollar in 2025 and declined more sharply against the euro, reflecting weaker relative performance versus other African currencies.

Why it mattersFaster inflation alongside a softer dinar could squeeze household purchasing power despite subsidised fuel prices.
