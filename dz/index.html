Algerian duo scores and assists in Paris FC's 3-0 win

Samir Chergui and Ilan Kebbal contributed significantly to Paris FC's victory over Nice in the second round of the French League.

Algerian players Samir Chergui and Ilan Kebbal were instrumental in Paris FC's decisive 3-0 victory against Nice in the second round of the French League. Chergui, starting for the first time this season, played a solid defensive game and scored the team's second goal after receiving a pass from Kebbal. His performance solidified his position in a competitive defense.

Kebbal, a winger, made a significant impact by being involved in two of the team's three goals. He first earned a penalty that opened the scoring and then provided the assist for Chergui's goal. Kebbal, who was substituted in the 83rd minute, demonstrated his considerable influence on Paris FC's playing style.

This strong showing from the Algerian pair comes at a crucial time. Chergui earned valuable points in a highly contested defensive line, while Kebbal continued to showcase his high potential despite ongoing questions about his future with the club. Both players' contributions were key to the team's success in the match.

Why it mattersAlgerian footballers Samir Chergui and Ilan Kebbal are directly affected by their performance in the French League, impacting their current team standing and future career prospects.

Algeria Shifts Military Doctrine to Proactive Stance

Analysts say the deployment of four Sukhoi Su-30 fighter jets to Niger signals a strategic move beyond defense.

Algeria has adopted a proactive military and security doctrine, shifting from a purely defensive posture to one that preemptively addresses threats. This change, driven by escalating regional challenges, particularly in the Sahel, and past security incidents like the Tigantourine events, emphasizes confronting threats at their source rather than waiting for them to reach Algerian borders. The nation aims to bolster its strategic presence in its immediate neighborhood, translating political and diplomatic stances into tangible field operations.

This new approach, supported by Algeria's 2020 constitution which allows for intervention abroad to protect regional stability and counter terrorist groups, reflects a desire to play a pivotal role in regional security. Experts note that Algeria possesses the capabilities and professional military to confront these risks, including terrorist group movements. The strategy integrates security with development, as seen in recent projects directed towards Niger and Chad.

The decision to send four Sukhoi Su-30 fighter jets to Niger's capital, following events in Niamey, is viewed by political analysts and strategic experts as more than a temporary military action. It signifies a fundamental transformation in Algeria's military and security thinking, driven by the need to manage burgeoning threats in its regional environment and leverage its strategic position. This proactive stance is seen as crucial for national security and regional stability.

Why it mattersAlgeria's proactive military stance is intended to protect its national security and regional stability by addressing threats before they reach its borders.

Algeria Approves New Earthquake Insurance Rates Based on Seismic Zones

Public authorities have revised compulsory natural disaster insurance premiums, linking them to property location and building compliance with seismic standards.

Algeria has implemented a new system for compulsory insurance against natural disaster risks, particularly earthquakes. The revised criteria and rates, approved by public authorities and published in the Official Gazette, now base insurance premiums on the property's seismic zone and its adherence to earthquake-resistant construction standards. This marks a departure from uniform pricing, introducing a tiered system that reflects varying risk levels and building integrity.

The new pricing structure, detailed in tables annexed to the decision, sets premiums for residential properties between 0.55 and 1.41 per thousand, and for industrial and commercial facilities between 0.37 and 0.94 per thousand. In low-risk Seismic Zone 0, rates are 0.55 and 0.37 per thousand respectively. Premiums increase with seismic zone classification, reaching up to 1.41 per thousand for properties in Zone 6 that do not meet construction standards.

This measure aims to incentivize compliance with safety regulations and enhance prevention against natural disasters. The decision also updates the benchmark price per built square metre for property valuation, ranging from 38,000 to 50,000 DZD for collective housing and 47,000 to 60,000 DZD for individual housing, varying by seismic zone. The new system replaces a decision from October 31, 2004.

Why it mattersProperty owners and businesses face new insurance costs that vary based on their location's seismic risk and their building's structural compliance.

Tougaï moves to new club in Libya

Algerian international defender Mohamed Amin Tougaï has signed a two-season contract with Libyan club Ahly Tripoli.

Algerian international player Mohamed Amin Tougaï announced his move to a new sports destination on Monday evening. The 26-year-old defender had been playing for Tunisian club Esperance since January 2020.

Tougaï has transferred to Libyan team Ahly Tripoli for a period of two seasons. The Egyptian coach Hossam El Badry, who previously managed ES Sétif, is in charge of Ahly Tripoli's technical staff.

Ahly Tripoli boasts a decorated history with 14 domestic league titles, 9 national cups, and 3 super cup titles. Tougaï will join compatriot defender Hussein Dehiri at Ahly Tripoli, who previously played for Paradou AC and USM Alger.

Al-Ittihad Club Nears Deal for Algerian Midfielder Boudawi

The Saudi club is in advanced negotiations to sign Hicham Boudawi from Nice before the transfer window closes on September sixth.

Al-Ittihad club is close to finalizing the transfer of Algerian midfielder Hicham Boudawi from French club Nice. Negotiations for Boudawi, 26, have progressed significantly, according to Saudi sports newspaper Al-Riyadiyah. The club is also pursuing Japanese player Ritsu Doan from Eintracht Frankfurt, with both deals reportedly nearing completion.

The Saudi club is preparing alternative options in case the primary targets are not secured before the transfer window's deadline on September sixth. Boudawi has played 207 matches for Nice since 2019, scoring 14 goals and providing 15 assists. If the Japanese player is signed, he would join for three seasons.

This intensified transfer activity comes as Al-Ittihad prepares for a match against Al-Nassr next Saturday in the fifth round of the Saudi League. The club is racing against time to complete its squad needs before the summer transfer window officially shuts.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the club's aggressive pursuit of new talent, which signals potential shifts in team performance and market value.
