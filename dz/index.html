Air Algérie fleet to reach nearly 100 aircraft as deliveries arrive

Air Algérie and its subsidiary Domestic Airlines will operate close to 100 aircraft once all ordered planes from Airbus, Boeing and ATR are delivered.

Air Algérie's fleet, including its subsidiary Domestic Airlines, will soon reach close to 100 aircraft as new planes ordered from Airbus, Boeing and ATR arrive. Transport Minister Saïd Sayoud told the National People's Assembly on Thursday, in reply to an oral question from an El Oued deputy, that the state has approved major investment in the airline.

Air Algérie has added 16 ATR 72-600 aircraft to Domestic Airlines. One has been received, two more are due before the end of the year and the rest in 2027. Sayoud said the planes are in near-constant use because demand on both carriers' routes is high. The fleet stood at 54 aircraft before the deliveries.

Air Algérie will also receive its seventh of 10 Airbus A330-900Neo aircraft this November, and 18 Boeing aircraft are on order. Sayoud said the airline is working to increase domestic flights, including to the far south of the country.

Why it mattersPassengers in the far south, where Domestic Airlines serves demand, should see more domestic flights as the new ATRs enter service through 2027.

HSBC quits Algeria after 18 years

The British bank will gradually cease operations in Algeria as part of a global group simplification strategy.

HSBC Bank Middle East Limited announced its decision to withdraw from the Algerian market after 18 years of operation. The British financial institution is exiting Algeria as part of a strategic review of its global wholesale banking operations. This move aims to simplify the group and focus on areas with clear competitive advantages and growth opportunities.

HSBC has been providing wholesale banking services, including international trade and payment solutions, to public entities and multinational corporations in Algeria for 18 years. The bank stated that the withdrawal process will be gradual to support its local clients and partners. HSBC has begun discussions with its Algerian clients to help them transition to alternative banking providers.

HSBC Holdings plc, headquartered in London, is one of the world's largest banking groups with assets totaling $3.445 billion and a presence in 56 countries. The bank also operates in other Middle Eastern countries and Turkey. HSBC has committed to fulfilling all its obligations to regulators, third parties, and the market throughout the liquidation process.

Why it mattersHSBC will gradually cease operations in Algeria, requiring local clients and partners to find alternative banking providers.

Zerhouni appointed head of Algerian diaspora scientific council

President Tebboune names the Algerian-American professor to lead the newly created body aimed at leveraging expatriate expertise for national development.

Professor Elias Zerhouni has been officially appointed president of the High Council of the Algerian Community Abroad, following a presidential decree by Abdelmadjid Tebboune. The decision, published in the official gazette, names the Algerian-American radiologist and medical professor to lead the newly established scientific council.

The High Council of the Algerian Scientific Diaspora, initiated by the head of state, aims to involve expatriate Algerian scientists in the country's development efforts. The council held its first meeting in Algiers in June, with Zerhouni serving as coordinator, and its missions were detailed in a decree published on September 7. Zerhouni stated that the goal is to collaborate on national development, bridging the knowledge of those abroad with those within Algeria.

Zerhouni, born in Nedroma in 1951, built a significant medical and professorial career in the United States, including a tenure as director of the National Institutes of Health (NIH) from 2002 to 2008. The council, comprising 39 members, met with President Tebboune in late September. Its mandate includes strengthening the participation of expatriate scientific expertise and proposing ways to connect them with the national higher education and research system.

Why it mattersProfessor Elias Zerhouni will lead a new council of 39 expatriate Algerian scientists tasked with contributing to the country's development.
