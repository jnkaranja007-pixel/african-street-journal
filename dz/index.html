Algerian Security Services Launch Coordinated Campaign Against Urban Gangs

Algeria's police and gendarmerie units are now conducting joint operations to dismantle criminal networks in urban areas, moving beyond previous sporadic efforts.

Algerian security services have initiated a more coordinated campaign against neighborhood gangs, a shift from earlier separate operations by police and gendarmerie. This forms part of a stringent security strategy to dismantle criminal networks operating in urban areas, which have increasingly threatened citizens' safety and expanded into drug distribution.

The operation follows directives from President Abdelmadjid Tebboune, Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces, who has emphasized a relentless fight against these gangs. Security services are described as fully mobilized to protect families and children, with authorities calling the current efforts a “joint war” against organized criminal groups.

Raids across Algeria, including large-scale operations in Algiers on Monday night, have resulted in numerous arrests and seizures. Algiers security authorities reported dismantling six criminal networks comprising 42 people, confiscating 58 bladed weapons, psychotropic substances, hard drugs, and 218,000 Algerian dinars in cash from criminal activities.

Why it mattersThis campaign directly impacts urban residents by aiming to reduce crime, improve public safety, and curb the spread of drugs and violence in their communities.

Israeli Leaders Threaten Gaza Residents Over Children's Kite Launches

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and War Minister Yisrael Katz have threatened to displace entire neighborhoods in Gaza if children continue to launch toy kites towards.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and War Minister Yisrael Katz recently threatened to displace residents of Gaza neighborhoods if children continue to launch toy kites. This reaction follows what they described as a "threat of launching balloons and paper planes from Gaza," leading to a "situation assessment session" on Sunday, August twenty-third.

The meeting included the Chief of Staff of the Israeli army, Eyal Zamir, the head of the National Security Council, and other senior army officials. Katz stated he directed the army to "act forcefully" against those launching the paper planes, evacuate neighborhoods, and target "terrorist infrastructure" in those areas.

Katz further equated every paper plane launch to a "full-fledged act of war," despite the absence of explosives or incendiary materials. He also claimed that a paper plane is like a drone, whether it carries explosives or not, raising questions about the severity of the response to children's toys.

Why it mattersGaza residents face potential displacement and military action due to children launching paper kites, impacting their safety and living conditions.

Wind overturns pleasure boat, kills two and injures six in Béjaïa

A sudden weather degradation on Monday evening caused a small pleasure craft to capsize off the west coast of Béjaïa, resulting in two fatalities and six injuries.

Two vacationers died and six others were injured after a pleasure boat overturned Monday evening near the west coast of the wilaya of Béjaïa. The incident occurred around 19 h 30 when powerful waves and sustained wind gusts caused the small vessel to capsize.

The calm of the seaside resort was abruptly broken by the rapid deterioration of the weather. Witnesses and citizens on the beach immediately mobilized to assist the occupants, but the intensity of the waves and strong currents hindered initial rescue efforts.

Civil Protection rescuers were quickly dispatched to the scene to secure the area and provide care for the survivors. The incident highlights the dangers of sudden weather changes for recreational activities along the coast.

Why it mattersTwo vacationers died and six others were injured, directly affecting their safety and well-being.

Algeria tightens border controls, expands anti-fraud network

Algerian authorities are reorganizing Customs services and quality control departments to intensify monitoring at land, sea, and air entry points and Customs-controlled areas.

The Director General of Customs, Major General Bakhouche, approved a new decree dated June 1, 2026, establishing Customs guard centers at land borders. These centers will provide continuous field presence, operating day and night through a patrol system, and offer operational support to Customs teams while coordinating with National Gendarmerie units.

The new centers are tasked with permanent guarding of the adjacent border strip, securing users and their properties, and ensuring the safety of seized goods until legal procedures are completed. Each center will be led by an officer with at least the rank of a squad officer, reporting to the head of the main inspectorate.

Additionally, the Ministry of Internal Trade and National Market Regulation, in coordination with the Ministry of Finance, increased the number of quality control and fraud suppression inspectorates to 53. This expansion covers land, sea, and air borders, as well as Customs areas and warehouses, replacing a previous list of locations with a new one that includes numerous sensitive sites across various provinces.

Why it mattersBusinesses involved in importing or exporting goods will face increased scrutiny and potentially altered logistics due to intensified border controls and expanded fraud suppression efforts.

Mali's Prime Minister Maïga receives advice from Algerian President Tebboune

Malian Prime Minister Abdoulaye Maïga stated that Algerian President Abdelmadjid Tebboune offered "wise advice" during their meeting in Algiers.

Prime Minister Maïga, visiting Algeria, conveyed a message from Mali's transitional president, General Assimi Goïta, thanking President Tebboune for an invitation to an official visit. Goïta views the invitation as a sign of affection and appreciation for him and the Malian people, and has agreed to the visit, with the date to be set through diplomatic channels.

Maïga highlighted the shared history and geography between Mali and Algeria, emphasizing that both countries aim to work together for the prosperity of their peoples. He referenced the historical struggles against colonialism and for human dignity, citing the universities of M'daourouch and Timbuktu as past centers of knowledge.

The Malian Prime Minister also noted the shared vision of leaders in Mali, Algeria, Niger, and Burkina Faso to make the region a peaceful space free from neo-colonialism, hegemony, and terrorism. This vision, he said, is part of the Confederation of Sahel States and aims for dignity-based development benefiting their populations, with Algeria committed to non-interference in Mali's internal affairs.

Why it mattersThe people of Mali and Algeria are affected by their leaders' shared vision for regional peace and development, which aims to improve their prosperity and security.
