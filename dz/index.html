Morocco, UAE, Israel align with Haftar forces in Libya

Rabat, Abu Dhabi, and Tel Aviv are converging around eastern Libya's military leadership.

Morocco has intensified its military and security cooperation with Khalifa Haftar's forces in eastern Libya in recent months. This move occurs amid regional rivalries and shifting power dynamics concerning the Libyan marshal's succession. The cooperation aligns with a tripartite coordination between Rabat, Abu Dhabi, and Israel, whose interests focus on supporting Haftar's camp.

Khaled Haftar, chief of staff for the eastern Libyan forces, visited Morocco from October 11 to 15, touring military installations and an airbase. Following this, a high-level security meeting in Benghazi on October 22 was co-chaired by Morocco's DGED director and the Libyan intelligence chief, with Saddam Haftar's presence indicating political dimensions beyond counter-terrorism discussions.

International media reported a meeting between Benjamin Netanyahu and Saddam Haftar in Abu Dhabi on September 29. Abu Dhabi has historically supported Haftar's forces, and Israel is developing contacts with family members. Morocco, a strategic partner of the UAE and linked to Israel by normalization agreements, is strengthening its military and security ties with the eastern Libyan command, fostering political and security cooperation with regional implications.

Why it mattersAlgeria is affected by the growing military and security cooperation between Morocco, the UAE, and Israel with Khalifa Haftar's forces in eastern Libya, which alters regional power dynamics.

Algerian physicist's life becomes book at SILA 2026

Author Rym Khelil will present "Nour. Noureddine Melikechi" at the Algiers International Book Fair.

Algerian physicist Noureddine Melikechi's life story is now a book titled "Nour. Noureddine Melikechi," authored by Rym Khelil. The book, published by Editions Barzakh, will be presented at the Salon international du livre d’Alger (SILA) 2026, which runs from October 29 to November 7. Melikechi is internationally recognized for his work in atomic physics, optics, and laser spectroscopy, and has contributed to NASA's Mars exploration missions.

Rym Khelil, born in Algiers in 1983, is an engineer by training who studied at the Ecole nationale polytechnique d’Alger and Ecole centrale de Paris. She currently works for an international energy company and lives in Malaysia, while also pursuing her passion for writing. Khelil decided to write Melikechi's biography after interviewing him in April 2021 for her project "Constellations du ciel d’Algérie," and was captivated by his brilliance and trajectory.

The book project emerged from Khelil's series of interviews with successful Algerians. She was struck by Melikechi's simplicity, sharp intellect, and his discussions about his childhood in Bab Ezzouar, Mars, and cancer detection. Khelil felt his exceptional journey deserved more than a brief mention in her earlier work.

Why it mattersAlgerians can learn about the life and achievements of a prominent Algerian scientist, Noureddine Melikechi, through the new book presented at the Algiers International Book Fair.

Brahimi scores twice to lead Al-Gharafa to victory

Algerian international Yacine Brahimi scored two goals in the final 10 minutes of the match.

Algerian international Yacine Brahimi starred for his club Al-Gharafa in their Qatar Stars League match against Qatar SC. Al-Gharafa defeated Qatar SC by two goals to nil, with Brahimi scoring both goals and earning man of the match honours.

Brahimi opened the scoring in the 74th minute and added his second goal in the 84th minute. The match also featured fellow Algerian internationals Adam Ounas and Ismael Bennacer, who both delivered strong performances.

The victory came in the eighth round of the Qatari league. Brahimi's brace secured the win for Al-Gharafa against Qatar SC.

Algerian-born entrepreneur aims to replace keyboard with AI

Neil Zeghidour, backed by Nvidia, is developing voice AI to make human-machine interactions more natural.

Algerian-born entrepreneur Neil Zeghidour wants to replace the keyboard with voice-activated artificial intelligence. Zeghidour, featured in L’Obs' "50 who will shape tomorrow" list, discussed his ambition on a YouTube podcast on October 25. His company, Gradium, aims to allow users to interact with machines simply by speaking.

Gradium's goal is to reduce reliance on keyboards by enabling natural voice commands for tasks like organizing travel or preparing documents. Developers can already use natural language to instruct AI agents for programming tasks. Zeghidour believes future portable devices like earbuds could become primary access points for these AI services, potentially reducing screen time.

Before Gradium, Zeghidour co-founded Kyutai, a non-profit AI research lab funded with 300 million euros, which developed the voice AI model Moshi. Commercial interest from companies in the gaming and smartphone sectors led to Gradium's creation to meet this demand. Gradium aims for more responsive and customizable voice AI than existing options like ChatGPT.

Why it mattersAlgerian-born entrepreneur Neil Zeghidour is developing voice AI technology to change how people interact with computers and devices.

Business · 2026-10-31

Sonatrach raises LPG prices for November

Algeria's national oil company increased propane by $155 to $825 per ton and butane by $300 to $1050 per ton.

Algeria's national oil company Sonatrach raised its official selling prices for Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) for November. The company increased the price of propane by $155 per ton, reaching $825. Butane prices rose by $300 per ton, to $1050.

These increases occurred amid seasonal demand and limited global supplies. Sonatrach's prices serve as a benchmark for markets on the Mediterranean and Black Seas, including Turkey. Propane and butane are key components of LPG, used as fuel for vehicles and heating, and as a petrochemical feedstock.

Saudi Aramco also raised its LPG prices for November, increasing propane by $40 to $720 per ton and butane by $50 to $780 per ton. Saudi Aramco's official selling prices are a reference for LPG supply contracts from the Middle East to the Asia-Pacific region.

Why it mattersAlgerian consumers and industries using LPG for fuel or petrochemicals will face higher costs.
