Moroccan parliament backs Sahara strategy

Joint session of both chambers of parliament convened on November 2 to endorse royal cabinet's new approach.

Morocco's parliament held a joint session on November 2 to solidify institutional backing for the royal cabinet's new strategy concerning the Western Sahara. This meeting followed a UN Security Council veto on October 30 and the expiration of MINURSO's mandate. The parliamentary gathering aimed to secure endorsement for the cabinet's directives on the disputed territory.

The parliamentary session revealed differing views on relations with Algeria, with some parties calling for improved bilateral ties while others accused Algiers of regional obstruction. The new Moroccan strategy focuses on three main areas: strengthening parliamentary diplomacy for international support, preparing legislative adjustments, and reducing the Security Council's role in the conflict.

The Moroccan parliament's ambition to diminish the Security Council's influence over the Western Sahara dossier has been met with criticism. Observers note that national parliamentary actions cannot unilaterally alter the territory's international status or override the people's right to self-determination under international law.

Why it mattersAlgeria is presented as responsible for political deadlock in the region by some Moroccan parliamentary factions.

Algeria mobilizes 67 billion dinars for western water supply

Work begins on connecting desalination plants to supply Tlemcen and Sidi Bel Abbès with drinking water.

Algeria's Minister of Water Resources and Hydraulic Security, Lounès Bouzegza, launched work on connecting new desalination plants in Tlemcen on Monday. The project involves laying 105 kilometers of pipes to supply drinking water to Tlemcen and Sidi Bel Abbès. The minister's visit to Tlemcen is the first stop in a tour across four wilayas to launch and inaugurate strategic projects.

The specific project in Tlemcen, at the Aïn Adjroud desalination site, has been allocated 67 billion Algerian dinars. The construction, divided into five lots awarded to national companies, is scheduled for completion within 16 months. This initiative aims to consolidate national hydraulic security and modernize water services.

New desalination plants are also under construction in Mostaganem and Chlef. The government aims to diversify water sources and expand the national hydraulic infrastructure network. These projects align with directives from President Abdelmadjid Tebboune to consolidate water security.

Why it mattersAlgerians in Tlemcen and Sidi Bel Abbès will receive improved drinking water supply through new infrastructure.

Algerian parliament to review fuel price changes

A finance committee proposed that parliament must approve any fuel price adjustments for 2027.

Algeria's National People's Assembly finance committee has proposed 26 amendments to the 2027 finance bill, including a rule requiring parliamentary approval for any changes to fuel prices. The committee also suggested customs and tax breaks for Algerians living abroad who wish to return home permanently, and extended building settlement deadlines to the end of 2027.

Committee rapporteur Bouchecha explained that the proposed amendments, which include linguistic corrections and new articles, aim to increase transparency and protect citizens' purchasing power. Article 218, as amended, would make fuel price reviews conditional on parliamentary consent within the finance law framework.

The committee also proposed a new article offering special facilities for Algerian expatriates residing abroad for at least three consecutive years who wish to return permanently. These individuals would be allowed to import equipment related to their economic or professional activities, exempt from foreign trade and exchange controls, as well as customs duties and fees.

Why it mattersAlgerian citizens will have their parliament involved in decisions affecting fuel prices.

Algerian workers warned over job burnout signs

A study found links between workplace rudeness and employees wanting to leave their jobs.

Algerian workers experiencing persistent fear of going to work, anxiety the night before, or physical symptoms like headaches and fatigue may be suffering from burnout. These signs indicate potential problems with the work environment or job tasks, according to a recent analysis.

A study analyzed 46 research papers and found a positive link between exposure to impolite behavior at work and a higher desire among employees to quit their jobs. The World Health Organization defines burnout as a professional phenomenon resulting from chronic workplace stress that is not managed successfully.

Burnout manifests in three ways: energy depletion, increased psychological distance from one's job, and a sense of negativity or reduced professional efficacy. These symptoms can affect life outside of work hours if they persist for weeks.

Why it mattersAlgerian workers experiencing fear, anxiety, headaches, or fatigue due to persistent workplace stress may suffer from burnout.
