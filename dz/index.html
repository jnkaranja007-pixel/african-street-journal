Algerian president pardons 5,539 prisoners

President Abdelmadjid Tebboune granted presidential pardon to 5,539 inmates on the 72nd anniversary of the Revolution.

President Abdelmadjid Tebboune has granted a presidential pardon to 5,539 inmates on the occasion of the 72nd anniversary of the Revolution of November 1, 1954. The Presidency of the Republic announced the decision on Saturday, October 31. This pardon applies to individuals, whether incarcerated or not, who received a final conviction before the decree was signed, provided their sentence or remaining sentence is 24 months or less.

However, the pardon does not extend to those convicted by military courts or for specific crimes including terrorism, treason, espionage, murder, drug trafficking, escape, parricide, violence against ascendants, indecent assault, and rape. Individuals convicted under law n° 06-01 related to corruption, money laundering, currency counterfeiting, smuggling, and infractions concerning foreign exchange and capital movements are also excluded.

The 72nd anniversary of the Revolution, celebrated on November 1, is a paid public holiday in Algeria. The additional evidence packet mentioned a weather alert for Algeria, with up to 40 mm of rain expected on Saturday and Sunday.

Algeria in talks to supply fertilizer to Bangladesh

Bangladesh, which imports 80% of its fertilizer, is seeking supply agreements with Algeria.

Bangladesh is seeking fertilizer supplies from Algeria as it imports 80% of its total demand. Discussions between the two nations advanced in September and October 2026, presenting a commercial opportunity for Algerian producers. No contract volumes or delivery schedules have been finalized yet.

The Bangladeshi Ministry of Agriculture confirmed the country's reliance on imports for its fertilizer needs. The Bangladeshi State Minister for Foreign Affairs, Humayun Kabir, urged stronger cooperation in fertilizers and energy with the Algerian ambassador in Dhaka on September 20. On October 5, Interior Minister Salahuddin Ahmed stated Dhaka's intention to secure long-term fertilizer and LNG supply agreements.

The Bangladeshi newspaper The New Nation highlighted the potential partnership, calling for balanced supply agreements that consider product quality, prices, transport costs, financing, and delivery times. This move aims to boost economic cooperation between the two countries.

Why it mattersAlgerian producers have an opportunity to supply fertilizer to Bangladesh, which relies on imports.

Russia vetoes US resolution on Western Sahara autonomy plan

Morocco's plan for Western Sahara autonomy faced international setback after Russia's veto of a US resolution.

Russia vetoed a United States resolution aimed at imposing Morocco's autonomy plan for Western Sahara on October 30. This action represents a significant disappointment for Morocco, which had previously normalized relations with Israel in September. Algerian diplomacy has consistently stated that sovereignty over a territory cannot be granted by a third party, regardless of global power.

Morocco had gambled on this plan in 2020, accepting a deal proposed by Donald Trump as part of the Abraham Accords. King Mohammed VI took a considerable risk, given domestic public support for Palestinians, especially after the Gaza war began. Morocco also invested heavily in arms deals and other agreements with Israel, even reportedly expelling Moroccans from their land for Israeli returnees.

The Moroccan government had interpreted Russia's abstention on resolution 2797 in October 2025 as a definitive closure of the Western Sahara issue in its favor. However, the text of the resolution did not eliminate the possibility of self-determination through a referendum, which is why Russia, China, and Algeria, then a non-permanent Security Council member, did not oppose it.

Why it mattersAlgeria, as a non-permanent member of the UN Security Council, was involved in the vote that did not oppose the resolution on Western Sahara.
