Algerian Police Investigate Wildfire Causes, Identify Victims

Scientific and technical police teams are collecting evidence from fire sites to determine causes and identify victims through biological samples.

Scientific and technical police teams from the National Gendarmerie have launched extensive field investigations across affected provinces to determine the actual causes of recent wildfires and establish the circumstances surrounding them. Teams from the National Institute of Criminal Evidence and Criminology are collecting evidence and biological samples for victim identification, coordinating with regional Gendarmerie units. The National Gendarmerie Command has mobilized all available resources to support this mission.

Under the supervision of institute experts, biological samples will be transported by air to Bouchaoui for comparison with family samples. A final report, including physical evidence and scientific analysis, will be submitted to judicial authorities. Crisis cells have been established at Gendarmerie headquarters and regional commands to manage the situation, with personnel assisting Civil Protection, the Army, police, and citizens in evacuations, body recovery, and providing aid.

Road security units are managing traffic and preventing access to fire zones, with some damaged roads closed. Special security formations have been deployed to restore calm and protect property in evacuated areas, with Gendarmerie patrols reassuring residents. The Ministry of National Defence confirmed its units are on alert and supporting firefighting efforts in Jijel and El Tarf provinces to reinforce Civil Protection and the Forestry Directorate.

Why it mattersThe investigations into the causes of the wildfires and the identification of victims directly affect the affected communities and their families.

Algeria Post Offers Free Card Replacements for Fire Victims

Algeria Post will replace lost or damaged cash cards free of charge for 10 days to aid those affected by recent fires.

Algeria Post has announced an exceptional measure to support citizens impacted by fires, offering free replacement of lost or damaged cash cards. This initiative is part of a national solidarity effort to assist individuals whose financial tools were destroyed by flames.

The postal service stated that the card replacements will be provided at no cost for a period of ten days. This aims to quickly restore affected individuals' access to postal and financial services. Eligibility will be determined based on residency in fire-affected areas.

Affected individuals are urged to visit their local post office to verify their eligibility and initiate the card renewal process. Algeria Post emphasized its commitment to supporting citizens, especially during difficult and exceptional circumstances.

Why it mattersCitizens who lost cash cards in fires can regain access to financial services through free replacements.

Ministry Opens Online Transfer Platform for University Students

Algerian university students can now submit transfer requests online through a dedicated platform until September 15.

The Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research has launched its online platform for university student transfers, effective August 30, 2026. This digital initiative aims to streamline administrative processes for students seeking to change institutions, fields of study, or obtain authorized transfers for the upcoming 2026-2027 academic year.

The entire transfer procedure is now exclusively online, part of the ministry's broader digital ecosystem policy. This move is intended to simplify previous administrative burdens, improve the efficiency of university commissions in processing applications, and ensure a transparent and traceable system for each student's request. The submission window remains open for seventeen days, closing on September 15, 2026.

Students are urged to exercise vigilance when submitting their applications, carefully reviewing all instructions on the portal. It is crucial to verify the accuracy of entered data and ensure all supporting documents meet regulatory requirements before final validation. The operation is a key part of preparing for the new academic year, which begins shortly after the application period closes.

Why it mattersUniversity students in Algeria are affected by this new online system, which simplifies the process of changing their academic institution or field of study.

Civil Protection Extinguishes 33 Fires in 24 Hours

Thirty-three fires impacting vegetation, crops, and date palm groves were extinguished in the last 24 hours.

Civil Protection services recorded thirty-five fires affecting vegetation, agricultural crops, date palm oases, and straw belts over the last twenty-four hours, ending at seven this morning. Of the thirty-five fires, thirty-three have been extinguished, while efforts continue on two ongoing blazes.

The two remaining fires are located in the wilayas of Tizi Ouzou and Jijel. In Tizi Ouzou, a fire in the forest of Aknou Tesselent, in the commune of Ain El Hammam, is still being fought. In Jijel, a fire broke out in the Said Laachach forest in the commune of Ziama Mansouria, where Civil Protection teams are still working to control and extinguish it.

The Civil Protection's report on the general fire situation indicates that the majority of incidents have been brought under control. The ongoing operations in Tizi Ouzou and Jijel highlight the persistent challenges faced in containing vegetation fires, particularly in forested areas.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected as fires destroyed vegetation, crops, and date palm groves.

Algerian Gendarmerie Investigates Fire Causes, Identifies Victims

Scientific police are collecting evidence from fire sites to determine the origins of the blazes and identify casualties through DNA analysis.

The Algerian National Gendarmerie has launched extensive field investigations into recent fires, deploying scientific and technical police units to collect evidence from affected areas. Their objective is to uncover the true causes of the widespread blazes and understand the circumstances surrounding them in the affected provinces. These teams, working in coordination with regional gendarmerie units, are utilizing all available material, technical, and human resources to gather crucial evidence.

Forensic experts are also conducting operations to identify victims by collecting biological samples for comparison with family members. These samples will be transported to the National Institute of Forensic Evidence and Criminalistics in Algiers for analysis. The final report, supported by physical evidence and scientific expertise, will be submitted to judicial authorities to aid in the investigation and prosecution.

In response to the fires, the Ministry of Defense confirmed that all military units are on high alert to assist affected citizens and contribute to firefighting efforts. Army units are actively engaged in supporting civil protection and forestry services in the provinces of Jijel and El Tarf, deploying personnel, firefighting aircraft, and heavy machinery to combat the spread of the fires.

Why it mattersScientific police are investigating the causes of fires and identifying victims, which affects judicial processes and public safety.
