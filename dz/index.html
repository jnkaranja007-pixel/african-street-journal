Storms in Algeria injure two people and bring down a wall and four trees

Civil protection services recorded the damage in several wilayas on Thursday morning as strong winds swept through the country.

Civil protection services reported material damage in several Algerian wilayas on Thursday morning after volatile weather and strong winds. In Mascara, a wall collapsed on a house in the municipality of Mascara, damaging the roof of the building and leaving two people in shock.

In Blida province, strong winds brought down two trees in the municipality of Blida and another in Boufarik. In the wilaya of Medea, one tree fell in the municipality of Medea because of the strong winds.

The incidents come as several regions of the country face unstable weather, and warnings about strong winds remain in place.

Algerian father jailed for taking son abroad

A father was sentenced to 10 months for leaving his three-year-old son in Algeria, blocking the mother's visitation rights.

A 34-year-old Algerian father received a 10-month prison sentence for taking his son to Algeria without his ex-wife's consent. The Lons-le-Saunier court in France convicted him on November 9 for failing to present the child, thereby preventing the mother from exercising her legal visitation rights.

The couple divorced in 2025, with the court granting custody to the father and visitation rights to the mother. In July 2026, the father took their three-year-old son to a village in Kabylie and left him with his ill grandmother, enrolling the child in a local school. He returned to France alone in mid-September.

The mother reported being unable to see her son, prompting legal action. During the hearing, the father claimed he would rectify his mistake, but his explanations regarding the child's situation and return plans appeared contradictory to the court. This is a first-instance ruling, and the father can appeal the decision.

Why it mattersA father's actions have resulted in a prison sentence and separated a child from his mother.

Algeria targets 31% greenhouse gas cut

Environment Minister Kaouther Krikou spoke at COP31 in Turkey on circular economy and waste reduction.

Algeria's Environment Minister Kaouther Krikou attended the opening of the UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Turkey, on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. She spoke at a round table on circular economy, waste valorization, and methane emission reduction.

The COP31 conference is being held in Turkey, with Algeria represented by a delegation led by Prime Minister Sifi Ghrieb. Minister Krikou, who is accompanying the Prime Minister, highlighted Algeria's national efforts in climate change adaptation and international commitments.

Krikou thanked Turkey for hosting the event and emphasized preventing waste generation, reducing its volume, and transforming it into resources. She reaffirmed Algeria's commitment to sustainable development and climate action, aligning with the Paris Agreement and Agenda 2030, including turning organic waste into compost for agricultural soils and recovering energy from biogas.

Why it mattersAlgeria aims to reduce its greenhouse gas emissions by 31% by transforming waste into resources for agriculture and energy.

Algerian entrepreneur wins French residency case

A French court overturned a deportation order for a 68-year-old Algerian business owner.

An Algerian woman, aged 68, has successfully challenged a deportation order in the administrative court of Melun, France. The prefect of Val-de-Marne had issued an order to leave France within 30 days on March 9, 2026, after refusing to renew her residency permit. The prefecture cited insufficient business revenue, stating her turnover was below the full-time minimum wage.

Her lawyer, Me Fayçal Megherbi, argued that the prefecture wrongly applied income criteria, violating the 1968 Franco-Algerian agreement. This agreement governs residency and professional practice for Algerian nationals. The Melun court agreed, censuring the prefecture's decision and its "error of assessment" regarding her business.

The court cancelled the deportation order and the refusal to renew her permit, ordering the prefecture to re-examine her application within three months. In the interim, she will receive a temporary residency permit with work rights. The French state was also ordered to pay her 1,200 euros in legal costs.

Why it mattersAn Algerian entrepreneur's right to reside and work in France was upheld by a court, preventing her deportation.

Business · 2026-11-12

Stream signs 500,000 TV export deal

Algeria's Bomare Company aims for over 85% of its new factory's output to go abroad.

Algerian television maker Stream, owned by Bomare Company, announced a contract for 500,000 units for the first quarter of 2027. The company organized an event on November 9 in Algiers to discuss its export strategy. Stream has been present internationally since 2007 and aims to boost its competitiveness in foreign markets.

Bomare Company founder and CEO Ali Boumediene stated that the company imports $55 worth of components for a product and exports it for $100. The company employs over 1,000 people and has a local integration rate of 45%. Boumediene confirmed that company profits are reinvested for development, and the capital has not been opened.

A new factory in Blida is designed to produce over 3 million televisions annually, with more than 85% intended for export. The announced turnover for the deal is between $12 and $15 million. Boumediene sees opportunities for Algerian appliance makers as Asian factories shift focus.

Why it mattersAlgerian electronics manufacturer Bomare Company is expanding its export business, aiming to sell half a million televisions abroad in early 2027.
