Bosnia seeks Algerian LNG and LPG supplies in talks with Arkab

Energoinvest's director general Mirza Ustamuvić met Algeria's Hydrocarbons Minister Mohamed Arkab on Monday, 16 November, to discuss gas deliveries to the Bosnian market.

Bosnia and Herzegovina has asked Algeria to supply its market with liquefied natural gas (GNL) and liquefied petroleum gas (GPL). The request was raised on Monday, 16 November, when Hydrocarbons Minister Mohamed Arkab received Mirza Ustamuvić, director general of the Bosnian energy company Energoinvest, who is in Algeria to take part in the Electricity Expo 2026.

According to a statement from the Ministry of Hydrocarbons, the talks covered ways to develop trade in hydrocarbons and prospects for partnership between the Sonatrach group and Energoinvest in oil and gas industry, commercialisation and related processing. They also discussed industrial and technical cooperation, including equipment manufacturing and technology transfer.

The approach follows a request from the Czech Republic in late October, during a visit by its Prime Minister to Algeria, to raise Algerian deliveries from 200 to 400 million cubic metres a year. Bosnia's contacts with Algiers have also intensified, including a videoconference between Arkab and Bosnia's federal energy minister Vedran Lakić in July.

French view on Algeria's independence largely calm

Most French people see Algeria's independence as positive for both nations and support continued cooperation.

A poll of 1,000 French adults aged 18 and over, conducted from October eight to nine, found that most French people hold a generally calm view of Algeria's independence. 76 percent of respondents believed Algeria's independence was a good thing for Algeria, and 75 percent felt it was a good thing for France. Support for cooperation with Algeria was high, with 67 percent favouring economic and energy collaboration and 65 percent supporting cultural and educational exchanges.

The survey, commissioned by the association Le Cercle algérianiste, also revealed generational differences. While 43 percent of 18 to 24-year-olds felt Algeria had upheld its commitments to France, only 12 percent of those aged 65 and over agreed. Regarding current relations, 85 percent of French people want France to continue asking Algeria to accept the return of its nationals under an order to leave French territory (OQTF).

Two-thirds of French people also support official recognition by France of past violence during the Algerian War. 74 percent favour wider access to French archives concerning the colonial period and the war, and 65 percent support official recognition of violence committed during that time. Among 18 to 24-year-olds, 78 percent believe France has a moral debt to Algeria, and 70 percent believe it has a material debt.

Algeria, Niger plan SME support for young trainees

A joint government initiative aims to link Algerian businesses with Nigerien graduates returning home.

Algeria is preparing to support small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) by connecting them with 500 young Nigeriens trained in the country. This program, officially launched on October 3 in Boumerdès, aims to create economic opportunities for these graduates upon their return to Niger. The initiative seeks to move beyond general ambition to a concrete approach for fostering business partnerships between the two nations.

The plan involves Algerian SMEs participating in the professional projects of returning Nigerien trainees who wish to establish businesses. This collaboration will be supported by organized assistance from both Algerian and Nigerien governments. The goal is to provide a framework for Algerian companies to forge partnerships and for graduates to develop their entrepreneurial activities with a reliable support system.

This initiative could offer additional value to the training by providing a more solid structure for companies to build partnerships. The proposal suggests that serious projects identified among the trainees should be studied and accompanied. This approach aims to prepare concrete business activities rather than just facilitating a return, addressing specific needs in Niger for equipment, services, and clientele.

Why it mattersAlgerian businesses will be linked with Nigerien trainees to develop professional projects, with government support, starting after the trainees return.

Algerian MP asks Air Algérie to open bookings a year ahead

Fares Rahmani says Air Algérie currently opens reservations only three or four months in advance, and ticket prices from France remain high.

Fares Rahmani, a deputy for Algerians living in Northern Europe, will put an oral question to the Transport Minister next Thursday, asking Air Algérie to open reservations a year in advance. He announced the plan on his Facebook page, and said the national airline currently opens bookings only three or four months ahead.

Rahmani said that for Algerians abroad, transport matters most, with lower fares and direct routes. Air Algérie has recently opened bookings for summer 2027, but fares from France remain relatively expensive. Rahmani, who says he proposed the amendment that cut the passport price for Algerians abroad, complained that several of his other amendments were rejected.

One rejected proposal would have let Algerian couples bring two cars home. The bureau of the National People's Assembly (APN) rejected it, according to Rahmani, who is a member of the Front El Moustakbal party.

Why it mattersAlgerians living abroad would be able to book Air Algérie flights a year ahead, if the Transport Minister takes up the question.
