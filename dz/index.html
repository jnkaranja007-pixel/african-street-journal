Algerian descendants seek stronger ties to homeland

Second and third generation Algerians are increasingly exploring their heritage, with some considering relocation.

Descendants of Algerian immigrants in France are showing a growing connection to Algeria, appearing on social media, in sports, and in entrepreneurship. For some, this connection is leading them to consider settling in Algeria, driven by family heritage, the search for opportunities, and feelings of stigmatization in France.

Testimonies from the diaspora highlight a desire to reconnect with ancestral roots, with some sharing their experiences of moving to Algeria and the challenges they face. While the full scale of this trend is hard to measure, it prompts questions about Algeria's current role in the plans of these descendants.

This phenomenon is linked to the history of earlier generations who moved to France for temporary work with the intention of returning home. Many of these workers, who came after World War II to fill labor needs, ended up building their lives and raising families in France, a situation often referred to as the 'myth of return'.

Why it mattersDescendants of Algerian immigrants are exploring their heritage and considering moving to Algeria.

Algerian Air adjusts two flights to Timimoun

Flights AH6156 and AH6157 will depart later due to a temporary airport closure.

Algerian Air announced changes to two flights between Algiers and Timimoun, scheduled for October 29 and November 2. The airline stated the adjustments are due to the temporary closure of Timimoun airport during the original flight times.

Flight AH6156 from Algiers to Timimoun will now depart at 20:00, arriving at 21:30. The return flight, AH6157 from Timimoun to Algiers, will depart at 22:30 and arrive in Algiers at midnight. The company advised customers to check their flight times via commercial agencies or the contact center.

The airline did not specify when Timimoun airport will reopen.

Why it mattersAlgerian Air customers travelling between Algiers and Timimoun on October 29 and November 2 will experience altered flight schedules.

Algeria sets October 31 deadline for 2027 import plans

Importers of live cattle, milk, dairy products, and poultry inputs must submit their 2027 provisional import programs by this date.

Algeria's Ministry of Agriculture has set October 31, 2026, as the deadline for importers of live cattle, milk, dairy products, and poultry inputs to submit their provisional import programs (PPI) for 2027. This submission is a mandatory step to obtain import licenses and initiate banking procedures for their operations. The directive was issued on October 7.

The veterinary services directorate emphasized that economic operators must accurately report the nature and projected quantities of products. Strict adherence to the deadline is required to ensure timely processing of these programs. The ministry aims to prevent market supply disruptions for essential products like meat and dairy.

Algeria introduced the PPI system in July 2025 to regulate and enhance import controls. All import operations now require prior validation of the PPI by the Ministry of Foreign Trade and Export Promotion.

Why it mattersAlgerian importers of specific agricultural products must submit their 2027 plans by October 31, 2026, to secure import licenses.

Tech · 2026-10-28

student launches Algeria's first traffic congestion index

Takieddine Rehail's platform analyzes traffic data from Google Maps for 27 Algerian cities.

Takieddine Rehail, a third-year student at ESTIN in Bejaïa, has developed the Algerian Urban Congestion Index. This platform uses Google Maps traffic data to monitor and analyze road congestion in 27 Algerian cities, including Algiers and Oran, without needing physical sensors.

The project, developed under Professor Faïcal Azouaou, aims to provide real-time information on traffic levels and allow comparisons between cities. Nesrine Mitouche, ESTIN's communications officer, stated the platform helps observe congestion changes throughout the day.

This tool can assist local authorities, urban planners, and transport engineers in addressing mobility issues. The platform is accessible at dz-traffic.estin.dz.

Why it mattersAlgerian cities experience dense traffic, and this tool provides data to help manage mobility issues.
