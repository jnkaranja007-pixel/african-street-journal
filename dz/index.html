Algeria Sends Military Aircraft to Niger, Drawing French Concern

French media express unease over Algeria's military support for Niger's authorities following a coup attempt, marking a shift in regional policy.

French media outlets, including Le Monde and Marianne, are closely following Algeria's decision to dispatch military aircraft to Niger to aid its authorities in confronting a recent coup attempt. This move, occurring nearly five days after the incident, signals a significant departure from Algeria's historical non-interference policy and its entry into a region Paris considers its traditional sphere of influence.

Le Monde described the development as a "historic and unprecedented turning point" in Algerian foreign policy, suggesting a move toward becoming a conventional military power rather than engaging in covert mediation. The publication noted that Algeria is asserting its right to openly assist a friendly state under threat, a stark contrast to its past restraint. This shift has also been framed as a potential challenge to remaining French influence in the Sahel.

The magazine Marianne characterized Algeria's action as crossing a long-held red line, despite Algeria's 2020 constitution allowing for such operations when national security is threatened. Algeria's decision was made within international law after an invitation from Niger's ruling authorities, with whom it shares a 1,200-kilometer border. The Algerian delegation was received by key Nigerien officials, including the Prime Minister and Defense Minister.

Why it mattersFrench media outlets are concerned that Algeria's military support for Niger's authorities represents a challenge to French influence in the Sahel region.

Algeria Achieves Best Ever Overseas Mediterranean Games Medal Haul

The nation secured 40 medals, including 13 golds, at the Taranto 2026 Games, marking a historic achievement outside of home soil.

Algeria concluded its participation in the 20th Mediterranean Games in Taranto with a record 40 medals, placing ninth overall. The haul of 13 gold, 8 silver, and 19 bronze medals represents the country's most successful performance in an edition of the Games held abroad. Algerian athletes particularly excelled in individual disciplines such as athletics, gymnastics, weightlifting, judo, karate, wrestling, and table tennis.

This achievement builds on the momentum from the Oran 2022 Games, where Algeria recorded its best-ever total medal count of 53. While the Taranto results are fewer in number, they signify a significant milestone for Algerian sports on foreign territory. Athletics was a standout, contributing eight medals, including three golds from Djamel Sedjati (800m), Said Ammouri (10000m), and Yasser Mohamed Triki (triple jump).

Gymnastics also saw considerable success, with four medals, three of which were gold, all attributed to Olympic champion Kaylia Nemour. She dominated the individual all-around and uneven bars, and contributed to a team bronze. Wrestling added seven medals, including one gold by Abdelkrim Ferhat, while weightlifting saw brothers Fares and Aymen Touairi secure three medals, including two golds, with Fares winning snatch gold and clean & jerk bronze, and Aymen taking clean & jerk gold.

Why it mattersAlgerian athletes have achieved their best-ever medal count in an overseas competition, showcasing national sporting prowess.

Algerian-born footballer Yonis Sanhaji switches allegiance to Algeria

The 21-year-old forward, who previously represented Spain's U19 team, has officially changed his sporting nationality to play for Algeria.

Yonis Sanhaji, a 21-year-old forward born in Spain to Algerian parents, has officially switched his sporting nationality to represent Algeria. The International Football Federation (FIFA) has listed Sanhaji at the top of players who have recently changed their sporting nationality, clearing him to wear the "Desert Warriors" jersey.

Sanhaji previously played for Spain's U19 team, with whom he won the European Championship in 2024, defeating France in the final. He has not yet played for Spain's senior team. His club career in Spain includes stints with Betis (reserve team), Tenerife, and Hercules, and he joined Real Jaen in the third division last August for one season.

This change allows Sanhaji, who holds FIFA's clearance, to now be eligible to play for the Algerian national team. His decision marks a significant potential addition to the Algerian squad, bringing European championship experience from his youth career.

Why it mattersAlgerian football fans will be interested in the potential impact of a European champion joining their national team.

Algeria Sends Warplanes to Niger, France Expresses Concern

French media reports highlight Algerian military support for Niger's government, signaling a shift in regional policy and drawing French attention.

French media outlets are closely monitoring Algeria's decision to send warplanes to support Niger's authorities in the wake of a failed coup attempt. This move, occurring days after the incident, suggests French unease and a departure from Algeria's traditional non-interventionist stance. Le Monde dedicated two articles to the development, calling it a "historic and unprecedented turning point in Algerian foreign policy" that could shape future Algerian actions in its unstable southern neighborhood.

Le Monde's reporting noted Algeria's shift from "secret mediation in gray areas" to openly offering aid to a threatened friendly state. This contrasts with Algeria's historical commitment to strict non-interference, a principle used to distinguish itself from colonial powers. The publication described the action as a public military display outside its borders, potentially diminishing French influence in a region Paris considers its backyard, especially as some parties accuse France of backing the coup.

Far-right French magazine Marianne provocatively headlined its coverage "Algeria crosses a red line it drew for over fifty years," framing the event as interference in a former French sphere of influence. However, Algeria's action was taken following an invitation from Niger's government, with which it shares a border of one thousand two hundred kilometers. This was underscored by the reception of the Algerian delegation by Niger's Prime Minister and Minister of Defense.

Opium Trade Fueled British Expansion, Sparked War

Britain used opium smuggling to balance trade deficits with China, leading to conflict and the cession of Hong Kong.

The European colonial powers, after the Industrial Revolution, employed opium as a tool to expand markets and assert dominance. This highly prevalent narcotic became a symbol for ways to pacify populations and distract them from daily affairs and public issues. Karl Marx himself used the phrase "religion is the opium of the people" to describe how the church pacified European populations for centuries.

The drug trade, particularly opium, was a devastating weapon before the advent of nuclear arms. The First Opium War, fought between China and Britain from 1839 to 1842, was primarily a trade dispute. Britain imported tea, silk, and porcelain from China, draining its silver reserves, which was the accepted currency of trade at the time.

To rectify this trade imbalance, Britain sought a commodity China desired, finding it in opium. Through smuggling and the monopoly held by the British East India Company, opium was cultivated and marketed in India, draining China's silver and causing inflation. China's attempts to destroy opium stocks and halt smuggling led to Britain's military action, resulting in China's defeat and the handover of Hong Kong.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the historical precedent of trade wars and colonial economic strategies driven by the opium trade.
