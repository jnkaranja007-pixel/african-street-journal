Algeria begins second phase of nuclear site cleanup

The army is removing radioactive waste from a former French nuclear test site in Tamanrasset.

Algeria has started the second phase of decontaminating former French nuclear test sites in the south. The operation, launched on Sunday, focuses on removing radioactive waste from the Beryl E2 underground test tunnel in In Ekker, Tamanrasset. This effort uses national expertise and resources under the supervision of the Ministry of National Defence.

The Ministry of National Defence announced the operation aims to address radioactive waste at the former French nuclear testing site. The work is coordinated with the National Agency for the Rehabilitation of Former French Nuclear Tests and Explosion Sites in Southern Algeria. France has not provided records or maps of nuclear waste burial sites.

This decontamination effort coincides with celebrations for the 72nd anniversary of Algeria’s Revolution. The High Command of the People’s National Army stated Algeria is continuing cleanup efforts despite France's refusal to acknowledge criminal responsibility.

Why it mattersAlgerians are affected by the ongoing cleanup of radioactive waste from former French nuclear test sites within their country.

Algerian diplomat Rahabi explains Russian veto on Western Sahara

Former minister Abdelaziz Rahabi criticizes US attempts to reduce MINURSO's role and shift focus from Sahrawi people.

Former Algerian minister Abdelaziz Rahabi stated that Russia's veto on a Western Sahara resolution on October 30th reflects its impatience with American unilateralism. Rahabi said those who supported Morocco must take responsibility for the new situation of the MINURSO mission, which he insists Morocco does not control.

Rahabi explained that the US resolution aimed to make Moroccan autonomy the sole negotiation basis and to reclassify Sahrawis as refugees. He noted this is the first time Russia has voted on the Sahrawi issue after eight years of abstention, viewing it as a response to US attempts to bypass established responsibilities.

The diplomat suggested that, following UN practice, the General Assembly should now debate the issue since Russia exercised its veto power. Rahabi believes the US strategy in Western Sahara mirrors its approach in Gaza, disregarding historical context and the interests of neighboring states like Algeria and Mauritania.

Why it mattersAlgeria, as a neighboring observer state, is directly affected by the strategic implications of the UN Security Council's actions regarding the Western Sahara conflict.
