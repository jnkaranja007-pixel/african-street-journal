Tebboune orders 10 radical measures for Algerian agriculture

President Tebboune banned fallow land and maize for fodder, aiming to boost production and market supply.

Algerian President Abdelmadjid Tebboune announced 10 radical measures to reform the agricultural sector on Sunday, November 8. The president instructed the government on a comprehensive overhaul of the sector's operations during a cabinet meeting. These directives followed a presentation by the Minister of Agriculture, Sid Ali Khaldi, amid a potato crisis that exposed sector weaknesses.

Key reforms include creating a new local agriculture organization plan for transparency and modern statistics, and establishing an immediate service for agricultural statistics. President Tebboune also ordered the creation of an office for dry legumes and grain storage under the OAIC. The Ministry of Agriculture will redistribute missions for fisheries and forests between a minister delegate and a secretary of state.

The president banned the practice of leaving land fallow, deeming it outdated, and prohibited the use of maize as green fodder for livestock. He stated this practice negatively impacted meat production and market supply, causing a grain maize shortage. Agricultural machinery will be made available to all production zones starting in November.

Why it mattersAlgerian farmers will no longer be allowed to leave land fallow and must stop using maize as fodder for livestock.

Algeria arrests chip firm leaders over subsidized oil speculation

Four individuals, including a chip company manager, face charges for illegally diverting state-subsidized cooking oil.

Algerian authorities arrested four people on Sunday, November 8, for allegedly diverting state-subsidized cooking oil for illegal sale to a chip manufacturer. The suspects face charges including illicit speculation and money laundering. A fifth individual identified by investigators remains at large.

The investigation, initiated by the Gendarmerie nationale in Chaïba, Tipaza, uncovered a scheme where cooking oil, intended for household consumption, was diverted for private industrial use. Among those arrested are the director and the supply manager of the chip company, alongside three other individuals. No invoices were issued for these transactions.

This case follows a series of investigations into foodstuff speculation in Algeria. In October, the public prosecutor's office in Algiers launched inquiries into allegations of food items being deliberately destroyed to create artificial shortages.

Why it mattersAlgerian citizens are affected by the illegal diversion of subsidized cooking oil, a product meant for household consumption, which can lead to shortages and price increases.

Pharmacist murdered in Constantine

The national union of pharmacists is demanding urgent security measures after a member was killed.

Pharmacist Mounir Nasri was murdered in the Daksi neighborhood of Constantine on Sunday, according to the National Union of Pharmacists of Algeria (Snapo). The circumstances of the killing were not detailed, but Snapo suggested it may be linked to the illegal trade of psychotropic drugs.

Snapo expressed deep sadness and strong condemnation of the crime, highlighting the fear and concern among pharmacy professionals. The union stated that protecting pharmacists and securing pharmacies has become an urgent necessity.

The union called on authorities to strengthen security measures for pharmacies and their staff. Snapo also reiterated its demand for the swift implementation of a digital platform to manage prescriptions for psychotropic medications.

Why it mattersA pharmacist was murdered in Constantine, causing distress and fear among pharmacy professionals in Algeria.

Business · 2026-11-09

Algeria launches initiative for 1,000 rural women entrepreneurs

The '1,000 Women: 1,000 Projects' initiative aims to transform traditional skills into sustainable businesses.

Algeria is launching the '1,000 Women: 1,000 Projects' initiative to support female entrepreneurship, particularly in rural and isolated areas. The National Agency for Microcredit Management (ANGEM) plans to assist at least 1,000 women, leveraging their inherited expertise in processing agricultural products, traditional foods, and handicrafts into profitable ventures.

The program focuses on enhancing the value of local resources by supporting women in transforming fruits and vegetables, preserving and drying produce, and developing artisanal activities. This initiative seeks to move women's production from limited home-based work to organized economic activities, providing training in cost management, pricing, and customer relations.

Siham Ben Jemil, Director General of ANGEM, stated the goal is to accompany and finance a thousand women nationwide, offering financial support, technical training, networking, and marketing mechanisms. The strategy aims to better utilize rural raw materials, reduce post-harvest losses, and create jobs in collection, processing, packaging, and distribution.

Why it mattersRural Algerian women with traditional skills in food processing and handicrafts will gain support to develop their businesses.
