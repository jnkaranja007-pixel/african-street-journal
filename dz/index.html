Algerian MPs limited to two questions monthly

The National People's Assembly has set a limit of two oral questions per deputy per month to streamline parliamentary oversight.

The National People's Assembly in Algeria has established a new rule limiting deputies to two oral questions per month directed at government ministers. This measure aims to regulate parliamentary oversight and manage the scheduling of ministerial responses, preventing the backlog of questions seen in previous legislative terms. Written questions, however, remain unlimited. The assembly's office is set to meet to review and schedule these oral questions for upcoming sessions.

This initiative is intended to organize the sessions dedicated to ministerial responses and ensure their timely programming, avoiding the accumulation of thousands of questions that occurred in past legislative periods. The new internal regulations also stipulate that the government must respond to both oral and written questions within a maximum of 30 days from the date of notification. If a minister claims lack of jurisdiction, the question will be redirected to the appropriate official.

Deputies are being advised to focus on the quality and relevance of their questions, ensuring they address citizens' concerns and national issues. The aim is to broaden parliamentary oversight to encompass various government sectors and policies, not solely local matters. Oral question sessions are typically held every two weeks, alternating between the National People's Assembly and the Council of the Nation, making the management of question numbers and scheduling crucial for effective oversight.

Why it mattersAlgerian citizens are affected by this as it may streamline government accountability on issues relevant to their daily lives.

Algeria coach considers positional changes amid defensive shortages

Coach Ibrahim Hamdani is exploring shifting players to new roles to address a lack of central defenders before upcoming matches.

Algeria's national football team faces a critical shortage of central defenders following the retirement of Issa Mandi. Coach Ibrahim Hamdani is reportedly considering unconventional positional changes for players like Imad Abdelli or Touba to partner Ramy Bensebaini in defense. This move aims to find a stable and effective partnership, as previous pairings like Samir Chergui and Zouheir Belcide have shown limitations, contributing to goals conceded against weaker opponents.

In attack and midfield, Hamdani is under pressure to create a cohesive unit from available talent and establish consistent roles for players. Failure to do so could result in key players like Rayan Cherki and Ilan Kebbal being overlooked for selection due to established pairings. The coach has previously experimented successfully with players like Touba and Billel in wider defensive roles, indicating a willingness to adapt tactics.

The strategy of positional flexibility is not unique, with international coaches like Flick at Barcelona successfully converting wingers to strikers and even experimenting with midfielders in defense due to injury concerns. This approach views players as versatile assets capable of filling multiple roles without disrupting tactical plans, a philosophy Hamdani may adopt to overcome Algeria's current squad challenges.

Why it mattersAlgerian football players may be asked to play in unfamiliar positions, potentially affecting their performance and selection.

Algerian social security agency adopts electronic signatures

The CNAS is preparing to integrate digital certification into its administrative processes to modernize operations and reduce paperwork.

Algeria's National Social Insurance Fund for Salaried Workers (CNAS) is moving towards digital document processing with the planned integration of electronic signatures. A meeting between the interim Director General of CNAS and the Director General of the Government Authority for Electronic Certification (AGCE) focused on implementing this digital certification into the document handling workflow.

This initiative is part of the CNAS's broader strategy to modernize its management systems and accelerate its digital transition. The move aims to streamline the processing of a large volume of documents handled by payment centers, progressively replacing paper with digital formats. This transition is expected to significantly lower administrative and technical burdens.

The collaboration between CNAS and AGCE marks a significant step in coordinating technical and organizational aspects for the project's launch. By adopting electronic signatures, the agency seeks to improve efficiency and reduce the operational costs associated with managing extensive paper-based records.

Why it mattersAlgerian citizens will experience faster processing of administrative documents related to social security.
