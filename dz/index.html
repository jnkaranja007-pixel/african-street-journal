Germany Prioritizes Algeria Ties, Sees Energy Investment Potential

German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul met President Tebboune, highlighting trade, investment, and Algeria's role in green hydrogen.

Germany's Foreign Minister, Johann Wadephul, stated that strengthening relations with Algeria is a top priority for Berlin following his meeting with President Abdelmadjid Tebboune. Wadephul expressed solidarity with Algeria during its wildfire crisis and extended condolences to victims' families. He noted the positive momentum in bilateral relations since Tebboune's visit to Germany in July.

Discussions between Wadephul and Algerian officials, including Foreign Minister Ahmed Attaf and the Minister of Hydrocarbons, focused on deepening trade and investment, particularly in the energy sector. Wadephul met with representatives of German companies operating in Algeria, emphasizing the considerable potential for expanded commercial ties and investment. Germany views Algeria as an important export market.

Algeria's position as a major gas supplier to the European Union and its potential to become a green hydrogen supplier were key discussion points. The ministers also addressed regional and international issues, including the Sahel, the Middle East, and the global impact of the Russian-Ukrainian war on food security. Wadephul stressed the importance of dialogue and trust-based cooperation in light of current geopolitical developments.

Why it mattersGerman investors are interested in expanding commercial ties and investing in Algeria, particularly in the energy sector.

Algerians Rally in Aid After Devastating Fires

Citizens across the country mobilized to collect and send aid to regions affected by widespread wildfires, demonstrating solidarity.

Following extensive wildfires that ravaged large areas of Jijel and other provinces, causing significant human and material losses, Algerians have responded with remarkable unity. From 69 provinces, people came together, sending images of solidarity through public and private Algerian television channels and social media. Men, women, youth, and elders participated in collecting and transporting aid, including mineral water, food, clothing, bedding, and medicine, to affected areas.

The outpouring of support significantly eased the burden on authorities, who are now focused on restoring essential services like electricity, gas, and water. They are also assessing damages to compensate those affected and provide temporary shelter for families whose homes were destroyed, with plans for rehousing them away from forest areas. Notably, young people, often stereotyped as apathetic, were at the forefront of these relief efforts, demonstrating enthusiasm and dedication.

The fires, which destroyed homes, farms, and property in areas including Jijel, Bejaia, and Tizi Ouzou, have prompted an investigation into their widespread nature and simultaneous ignition across multiple provinces. While authorities work to determine responsibility, the event has highlighted the resilience and generosity of the Algerian people, who have shown that even in the face of disaster, their spirit of compassion and mutual support shines brightly.

Why it mattersAlgerians affected by wildfires are receiving essential supplies and support from a nationwide solidarity effort, while authorities investigate the cause of the fires.

Algerian Football Federation Trial Delayed Again

Three former federation presidents and officials will now face corruption charges on September 29.

The economic and financial penal pole at the Sidi M’hamed tribunal has rescheduled the trial of the Algerian Football Federation corruption case to September 29. Kheireddine Zetchi, Charaf-Eddine Amara, and Djahid Zefizef, along with several former federal office officials, are expected to appear. Some of the accused remain in custody.

According to Echourouk media, the defense requested a postponement to review all case documents. The court granted this request, and a new date was set without addressing the substance of the allegations. This marks another delay in a case that has seen previous postponements for similar reasons.

The proceedings had begun with the usual protocol, including calling all parties and bringing the accused, including the three former federation presidents, into the courtroom. This is not the first time the trial has been postponed; a similar delay occurred in July for nearly identical reasons.

Why it mattersFormer leaders of the Algerian Football Federation face corruption charges, impacting the governance and financial dealings of the sport's governing body.

Algeria Health Ministry Bans 9 Diseases for 2027 Hajj Pilgrimage

New medical criteria, including severe kidney, heart, and respiratory conditions, will prevent pilgrims from participating in the Hajj.

Algeria's Ministry of Health has issued a directive barring individuals with nine specific medical conditions from undertaking the Hajj pilgrimage in 2027. The list, which includes acute renal failure, heart failure, acute respiratory illnesses, cirrhosis, severe neurological or psychiatric disorders, dementia, and high-risk or late-term pregnancies, aims to align with stricter health requirements from Saudi authorities.

The ministry has significantly tightened medical eligibility criteria for the upcoming Hajj season, responding to increased scrutiny from Saudi health officials regarding pilgrim fitness. Medical evaluation will now involve tripartite commissions, including a psychiatrist, conducting two to three weekly sessions, escalating to four during the assessment of independent candidates. These commissions will also require hair and nail samples for testing.

This decision follows an evaluation of the previous Hajj campaign, which, despite being generally satisfactory, highlighted persistent areas of vulnerability. The new measures, detailed in instruction number 07 dated July 13, 2026, are intended to ensure the health and safety of all pilgrims by enforcing more rigorous physical and mental health standards for participation.

Why it mattersIndividuals with specific chronic or infectious diseases are prevented from participating in the Hajj pilgrimage.

Amoura rejoins Nice, ending Algerian player rift

Algerian forward Amine Amoura has signed a one-year loan deal with French club Nice, aiming to revive his career and mend player relations.

Amine Amoura, 26, has joined French club Nice on a one-year loan with an option to buy, marking a return to French football after a less successful stint in Germany with Wolfsburg. His move aims to revitalize his career, which saw him perform better with Belgian club St. Gilloise, where he played in European competitions. This experience adds to his diverse career across five leagues, including Algerian, Swiss, Belgian, and German football.

Nice has a history of signing Algerian players, including former stars like Abdelaziz Bentifour and, more recently, Adam Ounas, Youcef Atal, Hicham Boudaoui, and goalkeeper Belkhenda. Amoura's arrival comes after a period of strained relations between Algerian players and the club, stemming from the political controversy surrounding Youcef Atal's support for Palestine. Atal, once a popular figure, was removed from the team.

Amoura, who was the top scorer in World Cup qualifiers despite an injury, hopes to quickly secure playing time at Nice to be considered for upcoming African Cup of Nations qualifiers. His technical skills and recent performances, including scoring twice against Bayern Munich, suggest he could become a fan favorite, especially with Hicham Boudaoui also confirmed to stay with the club.

Why it mattersAlgerian footballer Amine Amoura's transfer to Nice provides him with a platform to regain form and potentially secure a spot in the national team.
