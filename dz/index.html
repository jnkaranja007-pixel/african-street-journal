Hemdani names Algeria squad without Luca Zidane

Coach Brahim Hemdani selected 26 players for the CAN 2027 qualifiers against Togo, excluding Luca Zidane and Rayan Aït Nouri.

Algeria's national football team coach Brahim Hemdani announced a 26-player squad on Thursday, November 5, for two upcoming matches against Togo in the CAN 2027 qualifiers. Notably absent from the list are Luca Zidane and Rayan Aït Nouri, the latter being dropped due to a disciplinary issue during the last training camp. The squad includes two new players, Mohamed Azzi and Abderrahmane Rebbach, and recalls several players who missed the previous camp.

Hemdani explained Luca Zidane's exclusion was based on sporting criteria, specifically prioritizing goalkeepers with maximum playing time. "I made my choices on a sporting criterion, on this position which is really particular," Hemdani stated. "I needed to have a choice between three goalkeepers who had maximum playing time." Rayan Aït Nouri's absence is seen as a consequence of a past outburst, though his limited game time at Manchester City may also be a factor.

The midfield sees the return of Nabil Bentaleb and Hicham Boudaoui from injury, along with Titraoui. Abderrahmane Rebbach from Deportivo Alavés receives his first call-up, while Aouchiche and Zorgane are omitted. The attack remains largely unchanged, with Ghedjemis replacing Kebbal. The team will face Togo on November 14 and November 17.

Algerian physicist Noureddine Melikechi honoured at UMass

University of Massachusetts establishes a faculty innovation fellowship named after the Algerian dean.

The University of Massachusetts has permanently honoured Algerian physicist Noureddine Melikechi by naming a new faculty innovation fellowship after him. The program, called the "Dean Noureddine Melikechi Endowed Kennedy College of Sciences Faculty Innovation Fellowships," will fund professors with novel research and education ideas. The university launched the initiative with an initial investment of $2.5 million.

Melikechi, who is the dean of the Kennedy College of Sciences, expressed surprise and honour at the recognition, stating he did not expect such an accolade during his lifetime. He earned his physics degree from the University of Bab Ezzouar in Algiers before continuing his studies in the UK and later the US. His research spans atomic, molecular, and optical physics, including contributions to spectroscopy and biophotonics.

The fellowship aims to foster innovative research and education within the university's science programs. Melikechi is also a member of NASA's Mars Science Laboratory mission team and serves on Algeria's High Council of Scientists Abroad. He has received numerous awards, including a NASA Group Achievement Award in 2013.

Why it mattersAlgerian academics and researchers are recognized internationally for their contributions to science.

Algérie Poste urges card security measures

The postal service reminds cardholders to protect their PIN and keep cards visible during transactions.

Algérie Poste is reminding customers to adopt security measures when using electronic payment terminals (TPE) to prevent fraud. The service emphasizes that the security of card transactions relies heavily on user behavior, particularly concerning the confidential PIN code.

Customers are urged to never share their PIN with anyone, including merchants or callers, and to shield their hand while entering it on the terminal keypad. Algérie Poste also warns against fake phone calls from individuals claiming to be postal agents who request PINs or SMS-sent OTP codes. The rule is that a shared PIN is a compromised PIN.

Cardholders must also ensure their bank card remains visible throughout the entire transaction, from start to finish, and should never let it be taken behind a counter. Finally, customers are reminded to retrieve their card after payment is validated to avoid it being left behind, which could enable fraudulent operations.

Why it mattersAlgerians using electronic payment terminals are reminded of essential steps to protect their financial information and prevent fraudulent transactions.

Algeria ranks second for foreign students in France

Thousands of Algerian students received study visas for the 2025-2026 academic year.

Algeria is the second-largest country of origin for international students in France, a position that highlights the ongoing appeal of French educational institutions. For the 2025-2026 academic year, the French Embassy in Algeria and Campus France Algeria announced the issuance of thousands of study visas to Algerian nationals.

Data from Campus France, based on French Ministry of Higher Education and Research figures, shows that most Algerian students enroll in public universities. Others pursue studies in specialized institutions like business and engineering schools, art and architecture programs, and preparatory classes.

These new arrivals contribute to the academic exchanges between Algeria and France, reinforcing the strong interest Algerian youth have in French higher education programs, from bachelor's degrees to doctorates.
