Algeria inspects Moroccan toxic water pollution

Ministers visited Tlemcen to assess environmental damage from untreated industrial and wastewater discharges.

Algerian ministers inspected polluted water sites in Tlemcen on Tuesday, November 10, following instructions from President Abdelmadjid Tebboune. The delegation, including ministers of Environment, Hydraulics, Health, and Communication, examined the Mouillah wadi and Hammam Boughrara dam, areas affected by untreated industrial and wastewater from Morocco.

Minister of Interior Brahim Merad stated that analyses revealed alarmingly high chemical and biological indicators, pharmaceutical substances, and heavy metals, with some concentrations reaching 100 times authorized norms. He suggested human intervention was behind the pollution, which has already caused over 45 tonnes of dead fish in September and October 2024 and threatens irrigation for thousands of hectares.

Agricultural representatives reported crops at risk and farmers unable to use wadi water, impacting the region's agricultural activity. Algeria considers the pollution a national security issue and is working to ensure safe water distribution while seeking accountability for the environmental damage.

Why it mattersAlgerian farmers face threats to their crops and livelihoods due to polluted water from Morocco, impacting agricultural activity.

Algeria's weather office warns of gusts over 80 km/h in seven provinces

Violent winds are forecast to begin at 9 a.m. Wednesday and continue until 3 a.m. Thursday across several provinces.

The National Office of Meteorology has announced gusts of wind exceeding 80 km/h that will hit several provinces in Algeria on Wednesday. According to a warning from the office, violent winds will start at 9 a.m. and last until 3 a.m. on Thursday.

The provinces named in the alert include Béchar, Naâma, El Bayadh, Laghouat and Sidi Bel Abbès. Weather conditions are expected to remain unstable across several regions of Algeria in the coming hours.

The warning follows reports of rain, storms and strong winds earlier this week in several regions, according to a separate report on the forecast.

Why it mattersResidents of Béchar, Naâma, El Bayadh, Laghouat and Sidi Bel Abbès face gusts over 80 km/h from Wednesday morning until early Thursday.

Bomare Company targets export markets

The Algerian electronics manufacturer held an event in Algiers to discuss export strategies and industrial excellence.

Bomare Company held its second Afterwork event in Algiers on November 9 to focus on export as a driver for industrial performance. The company argued that export ambition can fuel economic growth, linking industry, compliance, innovation, and customer experience.

The event, attended by journalists and industry figures, explored how local companies can expand beyond national borders. Bomare Company, with export experience since 2007, emphasized that sustainable industry requires skills, logistics, and after-sales service, not just assembly.

The company's new factory can produce three million televisions annually, supporting its STREAM brand and enabling OEM/ODM partnerships. To enter European markets, Bomare Company integrates CE marking and RoHS directive compliance, reflected in its product durability and repairability score of 6.8/10.

Why it mattersAlgerian electronics manufacturers like Bomare Company are working to meet international standards for export.

Niger summons EU envoy over Algeria troop deployment comments

Niamey calls EU foreign affairs spokesperson's remarks 'condescending' and 'unacceptable'.

Niger summoned the European Union's interim charge d'affaires, Plai Voiomma, on Monday to protest remarks made by EU foreign affairs spokesperson Anouar Al Anouni regarding Algeria's military deployment.

Al Anouni stated on October 30 that the EU sought clarification on the "scope, objective, and basis" of the deployment, which Niger's government called "condescending" and "unacceptable" in a statement expressing indignation at the EU's persistent interference in sovereign states' internal affairs.

The Nigerien government demanded clarifications from the EU regarding repeated "disobliging interferences," while a civil society member from Arlit questioned why the EU did not challenge France's past uranium exploitation and environmental damage in Niger.

Why it mattersAlgeria's military actions in Niger have become a point of contention between Niger and the European Union, impacting diplomatic relations.
