France asks EU to probe Algeria trade dispute

Paris claims Algiers is violating its association agreement by blocking French imports, escalating economic tensions.

France has asked the European Commission to investigate Algeria's alleged violation of its association agreement with the EU, following unconfirmed reports of a ban on French imports. The French foreign ministry stated on Friday that the move stems from complaints by French companies about a collapse in their exports to Algeria. This action escalates a growing economic disagreement between the two nations.

The dispute intensified after French businesses reported a significant drop in sales to Algeria. Algeria, in turn, has accused the EU of blocking its steel exports, with President Abdelmadjid Tebboune demanding reciprocity in trade relations on December 18, 2025. He specifically mentioned the issue of low quotas for Algerian steel exports to the EU during an event at the national production fair.

Relations between France and Algeria have been strained, with a recent diplomatic chill following a Paris court's decision on July 9 to deny provisional release to an Algerian consular agent. The EU-Algeria Association Council has not met in several years, further complicating trade discussions. The French government is now seeking a European-level examination of Algeria's trade practices.

Why it mattersFrench companies face potential losses as France seeks EU action against Algeria's import restrictions.

Algerian bank launches online payments from abroad

The Crédit Populaire d’Algérie's new CPAzonepay service allows merchants to accept Visa and Mastercard payments from international customers.

The Crédit Populaire d’Algérie (CPA) launched CPAzonepay on Thursday, November 12, enabling Algerian merchants to accept online payments from abroad using Visa and Mastercard. This digital transformation initiative aims to support professional clients by facilitating international transactions and expanding commercial opportunities for Algerian businesses.

CPAzonepay is designed to help merchants digitize their payment services and grow their businesses by reaching international customers. The service simplifies payment processing, offers a smooth purchasing experience for customers, and ensures secure transactions. Merchants need a secure website with terms and conditions, a commercial registry or e-commerce entrepreneur card, and accounts in dinar and USD domiciled at CPA to use the service.

The bank stated that CPAzonepay reaffirms its commitment to digital transformation and contributes to expanding national products and services internationally. Merchants can apply by filling out a form at their CPA agency, integrating CPAzonepay onto their website, and then begin receiving international payments.

Why it mattersAlgerian merchants can now accept international payments via Visa and Mastercard, expanding their customer base and sales opportunities.

Algeria warns of climate change impacts on water and food

President Tebboune told COP31 that Algeria faces desertification, drought, and wildfires, and outlined national projects to cut emissions.

President Abdelmadjid Tebboune warned on Thursday, November 12, that Algeria is experiencing the effects of climate change, impacting its water and food security. In a speech read at the COP31 Summit in Antalya, Turkey, Tebboune stated that Algeria faces desertification, recurring droughts, and expanding wildfires. He noted that these issues are central to Algeria's public policies, which aim to balance development needs with global emission reduction efforts, despite the country's small contribution to global emissions.

Tebboune announced that Algeria has submitted its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), setting an unconditional target to reduce emissions by 14% by 2035, funded by national resources. The country is willing to increase this reduction to 31% with international support. Algeria is undertaking national projects, including a program to reach 15,000 megawatts of renewable energy capacity by 2035, adopting a national hydrogen strategy, and improving energy efficiency and public transport. Efforts also focus on reducing gas flaring and methane emissions.

Adaptation is a priority for Algeria, which is working to preserve water security through seawater desalination and treated water reuse. The country is also reviving the Green Dam project, combining desertification control with rural development. Tebboune highlighted that the scale of these adaptation needs exceeds national capabilities, indicating a need for international cooperation.

Why it mattersAlgeria's president stated that national projects to combat climate change, including renewable energy targets and water security measures, are being implemented.
