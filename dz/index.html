Hemdani names Algeria squad without Luca Zidane

Coach Brahim Hemdani selected 26 players for the CAN 2027 qualifiers against Togo, excluding Luca Zidane and Rayan Aït Nouri.

Algeria's national football team coach Brahim Hemdani announced a 26-player squad on Thursday, November 5, for two upcoming matches against Togo in the CAN 2027 qualifiers. Notably absent from the list are Luca Zidane and Rayan Aït Nouri, the latter being dropped due to a disciplinary issue during the last training camp. The squad includes two new players, Mohamed Azzi and Abderrahmane Rebbach, and recalls several players who missed the previous camp.

Hemdani explained Luca Zidane's exclusion was based on sporting criteria, specifically prioritizing goalkeepers with maximum playing time. "I made my choices on a sporting criterion, on this position which is really particular," Hemdani stated. "I needed to have a choice between three goalkeepers who had maximum playing time." Rayan Aït Nouri's absence is seen as a consequence of a past outburst, though his limited game time at Manchester City may also be a factor.

The midfield sees the return of Nabil Bentaleb and Hicham Boudaoui from injury, along with Titraoui. Abderrahmane Rebbach from Deportivo Alavés receives his first call-up, while Aouchiche and Zorgane are omitted. The attack remains largely unchanged, with Ghedjemis replacing Kebbal. The team will face Togo on November 14 and November 17.

Why it mattersAlgerian football fans will be interested in the national team's selection for the upcoming CAN 2027 qualifiers.

Algeria opens Tindouf free trade zone

The 200-hectare zone near Mauritania border will boost exports to West Africa.

Algeria is set to open a 200-hectare free trade zone in Tindouf before the end of 2026. The zone, located near the Mauritanian border, aims to increase trade with West African nations, particularly boosting Algerian exports. Minister of Foreign Trade Kamel Rezig announced the impending operational status on Thursday, November 5.

The Tindouf free trade zone is described as a strategic project, featuring six large warehouses, exhibition spaces, a goods control center, and administrative buildings for customs, banks, and insurance. It also includes health and security services, creating a comprehensive environment for economic operators. Construction began in 2024 with the laying of the first stone by Algerian President Abdelmadjid Tebboune and Mauritanian President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani.

Work on the zone is now fully complete, according to Minister Rezig, who stated that final reception procedures are underway. The government is also coordinating the creation of a public establishment to manage free trade zones. This initiative is intended to establish Tindouf as a central commercial and logistics hub to enhance economic presence in Africa.

Why it mattersAlgerian businesses will gain a direct gateway to Sahel markets, facilitating increased trade and economic activity.

Algeria produces 20 million tonnes of cement surplus

Algeria's cement exports grew twelvefold in four years, reaching $747 million in 2023.

Algeria now produces nearly 20 million tonnes of cement annually as surplus, having shifted from its past import reliance. The nation's cement producers are targeting export markets across Africa, Europe, and the United States. However, ports and the new European carbon tax are hindering growth.

With an annual production capacity of approximately 41 million tonnes in 2024 and a domestic consumption of around 23 million tonnes, Algeria has a substantial exportable surplus. This excess cement is becoming a key element in the country's economic diversification strategy, contributing to non-hydrocarbon exports which reached $5.16 billion in 2025.

Cement and clinker exports have seen dramatic growth, rising from $60 million in 2019 to $747 million in 2023, a more than twelvefold increase. Algeria became the world's second-largest clinker exporter in 2023 and the top exporter in Africa and second in the Mediterranean, with France, Italy, and Belgium as key buyers.

Why it mattersAlgeria's cement industry is generating significant export revenue, contributing to the national economy and diversifying away from hydrocarbons.

Algeria shares water desalination experience in Malta

Algerian Water Desalination Company presented its strategy for 2025-2030, focusing on brine valorization.

Algeria is enhancing its water desalination industry by seeking ways to utilize waste products and convert them into valuable resources. This initiative was the focus of Algeria's participation in a regional meeting held in Malta on November 3 and 4. The Algerian Water Desalination Company, a subsidiary of Sonatrach, showcased Algeria's experience in developing desalination facilities and improving their technical, energy, and environmental performance.

The meeting provided a platform to discuss national water security efforts and address water scarcity exacerbated by climate change. The company outlined its strategic vision for 2025-2030, particularly concerning the valorization of brine, a byproduct of desalination. This involves studying the potential for extracting economic, industrial, and environmental value from brine using scientific research and innovative technologies.

To achieve this, the company is pursuing scientific and technical cooperation with national institutions, including the National Observatory for Environment and Sustainable Development and universities. This collaboration aims to develop studies on brine characteristics and explore valorization methods. The company affirmed its commitment to sustainable desalination that balances water security, industrial efficiency, technological innovation, and environmental protection, aligning with national directives and Algeria's sustainable development commitments.

Why it mattersAlgerians face challenges with water scarcity due to climate change, and the country is developing strategies to improve water security through desalination technology.

Algeria starts potato destocking to ease market shortage

Potato prices have risen sharply since October 1, 2026, causing scarcity in markets.

Algeria has begun destocking potatoes from cold storage facilities in Aïn Defla, Médéa, Boumerdès, and Bouira provinces to address national market shortages. The commerce directorate in Ouled Djellal announced the operation on Wednesday, inviting fruit and vegetable operators to identify participating cold storage units. These reserves were initially established to regulate the market.

The scarcity follows a price cap of 80-90 Algerian dinars per kilogram for retail potatoes, implemented on October 1. Some traders are reportedly selling the tubers discreetly at much higher prices. Economist Brahim Guendouzi attributes the disappearance from stalls to the significant role of intermediaries in fresh produce distribution, stating that price controls and increased inspections have discouraged regular supply at traders' terms.

Guendouzi suggests that the government's destocking plan appears insufficient, citing a combination of factors including weak production, logistical challenges like transport and storage, and numerous intermediaries. He noted that the upcoming harvests might resolve the issue. Oued Souf, a major potato-producing region, estimates its annual output at 16 million quintals.

Why it mattersAlgerian households are facing a shortage of a commonly consumed vegetable due to market disruptions and price controls.
