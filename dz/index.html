Algerian Minister Pledges Full State Support for Farmers After Fires

The agriculture minister visited affected areas in Jijel to assess damage and promised comprehensive state compensation for all agricultural losses.

Algeria's Minister of Agriculture, Rural Development, and Fisheries, Sid Ali Khaldi, visited Jijel province on Thursday to survey the damage caused by recent forest fires. The minister's visit focused on assessing the impact on farmers, livestock breeders, and their agricultural operations in the affected municipalities of Ouled Yahya Khadrouch and Bouraoui Belhadf.

During his visit, the minister met with local residents and assured them of the state's commitment to fully support those affected by the fires, regardless of the scale of their losses. He stated that the agriculture sector has mobilized all human and logistical resources to accurately count and evaluate the damages.

The assessment is a precursor to compensation and the swift resumption of agricultural activities. The support will extend to all individuals who lost livestock, facilities, or agricultural equipment due to the fires. The ministry aims to expedite the process of rebuilding and recovery for the affected communities.

Why it mattersFarmers and livestock breeders in Jijel are directly affected by the state's commitment to provide full compensation for losses incurred from recent fires.

Algeria Plans Regional Hub for African Medicine Supply

An Algerian meeting with UN agencies discussed establishing a regional center to supply essential medicines produced locally across Africa.

Algeria is moving forward with plans to establish a regional center for supplying essential medicines produced locally. Minister of Pharmaceutical Industry Wassim Guédri met with representatives from UNICEF, the World Health Organization, and UNIDO to discuss international cooperation in this area. This initiative follows a technical mission to Copenhagen in July to study the project and aims to bolster pharmaceutical production and health security on the continent.

The proposed center is a direct outcome of the African Ministerial Conference on Local Production of Medicines held in Algiers in November 2025. The conference resulted in the "Algiers Declaration," which outlines several initiatives to enhance cooperation among African nations in local drug manufacturing. Participants emphasized the need for a joint roadmap and a shared work calendar for 2026-2027 to begin supplying African markets with Algerian-produced essential medicines.

The meeting concluded with a call to strengthen strategic partnerships between Algeria and its international collaborators. The focus remains on translating the ministerial conference's outcomes into tangible projects and initiatives. This effort is intended to reinforce Algeria's role in supporting continental health security and promoting integration within the pharmaceutical industry across Africa.

Why it mattersAlgeria's plan to create a regional medicine supply hub directly impacts African investors and pharmaceutical companies by potentially opening new markets and supply chains.

Mohand Bouraï Assumes Central Bank Governor Role

Mohand Bouraï officially took over as the new governor of the Bank of Algeria, succeeding Mohamed Lamine Loukal.

Mohand Bouraï assumed his duties as the new governor of the Bank of Algeria on Thursday, September 3. He replaces Mohamed Lamine Loukal, who was appointed Minister of Finance in the recent partial cabinet reshuffle. The handover ceremony was overseen by the outgoing governor and now Minister of Finance, Mohamed Lamine Loukal.

During the ceremony, Bouraï stated that Algerian banks possess sufficient liquidity and that the financing of the economy is experiencing notable growth. He indicated that the central bank is committed to supporting economic expansion through adequate financial resources.

The appointment of Bouraï marks a transition in the leadership of Algeria's central bank. His initial remarks suggest a focus on maintaining financial stability and encouraging economic development through robust banking sector liquidity and credit provision.

Why it mattersAlgerian banks have sufficient liquidity and financing for the economy is growing, according to the new central bank governor.

Algeria Postpones National Quran Week

The Ministry of Religious Affairs and Endowments announced the postponement of the 28th edition of the National Quranic Week, originally set to begin September 7, 2026.

Algeria's Ministry of Religious Affairs and Endowments announced on Thursday the postponement of the 28th edition of the National Quranic Week. The event was scheduled to commence on September 7, 2026, but has now been rescheduled to an unspecified later date.

The ministry stated that the new date for the Quranic Week will be announced in due course. They reiterated their hope that the occasion will serve as a blessed time for gathering around the Holy Book and celebrating its adherents. The previous edition of the National Quranic Week was held in the state of Boumerdes, starting September 15, 2025.

The Quranic Week typically includes national competitions for memorization and recitation of the Quran, alongside academic seminars and other activities. The postponement affects the planned schedule for these cultural and religious events across the country.

Why it mattersThe postponement of the National Quranic Week affects religious scholars, participants in competitions, and attendees who gather for recitations and seminars.

Algeria Launches Three New Digital Water Services

The High Commission for Digitalization introduced new online services for water connections and professional certifications for water sector businesses.

The High Commission for Digitalization announced the addition of three new digital services related to water resources through the National Portal for Government Digital Services. These services aim to expand digital offerings and enhance inter-sectoral interoperability, following directives from the President.

The new services include applications for connecting to the potable water network, allowing citizens to submit and track requests remotely. Another service enables online submission and tracking of applications for connection to the public sewage network, with electronic processing of requests.

The third service caters to businesses in the water sector, facilitating the submission, management, and tracking of applications for professional qualification and classification certificates. The High Commission indicated that more digital services will be introduced on the portal in the future.

Why it mattersBusinesses in the water sector can now submit and track applications for professional certificates online, streamlining administrative processes.
