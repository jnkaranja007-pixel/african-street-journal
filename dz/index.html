Algeria loses two key players before Togo match

Forward Mohamed Bachir Belloumi and defender Jaouen Hadjam are out with injuries for the CAN 2027 qualifiers.

Algeria's national football team will be without key players Mohamed Bachir Belloumi and Jaouen Hadjam for their upcoming CAN 2027 qualifying matches against Togo. The team, known as the Fennecs, faces Togo on November 14 in Tizi-Ouzou and again on November 17 in Lomé. Both players have been starters since coach Brahim Hemdani took charge in September.

The Algerian Football Federation confirmed that both Belloumi, an attacker for Hull City, and Hadjam, a defender for Brighton, are sidelined due to injuries. Goalkeeper Abdellatif Ramdane was also released from the squad by Hemdani following the death of his father. Defender Fahem Benaïssa-Yahia, who plays for Italy's Mantova 1911, has been called up to replace Hadjam.

Benaïssa, 24, is a versatile defender making his first appearance for the national team. Goalkeeper Rayane Yeseli of Olympique Akbou was summoned to replace Ramdane. No replacement has been named for Mohamed Bachir Belloumi. The Fennecs are preparing for the matches on November 14 and November 17.

Algeria targets $7 billion non-hydrocarbon exports by 2026

The country aims to increase non-hydrocarbon exports from over $5 billion in 2025 to $7 billion by 2026.

Algeria plans to boost its non-hydrocarbon exports to $7 billion by 2026, a significant increase from over $5 billion in 2025. This strategy involves expanding the variety of products sold internationally and reaching more countries, with export destinations growing from 33 to 57.

Ali Bey Naceri, Vice-President of the National Association of Algerian Exporters, stated that this expansion reflects a major shift beyond traditional European and African markets. New sectors like food processing, agriculture, manufacturing, and pharmaceuticals are now contributing to exports, alongside construction materials such as cement and ceramics.

The goal is to make non-hydrocarbon exports represent 50% of Algeria's total exports, a strategic priority for improving the trade balance and generating foreign currency. Naceri emphasized that exporting is evolving into a fully fledged business activity requiring investment and institutional support.

Why it mattersAlgerian businesses and the national economy will benefit from increased foreign currency earnings and a more diversified export base.

Spanish consulate staff invested 120 million DA in Algerian real estate

Investigators found six properties in Algiers linked to an illegal visa trafficking scheme operating for over a decade.

Spanish investigators have uncovered that part of the money from an illegal visa trafficking scheme at the Spanish Consulate in Algiers was invested in real estate. Six properties in Algiers, acquired between 2016 and 2021, were linked to the operation. The total value of these acquisitions amounts to 120 million Algerian dinars, approximately 783,919 euros.

The scheme involved the illegal issuance of visas, residency permits, and work authorizations for sums up to 25,000 euros per family. The investigation began in April 2026 with the arrest of Vicente Moreno, the consulate's deputy head, and a local employee. Moreno's Algerian wife was also investigated, and reports suggest the couple also owns property in Portugal.

The illegal activities are believed to have been ongoing for at least 10 years, facilitating the entry of Algerians into the Schengen area who did not meet the required conditions. The case was initiated following a report from the Spanish Ministry of the Interior concerning visa trafficking.

Why it mattersAlgerian citizens seeking to enter the Schengen area were affected by a visa trafficking scheme that involved illegal payments and property investment.

Politics · 2026-11-10

Algerian election posters hide female candidates' faces

Anonymised images on campaign posters for the November 28 local elections echo a trend seen since 2012.

Ahead of Algeria's local elections on November 28, many campaign posters feature candidates, primarily women, with only silhouettes or blank spaces instead of photographs. In some instances, even surnames are omitted, raising questions about voters' right to identify those seeking their votes.

This practice, which first appeared in the 2012 legislative elections and intensified by 2017, has led to candidates being dubbed "ghost women." In 2017, electoral monitoring bodies in some regions, like Bordj Bou Arreridj, demanded parties replace such posters within 48 hours or face removal.

An electoral official at the time stated that hiding candidates' faces was an affront to their image and that citizens have a right to know who they are voting for. Parties eventually complied, adding the faces of their female candidates to the posters.

Why it mattersAlgerian voters cannot identify all candidates, particularly women, on election posters for the upcoming local elections.

Algeria submits five royal tombs for Unesco heritage status

The culture minister announced the submission of five sites, including tombs in Tamanrasset and Constantine, on Sunday, November 8.

Algeria has submitted a dossier to Unesco seeking to list five royal tombs as universal material heritage sites. Minister of Culture and Arts Malika Bendouda announced the submission on Sunday, November 8, stating it was a significant step for the country's heritage.

The nominated sites include the royal tomb of Imedghassen in Batna, the royal tomb of Massinissa in Constantine, the tomb of Siga in Ain Témouchent, the tomb of Queen Tin Hinan in Tamanrasset, and the Djeddar tombs in Frenda, Tiaret. Bendouda highlighted that these tombs, spread across Algeria's east, west, and south, represent the nation's unified cultural and patrimonial identity.

This submission marks Algeria's first application for material heritage status since the Casbah of Algiers was classified in 1992. The dossier is among 35 selected by Unesco and will undergo evaluation by specialized consultation bodies.

Why it mattersAlgerians will see their nation's historical royal tombs considered for global heritage recognition.
