Algeria cuts passport fees for diaspora in Europe

New passport tariffs for Algerians living in Europe are now in effect, with adults paying 40 euros for a 28-page document.

Algeria has cut the stamp duties on passports for its citizens living in Europe. The Consulate General of Algeria in Brussels said in a notice published on Saturday, 14 November, that a 28-page passport will now cost adults 40 euros, and 20 euros for minors and students. The new rates took effect from the date of the notice.

The previous fees were 60 euros for a 28-page adult passport and 90 euros for the 48-page version. Under the new rates, the 48-page passport costs 60 euros for adults and 30 euros for minors and students. An interministerial order of 29 August sets the foreign-currency equivalent of the duties using annual exchange rates published by the Bank of Algeria.

The 2026 finance law sets the stamp duty at 6,000 dinars for a 28-page passport and 9,000 dinars for a 48-page one. Expedited service, with delivery within five days, costs 25,000 dinars and 45,000 dinars respectively. A replacement after loss or damage carries an extra 10,000 dinars for adults and 5,000 dinars for students and minors.

Why it mattersAlgerians in Europe who renew or replace passports will pay considerably less, with the lower fees applying from the date of the Brussels notice.

French exports to Algeria drop 34% in two months

Steel and food sectors are among those affected by the significant decline in trade between France and Algeria.

French exports to Algeria fell by 34% in August and September 2026 compared to the same period in 2025, according to French customs statistics reported by Le Monde.

This decline represents a loss of 241 million euros, with exports dropping from 707 million euros in 2025 to 466 million euros in 2026.

The figures also show a 43% decrease over two years, as exports in August and September 2024 reached 819 million euros.

Why it mattersFrench companies exporting to Algeria lost 241 million euros in two months, affecting sectors from steel to food.

Algeria's president threatens to strike drug sources abroad

President Abdelmadjid Tebboune called the flood of drugs into Algeria a real war and warned the country could be forced to act at the source.

President Abdelmadjid Tebboune has warned that Algeria may be forced to go to the source of drug manufacturing, calling the flood of narcotics into the country a real war against its people and youth. He made the remarks on Sunday, 15 November, at the opening of the 2026-2027 judicial year at the Supreme Court in Algiers, before a gathering of magistrates.

Tebboune said any such action would be taken under the right of legitimate defence. The military review El Djeich, published by the National People's Army (ANP), made a similar charge in its November issue, listing drug trafficking among hostile efforts against Algeria alongside disinformation campaigns and sabotage.

Earlier, on 3 October, Tebboune had said drugs had become a means of war against Algeria, describing it as a hybrid war waged by saboteurs targeting young people. Large quantities of drugs, mainly from Morocco and the southern borders, are regularly seized by ANP units and security services.

Why it mattersTraffickers moving drugs into Algeria face the threat of action at their source, and the state has already converted a Sahara military barracks at Tanezrouft into a prison for drug traffickers.

Algeria launches electronic land document certification

The national land directorate's digital portal now issues legally recognized electronic land documents.

Algeria's Directorate General of National Land (DGDN) has made land documents downloaded from its "Fada El Djazair" portal legally certified. The DGDN launched the third version of its digital portal on November 1, introducing electronic signatures that give documents official state recognition. This advancement aims to strengthen trust in digital land records and simplify procedures for citizens and institutions.

The electronic certification applies to cadastral extracts, copies of deeds, negative certificates, and cadastral copies. These PDF documents are signed electronically according to the mechanism recognized by the Electronic Certification Government Authority (AGCE). Users can verify the authenticity of these documents using Adobe Acrobat Reader, which shows a green indicator for valid signatures and content integrity.

The DGDN stated that electronically signed documents hold the same legal value as printed copies and do not require further printing for use. The "Fada El Djazair" portal has recorded over 8.8 million visits since the start of 2026, indicating significant public interest in these digital land services.

Why it mattersCitizens can now use legally recognized electronic land documents without needing to print them, simplifying administrative procedures.

Sport · 2026-11-16

Algeria loses Anis Hadj Moussa and Ibrahim Maza before Togo match

The Algerian Football Federation says both players have ankle sprains and will miss the CAN 2027 qualifier in Lomé on Tuesday.

Algeria's national team has lost two attacking players, Anis Hadj Moussa and Ibrahim Maza, before its CAN 2027 qualifier against Togo in Lomé on Tuesday 17 November. Both were starters in Saturday's 1-0 win over Togo in Tizi-Ouzou, the third match of the qualifying series.

The Algerian Football Federation (FAF) announced the withdrawals in a statement on Sunday. It said both players suffered ankle sprains in Saturday's match, and medical examinations on Sunday morning confirmed they will not travel to Lomé.

The absences add to a list of attacking players the squad is already missing, including Jaouen Hadjam, Mohamed Bachir Belloumi and Hicham Boudaoui. Coach Brahim Hemdani has replacements available, the FAF said, and a win in Lomé would move Algeria a step closer to the 2027 tournament.

Why it mattersFans following the qualifier will see a weakened attack for the Lomé match, with Algeria needing a win there to move closer to the 2027 tournament.
