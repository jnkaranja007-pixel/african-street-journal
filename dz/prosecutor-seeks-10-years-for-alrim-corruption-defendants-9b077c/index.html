Prosecutor Seeks 10 Years for ALRIM Corruption Defendants

Algerian state enterprise ALRIM faces allegations of squandering public funds, abuse of office, and contract violations in a Rouiba Court trial.

The Public Prosecutor at Rouiba Court has requested maximum penalties of 10 years imprisonment for defendants in a corruption case involving ALRIM, a state-owned construction firm. The prosecution seeks the confiscation of all seized assets, with a verdict scheduled for September 8. Charges include squandering public funds, granting unjustified advantages, and violating procurement laws through abuse of official positions.

The defendants, including former ALRIM CEO T.M. and business operators A.A., L.H., and S.A., are accused of deliberate misappropriation of public funds, forging commercial documents, and exploiting influence. The case originated from a complaint filed by ALRIM against EHA Construction manager A.A. and his company.

ALRIM, a joint-stock company in metal structures, operates under the IMETAL group. EHA Construction specializes in construction work, material supply, and maintenance. The alleged acts are described by the prosecution as undermining the national economy through illicit financial dealings and contract irregularities.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the alleged corruption and contract violations at ALRIM, a state-owned enterprise, which could impact business operations and public funds.
