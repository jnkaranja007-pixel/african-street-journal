Algeria warns of climate change impact at COP31

President Tebboune highlighted desertification, drought, and wildfires as major threats to Algeria's water and food security.

Sources: TSA Algerie and Algerie360 · 2026-11-13 · 1 min read

Algeria's President Abdelmadjid Tebboune warned on Thursday, November 12, that climate change is a present reality impacting the nation's water and food security. Speaking at the COP31 Summit in Antalya, Turkey, Tebboune stated that Algeria faces three major climate phenomena: desertification, recurring droughts, and expanding forest fires.

The president outlined Algeria's commitment to reducing emissions despite its small contribution, submitting a National Determined Contribution (NDC) aiming for a 14% reduction by 2035, financed nationally. Tebboune noted that this target could increase to 31% with international support. Algeria is also investing in 15,000 megawatts of renewable energy by 2035 and developing a national hydrogen strategy.

To adapt, Algeria is prioritizing desalination and water reuse, alongside reviving the Green Dam project to combat desertification and develop rural areas. Tebboune emphasized that the scale of these adaptation needs exceeds national capabilities, highlighting the need for international cooperation.

Why it mattersAlgeria is implementing national projects for renewable energy and water security, with a conditional emissions reduction target of 31% by 2035.
