Tunisian official warns racist words can incite violence

Khémaïs Gharbi states that public discourse can make hateful speech acceptable and lead to violence.

Source: Kapitalis · 2026-11-02 · 1 min read

Khémaïs Gharbi, a Tunisian official, warned that racist words can lead to violence by creating a climate where aggression becomes thinkable and acceptable. He explained that while insults and crimes are different, the path between them can be paved by hateful speech. Gharbi stated that public prejudice gains legitimacy when officials designate populations as scapegoats during sensitive times like election campaigns.

Gharbi highlighted that public figures can exploit fears and harden group identities by blaming specific populations for societal problems. He referenced Martin Luther King Jr.'s fight against the systemic dehumanization of Black Americans, noting that violence is supported by unjust institutions and daily humiliations. The official cautioned that psychological fragility alone does not explain racist violence, which can be fueled by ideology, organized groups, propaganda, or a sense of impunity.

The official emphasized that words shape the world and that public discourse can transform shameful ideas into acceptable ones. He stressed that racism begins not always with a blow, but sometimes with a joke, insinuation, nickname, or look of contempt that reduces individuals to their origin. Gharbi asserted that the danger grows when prejudice receives public legitimacy, making what was once shameful become speakable and eventually permissible.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens are affected as public discourse can normalize prejudice and incite violence against specific groups.
