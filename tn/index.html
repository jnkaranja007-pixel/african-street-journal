Tunisia Launches Floating Solar Study at Sidi El Barrak Dam

A pilot project aims to generate electricity and reduce water evaporation, with potential for wider application across Tunisian hydraulic structures.

The Tunisian Ministry of Agriculture has initiated a pilot study for a floating photovoltaic power plant at the Sidi El Barrak dam in Béja governorate. Launched on Thursday, August 27, 2026, the project seeks to produce electricity while simultaneously decreasing water loss through evaporation. The findings from this experiment are intended to determine the feasibility of implementing similar systems on other water reservoirs throughout Tunisia.

This initiative is part of the TECH-EAU program, which focuses on improving water resource management and adopting suitable technological solutions. The floating solar technology involves placing photovoltaic panels on water surfaces. At Sidi El Barrak, the dual objective is to utilize existing surface area for energy generation and to mitigate water evaporation, addressing both growing electricity demands and water scarcity pressures.

The study will thoroughly examine the technical, hydraulic, environmental, and economic aspects of installing the floating solar system at Sidi El Barrak. This detailed assessment is crucial for evaluating the technology's performance and suitability under Tunisian conditions before any consideration of expanding the project to other dams. The results will inform decisions on the device's effectiveness, energy yield, and impact on evaporation and the environment.

Why it mattersThis pilot project affects farmers by potentially improving water management and reducing evaporation from reservoirs, while also exploring new renewable energy sources for the country.

Tunisia Fires Threaten Forests, Prompting Rapid Response

Multiple blazes erupted in Jendouba governorate, including Aïn Draham and Aïn Soltane, affecting pine and maquis areas before being brought under control.

Three simultaneous fires broke out Thursday evening across the Jendouba governorate, specifically in Aïn Draham, Aïn Soltane, and Bousalem. The blazes, which impacted Aleppo pine forests and maquis vegetation, required an overnight effort from civil protection teams and other responders. These fires were ultimately brought under control by Friday morning.

The geographically dispersed nature of the incidents necessitated a multi-front response throughout the night. Firefighting operations continued for several hours amidst high temperatures and weather conditions that could have accelerated the spread of flames. While the fires are now contained, a consolidated assessment of the burned areas and material damage has not yet been released.

No official report has been issued detailing the exact extent of the land affected by the flames across the three distinct locations, nor the scale of any potential material losses. The origins of these fires remain undetermined. Authorities are expected to evaluate the impacted zones before providing a more precise damage assessment.

Why it mattersFarmers and investors may face losses from damaged forests and vegetation, while the diaspora has no direct connection to these events.

Tunisie Telecom Honors 437 Young Achievers

The telecommunications company recognized students and graduates for academic excellence during its annual "Journée du Savoir" event.

Tunisie Telecom celebrated academic success by honoring 437 students and graduates from employee families at its annual "Journée du Savoir" event held on August 27 and 28 at the El Ghazala Technology Park. Among the honorees were 8 students who received national distinctions, and the top seven baccalaureate graduates of 2026 were invited as guests of honor. The recognized students achieved excellent results during the 2025-2026 academic year, including those admitted to pilot colleges and high schools, new baccalaureate graduates, and higher education graduates.

The two-day event recognized 242 recipients on the first day, comprising higher education graduates and students who passed the end of basic education exam. The second day honored 195 students, including those from primary school and new baccalaureate graduates. Lassaâd Ben Dhiab, CEO of Tunisie Telecom, presided over the ceremony, joined by representatives from the General Federation of Telecommunications and the company's social fund.

Beyond awards, Tunisie Telecom highlighted its commitment to education through digital transformation and equal access to knowledge. Initiatives include support for school transport, educational accompaniment, and high-speed internet connections for educational institutions. The company is also developing a "smart classroom" project to integrate new technologies into learning and modernize the education system, backed by over 70,000 kilometers of fiber optic infrastructure.

Why it mattersTunisie Telecom's investment in digital infrastructure and educational initiatives directly impacts students and educational institutions.

Tunisian Government Restores Double Workday for Public Sector

State employees will return to a split workday schedule starting September 1, 2026, after a two-month summer period.

Tunisian public sector employees will resume a double workday schedule on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, as announced by the Government Presidency. This change marks the end of the summer schedule, which ran for two months and was associated with reduced productivity. The administration aims to increase output by reinstating the traditional winter working hours.

Under the new schedule, Monday through Thursday will feature two work sessions: the first from 8:30 to 12:30, followed by a break, and the second from 13:30 to 17:30. This structure applies to agents of the state, local authorities, and administrative public institutions.

Fridays will have adjusted hours, with a morning session from 8:00 to 13:00 and an afternoon session from 14:30 to 17:30. The Government Presidency issued a statement on Friday, August 28, 2026, detailing these changes intended to restore a more rigorous administrative rhythm.

Why it mattersPublic sector employees are directly affected by the return to a longer, split workday, impacting their daily routines and work-life balance.

Tunisian Wrestler Chahd Jeljeli Missing After Mediterranean Games Match

The 20-year-old athlete disappeared after her bronze medal bout in Taranto, Italy, with her belongings still in her room.

Tunisian wrestler Chahd Jeljeli, 20, has been missing since Tuesday, August 25, while participating in the Mediterranean Games in Taranto, Italy. Hatem Marnaoui, head of the official Tunisian delegation, confirmed on Thursday, August 27, that the delegation had lost contact with the athlete. Jeljeli competed for the bronze medal in the women's 57 kg wrestling category against Italy's Fabiana Rinella.

According to Marnaoui's account to TAP agency, Jeljeli remained with national coach Mohamed Ali Bouziane after her defeat to watch her teammates compete. She then reportedly left the venue without informing her coach, who was occupied with other matches amidst a large crowd. After the competitions concluded, her coach and teammates searched the venue unsuccessfully, initially assuming she had returned alone to the Mediterranean Village due to the frequent shuttle services.

Upon reaching the Mediterranean Village, the Tunisian team found no trace of Jeljeli. Marnaoui stated that all her personal belongings were still in her room. He was informed of the disappearance late Tuesday evening and subsequently contacted Tunisian authorities, the Tunisian National Olympic Committee, the Games organizing committee, and security services at the village. No official information has emerged regarding the circumstances or reasons for her disappearance.

Why it mattersThe disappearance of a young athlete during an international competition raises concerns for families and the diaspora about the safety and whereabouts of their relatives abroad.
