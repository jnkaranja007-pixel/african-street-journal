Ooredoo Tunisie named best 5G network by Ookla

The operator received the Speedtest Award for its performance in the first half of 2026, based on nearly two million user tests.

Ooredoo Tunisie has been named the best 5G network in Tunisia by Ookla, a global leader in connectivity intelligence. The award is based on analysis of consumer-initiated tests conducted via Speedtest during the first half of 2026.

The operator achieved a Speedtest Connectivity Score of 73.74, surpassing competitors based on factors including download speed, web browsing, and video streaming. Ookla's evaluation combined these elements, with speed accounting for 50% of the final score.

Ooredoo Tunisie launched its commercial 5G services in February 2025, investing in infrastructure to meet growing demand. The company aims to continue strengthening its network and exploring new digital uses.

Why it mattersTunisian consumers using Ooredoo's 5G network will experience superior digital performance, including faster speeds and better streaming.

Tunisia expects 2.7% growth in 2026

The government forecasts economic expansion despite a widening current account deficit due to energy costs.

Tunisia's economy is projected to grow by 2.7% in 2026, according to Prime Minister Sarra Zaâfrani Zenzri. This forecast, presented to parliament, follows a 2.4% GDP growth in the first half of 2026, indicating a continued economic recovery.

Despite signs of recovery, external imbalances persist, with the current account deficit expected to reach 3.9% of GDP in 2026, up from 2.5% in 2025. This widening deficit is attributed to trade pressures and increased energy imports. Foreign exchange reserves currently cover 93 days of imports.

The government also reported a decrease in the unemployment rate to 14.9% in the second quarter and a slight drop in inflation to 5.3% by September. Tourist revenue rose by 4.5% to nearly 6.3 billion dinars by September 30, while remittances from Tunisians abroad increased by 5%.

Why it mattersTunisians will experience economic growth and a slight decrease in unemployment, but face persistent external financial pressures.

Tunisia proposes lower import taxes for solar equipment

The 2027 finance bill includes a 10% duty on solar sensors and tax exemptions for battery imports.

Tunisia's draft 2027 finance law proposes a 10% import duty on solar sensors and tax exemptions for batteries used in electricity storage. The government also plans to expand financing options for companies undertaking environmental upgrades. These measures aim to reduce equipment costs and encourage investment in renewable energy.

Prime Minister Sarra Zaâfrani Zenzri presented these proposals, detailed in articles 49, 50, and 51 of the draft law, to the Assembly of people's representatives and the National council of regions and districts on Monday, November 9. The measures require final adoption of the finance bill.

The proposed fiscal incentives are part of Tunisia's energy transition strategy. The government seeks to facilitate the adoption of renewable energy technologies by making them more affordable for businesses and consumers.

Why it mattersTunisian businesses investing in renewable energy equipment will see reduced import costs.

Tunisia tourism faces climate, water, energy challenges

Houssem Ben Azouz of Fi2T calls for reforms to boost profitability and resilience in the sector.

Tunisia's tourism sector needs reforms to improve company profitability, water and energy security, and climate adaptation, according to Houssem Ben Azouz, general coordinator of the Tunisian Interprofessional Federation of Tourism (Fi2T). He stated that the sector must become more profitable, resilient, and a sustainable creator of wealth.

Ben Azouz highlighted that the summer 2026 heatwaves and water and electricity shortages revealed the sector's vulnerability, potentially harming visitor experience and the destination's image. He proposed prioritizing business continuity plans, adapting services to heat, and enhancing coordination between authorities and professionals.

Fi2T is advocating for a new tourism model focusing on average tourist spending and stay duration, alongside profitability and regional benefit distribution. Ben Azouz called for a national roadmap for resilient, sustainable, and competitive tourism, emphasizing energy efficiency, solar power, water rationalization, and financing for the transition.

Why it mattersTunisia's tourism businesses face challenges from climate change, water scarcity, and energy supply issues that threaten their profitability and sustainability.

Tunisia revises university lecturer status

Ministry of Higher Education reopens review of 1993 decree to redefine academic roles and responsibilities.

Tunisia's Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research has restarted the process of revising the status of university lecturers. This move follows years of discussion and proposals aimed at updating the regulations governing academic careers.

The current framework, based on a 1993 decree, is considered outdated as the university landscape has significantly changed with massification of higher education and internationalization of research. The revision seeks to acknowledge the diverse activities of academics beyond teaching and research, such as program design, lab management, and international collaboration.

The ministry aims to redefine the professional identity of academics, their missions, and the institutional conditions for their work. This reform intends to address issues of workload recognition, equity, and skill valorization within the university system.

Why it mattersUniversity lecturers in Tunisia will have their professional roles and responsibilities redefined by the government.
