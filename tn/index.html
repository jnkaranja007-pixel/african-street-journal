Tunisian Fuel Transporters Announce September Strike Over Unmet Agreements

A two-day strike by fuel transporters is planned for September 23-24, citing unfulfilled prior agreements and social security obligations, potentially disrupting fuel supplies.

Tunisian fuel transporters have announced a sector-wide strike for September 23 and 24, threatening to create long queues at gas stations. The General Union of Tunisian Labor (UGTT) private sector department issued the strike notice on August 27, 2026, citing administrative parties' failure to implement previous agreements, notably one from May 2, 2019, and to meet legal obligations regarding social coverage with the CNSS.

Representatives from the transport sector met at the UGTT headquarters to finalize the strike action, which will affect all workplaces nationwide. The notice was sent to several ministries, including Social Affairs, Industry, Energy, Interior, Commerce, and Transport, as well as industry associations like Utica and the National Chamber of Dangerous Goods Transport. This action follows a surprise strike on August 20 that disrupted fuel distribution.

Despite the strike announcement, Agil, a fuel distribution company, stated that its stocks in Radès and Skhira are sufficient and deliveries are proceeding normally. Agil's CEO, Khaled Bettine, reassured the public on August 26, emphasizing that the company has no supply issues. However, his comments specifically address Agil's operations and do not guarantee the availability of fuel from all distributors, leaving potential concerns for motorists.

Why it mattersFuel transporters are striking because of unmet agreements and social security obligations, which could affect fuel availability for all drivers.

Algerian Fires Kill 12, Injure Dozens

Forest fires raging across northern Algeria have claimed at least 12 lives and injured dozens, forcing evacuations and road closures in affected regions.

At least 12 people have died and dozens more have been injured in wildfires sweeping through several northern Algerian regions, including Jijel, Béjaïa, and Tizi Ouzou. The Interior Minister, Saïd Sayoud, reported five fatalities in Jijel, four in Béjaïa, and three in Tizi Ouzou. Many families have been evacuated from their homes as emergency services continue to battle the blazes, which are exacerbated by high temperatures and dry conditions.

In Béjaïa, the situation is particularly critical, with 54 individuals treated for burns, six of whom are in serious condition. Local media reported that three women from the same family in Tizi Ouzou died from smoke inhalation. The fires have prompted the closure of several roads in the eastern part of the country, with authorities advising motorists to avoid affected areas in Béjaïa, Jijel, and Sétif.

As of Wednesday evening, 159 vegetation fires had been recorded nationwide, with 69 still active. Béjaïa had the highest number of active fires with 18, followed by Skikda with 13. The Civil Protection deployed significant resources, but strong winds are hampering firefighting efforts. These incidents follow a pattern of deadly wildfires in Algeria, with Béjaïa experiencing similar events in 2023.

Why it mattersThe fires directly impact residents in affected regions through loss of life, injuries, and displacement from homes.

Tunisian Wrestler Chahd Jeljeli Missing After Mediterranean Games Match

The 20-year-old athlete disappeared Tuesday after her bronze medal fight in Taranto, Italy, with her personal belongings still in her room.

Tunisian wrestler Chahd Jeljeli, 20, has been reported missing since Tuesday, August 25, while participating in the Mediterranean Games in Taranto, Italy. Hatem Marnaoui, head of the official Tunisian delegation, confirmed on Thursday, August 27, that the athlete has not been heard from. Jeljeli competed for the bronze medal in the women's 57 kg wrestling category against Italy's Fabiana Rinella.

According to Marnaoui, Jeljeli remained at the venue after her match to watch her teammates compete. She reportedly left the hall without informing her coach, Mohamed Ali Bouziane, who was occupied with other events amidst a large crowd. After the competitions concluded, her coach and teammates searched for her but could not find her, initially assuming she might have returned to the Mediterranean village alone.

Upon returning to the Mediterranean village, the Tunisian team found no trace of Jeljeli, and her personal belongings were still in her room. Marnaoui stated he was informed of her disappearance late Tuesday evening and subsequently contacted Tunisian authorities, the Tunisian National Olympic Committee, the organizing committee, and security services. No official information has emerged regarding the circumstances or reasons for her disappearance.

Why it mattersThe disappearance of a young athlete at an international sporting event raises concerns for her family and the Tunisian diaspora about her safety and well-being.

Tunisian Migrants Drown, Protests Erupt Over Border Town's Economy

Hundreds attended the funeral of 11 young men from Ben Guerdane who died in a shipwreck off Zarzis, sparking protests over the town's reliance on cross-border trade.

The funeral of 11 young men from Ben Guerdane, who drowned in a shipwreck off Zarzis, drew hundreds of residents to the Libyan border town. The ceremony on Wednesday, August 26, 2026, highlighted local tensions, with observers noting the absence of local and national officials. The tragedy has fueled protests and unrest in Ben Guerdane, where dozens of young people blocked roads and set fires during nighttime demonstrations.

The 11 Tunisians, all from Ben Guerdane, died when their irregular migrant boat capsized off Zarzis. Their bodies, recovered and identified, were returned to their families for burial. The boat, carrying 15 young men mostly from Ben Guerdane, departed Zarzis on August 19. According to the sole survivor, the vessel sank approximately five hours later due to water infiltration, deteriorating weather, and overloading with fuel and water containers.

The survivor, who endured over two days at sea before rescue by a Libyan vessel, reported that 11 passengers wore life vests while four did not. Tunisian authorities have launched a judicial investigation into the clandestine crossing. The incident follows a pattern of increasing migratory pressure from Tunisia's southeastern coast towards Italy, often involving youth from border regions. Residents and political parties are demanding urgent solutions for the area's development.

Why it mattersThe drowning of 11 young men from Ben Guerdane and subsequent protests highlight the precarious economic situation and reliance on cross-border trade in this Libyan border town, affecting livelihoods and potentially fueling irregular migration.
