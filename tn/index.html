Tunisia forecasts strong winds, dust in the south and rough northern seas

Maximum temperatures of 27 to 32 degrees Celsius are expected on Thursday, November 12, 2026, with weak rain in the far north overnight.

Tunisia will have partly cloudy weather on Thursday, November 12, 2026, with maximum temperatures generally between 27 and 32 degrees Celsius. South winds will blow fairly strongly to locally strong in several regions, and the sea will become very rough to choppy in the north, according to the National Institute of Meteorology (INM).

The INM says the sky will be partly veiled during the day and become very cloudy at night in northern regions. Gusts in the south could raise sand and dust, reducing visibility. Maximum temperatures will stay stable, around 24 degrees in the western highlands.

Weak rain is also expected in the extreme north during the night. The forecast reports no further changes to the outlook for the coming days.

Why it mattersDrivers, sea users and residents in the south face reduced visibility from dust and winds, while northern coastal users face rough seas.

Tunisia presidency budget to rise 8.22% in 2027

The presidency's 2027 budget will be 248.576 million dinars, up from 2026, according to Mourad Heloumi.

Tunisia's presidency will receive a budget of 248.576 million dinars in 2027, an increase of 8.22% compared to 2026. Mourad Heloumi, the first advisor to the president for common services, presented this figure on Wednesday, November 11, during a review of the presidency's draft budget at the Assembly of People's Representatives. The funds are allocated to national security and external relations, presidential security and protection, and steering and support programs.

Heloumi highlighted national security strategies including counter-terrorism (2023-2027), border security (2024-2028), cybersecurity (2026-2030), and pharmaceutical strategy (2025-2034). The presidency also supported the development of a national artificial intelligence strategy focused on ethical use and development goals. Discussions also covered combating drug trafficking, human trafficking, irregular migration, and road safety, emphasizing inter-institutional coordination.

The administrative mediator's office received 2,405 requests in 2025, processing 1,629, a reported 21.5% increase. The Fida Foundation, supporting victims of terror attacks, and the Tunisian Institute for Strategic Studies were also mentioned. Administrative and financial control focused on 51 reports from control bodies and 21 inspection reports, examining procedures and cereal import-export operations.

Why it mattersThe Tunisian presidency's budget for 2027 has been set at 248.576 million dinars, an increase of 8.22% from the previous year.

Tunisia focuses on execution at 40th business event

The annual Journées de l’Entreprise in Sousse will prioritize actionable recommendations over diagnostics.

Tunisia's 40th Journées de l’Entreprise will take place in Sousse from December 10 to 12, 2026, focusing on the challenge of executing economic reforms and strategies. Organized by the Institut arabe des chefs d’entreprise (IACE), the event aims to move beyond identifying problems to proposing concrete, directly applicable solutions. This edition, held under the patronage of the President of the Republic, addresses the theme "The Company Facing the Challenge of Execution: From Reform to Transformation."

Organizers note that results from government actions are slow to materialize and be felt, prompting a shift in focus to the 'how' of implementation. The program will explore both external geopolitical constraints and internal company-level barriers to transformation. International figures will discuss geopolitical impacts, and a special session will address energy challenges. The CEO Masterclass will examine internal mechanisms hindering progress and present levers for action.

The second day will feature Recommendation Workshops on eight themes, where participants will exclusively present concrete, applicable recommendations rather than engaging in debates. These recommendations will be synthesized and shared after the event. A "Challenge Session" will also broaden the discussion to include water stress alongside energy concerns, reflecting the event's commitment to practical outcomes.

Why it mattersTunisian businesses and government officials will focus on implementing reforms and strategies to achieve tangible economic results.

Tunisia and Libya seek joint roadmap to boost cooperation

Tunis hosted preparatory talks for the 23rd High Joint Commission session, which is due to be held in Tripoli.

Tunisia and Libya are seeking to give new momentum to their bilateral cooperation, particularly in security, economic and trade matters. Preparatory talks for the 23rd session of the High Joint Commission took place in Tunis on November 10 and 11. Tunisian Foreign Minister Mohamed Ali Nafti called for a common roadmap to follow up and implement the recommendations made.

The preparatory commission was co-chaired by Samir Mansar, director general for bilateral relations with Arab countries at Tunisia's Foreign Ministry, and Achraf Al-Tayeb, director of international cooperation at Libya's Foreign Ministry. The delegations examined how to activate existing agreements and draft new legal texts for the next session. Both sides highlighted the complementarity of their economies and their geographic and cultural proximity.

Economic, commercial and investment cooperation were also on the agenda, alongside security. The 23rd High Joint Commission session is scheduled to take place in Tripoli, where the draft legal texts prepared by both delegations are to be reviewed.

Tunisia chairs Arab health council meeting

Health Minister Mustapha Ferjani proposed an Arab network for medical simulation centers.

Tunisia is leading the Arab Council of Health Specialties and aims to boost cooperation by better using medical talent abroad. Health Minister Dr. Mustapha Ferjani stated this at a November 11-12, 2026 conference in Cairo focused on expatriate Arab health professionals supporting regional health systems.

Ferjani, presiding over the council, highlighted its role in unifying training standards and specialization criteria across Arab nations. He praised expatriate doctors for contributing to training programs, research, and knowledge transfer, urging them to build lasting connections between their residence countries and homelands.

The minister proposed creating an Arab network of medical simulation centers and an Arab center of excellence for AI in health, offering Tunisia as a host. Tunisia remains committed to strengthening Arab health integration and leveraging expatriate expertise to improve healthcare quality and resilience.

Why it mattersTunisia's health minister proposed new initiatives to connect Arab medical professionals abroad with their home countries.
