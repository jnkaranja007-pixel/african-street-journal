Tunisian students blocked from studying in Romania

Dozens of Tunisian students admitted to Romanian universities have had their visas and passports held by the Romanian embassy in Tunis for weeks.

Dozens of Tunisian students officially admitted to Romanian public universities for the current academic year are facing a critical administrative situation. The students have completed all required procedures, including obtaining acceptance letters from the Romanian Ministry of Education, submitting compliant files, and paying tuition fees in foreign currency. However, their long-stay student visas (D/SD) remain blocked, their applications are frozen without explanation, and their passports have been held by the Romanian embassy in Tunis for several weeks.

This unilateral consular blockage is causing dramatic consequences for the students and their families, threatening academic expulsion and the loss of an entire study year. Families have invested significant financial resources for tuition payments. The prolonged retention of identity documents without legal basis is also seen as an infringement on freedom of movement.

An urgent appeal has been made to Tunisia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Migration and Tunisians Abroad to intervene with the Romanian diplomatic representation in Tunis. The ministry is asked to demand the immediate return of passports and the unblocking of student applications. Romanian authorities are called upon to resolve the contradiction where state universities validate and collect inscriptions while their consulates impede student entry.

Why it mattersTunisian students seeking higher education abroad face academic and financial loss due to blocked visas and retained passports.

Business · 2026-11-15

Tunisia moves to unblock three stalled investment megaprojects

The finance ministry will ask the developer of Tunis Financial Port to revise its timetable and sign a contract amendment.

Tunisia's government is trying to revive three large investment projects that have fallen badly behind schedule: Tunis Financial Port, Tunis Sports City and La Porte de la Méditerranée, better known as Sama Dubai. Economy and Planning Minister Samir Abdelhafidh set out their status on Saturday, 14 November 2026, during a parliamentary budget session at the Bardo palace.

For Tunis Financial Port, whose investment agreement was approved by law 55 of 2008, the ministry has taken the matter to the committee that grants approvals. The developer has been asked to revise its timetable and phases and to propose an amendment to the agreement. For Tunis Sports City, approved by law 31 of 2008, electricity and telecommunications network studies have been validated and funding for a link to the external natural gas network has received an in-principle agreement, though the overall project has not yet been formally launched.

Sama Dubai, a large property and urban development planned around Tunis's southern lake, has been halted since 2011, and the minister confirmed it is among the three projects the ministry is tracking. He gave no further details on measures or a possible restart date. The approvals committee will steer all three projects.

Why it mattersInvestors in the developers' contracts face a revised timetable and contract amendment for Tunis Financial Port, with no restart date yet for Sama Dubai.

Politics · 2026-11-15

Tunisian minister meets diaspora in Ghana

Foreign Minister Mohamed Ali Nafti discussed consular services and bilateral ties with Tunisians in Accra.

Tunisia's Foreign Minister, Mohamed Ali Nafti, met members of the Tunisian community in Accra, Ghana, on Saturday. The meeting took place alongside the 27th extraordinary session of the African Union Executive Council, which the minister attended.

Minister Nafti conveyed President Kaïs Saïed's attention to Tunisians abroad and the ministry's efforts to improve consular services and communication with the diaspora. He also recalled historical ties between Tunisia and Ghana, including their shared roles in liberation struggles.

Tunisians in Ghana expressed their commitment to their country and their role as a bridge between the two nations. They were encouraged to promote Tunisia's image and support national development and bilateral cooperation.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens living abroad were assured of government attention to their concerns and improved consular services.
