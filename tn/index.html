Tunisia launches €60 million green finance programme for small businesses

The facility will channel an initial €40 million through local banks, with EU grants paid to firms after their projects are verified.

Tunisia launched the Green Economy Financing Facility in Tunis on 16 November 2026, with about €60 million to help small and medium-sized businesses invest in energy and environmental improvements. The programme is a partnership between the European Union, the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD) and Tunisian authorities, and was announced by the EU ambassador to Tunisia, Giuseppe Perrone.

In its first phase, €40 million in credit lines will go to Tunisian banks, which will finance the investments of eligible firms. Amen Bank and the Union Bancaire pour le Commerce et l'Industrie (UBCI) each have an initial €10 million line, and a second €10 million agreement with UBCI was signed at the launch. Attijari Bank has signed a memorandum of understanding for a similar operation. Grants of 10 to 15% of eligible investment value are paid to firms after the EU verifies that projects have been completed.

The programme focuses on energy efficiency, renewable energy, sustainable construction and the circular economy, with water management and climate adaptation projects also included. Over time it will be extended to leasing companies and microfinance institutions.

Why it mattersBusinesses that complete verified energy and climate projects receive EU grants of 10 to 15% of their investment, while loans come through local banks.

Tunisia advances Grand Tunis mobility plan toward 2040

The study covers four governorates and will assess transport scenarios before officials select a plan for the region.

Tunisia’s equipment and housing minister, Salah Zouari, reviewed progress on a strategic urban mobility study for Grand Tunis on Tuesday, 17 November. The plan, being developed with support from the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA), is intended to guide transport and urban planning in the region through 2040.

The study, led by the Grand Tunis Urban Planning Agency, covers Tunis, Ariana, Ben Arous and Manouba. A household travel survey conducted from January to March 2026 gathered information from a sample of 7000 households across the four governorates to map current travel patterns and inform future planning scenarios.

Officials are to complete and evaluate the proposed scenarios before choosing one for Grand Tunis. The reference plan is intended to guide public policy and planned investment, while addressing congestion, pollution, travel safety and sustainability.

Why it mattersPublic agencies will use the selected scenario to guide transport policy and investment across four governorates.

Tunisia advances North African drug-regulation coordination in Cairo

At the committee’s second meeting, Tunisia and regional partners reviewed work on inspections and agreed on strategic documents for harmonising medicine procedures.

Tunisia took part on 17 and 18 November in Cairo in the second meeting of a North African initiative to coordinate medicine regulation. The gathering reviewed progress and work by groups covering inspections, pharmacovigilance, quality management and information systems.

The National Medicines and Health Products Agency (ANMPS), which represents Tunisia in the initiative, said the country holds its secretariat. Its director general, Soumaya Miled, represented Tunisia. Participants approved several strategic documents and examined ways to unify procedures for evaluating and registering medicines.

The initiative seeks to improve access to safe, effective and quality medicines while strengthening health security and pharmaceutical sovereignty across North Africa. Its inaugural meeting was held in Cairo from 18 to 20 February 2025, when the steering committee’s bureau was formed and initial strategic directions set.

Why it mattersMedicines regulators across North Africa are examining unified evaluation and registration procedures, with participants approving strategic documents at the Cairo meeting.

Tunis court sentences Leïla Trabelsi’s sister to 21 years

The financial-corruption chamber also imposed heavy fines in a case involving corruption and the appropriation of public funds.

A specialised financial-corruption chamber at Tunis’s Court of First Instance sentenced a sister of Leïla Trabelsi, wife of former president Zine El Abidine Ben Ali, to 21 years in prison on Monday, 16 November 2026. The case concerned financial and administrative corruption and the appropriation of public funds, Webdo reported, citing Mosaique FM.

The woman was tried while at liberty and did not attend Monday’s hearing. Her lawyer sought a postponement, citing health reasons that prevented her from appearing, but the chamber deliberated and issued its ruling. The court also imposed heavy financial fines; the report did not give their amount.

The same chamber sentenced her to four years in January 2026 in a money-laundering and foreign-exchange case. In a separate case over a public contract won by a company run by the woman and her husband, she, her husband and two foreign nationals received six-year terms and fines exceeding 20 million dinars. The four defendants appealed that ruling.

Why it mattersThe woman now faces a 21-year prison term and heavy fines, alongside earlier sentences including a six-year term in a public-contract case.

Tunis hosts six-day Italian-Tunisian arts festival in the medina

The programme runs through 21 November across cultural venues and schools, with most events free to attend.

The second Mediterraneo in movimento festival opened on 16 November at the Sainte-Croix cultural complex in Tunis’s medina, with events also taking place at other cultural venues and educational institutions in the capital. The six-day programme brings together art, music, cinema, theatre, photography and creative workshops focused on Italy and Tunisia.

Organised by Italian association Tararì Tararera Aps and partners in Bentivoglio and Cento, the festival includes an exhibition pairing Tunisian artist Aymen Mbarki with Italian artist Marco Pellizzola. A photography show by Tunisian photographer Nizar Rejeb presents 32 black-and-white images of Rome. Most events are free, and the first edition drew more than 500 people.

Italian director Pietro Marcello is due to lead a masterclass at the Higher Institute of Multimedia Arts on 20 November, followed by a screening of his film Martin Eden at the Tunisian Cinematheque. The festival closes on 21 November with a performance by the Coro delle Mondine of Bentivoglio, a concert by Tunisian musician Ragheb Ouergli and a public gathering.

Why it mattersMost festival events are free, and organisers are seeking to build a lasting cultural cooperation network between Tunisia and Italy.
