Tunisian organic exports reach 741 MD

Organic olive oil and dates drove a 32.7% year-on-year increase in export revenue for Tunisia's organic products.

Tunisian organic product exports generated 740.92 million dinars (MD) in the first nine months of 2026, a 32.7% rise from the previous year.

Organic olive oil accounted for 90.1% of this revenue, with 51,290 tonnes exported, while organic dates contributed 105.8 MD from 9,843.6 tonnes.

These organic goods were sold in 46 countries, with Italy, the United States, and Spain being the top destinations for olive oil, and Germany leading for dates.

Why it mattersTunisian farmers and exporters earned more revenue from organic products sold abroad.

Tunisia forecasts partly cloudy skies and 30°C highs

Temperatures will range from 23°C to 30°C across the country on Friday, October 23, 2026.

Tunisia will experience partly cloudy skies on Friday, October 23, 2026, with sunny spells expected during the day. Maximum temperatures will reach 30°C in the south, while northern and central regions will see highs between 23°C and 27°C. The wind will blow from the southeast with low to moderate intensity.

The national meteorological institute forecasts generally calm seas, becoming locally agitated on some coasts. Overnight, cloudy skies are expected nationwide with scattered showers on southern coastal areas. Strong winds are predicted near the coast, with moderate winds inland.

Nighttime temperatures are forecast between 16°C and 21°C, dropping to around 13°C in the western mountainous zones. The sea will be particularly agitated north of the country overnight.

Why it mattersTunisians will experience varied weather conditions with mild to warm temperatures across the country.

Ailes Cargo launches regular cargo flights to Europe

Tunisian airline Ailes Cargo will begin five weekly cargo flights to Belgium and France on October 25, 2026.

Tunisian airline Ailes Cargo will launch regular air cargo flights to Belgium and France on October 25, 2026.

The company will operate five weekly flights, with four connecting Tunis-Carthage Airport to Liège, Belgium, and one to Paris-Charles-de-Gaulle in France, using two Boeing 737 cargo planes each with a 18-tonne capacity.

These new routes aim to meet the needs of Tunisian industrialists in sectors like automotive, aeronautics, and textiles, reducing their reliance on non-regular freight solutions and limiting foreign currency payments to foreign transport providers.

Why it mattersTunisian businesses in the automotive, aeronautics, and textile sectors will benefit from more reliable and potentially cheaper shipping options for urgent parts and goods.

Tunisia budgets 11.8 billion dinars for 2027 subsidies

Energy will receive nearly 60% of the total, with 7.09 billion dinars allocated.

Tunisia's draft state budget for 2027 allocates 11.846 billion dinars for subsidies, with energy products and electricity set to receive 7.09 billion dinars. Basic goods will be allocated 4.036 billion dinars, and transport will receive 720 million dinars. These figures are based on an assumption of oil priced at $80 per barrel.

The energy sector's allocation represents 59.8% of the total subsidy envelope. The Tunisian Electricity and Gas Company (STEG) is expected to receive 3.676 billion dinars, while the Tunisian Company of Refining Industries (STIR) will get 3.414 billion dinars. These allocations include provisions for settling some accumulated debts of these public companies.

Subsidies for basic goods are projected at 4.036 billion dinars, or 34.1% of the total, a decrease from 2026's projected share. The government plans to improve efficiency, control costs, and combat smuggling and speculation. Efforts will also focus on reducing import dependency and improving water resource management.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens will be affected by the allocation of 11.8 billion dinars in subsidies for energy, basic goods, and transport in the 2027 budget.

Tunisia scientists urge action on climate change impact

Researchers and farmers met in Tunis to discuss translating scientific findings into practical steps to secure food supplies.

Tunisia's Institute for Arid Land Research organized a national seminar in Tunis on October 21-22 to discuss the impact of climate change on food security. The event brought together researchers, academics, farmers, and government officials to explore how to turn scientific research into concrete actions on the ground. The past cereal season yielded 11 million quintals, but officials noted that better results were possible with improved technical practices and climate adaptation.

Radhouane Nsiri, head of department at the National Institute for Major Crops, stated that while the past season had good rainfall overall, some areas experienced drought, leading to lower-than-expected yields. He highlighted that Tunisia ranks 12th globally in scientific research in agriculture, according to Mondher Ben Salem, director of the Tunisian Institute of Agronomic Research. However, Ben Salem pointed out a gap in translating this scientific advancement into practical plans that benefit the agricultural sector and improve farmers' livelihoods.

The seminar emphasized the need to implement scientific findings to mitigate climate change effects on agricultural production and ensure long-term food security. Ben Salem stressed that relying on national production is crucial, especially given potential international supply uncertainties. He also differentiated between food security and agricultural sovereignty, advocating for a focus on domestic output to achieve self-sufficiency.

Why it mattersTunisian farmers and the nation's food security are directly affected by climate change impacts on crop yields and the implementation of scientific research.
