Algeria Converts Desert Barracks into High-Security Prison for Drug Barons

The High Security Council has decided to transform a military barracks in the remote Tanezrouft desert into a high-security facility for drug traffickers.

Algeria is intensifying its response to drug trafficking with a decision to convert a military barracks in the Tanezrouft desert into a high-security prison. The High Security Council, led by President Abdelmadjid Tebboune, made the announcement on Wednesday as part of a broader national strategy to combat drug and psychotropic trafficking. This move aims to strengthen coordination and capabilities against major trafficking networks.

The chosen location, Tanezrouft, is an isolated and inhospitable region in southwestern Algeria, near the Malian border, known for extreme temperatures and vast arid landscapes. The facility will be established within an existing military structure, not a new construction. Details regarding the prison's capacity, operational start date, and the specific categories of inmates have not yet been disclosed by the presidency.

In addition to the new prison, Algeria will establish a specialized security agency focused on combating drug trafficking, modeled after international bodies like the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration. This dual approach signifies the government's commitment to a more targeted and robust strategy against significant drug trafficking organizations operating within and through the country.

Why it mattersDrug traffickers and barons will be incarcerated in a high-security prison located in the remote Tanezrouft desert.

EU Funds Aid for Tunisian Fire Victims

The European Union has released two hundred thousand euros to support communities impacted by severe fires.

The European Union has allocated two hundred thousand euros in emergency humanitarian funding for Tunisian communities affected by severe fires that began in early June. This financial aid will bolster the efforts of the Tunisian Red Crescent, supported by the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies, to address a crisis exacerbated by high temperatures, dry vegetation, and strong winds. The funding is intended to provide essential relief and support to those most vulnerable.

According to the Tunisian Red Crescent, fires have already destroyed approximately twelve thousand hectares of forests and agricultural land this season, impacting nearly forty-one thousand four hundred seventy-nine people. These figures include individuals directly affected by the flames and those experiencing disruptions to electricity and water supplies. The fires have damaged or destroyed homes, businesses, farmlands, and fruit trees, severely affecting rural and forest communities reliant on agriculture and natural resources.

The European Union delegation in Tunis stated the funding will facilitate the provision of shelter, essential household items, food aid, financial assistance, and psychosocial support. Activities will also cover water, sanitation, hygiene, and local community engagement. This six-month operation, running until the end of February two thousand twenty-seven, aims to reach fifteen thousand directly affected individuals and is part of the EU's contribution to the IFRC's Disaster Relief Emergency Fund.

Why it mattersThe European Union's two hundred thousand euro aid package will directly assist Tunisian communities affected by recent fires by providing shelter, food, and financial support.

Tunisian Court Rejects Appeals in State Security Plot Case

The Court of Cassation upheld convictions for 44 individuals in a case involving accusations of plotting against state security.

Tunis, Tunisia – The Court of Cassation in Tunis has finalized the "conspiracy against state security 1" case by rejecting 46 appeals. On Thursday, September 3, 2026, the court dismissed 44 appeals on their merits and two on procedural grounds, according to judicial sources cited by TAP. This decision concludes a multi-year legal process that has seen significant political figures, businessmen, lawyers, and media personalities facing charges.

The case, which began in February 2023, involved accusations of conspiracy against internal and external state security, forming and joining a terrorist group, and inciting disorder and violence. Some charges also related to undermining food security and the environment. In a previous appeal ruling on November 28, 2025, the Tunis Court of Appeal had issued sentences ranging from five to 45 years imprisonment for those convicted.

Notable figures among those facing charges included Ahmed Néjib Chebbi, Chayma Issa, and Jaouhar Ben Mbarek, with sentences varying significantly from initial rulings. For instance, Kamel Letaïef's sentence was reduced from 66 to 45 years, while Khayam Turki's was reduced from 48 to 35 years. Noureddine Bhiri saw his sentence reduced from 43 to 20 years.

Why it mattersThe definitive rejection of appeals means that the sentences handed down in the state security plot case are now final, impacting the freedom and legal standing of over 40 individuals.

Tunisia Phosphate Uranium Powers Nuclear Energy Plan

Tunisia considers using uranium from its phosphate reserves to build a national nuclear power plant amid a structural energy deficit.

Tunisia faces a severe structural energy deficit, leading to social and economic disruptions like frequent power outages, increased industrial costs, and reduced purchasing power. This crisis is exacerbated by climate change impacts on water resources and consumption peaks, volatile global hydrocarbon markets affecting public finances, and slow progress in renewable energy transitions that are unlikely to meet current demand.

In response to this urgent situation, the idea of utilizing uranium found in Tunisian phosphates to fuel a national nuclear power plant is being presented not as a temporary fix, but as a historic opportunity for strategic change. The country possesses sufficient phosphate reserves containing uranium, which could provide a stable, controllable, and local energy source, potentially driving long-term energy, water, industrial, and geopolitical sovereignty.

Recent European experiences highlight the risks of poor long-term energy planning, with regrets over nuclear phase-outs and renewable energy delays contributing to unprecedented energy cost increases. Germany's economic struggles, marked by industrial bankruptcies, are linked to questionable energy choices and dependence on Russian gas. Tunisia is questioning whether to follow such failed models or secure its future with a stable, local energy source instead of relying on volatile global imports.

Why it mattersTunisia's industrial sector and overall economy are affected by the proposed nuclear energy plan, which aims to stabilize energy costs and ensure energy independence.

Tunisia Adjusts Poultry Production After Heatwave Losses

The Ministry of Agriculture will assess damages from July and August heatwaves, which caused poultry mortality, slow growth, and reduced productivity.

Tunisia's agriculture ministry is preparing to adjust poultry production following significant losses incurred during an exceptional heatwave in July and August. The extreme temperatures led to poultry mortality, stunted growth, and decreased productivity, particularly affecting broiler farms. This situation was exacerbated by summer electricity and water supply disruptions, which hampered essential ventilation and cooling systems in many small and medium-sized farms.

Salwa Dhaouadi, from the Directorate General of Agricultural Production, stated that the ministry will collaborate with sector stakeholders to precisely evaluate the recorded losses. This assessment aims to determine necessary measures for production adjustments. While some slaughterhouses also experienced disruptions, professionals indicate that the current monthly production drop does not yet constitute a widespread supply crisis, according to the National Chamber of Poultry and White Meat Merchants.

Beyond immediate loss assessment, the ministry plans to learn from this climatic event by adapting farms to climate change and enhancing their resilience to power interruptions. Investing in renewable energy sources for farms is being considered as a key strategy to ensure the continuous operation of critical equipment like ventilation and cooling systems, vital for maintaining suitable conditions for poultry.

Why it mattersPoultry farmers face production losses and potential adjustments due to heatwave impacts and infrastructure failures.
