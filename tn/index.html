STEG Announces New Power Cuts Wednesday

The Tunisian electricity and gas company will implement rotating power outages between noon and five p.m. due to network strain.

The Société tunisienne de l’électricité et du gaz (STEG) announced potential rotating power cuts for Wednesday, September 2, 2026, between noon and five p.m. These intermittent interruptions aim to maintain the electrical system's safety and continuity, depending on network conditions and the balance between production and consumption. The STEG noted that power could be restored without prior notice, a measure that has become frequent during periods of high electricity demand, particularly due to elevated temperatures.

Several regions across the country are expected to be affected, including Grand Tunis, Cap Bon, Zaghouan, Bizerte, the Center (Sousse, Monastir, Mahdia, Kairouan), Sfax, the South (Gabès, Médenine, Djerba), and the Southwest (Gafsa, Tozeur, Kasserine). The STEG cautioned that the list of affected areas might change based on the network's status. These cuts are designed to prevent further degradation of the national electrical system, following a summer marked by similar disruptions.

In parallel, the Société nationale d’exploitation et de distribution des eaux (Sonede) reported water supply disruptions in southern Tunis, Ben Arous, Ezzahra, Zaghouan, and Grombalia. These issues stem from a sudden technical failure on a main water pipe and a consumption surge due to a heatwave. Full water supply restoration is anticipated by ten p.m. on Wednesday, September 2, 2026, after repair work is completed.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in multiple regions of Tunisia face disruptions to both electricity and water services, impacting daily life and operations.

Tunis Supermarkets Distribute 2.3 Million Water Bottles Daily

Supermarkets in Greater Tunis are distributing 2.3 million bottles of mineral water daily to meet unprecedented market demand.

Supermarkets across Greater Tunis are now distributing approximately 2.3 million bottles of mineral water each day to address significant market pressure. Hedi Baccour, president of the Chamber of Commerce of Large Surfaces, announced this exceptional measure to ease supply tensions. This comes as hypermarkets typically account for only 5% of national bottled water consumption, highlighting the current extraordinary demand.

The surge in demand occurred during a period when the national regulatory stock, usually holding between 40 and 50 million bottles, was depleted before mid-July. This critical situation has prompted producers to consider increasing the volume of this strategic stock to prevent future shortages. The regulation of distribution channels nationwide was recently discussed in a meeting between industry professionals and the Ministry of Commerce.

This increased distribution in Greater Tunis, reaching over 2.3 million bottles daily between Friday and Monday, August 31, underscores the intense pressure on the market this summer. Despite these large volumes, supermarkets represent only 5% of national consumption, indicating that the supply issues are not solely attributable to large retailers but rather to the overall balance of production, storage, transport, and distribution.

Why it mattersConsumers in Greater Tunis are affected by increased daily distribution of bottled water due to high demand and depleted national stocks.

Tunisian Treasury Bonds Grow 25.45% to 37.5 Billion Dinars

Tunisia's state debt has accelerated its reliance on domestic financing, with treasury bonds reaching 37.512 billion dinars by August 2026.

Tunisia's total outstanding treasury bonds reached 37.512 billion dinars by the end of August 2026, marking a significant increase of 7.611 billion dinars over the past year. This represents a 25.45% rise, indicating the government's growing dependence on domestic borrowing. Medium and long-term instruments, known as Assimilable Treasury Bonds (BTA), constitute over 95% of this total, with short-term bonds (BTCT) making up the remainder.

The dominance of BTAs is evident, accounting for 35.803 billion dinars of the total, while short-term bonds amount to 1.709 billion dinars. This structure highlights that state financing is primarily secured through longer-term debt instruments. This acceleration in domestic financing occurs as bank credit to the economy also shows signs of recovery, growing by 2.4% in the first half of 2026.

While bank credit to the economy has increased, it is not yet clear if the expansion of public securities is directly hindering economic financing. The total outstanding amount of 37.512 billion dinars reflects all treasury bonds in circulation. For comparison, banks held 25.7 billion dinars in treasury bonds at the end of 2025, a 39.4% annual increase, but this figure specifically represents the banking sector's investments and not the total public debt.

Why it mattersTunisia's government is increasingly relying on domestic borrowing through treasury bonds to finance its needs, impacting the financial landscape.

Tunisian Police Arrest Suspect in 17 Cases

Security forces in Ghardimaou apprehended a man wanted for drug trafficking, theft, and assault.

Security units in Ghardimaou, Jendouba, have arrested a man described as dangerous, who was the subject of 17 arrest warrants. The individual is sought for drug trafficking, theft, robbery, and assault, according to a security source cited by Jawhara FM. The operation was the result of a planned ambush coordinated with the public prosecutor's office.

Forces conducted a dawn raid on Wednesday, leading to the suspect's capture. During the intervention, authorities seized a significant quantity of resin cannabis, mobile phones, and cash believed to be proceeds from illicit activities. The operation aimed to dismantle a drug trafficking network operating in the region.

The arrest is part of ongoing efforts by Tunisian security forces to combat organized crime and enhance public safety. The suspect will face charges related to the multiple offenses for which he was sought. Further investigations are expected to uncover additional details about his criminal activities.

Why it mattersThe arrest of a suspect involved in drug trafficking and theft affects the safety and security of residents in the Ghardimaou region.

Tunisia Wind Farm Secures Japanese Carbon Credit Subsidy

The 75 MW El Fahs wind project will receive Japanese funding through a bilateral carbon credit mechanism, boosting its financial structure.

Tunisia's 75 MW El Fahs wind farm, located in the Zaghouan governorate, has been selected to receive a Japanese subsidy under the bilateral carbon credit mechanism between the two countries. The announcement was made on September 2nd by the Japanese Embassy in Tunisia. This development marks a significant step for the project, which is being developed by Eurus Energy and Scatec. The wind farm is expected to prevent approximately 88,367 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent emissions annually.

The project's selection for the Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM) in July paves the way for Japanese financial support to aid its realization. While the exact subsidy amount has not been disclosed, this backing is separate from the 25-year power purchase agreement already secured by Scatec. The JCM mechanism aims to support projects that reduce greenhouse gas emissions, aligning low-carbon energy infrastructure development with emission reduction credits.

The 75 MW onshore wind farm's capacity, developers, and power purchase agreement were previously known. The crucial new element is its eligibility for Japanese carbon credit mechanism funding, which is intended to strengthen the project's financial setup and facilitate its construction. This selection does not yet provide a precise timeline for the project's commissioning or the final subsidy amount.

Why it mattersThe El Fahs wind farm project is affected by receiving Japanese funding through a carbon credit mechanism, which will support its financial structure and realization.
