Tunisia energy deficit hits 10.48 billion dinars

Foreign exchange reserves fell to cover 93 days of imports by October 23, 2026.

Tunisia's energy trade deficit reached 10.48 billion dinars, nearly half of the country's total trade deficit. Foreign exchange reserves dropped to cover 93 days of imports as of October 23, 2026, down from 107 days a year earlier. These figures were presented on October 29 by Finance Minister Mechkat Salma Khaldi during a budget execution hearing.

Imports grew by 13.1% while exports increased by 8.6%, widening the trade balance pressures and increasing external financing needs. The energy deficit alone accounted for approximately 50% of the overall trade deficit. The minister also noted potential impacts from the Middle East geopolitical crisis on trade and energy prices.

Despite these challenges, tourism revenue rose by 3.7% and remittances from Tunisians abroad increased by 4.4%, helping to finance part of the external deficit. The government is also developing a plan to integrate informal economic activities into the legal framework to broaden the formal economic base and improve tax compliance.

Why it mattersTunisia's economy faces significant pressure from a large energy deficit and declining foreign exchange reserves, impacting its financial stability.

Tunisian journalists' union and women's group pledge cooperation

The National Syndicate of Tunisian Journalists and the Tunisian Association of Democratic Women met on October 29, 2026, to discuss press freedom.

The president of the National Syndicate of Tunisian Journalists (SNJT), Zied Dabbar, met with a delegation from the Tunisian Association of Democratic Women (ATFD) on Thursday, October 29, 2026. The ATFD delegation, led by its president Raja Dahmani, congratulated the SNJT on its new executive bureau.

During the meeting, the ATFD reaffirmed its solidarity with journalists and its support for initiatives defending freedom of the press and expression. Both organizations agreed to strengthen their cooperation to protect fundamental rights and combat restrictions and prosecutions targeting media professionals, according to an SNJT statement.

The two groups also discussed the state of freedoms in Tunisia and the challenges facing the journalism profession. This meeting occurred on the same day the head of government, Sarra Zaafrani Zenzri, met with the Tunisian community in Russia to discuss their contributions to national development.

Why it mattersJournalists and media professionals in Tunisia face restrictions and prosecutions, and their freedoms are under discussion.

Tunisia braces for thunderstorms and scattered rain

The national weather service forecasts localized storms and scattered showers across the country on Friday, October 30, 2026.

Tunisia will experience cloudy skies on Friday, October 30, 2026, with a risk of localized thunderstorms in the morning and scattered rain, particularly along the coast. Maximum temperatures will range between 22 and 26 degrees Celsius depending on the region. The wind will blow from the northwest at a weak to moderate intensity across the territory, and the sea will be slightly to moderately rough along the coasts.

The Institute National de la Météorologie (INM) predicted scattered, temporarily stormy showers for Thursday, October 29, 2026, which could be locally abundant with hail in some areas. These rains were expected to spread from the North to the Center and South throughout the day. Maximum temperatures on Thursday were forecast between 20 and 25 degrees Celsius, reaching 27 degrees Celsius in the Southwest.

On Thursday, the wind was expected to blow from the northwest in the North and Center, and from the east in the South. It could strengthen temporarily during stormy episodes, especially in the far South. The sea was predicted to be slightly agitated.

Why it mattersTunisians across the country face potential disruptions from thunderstorms, scattered rain, and hail on October 29 and 30, 2026.

Club Africain beats Bizertin 3-2 in Ligue 1

Espérance de Tunis drew 0-0 with Club Sportif Sfaxien, while Stade Tunisien won 1-0.

Club Africain secured a 3-2 victory over Club Athlétique Bizertin in a delayed Ligue 1 match on Thursday, October 29. This win allows Club Africain, who had nine points from four games, to improve their league position after their African Champions League qualification. The defeat ended Club Athlétique Bizertin's positive run, which included a 3-1 win against ES Hammam-Sousse.

In Sfax, Club Sportif Sfaxien and Espérance de Tunis played to a goalless draw. Espérance de Tunis, with 12 points from five matches before this game, and Club Sportif Sfaxien, with eight points, both failed to gain ground in the race for the top places. Stade Tunisien returned to winning ways with a 1-0 victory over Espérance de Zarzis.

Espérance de Tunis missed an opportunity to extend their lead with the draw, while Club Sportif Sfaxien also failed to gain ground. Stade Tunisien's win follows a previous loss to US Ben Guerdane and aids their league progression. These matches were part of the sixth round of Ligue 1.

Why it mattersTunisian football fans following the domestic league will be interested in these results and their impact on team standings.

Tunisia podcasts festival returns after three-year break

The Podcast Days event will feature creators from Algeria and Marseille, celebrating sound creation.

The Tunisia Podcasts Festival, known as Podcast Days, will take place in Tunis on Friday, November 6 and Saturday, November 7, 2026. The event will be held at the auditorium of the Institut français de Tunisie (IFT) and L’Art Rue (Dar Bach Hamba) in the medina. This festival celebrates sound creation through meetings, masterclasses, round tables, public recordings, and immersive experiences.

Entry to the festival is free upon registration, following a three-day event in December 2023 that gathered podcasters, listeners, and professionals. This year's event is more condensed, featuring an opening evening, a full day, and a closing evening. Guests from Algiers and Marseille are expected to attend.

Raouia Kheder, founder of the Tunisia Podcasts Festival, stated that Tunisian podcasts have grown significantly since 2023, finding their voices, audiences, and models. She added that the Podcast Days aim to provide a space for creators to meet, learn, and observe work from across the Mediterranean.

Why it mattersTunisian podcast creators and listeners can engage with international peers and learn about new trends in sound creation.
