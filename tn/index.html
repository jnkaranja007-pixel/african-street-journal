Tunisia energy deficit hits 10.48 billion dinars

Foreign exchange reserves fell to cover 93 days of imports by October 23, 2026.

Tunisia's energy trade deficit reached 10.48 billion dinars, nearly half of the total trade deficit, as foreign exchange reserves dropped to cover 93 days of imports by October 23, 2026. This compares to 107 days of import cover a year earlier. The figures were presented on October 29 by Finance Minister Mechkat Salma Khaldi during a budget execution hearing.

Minister Khaldi stated that economic indicators show relative stability despite challenges. Imports rose 13.1% while exports grew 8.6%, increasing pressure on the trade balance and external financing needs. The energy deficit alone accounted for approximately 50% of the overall trade deficit.

Tourism revenue increased by 3.7% and remittances from Tunisians abroad rose by 4.4%, helping to finance part of the external deficit. However, the geopolitical crisis in the Middle East could impact trade and energy prices. The government plans to integrate the informal sector into the legal framework to boost formal economic activity and tax revenue.

Why it mattersTunisia faces a significant energy deficit and declining foreign exchange reserves, impacting the national economy.

Tunisia podcasts festival returns after three years

The event will feature creators from Algeria and Marseille, alongside Tunisian podcasters.

The Tunisia Podcasts Festival, known as Podcast Days, will take place in Tunis on Friday, November 6, and Saturday, November 7, 2026. The festival will be held at the Institut français de Tunisie and L’Art Rue, celebrating sound creation through meetings, masterclasses, and public recordings. Entry is free but requires registration.

This marks the festival's return after a three-year absence, with a more condensed format than its 2023 debut. Raouia Kheder, the festival's founder, stated that Tunisian podcasts have grown significantly since 2023, finding their voices and audiences. The event aims to provide a space for creators to connect and observe international trends.

Guests from Algiers and Marseille are expected to attend, highlighting the event's continental and cross-Mediterranean reach. The program includes a mix of professional development and immersive experiences for attendees.

Why it mattersTunisian podcasters and sound creators will benefit from networking and learning opportunities at the festival.

Club Africain beats CAB 3-2 in delayed match

Hamza Khadraoui scored twice and added an assist to help Club Africain secure a vital win.

Club Africain secured a dramatic 3-2 victory over Club Athlétique Bizertin (CAB) on Thursday, October 29, in a rescheduled sixth-round Ligue 1 match. The win allows Club Africain to improve its standing in the league table. Hamza Khadraoui was instrumental, scoring two goals and providing an assist. This victory follows Club Africain's qualification for the African Champions League group stage.

The match against CAB, who had won their previous game 3-1, ended a positive run for the visitors. In another delayed fixture, Club Sportif Sfaxien (CSS) and Espérance de Tunis played to a goalless draw (0-0) at the Taïeb Mhiri stadium. Before this round, Espérance de Tunis had 12 points from five matches, while CSS had eight points from four games.

The third delayed match saw Stade Tunisien defeat Espérance de Zarzis 1-0. This win marks a return to form for Stade Tunisien after a previous loss. The results mean neither CSS nor Espérance de Tunis made a decisive move in the race for the top positions in the league.

Why it mattersTunisian football fans will be interested in the performance of their clubs in the national league.

Korbous thermal springs treat 92% Maghreb visitors

Tunisia's Korbous station uses 2000-year-old Aïn Echfa source for rheumatism and respiratory ailments.

The small town of Korbous, 60 km from Tunisia's capital, offers seven thermal springs for health tourists. The main source, Aïn Echfa, also known as La grande Source, has been used for over 2000 years and is exploited by the Korbous thermal station at 57.5°C.

Aïn Echfa's waters are recommended for rheumatism, respiratory, and dermatological issues, treating conditions like arthrosis and eczema. The spa facility, which operates separately for men and women, hosts patients covered by the Cnam national health insurance and has agreements with French veterans and Tunisian companies.

92 percent of Korbous's visitors are from the Maghreb, with 70% being Tunisians, while 8% are European tourists, primarily German and French. The facility undergoes monthly hygiene checks by the Office du thermalisme and regional health agents.

Why it mattersTunisians and other Maghreb residents benefit from Korbous's thermal waters for treating various health conditions.

Luca Bianchini novel explores Tunisian family life

The book "Le ragazze di Tunisi" details Sicilian emigration to Tunisia and a mother's struggles in the 1959-1961 period.

Luca Bianchini's novel "Le ragazze di Tunisi" portrays the lives of the Brancata family, Sicilian emigrants in cosmopolitan Tunisia between 1959 and 1961. The story follows Maria, a seamstress raising three daughters as Tunisia approaches independence. Bianchini's work captures the city of Tunis suspended between past charm and the dawn of independence, weaving together first loves and the impending farewells.

The novel focuses on Maria, a 38-year-old seamstress in the Borgel neighborhood, who supports her family while her husband works in the countryside. Her eldest daughter, Anna, 16, attends a good school thanks to her uncle Jojo and dreams of love, while her sisters Vitina and Pupetta have their own preoccupations. Anna finds companionship with her French friend Marinette, who introduces her to cinema and fashion.

Bianchini researched his mother's family history for the novel, noting that the Brancatas, like many Sicilian families in Tunisia, sought opportunity. The family lived harmoniously with French residents, Tunisians, Jews, and Maltese. The narrative captures a period of transition, with the approaching independence of Tunisia signaling an imminent departure for many.

Why it mattersThe novel "Le ragazze di Tunisi" by Luca Bianchini explores the lives of Sicilian emigrant families in Tunisia during a period of significant political change.
