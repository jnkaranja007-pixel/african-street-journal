UGTT announces September strike for fuel transporters

The private sector department of the Tunisian General Labor Union (UGTT) issued a strike notice for September 23 and 24, 2026, affecting hydrocarbon transport workers nationwide.

The UGTT's private sector department announced a strike for hydrocarbon transport workers on September 23 and 24, 2026. This action stems from the administrative parties' alleged failure to implement prior agreements, including one from May 2, 2019, and legal obligations like social security coverage with the National Social Security Fund (CNSS). The notice was sent to several ministries and industry representatives.

Conversely, the General Federation of Oil and Chemical Products, through its Secretary General Selim Sehimi, denied any new strike was scheduled or notice filed as of August 26, 2026. Sehimi stated that negotiations with supervisory authorities had not yet begun, and a strike could only occur at least ten days after an official notice, which had not been issued.

This conflicting information follows a brief work stoppage by hydrocarbon truck drivers on August 20, which disrupted fuel supplies to several service stations before being lifted the same evening. The ongoing lack of negotiations between the federation and the ministries of Energy, Mines, and Social Affairs leaves the social conflict unresolved.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens could face fuel shortages and long queues at petrol stations if the announced strike proceeds, impacting their daily commutes and access to essential services.

STEG announces new power cuts across Tunisia this Thursday

The Tunisian Electricity and Gas Company (STEG) will implement rotating power outages between 9h30 and 18h today to balance production and consumption.

The Tunisian Electricity and Gas Company (STEG) has announced new rotating electricity cuts across several regions of the country this Thursday, August twenty-seventh. These interruptions, which could occur intermittently between 9h30 and 18h, aim to maintain the security and continuity of the electrical system by balancing available production with consumption. In Greater Tunis, any single outage period is not expected to exceed two hours.

This measure follows similar episodes in recent days, including yesterday, Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, when STEG announced rotating cuts between 11h30 and 18h, and then again between 21h and midnight. The company stated that the list of affected zones is subject to change based on network conditions and the balance between production and consumption.

Numerous areas are affected, including Mornag, Borj Cédria, Radès, La Goulette, Ben Arous, Mégrine, Cité Ghazala, Mhamdia, Le Kram, El Medina El Jadida, Jardins d’El Menzah, Borj Touil, Sanhaja, Jadaida, Manouba, Aïn Zaghouan, Ennasr, and La Soukra in Greater Tunis. Other regions like Cap Bon, Zaghouan, Bizerte, the Northwest, Center, Sfax, South, and Southwest are also included in STEG's advisories, with residents advised to take precautions.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in the listed regions will experience power outages, disrupting daily activities and operations.

Wildfires kill twelve people in northern Algeria

Forest fires raging since Wednesday have claimed at least twelve lives across the wilayas of Jijel, Béjaïa, and Tizi Ouzou in northern Algeria, according to the Interior Minister.

At least twelve people have died in forest fires that have been active since Wednesday across several regions in northern Algeria. Interior Minister Saïd Sayoud reported five deaths in Jijel, four in Béjaïa, and three in Tizi Ouzou. Dozens more have been injured, with 54 people suffering burns in Béjaïa, six of whom are in serious condition and receiving care at Souk El Tenine hospital.

The fires have forced multiple families to evacuate their homes in Béjaïa and Jijel. Local media showed residents fleeing areas threatened by flames and smoke. In Tizi Ouzou, local media reported that three women from the same family died from smoke inhalation in Boghni. The progression of the fires has also led to road closures in the eastern part of the country, with the National Gendarmerie advising motorists to avoid affected areas near Béjaïa, Jijel, and Sétif.

As of Wednesday evening, 69 out of 159 reported vegetation fires were still active across several wilayas, according to the Civil Protection. Béjaïa had the highest number of active fires with 18, followed by Skikda with 13, Tizi Ouzou with 12, and Jijel with seven. Strong winds are complicating firefighting efforts, which are ongoing amidst high temperatures and drought conditions conducive to fire spread.

Why it mattersResidents in the affected wilayas face immediate dangers from the fires, including loss of life, injuries, displacement from homes, and disruption to daily life and travel.

Tunisian Wrestler Chahd Jeljeli Disappears from Mediterranean Games in Taranto

Twenty-year-old Tunisian wrestler Chahd Jeljeli has been reported missing since Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, during her participation in the Mediterranean Games in Taranto.

The head of the official Tunisian delegation, Hatem Marnaoui, confirmed on Thursday, August twenty-seventh, that the delegation has had no news of the athlete. Jeljeli had competed on Tuesday for the bronze medal in women's wrestling in the 57 kg category against Italy's Fabiana Rinella.

According to Marnaoui, after her defeat, Jeljeli remained with national coach Mohamed Ali Bouziane to watch other matches. She then reportedly left the venue without informing her coach, who was busy following other competitions amidst a large crowd. Her coach and teammates searched for her in the hall but could not find her, assuming she might have returned to the Mediterranean Village alone due to the numerous shuttles available.

Upon reaching the Mediterranean Village, the Tunisian team found no trace of the wrestler, with all her personal belongings still in her room. Marnaoui stated he was informed of her disappearance late Tuesday evening and subsequently contacted Tunisian authorities, the Tunisian National Olympic Committee, the Games organizing committee, and security services at the village. No official information regarding the circumstances or reasons for her disappearance has been released.

Why it mattersThe disappearance of a prominent athlete during an international competition impacts the safety and well-being of participants and raises concerns for their families.

Galiton Lighthouse Switches to Solar Power, Ending Diesel Reliance

Tunisia's Galiton lighthouse, a critical maritime signal in the Mediterranean, converted from diesel generators to solar energy in the mid-1990s, improving operational efficiency.

The Galiton lighthouse, a 14-meter structure on a rocky islet 168 meters above the Mediterranean, transitioned from diesel-powered generators to solar energy in the mid-1990s. This modernization involved installing photovoltaic panels and batteries designed for extended autonomy, even without direct sunlight. Previously, diesel fuel had to be transported to the remote islet and then to the lighthouse buildings, a process made difficult by rough seas.

This technological shift ended decades of logistical challenges associated with supplying the lighthouse. Military helicopters were reportedly used to transport some of the new equipment, first to La Galite island and then to Galiton, according to a direct witness of the operations. The lighthouse, one of North Africa's most spectacular maritime signals, has been in service since either May 1919 or 1920, depending on the source.

The Tunisian maritime signaling system, including the Galiton lighthouse, is managed by the Service des Phares et Balises under the Ministry of Defense and the National Navy. Galiton is part of Tunisia's twenty historic lighthouses, mostly built between the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, which are considered national heritage sites.

Why it mattersThe change to solar power at the Galiton lighthouse affects maritime safety for ships navigating Tunisian waters by ensuring continuous operation of a critical signal.
