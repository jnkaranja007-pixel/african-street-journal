Lawyer detained amid drug bust with 8kg cocaine seizure

Judicial sources confirmed lawyer Ghazi Mrabet's detention following a raid that uncovered significant narcotics and cash.

Tunisian authorities have detained lawyer Ghazi Mrabet for five days following a judicial order. A judicial source confirmed that Mrabet was apprehended alongside a man wanted for drug trafficking and already serving a 40-year sentence. The detention occurred after security forces searched the wanted man's residence, seizing over eight kilograms of cocaine and four kilograms of cannabis. Approximately 394,000 dinars in cash were also confiscated during the operation.

According to the judicial source, Ghazi Mrabet was present with the wanted individual at the time of the raid. The lawyer is accused of attempting to obstruct the security forces' intervention, allegedly to facilitate the escape of the wanted man. Both individuals were ultimately arrested, and investigations are ongoing to clarify the exact circumstances and potential responsibilities in the case.

The arrests and subsequent detention have drawn reactions from other legal professionals. Several lawyers have publicly stated their concerns on social media, reminding others of the professional prerogatives afforded to legal practitioners. The ongoing investigations aim to fully determine the facts surrounding the incident and the extent of any involvement.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the seizure of significant narcotics and cash, alongside the detention of a lawyer, may indicate broader instability or corruption impacting business operations.

Ooredoo Tunisie and Club Africain renew long-term sports sponsorship

The telecommunications company and historic football club extend their partnership, first established in 2008, to support athletic ambitions and youth initiatives.

Ooredoo Tunisie announced on Thursday the renewal of its sponsorship with the Club Africain, a collaboration dating back to 2008. The companies describe this as one of Tunisia's most enduring sports sponsorships. The renewed agreement aims to support the club's athletic activities and develop new initiatives for supporters and young people. This partnership is intended to bolster the Club Africain's national and continental sporting goals.

Eyas Assaf, Ooredoo Tunisie's General Manager, highlighted the importance of continuity, stating that successful partnerships are built on trust, consistency, and a shared vision. He emphasized Ooredoo's commitment to supporting the Club Africain and investing in initiatives that foster the development of Tunisian sports and create a lasting positive impact, particularly for youth empowerment. Ooredoo views sport as a platform for ambition, perseverance, and teamwork.

Club Africain President Mohsen Trabelsi expressed pride in continuing the historic partnership, noting that the renewal will enable the club to further its development and strengthen its capabilities to meet its sporting ambitions. He underscored the shared vision developed over the years, stating that continuing the journey with Ooredoo is a natural step based on common objectives. The collaboration is seen as strategic, aligning with shared values of ambition, excellence, and commitment to youth and Tunisian sports.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the continued sponsorship which supports a major sports club, potentially impacting brand visibility and market engagement within Tunisia.

Tunisian Court Rejects Appeals in State Security Plot Case

The Court of Cassation upheld convictions in a case involving accusations of conspiracy against state security, with sentences ranging up to 45 years.

Tunisia's Court of Cassation has finalized the state security plot case by rejecting 46 appeals. The court dismissed 44 appeals on their merits and two on procedural grounds, confirming earlier rulings. This decision concludes a multi-year legal process stemming from an investigation launched in February 2023. The case initially involved over 40 individuals, including politicians, business figures, lawyers, and media personalities, accused of plotting against state security, forming a terrorist group, and inciting disorder.

The appeals followed a November 2025 appeals court ruling that imposed sentences from five to 45 years for defendants, with some receiving acquittals. Notable figures like Kamel Letaïef, Khayam Turki, and Noureddine Bhiri saw their sentences adjusted in the appellate court. Letaïef's sentence, for instance, was reduced from 66 to 45 years, while Turki's went from 48 to 35 years, and Bhiri's from 43 to 20 years.

The Courant démocrate (Attayar) party criticized the court's decision, labeling the trial a political process aimed at silencing dissent rather than ensuring justice. The party called for public protest against what it described as the instrumentalization of the judiciary for political retribution and urged democratic forces to defend judicial independence and civil liberties.

Why it mattersThe finalization of the state security plot case affects political figures and business individuals accused of conspiring against the state, solidifying their convictions and sentences.

Tunisia Phosphate Uranium Powers Nuclear Energy Plan

Tunisia considers using uranium from its phosphate reserves to build a national nuclear power plant amid a structural energy deficit.

Tunisia faces a structural energy deficit causing severe social and economic disruptions, including frequent power outages, increased industrial costs, and reduced purchasing power. This crisis is exacerbated by climate change impacts on water resources and consumption peaks, global hydrocarbon market volatility affecting public finances, and slow progress in renewable energy transition, which is unlikely to cover the current deficit.

In response to this urgent situation, the idea of utilizing uranium present in Tunisian phosphates to fuel a national nuclear power plant is being proposed not as a temporary fix, but as a strategic opportunity for energy independence. The country possesses sufficient phosphate reserves containing uranium, which could drive long-term energy, water, industrial, and geopolitical sovereignty.

Recent European experiences highlight the risks of poor long-term energy planning, with regrets over nuclear phase-outs contributing to soaring energy costs and economic fragility, as seen in Germany's industrial struggles linked to energy choices and dependence on Russian gas. Tunisia is questioning whether to repeat failed models or secure its future with a stable, local energy source instead of relying on volatile global gas imports.

Why it mattersTunisia's potential shift to nuclear energy derived from its phosphate reserves could offer a stable, local power source, impacting industrial costs and national economic stability.

Tunisian Entrepreneurs Win Social Entrepreneurship Awards

Anis Ben Ghalia's CHITELIX project took first prize at the 16th annual POESAM awards in Tunis, recognizing innovative social and environmental solutions.

Orange Tunisie announced the national laureates for the 16th Prix Orange de l’Entrepreneur Social en Afrique et au Moyen-Orient (POESAM) on Thursday, September 3, in Tunis. Anis Ben Ghalia's CHITELIX project, focused on valorizing blue crab waste for sustainable biomaterials, secured the top national honor. Amal Chebbi's PigmentOCO, a sustainable textile technology initiative, earned second place and the National Women's Prize, while Nejah Cherif's HandiSuccess International, a digital platform for disabled individuals' professional inclusion, took third.

This year's competition saw 156 projects submitted to Orange Tunisie, spanning sectors such as health, education, agriculture, energy, and environment, all leveraging technology to address socio-economic and environmental challenges. Six finalists presented their initiatives to a jury of entrepreneurial, technological, and investment experts. The national winners receive cash prizes of 15,000, 10,000, and 5,000 Tunisian dinars respectively, with Amal Chebbi receiving an additional 10,000 dinars in equipment for her dual win.

The three national laureates will advance to the international POESAM finals, competing against winners from other Orange Group countries in Africa and the Middle East. International prizes range from 25,000 euros for first place to 10,000 euros for third. Additionally, women-led projects can compete for the International Women's Prize, which offers 20,000 euros, with Amal Chebbi also eligible for this award through PigmentOCO.

Why it mattersTunisian entrepreneurs are recognized for innovative projects addressing social and environmental issues, with potential for international recognition and funding.
