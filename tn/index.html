Tunisia and Iran discuss regional crisis

Foreign ministers Mohamed Ali Nafti and Abbas Araghchi spoke by phone on November 6, 2026, about de-escalation.

Tunisian Foreign Minister Mohamed Ali Nafti spoke with his Iranian counterpart Abbas Araghchi by phone on Friday, November 6, 2026. They discussed regional developments and the need for dialogue to resolve the current crisis. The call aimed to strengthen bilateral relations between Tunis and Tehran. Both ministers stressed the importance of finding a solution to end the crisis and preserve regional security and stability.

The Tunisian foreign ministry stated that the conversation reflected agreement on prioritizing diplomatic channels amid regional tensions. No further details were provided on specific initiatives or potential actions. This exchange follows a period of gradually strengthening ties, including Tunisian President Kaïs Saïed's historic visit to Tehran in May 2024.

Diplomatic engagement between the two nations has continued, with Nafti and Araghchi meeting in Portugal on November 27, 2024, to discuss cooperation and regional issues. Another call on February 8, 2025, focused on enhancing bilateral cooperation. Economic discussions have also occurred, including efforts to revive a joint business council.

Why it mattersTunisia and Iran are prioritizing diplomatic solutions to regional crises, seeking to preserve stability in Arab and Islamic nations.

Tunisia's penal code dates to French protectorate

Laws on political offenses and state security need updating, with calls to recognize political prisoners.

Many laws currently in force in Tunisia originate from the French protectorate era, making it necessary to revise the Penal Code, which has become obsolete over time. Articles related to state security offenses require updating, and there is a call to recognize the status of political prisoners.

Before 1910, laws were drafted by the Tunisian Government Secretariat, but a Justice Secretariat was formed that year, led by Bernard Roy. This body oversaw commissions creating Tunisian laws, managing local judicial affairs, and publishing civil, commercial, and penal laws. This led to the promulgation of four codes: Obligations and Contracts (1906), Civil Procedure (1910), Penal Code (1913), and Criminal Procedure (1921).

Legislation for political offenses was directly copied from French law, unlike other legal areas which had a mixed system. These French laws were adapted to Tunisia's specific conditions. Before the protectorate, the 1861 'Law on Crimes' provided the basis for judging political cases, though it implicitly retained Islamic repressive systems for rebellion.

Why it mattersTunisians are affected by outdated laws from the French protectorate era that govern political offenses and state security.

Tunis high school theft suspect arrested

Police recovered a stolen television, projector, and computer from a suspect in Jebel Lahmar.

Tunisian police arrested a suspect on Friday, November 6, 2026, in connection with a theft at a Tunis high school. The individual, who resides in Jebel Lahmar, is accused of stealing equipment during the night of November 4 to 5.

The stolen items included a large television, a projector, and a computer, according to a source from the National Security Directorate (DGSN). The DGSN source stated that the investigation led to the arrest of the main suspect and the recovery of all stolen property.

The arrested individual has been taken into custody as the investigation continues.

Tunisian post issues stamp celebrating breastfeeding

A new 1.500 D stamp, featuring art by Feryel Lakhdar, was released on November 5, 2026.

The Tunisian Post has issued a new stamp to celebrate the national breastfeeding week. The stamp, valued at 1.500 D, features artwork by Feryel Lakhdar and was printed in an offset run of 300,000 copies. It entered circulation on Thursday, November 5, 2026.

This stamp issuance is a result of collaboration between the Tunisian Post, the Hanen association for the promotion of breastfeeding, and artist Feryel Lakhdar. Lakhdar, a strong advocate for breastfeeding, dedicated her artistic talent to this cause. The Hanen association operated under Professor Zahra Marrakchi during this initiative.

The stamp's release coincides with the national breastfeeding week. The artwork was created by Feryel Lakhdar, who is a mother and a proponent of breastfeeding.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens will see a new stamp celebrating breastfeeding, a public health initiative.
