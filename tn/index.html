Tunisian Police Seize 70 Kilos of Gold in Money Laundering Probe

Authorities arrested thirteen individuals suspected of operating a network that laundered nearly two tons of gold over six years.

Tunisian financial police have seized over 70 kilograms of gold and 4.5 kilograms of silver in an investigation into a money laundering and tax evasion network. The operation, led by the economic and financial investigations unit in El Gorjani, resulted in the detention of thirteen people. Investigators suspect the network, composed of jewelers and factory owners, used shell companies and exploited artisan supply records to launder gold of unknown origin into legal channels over the past six years.

The alleged scheme aimed to introduce precious metals into legitimate trade circuits, with illicit profits reportedly reinvested in real estate and luxury goods. Authorities also confiscated over 120,000 dinars in cash and nine luxury vehicles valued at approximately 25 million dinars. The suspects face charges including criminal conspiracy, forgery, and habitual money laundering.

The investigation began following intelligence gathering and intensive surveillance of gold markets. The police are continuing their work to uncover the full extent of the network's operations, the origin of the seized metals, and the total value of the suspected financial transactions. The accusations against the detained individuals are subject to the ongoing investigation and judicial decisions.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the discovery of a large-scale money laundering operation that reinvested illicit profits into real estate and luxury goods.

Tunisian Actress Amel Baccouche Dies at 77

Amel Baccouche, known for her role as Jannet in the popular sitcom Choufli Hall, passed away after a recent health decline.

Tunisian actress Amel Baccouche died on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, at the age of 77. She was known for her role as Jannet in the series Choufli Hall, alongside other actors like Kamel Touati and Mouna Noureddine. Baccouche had recently been recovering from a health issue but her condition worsened in the days leading up to her death.

Baccouche began her acting career in the 1970s with the Troupe permanente du Kef. Her career spanned decades across theater, television, and radio fiction. She also taught theatrical education. Her role as Jannet the clairvoyant in Choufli Hall, which aired between 2005 and 2009, brought her widespread recognition.

Beyond her iconic role, Baccouche appeared in numerous other productions including El Khottab Al Bab, Dhafayer, Gamret Sidi Mahrous, and Awled Moufida. Her career, which began on the stage, was marked by a lasting presence in Tunisian dramatic arts, contributing to various creative registers.

Why it mattersThe death of actress Amel Baccouche marks the loss of a significant figure in Tunisian cultural heritage.

Tunisia Projects 1150 Female Ventures by 2026

The Raïdet program aims to launch approximately 1150 projects by the end of 2026, supported by an estimated 10 million dinars.

Tunisia's Raïdet program is projected to facilitate the launch of around 1150 female-led projects by the close of 2026, with an estimated financial backing of 10 million dinars, according to the 2027 economic budget draft. This initiative is part of broader efforts to bolster female entrepreneurship and enhance the economic integration of vulnerable women and families. A national digital platform will be established to aid in the marketing of products from women and young girls, particularly in rural areas, improving their market access.

Further support includes a free hotline to guide aspiring female entrepreneurs to available programs and funding structures, alongside training and mentorship. An initiative will span all governorates to standardize the entrepreneurial journey from conception to post-completion follow-up. The program also plans to evaluate its 2022-2025 performance, introduce a new financing line for higher education and vocational training graduates, and draft a decree for a new national female entrepreneurship program.

Specific allocations include 2 million dinars to create 200 income sources for agricultural workers and 1.5 million dinars for the Samida program, aimed at women experiencing domestic violence, to establish 150 income sources. Additionally, 4 million dinars are designated for vulnerable families to create approximately 480 income sources, targeting those affected by climate change or with children in care facilities.

Why it mattersFemale entrepreneurs and vulnerable families in Tunisia will benefit from new programs and funding aimed at creating income sources and improving economic integration.

Tunisian court issues suspended prison sentences to two activists

Rachid Othmani and Souheil Chebbi will be released from custody following their convictions related to the September nineteenth demonstration.

A Tunisian court has handed down six-month suspended prison sentences to Rachid Othmani and Souheil Chebbi. The two men were arrested following a public demonstration that took place on September 19.

According to the Al-Soumoud Flotilla Committee, the ruling results in the immediate release of both individuals on Tuesday, October 20, 2026. The organization has publicly requested that authorities drop all remaining legal proceedings against its members and supporters who were detained during the same event.

The committee stated that these judicial outcomes will not discourage their ongoing efforts. They emphasized that their advocacy for justice remains linked to the broader cause of militant freedom in the country.

Why it mattersThe two activists are directly affected by the court ruling which determines their immediate release from prison after serving time for their participation in a protest.

Orange Tunisie Uses Sport to Teach Teens Online Safety

The company organized a day of games and sports to educate 120 adolescents about digital risks.

Orange Tunisie, in partnership with the Tunisian School and University Sports Federation, hosted its second #ForGoodConnectionsDay on October 17, 2026. The event in Tunis gathered 120 adolescents, aged 11 to 15, for sports and games aimed at promoting safer and more responsible internet use. Activities included football matches, olympiads, and card games designed to help participants identify online risks and develop appropriate responses.

The initiative connects the values of sportsmanship, such as respect and solidarity, to online interactions, emphasizing that these principles apply to digital exchanges. This approach aims to equip young people with practical guidance for navigating cyberbullying, inappropriate content, and hyperconnectivity. Since June 2025, the #ForGoodConnections program has reached nearly 10,000 beneficiaries across Tunisia, including students, parents, and teachers.

The event highlighted the importance of collaboration between businesses and educational bodies in preventing digital risks. Orange Tunisie employees volunteered alongside federation supervisors and children of Orange Tunisie staff. This shared responsibility approach underscores the growing need for awareness as digital tools become central to adolescents' daily lives.

Why it mattersAdolescents aged 11 to 15 are directly affected by learning to navigate online risks through sports and games.
