Tunisie Telecom Honors Taekwondo Champion Firas Kattoussi

The national operator celebrated Kattoussi's Mediterranean gold medal and reaffirmed its three-year sponsorship deal.

Tunisie Telecom hosted a ceremony at its headquarters on August 31, 2026, to honor Tunisian taekwondo champion Firas Kattoussi following his gold medal win at the Mediterranean Games in Taranto on August 23. The company, which has a three-year sponsorship contract with Kattoussi, intends to continue its support for his upcoming international competitions. This event marks another tribute to Kattoussi, who also won Olympic gold at the Paris 2024 Games.

The ceremony served to celebrate Kattoussi's latest achievement in Taranto, where he secured first place in his category. This victory adds to his international accolades, following his Olympic triumph. Tunisie Telecom used the occasion to reiterate its backing for the athlete and its broader commitment to supporting Tunisian sports figures. The company's CEO, Lassâad Ben Dhiab, praised Kattoussi's discipline and perseverance, stating that his journey embodies the company's values of excellence.

Looking ahead, Kattoussi is preparing for the 2027 World Championships and the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, with Tunisie Telecom's support being crucial for his training. The company views this partnership as part of its corporate social responsibility, aiming to support Tunisian youth and enhance the country's international profile through sports. Kattoussi is recognized as a leading Tunisian athlete in high-level sports.

Why it mattersTunisie Telecom is supporting Firas Kattoussi's athletic career through a sponsorship deal, aiming to enhance the company's image and support national talent.

Gafsa Activists Mobilize for Detained Flottille Soumoud Members

A protest is scheduled for Thursday outside a Carrefour supermarket accused of financial complicity in attacks against Palestinians.

The Gafsa regional office of the Flottille Soumoud Commission has called for general mobilization to support its members and express solidarity with the Palestinian people. A rally is planned for Thursday, September 3, 2026, at 10 a.m. in front of the Carrefour store in Gafsa. Organizers accuse the supermarket of financial complicity in actions against Palestinians.

This initiative arises amidst growing concern for the health of Wael Nawar, who is on his seventeenth consecutive day of a hunger strike in his cell. Ghassen Henchiri is also reportedly suffering from the consequences of injuries sustained in detention and a lack of adequate medical care during his current imprisonment.

The commission is urging Gafsa residents, progressive forces, and citizens committed to liberties to gather in large numbers for this solidarity protest. The call to action was disseminated via a note from the Commission on Tuesday, September 1, 2026.

Why it mattersTunisian activists are protesting against a supermarket, leading to potential disruptions and public attention on the company's financial dealings.

Tunisian District Identifies Five Future Crises

A regional political body has compiled a list of potential challenges including school openings, water, energy, food, and flood risks.

Tunisia's second district, encompassing Tunis, Ariana, Ben Arous, Manouba, Zaghouan, and Nabeul, has drafted a list of anticipated crises for the next six months. Anis Khelfaoui, a deputy representing Nabeul, announced on Tuesday that this inventory of expected challenges is paired with identified intervention priorities. Khelfaoui stated that meetings with central administrations and relevant ministries, in coordination with the government presidency, will be scheduled to discuss these matters.

The identified issues span school reopenings, water supply, energy security, food security, and the risk of floods. These concerns extend beyond individual governorates, directly impacting public services, purchasing power, infrastructure, and the authorities' capacity to manage tense periods. The upcoming school year is an immediate priority, focusing on student reception conditions, transportation, and the availability of necessary supplies and services.

Water supply is another significant concern, with the district prioritizing anticipation of supply difficulties and securing distribution amid growing pressure on water resources. Energy security, ensuring a stable electricity grid and meeting peak demand, and food security, addressing price fluctuations and supply chain issues, are also strategic priorities. Flood risk prevention, involving monitoring hydraulic infrastructure and exposed areas, completes the list of anticipated crises.

Why it mattersThe district's proactive identification of potential crises in water, food, and energy security affects the daily lives and economic stability of residents.

Tunisian Phosphate Workers Strike Over Safety and Unpaid Bonuses

Agents and cadres at the Compagnie des phosphates de Gafsa began a three-day work stoppage, with union reports indicating a 95% participation rate.

A three-day strike by agents and cadres of the Compagnie des phosphates de Gafsa (CPG) commenced on Tuesday, September 1. The general secretary of the mining federation, Rim Helal, stated that union reports showed approximately 95% participation by 8 a.m. The work stoppage, initially announced as a notice, is now in its effective phase and is scheduled to continue through Thursday, September 3.

The Tunisian General Labour Union (UGTT) detailed the workers' demands, which extend beyond salary increases to include workplace safety, improved working conditions, and the implementation of prior agreements. A significant financial claim involves a productivity bonus that has reportedly remained unpaid for two years. The UGTT emphasized that workers' rights are not privileges but earned entitlements.

The union also rejected characterizations of the strike as solely a wage dispute, arguing that addressing the workers' concerns is crucial for the company's long-term viability and the protection of its workforce. The immediate impact of the strike on phosphate extraction, processing, and shipping operations remains to be determined.

Why it mattersThe strike affects CPG workers by halting operations over safety and unpaid productivity bonuses, impacting their immediate financial rights and working conditions.
