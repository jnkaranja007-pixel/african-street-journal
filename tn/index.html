Tunisian police dismantle drug ring in Tunis, arrest three suspects

Investigators also seized a quantity of narcotics and cash from the illegal trade, officials said.

Tunisian security units dismantled an international drug trafficking network operating in Tunis and arrested three of its main members, who have been placed in detention. The investigation was carried out by the Tunis research and investigation brigade in coordination with the intelligence directorate of the National Guard.

A security source cited by the TAP news agency said two Tunisian nationals with previous convictions, who already had arrest warrants against them, were among the suspects. The third is a foreign national accused of smuggling cannabis into Tunisia. A large quantity of narcotics and money from the illicit activity were also seized, and the public prosecutor ordered the three suspects detained while the investigation continues.

Separately, National Guard units dismantled a network operating between Sousse and Kairouan that targeted young people, arresting its main suspect and seizing cocaine and cannabis meant for nightclubs in the region. Security units are still working to identify and arrest other people involved.

Why it mattersThree suspects are in custody, and investigators are still looking for further members of the network, which also supplied cocaine and cannabis to nightclubs in the region.

Tunisia faces cooler temperatures and rain

Winds will be strong near the northern coast and in the south, with local sandstorms possible.

Tunisia will experience a slight drop in temperatures on Friday, November 13, 2026, with scattered rain expected in some areas. Maximum temperatures will range between 25 and 30°C, dropping to around 22°C in western regions.

The sky will be partly cloudy across most of the country, with denser clouds and scattered rain possible along the northern coast and in the far south. Strong westerly winds will blow near the northern coast and in the south, potentially causing local sandstorms that reduce visibility in southern zones.

Sea conditions will be rough to very rough along the northern coast and agitated elsewhere. Overnight temperatures are forecast between 18 and 23°C, with a low of about 14°C in the western highlands.

Why it mattersResidents across Tunisia will experience cooler weather and potential rainfall on Friday, November 13, with strong winds affecting coastal and southern areas.

Tunisia to overhaul military service law with higher pay and training

Defence Minister Khaled Sehili says a new draft law to replace the 2004 national service law is complete and aims to make the service more attractive and flexible.

Defence Minister Khaled Sehili announced on Thursday, 12 November 2026, that the government has finished drafting a new law to replace Law No. 1 of 2004 on national service in Tunisia. He presented the main lines of the draft during a joint session of the Assembly of the Representatives of the People and the National Council of Regions and Districts, which was devoted to the defence ministry's 2027 budget.

The draft promises a higher monthly allowance for young conscripts, though Sehili did not say by how much. It would also widen vocational training open to conscripts, so they can gain skills that count in the job market, and create more routes into public sector jobs, with the details still to be set out. The text would diversify the ways of fulfilling national duty and digitise and simplify procedures. Sehili urged students to regularise their status with the national service and contact the competent services.

The new text must still pass the required institutional process before it can be adopted and take effect.

Why it mattersConscripts would receive a higher monthly allowance and access to vocational training, and students must regularise their national service status.

Politics · 2026-11-13

Tunisia marks 70 years with UN

Tunisia reaffirms commitment to UN principles and seeks reform to end double standards.

Tunisia celebrated its 70th anniversary of joining the United Nations on Friday, reflecting on its contributions to peace, security, and international cooperation.

The nation reiterated its dedication to the UN Charter's principles, including state sovereignty and peaceful dispute resolution, highlighting its role as a reliable partner in its regional spheres.

Tunisia also called for UN reform to enhance its credibility and effectiveness, aiming to end selective application of international law and support national development priorities through UN cooperation programs.

Why it mattersTunisia's commitment to UN principles and its call for reform directly impact its international relations and development cooperation.
