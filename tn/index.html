Tunisian Institute issues orange alert for 19 governorates amid heatwave

Temperatures are forecast to reach up to 48°C on Tuesday and Wednesday, exceeding seasonal norms by 7 to 11 degrees, prompting warnings from the National Institute of Meteorology.

Tunisia is experiencing a new heatwave, with 19 out of 24 governorates placed under orange vigilance by the National Institute of Meteorology (INM). Maximum temperatures reached 46°C on Monday and are expected to climb to 48°C on Tuesday and Wednesday, with sirocco winds anticipated to continue until the end of the week. Five other governorates, including Grand Tunis and Nabeul, are under yellow vigilance.

The INM warns that the heatwave poses risks for vulnerable groups, including the elderly, children, and individuals with chronic illnesses. Sports enthusiasts and outdoor workers are advised to take extra precautions against dehydration and heatstroke. The INM recommends regular water intake and avoiding outdoor activities between 12h and 16h.

The National Civil Protection Office has also issued a warning about increased fire risks due to the high temperatures, which are expected to range between 40°C and 45°C. Citizens are urged to prevent fires by not leaving glass items in natural spaces, avoiding burning waste, and refraining from lighting fires near agricultural areas. The Civil Protection units responded to 124 fire extinguishing operations in the last twenty-four hours.

Why it mattersThe heatwave directly affects the safety and daily lives of all residents, particularly vulnerable populations and those working outdoors, due to extreme temperatures and increased fire risks.

Tunisian court orders activist Seifeddine Arfaoui to appear August 27

A public prosecutor in Tunis issued an arrest warrant for Seifeddine Arfaoui, an activist with the Nafas movement, on Monday, August 24, 2026.

The court also set a hearing for August 27 for Arfaoui to appear before the summer correctional chamber of the Court of First Instance in Tunis. He is being prosecuted for social media posts deemed insulting to President Kaïs Saïed.

Arfaoui had organized a protest against the Kaïs Saïed regime on Thursday, March 20, in Tunis. The decision to issue the warrant and schedule the hearing was made by the public prosecutor's office at the Court of First Instance in Tunis.

This development follows a period of heightened political activity, including the president's recent visit to the Nebhana dam, where he ordered immediate cleaning operations. The legal action against Arfaoui highlights ongoing tensions related to freedom of expression and political dissent in Tunisia.

Why it mattersPolitical activists and those who engage in online commentary about the president face potential legal action and detention.

Tunisian Post Agent Detained Over Alleged Fund Diversion

A Tunisian Post agent in Hammam-Lif has been placed in custody following a judicial order regarding suspicions of diverting customer funds.

The public prosecutor at the Ben Arous Court of First Instance ordered the detention of a Tunisian Post agent, suspected of diverting customer funds. This action follows a video circulated on social media, showing an elderly woman attempting to withdraw 800 dinars, but 832 dinars were reportedly debited from her account.

The video, which sparked public outrage, prompted the Tunisian Post to announce an investigation on Sunday, August twenty-third, 2026. The institution stated it would not tolerate any proven misconduct and would apply the law rigorously to those found responsible.

The case has now moved to judicial proceedings, with the Hammam-Lif judicial police brigade handling the agent's custody. While the agent is suspected of diversion, the investigations will determine the exact circumstances and establish if an infraction occurred, as placement in custody does not pre-judge guilt.

Why it mattersCustomers of the Tunisian Post are affected by potential financial misconduct, raising concerns about the security of their transactions and trust in public services.

Tunisian cash in circulation hits record 30 billion dinars, pressuring banks

The amount of banknotes and coins outside the banking system reached 30.04 billion dinars by August 21, 2026, a 16% increase from the previous year.

The volume of cash circulating in Tunisia has reached an unprecedented 30.04 billion dinars, equivalent to approximately 10.5 billion dollars, according to data from the Central Bank of Tunisia reported by Reuters. This record level, up from 25.9 billion dinars a year prior, indicates a growing preference for physical currency over bank deposits.

This increase in cash outside the banking system could strain banks' liquidity management and reduce funds available for financing households and businesses, Reuters notes. The phenomenon occurs despite a rise in electronic payments, which accounted for 42% of monetary operations in the first half of 2026, compared to 58% for cash withdrawals.

The Central Bank attributes the rise in cash circulation to several factors, including seasonal spending for Ramadan, holidays, school re-entry, the summer season, and agricultural campaigns. Additionally, changes in cheque regulations in 2025, which tightened rules and increased penalties for invalid cheques, may have prompted some individuals and businesses to withdraw more cash for transactions.

Why it mattersThis record cash circulation affects Tunisian households and businesses by potentially limiting the availability of bank financing for loans and investments.

Tunisian Parliament Debates Reclassifying State Agents' Qualifications

A new bill aims to reassign and reclassify state agents based on their education and skills, addressing underutilized human capital within the public administration.

The Tunisian parliament is set to debate a new bill focused on reclassifying and reassigning state agents according to their qualifications. This initiative seeks to address the issue of public sector employees holding university degrees that are not fully utilized in their current roles, grades, or responsibilities.

The proposed legislation aims to correct situations where agents possess advanced qualifications, such as licenses, master's degrees, or other certifications, but are assigned to tasks that do not leverage their expertise. Examples include IT graduates performing repetitive tasks or lawyers engaged in operations that do not use their legal knowledge.

While the reclassification could provide statutory regularization, critics argue that simply reclassifying without reassigning roles and reviewing missions might only shift the problem. The goal is to transform existing skills into effective execution capacity within the administration, rather than just adjusting grades or salaries.

Why it mattersState agents with advanced degrees are affected as their qualifications may be reclassified, potentially leading to new assignments that better match their skills.
