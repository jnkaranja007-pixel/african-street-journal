Tunisia receives 61 million euro donation from Netherlands

The funds will support the fourth and fifth phases of a coastal protection program.

Tunisia signed a donation agreement with the Kingdom of the Netherlands for nearly 61 million euros to fund its coastal protection program. The agreement was signed on November 10, 2026, by Foreign Minister Mohamed Ali Nafti and Melanie Maas Geesteranus, Director General of Invest International B.V. The Dutch funding will support the fourth, fifth, and an additional phase of the program, which began in 2013.

The donation will finance protection work in Bizerte, the Sousse-Sud to Skanès area, Qaraiya in Monastir, and the northeast coast of Djerba. It will also fund a study for coastal protection from Beni Khiar to Yasmine Hammamet. The program aims to protect beaches, infrastructure, economic activities like tourism and fishing, and the livelihoods of coastal populations.

This Dutch contribution reinforces existing cooperation with Germany, which has supported the program since 2013 through KfW. The Netherlands will also provide expertise in coastal engineering, water management, and coastal zone protection. The program aims to combat coastal erosion and the effects of climate change.

Why it mattersThe donation will help protect coastal areas from erosion and climate change impacts, preserving infrastructure and livelihoods.

BTK Bank and Tecnocasa Tunisie partner for property access

The agreement aims to simplify the property buying process for individuals, investors, and Tunisians abroad.

BTK Bank and TECNOCASA TUNISIE signed a strategic partnership agreement on November 11, 2026, at the bank's headquarters in Tunis. This collaboration unites BTK Bank's financial expertise with TECNOCASA's real estate intermediation network.

The deal will offer integrated support from property search to financing for individuals, investors, and Tunisians living abroad. Lassaad Ben Romdhane, General Director of BTK Bank, stated that this partnership builds on a previous collaboration in Paris in June 2026.

Skander Ben Osman, Manager of Tecnocasa Tunisie, noted that the partnership allows real estate advisors to offer financing solutions from the project's outset, specifically addressing overseas Tunisians wishing to invest in their country.

Why it mattersTunisians abroad can now access integrated property search and financing services through this partnership.

Tunisia wins silver at Dakar 2026 Youth Olympics

Taekwondo athlete Yasmine Haouani secured Tunisia's second silver medal in the -55kg category.

Tunisian taekwondo athlete Yasmine Haouani won a silver medal in the under-55kg category at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games on Tuesday, November 10. She lost the final match 0-2 to Senegal's Marie Sarr, securing Tunisia's second medal of the event.

Haouani reached the final by defeating Simisile Ziyanda Thwala of Eswatini 2-0 in the quarterfinals and Aline Herrera Martinez of Mexico 2-1 in the semifinals. The Ministry of Youth and Sports congratulated Haouani, noting this is the second silver medal for Tunisia at these games and the second historical medal for Tunisian taekwondo at the Youth Olympics.

The first Tunisian medal was a silver in discus throw by Katr Ennada Jaoued on Sunday. Tunisia is participating in the Dakar 2026 Games with a delegation of 42 athletes across 16 sports.

Why it mattersTunisian athletes are competing and winning medals at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games.

Tunis hosts 27th Theatre Days from November 21

12 productions from 11 Arab and African countries will compete for the Golden Tanit award.

Tunis will host the 27th edition of the Journées théâtrales de Carthage (JTC) from November 21 to 28, 2026. The event, presented at the Cité de la culture, aims to connect Arab, African, and international theatre scenes while focusing on training and future discussions. The festival's artistic director, Slim Sanhaji, described this edition as a "harvest event" reflecting collective work and collaborations.

The official competition will feature 12 productions from 11 countries, including Senegal, Lebanon, Egypt, and Morocco, alongside Tunisia's two entries: "Tchaïka" by Moez Mrabet and "Les Couloirs de la peur" by Mohamed Dhia Mansouri. An international symposium on theatre financing and production systems in the Arab world will take place from November 22 to 24.

The opening night on November 21 will feature "Soleil d’Orient… Une seule terre, un seul cœur," a performance from Syria by the Enana troupe. The jury for the competition will be chaired by former Tunisian Minister of Culture, Abderraouf El Basti.

Why it mattersTunisian theatre professionals and artists will participate in and be recognized at this major regional festival.

Business · 2026-11-11

Tunisia drafts specifications to regulate motorcycle sales

The Commerce Ministry wants sales structured around professional networks, with about two million motorcycles on the roads but only 20,000 registered.

Tunisia's Ministry of Commerce is preparing a new set of specifications to regulate how motorcycles are sold, including how distribution channels are organised and the conditions under which vendors may sell. Salwa Fatnassi, director of quality and consumer protection at the ministry, confirmed the draft on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, in a statement to Mosaique FM.

Fatnassi said the aim is to bring two-wheeler sales under a stricter framework built around professionals and organised sales networks, similar to the car sector. The ministry says this should also make motorcycles easier to register and easier to trace. The country has nearly two million motorcycles but only 20,000 are registered. A separate technical regulation for motorcycle helmets is also being prepared.

The exact conditions for sellers, how the rules will be applied, and when they will take effect have not yet been set. The status of motorcycles already sold and circulating without registration is also still to be clarified.

Why it mattersMotorcycle sellers will have to meet the new sales conditions and distribution rules once the specifications are finalised, and owners of unregistered bikes face a yet-to-be-clarified situation.
