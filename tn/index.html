Tunisia plans 8.2% rise in public sector wages for 2027

The government expects to spend 27.349 billion dinars on salaries, an increase from 25.267 billion dinars in 2026.

Tunisia's draft budget for 2027 allocates 27.349 billion dinars for public sector salaries, an 8.2% increase from the estimated 25.267 billion dinars in 2026. This spending represents 39.7% of the budget excluding debt repayment and 13.7% of the gross domestic product. The budget also plans for 32,557 new positions, with education and higher education receiving the largest share of new hires at 7,388 positions.

Education will receive the largest portion of the salary budget, with 8.220 billion dinars, or 30% of the total. The Ministry of Interior follows with 4.404 billion dinars (16%), then National Defense with 3.307 billion dinars (12%), and Health with 3.172 billion dinars (12%). The draft budget also includes provisions for regularizing the status of workers over 45 and holders of doctorates.

The government aims for a 3.1% growth rate in 2027, though some business leaders find the economic recovery support insufficient. Yassine Gouiaa, president of the National Organization of Entrepreneurs, stated that the 230 million dinars allocated for economic recovery is too small, especially given the current crisis and rising energy costs. He noted that previous financing lines for startups have not yet been implemented.

Why it mattersTunisian public sector employees will see an increase in their salaries in 2027.

Tunisia court upholds state security plot sentences

The Court of Cassation confirmed prison terms ranging from three to 35 years for 21 accused in a state security plot case.

Tunisia's Court of Cassation upheld all appeals against judgments in the "plot against state security 2" case on Wednesday, October 21, 2026. The prison sentences, initially issued in February 2026 and ranging from three to 35 years, are now final. The Tunis Court of Appeal's criminal chamber, specializing in terrorism cases, had delivered its rulings on February 3, 2026.

Rached Ghannouchi, leader of the Ennahdha movement, and Kamel Bedoui received 20-year sentences, an increase from their first-instance judgments. Former security officials Mehrez Zouari and Abdelkrim Abidi were sentenced to seven years each, down from 12. Fathi Beldi and Samir Hannachi received 15 years apiece. Several defendants, including Nadia Akacha and Mouadh Ghannouchi, were sentenced to 35 years in absentia. Some sentences include five years of administrative surveillance post-release.

The case involves accusations of forming a group linked to terrorist offenses, inciting violence, and plotting against internal state security. Charges also included preparing to alter the state's form and recruiting for terrorist acts. The court also confirmed a two-year sentence for Abdelkrim Harouni in a separate corruption case, alongside sentences for businessman Mohamed Frikha and former oil company executives.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens face prison sentences for alleged plots against state security and corruption.

Tunisian electricity company restores website after security alert

Steg reactivated its online services after implementing fixes for a global PHP2Shell vulnerability.

Tunisia's electricity and gas company, Steg, has restored its official website and online services after a temporary shutdown. The company had proactively disabled its digital platform due to a reported global computer vulnerability. This measure aimed to protect Steg's information system from potential malicious exploitation of the security flaw.

Steg stated that after applying necessary patches and ensuring security conditions were met, the platform is operational again. However, access to the website and its digital services is currently restricted to users within Tunisia. The company had announced the temporary suspension on October 22, 2026, as a precautionary step.

The global vulnerability, identified as PHP2Shell, affects the widely used PHP programming language. Millions of exploitation attempts were reported after its disclosure on October 21 and 22, 2026. Other Tunisian public bodies, like the ATCT, also temporarily suspended their sites to protect user data and systems.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens relying on the Steg website for electricity services faced temporary disruption.

Tunisia joins global soil science decade

The nation participates in a UN-backed initiative to improve soil health for food security by 2034.

Tunisia is participating in the global Soil Science Decade, running from 2025 to 2034, with the theme 'Healthy soils for humanity'. This initiative aims to foster the creation and sharing of practical scientific knowledge to enhance agricultural performance and ensure food security by 2050.

The plan for the decade includes strategic publications like books and policy briefs, alongside events such as webinars and conferences. The recent World Food Day celebration on October 16, under the FAO's guidance, provided an occasion to advance these goals.

The focus on pedology, the study of soil, supports the sharing of scientific knowledge to address immediate needs while planning for future food security.

Why it mattersTunisian farmers will benefit from improved soil science knowledge to boost agricultural performance and food security.
