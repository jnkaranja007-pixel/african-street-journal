Tunisian court sentences Riad Ben Fadhl to 20 years

18 other defendants received nine-year sentences in a corruption and money laundering case.

A specialized criminal court in Tunis sentenced Riad Ben Fadhl, coordinator for the Al Qotb party, to 20 years in prison on October 29, 2026. 18 other defendants in the same financial corruption and money laundering case received nine-year sentences. The charges relate to the management and acquisition of assets confiscated after the revolution.

The case dates back to November 2023, when Ben Fadhl was arrested upon returning to Tunis-Carthage airport. He had been detained since November 24, 2023, following a judicial order. The court rejected requests for his release on October 12, 2026, before issuing the sentence. Most of the 18 other defendants appeared in court as free individuals.

The Committee for the Respect of Liberties and Human Rights in Tunisia (CRLDHT) condemned the verdict as an act of repression and demanded Ben Fadhl's immediate release. The Al Qotb party stated Ben Fadhl is paying for his opposition to authoritarianism. Ben Fadhl's defense argued that acquisitions were made through official tenders and that evidence was insufficient.

Why it mattersRiad Ben Fadhl, a political figure and entrepreneur, received a lengthy prison sentence for corruption charges, impacting his freedom and political engagement.

Tunisia and Russia discuss cooperation after Africa summit

Foreign Minister Nafti met Russian Ambassador Zolotov to follow up on the Russia-Africa summit and strengthen bilateral ties.

Tunisian Foreign Minister Mohamed Ali Nafti met Russian Ambassador Alexander Zolotov on Thursday in Tunis to discuss concrete cooperation projects following the third Russia-Africa Summit held in Moscow on October 28-29. The two officials reviewed ways to strengthen bilateral relations and cooperation in areas of mutual interest, marking the 70th anniversary of diplomatic ties between Tunisia and Russia.

Nafti emphasized Tunisia's desire to boost cooperation in the energy sector, seeking new partnership opportunities and economically viable projects to enhance national energy security. Discussions also covered strengthening economic, scientific, cultural, technical, and tourism exchanges, as well as consolidating the legal framework for bilateral cooperation and accelerating ongoing joint projects.

On consular matters, Nafti called for increased coordination with Russian authorities to facilitate the processing of consular issues and protect Tunisian citizens' rights. Ambassador Zolotov affirmed his readiness to coordinate with his country's authorities to implement agreed commitments and advance Tunisian-Russian cooperation across various sectors.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens' rights and consular issues are directly affected by the call for increased coordination between Tunisia and Russia.

Tunisia plans 32.6 billion dinars investment for 2027

The country aims for a 14.1% increase in total investments, with 1.2 billion dinars allocated to road infrastructure.

Tunisia plans to increase its total investment volume to 32.6 billion dinars in 2027, up from 28.6 billion expected in 2026. This represents a 14.1% progression, with the investment rate projected to reach 16.4% of GDP. Foreign direct investment is estimated to rise by 20% to 4.8 billion dinars.

A significant portion, 1.2 billion dinars, is earmarked for road infrastructure, including highway projects like the Tunis-Jelma route and the doubling of national road 13. Agricultural investments are expected to reach 1.532 billion dinars, a 7.1% increase. Industrial investments are set to grow by 17.8%, with 180 hectares of industrial zones planned for completion.

Energy projects, such as the Sousse combined cycle power plant and solar facilities, are also included. The government anticipates a 8.8% growth in services, driven by transport investments. These figures are projections, and their realization depends on the execution of scheduled projects.

Why it mattersTunisia's planned investments in infrastructure, agriculture, industry, and energy will affect businesses and economic growth.

Tunisia signs stadium reconstruction contract

A Chinese consortium will rebuild the El Menzah Olympic Stadium for up to 600 million dinars.

Tunisia has signed a contract to reconstruct the El Menzah Olympic Stadium. The agreement, signed on Thursday, October 29, 2026, involves the Tunisian state and a consortium of two Chinese companies, China Railway Construction Engineering Group (CRCEG) and China CAMC Engineering (CAMCE). The project's total cost is estimated between 520 and 600 million dinars.

The reconstruction and modernization will include the stands, athletics track, and other sports facilities. The work is expected to take between 30 and 36 months. The signing ceremony was attended by the Minister of Equipment and Housing, Salah Zouari, and the Chinese Ambassador to Tunisia, Wan Li.

The project aims to transform the stadium into a modern sports facility with enhanced safety, accessibility, and spectator comfort. Improvements will also focus on public circulation and equipment optimization.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens will benefit from a modernized sports stadium with improved facilities and safety.

Semia Setti's new novel sells out at Tunis book fair

The author's second book, "Je suis née à six ans," explores childhood trauma and self-reconstruction.

Semia Setti's new novel, "Je suis née à six ans," published in October 2026 by Arcadia Tunisie, sold out its initial stock at the ongoing National Book Fair in Tunis. Readers will need to wait until next week for the book to be available in Tunisian bookstores.

The novel's title, "I was born at six years old," refers to a profound childhood separation experienced at an airport. The narrative focuses on the pain of separation and the subsequent process of self-reconstruction, with the author using concrete details to convey emotional distress.

This is Setti's second novel published by Arcadia Tunisie; her first, "Aimer n'a pas suffi," was released in August 2025 under the pseudonym Sam Bröcheler. The author has now chosen to use her real name, Semia Setti, for this latest publication.

Why it mattersTunisian readers are directly affected by the unavailability of a newly released novel by a local author.
