Club Africain leads TP Mazembe 1-0 at half-time

Taddaeus Fomakwang scored in added time for Club Africain in their African Champions League qualifier.

Club Africain took the initiative from the start against TP Mazembe, seeking an early lead. Despite creating chances in the opening minutes, the TP Mazembe goalkeeper denied them. In the 45th minute, Taddaeus Fomakwang scored a goal in first-half stoppage time, giving Club Africain a 1-0 advantage at halftime.

The match, held on October 25, 2026, at Radès' Hammadi-Agrebi Stadium, is the second leg of the African Champions League preliminary round. Club Africain's supporters helped fund the free broadcast of the match on the All4One platform on YouTube after the club could not secure a broadcast rights deal.

The first leg between the two teams on October 17, 2026, in Lubumbashi ended in a 1-1 draw. Josué Kazema scored for TP Mazembe, and Alain Kanté equalized for Club Africain shortly after.

Why it mattersTunisian football club Club Africain is playing TP Mazembe in an African Champions League qualifier.

Tunisia mobilizes 150 million dinars for olive oil purchases

Government increases maritime transport support for packaged olive oil exports to 60%.

Tunisia's government has allocated 150 million dinars in bank financing to support the 2026-2027 olive oil campaign, enabling the National Oil Office (ONH) to purchase from producers.

The measures include a storage program for up to 60,000 tonnes and an increased subsidy for maritime transport of packaged olive oil exports, raised from 50% to 60%. An additional 20 million dinars in subsidized credit is available for small farmers through the Tunisian Solidarity Bank (BTS).

The government also plans a 100 million dinar financing line for 2027-2030. The storage program, capped at 20 million dinars, aims to support the commercialization of production, with ONH's own storage capacity at 100,000 tonnes.

Why it mattersTunisian farmers will benefit from government support for olive oil purchases and export incentives.

Tunisia faces barley seed shortage for 2026-2027 season

Only 12% of estimated barley seed needs are covered, with 12,000 quintals available against 100,000 required.

Tunisia's agricultural sector faces a significant shortfall in controlled barley seeds for the upcoming 2026-2027 campaign. As of October 16, only 12,000 quintals were available, representing just 12% of the estimated 100,000 quintals needed. These figures were presented by the Ministry of Agriculture to a parliamentary committee on October 22.

Across all seed types, 232,000 quintals of selected seeds were available between August and October 16, against a forecast of 492,000 quintals of subsidized seeds. While the deficit in controlled barley seeds is a concern, the ministry's representatives noted that the supply schedule and potential additional deliveries are yet to be finalized, leaving room for further clarification on meeting the 100,000 quintal requirement.

The ministry also reported a stock of 69,000 tonnes of ammonium nitrate fertilizer, with plans to import an additional 110,000 tonnes. For the 2026-2027 campaign, 1.458 million hectares are planned for major crops, including 77,000 hectares for irrigated cereals. Land preparation is progressing, with deep plowing at 64% completion.

Why it mattersTunisian farmers face a shortage of essential barley seeds needed for the upcoming planting season.

Tunisian women's union office seized over 1987-2012 debts

The UNFT faces asset seizure and unpaid staff salaries due to accumulated debts from previous administrations.

The Union nationale de la femme tunisienne (UNFT) has denounced the seizure of its president's office and meeting room. The organization is demanding urgent payment of unpaid salaries and reinstatement of social coverage for its agents and executives, which has been interrupted for two years.

The UNFT's central committee attributes the crisis to accumulated debts from prior periods and the cancellation of a subsidy for staff salaries. This situation has led to a series of seizures to recover due sums, though the UNFT refuses responsibility for debts accumulated since 1987.

The seizure stems from a 14-year-old court judgment related to debts covering 1987 to 2012, predating the current leadership. Despite these financial difficulties, the UNFT intends to continue its work on combating violence against women and supporting vulnerable women.

Why it mattersThe UNFT's financial crisis and asset seizure directly affect its employees and its ability to operate.
