Tunis court sentences neighbour to death for child murder

A young man will be hanged for strangling a six-year-old girl and hiding her body on a neighbour's roof in 2025.

A criminal court in Tunis has sentenced a young man to death by hanging for the premeditated murder of his six-year-old neighbour. The court found the man guilty of strangling the child with a sock in 2025. He then wrapped her body in curtain fabric and hid it on a neighbour's roof.

The crime began with the girl's disappearance, causing distress to her family and neighbours in the El Omrane El Aala district. Investigations led to the neighbour, known to the victim and other children in the area. He admitted to luring the girl to his home after she returned alone.

The court's decision followed the examination of the case and the evidence presented. The convicted man had previously hidden at a friend's house in Ettadhamen after the murder. The body was discovered shortly after being concealed on the roof.

Tunisia seeks more Arab agricultural investment

Tunisia's Economy Minister met with the Arab Organization for Agricultural Development president to boost projects.

Tunisia aims to increase investment from the Arab Organization for Agricultural Development (AAAID) within the country. Minister Samir Abdelhafidh met with AAAID president Obaid Saif Hamad Al Zaabi on October 30, 2026, to discuss expanding cooperation and developing new agricultural projects. The discussions focused on enhancing food security through these initiatives.

The meeting highlighted the agricultural sector's place in Tunisia's upcoming five-year development plan, which aims to boost production and modernize farming systems. Minister Abdelhafidh expressed a desire for greater AAAID activity and investment in Tunisia, benefiting both parties. President Al Zaabi confirmed his readiness to explore new investment proposals and facilitate access to AAAID's financing and technical support for Tunisian entities.

This collaboration could lead to new agricultural projects and provide technical and financial assistance to Tunisian operators. Food security is a stated priority in Tunisia's national economic planning. The AAAID president was satisfied with the progress of existing projects in Tunisia.

Why it mattersTunisia's agricultural sector and food security are prioritized for development and investment.

Tunisia braces for thunderstorms and strong winds

The National Institute of Meteorology forecasts heavy rain and rough seas across several regions on Sunday, November 1, 2026.

Tunisia will experience thunderstorms and strong winds on Sunday, November 1, 2026, according to the National Institute of Meteorology (INM). The northeast and southeast regions are expected to receive temporarily stormy rain, while other areas will see cloudy skies.

Winds will blow from the north, becoming strong near the coasts and on the highlands. The sea will be rough to very rough along the northern coasts, requiring special attention for maritime activities. Temperatures will range from 15 to 21°C in the north and west, and 21 to 24°C elsewhere.

The INM also reported scattered, stormy rain in the north and south, with very cloudy skies in some parts. The Gulf of Hammamet will see rough seas, while other coasts will be less agitated. Overnight temperatures are predicted between 9 and 13°C in western regions and 14 to 18°C in the rest of the country.

Why it mattersTunisians across the country will face unstable weather conditions including rain, storms, and strong winds, impacting daily activities and maritime operations.

Tunisia transport attacks cost 4 million dinars

Attacks on trains, metros, and buses in 2025-2026 damaged property and affected 362 people, according to the transport minister.

Attacks on public transport in Tunisia caused approximately 4 million dinars in material damage during 2025-2026, stated Transport Minister Rachid Amri. The minister also reported that 362 individuals, including drivers and sector agents, were affected by these incidents. Amri presented these figures on Saturday, October 31, in Ben Arous during a meeting focused on preserving public transport services.

The reported cost covers damage to multiple public transport vehicles, with no detailed breakdown provided for specific operators or the two years involved. Minister Amri noted that these expenses could have funded other developments within the transport sector. For example, replacing broken train windows due to thrown stones in 2025 alone cost the SNCFT around 162,000 dinars, a figure separate from the 4 million dinar total which covers broader damages across networks.

Beyond material repairs, Amri highlighted the disruption these attacks cause to passengers, impacting their ability to reach work, appointments, or medical facilities. The SNCFT has also emphasized the risks posed by stone-throwing incidents to both passengers and staff. To address these issues, a collective prevention strategy involving families, schools, and civil society has been proposed to protect transport infrastructure and personnel.

Why it mattersPassengers and transport workers in Tunisia are directly affected by damage to public transport and disruptions to services caused by attacks.

Tunis police arrest 12 for stadium violence

12 individuals were arrested in Tunis for alleged involvement in stadium vandalism, theft, and drug offenses.

Tunisian police arrested 12 individuals in Tunis early this morning as part of a security campaign. The arrests were made by the Sidi El Béchir judicial police brigade.

These suspects were wanted by various security and judicial units for alleged involvement in stadium vandalism, theft, physical assaults, and drug trafficking and possession. All 12 have been placed in custody while investigations continue.

The arrests were reported by Mosaïque FM, citing a source close to the investigation.

Why it matters12 individuals in Tunis face legal proceedings for alleged criminal activities including stadium violence and drug offenses.
