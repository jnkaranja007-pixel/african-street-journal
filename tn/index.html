Tunisia road accidents fall but fatalities rise

Road accidents in Tunisia decreased by nearly 10% by October 29, 2026, but the number of deaths increased by 13.19%.

Tunisian roads saw 4102 accidents by October 29, 2026, a 9.95% decrease from the same period in 2025. However, fatalities climbed to 1141, an increase of 13.19% compared to the previous year, according to the National Road Safety Observatory (ONSR). The number of injured also fell by 12.13% to 5293 people.

Speed was identified as a major factor in road deaths, contributing to 410 fatalities. This rise in deaths, despite fewer accidents, highlights a serious safety concern. The ONSR's provisional figures, recorded up to October 29, 2026, indicate a worrying trend as the year concludes.

Tunisia has set a national strategy to reduce road deaths and serious injuries by 10% by 2027, 25% by 2030, and 50% by 2034. The plan includes deploying smart cameras and radars, improving infrastructure, and revising vehicle regulations.

Why it mattersTunisian road users are experiencing fewer accidents but facing a higher risk of death when accidents do occur.

Espérance sportive de Tunis plans strategic project

The Tunisian club will present its new initiative on January 15, 2027, its 108th anniversary.

Espérance sportive de Tunis (EST) announced on Sunday the start of preparations for a strategic project. The club plans to officially present this initiative on January 15, 2027, marking its 108th anniversary.

A working group, including members of the EST family, has been formed to prepare the project. This team will collaborate closely with the club's management, various commissions, and supporter groups both in Tunisia and abroad.

The club stated that this project aims to unite all its components around a collective effort. Details of the initiative have not yet been revealed.

Why it mattersThe strategic project aims to mobilize all components of Espérance sportive de Tunis around a collective initiative.

Club Bizertin debt reduced by half

Ahmed Karoui's family offers to forgive 700,000 dinars of the club's 1.4 million dinar debt.

The family of the late Ahmed Karoui, former president of Club Athlétique Bizertin (CAB), has proposed to settle the club's debt by forgiving half of the total amount owed.

The family is willing to accept 700,000 dinars in installments, waiving the remaining 700,000 dinars of the 1.4 million dinar debt. This offer includes legal fees and interest accrued during Karoui's presidency from 2007 to 2009.

Lawyer Yassine Younsi reported the proposal on the "Samdi Sport" show on October 31. The family stated they would finalize this financial effort if CAB officials contact them for an amicable agreement to protect the team's interests.

Why it mattersThe Club Athlétique Bizertin faces a reduced debt of 700,000 dinars, allowing it to continue operations.

Tunisia transport attacks cost 4 million dinars

Attacks on trains, metros, and buses in 2025-2026 damaged property and affected 362 people, according to the transport minister.

Agressions against public transport in Tunisia caused approximately 4 million dinars in material damage between 2025 and 2026. Minister of Transport Rachid Amri reported on Saturday, October 31, that 362 people were also affected, including drivers and sector agents.

The estimated cost covers damage to multiple collective transport vehicles, without a breakdown by operator or year. Amri stated these expenses could have funded other sector developments, citing the cost of replacing broken train windows as an example. In 2025 alone, the SNCFT reported 162,000 dinars for shattered train windows due to thrown stones.

Beyond material repairs, Amri highlighted the disruption to travelers, noting that delayed or immobilized vehicles can cause people to miss work, appointments, or medical visits. He emphasized that the harm to citizens extends beyond the monetary value of the damage, and called for a collective prevention effort involving families and institutions to protect public transport.

Why it mattersTravelers in Tunisia face disruptions and potential delays due to attacks on public transport, impacting their ability to reach work, appointments, or medical care.

Tunisian official warns racist words can incite violence

Khémaïs Gharbi states that public discourse can make hateful speech acceptable and lead to violence.

Khémaïs Gharbi, a Tunisian official, warned that racist words can lead to violence by creating a climate where aggression becomes thinkable and acceptable. He explained that while insults and crimes are different, the path between them can be paved by hateful speech. Gharbi stated that public prejudice gains legitimacy when officials designate populations as scapegoats during sensitive times like election campaigns.

Gharbi highlighted that public figures can exploit fears and harden group identities by blaming specific populations for societal problems. He referenced Martin Luther King Jr.'s fight against the systemic dehumanization of Black Americans, noting that violence is supported by unjust institutions and daily humiliations. The official cautioned that psychological fragility alone does not explain racist violence, which can be fueled by ideology, organized groups, propaganda, or a sense of impunity.

The official emphasized that words shape the world and that public discourse can transform shameful ideas into acceptable ones. He stressed that racism begins not always with a blow, but sometimes with a joke, insinuation, nickname, or look of contempt that reduces individuals to their origin. Gharbi asserted that the danger grows when prejudice receives public legitimacy, making what was once shameful become speakable and eventually permissible.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens are affected as public discourse can normalize prejudice and incite violence against specific groups.
