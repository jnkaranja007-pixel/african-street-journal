Education · 2026-11-17

Tunisia and Wyoming expand academic ties to energy and water

The US ambassador outlines plans for new economic and technological projects following a university exchange program started in October 2024.

Tunisia and the American state of Wyoming are expanding their university partnership, launched in October 2024, into economic and technological cooperation covering renewable energy, water management, and scientific research. Bill Bazzi, the United States ambassador to Tunisia, announced the broader ambitions in an interview broadcast on November 13, 2026, following a visit to Wyoming earlier in the month that included a stop at a university facility in Sfax.

The cooperative framework involves five Tunisian institutions: the universities of Tunis, Sfax, and Jendouba, alongside the National Agronomic Institute of Tunisia and the National Institute for Agricultural Research of Tunisia. Ambassador Bazzi stated that the partnership brings together the governor of Wyoming's office, the state National Guard, and the University of Wyoming to attract renewable energy firms and explore technical solutions for water retention and rainwater recovery following severe droughts and energy disruptions.

Beyond higher education mobility, the initiative targets commercializing innovations in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics fields while leveraging Tunisia's mining experience in phosphate alongside agriculture, tourism, and education sectors.

Why it mattersFive Tunisian universities and agricultural institutes participate in the exchange program with Wyoming, which now targets renewable energy investments and water management solutions.

Tunisia faces Libya in Tripoli for Africa Cup of Nations qualifier

The national football team plays away after a previous defeat in Sfax.

Tunisia plays away against Libya on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, during the fourth matchday of the Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers in group H. The fixture starts at 17h00 at the June Eleventh Stadium in Tripoli, following a 0-2 defeat against the same opponent on Friday in Sfax. Webdo reported that the match will be played behind closed doors because of a disciplinary sanction issued against the Libyan Football Federation by the Confederation of African Football.

Viewers across the region can watch the broadcast live on beIN Sports ar 1 for MENA and beIN Sports fr 2 in France. Supporters inside Tunisia can also tune in through the digital terrestrial network on Al Wataniya 1, alongside streaming options on beIN CONNECT and TOD.

The team seeks recovery in this return fixture to revive qualification prospects after losing the previous encounter. Additional television coverage details were confirmed by La Presse de Tunisie ahead of the kickoff in Libya.

Why it mattersTunisian football supporters can watch the televised qualifier broadcast on Al Wataniya 1 or stream it via digital platforms starting at 17h00.

CCITF hosts transport and logistics forum in Tunis on Dec. 2

The French-Tunisian Chamber of Commerce and Industry gathers sector experts and officials to present a logistics study to 2040.

The French-Tunisian Chamber of Commerce and Industry (CCITF) will host its first sector day dedicated to transport and logistics challenges on Wednesday, 2 December 2026, at the Radisson Blu Tunis on Avenue Mohammed V.

The morning event brings together professionals, experts, and representatives from the transport ministry, the Directorate General of Customs, and the National Customs School to discuss corporate competitiveness.

Attendees will review findings from a CCITF business survey, examine a long-term logistics study titled Vision 2040 focusing on port infrastructure and decarbonation, and witness a partnership agreement signed with the customs school.

Why it mattersBusiness leaders and transport officials meet to review logistics bottlenecks and sign a partnership agreement on Wednesday, 2 December 2026.

Climate · 2026-11-17

Epson Tunisie plants 2 000 trees in Rafraf to reach 4 000 total

The technology firm planted trees in a forest damaged by fires during the National Tree Day celebration.

Epson Tunisie planted 2 000 trees at Mount Nadhour in Rafraf, located in the Bizerte governorate, to celebrate the National Tree Day. La Presse de Tunisie reported that the effort targeted a forest zone damaged by fires, bringing the total number of trees planted by the company over the past two years to 4 000. An earlier operation took place in Zaghouan in 2025 with an identical planting of 2 000 trees.

The project was carried out with the Tounes Clean-Up association, the Soli & Green collective, and the regional agricultural development commissioner's office in Bizerte, known as the CRDA. Imen Gorrab, retail sales director for Tunisia, Algeria and the Indian Ocean at Epson, stated that the initiative aligns with the corporation's environmental vision for the year 2050. Employees and local partners took part in the work to rehabilitate the forest cover and protect local biodiversity.

Tunisia's broader national forest restoration campaign for the 2026-2027 period targets 24 000 hectares and produces 10 million forest plants. The national strategy aims to restore 30 000 hectares of forests by the year 2035. Cooperation between public authorities, private companies and civil society organizations supports these ongoing conservation efforts across the country.

Why it mattersThe national forest restoration campaign targets 24 000 hectares and produces 10 million plants for the 2026-2027 period.

Health · 2026-11-17

Faculty of Medicine in Sousse hosts Sadok Besrour awards

Two researchers will receive 5,000 Canadian dollars each during the ceremony on Friday, 20 November 2026.

The Faculty of Medicine of Sousse will host the official ceremony for the Dr Sadok Besrour Excellence Awards in medicine and health sciences on Friday, 20 November 2026, according to Kapitalis. Created in 2007, the prizes recognize teachers and researchers contributing to medical research and health training in Tunisia through a partnership involving the Dr Sadok Besrour Foundation, the University of Montreal, and the Tunisian Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research.

Two distinctions will be awarded during the event, each carrying a value of 5 000 Canadian dollars. One prize honors excellence in health sciences pedagogy, while the other focuses on fundamental research and its impact. The ceremony will also feature plenary conferences, including a presentation by Professor Latifa Lassouad from the University of Sousse and a conference from the Centre for Pedagogy applied to Health Sciences of the University of Montreal.

The event forms part of international health partnerships, following a University of Montreal agreement announced in January 2025 to host Tunisian medical academics for specialized training. In March 2026, the University of Oxford announced 2 million pounds sterling in support from the Dr Sadok Besrour Foundation to strengthen primary health care research and leadership in Tunisia.
