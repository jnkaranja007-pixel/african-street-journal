Tunisia Ministry Reports 633 Heat-Related Health Consultations

The Ministry of Health has documented 633 health consultations and treatments related to heatwave repercussions between July 1 and August 29.

The Ministry of Health reported 633 health consultations and treatments linked to the heatwave's effects between July 1 and August 29, 2026. This figure, released after alerts from young doctors, does not include any death toll. The ministry stated it is monitoring the situation with regional health structures, but did not specify hospitalization numbers, severity of cases, or regional breakdowns.

The absence of death statistics follows an alert from the Tunisian Organization of Young Doctors, whose president claimed 13 deaths in 24 hours and 21 heatstroke cases requiring intensive care at Sfax's Habib-Bourguiba hospital. The Ministry of Health's statement did not address these specific figures or provide any mortality data, leaving the total human impact of the heatwave unconfirmed.

Despite the lack of mortality figures, the Ministry of Health emphasized the continuity of healthcare services, reporting no interruptions and highlighting coordination mechanisms. They acknowledged the efforts of medical staff and renewed public health recommendations, such as avoiding sun exposure during peak hours and staying hydrated, particularly for vulnerable groups.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Health's report of 633 heat-related health consultations affects the public by providing an official, though incomplete, measure of the heatwave's health impact.

Cinéma Jet brings film events to Zaghouan and Jendouba

The cultural event will feature European and Tunisian films, workshops, and masterclasses in two governorates.

The second edition of Cinéma Jet will take place from September 3 to 13 in Zaghouan and from September 30 to October 11 in Jendouba. This initiative, supported by the European Union Delegation in Tunisia and member states, aims to bring cinema to diverse audiences across the country. Following its first edition, Cinéma Jet continues its mission to decentralize culture and make cinema accessible beyond traditional venues.

In Zaghouan, the event will offer more than just film screenings. It will include artistic workshops, meetings with professionals, masterclasses, collective creations, and immersive experiences designed to engage attendees as both viewers and creators. The program features Tunisian and European films, short and feature-length, fostering dialogue between Tunisian and European cultures. Participating European countries include Germany, France, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, Poland, Belgium, and Switzerland.

Workshops will cover various artistic disciplines, such as theater, dance, illustration, and photography, with a focus on youth engagement and risk awareness. Participants will also learn filmmaking techniques using smartphones. A VR Corner, in partnership with the French Institute of Tunisia, and a CinéDJ event, organized by the Goethe-Institut Tunis, will showcase new forms of storytelling and live musical accompaniment to films.

Why it mattersTunisian and European audiences will engage with films and artistic workshops, fostering cultural exchange.

Tunisian Deputies Increase Support for Extraordinary Parliamentary Session

Sixty-one lawmakers have signed a petition to convene an extraordinary session, with official submission expected this week.

The number of Tunisian deputies seeking an extraordinary parliamentary session has risen to 61, an increase of five since Saturday. This petition is slated for official submission to the Assembly of Representatives of the People (ARP) on Monday, August 31, or Tuesday, September 1. Mohamed Ali, one of the initiative's instigators, confirmed on Sunday that the list of signatories remains open.

This submission marks a critical step, as it moves beyond gathering signatures to formally registering the request for a session. The deputies aim to dedicate this extraordinary meeting to addressing pressing social and economic issues, including water and electricity cuts, rising prices, and product shortages. They intend to question the government's management of these essential service degradations.

The ARP currently has 152 active members. The petition's progression from 56 to 61 signatures highlights a growing consensus among lawmakers to formally address the nation's current challenges. The focus remains on the official registration and subsequent convocation of the session to debate these critical matters.

Why it mattersSixty-one deputies are affected by the need to address the country's social, economic, and public service situation, including issues like water and electricity cuts and rising prices.

Tunisia Floats Costly Power Plant Amidst Grid Failures

A proposed floating power plant to address Tunisia's electricity deficit is criticized for its high cost, technical shortcomings, and delayed arrival.

Tunisia's state electricity company is publishing rolling blackouts schedules as a plan to lease a floating power plant is reportedly being prepared away from public discussion. This ship-based power station, intended as a summer solution, is identified as one of the most expensive options globally. Critics argue it is technically unjustified, will arrive too late, and is being negotiated without transparency guarantees.

The country's electricity deficit is not due to a lack of installed capacity but stems from aging thermal power plants, a fragile transport grid during peak hours, and a near-total halt in new production projects since 2019. Renewable tenders have been delayed or abandoned, and combined cycle projects await funding and permits. Leasing a floating plant does not address these underlying issues; it merely postpones the problem by converting a capital investment into an operational expense that leaves no lasting asset.

The justification for a direct negotiation is urgency, but the timeline for a floating plant is problematic. Contract signing, ship mobilization, transit, mooring, grid connection, fuel supply, and testing take months, not weeks. Recent experience in Gabon showed significant delays in connecting a 150 MW unit. For Tunisia, this means the plant could become operational after the summer peak, rendering the initial justification for its expense moot, yet the contract would still require payment regardless of usage.

Why it mattersTunisia's electricity deficit is being addressed with a costly, temporary solution that does not fix underlying infrastructure and project development issues.

Politics · 2026-08-31

56 Deputies Demand Government Account for Public Services

Lawmakers are calling for an extraordinary parliamentary session to address water and electricity cuts, rising prices, and supply shortages.

Fifty-six Tunisian deputies have formally requested an extraordinary parliamentary session to be convened during the assembly's recess. The group, which surpasses the constitutional threshold, aims to question government officials regarding the nation's social and economic situation. Their demands stem from widespread public discontent over essential services.

The deputies specifically cited recurring water and electricity outages, significant price increases across various goods, and difficulties in obtaining certain products as key issues. They argue that the current problems go beyond temporary malfunctions, raising concerns about the state's capacity to manage crises and meet citizens' basic needs.

This initiative seeks to hold the government accountable for the perceived degradation of public services. The lawmakers believe the situation warrants a thorough parliamentary review to understand the root causes and potential solutions for these pressing national challenges. The session's outcome remains uncertain, but the constitutional threshold for its convening has been met.

Why it mattersFifty-six deputies are affected by the degradation of public services and are demanding accountability from the government.
