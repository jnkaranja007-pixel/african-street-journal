Tunisia risks summer power cuts as projects lag

National electricity demand grows 5% annually, requiring 300 MW more power next year.

Tunisia may not finish projects intended to prevent electricity outages by summer 2027, warned Ali Kenzari, president of the national union for photovoltaic equipment installers and maintainers. National electricity demand is increasing by 5% each year, requiring an additional 300 megawatts of capacity next year. Kenzari stated that the timeline for announced projects does not guarantee their completion before summer 2027, risking delays in boosting production capacity.

Kenzari anticipates an electricity deficit that could reach 2,800 MW next year, following a gap of 2,000 to 2,500 MW during last year's peak consumption. He noted that government plans to improve existing power plants, which involve new tenders, might not be implemented until 2028. The government announced measures in early November to increase production before summer 2027, including 300 MW from renewables and 200 MW from gas engines.

As an immediate solution, Kenzari suggested Tunisia increase electricity imports from Algeria, recalling that the current 800 MW exchange agreement was insufficient last year. He also highlighted the potential of solar photovoltaic energy, stating that installed capacity should reach at least 2,500 MW to strengthen national production and reduce reliance on conventional sources.

Why it mattersTunisia faces a potential electricity deficit of 2,800 MW next year, impacting national supply and requiring increased imports.

Tunisia identifies nearly 79,000 undeclared businesses

The finance minister revealed the scale of the informal economy, which accounts for 35.2% of activity.

Tunisia has identified nearly 79,000 economic activities operating without official declaration between 2022 and September 2026. Finance Minister Michket Slama Khaldi announced the figure of 78,688 undeclared activities on Friday before a joint parliamentary session discussing the 2027 state budget. These findings highlight the significant extent of economic operations outside the formal tax system.

The minister stated that the informal economy represents 35.2% of Tunisia's economic activity, a figure that drops to 28.5% when the agricultural sector is excluded. Khaldi emphasized the need to strengthen control mechanisms and inter-agency coordination to address these undeclared operations. In 2025, fiscal interventions generated approximately 7.3 billion dinars in revenue, and a campaign to broaden the tax base and combat evasion is underway.

Fiscal revenues now constitute 91.4% of the state's own resources, a trend attributed to a decline in non-fiscal revenue rather than increased tax pressure. The minister called for intensified efforts to create wealth and diversify state budget funding sources to reduce reliance on debt. A public awareness campaign is also being conducted to encourage formalization.

Why it mattersNearly 79,000 economic activities operating outside the tax system were identified, impacting state revenue and requiring strengthened controls.

Tunisian car dealers call for lower duties on imported car parts

The national chamber says eight active car plants face double taxation, and it wants tariffs removed on components used for local assembly.

Ibrahim Debbache, president of the National Chamber of Car Concessionaires and Manufacturers (CNCCA), has asked Parliament's finance committee to cut customs duties on car components imported for assembly in Tunisia. He told Mosaique FM on Friday, November 13, that the measure would encourage local investment and bring new vehicles within reach of ordinary buyers.

Debbache said the duties on components are followed by consumption taxes when the assembled car is sold, which he called a double tax that makes locally built vehicles less competitive. He said the chamber's preferred option is to remove duties entirely on components used in local production, and that the eight plants now operating in the sector are struggling. The proposal was put forward during hearings on the 2027 finance bill.

Debbache also linked the proposal to demand, saying the market needs between 80,000 and 90,000 vehicles a year and that more than 30,000 enter through the parallel market. He argued that cheaper local production could eventually support exports, and he urged that it be built into the "A car for every family" project, which was planned in the 2026 finance law but has not been implemented.

Tunisia loses 0-2 to Libya in CAN 2027 qualifier in Sfax

The Carthage Eagles remain without a win in Group H after a penalty by Ali Youssef and a second goal from Hamdou El Houni.

Tunisia lost 2-0 to Libya on Friday 13 November at the Taïeb Mhiri Stadium in Sfax, in the third round of CAN 2027 qualifying for Group H. Tunisia have still not won in the group, after draws against Uganda (1-1) and Botswana (2-2).

Libya took the lead in the 44th minute, when Ali Youssef converted a penalty awarded after a foul by Ali Maâloul. Hamdou El Houni made it 2-0 from the penalty spot in the 64th minute. Tunisia created several chances in the first half, including a header by Mohamed Dräger in the 23rd minute, but could not score.

Tunisia remain last in Group H with two points, while Libya move level with Uganda at the top on four points, though Uganda have played two matches to Libya's three. Uganda play Botswana on Saturday 14 November. The return match against Libya is scheduled for Tuesday 17 November.

Why it mattersTunisia must beat Libya in the return match on Tuesday 17 November to keep their qualification chances alive.

Tunisian olive oil expert joins jury at Buenos Aires competition

Professor Imene Trabelsi Trigui, a Tunisian specialist in olive oil, is serving as a jury member at the fifth Buenos Aires International Olive Oil Competition, which brings.

Professor Imene Trabelsi Trigui, a Tunisian specialist in olive oil, was due to sit on the jury of the fifth Buenos Aires International Olive Oil Competition (BAIOOC) on November 13 and 14, 2026, in Argentina's capital. The competition has been held there since 2021 and aims to promote the quality of extra virgin olive oil.

The Argentine Embassy in Tunisia congratulated Trabelsi Trigui in a statement, saying it was pleased that a Tunisian expert would bring her knowledge and experience to an event held in Argentina. The embassy said she would show the know-how, tradition and excellence of Tunisian olive growing.

The embassy described the appointment as a chance to celebrate ties between Argentina and Tunisia, two countries that share a love of the olive tree. Local outlet Webdo reported the same appointment, describing Trabelsi Trigui as an ambassador for Tunisian olive oil in Argentina.

Why it mattersTunisian olive growers and exporters gain a respected national voice on the jury of an international competition that judges extra virgin olive oil quality.
