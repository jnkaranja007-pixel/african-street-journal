Tunisia expands remote medical consultations

Sfax digital hospital connects Gafsa and Skhira facilities for neurology, cardiology, and nephrology care.

Tunisia's Sfax Digital Hospital is increasing access to specialized healthcare by expanding remote medical consultations in neurology, cardiology, and nephrology. This initiative aims to bring health services closer to citizens, reduce travel to major hospitals, and improve patient follow-up. The Ministry of Health announced on Thursday, November 5, 2026, that several hospitals now offer these remote consultations coordinated with Sfax university hospital teams.

In Gafsa governorate, the Houcine Bouzaïane Regional Hospital has implemented remote neurology consultations in partnership with the Sfax University Hospital Habib Bourguiba's neurology department. Simultaneously, the Skhira district hospital has begun offering remote cardiology consultations, coordinated with the Sfax University Hospital Hédi Chaker's specialized department. The nephrology service at the same university hospital also provides remote consultations, allowing patients specialized follow-up without needing to travel to Sfax.

These services are organized on a weekly schedule, coordinated with the central team of the Digital Hospital under the Ministry of Health. The system is expected to gradually extend to other medical fields, with ongoing work to integrate new specialties and connect more health facilities in neighboring governorates. The Ministry of Health also aims to reduce pressure on hospitals, shorten waiting times, and lower patient travel costs.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens in Gafsa and Skhira governorates can now access specialized medical care remotely, reducing their need to travel for consultations in neurology, cardiology, and nephrology.

Tunisia jails former tobacco boss for 18 years

The former head of the national tobacco and matches company was also ordered to repay over 90,000 dinars.

Tunis's Court of First Instance has sentenced the former chief executive of the national tobacco and matches company to 18 years in prison. The court convicted him in absentia for embezzling large quantities of cigarettes intended for sale. He was found guilty of exploiting his public office to cause financial harm to the administration and gain an improper advantage.

The court also ordered the former official to return more than 90,000 dinars to the public enterprise. The charges against him included abusing his position for personal or third-party benefit. The ruling was delivered by the criminal chamber of the Tunis court.

The former leader's conviction by default means he was not present for the trial. The Régie Nationale du tabac et des allumettes (RNTA) is the state-owned entity involved in the case.

Tunisia temperatures rise above seasonal norms

Meteorologists forecast warmer weather across North Africa, with Tunisia expecting highs up to 28°C.

Tunisia is experiencing an unusual temperature increase as winter approaches, with thermometers set to rise above seasonal averages across the country. Mehrez Ghannouchi from the National Institute of Meteorology reported on Wednesday, November 4, that this warming trend will affect all Tunisian regions and several other North African nations.

In Tunis, maximum temperatures are predicted to reach 25°C on Friday, November 6, climbing to 26°C on Monday and peaking at 28°C by Tuesday and Wednesday, November 10 and 11. These warmer days will be accompanied by cooler nights, with minimum temperatures ranging from 14°C to 17°C.

Seasonal forecasts for November to January 2026-2027 indicate generally higher temperatures than usual for Tunisia. The institute also anticipates below-average rainfall, particularly in the northern and central regions, a trend that warrants attention as the winter period is important for water resources and agricultural activities.

Why it mattersTunisians will experience warmer than usual weather in the coming days and throughout the winter, potentially impacting water resources and agriculture.

Tunisian man admits killing pregnant wife in Montreal

Mustapha Khalil Mechken pleaded guilty on November 3, 2026, to the second-degree murder of his wife, Narjes Ben Yedder.

Mustapha Khalil Mechken, a Tunisian living in Montreal, Canada, pleaded guilty on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, to the second-degree murder of his wife, Narjes Ben Yedder. She was 10 weeks pregnant with their first child when Mechken killed her in 2024 with 49 stab wounds, many to her abdomen. He calmly reported the crime to emergency services, stating he was angry and killed her.

The 45-year-old Mechken, who married Ben Yedder, 32, two years prior, admitted he did not want a child and was concerned about a drop in family income due to maternity leave. The prosecution is seeking a life sentence with no parole for 22 years, with the judge expected to deliver a decision next week. Mechken remained impassive during the court proceedings.

Investigators found Mechken had searched online for information regarding inheritance of bank accounts, assets, and tax-free savings accounts if a spouse dies, and also researched "burn out" and intrusive thoughts. He reportedly took two large kitchen knives to attack his sleeping wife in their bed.

Why it mattersA Tunisian national living in Canada has been convicted of murdering his pregnant wife, impacting the Tunisian diaspora in Montreal.

Health · 2026-11-06

Tunisia suspends pharmacy payments over tier-pay dispute

The social affairs minister ordered payments halted until pharmacies end their tier-pay system suspension.

Tunisia's Minister of Social Affairs, Issam Lahmar, ordered a halt to payments owed to private pharmacies on November 5, 2026. This action is a response to the pharmacies' suspension of the tier-pay system, which began on November 1, 2026, due to unpaid debts from the National Health Insurance Fund (CNAM). The minister stated payments will remain blocked until pharmacies resume their participation in the tier-pay system.

The National Council of the Order of Pharmacists of Tunisia (CNOPT) expressed surprise at the minister's declarations, warning that the situation could worsen the ongoing crisis in the medicine sector. The CNOPT stated that the suspension of the tier-pay system by pharmacies was a reaction to persistent payment delays and accumulated debts from CNAM, causing significant financial strain and threatening their operations.

The conflict's consequences extend beyond private pharmacies, potentially destabilizing the entire pharmaceutical sector, including wholesale distributors and manufacturers. The CNOPT highlighted risks to financial balances, jobs, business continuity, and the availability and supply of medicines for patients. The council called for dialogue and sustainable solutions to settle debts and ensure the sector's continued operation.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens relying on the tier-pay system face potential disruptions in medicine availability and continuity of treatment.
