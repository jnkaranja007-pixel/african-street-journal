Tunisia awards Elmed cable contract

Prysmian secures 460 million euro deal for 200-kilometer subsea power link to Italy.

Tunisia and Italy's Elmed electricity interconnection project has entered a decisive phase with the awarding of key contracts. The project, linking the two Mediterranean shores via the Strait of Sicily, will have a 600-megawatt bidirectional capacity and an estimated total cost of 1.42 billion euros. Hitachi Energy will build conversion stations in Tunisia and Sicily for approximately 770 million euros, while Prysmian secured the 460 million euro contract for the 200-kilometer subsea cable.

The Elmed project's second phase involves strengthening Tunisia's national grid to integrate renewable energy and transmit power from southern regions. This includes a 400-kilovolt overhead line between Grombalia and Kondar, covering about 85 kilometers, and 20 kilometers of additional connection lines. The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (BERD) signed a 43 million euro financing agreement on January 15, 2026, for this infrastructure, with the loan to Steg to be repaid over 18 years.

Parliamentary review of the financing guarantee agreement is underway, with a request for urgent examination. The Elmed project is supported by the European Union with a 307.6 million euro grant, alongside funding from the World Bank, BERD, the European Investment Bank (BEI), and Germany's KfW. The subsea link's commissioning is currently planned between 2028 and 2030.

Why it mattersTunisian electricity infrastructure and national grid are being upgraded and financed through international agreements, impacting the national electricity company Steg.

Tunisie Autoroutes plants 100,000 trees by 2027

The company will also maintain existing green spaces along its highway network as part of a broader environmental initiative.

Tunisie Autoroutes announced a plan to plant approximately 100,000 trees and shrubs during the 2026-2027 season. This operation, which will be deployed across 10 districts, aims to increase vegetation along the highway network.

The program is coordinated with the Directorate General of Forests and regional services, operating under a framework agreement until 2030. It includes maintaining existing green spaces at rest areas, control centers, and toll stations.

These efforts are part of the company's commitment to the "Pact for a Green Tunisia" and contribute to reducing fire risks and enhancing user safety. Seasonal tasks like grass cutting and pruning are also scheduled.

Why it mattersTunisian residents using the highway network will see increased vegetation and maintained green spaces, contributing to a greener environment.

Tunisia braces for higher temperatures and strong winds

Maximum temperatures will reach 28°C in most regions on Monday, November 9, 2026.

Tunisia will experience cloudy skies and a slight rise in temperatures on Monday, November 9, 2026. The National Institute of Meteorology (INM) forecasts maximum temperatures between 23°C and 28°C across most of the country.

Temperatures will not exceed 21°C in the western highlands. Winds will blow from the south, becoming relatively strong to strong near the coasts and on the highlands, while remaining light to moderate elsewhere. The sea will be generally slightly rough, becoming very rough in the Gulf of Hammamet.

The INM issued these forecasts for Monday, November 9, 2026. The weather conditions are expected to affect daily activities and maritime travel.

Why it mattersTunisians will experience higher temperatures and stronger winds affecting their daily lives and travel plans.
