Tunisia plans robotic surgery training center

Tunisia and South Korea agreed to establish a simulation-based robotic surgery training center for African doctors.

Tunisia will host experts from the International Vaccine Institute (IVI) from December 1 to 4, 2026, to support the Tunisia BioHub project. The visit, funded by South Korea, aims to advance research, training, and biological manufacturing.

An action plan was also adopted to create a simulation-based robotic surgery training center in Tunisia. This facility will cater to Tunisian and other African physicians, building on existing Tunisian-South Korean cooperation in robotic surgery.

Discussions also covered artificial intelligence applications in healthcare, including research, training, technology transfer, and service improvement. The Tunisian Health Minister also met with his Turkish and Qatari counterparts to explore cooperation in digital health and pharmaceuticals.

Why it mattersTunisian and African doctors will gain access to advanced robotic surgery training.

Tunisian lawmakers question 2027 growth target

A proposed finance bill aims for 3.1% growth but faces skepticism over insufficient measures for investment and industry.

Tunisia's proposed finance bill for 2027, focused on wealth creation and investment, is facing scrutiny in the Assembly of People's Representatives (ARP). Lawmaker Issam Chouchane has voiced concerns that the proposed economic measures may not be enough to achieve the targeted 3.1% growth rate. He suggested that a supplementary finance law might be necessary if the initial proposals fall short.

Maher Ktari, president of the ARP's Finance and Budget Commission, stated that the 2027 bill prioritizes investment and economic development, differentiating it from the more socially focused 2026 law. However, Chouchane pointed out that certain provisions might not adequately protect Tunisian industry and called for revisions. Ktari emphasized the need to boost exports and investment to strengthen foreign currency reserves and support economic recovery.

Chouchane also highlighted the difficulty of assessing the finance bill without a detailed explanatory document, which could lead to varied interpretations. He believes the measures for startups and small businesses are insufficient for the 3.1% growth objective. The possibility of a supplementary finance law depends on factors like oil and grain prices, inflation, and exchange rates, with the government yet to make a decision.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens and businesses are affected by the proposed finance bill's potential to not meet its economic growth targets, impacting investment and industry protection.

Tunis police arrest three brothers for drug trafficking

Authorities seized cocaine, a scale, and cash during a raid on a home in El Hafsia, Tunis.

Police in Tunis arrested three brothers on Wednesday morning for allegedly trafficking and distributing drugs. The arrests occurred after the judicial police brigade of Bab Souika raided a residence in the Houmet El Jraba neighborhood of El Hafsia, within the Medina of Tunis.

During the operation, the three individuals were apprehended in the act of selling narcotics. A subsequent search of the premises led to the seizure of a significant quantity of cocaine, a small electronic scale used for weighing drugs, and a substantial amount of money believed to be proceeds from their criminal activities.

The public prosecutor has ordered the three suspects to be held in custody while investigations continue. They are awaiting their appearance before the court.

Why it mattersTunisian residents are affected by the arrest of three brothers involved in drug trafficking and the subsequent seizure of illegal substances.

Tunis court postpones journalist Zied El-Heni trial

The journalist's support committee is demanding his release after the case, which concerns leaked recordings, was adjourned for the fifth time.

The trial of journalist Zied El-Heni has been postponed again by the Tunisian justice system, with the next hearing set for October 27. This marks the fifth adjournment for the case, which involves leaked audio recordings and alleged financial misconduct linked to an Islamist party. El-Heni was initially called as a witness but later faced legal proceedings.

The fifth correctional chamber of the Tunis Court of First Instance, which handles terrorism cases, announced the postponement. El-Heni's support committee expressed disappointment with the delay and reiterated their call for his immediate release. The case dates back to September 2023 and centers on audio recordings attributed to Mondher Ounissi.

The recordings reportedly detail Ounissi's meetings with influential businessmen and touch upon alleged financial irregularities associated with the Islamist party. The previous hearing for this case was scheduled for October 16, but was also adjourned, leading to the current repeated delays.

Why it mattersJournalist Zied El-Heni is affected by the postponement of his trial, which could lead to further legal uncertainty.

Politics · 2026-10-22

Tunisia aims to halve road deaths by 2034

The government plans to reduce road fatalities and serious injuries by 50% over the next decade through infrastructure upgrades and new technology.

Tunisia has set ambitious targets to significantly reduce road traffic deaths and serious injuries by 2034. The national strategy aims for a 10% reduction by 2027, followed by 25% by 2030, and a final 50% reduction by 2034. This plan is part of a broader security program that includes modernizing security forces' equipment, such as installing smart radars and acquiring water-bombing planes and rescue helicopters.

To achieve these road safety goals, the strategy focuses on five key areas: improving infrastructure, enhancing user safety, promoting multimodal urban transport, implementing intelligent transport systems, ensuring vehicle safety, and strengthening emergency response services. The plan also involves implementing an automated system to control traffic violations using cameras and smart radars, revising technical inspection sanctions, and tightening rules on non-approved spare parts.

The security program also prioritizes strengthening operational, logistical, and digital capabilities to combat evolving crime, including economic and cyber offenses, money laundering, smuggling, and irregular migration. This involves modernizing security units, constructing and expanding police and national guard stations, and continuing efforts against violent extremism and terrorism.

Why it mattersTunisian citizens will be affected by a reduction in road traffic deaths and serious injuries.
