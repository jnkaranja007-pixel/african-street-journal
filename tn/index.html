Tunisia ministry supports children after mother's murder

The ministry intervened to protect the identities of the victim's minor children following the crime in Jebal Jelloud.

Tunisia's Ministry of Family, Women, Childhood and Elderly Affairs intervened to support the minor children of a mother murdered in Jebal Jelloud, Tunis. Ministry services assessed the family's social situation under judicial supervision to identify needs and provide necessary support, including psychological care. The ministry aims to protect the children from risks associated with online exposure of their identities.

The ministry's regional services are coordinating to organize rigorous social follow-up and emergency psychological support for the children affected by the tragedy. This action follows the crime where the 22-year-old victim died from her husband's violent blows. The husband has a history of repeated domestic violence.

The ministry issued a statement reminding users of social media and digital spaces to preserve the children's best interests and refrain from disclosing their personal data. It warned against disseminating photographs or information that could indirectly identify the children, recalling legal provisions for personal data protection.

Why it mattersThe minor children of a murdered mother in Jebal Jelloud are receiving support and protection for their identities from the Ministry of Family.

Tunisia forecasts warmer, drier winter

Temperatures are expected to be above seasonal norms from November 2026 to January 2027, with less rain than usual.

Tunisia will experience warmer weather on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, with temperatures reaching up to 25°C in some areas. Scattered showers are expected along the northern coast, while the rest of the country will see cloudy skies.

The National Meteorology Institute (INM) forecasts that November 2026 through January 2027 will be warmer than average. The institute also predicts a potential rainfall deficit, particularly in the northern and central regions, contrasting with typical winter patterns.

Average temperatures usually range from 8°C to 15°C in the north and center, and 12°C to 16°C in the south during these months. Rainfall normals for November to January vary significantly, with the north typically receiving the most precipitation.

Why it mattersTunisians will face warmer conditions and less rain than usual during the upcoming winter months, affecting daily life and potentially water resources.

Tunisia president revives Djerba bridge project

President Kaïs Saïed discussed a 2.4-kilometer bridge linking Djerba to the mainland with his infrastructure minister.

President Kaïs Saïed discussed a proposed 2.4-kilometer bridge to connect Djerba island with the mainland at El Jorf. The meeting with Infrastructure Minister Salah Zouari on November 2, 2026, aimed to replace current ferry services and the Roman road with a permanent link. This project seeks to ease travel for residents and visitors who face delays with the existing maritime transport.

The bridge would span from Ajim on Djerba to El Jorf on the mainland, requiring road improvements on the island. Initial studies in October 2023 estimated the cost between 900 million and one billion dinars, with a projected construction time of three and a half years. SCET-Tunisie conducted the technical, economic, financial, and environmental studies with funding from the French Development Agency.

The Djerba bridge has been considered for years to boost tourism and the island's economy. In February 2025, the General Authority for Public-Private Partnership still listed it as a planned operation. President Saïed also urged a reform of public procurement rules to speed up project completion and combat corruption.

Why it mattersTunisian residents and visitors to Djerba island will experience reduced travel difficulties if the bridge project is completed.

Tunis hosts 11th human rights film festival

The festival runs November 3-7, 2026, at Ciné-Théâtre Le Rio with a focus on climate justice.

Tunis is hosting the 11th Human Screen Festival, an international film event organized by ACTIF with the National Centre for Cinema and Image (CNCI). The festival runs from November 3 to 7, 2026, at the Ciné-Théâtre Le Rio under the slogan "Occupy the screen, occupy the space."

This year's theme is "She resists, she adapts: cinema, civil society and climate justice," and the programming features international films on human rights, women's resistance, migration, identity, and social and climate justice.

The festival uses cinema as a space for expression and reflection on contemporary human and social issues, highlighting civil society's role in defending rights and freedoms.

Why it mattersTunisians can attend the Human Screen Festival in Tunis to engage with films on human rights and climate justice.
