Tunisia Launches Pilot Floating Solar Study at Sidi El Barrak Dam

A pilot study to install floating solar panels on the Sidi El Barrak dam aims to generate electricity and reduce water evaporation.

Tunisia's Ministry of Agriculture launched a pilot study on August 27 to assess the installation of floating solar panels at the Sidi El Barrak dam in Béja governorate. The initiative seeks to produce electricity while simultaneously decreasing water loss through evaporation from the reservoir. The findings from this experiment will determine the feasibility of replicating this technology on other Tunisian water bodies.

This floating solar system involves placing photovoltaic panels on the surface of a water reservoir. At Sidi El Barrak, the dual purpose is to utilize existing surface area for power generation and to mitigate water loss due to evaporation. This project addresses Tunisia's growing electricity demands and the increasing pressure on its water resources, aligning with the TECH-EAU research program focused on water management and technological solutions.

The ministry emphasized that this is not yet an announcement for a full-scale solar plant construction. The current phase involves an in-depth study of the pilot site's technical, hydraulic, environmental, and economic aspects. Conclusions on the system's relevance, energy efficiency, and impact on evaporation are needed before any consideration of expanding the technology to other dams.

Why it mattersThe study affects Tunisia's water security and renewable energy development by testing a technology that could reduce water evaporation and generate electricity.

Tunisian judoka Oumaima Bdioui wins silver at Tarente 2026 Mediterranean Games

Oumaima Bdioui secured the silver medal in the -48kg category, marking Tunisia's first judo podium at the Games.

Tunisian judoka Oumaima Bdioui earned a silver medal in the under-48kg category at the Tarente 2026 Mediterranean Games. She was defeated in the final by Turkey's Tuğçe Beder. This podium adds to Bdioui's successful season, which included a victory at the Tunis African Open and a bronze medal at the African Championships. Her performance in Tarente represents the first judo medal for Tunisia in this edition of the Games.

Bdioui advanced to the final by defeating Algeria's Rahma Ghalima in the quarterfinals and Italy's Giulia Ghiglione in the semifinals. The final match concluded with a referee's decision in favor of her opponent. Bdioui stated that her opponent had studied her style and limited her attack options. Tunisian national coach Nihel Cheikhrouhou expressed satisfaction, viewing the result as excellent preparation for the upcoming World Championships in Azerbaijan.

This silver medal follows Bdioui's gold at the Tunis African Open in February and bronze at the African Seniors Championships in Nairobi in April. She also won bronze at the 2022 Oran Mediterranean Games. With this latest achievement, Tunisia now holds 15 medals at the Tarente Games: 2 gold, 4 silver, and 9 bronze.

Why it mattersTunisian judoka Oumaima Bdioui's silver medal win at the Tarente 2026 Mediterranean Games provides Tunisia with its first judo podium at the event.

Tunisian Government Restores Double Workday for Public Employees

State employees will return to a split workday schedule starting September 1, 2026, after a summer of reduced hours.

Tunisia's government announced the return to a double workday for state employees, local authorities, and administrative public institutions. This change, effective Tuesday, September 1, 2026, marks the end of the summer's single-session schedule, which was associated with decreased productivity.

The Presidency of the government issued a statement on Friday, August 28, 2026, detailing the new administrative hours. From Monday to Thursday, work will be divided into two sessions: 8:30 to 12:30 and 13:30 to 17:30. This aims to boost efficiency after the summer break.

Friday's schedule will also be split, with a morning session from 8:00 to 13:00 and an afternoon session from 14:30 to 17:30. This administrative shift is intended to re-establish a more rigorous work rhythm across public sector entities.

Why it mattersPublic sector employees in Tunisia will work longer hours with the return to a double workday, affecting their daily schedules and potentially their work-life balance.

Tunisia Destroys 33,000 School Supplies for Heavy Metals

Authorities have removed over 33,000 non-compliant school supplies containing heavy metals from the market since the start of 2026.

Tunisian authorities have intensified checks on school supplies, destroying or re-exporting 33,084 items found to contain heavy metals since the beginning of 2026. Salwa Fatnassi, director of quality and consumer protection at the Ministry of Commerce, stated that these products were removed due to the presence of substances posing health risks, particularly for children. This action represents an increase from the 30,648 items previously reported as non-compliant earlier in August.

In addition to the destroyed or re-exported items, another 2,490 articles have been prohibited or seized. This separate category includes 1,926 items designed to resemble food, which present a particular risk due to their appearance. These figures highlight distinct actions taken against different types of non-compliant products entering the market.

Overall, Tunisia imported 66,257,591 school supply items since the start of the year, with approximately 90% of products sold being imported. The significant volume of imports underscores the necessity for stringent controls to ensure the safety and conformity of goods intended for students as the school year approaches.

Why it mattersOver 33,000 school supplies containing heavy metals were removed from the market, posing a potential health risk to children.

Tunisian Insurers Eye Residential Solar Panel Coverage

A new voluntary insurance product could offer a solution for protecting growing photovoltaic investments against diverse risks.

Residential solar panel installations are increasingly viewed as significant patrimonial and energy investments, necessitating tailored protection. These systems, designed for decades of operation, face numerous risks including weather events, fires, electrical faults, installation defects, building damage, theft, aging, production interruptions, and third-party liability. Insurance companies are exploring this area as a potential new market, but effective coverage requires a clear understanding of the specific risks associated with each installation.

To address this, an independent risk assessment process is proposed. This would involve a third party evaluating installations to provide insurers with a comprehensive tool for understanding, qualifying, and managing photovoltaic risks. The assessment would cover documentation, technical aspects of components like modules and inverters, and specific risk analyses for electrical issues, fire, weather, and maintenance. This objective qualification allows insurers to assess installations beyond mere power output or value.

The development of a standardized indicator, the 'PV Risk Score,' is suggested to make the risk assessment process objective and scalable. This score would synthesize the findings from the independent expertise, providing insurers with a quantifiable measure of an installation's risk profile. Such a system could facilitate the creation of appropriate insurance policies for the growing number of residential solar energy systems.

Why it mattersInsurers and investors in Tunisia are affected by the need for objective risk assessment to underwrite photovoltaic installations, potentially creating new insurance products and investment security.
