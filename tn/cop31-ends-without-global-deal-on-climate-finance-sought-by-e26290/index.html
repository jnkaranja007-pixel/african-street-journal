COP31 ends without global deal on climate finance sought by Tunisia

Tunisia estimates $55 billion in climate-finance needs for 2026-2035, including $10.7 billion for water and about $8 billion for agriculture.

Source: Webdo · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

The Conference of the Parties (COP31) climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, ended on 22 November without an overall political agreement after two weeks of negotiations. Tunisia had sought accessible, predictable climate finance to carry out adaptation projects, and the conference failed to settle two political decisions proposed by its presidency.

Tunisia’s national statement on 18 November called for sufficient funds that could be mobilised within timeframes suited to local needs. Its third nationally determined contribution estimates climate-finance needs at about $55 billion for 2026-2035, with nearly $29 billion for adaptation. Water accounts for $10.7 billion of that adaptation need, while agriculture and food security account for about $8 billion.

The conference reached negotiated decisions on a just transition and advanced several sector initiatives, but those outcomes did not produce an overall political compromise. Existing funding arrangements and bilateral cooperation opportunities remain open, Webdo reported.

Why it mattersWater and agriculture projects in Tunisia are among the adaptation priorities, with estimated needs of $10.7 billion and about $8 billion respectively.
