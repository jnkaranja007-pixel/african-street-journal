Tunisia says alternative sentences exclude crimes

Justice Minister Leïla Jaffel told Parliament the measures apply only to eligible offences and require the convicted person’s consent.

Source: La Presse de Tunisie · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Tunisia’s Justice Minister, Leïla Jaffel, told Parliament on Saturday that alternatives to prison do not apply to crimes and are limited to offences that meet legal conditions. She said the convicted person’s consent is one of the criteria.

Jaffel gave the figures during a joint plenary session on prisons and release mechanisms. Between 1 January and 17 November 2026, judges responsible for enforcing sentences granted conditional release to 3 423 detainees. A total of 10 363 detainees were released during that period, she said.

Jaffel also cited 13 164 beneficiaries of presidential pardons and conditional-release measures as she explained the legal routes out of detention to lawmakers.

Why it mattersPeople convicted of eligible offences must consent to an alternative sentence, while those convicted of crimes are excluded.
