Tunisia and Wyoming expand academic ties to energy and water

The US ambassador outlines plans for new economic and technological projects following a university exchange program started in October 2024.

Sources: Webdo and La Presse de Tunisie · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Tunisia and the American state of Wyoming are expanding their university partnership, launched in October 2024, into economic and technological cooperation covering renewable energy, water management, and scientific research. Bill Bazzi, the United States ambassador to Tunisia, announced the broader ambitions in an interview broadcast on November 13, 2026, following a visit to Wyoming earlier in the month that included a stop at a university facility in Sfax.

The cooperative framework involves five Tunisian institutions: the universities of Tunis, Sfax, and Jendouba, alongside the National Agronomic Institute of Tunisia and the National Institute for Agricultural Research of Tunisia. Ambassador Bazzi stated that the partnership brings together the governor of Wyoming's office, the state National Guard, and the University of Wyoming to attract renewable energy firms and explore technical solutions for water retention and rainwater recovery following severe droughts and energy disruptions.

Beyond higher education mobility, the initiative targets commercializing innovations in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics fields while leveraging Tunisia's mining experience in phosphate alongside agriculture, tourism, and education sectors.

Why it mattersFive Tunisian universities and agricultural institutes participate in the exchange program with Wyoming, which now targets renewable energy investments and water management solutions.
