Tunisia’s medicines agency puts 50-dinar fee on pharmacies, not patients

The charge covers administrative review of exceptional orders for treatments not authorised for sale in Tunisia, the agency said.

Source: Webdo · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Tunisia’s National Agency for Medicines and Health Products (ANMPS) said pharmacies, not patients, must pay a 50-dinar fee for exceptional orders of medicines not authorised for sale in the country. The agency issued the clarification on Sunday, 22 November, after social media posts raised concerns that patients would face an added charge.

The fee covers the agency’s administrative review of an order dossier and the medicine. Such orders are for a patient’s specific therapeutic need, including cases with no suitable alternative on the Tunisian market; a medical file, prescription and pharmacist’s request are required. Supply remains subject to regulatory approvals.

A ministerial order published in the Official Gazette on 13 November set the charge. The private pharmacists’ owners’ union had criticised the administrative steps on 16 November and questioned who would pay.

Why it mattersPharmacies filing these exceptional orders must cover the 50-dinar administrative charge rather than pass it to patients.
