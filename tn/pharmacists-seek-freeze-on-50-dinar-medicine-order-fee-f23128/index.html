Pharmacists seek freeze on 50-dinar medicine order fee

Tunisia’s pharmacy union wants the charge suspended until the health minister makes a clear, final decision on its legal basis.

Source: La Presse de Tunisie · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Tunisia’s private pharmacy union is seeking an immediate, temporary freeze on a 50-dinar fee for processing orders of confirmed medicines. It wants the charge suspended until the health minister makes a clear, final decision.

Emna Abbes, the union’s deputy secretary-general, said pharmacies need clarification of the legal basis for paying the fee and called for the procedure to be reviewed with professional organisations. She said it would add financial and administrative costs and could complicate procedures and disrupt services for citizens.

Abbes also said payments owed to private pharmacies by the National Health Insurance Fund remain unsettled. The National Agency for Medicines and Health Products said citizens will not pay an extra 50 dinars for medicines, and that the fee does not apply to usual medicines stocked by pharmacies.

Why it mattersPrivate pharmacies are still waiting for sums owed by the national health insurer while the health minister must decide whether the 50-dinar processing fee stands.
