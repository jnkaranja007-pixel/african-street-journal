Tunisia's training system faces a skills mismatch as AI reshapes work

Degrees alone no longer guarantee lasting employment as AI automates writing, research, programming and data analysis, an article argues.

Source: Kapitalis · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Tunisia faces a growing gap between the qualifications its education system produces and the skills employers need, as artificial intelligence changes which work carries value. In an article in Kapitalis, Abdelwaheb Ben Moussa argues that the country should not choose between university and vocational training, but build a model that turns knowledge into skills and skills into economic value.

Ben Moussa writes that AI already automates some tasks in writing, research, programming, translation and data analysis. A degree is still required in many professions, he says, but it no longer guarantees lasting employment on its own. Workers who can define a problem, check information, interpret results and take responsibility for decisions, he argues, will be better placed than those limited to repetitive tasks.

The article cites remarks by Alex Karp, chief executive of Palantir Technologies, who said at Davos in January 2026 that AI could threaten some jobs in the humanities and that technical training would keep particular value. In a March interview he said people with vocational training and neurodivergent profiles were well placed for the change. The article notes these views are not a scientific consensus.

Why it mattersGraduates who hold only routine skills face shrinking demand, while employers must find workers able to interpret and decide.
