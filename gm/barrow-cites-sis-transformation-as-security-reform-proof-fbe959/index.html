Barrow cites SIS transformation as security reform proof

President Adama Barrow stated the intelligence service's evolution demonstrates significant progress in security sector reforms.

Source: The Point · 2026-11-03 · 2 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

President Adama Barrow has identified the transformation of The Gambia's intelligence service into the State Intelligence Service (SIS) as a key indicator of security sector reforms under his leadership. In an interview with QTV journalist SO Nyassi, Barrow contrasted the past fear associated with the former National Intelligence Agency (NIA) with the current professional approach of the SIS. He stated that even the SIS Director General's visits now inspire no fear, unlike before.

An insider familiar with SIS operations noted that the Director General's directive to eliminate operational excesses has been a guiding principle for nearly a decade. Barrow's remarks align with his 2017 campaign promise to reform the security architecture and strengthen democratic governance. The government has stressed that security institutions must operate professionally, respect the rule of law, and uphold citizens' rights.

The SIS recently validated the State Intelligence Service Bill, intended to replace the former NIA's legal framework and considered a vital part of the reform agenda. Barrow also commented on ECOMIG forces, describing them as complementary to Gambian services in maintaining peace and supporting democracy, and affirmed their eventual departure. He highlighted the adoption of a national defence policy as a milestone in building professional, accountable, and democratic institutions.

Why it mattersGambians can expect security services to operate more professionally and with greater respect for their rights.
