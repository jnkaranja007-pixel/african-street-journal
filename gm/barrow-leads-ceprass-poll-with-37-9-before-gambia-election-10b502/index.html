Barrow leads CepRass poll with 37.9% before Gambia election

The survey found 52% dissatisfied with government delivery on promises and 80% rating the national economy badly.

Sources: The Point, The Alkamba Times and Kerr Fatou · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Among respondents holding valid voter cards, 37.9% said they would back the incumbent, according to a survey by the Centre for Policy, Research and Strategic Studies (CepRass). The Gambia’s presidential election is scheduled for December 5.

United Democratic Party leader Ousainou A.N.M. Darboe received 24.2% and Talib Ahmed Bensouda 11.3%. 52 percent of respondents judged the government’s delivery on promises ineffective or very ineffective, 80% rated the national economy badly, and 43% named the cost of living as the country’s biggest concern.

Fieldwork ran from October 17 to November 1, with 1,180 people questioned in 118 enumeration areas across all eight local government areas. CepRass said the figures capture opinion during fieldwork and should not be read as a forecast of the result. Candidate competence was the leading stated influence on voting decisions, cited by 39%.

Why it mattersVoters face the December 5 election after 43% of respondents named the cost of living as the country’s biggest concern.
