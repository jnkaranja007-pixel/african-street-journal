ICJ rules Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya, orders reparations

The ruling, brought by The Gambia in 2019 and backed by the Organization of Islamic Cooperation, is final with no possibility of appeal.

Sources: The Alkamba Times and Africanews · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

The International Court of Justice ruled on Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya people during the military's "clearance operations" of 2016 and 2017, in breach of the 1948 Genocide Convention. It is the first time the UN's top court has found a State responsible for genocide, and it ordered Myanmar to make reparations for the harm inflicted.

The Gambia instituted the case in November 2019, backed by the Organization of Islamic Cooperation, on the basis that every State party to the Convention shares the duty to prevent and punish genocide. President Adama Barrow paid tribute to Rohingya survivors and witnesses whose testimony proved decisive, and commended Attorney General Dawda A. Jallow, his predecessor Abubacarr M. Tambadou and the legal team.

The Gambia has called on Myanmar to comply fully and without delay, and urged the United Nations, the Organization of Islamic Cooperation and the wider international community to support implementation. That includes payment of reparations and conditions that would allow the safe and voluntary return of Rohingya refugees, more than 700,000 of whom were forced into Bangladesh.

Why it mattersMyanmar must now pay reparations and create conditions for the safe return of Rohingya refugees, and the ruling cannot be appealed.
