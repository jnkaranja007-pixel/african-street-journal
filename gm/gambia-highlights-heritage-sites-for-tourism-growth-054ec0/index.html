Gambia highlights heritage sites for tourism growth

NCAC Director General Hassoum Ceesay promotes Kunta Kinteh Island and Wassu Stone Circles beyond beach tourism.

Source: The Point · 2026-10-26 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The Gambia's National Centre for Arts and Culture (NCAC) is promoting cultural heritage sites to boost tourism beyond beaches. Director General Hassoum Ceesay highlighted Kunta Kinteh Island and the Wassu Stone Circles, both UNESCO World Heritage Sites, in an interview at the NCAC headquarters.

Ceesay described Kunta Kinteh Island, formerly James Island, as a site of horror and terror, noting its role as a departure point for slaves during the Transatlantic Slave Trade. Renamed in 2011 to honor Alex Haley's "Roots" hero, the island served as a barracoon where captives were held until ships were full.

The government has plans to enhance Kunta Kinteh Island's appeal and educational value, while the Wassu Stone Circles are also recognized as an archaeological enigma and a key tourism attraction. Ceesay aims to give these sites their true meaning for Gambians and humanity.

Why it mattersGambians and humanity can learn about the historical significance of cultural heritage sites like Kunta Kinteh Island and the Wassu Stone Circles.
