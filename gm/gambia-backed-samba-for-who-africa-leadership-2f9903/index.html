Gambia backed Samba for WHO Africa leadership

A Gambian delegation lobbied nations in 1999 for Dr Ebrahim Malick Samba's re-election to lead WHO's regional committee.

A Gambian delegation traveled to Windhoek, Namibia, in September 1999 to support the re-election of Dr Ebrahim Malick Samba as the World Health Organisation (WHO) Regional Director for Africa. The delegation, including the then permanent secretary at the Ministry of Health and Social Welfare, engaged with regional counterparts to secure support for Dr Samba, whose reputation in African public health was already established.

Dr Samba, who served as WHO Regional Director for Africa from 1995 to 2005, had previously led the WHO Onchocerciasis Control Programme from 1980 to 1994, an initiative that required sustained regional cooperation to control river blindness across national borders. His leadership in this program demonstrated the achievements possible through collaboration and expertise.

Beyond his international roles, Dr Samba was instrumental in supporting the establishment of medical education in The Gambia. Through WHO's Brazzaville office, technical assistance and professional expertise were provided to develop the initiative, fostering local capacity and recognizing the importance of trained professionals in delivering healthcare.

Why it mattersThe re-election of Dr Ebrahim Malick Samba as WHO Regional Director for Africa impacts the region by ensuring continued leadership in public health initiatives and the development of medical education within The Gambia.
