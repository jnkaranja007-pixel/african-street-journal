Gambia fish vendors face high prices, theft

Women fish vendors in Tanji are struggling with escalating fish costs, insufficient workspace, and persistent theft, impacting their ability to earn a living.

Fish vendors at Tanji Riverside are appealing to the government for intervention, citing escalating fish prices, insufficient workspace, and ongoing theft as major threats to their businesses. Vendors report that middlemen inflate prices, leaving them with little to no profit after purchasing fish at rates between D3,500 and D15,000 per pan, depending on the type. Many vendors are left with less than D200 at the end of the day, struggling to support their families.

The vendors also highlighted inadequate infrastructure, with the designated area for smoking and drying fish being too small and congested. This lack of space forces them to conduct both activities in the same area, hindering efficiency. Furthermore, they face challenges with capital and equipment, making it difficult to purchase fish directly from boats, which are often bought up by retailers at prices as high as D7,000 per batch.

Security is another significant concern, as vendors report frequent theft of their belongings. They are appealing for government assistance to provide security and monitor their selling areas. While recent lighting improvements are appreciated, vendors request expanded space, storage facilities, improved security, and measures to make fish more affordable to sustain their livelihoods.

Why it mattersFish vendors are affected by high purchase prices, theft, and inadequate facilities, reducing their profits and ability to support their families.
