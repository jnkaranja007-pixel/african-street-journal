Ousainou Darboe ends his sixth presidential bid without winning

The 78-year-old UDP leader, who has never been president, is one of four former UDP members cleared to stand in December's ballot.

Source: The Alkamba Times · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Ousainou Darboe, a 78-year-old human rights lawyer who has led the United Democratic Party (UDP) since founding it in 1996, is contesting the presidency for the sixth time. Each earlier attempt, in 1996, 2001, 2006, 2011 and 2021, ended with him as runner-up, first to Yahya Jammeh and later to Adama Barrow. He has never held the office.

Darboe was jailed in 2016 after UDP youth leader Ebrima Solo Sandeng died in custody following a protest for electoral reform, and he led a demonstration demanding Sandeng's return "dead or alive". He was released days after Barrow defeated Jammeh in December 2016, later served as Foreign Minister and then as Vice President from June 2018 to March 2019, and has since returned to opposition.

Of the men cleared for the December 2026 ballot, four once belonged to the UDP. Barrow, the incumbent seeking a third term under the National People's Party (NPP), is one of them; Talib Ahmed Bensouda and Lamin J. Darbo, both independents or leaders of new parties, are the others.

Why it mattersFour candidates on the December 2026 ballot once belonged to the UDP, so the party's split now shapes who Gambian voters can choose from.
