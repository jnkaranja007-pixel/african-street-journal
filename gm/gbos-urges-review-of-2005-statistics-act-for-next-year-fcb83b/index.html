GBoS urges review of 2005 Statistics Act for next year

Deputy Statistician-General Alieu Saho says the law predates smartphones, big data and artificial intelligence and leaves data-sharing rules unclear.

Source: The Alkamba Times · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

The Gambia Bureau of Statistics (GBoS) called on Wednesday for the Statistics Act of 2005 to be modernized, saying the law no longer fits how the country collects and reports data. Deputy Statistician-General Alieu Saho, speaking at a national statistics forum, said a revised law should be in place next year.

Saho said the Act was written for a largely paper-based system, while ministries, hospitals, schools, the tax authority, immigration and police now generate digital data. He said the law sets no clear obligation to share information and provides inadequate safeguards for data quality. He called for stronger coordination and routine sharing of reliable statistics.

Sarata Konateh, chief executive of the Gambia Chamber of Commerce and Industry and a National Statistics Council member, said the country needs predictable domestic funding rather than relying mainly on development partners. The council will press the Government and Ministry of Finance for stable allocations to GBoS; the National Strategy for the Development of Statistics III runs from 2024 to 2028.
