Gambia signs agreements for 52-megawatt-peak solar plant in Soma

The project pairs a 40.9-megawatt-hour battery system with a power purchase deal between the national utility and a private consortium.

Source: Kerr Fatou · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

The Gambian government has signed two agreements for a 52-megawatt-peak solar photovoltaic plant in Soma, with a 40.9-megawatt-hour battery storage system. The Ministry of Petroleum, Energy and Mines announced the agreements in a statement on November 18, 2026.

One agreement is a Power Purchase Agreement between the National Water and Electricity Company (NAWEC), the state utility, and a private consortium called AEE and HGR. The other is an Implementation Agreement between the government and that consortium. The project will be developed under an independent power producer framework, which lets private developers generate electricity for sale to the national utility.

The minister, speaking at the signing ceremony, called the project a significant milestone for energy security and affordable electricity. He highlighted the site's proximity to a 225-kilovolt substation in the regional network run by the Organisation pour la Mise en Valeur du Fleuve Gambie, a body that manages the Gambia River basin's energy grid.

Why it mattersNAWEC and the AEE and HGR Consortium are bound by the power-purchase and implementation agreements for the 52-megawatt-peak project.
