ECOWAS gives Gambia election bodies US$480,000

The electoral commission will use its share to train 8,851 polling staff ahead of the December 5 presidential election.

Sources: The Alkamba Times and Kerr Fatou · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) has provided US$480,000 to back The Gambia's presidential election on December 5. Namizata Meite Sangare, the bloc's resident representative and head of mission in The Gambia, handed over the grants in Banjul on Wednesday, November 25.

Joseph Colley, chairman of the Independent Electoral Commission, said the commission will spend its share preparing 8,851 polling staff. Other recipients include the National Human Rights Commission, the Gambia Bar Association and several civil society groups. The Gambian chapter of the Inter-Africa Network for Women, Media, Gender and Development (FAMEDEV) plans a women-led election observatory and an early warning system to head off conflict.

Observers from the bloc have been in the country on a long-term basis since November 10, and short-term monitors are due to arrive before polling day. ECOWAS has also offered technical and logistical help, including training on how to count and send results.

Why it mattersPolling staff across The Gambia will be trained with money from the grant before the vote, and women, young people and people with disabilities are targeted for wider participation.
