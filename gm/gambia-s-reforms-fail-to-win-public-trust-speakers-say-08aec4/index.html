Gambia's Reforms Fail to Win Public Trust, Speakers Say

Despite democratic progress, Gambians show declining confidence in key institutions, according to a WADR forum.

A forum convened by WADR highlighted that while The Gambia has advanced democratically, reforms must translate into public trust. Speakers noted that citizens need credible information to participate effectively and that institutions must function to support democracy. The discussions involved government officials, civil society, media, and legal experts examining democratic resilience, civic space, and accountability.

Agnes E. John-Thomasi of WADR stated that the project aims to equip citizens with information for meaningful participation and to create community dialogue spaces. She emphasized that The Gambia's democratic journey requires continuous effort, including functioning institutions, informed citizens, respect for the rule of law, and accountability from leaders. She also urged media to remain professional and impartial in scrutinizing institutions and combating disinformation.

Gambia Press Union President Isatou Keita asserted that democracy hinges on trust, noting that while Gambians reject dictatorship, confidence in the presidency, parliament, and courts has fallen since 2018. She pointed to incomplete reforms, including constitutional reform, and gaps in judicial confidence and security sector legislation. Hannah Forster of the African Centre for Democracy and Human Rights Studies added that democratic consolidation requires strengthening institutions and fostering a culture of responsible power, measured by the protection of human dignity and rights.

Why it mattersGambians are affected by declining trust in their government institutions, which undermines their confidence in the democratic process.
