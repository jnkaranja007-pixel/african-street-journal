Gambia Bar Association questions Chief Justice appointment case delay

The association says its constitutional challenge filed in July has not been assigned for hearing.

The Gambia Bar Association (GBA) is concerned that a constitutional case challenging Chief Justice Edi M.O. Faal's eligibility has not been assigned for hearing since it was filed on July 31, 2026.

The GBA stated on Monday, November 9, 2026, that the Supreme Court Judiciary has failed to take substantive steps on the matter, which questions the qualifications of the nation's highest judicial officer.

The association's lawyers wrote to the Registrar of the Supreme Court on October 26, 2026, and were told the file was with the Chief Justice for directives, a response the GBA found inadequate given his personal interest in the case.

Why it mattersThe delay in hearing the constitutional challenge risks undermining public confidence in the administration of justice.

Scorpions depart Gambia for Ivory Coast

The national football team travels to Bouaké for crucial Afcon qualifiers against the Elephants.

The Gambia's Scorpions left the country on Tuesday for Bouaké, travelling through Abidjan. They face Ivory Coast there on Thursday in Matchday 3 of the qualifying group for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations, known as AFCON PAMOJA 2027.

Head Coach Johnathan McKinstry named a final 25-man squad for the double-header with the Elephants. The team held its first training session on Monday at the Independence Stadium in Bakau.

Both nations have six points after winning their earlier games against Ghana and Somalia. Ivory Coast top the group on goal difference, and whoever wins both meetings could secure a place at the 2027 finals. The return leg is set for November 17 at the Independence Stadium.

Why it mattersThe national team's results in these two matches will decide whether Gambia can qualify for the 2027 finals.

Gambia Revenue Authority boosts customs compliance

The second phase of a post-clearance audit reform programme aims to modernise regulations and improve efficiency.

The Gambia Revenue Authority (GRA) has begun the second phase of its Post-Clearance Audit (PCA) capacity-building programme with the World Customs Organization (WCO). The aim is to tighten customs compliance, lift revenue collection and bring the audit framework into line with international practice. The programme will review, amend and modernise the existing PCA Regulations and Manual.

Commissioner General Yankuba Darboe, speaking at the opening, said the revised framework should make audits more efficient and boost domestic revenue. He said revenue recovery by the PCA Unit rose from about D15 million in 2023 to about D27 million in 2025. Importer compliance also improved between 2024 and 2025, he added.

Since becoming fully operational, the Unit has audited more than 60 major importers, from 2023 to date. Representatives of the UK's tax and customs agency, HM Revenue and Customs, and of the WCO praised The Gambia's trade facilitation reforms. The UK-supported Accelerate Trade Facilitation Programme is now in its eleventh year, with funding secured through 2029.

Why it mattersImporters and traders face the revised audit regulations and manual, which are meant to guide how the PCA Unit checks their customs declarations.

Mustapha Jadama wins Gambia's first Olympic medal

Young athlete Mustapha Jadama secured a silver medal in the men's 400m final at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games.

Young Gambian athlete Mustapha Jadama won a silver medal in the men's 400m final at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games in Senegal on Monday.

Jadama finished second in 46.93 seconds, trailing gold medallist Saeed Omar of the UAE, who ran 46.40 seconds, and finishing ahead of China's Liu Han, who took bronze in 47.16 seconds.

The Gambia National Olympic Committee stated that Jadama is a history-maker and a national hero who opened the door for future Gambian athletes.

Why it mattersMustapha Jadama's historic silver medal brings national pride and recognition to Gambian athletes.

Gambia receives credentials of new Chinese ambassador Xiao Yewen

Xiao Yewen pledged to strengthen people-to-people exchanges and bilateral cooperation during a State House ceremony in Banjul.

President Adama Barrow received the Letters of Credence of H.E. Xiao Yewen, China's new ambassador to The Gambia, at the State House in Banjul. Foreign Minister Seringe Modou Njie accompanied the ambassador and his delegation.

In his remarks, Xiao pledged to strengthen people-to-people exchanges, enhance bilateral cooperation and support The Gambia's development priorities while exploring new areas of cooperation. Barrow welcomed him and said the partnership is amplified through the FOCAC forum. He also sent a goodwill message to President Xi Jinping.

The two countries say their ties rest on mutual respect, solidarity and cooperation, and have grown through work in infrastructure, healthcare, agriculture, education and human resource development.

Why it mattersGambians and business partners gain a named Chinese diplomatic contact for cooperation in infrastructure, healthcare, agriculture and education.
