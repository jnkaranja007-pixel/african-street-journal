Gambia wins heritage jollof and chef of the year at WAFFEST

The West Africa Food Festival concluded with Gambia securing top culinary awards, showcasing regional food traditions.

The 2026 West Africa Food Festival (WAFFEST) concluded in The Gambia with the host nation winning the Heritage Jollof Rice and Chef of the Year titles. The Gambia also secured first place in the Bread Bakery and Barbecue (BBQ) categories during the week-long event held from October 15 to 21. This eighth edition brought together chefs from across Africa to celebrate West African cuisine.

Team Gambia successfully defended its 2025 Heritage Jollof Rice title, finishing ahead of Liberia and Burkina Faso. In the Chef of the Year competition, The Gambia placed first, followed by Sierra Leone and Nigeria. The host country also took first in Bread Bakery and BBQ, with Nigeria and Liberia securing second places in those categories respectively.

Tourism Minister Abdoulie Jobe commended the organisers and participants, stating the festival strengthened tourism and showcased the region's culinary heritage internationally. He encouraged hotels and restaurants to promote Gambian cuisine and source ingredients locally. The minister assured government support for the Gambia Chefs Association to sustain momentum through training and innovation.

Why it mattersThe festival celebrated West African cuisine and heritage, impacting tourism and skills development in The Gambia.

Barrow warns over protest near State House

President Adama Barrow described a candlelight march towards the State House as a treasonable offence.

President Adama Barrow warned Banjul North National Assembly Member Momodou Lamin Bah against organising future protests, describing a recent candlelight march towards the State House as a treasonable offence. The President stated on 19 October 2026 that the protest, organised after a 48-hour electricity outage in Banjul, crossed a red line. He cautioned that any repeat would result in severe consequences, contrasting his government's response with that of former President Yahya Jammeh.

Mr Barrow also highlighted government development projects in Tobacco Road, suggesting the NAM had not assisted his constituents or supported government efforts. He urged voters to consider electing a different representative. The President made these remarks during his Meet the People Tour in Banjul, where he also visited skills training centres, a mini-stadium renovation, and a water storage tank.

Hon. Bah responded by rejecting the President's claims, calling the protest peaceful, lawful, and dignified. He stated it was organised to demand accountability and improved service delivery, with candles held instead of weapons. Bah asserted the demonstration followed required notification and was protected under the constitution guaranteeing freedom of assembly and expression.

Why it mattersPresident Barrow's strong reaction to a protest affects the political climate for citizens and their representatives.

Darboe vows to revive Gambia's agriculture and fisheries

The UDP leader plans to cut government waste to fund free fertiliser for farmers and review fishing deals.

UDP leader Ousainou Darboe has pledged to revitalise The Gambia's economy by focusing on agriculture and fisheries if elected president. Darboe outlined a plan to cut government waste, including abolishing advisory positions, to fund initiatives like providing free fertiliser to farmers. He also intends to review agreements with fishing companies to ensure they benefit Gambians and encourage local fish processing.

Darboe stated that rationalising expenditure would free resources for sectors directly impacting citizens, with agriculture being a top priority. He plans to substantially increase support for farmers to boost food production and reduce reliance on imports. Investments in modern storage facilities for women gardeners are also part of his agenda to prevent post-harvest losses.

The UDP leader aims to strengthen commercial agriculture and end heavy rice import reliance by collaborating with private enterprises. He recalled a time when The Gambia was more self-reliant and wants to create an enabling environment for farmers and agricultural businesses. Darboe also wants fishing companies to process their catches locally to create jobs and boost economic activity.

Why it mattersGambia's farmers and fishers will benefit from increased support and revised agreements aimed at boosting local production and employment.

Cuban community in Gambia marks National Culture Day

The Cuban Embassy and Medical Brigade in Banjul celebrated the birth of Cuba's national anthem on October 20.

Cuban nationals and Gambian professionals gathered in Banjul on October 20 to celebrate Cuba's National Culture Day. The event, held by the Cuban Embassy and the Cuban Medical Brigade, commemorated the birth of Cuba’s national anthem, La Bayamesa, which originated from a march written in 1868.

Ambassador Carmelina Ramírez Rodríguez stated that Cuban culture reflects the nation's history and resilience, fostering social cohesion. She highlighted that Cuban identity, or "Cubanidad," extends beyond birthplace and emphasized the anthem's historical call for independence from Spanish rule.

The celebrations also acknowledged the deep historical links between Cuba and Africa, stemming from the transatlantic slave trade. Rodríguez noted that African heritage significantly shaped Cuban identity, contributing to traditions and the spirit of resistance, and that culture remains vital for preserving freedom and sovereignty.

Why it mattersThe Cuban community in Gambia celebrated their national culture and historical ties to Africa.

UK-Gambia health research partnership marks eight decades

The Medical Research Council Unit The Gambia celebrated its long-standing collaboration with the UK at an open day event.

The Medical Research Council (MRC) Unit The Gambia at the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine hosted an open day on Wednesday, celebrating nearly eight decades of health research and partnership between the United Kingdom and The Gambia. Established in 1947, the unit is the UK’s largest investment in health research in a low- and middle-income country, supported by UK Research and Innovation.

Deputy British High Commissioner Ella Penton-Voak highlighted the global impact of research from The Gambia, citing insecticide-treated bed nets as a key factor in transforming malaria prevention worldwide. She noted that the recent Edinburgh Prize for Global Health Impact awarded to the Ministry of Health and the MRC recognized this achievement. The MRC also trains scientists through partnerships with UK universities and African institutions, strengthening research capacity across the continent.

Permanent Secretary at the Ministry of Health, Lamin Dampha, emphasized the open day's role in fostering public trust and understanding of the MRC's work. He commended the unit's support for The Gambia's immunization program, tuberculosis research, and its vital role during the COVID-19 pandemic. Dampha encouraged students to become future public health leaders, noting that Gambian communities have been partners in research for generations.

Why it mattersThe partnership between the UK and The Gambia in health research directly benefits Gambian communities through improved health policies, saved lives, and strengthened health systems.
