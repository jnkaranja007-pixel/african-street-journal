Court hears lawyer Essa Faal owed money in D58.9m fraud case

GACH Global Trading Company Limited is suing two former officials over funds allegedly obtained through a fraudulent petroleum supply deal.

Essa Faal, a prominent Gambian lawyer and politician, came up on Wednesday during a civil fraud case over a D58.9 million dispute at the High Court in Banjul. During cross-examination, plaintiff's counsel Ida Drameh asked second defendant Saihou Drammeh whether he knew Faal, and he said he did. Drameh then put it to him that Faal had subsequently owed money, which Drammeh acknowledged before Justice Ebrima Jaiteh.

Drammeh rejected the claim that he had said Faal used the money on politics, answering "No, My Lord" when asked whether he had made that statement. Earlier in the hearing, Drameh had suggested Drammeh complained that Kebba Ceesay registered two apartments valued at €48,000 in his own name, an allegation Drammeh also rejected.

The suit, GACH Global Trading Company Limited against Kaddijatou Kebbeh and Saihou Drammeh, was filed in January 2022. The company seeks over D58.9 million and $221,000 tied to a September 2021 petroleum product agreement, plus interest of 24% and 4% until payment.

Why it mattersIf the court finds for GACH Global, the two defendants must repay D58.9 million plus $221,000 and pay interest of 24% and 4%.

Darbo submits presidential nomination papers

Independent candidate Lamin J. Darbo is backed by the Team Gambia Grand Coalition for the December 5 election.

Lamin J. Darbo submitted his nomination papers to contest the December 5 presidential election as an independent candidate backed by the Team Gambia Grand Coalition. He presented his documents, including citizenship, age, and voter registration details, to the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) on Wednesday, November 11, at Election House in Kanifing. IEC chairperson Joseph Colley confirmed that all items on the checklist were provided and that the commission would conduct due diligence before a final decision.

Darbo clarified that he was not representing his former party, the National Unity Party, and had submitted a resignation letter. Coalition coordinators and veteran politicians Halifa Sallah and Sidia Jatta accompanied him. Colley noted that the law permits an independent candidate to be backed by a coalition and asked Darbo to clarify his status to avoid confusion. The IEC reviewed Darbo’s proposed campaign colours and issued him a nomination receipt, a copy of his documents, and the election campaign code.

Endorsed candidates will be invited to coordinate campaign schedules on November 15 and sign a peace pact on November 16. Separately, presidential aspirant Prince Ebrima Sanyang of the Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF) decided not to submit his nomination papers due to an unresolved issue with his voter registration record, questioning the IEC’s ability to guarantee credible elections.

Why it mattersLamin J. Darbo's nomination receipt and campaign code were issued by the IEC, with endorsed candidates to coordinate schedules on November 15.

Gambia urged to sign fisheries transparency deal

Stakeholders want a Memorandum of Understanding with the Fisheries Transparency Initiative to improve marine surveillance and data collection.

Fisheries stakeholders in The Gambia are calling on the government to sign a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the Fisheries Transparency Initiative (FiTI). The appeal, made at a conference in Banjul on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, aims to enhance transparency in the country's seas and strengthen territorial water protection through better surveillance and data collection.

Dawda Foday Saine, Secretary General of the African Confederation of Professional Organizations of Artisanal Fisheries (COAPA), stated that joining FiTI would build trust among stakeholders and attract donor support. He explained that FiTI is a global partnership promoting sustainable fisheries through transparency and collaboration, helping national authorities improve access to fisheries management information.

The FiTI membership process requires a letter of intent, an MoU signing, and the formation of a Multi-Stakeholder Group (MSG) to advise the government. Godfred Ameyaw Asiedu, FiTI Regional Coordinator for Anglophone Africa, confirmed the organization supports coastal states in improving fisheries governance through transparency.

Why it mattersStakeholders are urging the government to sign an MoU with FiTI to improve transparency and attract donor support for the fisheries sector.

Gambia hosts regional maritime ministers in Banjul

Ministers and experts from West and Central Africa will weigh a proposed regional maritime bank and the transformation of MOWCA into a continental body.

The Ministry of Transport, Works and Infrastructure will host the 12th Ordinary Session of the Bureau of Ministers of the Maritime Organization of West and Central Africa (MOWCA) on 16 and 17 November 2026 in Banjul. Ministers, senior government officials and maritime experts from across the region will attend.

Discussions will cover the implementation of MOWCA's 2026 work programme, the proposed Regional Maritime Development Bank, institutional and financial reforms, and member states' contributions under the Regional Maritime Fund. Delegates will also consider the proposed transformation of MOWCA into the African Maritime Organization and a proposal to convene the first MOWCA Summit of Heads of State. Counsel Olimatou Danso-Malang, Director General of the Gambia Maritime Administration, said regional commitments must be turned into concrete action on maritime safety, security, port development and the blue economy.

Recommendations from the technical Meeting of Experts will be consolidated into draft recommendations for the Bureau of Ministers to consider. Earlier this year, Minister Ebrima Sillah assumed the MOWCA Chairmanship, and Danso-Malang was elected Chairperson of the Committee of Experts.

Why it mattersMember states will decide on the funding and institutional reforms that govern how MOWCA's maritime programmes are paid for and run.

UK and Gambia honour fallen soldiers at Fajara remembrance service

The British High Commission in Banjul and the Gambian government held an Act of Remembrance for those who died in the world wars and later conflicts.

Officials, security personnel, religious leaders, diplomats, veterans and members of the public gathered at the Commonwealth War Graves Cemetery in Fajara for a solemn Act of Remembrance. The ceremony honoured people who died in the two World Wars and later conflicts, with special tribute to Gambian soldiers who fought alongside British and Allied forces in Africa and the Far East.

The British High Commissioner took as this year's theme "Service in a Changing World", saying that modern security challenges keep changing while courage, duty, professionalism and selfless service stay the same. More than 20 million soldiers died in the First World War alone, and millions more in the Second World War and the conflicts that followed, a reminder of the human cost of war.

The cemetery holds the graves of 236 Gambian servicemen who died in the conflicts. The ceremony also acknowledged the growing ties between the British and Gambian armed forces, which rest on shared values, mutual respect and a common commitment to peace and security.

Why it mattersGambian families and veterans gain formal recognition of their dead, with 236 servicemen commemorated at the Fajara cemetery.
