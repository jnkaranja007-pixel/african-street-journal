Coalition 2026 unveils 66 actions for Gambia by 2031

The manifesto, titled ‘The Gambia We Deserve’, outlines three pillars and 13 policy points.

Coalition 2026, led by UNITE leader Talib Ahmed Bensouda, launched its manifesto ‘The Gambia We Deserve’ on October 22, 2026. The plan details 3 pillars, 13 policy points, and 66 priority actions to be completed by 2031. Bensouda stated the manifesto is built around governance and trust, human development, and economic integrity.

The manifesto includes a constitutional review within two years and aims to raise health spending to 15% of the budget. A National Development Bank will be established to boost job creation and economic growth. Bensouda explained the 66 actions align with The Gambia’s age of 66 in 2031, when the plan concludes.

Bensouda highlighted current challenges including a high cost of living, youth unemployment, and difficulties in farming. He noted that despite the country's potential and hard-working population, families struggle and young people seek opportunities. The coalition aims to transform The Gambia by turning potential into progress and resources into opportunity.

Why it mattersGambians will be affected by the proposed changes to governance, human development, and economic integrity, including health spending and job creation.

Gambia highlights slave trade heritage sites

The National Centre for Arts and Culture promotes Kunta Kinteh Island and Wassu Stone Circles for tourism.

The Director General of The Gambia's National Centre for Arts and Culture, Hassoum Ceesay, is promoting two UNESCO World Heritage Sites to boost tourism beyond beaches. Ceesay highlighted Kunta Kinteh Island, formerly James Island, which served as a departure point during the Transatlantic Slave Trade. He also discussed the Wassu Stone Circles, an archaeological site.

Ceesay described Kunta Kinteh Island as a place of "horror and terror," where captives were held in dungeons before being transported to the New World. The island, renamed in 2011 to honor Alex Haley's "Roots" hero, is compared to Goree Island in Senegal and Elmina Castle in Ghana. The government plans to enhance the island's appeal and educational value.

The Wassu Stone Circles are also a UNESCO World Heritage Site, though Ceesay provided fewer details on this location in the interview. The NCAC aims to showcase these cultural assets to Gambians and the world, moving tourism beyond traditional sun and beach offerings.

Why it mattersGambians and humanity can learn about the country's cultural heritage, including sites related to the slave trade.

Gambia civic leaders urge tolerance before 2026 election

National Council for Civic Education warns 35 constituencies are high-risk for electoral violence.

Gambian civic leaders are calling for tolerance and unity ahead of the December 5, 2026 presidential election. At a workshop, Chief Civic Education Officer Yusupha Bojang warned against divisive language in political discourse, stating elections should be a marketplace of ideas, not a battleground for insults or polarization.

Bojang revealed that early warning analysis shows 35 of the country’s 53 constituencies are rated high or very high risk, a concern requiring vigilance from all stakeholders. He urged political leaders to publicly condemn hate speech, reminding them that rivals are not enemies.

The Inter-Party Committee (IPC) also reaffirmed its dedication to fostering peace, inclusivity, and mutual respect. Pamela Camara of Catholic Relief Services stressed that peace is nurtured daily, urging citizens to use their voices for healing and trust.

Why it mattersGambian citizens preparing for the 2026 presidential election are being urged to promote peace and unity to safeguard democracy.

Gambia finance minister proposes D66.78B budget

The proposed 2027 national budget includes D16.17 billion for debt servicing and D6.06 billion for education.

The Minister of Finance and Economic Affairs will present the 2027 national budget of D66.78 billion to the National Assembly today. This represents a 12.5 per cent increase from the D59.36 billion approved for 2026.

Initial estimates show debt servicing will be the largest expenditure at D16.17 billion, about one-third of the D48.6 billion financed directly by the government. Basic and secondary education receives the largest sectoral allocation with D6.06 billion, followed by health at D4.10 billion.

The budget projects domestic revenue at D37.39 billion, with tax revenue expected to rise to D31.47 billion. Total revenue and grants are projected at D56.94 billion, leading to a gross budget deficit of D2.30 billion, or 0.99 per cent of the projected GDP. The government plans to borrow D7.63 billion domestically and D2.21 billion from foreign sources.

Why it mattersThe proposed budget affects all Gambians through government spending priorities and revenue collection.

Gambia electoral commission delays voter roll release

The Independent Electoral Commission cannot release raw voter data until court revisions are complete for the December 2026 election.

The Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) in Gambia will not release raw voter roll data until Revising Courts finish their hearings and magistrates sign off on necessary changes. This decision comes amid calls for transparency ahead of the December 2026 presidential election. The IEC chairman stated on Thursday that the commission must first receive directions on which names to keep or remove from the register before finalizing the voter count.

IEC chairman explained that releasing the data prematurely would be illogical, as the final number of registered voters can only be determined after legal revisions. He assured stakeholders that the data will be shared with all political parties once the process is concluded, noting that magistrates are still actively handling cases before the Revising Courts. The commission has established procedures for providing voter information to parties and other election participants.

The IEC is also addressing other electoral preparations, including polling station arrangements and the involvement of political parties and their agents. The chairman emphasized the commission's commitment to making the election as inclusive as possible and urged stakeholders to consider the practical challenges of election preparation. The completion of the Revising Courts' work remains a prerequisite for releasing the raw voter roll data.

Why it mattersGambians awaiting the December 2026 presidential election will have to wait longer for access to raw voter roll data.
