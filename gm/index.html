UN Rapporteur Urges Gambia to Abolish Death Penalty

The United Nations Special Rapporteur on extrajudicial executions called for the complete removal of the death penalty from the constitution.

The United Nations Special Rapporteur on extrajudicial, summary or arbitrary executions, Morris Tidball-Binz, urged The Gambia to fully abolish the death penalty. He stated that its retention in the Constitution is inconsistent with international law, despite significant reforms. Tidball-Binz, a forensic doctor, made these remarks at the conclusion of a 12-day visit to assess the right to life and accountability for past crimes.

The Rapporteur noted that The Gambia abolished the death penalty in 1993, becoming a human rights beacon, but it was reinstated after a 1994 coup. He recalled the 2012 executions of nine death row inmates, describing them as arbitrary killings and forced disappearances, with their bodies never recovered. While welcoming the 2025 Criminal Act that removes the death penalty, he stressed its constitutional presence remains an issue.

Tidball-Binz praised The Gambia's progress since 2017, including reforms in the justice system and improved prison conditions. He highlighted the appointment of a Special Prosecutor in September 2026 for high-profile cases and acknowledged investigations into recent killings, such as the 2018 Faraba Banta student deaths and the 2025 death of Omar Badjie, noting that impunity has ended.

Why it mattersThe retention of the death penalty in the constitution affects the country's adherence to international human rights obligations.

Gambia fish vendors face high prices, theft

Women fish vendors in Tanji are struggling with escalating fish costs, insufficient workspace, and persistent theft, impacting their ability to earn a living.

Fish vendors at Tanji Riverside are appealing to the government for intervention, citing escalating fish prices, insufficient workspace, and ongoing theft as major threats to their businesses. Vendors report that middlemen inflate prices, leaving them with little to no profit after purchasing fish at rates between D3,500 and D15,000 per pan, depending on the type. Many vendors are left with less than D200 at the end of the day, struggling to support their families.

The vendors also highlighted inadequate infrastructure, with the designated area for smoking and drying fish being too small and congested. This lack of space forces them to conduct both activities in the same area, hindering efficiency. Furthermore, they face challenges with capital and equipment, making it difficult to purchase fish directly from boats, which are often bought up by retailers at prices as high as D7,000 per batch.

Security is another significant concern, as vendors report frequent theft of their belongings. They are appealing for government assistance to provide security and monitor their selling areas. While recent lighting improvements are appreciated, vendors request expanded space, storage facilities, improved security, and measures to make fish more affordable to sustain their livelihoods.

Why it mattersFish vendors are affected by high purchase prices, theft, and inadequate facilities, reducing their profits and ability to support their families.

Gambia President Promises Peaceful, Transparent December 5 Election

President Adama Barrow assured citizens that the upcoming presidential election will be conducted peacefully and transparently, with results respected internationally.

President Adama Barrow has pledged that the December 5 presidential election in The Gambia will be peaceful, transparent, and credible. Speaking in Bijilo, Barrow stated his administration is committed to safeguarding the integrity of the polls, ensuring the entire world can witness its transparency. He emphasized that the election results will be respected globally, as Gambians are focused on building and strengthening their democracy.

Barrow urged opposition parties to concentrate on campaigning rather than confrontation, asserting that democratic means are the only legitimate path to power. He highlighted that peace is a shared responsibility, essential for protecting the nation and its democratic foundations. The president also used the occasion to showcase development projects in the West Coast Region, including road works, and addressed concerns about incomplete facilities like the Bijilo hospital and a skills centre, noting proposals for their completion.

The president's remarks came during the final meeting of his Meet the People Tour in Kombo. He acknowledged the community's appreciation for his visit and reiterated his government's commitment to addressing community needs as resources permit. Barrow stressed that continued support from development partners is linked to The Gambia's democratic credentials, encouraging a focus on constructive political engagement.

Why it mattersGambian citizens are directly affected by President Barrow's assurances of a peaceful and transparent election process, which impacts their right to vote and the stability of their democracy.

State Opposes Lt. Col. Sanna Manjang’s Bid to Dismiss Desertion Charges

Prosecutors argue that the officer remained subject to military law throughout his nine-year absence from the Gambia Armed Forces.

The State has formally opposed an application by Lt. Col. Sanna Manjang to strike out desertion charges currently before Justice O. Cham at the Fajara Barracks. In an affidavit filed by the Attorney General’s Chambers, the prosecution maintained that Manjang’s prolonged absence from duty did not automatically terminate his membership in the Gambia Armed Forces.

The legal challenge follows a motion filed by the defense on October 15, 2026. The State contends that Manjang was on active service when he left the country and failed to formally resign or return issued military property. Prosecutors further disputed claims that he fled due to fears of arrest, noting he remained absent until his January 2026 detention by Senegalese authorities.

The prosecution argued that whether the accused was on active service remains a legal question for the court to decide based on evidence. They urged the court-martial to reject the defense's request to replace the desertion charge with a lesser offense of absence without leave, asserting that the existing charges are competent under military law.

Why it mattersThe proceedings affect Lt. Col. Sanna Manjang, who faces a court-martial for desertion and potential military legal consequences following his nine-year absence.

ECOWAS and AES launch ministerial dialogue to strengthen regional cooperation

Representatives from the two regional blocs met in Dakar to establish a framework for future cooperation and foster stronger ties across West Africa.

The Economic Community of West African States and the Alliance of Sahel States held an inaugural ministerial session in Dakar on 17 October 2026. This meeting aimed to build on previous consultations from May 2025 to create a balanced cooperation framework. The delegation heads, Cheikh Niang and Karamoko Jean-Marie Traoré, jointly chaired the session to address regional interests.

Both parties agreed to form joint thematic working groups to conduct technical consultations. These groups will develop a comprehensive Cooperation Framework Agreement for ministerial review. The discussions emphasized principles of sovereignty, mutual respect, and the welfare of West African citizens, reflecting the deep historical and economic bonds shared by the nations involved.

The dialogue is considered a milestone for maintaining regional cooperation amid ongoing political and security developments. A joint communiqué was adopted in English, French, and Portuguese to formalize the commitment to future relations. This initiative seeks to safeguard the interests of their respective populations through practical and structured engagement.

Why it mattersThe dialogue directly affects citizens of West African nations by establishing a new framework for regional cooperation that aims to safeguard their collective interests.
