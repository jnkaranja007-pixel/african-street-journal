Caritas-Gambia trains peacebuilders for 2026 elections

The HOPE project equips local leaders and community members with skills to prevent conflict and reduce polarization before the upcoming elections.

Caritas-Gambia has launched a new initiative to foster peace and reduce political polarization ahead of the 2026 elections. The Harmony, Outreach, Peace and Engagement (HOPE) project began training participants yesterday at the Governor's Office in the West Coast Region, with a second phase scheduled for September 3rd in the Lower River Region. National Director Francis D. Mendy stated the project aims to strengthen social cohesion and democratic resilience by empowering youth and women in peacebuilding and electoral processes.

Funded by Catholic Relief Services (CRS), the training focuses on providing practical skills in mediation, dialogue, and early conflict detection. Mendy emphasized the importance of inclusive dialogue between communities and political actors to prevent conflict and help local leaders resolve election-related disputes. The initiative also highlights the crucial role of religious leaders, chiefs, community leaders, youth, women representatives, and civil society organizations in building a peaceful and democratic Gambia.

West Coast Region Deputy Governor Musa Amul Nyassi urged Gambians, especially young people and women, not to be manipulated by politicians into electoral violence. He reminded citizens of their constitutional right to support any party or candidate, stressing that political differences should not disrupt peace and coexistence. Participants are encouraged to apply the acquired knowledge and skills to promote harmony within their communities.

Why it mattersThe training aims to prevent conflict and reduce polarization among communities and political actors, directly impacting the peaceful conduct of the 2026 elections.

US Visa Curbs Signal Warning to Africa

Washington's tightened immigration policies, citing security and screening concerns, prompt a strategic reevaluation for African nations.

The United States has partially suspended visa issuance for Gambian nationals, affecting visitor, student, exchange, and immigrant visas. This move, attributed by the U.S. administration to security, screening, information sharing, and visa overstay concerns, is viewed not just as an inconvenience but as a strategic warning to the African continent.

Analysts suggest these restrictions can reverberate through international business, medicine, technology, trade, investment, and entrepreneurship. African migrants are integral to a transnational economic ecosystem that links households, companies, universities, and markets across the Atlantic.

The U.S. administration has progressively tightened immigration and visa policies. In January 2026, Washington implemented these partial suspensions, impacting Gambians seeking to visit, study, trade, work, or reunite with family in the United States, subject to specified exceptions.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as restrictions can reverberate through international business, trade, investment, and entrepreneurship, disrupting economic ecosystems.

Ministers Find Markets Unsanitary Amidst Vendor Complaints

Environment Minister Rohey John Manjang expressed devastation over market conditions, promising central government intervention with local councils.

Ministers inspecting Serrekunda Market discovered vendors operating in unsanitary conditions, surrounded by accumulated waste and filth. Traders voiced frustration, citing neglect by the Kanifing Municipal Council despite regular payments of duties and fees. While a council waste truck was observed clearing waste, vendors demanded a more consistent and robust waste management system to combat unbearable odors.

The inspection, which is set to continue in Latrikunda, along the Coastal Road, and Brikama, aims to engage vendors on improving cleanliness and waste management. Vendors emphasized the need for sanitation prioritization to ensure healthier trading spaces for both themselves and customers, urging stronger collection services to restore public confidence in market hygiene.

Environment Minister Rohey John Manjang stated she was saddened by the market conditions and acknowledged the councils' failure to maintain facilities despite collecting revenue. She assured that the Central Government would collaborate with the councils to develop a practical plan addressing the sanitation challenges, recognizing the impact on market operations and public health.

Why it mattersVendors and customers are directly affected by poor market sanitation, which impacts health, trade, and public confidence.

Brikama Area Council Refinances D58 Million Debt

The council seeks to restructure its outstanding vehicle purchase payments to free up funds for new acquisitions or development projects.

Brikama Area Council (BAC) is proposing to refinance approximately D50 million of its D58 million outstanding debt from a 2024 hire-purchase agreement for waste management vehicles and heavy machinery. The original agreement with Espace Motors was for D101 million, with D43 million already paid, leaving D58 million due in 2026 and 2027. The council aims to secure a new facility from Ecobank, repayable over five years, to manage this obligation.

Chairman Yankuba Darboe stated that this restructuring is intended to release an estimated D20 million in 2026 and D23 million in 2027. These funds could then be allocated to purchasing additional vehicles or supporting other development initiatives within the council's jurisdiction. The council's decision hinges on whether this refinancing offers a better financial outcome compared to the existing payment schedule and other potential financing options.

The move prompts a broader discussion on public institutional finance in The Gambia, questioning when refinancing strengthens an entity and when it merely shifts current liabilities to future budgets. The assessment of BAC's proposed transaction requires a comprehensive analysis of its full financing costs, repayment structure, liquidity impact, associated risks, opportunity costs, asset performance, and implications for future budgets, beyond just the headline figures.

Why it mattersThe Brikama Area Council's debt refinancing decision affects its future budgets and capacity for development projects, impacting public finances.

Lawyer Warns Jammeh Return Alliance Tests Gambia Justice

Human rights lawyer Reed Brody stated that any political deal including former dictator Yahya Jammeh's return would challenge the nation's accountability system.

Hungarian-American human rights lawyer Reed Brody has warned that any political alliance aiming to facilitate former dictator Yahya Jammeh's return would severely test Gambia's justice system. Brody stated that such a deal would directly challenge whether accountability can survive electoral politics, especially after years of testimony detailing atrocities. He emphasized that Gambians expect trials, not a negotiated homecoming, and that Jammeh is not above the law.

Brody further clarified that the 2017 Joint Declaration did not guarantee Jammeh safe return and contained no provisions against prosecution, as international law prohibits amnesty for severe crimes. He noted that Gambia has established significant infrastructure for prosecution, including the 2024 Special Accountability Mechanism Act and the December approval of a hybrid Special Tribunal by Ecowas. The naming of Martin Hackett as Special Prosecutor this year marks institutional progress.

Despite institutional advancements, Brody cautioned that justice delivered is not the same as justice on paper. He stressed that the Special Prosecutor's Office requires resources, forensic capacity, and political backing to convert findings into indictments, a process that becomes precarious during an election year. Human activist Madi Jobarteh echoed these concerns, calling any coalition considering Jammeh's return an affront to victims and a threat to national unity.

Why it mattersGambians who suffered under the former dictator Yahya Jammeh's regime are affected by the potential for a political alliance that could allow his return, thereby undermining the justice process and their expectation of accountability.
