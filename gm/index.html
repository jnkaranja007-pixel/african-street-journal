Gambia begins repaying D563.8 million bus loan

The government made its first principal and interest payment in May 2026 for buses procured for GTSC.

The Gambian government has started repaying a D563.8 million loan for Gambia Transport Service Company (GTSC) buses. The first payment covering principal and interest was made in May 2026. This repayment plan is part of a D563,779,771.50 financing arrangement with the Social Security and Housing Finance Corporation (SSHFC).

Finance Minister Seedy Keita informed the National Assembly that the loan will be repaid through 10 semi-annual instalments. The repayment schedule began on April 30, 2026, and is set to conclude by October 30, 2030. The initial instalment, due April 30, 2026, was paid on May 12, 2026.

Lawmakers questioned the minister on the total amount repaid to date, but Keita stated only the first instalment had been paid. The loan supports the procurement of 70 buses for GTSC, a public transport provider. The government is obligated to continue these payments to the SSHFC every six months until the loan's conclusion.

Why it mattersThe Gambian government is repaying a significant loan for public transport buses, affecting its financial obligations to the SSHFC.

Gambia develops clean cooking strategy

The initiative, supported by the EU and Netherlands, aims to promote safer, cleaner cooking solutions across the nation.

Gambia is creating a National Clean Cooking Strategy to promote safer and more sustainable cooking methods. This effort is part of the Regional Clean Cooking Action in West Africa (ReCCAWA) programme, which supports clean cooking technologies in 12 West African nations. A national workshop was held on Wednesday at the Sir Dawda Kairaba Jawara International Conference Centre to discuss the strategy's development.

The programme, funded by the European Union and the Netherlands Ministry of Foreign Affairs through ECOWAS, targets demand, supply, and policy for clean cooking. Carmen, representing the Netherlands Enterprise Agency (RVO), stated that clean cooking is vital for the health of women and children, environmental protection, and business growth. She highlighted that women and children suffer from toxic smoke and that firewood use pressures forests.

The Deputy Head of the EU Delegation to The Gambia, Raphaël Brigandi, linked clean cooking to environmental protection, women's empowerment, and private sector development. He expressed hope that the workshop would lead to a strong national strategy guiding future policies and actions. The strategy aims to improve health, protect the environment, empower women, and create business opportunities.

Why it mattersGambia's women and children are vulnerable to harmful smoke from traditional cooking methods, and the country's forests face pressure from firewood use.

Gambia university staff threaten strike over welfare

The University of The Gambia Faculty and Staff Association notified the Department of Labour of possible industrial action.

The University of The Gambia Faculty and Staff Association (UTGFSA) has formally notified the Department of Labour of its intention to embark on industrial action. The planned action, to be taken pursuant to Section 175(1)(a) of the Labour Act, 2023, follows outstanding demands concerning staff welfare and university governance. The Association said it had given authorities time to act on resolutions adopted on 16 July 2026 but received no substantive response.

In a letter dated 28 October 2026 to the Commissioner of Labour, the UTGFSA stated that the continued absence of engagement is disappointing. The Association argued that the unresolved matters affect staff welfare, rights, and working conditions, as well as the university's governance and institutional stability. They are particularly concerned about the failure to implement Governing Council decisions.

The UTGFSA listed five major areas requiring urgent attention, citing a persistent failure to uphold established governance procedures and directives. The Association stressed that executive authority must be accompanied by accountability and respect for oversight mechanisms. The university cannot function effectively if regulations are selectively applied or legitimate concerns are met with silence, they stated.

Why it mattersUniversity staff in Gambia are threatening industrial action due to unresolved issues affecting their welfare and the institution's governance.

Gambia launches hub for 20 returning migrants

Kanifing Municipal Council opens a resilience center offering business support, childcare, and psychosocial services.

The Kanifing Municipal Council (KMC) launched the Kanifing Migrant Resilience Hub on October 29, 2026, to help 20 returning migrants rebuild their lives. Supported by the Mayors Migration Council's Global Cities Fund 2025, the Hub will connect returnees with services and opportunities for sustainable reintegration.

Lord Mayor Talib Ahmed Bensouda stated the initiative addresses the "painful reality" of young people leaving Gambia via the "backway" due to a lack of opportunity. He praised the diverse business ideas from participants, including waste collection, delivery services, tailoring, and catering, highlighting their potential to create jobs and boost the local economy.

The Hub provides on-site childcare and psychosocial support to enable participants to focus on their enterprises. KMC aims to strengthen collaboration with stakeholders like Startup Incubator Gambia to create lasting opportunities for returnees and build an inclusive community.

Why it mattersReturning migrants in Kanifing will receive support to start businesses and reintegrate into the local economy.

EU pledges long-term duty-free access for African exports

Trade between the European Union and Africa reached €357 billion in 2025, a 51% increase since 2016.

The European Union has committed to maintaining indefinite duty-free and quota-free access for exports from Africa’s least-developed countries to its market. This commitment, part of the 25-year-old Everything But Arms (EBA) scheme, ensures continued access for eligible nations to a market of 450 million consumers without import duties.

Olof Gill, European Commission spokesperson for Trade, Economic Security and Customs, stated that all exports from EBA countries enjoy tariff-free, quota-free access to the EU. This trade arrangement is part of a growing commercial relationship that saw EU-Africa trade reach €357 billion in 2025, an increase from €236 billion in 2016.

The EU views its engagement with Africa as a partnership, aiming to support Africa’s industrialisation and job creation while diversifying its own economic partnerships and building resilient supply chains. In 2025, EU exports to Africa were valued at €167 billion, while imports from Africa reached €102 billion.

Why it mattersAfrican countries, including Gambia, will continue to benefit from preferential access to the European market for their exports, supporting economic development.
