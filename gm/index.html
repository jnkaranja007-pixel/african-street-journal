Eight presidential candidates sign peace pact for December election

The candidates pledged to reject violence, intimidation, and inflammatory rhetoric ahead of the December 5 vote.

Eight presidential candidates in The Gambia signed an 11-article Peace Pact at the Sir Dawda Kairaba Jawara International Conference Center, committing to peaceful, issue-based campaigns before the December 5, 2026 election. The agreement, facilitated by the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) and International IDEA, was signed as the official campaign period began on November 17. Incumbent President Adama Barrow and his main challenger, A.N.M. Ousainou Darboe, were among the signatories.

The pact requires candidates to reject hate speech, tribal bigotry, and the irresponsible use of digital media, while respecting press freedom. IEC Chairman Joseph Colley reminded signatories that the Code of Election Campaign Ethics is legally binding. International IDEA Country Director Mrs Jainaba Faye stated the pact's value depends on candidates' conduct, emphasizing it remains "only paper until it is lived."

The National Elections Response Group Chairman David Darrow-Tommy urged candidates to prioritize national interests over personal ambitions, noting their words carry immense weight with supporters. Switzerland’s Ambassador to The Gambia, Pedro Mona, highlighted the importance of respecting democratic institutions and ensuring political opponents do not become enemies.

Why it mattersEight presidential candidates have committed to peaceful campaigning for the December 5 election, with the Independent Electoral Commission stating the code of ethics is legally binding.

Ousainou Darboe ends his sixth presidential bid without winning

The 78-year-old UDP leader, who has never been president, is one of four former UDP members cleared to stand in December's ballot.

Ousainou Darboe, a 78-year-old human rights lawyer who has led the United Democratic Party (UDP) since founding it in 1996, is contesting the presidency for the sixth time. Each earlier attempt, in 1996, 2001, 2006, 2011 and 2021, ended with him as runner-up, first to Yahya Jammeh and later to Adama Barrow. He has never held the office.

Darboe was jailed in 2016 after UDP youth leader Ebrima Solo Sandeng died in custody following a protest for electoral reform, and he led a demonstration demanding Sandeng's return "dead or alive". He was released days after Barrow defeated Jammeh in December 2016, later served as Foreign Minister and then as Vice President from June 2018 to March 2019, and has since returned to opposition.

Of the men cleared for the December 2026 ballot, four once belonged to the UDP. Barrow, the incumbent seeking a third term under the National People's Party (NPP), is one of them; Talib Ahmed Bensouda and Lamin J. Darbo, both independents or leaders of new parties, are the others.

Why it mattersFour candidates on the December 2026 ballot once belonged to the UDP, so the party's split now shapes who Gambian voters can choose from.

Gambia aims for 200,000 tonnes of rice by 2030

The government plans to boost rice production through land development, mechanisation, and improved seeds.

The Gambia's Agriculture Minister Dr. Demba Sabally announced a target to increase annual rice production to 200,000 metric tonnes by 2030. The country currently produces about 47,000 metric tonnes of rice, and the government aims to reduce reliance on imports. Sabally spoke on Friday, 13 November 2026, at the Mansa Kunda Ministerial Town Hall Meeting.

To achieve this goal, the government plans to develop 20,000 hectares for rice cultivation and has supplied over 100 rice mills to boost local processing capacity. National rice seed output has risen from fewer than 400 metric tonnes in 2022 to more than 1,000 metric tonnes, with a target of 2,000 metric tonnes of quality climate-smart seeds next year. Development partners, including the Chinese Embassy and the Republic of Korea, are supporting these efforts.

Investments in agricultural mechanisation include the deployment of tractors and combine harvesters to improve land preparation, planting, and harvesting. The government has also provided drying floors and other facilities to reduce post-harvest losses and increase farmers' earnings. Farmers have received free and subsidised seeds, fertilisers, and herbicides to lower production costs and encourage more cultivation.

Why it mattersThe government aims to increase rice production to 200,000 metric tonnes by 2030, impacting food security and reducing import dependence.

Technical experts open Banjul talks on maritime reforms

Experts from member states and port authorities are shaping recommendations on the work programme of the Maritime Organization for West and Central Africa.

Technical experts from across West and Central Africa began talks in Banjul on Monday on maritime reforms, institutional strengthening and regional integration. The Committee of Experts brings together representatives of member states, heads of maritime administrations and port authorities, ahead of the 12th Ordinary Session of the Bureau of Ministers of the Maritime Organization for West and Central Africa (MOWCA).

Olimatou Danso Malang, representing the Gambia Maritime Administration, said the gathering was a key opportunity to advance collective interests and called for practical, measurable actions. MOWCA Secretary-General Dr. Paul Adalikwu said the experts' input would guide ministers on issues affecting the organisation and the wider regional maritime domain. Permanent Secretary Samba C. Mballow, representing the Minister of Transport, Works and Infrastructure, described the committee as the technical backbone of the organisation's decision-making.

Agenda items include implementing MOWCA's 2026 Work Program, operationalising the Regional Maritime Development Bank, and member states' contributions under the Regional Maritime Fund. Experts are also expected to review the proposed transformation of MOWCA into the African Maritime Organization and proposals for convening the first MOWCA Summit of Heads of State.

Why it mattersMember states and port authorities are reviewing the Regional Maritime Development Bank and member contributions to the Regional Maritime Fund, which set how the sector is financed.

New Chinese envoy vows to deepen Gambia-China ties

Ambassador Xiao Yewen pledged to boost cooperation in trade, infrastructure, agriculture, health, and education.

The new Chinese Ambassador to The Gambia, Xiao Yewen, has pledged to strengthen ties between Beijing and Banjul. At a reception on Thursday, 12th November 2026, marking the start of his official duties in Banjul, he said he is ready to work alongside the Government, Parliament, political parties, businesses, youth groups and every sector of society.

Speaking at the Sir Dawda Kairaba Jawara International Conference Centre, Xiao Yewen said the aim is to widen exchanges in trade, infrastructure, agriculture, health, education and vocational training. Government officials, diplomats and guests attended the event, which included cultural performances. He noted that China offers a zero-tariff policy to The Gambia and other African diplomatic partners, and that the Greater Banjul Area Electricity Improvement Project will soon break ground.

Over the past decade, China has funded study places for around 400 outstanding young Gambians in China, he said, and the Confucius Institute at the University of The Gambia is a channel for learning about each other. He also pledged closer coordination with Gambia in multilateral forums to defend the shared interests of developing countries.

Why it mattersThe new Chinese Ambassador pledged to deepen cooperation in trade, infrastructure, agriculture, health, and education, with a zero-tariff policy and an upcoming electricity project benefiting Gambians.
