Gambia begins repaying D563.8 million bus loan

The government made its first principal and interest payment in May 2026 for buses procured for the Gambia Transport Service Company.

The Gambian government has started repaying a D563.8 million loan for buses acquired by the Gambia Transport Service Company (GTSC). The first payment covering principal and interest was made in May 2026, according to Finance Minister Seedy Keita.

Minister Keita informed the National Assembly that the D563,779,771.50 financing will be repaid over 10 semi-annual instalments. The repayment schedule began on April 30, 2026, and is scheduled to conclude by October 30, 2030.

The loan was provided by the Social Security and Housing Finance Corporation (SSHFC) to fund the purchase of GTSC buses. The government is expected to continue these bi-annual payments until the loan is fully settled.

Why it mattersThe government is repaying a significant loan for public transport buses, impacting public finances.

Gambia develops clean cooking strategy

The initiative, supported by the EU and Netherlands, aims to promote safer, cleaner cooking solutions across the nation.

Gambia is creating a National Clean Cooking Strategy to promote safer and more sustainable cooking methods. This effort is part of the Regional Clean Cooking Action in West Africa (ReCCAWA) programme, which supports clean cooking technologies in 12 West African nations. A national workshop was held on Wednesday at the Sir Dawda Kairaba Jawara International Conference Centre to discuss the strategy's development.

The programme, funded by the European Union and the Netherlands Ministry of Foreign Affairs through ECOWAS, targets demand, supply, and policy for clean cooking. Carmen, representing the Netherlands Enterprise Agency (RVO), stated that clean cooking is vital for the health of women and children, environmental protection, and business growth. She highlighted that women and children suffer from toxic smoke and that firewood use pressures forests.

The Deputy Head of the EU Delegation to The Gambia, Raphaël Brigandi, linked clean cooking to environmental protection, women's empowerment, and private sector development. He expressed hope that the workshop would lead to a strong national strategy guiding future policies and actions. The strategy aims to improve health, protect the environment, empower women, and create business opportunities.

Why it mattersGambia's women and children are vulnerable to harmful smoke from traditional cooking methods, and the country's forests face pressure from firewood use.

Gambia launches hub for 20 returning migrants

Kanifing Municipal Council offers services and business support to help returnees rebuild lives.

The Kanifing Municipal Council (KMC) launched the Kanifing Migrant Resilience Hub on October 29, 2026, to support 20 returning migrants. The initiative, backed by the Mayors Migration Council's Global Cities Fund 2025, aims to help returnees rebuild their lives and reintegrate sustainably. Lord Mayor Talib Ahmed Bensouda led the launch, emphasizing the council's commitment to inclusive development.

Mayor Bensouda stated the hub addresses the reality of young people leaving Gambia due to perceived lack of opportunity. He praised the courage of those who returned and are now seeking to restart their lives. The Hub will connect participants with services, resources, and opportunities to strengthen their livelihoods and confidence. Businesses supported include waste collection, delivery services, tailoring, and car painting.

The Resilience Hub includes a childcare centre and psychosocial support services to allow participants to focus on their enterprises. KMC aims to foster collaboration with entities like Startup Incubator Gambia to create lasting opportunities. The council seeks to make Kanifing a place where young people can succeed locally by starting businesses and creating jobs.

Gambia reaffirms UN Charter principles

Vice President Jallow called for stronger partnerships to advance global peace and human rights at UN Day.

Vice President Mohammed B.S. Jallow stated on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, that The Gambia remains committed to the UN Charter's principles. Speaking at the 81st United Nations Day celebration in Bijilo, he emphasized the need for international cooperation to tackle global challenges.

The event, themed “Restoring Trust, Managing Transformation: A United Nations That Delivers for All,” saw VP Jallow commend the UN Country Team for supporting The Gambia’s National Development Plan, ‘YIRIWA’ 2023–2027. He stressed that restoring trust requires transparency and accountability for tangible improvements in people’s lives.

UN Resident Coordinator Karl Frédérick Paul noted that while trust is tested globally, commitment to multilateralism should not waver. He referenced conflicts in Sudan, Gaza, and Ukraine, where civilians suffer from peace failures and climate disruption.

Darboe pledges 24-hour power for Gambia's hospitals

The opposition leader also vowed to improve maternal healthcare and fight corruption if elected president.

United Democratic Party leader Ousainou Darboe pledged to ensure 24-hour electricity for Gambian hospitals and improve maternal healthcare if his party wins the December presidential election. Darboe stated that quality healthcare is a fundamental right for all Gambians, regardless of their location.

Speaking from his Pipeline residence, Darboe said a UDP government would equip hospitals with reliable power and essential medical equipment. He also plans to increase public health education to reduce maternal and infant mortality, arguing that power outages should not endanger patients.

Darboe also vowed to combat corruption and wasteful spending, promising to restore accountability and transparency in government. He asserted that public funds would be used for the benefit of Gambians, not squandered on low-value projects.

Why it mattersGambia's hospitals will receive reliable electricity and better maternal care under a potential UDP government.
