Gambia targets 3.21% GDP revenue gain by 2030

The nation aims to boost its tax-to-GDP ratio through reforms outlined at the African Tax Administration Forum in Cairo.

The Gambia aims to increase its domestic revenue by 3.21 percentage points of GDP by 2030 under its first Domestic Resource Mobilization Strategy (DRMS) for 2026–2030. Finance Minister Seedy K.M. Keita announced this goal on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, at the African Tax Administration Forum in Cairo, Egypt. He noted that The Gambia’s tax-to-GDP ratio rose from 9.8 per cent in 2020 to about 13 per cent by the end of 2025, but still lags the sub-Saharan African average.

Minister Keita stated that the Gambia Revenue Authority (GRA) collected over D3 billion in March 2026, a monthly record for the institution. The DRMS integrates tax policy, revenue administration, and non-tax measures to approach the sub-Saharan African average tax effort by 2030. Key reforms include investing in digital tax administration, improving tax policy, strengthening public trust, and ensuring national ownership of revenue mobilization.

The GRA is implementing an Integrated Tax Administration System, rolling out electronic VAT invoicing, and introducing digital compliance tools for rental income and the telecommunications sector. The ministry and GRA are also reviewing the Income Tax and Value Added Tax Acts to enhance taxation of digital services and transfer pricing. A standalone Tax Administration Act is also under development.

Why it mattersThe Gambia's new strategy aims to increase domestic revenue by 3.21 percentage points of GDP by 2030, affecting national development priorities.

EU deploys 16 observers for Gambia election

The European Union Election Observation Mission will monitor the December 5, 2026 presidential polls.

16 long-term observers from the European Union have been deployed across The Gambia to monitor the December 5, 2026 presidential election. The European Union Election Observation Mission (EU EOM) announced the deployment on Thursday, November 5, from its headquarters in Banjul. Chief Observer Reinhold Lopatka stated the mission aims to contribute to an inclusive, credible, and transparent electoral process.

The observers, from 15 EU member states and Switzerland, will visit different regions to assess the electoral environment. They will engage with election officials, candidates, political parties, civil society, and media. The mission’s core team arrived on October 21 to evaluate the political and legal framework. Lopatka noted that the EU does not certify election results, which remains the responsibility of The Gambia's Independent Electoral Commission.

A further 20 short-term observers and a seven-member European Parliament delegation will join before election day. The EU mission will stay until the process is complete, with Lopatka set to present preliminary findings two days after the vote. The EU EOM was invited by Gambian authorities to observe the polls.

Why it mattersGambian citizens will have international observers monitoring their presidential election on December 5, 2026.

Gambia medical council delays block 35+ doctors

China-trained graduates face licensing hurdles, prompting some to seek work abroad.

More than 35 Gambian medical graduates trained in China are unable to work in the country due to prolonged licensing delays by the Medical and Dental Council. The China-Gambia Alumni Students’ Association reported that these doctors completed their studies and clinical training, with some having finished mandatory internships in China and rotations at Edward Francis Small Teaching Hospital. The association stated that the Council recognizes their degrees but not their internship certificates, unlike graduates from other countries.

The association is calling on the government and parliament to intervene in the dispute, as over 35 doctors have already left The Gambia for countries like the US and Morocco due to frustration with the Council. Some graduates are considering returning to Morocco, Europe, Asia, or America where they can obtain licenses without redoing internships. The graduates also question why they face additional internship requirements when they sat the same licensing examination in November 2024 and August 2026.

Modou Secka, the association's president, warned that these delays exacerbate The Gambia's doctor shortage, pushing the doctor-to-patient ratio further below the World Health Organization's standard. He noted that the lack of a clear registration path hinders career planning and wastes public and international investment in medical education. Graduates who faced similar issues previously returned to Morocco for licensing.

Why it mattersGambia's health system faces a shortage of doctors because the Medical and Dental Council is delaying the licensing of over 35 China-trained medical graduates.

Gambia opposition leader files for election despite abuse claims

Talib Ahmed Bensouda submitted candidacy papers for the December 5 presidential election despite a government report alleging impropriety.

Gambia's opposition leader Talib Ahmed Bensouda filed his candidacy papers on Friday for the December 5 presidential election. Bensouda, leader of the UNITE-led coalition, submitted his papers despite a government report last week alleging financial improprieties and abuse of office during his eight years as mayor of Kanifing Municipality.

Bensouda's UNITE party dismissed the allegations as a political manoeuvre by the ruling National People's Party (NPP) government. Supporters celebrated Bensouda's move, highlighting his popularity gained through regular waste collection and his rising political profile amidst the nation's prolonged electricity cuts.

The presidential election will see President Adama Barrow running for a third term. Leading opposition candidate Ousainou Darboe of the United Democratic Party has also declared his intention to run. Bensouda would be disqualified from standing if the allegations against him are proven.

Team Gambia flag bearer pledges five-year term

Dr Lamin J. Darboe says his government will focus on rebuilding and reforms, not personal gain.

Dr Lamin J. Darboe, flag bearer for the Team Gambia Grand Coalition 2026, has promised a five-year transitional government focused on national rebuilding, economic growth, and institutional reform if elected in December. Darboe stated on 5 November 2025 that he would not contest the 2031 presidential election, ensuring an open contest without incumbency advantage.

Darboe described his candidacy as a national duty to bring about desired change, emphasizing that the government would not replace one political establishment with another. He outlined aspirations for dignity, opportunity, justice, prosperity, and accountability, aiming to reduce poverty and dependence on external aid through support for mechanised farming, industry, business, investment, and skills training.

The coalition plans to reform education for practical skills and make quality healthcare affordable. Constitutional and institutional reforms will strengthen the rule of law, protect rights, and separate government powers. Darboe also pledged to prevent governments from using public resources for political advantage, citing concerns about political influence at Gambia Radio and Television Services.

Why it mattersGambians will experience a government focused on national rebuilding, economic growth, and institutional reform, with pledges for better education, healthcare, and fairer political contests.
