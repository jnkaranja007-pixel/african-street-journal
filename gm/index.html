Gambia addresses over half of road deficit

The government has built 1,200 kilometres of roads since 2017, with 1,600 more under survey.

The Gambian government has completed more than half of its estimated 4,000-kilometre road infrastructure deficit through projects started in 2017. Minister of Transport Ebrima Sillah stated on Friday, November 6, 2026, that 1,200 kilometres of roads are now built, and another 1,600 kilometres are being surveyed nationwide.

Sillah explained that new housing developments without roads contribute to the deficit, requiring government intervention for access. The World Bank is studying 500 kilometres of roads and may finance 150 kilometres, while seeking partners for the remaining 350 kilometres. The Sukuta-Jabang overpass, an OIC project, is set for inauguration by President Adama Barrow.

Addressing property demolitions for road expansion, Sillah noted delays due to ownership disputes and the need to relocate utilities. He also urged motorists to yield to ambulances to improve emergency response times.

Why it mattersGambia's government is working to improve road infrastructure, impacting citizens' daily travel and access to services.

Gambia elected to ATAF Council

The country will help shape the African Tax Administration Forum's direction for two years.

The Gambia Revenue Authority (GRA) joined tax administrators from 38 African nations in Cairo, Egypt, for the African Tax Administration Forum (ATAF) annual meetings from November 1 to 5, 2026. The meetings focused on Africa's path to financing its own development through strengthened domestic revenue mobilisation. Minister of Finance Seedy M.K. Keita delivered a keynote address on translating global commitments into national reforms.

GRA Commissioner General Yankuba Darboe participated in discussions on domestic resource mobilisation, highlighting the need for tax reforms tailored to individual African countries. He shared The Gambia's experience with modernising customs operations using ASYCUDA World and the National Single Window, alongside plans for an Integrated Tax Administration System (ITAS). Partnerships supporting digital excise stamps and fuel marking were also cited as ways to improve visibility and address revenue leakages.

During the 9th ATAF General Assembly on November 4, 2026, The Gambia was elected to the ATAF Council for the 2026–2028 term. This election provides an opportunity for the country to contribute to the strategic direction of the continental tax body.

Why it mattersThe Gambia's election to the ATAF Council allows it to influence continental tax policy and contribute to Africa's financial self-reliance.

Gambia distributes 12 tractors to farmers

The Ministry of Agriculture aims to boost food production and modernise farming with new machinery.

The Ministry of Agriculture handed over 12 tractors to farmers on Friday at the Agricultural Soil Campus in Yundum. This distribution is part of the government's agricultural mechanisation programme to increase food production and modernise farming.

Minister of Agriculture Dr. Demba Sabally stated the initiative will improve productivity by giving farmers better access to machinery, following an earlier distribution of 25 tractors. The programme uses a cost-sharing model where government and farmers each pay 25 per cent upfront, with the remaining 50 per cent paid over four years.

NACOFAG President Musa F. Sowe said the tractors will help farmers increase production, cut costs, and cultivate more land. The Regional Agricultural Director for the West Coast Region, Bakary Gassama, noted a new strategy was adopted after some farmers failed to repay under a 2020 scheme.
