Gambia adds 21MW to grid, signs solar project

Energy minister apologises for recent power outages and announces upgrades to water infrastructure.

The Gambia's national electricity grid will receive an additional 21 megawatts (MW) from two newly installed generators, with another planned for the coming months. The Minister of Energy, Petroleum and Mines, Nani Juwara, announced these additions and the signing of a major solar energy project at the Mansa Kunda Ministerial Town Hall Meeting on Friday.

Juwara apologised for recent power disruptions, stating that the government has taken steps to prevent recurrence and is investing in water infrastructure, including the inauguration of the Brikama Water Storage Tank Project and ongoing construction of treatment plants in Sanyang and Sifoe. The National Water and Electricity Company (NAWEC) is also replacing ageing pipelines in Bundung.

Separately, Mrs Kani Jobe, head of the Petroleum Commission, confirmed that The Gambia remains at the oil exploration stage and has not begun commercial production or exports. She noted that a Petroleum Exploration, Development and Production Licence Agreement has been signed with Eni Gambia Ltd for offshore hydrocarbon exploration.

Why it mattersGambia's citizens are affected by recent power outages and improvements to electricity and water services, while the nation is at the oil exploration stage.

Gambia election sees 25 aspirants seek nomination

The Independent Electoral Commission has opened the nomination window for 25 presidential hopefuls ahead of the December 5 poll.

Gambia will hold its presidential election on December 5, with 25 aspirants showing interest in nomination. The Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) has set November 3 to 14, 2026, for candidates to file their papers. This period is anticipated to be the busiest nomination window in recent history, indicating a large number of political parties, coalitions, and independent candidates vying to succeed President Adama Barrow.

Among the prominent figures expected to file nominations are incumbent President Adama Barrow of the National People's Party (NPP), Lawyer Ousainu Darboe representing the UDP-led Gambia First Coalition, and Kanifing Mayor Talib Ahmed Bensouda of UNITE and Coalition 2026. Candidates must meet constitutional requirements, including citizenship, age, and voter support, and pay a nomination deposit. The IEC has scheduled specific dates for each aspirant and party, with UNITE and its partners due on November 7.

Following the nomination period, the IEC will vet the submissions and publish a final list of candidates. This process will pave the way for official campaigning to commence before Gambians head to the polls on December 5 to elect their next president.

Why it mattersGambia's citizens will elect a new president on December 5, with 25 individuals seeking nomination to run.

Barrow cites SIS transformation as security reform proof

President Adama Barrow stated the intelligence service's evolution demonstrates significant progress in security sector reforms.

President Adama Barrow has identified the transformation of The Gambia's intelligence service into the State Intelligence Service (SIS) as a key indicator of security sector reforms under his leadership. In an interview with QTV journalist SO Nyassi, Barrow contrasted the past fear associated with the former National Intelligence Agency (NIA) with the current professional approach of the SIS. He stated that even the SIS Director General's visits now inspire no fear, unlike before.

An insider familiar with SIS operations noted that the Director General's directive to eliminate operational excesses has been a guiding principle for nearly a decade. Barrow's remarks align with his 2017 campaign promise to reform the security architecture and strengthen democratic governance. The government has stressed that security institutions must operate professionally, respect the rule of law, and uphold citizens' rights.

The SIS recently validated the State Intelligence Service Bill, intended to replace the former NIA's legal framework and considered a vital part of the reform agenda. Barrow also commented on ECOMIG forces, describing them as complementary to Gambian services in maintaining peace and supporting democracy, and affirmed their eventual departure. He highlighted the adoption of a national defence policy as a milestone in building professional, accountable, and democratic institutions.

Why it mattersGambians can expect security services to operate more professionally and with greater respect for their rights.

Gambia coalition seeks halt to new fishing licences

A fisheries coalition warns overfishing threatens fish stocks and local livelihoods, urging a freeze on new industrial licences.

The National Coalition for Sustainable Fisheries in The Gambia has called for a freeze on new industrial fishing licences. The group warned that continued pressure on marine resources could further threaten fish stocks and the livelihoods of local fishing communities.

Dawda Foday Saine, coordinator of the coalition, stated that reducing fishing pressure and strengthening enforcement are necessary for fish stocks to recover. He expressed concern over illegal fishing, fishmeal production, and competition between industrial and small-scale fishermen, noting that bonga and sardinella are overexploited.

Greenpeace Africa's Mamadou Kaly Ba said the coalition developed a Sustainable Fisheries Charter with 13 commitments for 2030, which will be presented to presidential candidates. The Charter aims to protect the ocean, improve livelihoods, and address food insecurity and unemployment.

Why it mattersLocal fishing communities face threats to their livelihoods and access to affordable food like bonga and sardinella due to overfishing.
