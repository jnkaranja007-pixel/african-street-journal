Barrow leads CepRass poll with 37.9% before Gambia election

The survey found 52% dissatisfied with government delivery on promises and 80% rating the national economy badly.

Among respondents holding valid voter cards, 37.9% said they would back the incumbent, according to a survey by the Centre for Policy, Research and Strategic Studies (CepRass). The Gambia’s presidential election is scheduled for December 5.

United Democratic Party leader Ousainou A.N.M. Darboe received 24.2% and Talib Ahmed Bensouda 11.3%. 52 percent of respondents judged the government’s delivery on promises ineffective or very ineffective, 80% rated the national economy badly, and 43% named the cost of living as the country’s biggest concern.

Fieldwork ran from October 17 to November 1, with 1,180 people questioned in 118 enumeration areas across all eight local government areas. CepRass said the figures capture opinion during fieldwork and should not be read as a forecast of the result. Candidate competence was the leading stated influence on voting decisions, cited by 39%.

Why it mattersVoters face the December 5 election after 43% of respondents named the cost of living as the country’s biggest concern.

ActionAid urges peaceful campaigning ahead of Gambia’s Dec. 5 vote

The non-partisan group called on candidates, parties and supporters to obey electoral laws and keep political competition free of intimidation and violence.

ActionAid International The Gambia has urged political parties, presidential candidates, supporters and the public to campaign peacefully and follow electoral laws as campaigning begins for the Dec. 5, 2026, presidential election. In a statement issued Monday, the non-partisan organisation said political competition must respect peace and tolerance.

The organisation called on party leaders and candidates to honour the Peace Pact they signed through their conduct during the campaign, on election day and after results are announced. It urged the Independent Electoral Commission to act independently and fairly, and security agencies to protect citizens and electoral participants while exercising restraint.

ActionAid also asked citizens, media and social media users to respect differing views and verify information before sharing it. It said voters must be able to cast ballots without intimidation, while electoral complaints should be resolved through legal and institutional processes.

Why it mattersVoters must be able to cast ballots without intimidation on Dec. 5, while complaints are to be handled through legal and institutional processes.

Bensouda pledges Banjul-Barra bridge before 2036 if elected

The Coalition 2026 presidential candidate says the link would strengthen trade and help make the North Bank Region The Gambia's commercial capital.

Talib Ahmed Bensouda, the presidential candidate of Coalition 2026, has pledged to build a bridge linking Banjul and Barra before 2036 if elected. He made the promise at spot meetings in Essau and Pakau Njoku and at a rally in Munyagen, in Jokadou.

Bensouda said the North Bank could become The Gambia's commercial capital, given its location near Senegal and regional cities such as Dakar and Bamako. He said the bridge is a key commitment in his coalition's manifesto and that without such a link the region would struggle to reach its full economic potential.

He said the bridge was something the government under President Barrow could not deliver. "Within 10 years, we will bridge Banjul and Barra," Bensouda said. "Before 2036, there will be a Banjul-Barra Bridge."

Why it mattersCommercial activity on the North Bank depends on the link, Bensouda says, so the bridge would decide whether the region grows into a trading hub.

Germany equips Gambian hospital's children's emergency unit with heart scanner

The German Embassy also paid for five days of echocardiography training for resident doctors at Edward Francis Small Teaching Hospital.

The German Embassy in The Gambia has donated an echography machine to the Emergency Paediatric Unit at Edward Francis Small Teaching Hospital (EFSTH) to help doctors diagnose congenital heart defects in children on time. The embassy also sponsored a five-day echocardiography course for selected resident doctors so they can operate the machine.

Dr. Sara Loetz, head of the unit, said children make up about 40% of The Gambia's population and that the neonatal unit alone had more than 135 babies in far too little space last week. She said about 1% of babies worldwide are born with congenital heart conditions. Professor Augusta Eneh, head of the hospital's paediatric department, said the country's ability to diagnose these defects quickly had been limited by a lack of diagnostic tools and specialised training.

Dr. Cherno Jallow, deputy chief medical director at EFSTH, said the embassy had gone beyond giving a machine by building staff capacity to use it. German Envoy Klaus Botzet said machines do not work by themselves and depend on trained people.

Why it mattersResident doctors will learn to run the machine in a five-day course, and mothers of children with suspected heart defects can expect earlier diagnoses.

Sport · 2026-11-18

Gambia lets 2-1 lead slip to draw with Ivory Coast

Musa Barrow scored a 79th-minute penalty, but Ivory Coast equalised with the final kick after Musa Badamosi was sent off.

The Gambia drew 2-2 with Ivory Coast at Independence Stadium after surrendering a 2-1 lead in the closing minutes. Yankuba Minteh equalised in the 68th minute, and Musa Barrow converted a penalty in the 79th to put the Scorpions ahead.

Musa Badamosi was shown a red card after a late confrontation, leaving The Gambia with 10 players. Coach Jonathan McKinstry said the team had lost its discipline, and said Ivory Coast scored with the final kick of the match after stoppage time stretched toward nine minutes.

McKinstry said he would review video of the incident that led to Badamosi’s dismissal, while declining to use the decision as an excuse. He said The Gambia had played well enough to earn the lead but gave it away through a collective failure of discipline.

Why it mattersMcKinstry said the team must review the red-card incident on video after Badamosi’s dismissal left The Gambia with 10 players.
