Gambia election: Nine cleared for December 5 vote

Incumbent Adama Barrow and veteran opposition leader Ousainou Darboe are among the nine candidates cleared to stand.

Nine candidates will contest The Gambia's December 5 presidential election after the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) closed nominations on Saturday. 11 aspirants withdrew and seven nominations were rejected during the process, which opened on November 3. Official campaigning starts on November 16 and runs until December 3.

Incumbent Adama Barrow, 61, of the National People's Party is seeking a third term, while A.N.M. Ousainou Darboe, 78, of the United Democratic Party is making his sixth presidential bid. Isatou Touray, standing as an independent, is the first woman to appear on a Gambian presidential ballot. Talib Ahmed Bensouda, 40, of the UNITE party, stepped down as Mayor of Kanifing after eight years to run. IEC Chairman Joseph Colley urged candidates to campaign within the law.

On Sunday the IEC will harmonise candidates' campaign programmes to avoid clashes, and on Monday the Commission, International IDEA and the candidates will sign a Peace Pact in Banjul. Earlier, the IEC rejected Bakusa Dukureh over dual citizenship and an unauthenticated secondary school certificate, and Kebba Bojang over conflicting birth dates.

Why it mattersVoters now face a fixed field of nine names, and campaigning must end by December 3 with the rules agreed in the Peace Pact.

Gambian parties and stakeholders sign Janjanbureh peace accord before December vote

The revised accord commits parties to peaceful, inclusive and credible polls on December 5, with the EU planning an independent observer mission.

On Thursday, November 12, political parties and national and international stakeholders signed a revised peace accord at the International Conference Centre, pledging to support peaceful, inclusive and credible polls for The Gambia's December 5 presidential election. Signatories included registered parties, the Independent Electoral Commission, the United Nations, the African Union, ECOWAS, the European Union, civil society groups and religious communities, all convened by the Inter-Party Committee (IPC).

Among the accord's aims are preventing electoral violence, intimidation, hate speech and political intolerance, and it calls on parties to resolve disputes through dialogue and lawful means. IPC Co-Chair Ejatou Jallow said competition should never lead to violence, and urged that women, young people and marginalised groups take part without fear. UNDP Resident Coordinator Mandisa Mashologu said the accord's success would depend on party leaders and their supporters throughout the election period.

EU representative Raphael Brigandi called the signing a serious commitment to the country's democracy and said the EU would deploy an independent Election Observation Mission at the government's invitation. ECOWAS representative Namizata Meite Sangare urged parties to share the accord's commitments with candidates, local officials, polling agents and supporters across the country.

Why it mattersParties, candidates and polling agents are now bound by written commitments against intimidation and hate speech ahead of the December 5 vote.

Gambia's NDP accuses electoral commission of opacity over Bojang candidacy

The National Democratic Party says the Independent Electoral Commission denied its representatives access to its premises while they sought details of leader Kebba Madi Bojang's.

The National Democratic Party (NDP) has accused the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) of a lack of transparency and fairness in handling the presidential nomination of its leader, Kebba Madi Bojang, for the December 5 election. In a press statement, the party said its representatives were reportedly denied access to IEC premises while asking about the status of Bojang's papers.

IEC Chairman Joseph Colley rejected Bojang's nomination earlier this week, citing a mismatch between his date of birth on West African Examinations Council (WAEC) records, March 15, 1995, and on his identity document, March 15, 1975. The commission has confirmed five other candidates and rejected two more over eligibility concerns. Bojang, 51, submitted his papers on November 6 and insists they are authentic.

Bojang said he had asked the IEC for proof of the date it obtained from WAEC but had not received it, and that he planned to consult his lawyer and write to the commission to challenge the decision. The NDP demanded an immediate explanation from the IEC, which had not yet publicly responded to its latest claims about access to its premises.

Why it mattersBojang's rejected nomination means the party's presidential candidate cannot stand unless the commission's decision is overturned through his legal challenge.

Lahaiba FA beat Medina FC 4-3 on penalties to win SESDO League Cup

Lahaiba FA won the Serrekunda East Sports Development Organization (SESDO) 2026 Africell League Cup after a goalless draw, taking the penalty shootout 4-3 at the East Mini Stadium.

Lahaiba FA beat Medina FC 4-3 on penalties after a 0-0 draw in regulation time, lifting the Serrekunda East Sports Development Organization (SESDO) 2026 Africell League Cup trophy at the East Mini Stadium. The final was played before a capacity crowd.

Alagie B. Ceesay fired wide for Lahaiba in the 13th minute, and Sainey Manga saved a set-piece from Ebrima Sanneh in the 31st. Manga, Lahaiba's goalkeeper, was named Man of the Match, receiving a certificate, a pair of boots and D2,000 from Burr Fashion Plug. Play stopped for nearly seven minutes after Manga was injured in the second half.

Lahaiba head coach Alfusainey Sanneh (Zidane) said he was the first coach to take a Fajikunda side to the SESDO League Cup title. Medina head coach Haruna Jabang said his side had lost back-to-back finals. As champions Lahaiba received gold medals, the giant trophy and D100,000, while Medina collected silver medals and D70,000.

Why it mattersLahaiba FA receives D100,000 in prize money as champions, while runners-up Medina FC receive D70,000.
