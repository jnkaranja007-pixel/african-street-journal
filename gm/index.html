Barrow nominated for December 5 presidential election

President Adama Barrow filed nomination papers as the NPP candidate seeking a third five-year mandate.

President Adama Barrow was nominated on November 10, 2026, to run for president on December 5, 2026, as the candidate for the National People’s Party (NPP). His nomination followed a large procession of over 100,000 NPP members and supporters who accompanied him to the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) headquarters.

IEC Chairman Joseph Colley confirmed that Barrow submitted all required nomination documents. After the nomination, Barrow called for peace and respect for the rule of law, promising an ambitious transformation agenda for 2027–2031 focusing on agriculture, healthcare, education, electricity, and roads. Political commentators described the nomination procession as the largest political crowd in Gambian history.

Barrow, 61, is seeking a third five-year term after winning in 2016 and 2021. He stated that the Gambian people decide who serves as president and that he would ensure a new constitution with term limits if he secures an absolute majority in the National Assembly. The nomination window runs until November 14, with opposition leader Ousainou Darboe having already filed.

Why it mattersPresident Barrow's nomination for a third term and his comments on term limits directly affect Gambian voters and the country's constitutional future.

Lamin J. Darboe files nomination for Gambia presidential election

Team Gambia aspirant pledges rule of law and transparency if elected president on December 5.

Lamin J. Darboe, presidential aspirant for the Team Gambia Grand Coalition, submitted his nomination papers to the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) on Wednesday for the December 5 election. He pledged to establish a government founded on the rule of law, transparency, and respect for citizens’ dignity if elected.

Darboe stated that good governance is his foremost priority, arguing that meaningful national development cannot occur without accountable and transparent leadership. He believes The Gambia’s persistent governance challenges since independence in 1965 have contributed to the country’s stagnation.

Lawmaker Almameh Gibba, contesting under the APRC No To Alliance, also submitted his papers, outlining priorities including national unity, improved health outcomes, and food self-sufficiency. Gibba also criticized the slow pace of security sector legislative action and called for a full accounting of assets linked to the former president.

Why it mattersGambia's presidential election on December 5 will determine the country's future leadership and governance approach.

Japan and UNICEF hand scabies supplies to Gambian health authorities

Medicines, nutrition and hygiene supplies target about 157,000 people in the West Coast, Central River and Upper River regions.

Japan, working with UNICEF and the Gambian government, handed over medical, nutrition and water, sanitation and hygiene supplies to address a scabies outbreak. The ceremony took place on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, at the West Coast Regional Health Directorate in Brikama. The supplies target about 157,000 people across three regions.

Japan's Ambassador to The Gambia, Takeshi Akamatsu, said infectious diseases threaten children's health in overcrowded communities with limited access to safe water. He said tackling scabies requires early detection, treatment, better hygiene and community awareness, not only medicine. UNICEF Country Representative Nafisa Binte Shafique said the project also aims to strengthen community-based health systems.

Under the project, more than 400 healthcare workers and surveillance officers have been trained, and over 68,000 children under five have been screened for malnutrition. Safe handwashing and sanitation facilities are being provided for more than 55,000 people. Director of National Health Services Dr. Momodou T. Nyassi said the supplies were funded through Japan's 2025 Supplementary Budget.

Why it mattersChildren under five and residents of three regions gain malnutrition screening, trained frontline health workers and new handwashing and sanitation facilities.

Gambia rejects GBA concerns over court case delay

Information Minister Dr Ceesay stated the government respects the rule of law amid Bar Association worries.

The Gambian government has dismissed concerns from the Gambia Bar Association (GBA) regarding delays in assigning a constitutional case. Information Minister Dr Ismaila Ceesay stated on November 11, 2026, that there was no deliberate attempt to stall the proceedings, asserting the government's respect for the rule of law and judicial independence.

Dr Ceesay, speaking on Coffee Time with Peter Gomez, called the GBA's suggestion of deliberate stalling an "opinion" and not a fact, which the government "does not accept." The GBA had expressed worry that the case, involving the Chief Justice as a defendant, had been unassigned for over three months and questioned its referral to him for corrections.

The Minister declined to comment on whether the case should be removed from the Chief Justice's purview, citing potential use against him in court. He encouraged continued engagement between the Bar Association and the government, emphasizing that due process is essential for all cases.

Why it mattersThe government's response to the Gambia Bar Association's concerns impacts public trust in the judicial process.

Papa Secka declares independent presidential bid

The 55-year-old aspirant aims to reset the nation by tackling corruption and unemployment.

Papa Secka, 55, filed his nomination papers for the 2026 presidential race as an independent, saying it is time to reset The Gambia. He named corruption, mismanaged natural resources, insecure land ownership and high youth unemployment as the country's main problems, and said he found no existing party platform that matched his agenda.

Secka called the management of natural resources a critical national failure, pointing to fish that have become scarce, and said the country must regain control of its resources and land. On corruption, he said reform must begin with personal integrity and a proven track record, and he cited his public service and honesty as the basis for his candidacy. Some required documents, including supporters' signatures and the D5,000 deposit, were still outstanding when the nomination was reviewed.

Secka said he wants affordable, reliable energy to draw investors, arguing that industrialisation would create jobs and that targeted training would prepare young people to fill them. He confirmed he does not hold dual nationality. He said he would work with Gambians who share his agenda rather than form coalitions for their own sake.

Why it mattersYoung Gambians seeking jobs and investors weighing energy costs are the groups Secka's agenda names directly, and his plan depends on their support.
