Gambia spent D785 million on diesel subsidies

Finance Minister Seedy Keita announced the subsidies were to cushion rising fuel prices between March and September 2026.

The Gambian government spent D785.13 million on diesel subsidies and D42.46 million on petrol subsidies between March and September 2026. Finance Minister Seedy Keita announced these figures on Friday, October 23, 2026, during his presentation of the 2027 budget speech to the National Assembly. These subsidies were implemented to lessen the impact of increased fuel prices on citizens.

Minister Keita stated that domestic retail prices for petrol and diesel had risen by 33.1% and 41.8% respectively during the same six-month period. He explained that these subsidies ensured fuel prices remained lower than they would have been if international price increases were fully passed on. The government's 2027 budget theme is “Deepening Economic Resilience while Enhancing Fiscal Sustainability”.

The minister also reported that inflation had risen to 6.5% in August 2026 before falling to 6.1% in September. He noted that downside risks to the economy remained significant. The government aims for a fiscal deficit of 1% of GDP in 2027 for all funds. Domestic revenue collection reached D20.27 billion by the end of August 2026, a 7.4% increase from the previous year.

Why it mattersGambian citizens were protected from higher fuel costs due to government subsidies.

Coalition 2026 unveils 66 actions for Gambia by 2031

The manifesto promises constitutional review, increased health spending, and a new development bank.

Coalition 2026, led by UNITE leader Talib Ahmed Bensouda, launched its manifesto titled ‘The Gambia We Deserve’ on October 22, 2026. The plan outlines 3 pillars, 13 policy points, and 66 priority actions to be completed by 2031. Bensouda stated the manifesto is built around governance and trust, human development, and economic integrity.

The manifesto includes a constitutional review and referendum within two years and aims to raise health spending to 15% of the budget. It also proposes establishing a National Development Bank to drive job creation and economic growth. Bensouda highlighted that Gambians face a high cost of living, youth unemployment, and challenges in farming.

The coalition's vision is structured around three pillars: governance and trust, human development, and economic integrity. Bensouda assured that the manifesto contains specific actions with proposed deadlines, aiming to deliver what matters with discipline, transparency, and results.

Why it mattersCoalition 2026's manifesto proposes reforms and actions that directly affect Gambian citizens if elected.

25 aspirants pick up nomination forms for Gambia election

The Independent Electoral Commission will start accepting nominations from November 3 to November 14 for the December 5 presidential vote.

25 presidential aspirants have collected nomination forms as The Gambia prepares for its 5th December 2026 election. The Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) announced that the official nomination period will run from November 3 to November 14. IEC Chairperson Joseph Colley stated that 23 forms had already been collected by potential candidates.

Colley said the IEC would announce a schedule for candidates to submit their credentials and meet legal requirements. He urged all aspirants to abide by the stipulated regulations for an orderly and transparent process. The IEC is also finalizing the voter register, recruiting polling staff, and expanding voter education.

The IEC is concerned about misinformation and fake news on social media impacting the democratic process. Colley called on political actors and social media users to ensure information about the electoral process is accurate and verifiable. Revising Court hearings on voter registration objections are nearing completion, with 1,694 objections submitted.

Why it mattersGambia's presidential election will proceed with 25 aspirants collecting nomination forms, affecting citizens' choice of leader.

Gambia farmers say new roads do not end hunger

Residents in Kiang Sankandi and Ballen villages say infrastructure projects have not addressed food security or income needs.

New roads and expanded electricity networks are changing parts of The Gambia, but rural communities say these projects have not addressed their most urgent needs for food, farming support, and income.

Farmers in Kiang Sankandi and Ballen villages say improved transport links do not put food on tables or provide year-round resources, with one farmer stating, "Development starts from the stomach."

Residents welcome investments in water and electricity but argue that infrastructure should be linked to people's needs, with a preference for stronger agricultural support over road construction due to escalating living costs.

Why it mattersFarmers in rural communities are directly affected by the government's development priorities, which they feel neglect agriculture and food security.

Gambia highlights heritage sites for tourism growth

NCAC Director General Hassoum Ceesay promotes Kunta Kinteh Island and Wassu Stone Circles beyond beach tourism.

The Gambia's National Centre for Arts and Culture (NCAC) is promoting cultural heritage sites to boost tourism beyond beaches. Director General Hassoum Ceesay highlighted Kunta Kinteh Island and the Wassu Stone Circles, both UNESCO World Heritage Sites, in an interview at the NCAC headquarters.

Ceesay described Kunta Kinteh Island, formerly James Island, as a site of horror and terror, noting its role as a departure point for slaves during the Transatlantic Slave Trade. Renamed in 2011 to honor Alex Haley's "Roots" hero, the island served as a barracoon where captives were held until ships were full.

The government has plans to enhance Kunta Kinteh Island's appeal and educational value, while the Wassu Stone Circles are also recognized as an archaeological enigma and a key tourism attraction. Ceesay aims to give these sites their true meaning for Gambians and humanity.

Why it mattersGambians and humanity can learn about the historical significance of cultural heritage sites like Kunta Kinteh Island and the Wassu Stone Circles.
