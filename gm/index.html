Matty Jobe Becomes Face of Gambia International Fashion Week 2026

The upcoming fashion event, themed 'Roots to Runway — Luxury Born in Gambia,' aims to boost local talent and the economy.

Gambia International Fashion Week (GIFW) has appointed Matty Jobe as the face for its 2026 edition, scheduled from November 10 to 17. The event, under the theme 'Roots to Runway — Luxury Born in Gambia,' seeks to enhance visibility, training, and career opportunities for young Gambian designers, stylists, and models. Official casting calls are set for October 18 at Arch 22 in Banjul.

Founded in 2023 by Ismaila Jallow as a non-profit initiative, GIFW aims to provide a clear path for creatives within the fashion industry, both locally and internationally. Event Coordinator Fatima Muloshi stated that the platform's vision is to ensure that talent does not need to leave the country for opportunities, fostering skills development, business creation, and job growth through fashion.

This year's GIFW will focus on sustainability through recycling, promoting 'Made in Gambia' products, and positioning the country as a global fashion destination. The 'Made in Gambia' pillar specifically encourages stronger connections between farmers, artisans, designers, and consumers, thereby supporting local livelihoods and the economy. Winners will also receive a study trip to international fashion universities.

Why it mattersYoung Gambian creatives and designers are directly impacted by the opportunities for visibility, training, and international exposure provided by the fashion week.

Gambia Backs Samba for WHO Leadership

A Gambian delegation lobbied for Dr. Ebrahim Malick Samba's re-election to lead WHO's African region, highlighting his past successes.

In September 1999, a Gambian delegation traveled to Windhoek, Namibia, to support the re-election of Dr. Ebrahim Malick Samba for a leadership role within the World Health Organization's Regional Committee for Africa. The delegation, including the author who was then permanent secretary at the Ministry of Health and Social Welfare, engaged with regional counterparts to secure support for Dr. Samba, whose reputation in African public health preceded him.

Dr. Samba's prior service included leading the WHO Onchocerciasis Control Programme from 1980 to 1994, an initiative that required extensive regional cooperation to combat river blindness across borders. His tenure as WHO Regional Director for Africa from 1995 to 2005 was marked by a commitment to strengthening health systems by supporting the establishment of medical education in The Gambia.

This support involved providing technical assistance and professional expertise through WHO's office in Brazzaville, collaborating with Gambian and regional professionals. The initiative to build medical education within The Gambia was seen as a significant advancement for the country's health infrastructure and capacity.

Why it mattersThe re-election of Dr. Samba as WHO Regional Director for Africa and the support for medical education in The Gambia directly impacts the nation's health sector and its professionals.

Gambia's Reforms Fail to Build Public Trust, Speakers Warn

Despite democratic progress, Gambians lack confidence in key institutions, with reforms needing to translate into tangible improvements for citizens.

Speakers at the WADR Forum urged Gambian authorities to ensure that democratic reforms translate into public trust, particularly as the nation approaches presidential elections. Agnes E. John-Thomasi of WADR highlighted the project's aim to provide citizens with credible information for meaningful participation and to foster community dialogue, noting that while The Gambia has progressed, democracy requires functioning institutions and informed citizens.

Isatou Keita, President of the Gambia Press Union, stated that democracy hinges on trust, citing Afrobarometer data showing declining confidence in the presidency, parliament, and courts since 2018, despite widespread rejection of dictatorship. She emphasized that while reforms like the Anti-Corruption Commission and Access to Information law are positive steps, incomplete processes such as constitutional reform and gaps in judicial confidence require urgent attention.

Hannah Forster, Executive Director of the African Centre for Democracy and Human Rights Studies, stressed that democratic consolidation involves strengthening institutions and fostering a culture of responsible power, measured by the protection of human dignity and rights. She called for greater inclusion of women, youth, and persons with disabilities in political processes, noting pending legislation for reserved seats in the National Assembly.

Why it mattersGambians are affected because declining trust in institutions like the presidency, parliament, and courts undermines their confidence in the democratic process and the effectiveness of reforms.

Ceesay takes over Gambia Football Federation leadership

Kemo Ceesay formally assumed duties as the new president of the Gambia Football Federation, succeeding Lamin Kaba Bajo after his 12-year tenure.

Kemo Ceesay officially began his term as the new president of the Gambia Football Federation (GFF) yesterday at a ceremony at Football House. He took over from Lamin Kaba Bajo, who retired after 12 years. The GFF Secretariat pledged its continued support to the new leadership for the ongoing development of football in the country.

Outgoing president Lamin Kaba Bajo thanked stakeholders for their support and congratulated Ceesay. Bajo highlighted governance reforms, including term limits, and commended the electoral committee for a fair election process. He urged public and stakeholder support for the new administration, stating their success would benefit Gambian football.

In his acceptance speech, President Ceesay reiterated his commitment to advancing football in The Gambia. He presented a certificate of merit to Bajo, a gesture also made by the GFF Secretariat. The handover concluded with the signing of documents, marking the beginning of a new era for football governance, followed by a tour of the GFF Secretariat.

Why it mattersThe election of a new president for the Gambia Football Federation signals a change in leadership and a renewed focus on football development within the country.

Gambia Expands Mobile Numbers to One Billion

The country is migrating to a 9-digit mobile number system to accommodate growth and multiple phone usage.

Gambia is transitioning to a 9-digit mobile numbering system, increasing its total capacity from 10 million to one billion numbers. This expansion, supported by the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA) and government ministries, addresses the inadequacy of current numbers for a population of 2.8 million, where many individuals use multiple devices. Africell customers will see an '87' prefix added to their existing numbers.

The initial phase involves a parallel-run system, allowing both old and new numbers to function simultaneously until the public is accustomed to the change. This approach aims to prevent immediate disruption, with the parallel system remaining in place indefinitely. The move is driven by significant growth in telecommunications over the past decade, with mobile phones now central to communication and digital financial services, necessitating number recycling.

While the immediate impact on customers is expected to be minimal, users may encounter changes when setting up applications like WhatsApp, as verification codes will eventually be directed to new numbers. Existing WhatsApp accounts will continue to work during the parallel phase unless migrated to a new device. International calls might experience temporary disruptions as external partners adjust their systems to recognize the new format. Africell is introducing a Contact Updater feature to assist customers with the transition.

Why it mattersThe migration to a 9-digit mobile number system affects all Gambians by changing how they make and receive calls and digital communications.
