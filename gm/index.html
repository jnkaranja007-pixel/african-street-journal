Gambian journalists fault electoral commission over candidate asset scrutiny

Nine presidential candidates have been cleared for the 5 December vote, but the electoral commission has not opened their asset declarations to public review.

Scores of Gambian journalists, including former Information Minister Demba Ali Jawo, have condemned the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) for failing to require presidential candidates to declare their assets and allow public scrutiny before the 5 December election. Journalists say the omission breaches Section 51 of the Gambian Constitution, which they argue requires the commission to oversee asset declarations and public scrutiny of candidates' documents.

Jawo called the omission a failure that does not augur well for transparency. Omar Bah, editor of The Standard newspaper, said that if the commission collects declarations but refuses scrutiny, the exercise is merely a box ticked in secret. Journalists have urged the IEC to reverse course immediately and carry out the declarations and public scrutiny, warning that proceeding without them would violate the law.

The IEC approved nine candidates, eight men and one woman, on 15 November. Dr Touray becomes the first Gambian woman to be cleared as a presidential candidate.

Austrian investor disputes Gambian partners over Bitcoin Towers

DI Manuel Stofleth-Mitterer claims financial impropriety and intimidation in projects including Bitcoin Towers, Kasumai Real Estate, MASThave Real Estate, and Tujereng Housing.

An Austrian investor, DI Manuel Stofleth-Mitterer, has appealed to the Gambian government for intervention in a dispute with local business partners over real estate and cryptocurrency ventures. Stofleth-Mitterer, who relocated to The Gambia in 2021 with his wife to invest, alleges financial impropriety, broken agreements, and intimidation tactics aimed at forcing him out of projects including Bitcoin Towers, Kasumai Real Estate Company, MASThave Real Estate Company, and the Tujereng Housing Project.

The dispute reportedly began in 2024 when Stofleth-Mitterer extended a €165,000 loan to Ebrima Solomon Tamba, proprietor of Kasumai Real Estate Company, to settle a bank obligation. Stofleth-Mitterer claims Tamba's failure to repay the loan, as per their agreement, should result in the forfeiture of Tamba's shares in their joint companies. He also alleges that Tamba, with Sang Sylva, overcharged him for official fees related to the Edge/Bitcoin Tower property.

Stofleth-Mitterer and his wife, both architects, initially chose The Gambia over other countries for investment after researching potential locations. Their vision for the country included developing properties, attracting international investors, creating jobs, and opening The Gambia to broader markets. Stofleth-Mitterer described the Bitcoin Towers project as a "piece of freedom" and a "healing home," seeking entrepreneurs with open mindsets.

Barrow launches 7.7km road widening project in Greater Banjul

President Adama Barrow laid the foundation stone for the project and inaugurated the newly completed Sukuta-Jabang Road on Sunday.

President Adama Barrow on Sunday laid the foundation stone for a 7.7-kilometre road-widening project running from Kairaba Avenue through Atlantic Road to Old Cape Road in the Greater Banjul Area. He also inaugurated the newly completed Sukuta-Jabang Road.

Barrow said rapid urban growth and a sharp rise in the number of vehicles have put heavy pressure on the road network, causing persistent congestion, longer travel times and higher vehicle maintenance costs. Commuters, traders, students and workers who use the corridor daily are among those most affected. The project will add expanded lanes, improved drainage, street lighting, organised junctions, pedestrian walkways, cycling lanes and green spaces.

He directed the Ministry of Transport, Works and Infrastructure, the National Roads Authority and municipal authorities to supervise the work, and urged contractors to meet technical specifications and finish within the agreed timeframe. He called for local engineers, artisans and labourers to be involved, and asked utility providers to limit disruptions to water and electricity. Motorists and residents were asked to be patient during traffic diversions and dust.

Why it mattersCommuters, traders, students and workers on the Kairaba Avenue to Old Cape Road corridor face longer travel times and higher vehicle costs, and the widening is the remedy the government has committed to.

Gambia rural youth demand voice beyond ballot

Young Gambians in farming communities want their concerns addressed by leaders, not just during elections.

Young people in rural Gambia are increasingly vocal about their desire to influence political decisions that affect their daily lives, including employment, education, and access to services. Edrisa Nyass, a rural youth, stated that participating in the upcoming presidential election is an opportunity to choose leaders who understand the challenges faced by ordinary people, especially in rural areas. He called for honesty, accountability, and transparency from leaders, emphasizing that young people should be involved in present decision-making, not just be referred to as 'the future'.

While many rural youths engage in politics through discussions, community meetings, and campaign activities, some remain disconnected due to economic hardship, limited information, and distrust in political leaders. Amie Keita, North Bank Regional Youth Vice Chairperson, highlighted that young women face additional barriers such as cultural norms, financial constraints, and gender-based discrimination, with politics often perceived as a male-dominated space. For some, economic hardship makes political participation feel like a luxury.

The upcoming presidential election is seen by rural youth as a critical moment to exercise their democratic rights and seek meaningful change. Their participation extends beyond casting votes, encompassing active engagement in political discourse and community organizing. However, the effectiveness of their voices in shaping policy and community development beyond election day remains a significant concern for many.

Why it mattersRural youth in Gambia are seeking to influence leadership decisions on issues like employment, education, and services, with the upcoming election being a key opportunity for their voices to be heard.

Darboe's UDP gains three alliance partners before December 5 vote

The Gambia First Coalition, led by the United Democratic Party, was sealed at a Manjai ceremony attended by senior members and journalists.

On Sunday, November 15, 2026, the UDP-led Gambia First Coalition signed its partnership and won the backing of three political leaders, ahead of The Gambia's presidential election on December 5.

At the UDP bureau in Manjai, party leader and presidential candidate Ousainu Darboe welcomed Momodou Bah, leader of the AFRO Movement and a former presidential aspirant, Dr. Dumbuya of the Democratic Party, and Musa Jallow of the GRDP. Darboe said the alliance gathered people with the experience and commitment needed to govern, and that the signing reflected public confidence in its plans for the country's problems.

Darboe also said at least 3,000 young people declared on Saturday that they would vote for the UDP, with many posting their voter cards on Babucarr Bob Keita's social media platform. He said he first met Bah in 2021 at the launch of Bah's book on Senegal and The Gambia relations, and that he had spoken with Dr. Dumbuya in the weeks before the ceremony.

Why it mattersThe three partners add their supporters to the UDP ticket in the run-up to the December 5 vote.
