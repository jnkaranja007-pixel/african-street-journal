Gambia drug agency arrests 51 in nationwide sweep

Suspected cannabis, cocaine, ecstasy, and crystal meth seized in operations across five regions.

The Drug Law Enforcement Agency, The Gambia (DLEAG) arrested 51 suspected drug offenders nationwide in operations conducted between October 14 and October 28, 2026. The agency seized various prohibited and controlled drugs, including cannabis, kush, cocaine, crack cocaine, ecstasy, and crystal meth. The suspects include 48 Gambians and three Senegalese nationals, with ages ranging from 16 to 42.

DLEAG Public Relations Officer Dawda Sanyang stated that 30 suspects were apprehended in the West Coast Region, 12 in the Kanifing Municipality, five in the North Bank Region, three in Banjul, and one in the Central River Region. Among those arrested were Alagie Njie and Yasaye Ndure, found with suspected skunk concealed in a breadcrumb tin, and Kebba Mbye, found with suspected crack cocaine. All 51 suspects are in custody as investigations continue.

The operations aim to combat drug dealing and trafficking, safeguard public health, and promote a drug-free Gambia. DLEAG warned the public against obstructing officers and urged cooperation with law enforcement. Seizures included 51 parcels of suspected cannabis sativa and 81 pieces of suspected kush from one suspect, and 93 wraps of suspected cannabis sativa and 85 pieces of suspected kush from two others.

Why it matters51 individuals are in custody facing drug-related charges, impacting their freedom and futures.

Russia and Africa agree on equal partnership

The third Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow focuses on practical projects in energy and infrastructure.

Russia and African nations are pursuing a partnership based on concrete projects and national priorities, moving beyond empty promises. This approach aligns with the African Union’s Agenda 2063 and the principle of "African solutions to African problems." Russia aims to implement joint projects in sectors like education, science, culture, and healthcare, delivering tangible benefits.

The Russia-Africa Summits serve as the main platform for this cooperation, with the first held in Sochi in 2019 and the second in St. Petersburg in 2023. The second summit saw delegations from 48 countries, including 27 heads of state or high-ranking officials. The current third summit in Moscow features around 40 sessions focused on practical results.

For The Gambia and other African nations, this partnership is structured around identified priorities such as energy and food security. The collaboration includes technology transfers, professional training, and joint infrastructure and industrial projects, continuing a historical support for liberation and development.

Why it mattersThe Gambia and other African nations can benefit from Russia's focus on joint projects in critical development areas like energy and infrastructure.

Gambia entrepreneurs join green economy fellowship

Acumen's six-month program trains leaders in climate and green business with grants up to $50,000.

The Green Rise Africa Fellowship, a program by Acumen and Masafa Foundation, is empowering Gambian entrepreneurs in the climate and green economy. Representatives from Acumen spoke on Thursday at African Princess Hotel, emphasizing the program's focus on moral leadership and change-making. The fellowship is a six-month hybrid program combining virtual and in-person sessions across West Africa. Acumen's mission is to invest in entrepreneurs building inclusive societies.

The curriculum focuses on self-reflection, values-driven leadership, and business resilience, with workshops on Authentic Voice and Adaptive Leadership. Oluchi Okoroafor, Acumen’s fellowship senior associate, stated the program goes beyond technical training, asking leaders to examine their values. Graduates gain lifelong membership in Acumen’s global network of over 2,000 fellows, with access to peer collaboration and funding opportunities. The Angels Green Rise Africa program offers grants of $25,000 or $50,000.

Local entrepreneurs like Beran Dondeh Gillen of The Hub Ltd described the program as transformational, validating his commitment to business integrity. Mariam Sarge of Electric Energy Solution learned to delegate and amplify her business story, exploring new opportunities. Acumen aims to nurture leaders tackling poverty and climate challenges with integrity as the fellowship expands across 15 countries.

Why it mattersGambian entrepreneurs are gaining access to training, a global network, and potential funding to grow their businesses in the green economy.

Gambia awaits AfDB grant for second green ferry

Construction of a new ferry is stalled pending a decision on funding from the African Development Bank.

The construction of Gambia's second new Green Ferry has not yet started as the government awaits a decision from the African Development Bank (AfDB) on a grant application.

During a National Assembly session, a parliamentarian inquired about the ferry's arrival, prompting the government to state it was expected 16 months after construction begins, which is contingent on the AfDB's funding approval.

The government expects to proceed with the project's next stage once the AfDB makes its decision on the grant application, which will impact the ferry's delivery timeline.

Why it mattersThe delivery of a second green ferry, intended to improve transport for people and goods, is delayed due to pending grant approval from the African Development Bank.

Barrow blames UDP for stalled constitution

President Adama Barrow stated opposition lawmakers, particularly the UDP, voted against the draft bill in the National Assembly.

President Adama Barrow has blamed the United Democratic Party (UDP) for the failure to pass a new constitution, stating opposition lawmakers voted against the draft bill in the National Assembly. Barrow said his administration initiated the reform process but lacked the required parliamentary support to pass the legislation. An earlier attempt failed at the National Assembly stage, prompting his party to seek a stronger mandate in the 2021 elections.

The ruling National People’s Party (NPP) secured a simple majority after the 2021 election, but its approximately 35 supporting votes were insufficient to meet the threshold of over 40 votes needed. He questioned continued criticism over stalled reforms when legislation requires support from across the political divide.

The 1997 Constitution remains in force after the 2020 draft constitution failed to secure the necessary support. Barrow stated he could not guarantee passage of a new constitution without his party securing a legislative majority in the next election. If granted the numbers, his party would prepare a new draft and campaign for it, with the legislative process determining its progression.

Why it mattersGambia's citizens are affected by the continued use of the 1997 Constitution due to the failure to pass a new one.
