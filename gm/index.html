Gambia National Kills Two, Dies After Tucson Bar Shooting

Police are investigating the Monday morning shooting at Venture-N Bar as a hate crime after the shooter, a Gambian national, died from injuries.

A Gambian national identified as 44-year-old Ousman Ceesay died after a shooting that killed two other men outside the Venture-N Bar in Tucson early Monday morning. Police reported that Ceesay confronted victims Vincent Anthony Siqueiros, 42, and Cameron Davis Capara, 33, at approximately 12:30 a.m. and allegedly shot them both in the parking lot. Both victims were pronounced dead at the scene.

Ceesay, the alleged shooter, sustained life-threatening injuries and was transported to a hospital where he was pronounced dead shortly after. The Tucson Police Department stated that Ceesay had a note listing several LGBTQIA+ businesses. Chief of Police Monica Prieto called the incident a "senseless loss of life" and a "bias-based crime," emphasizing that such acts have no place in the community. Investigators are examining evidence to determine the full circumstances.

Law enforcement officials believe the shooting was an isolated incident with all known suspects deceased. Pima County Attorney Laura Conover expressed solidarity with the LGBTQ+ community, stating that no one should fear being targeted for their identity or gathering places. The Tucson Police Department has increased police presence around LGBTQIA+ establishments and is coordinating with community organizations to offer support.

Why it mattersThe shooting affects the LGBTQIA+ community by creating fear and pain, leading to increased police presence around their establishments.

Gambia Youth Unemployment Understated, Policy Needs Fuller Picture

More than four in ten Gambians are between 15 and 35, yet official youth unemployment figures fail to capture underemployment and discouraged workers.

The Gambia faces a demographic turning point, with over four in ten citizens aged 15 to 35, and the majority of the population young enough to contribute significantly to the economy. While official youth unemployment stands at 8.3 percent, this figure is considered an understatement. A more comprehensive measure, including underemployment and those who have stopped seeking work, reveals a greater extent of youth labour underutilization, suggesting substantial untapped potential.

The 2025 Human Development Report highlights foundational gains, including near-universal basic education enrolment and increased life expectancy from 56.9 to 62.9 years. However, translating educational access into achievement remains a challenge, with only 26.4 percent of 15-to-30-year-olds completing their educational level. Secondary enrolment reached approximately 50 percent in 2022, falling short of the 70 percent national target, with rural areas and young women experiencing significantly lower completion rates.

Economic momentum is evident, with tourism receipts nearly doubling between 2022 and 2023. Agriculture continues to be a primary employment source, and sectors such as agro-processing and ICT-enabled services are identified as key growth areas for creating durable jobs. The focus is shifting towards the quality of jobs, aiming to foster small enterprises and provide secure livelihoods, moving beyond short-term project-based schemes to sustainable employment opportunities.

Why it mattersYoung Gambians are affected by underemployment and a lack of quality job opportunities that fail to absorb their ambition and skills into the economy.

Assembly Considers Bill to Regulate Gambia's Markets

A proposed law aims to establish the country's first comprehensive framework for managing and operating all markets, addressing years of disorder.

The Gambian National Assembly is set to consider a new bill that seeks to regulate the nation's markets, which have long suffered from poor sanitation and unclear management structures. National Assembly Member Hon Musa Cham introduced the bill, which has already passed its First Reading. It aims to create a clear legal framework for traders, vendors, and local authorities, addressing the current absence of specific market regulations.

Cham stated that the bill's objective extends beyond mere regulation to the transformation of trading environments into safer, cleaner, and better-organized spaces. The proposed legislation intends to standardize the management of both public and private markets nationwide, define the rights and responsibilities of traders and consumers, and clarify the roles of various governing bodies. This initiative seeks to ensure markets operate under clear and enforceable national standards.

The bill's progression through the National Assembly, from Committee Stage to Third Reading and finally to Presidential Assent, is crucial for its enactment. Cham emphasized the vital role of markets as the backbone of livelihoods for thousands of Gambians, asserting that legislation must reflect these realities and provide a proper legal framework for an important part of the national economy.

Why it mattersTraders and consumers will be affected by new rules regarding market operations, safety, and sanitation.

Football Administrator Bajo Honors Colleague Jammeh

Former Gambia Football Federation president Lamin Kaba Bajo lauded Bakary Jammeh for loyalty and support during their 12-year tenure.

Former Gambia Football Federation (GFF) president Lamin Kaba Bajo has publicly honored his long-time colleague and first vice president, Bakary Jammeh. The tribute comes as both officials concluded 12 years in office last weekend, adhering to a term limit they themselves established. Bajo described his relationship with Jammeh as dating back to 1978 when they first met as students at Muslim High School.

Bajo's tribute highlighted Jammeh's career progression, noting his early roles in finance at MRC and later at the Social Security and Housing Finance Corporation (SSHFC), where he pursued an MBA. Jammeh also co-founded ELTON Oil Marketing Company. Beyond his corporate roles, Jammeh was a significant figure in Gambian football, notably serving as Secretary General for Real de Banjul, a position Bajo credited with contributing to the club's success.

The former GFF president emphasized Jammeh's unwavering support and loyalty during his own leadership, stating that Jammeh consistently stood by him, taking on challenges and ensuring his confidence. Bajo expressed gratitude for Jammeh's dedication, acknowledging the sacrifices made and the pain endured for their shared endeavors in football administration.
