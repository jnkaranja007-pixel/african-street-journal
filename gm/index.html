Forum convenes stakeholders for peaceful Gambian election

National security adviser assures commitment to a violence-free December 5 presidential election amid democratic progress.

Key stakeholders gathered for a three-day forum to analyze concerns about democracy and the upcoming presidential election in The Gambia. The event, funded by the European Union through the E-MAM Project implemented by WANEP, aims to ensure a peaceful and violence-free election on December 5.

National Security Adviser Abubacarr Sulayman Jeng stated that security agencies are prepared to collaborate with all stakeholders to safeguard the electoral process and ensure the outcome reflects the people's will. He emphasized the nation's progress in restoring democratic norms since 2016, calling it hard-won and a shared responsibility to protect.

The forum highlighted the importance of identifying potential threats and strengthening preventive measures, drawing on regional experiences to mitigate electoral risks. The European Union, a supporter of the initiative, stressed that peaceful elections require collective responsibility, trust, and communication, providing a space for dialogue and practical solutions.

Why it mattersThe forum aims to ensure a peaceful and violence-free presidential election on December 5, affecting all Gambian citizens who will cast their votes.

FAO, Agric Ministry Boost Youth and Women in Agribusiness

New initiatives aim to build capacity for responsible agribusinesses, improve livelihoods, and expand value addition and market opportunities for Gambian youth and women.

The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and The Gambia's Ministry of Agriculture have launched new projects to enhance the involvement of young people and women in agribusiness. These initiatives are designed to build capacity for establishing responsible and inclusive agribusinesses, ultimately improving livelihoods and expanding opportunities in value addition, financing, and market access. The program builds on previous work, having already provided investment-readiness coaching to over 70 young entrepreneurs.

The Agri-Accelerator 2.0, with a budget of US$255,000, will run from August 2026 to September 2028, aiming to strengthen the skills of 200 young women and men in agribusiness financing, responsible investment, marketing, and sustainable practices. Additionally, it will support 100 youth-led agribusinesses in applying Responsible Agricultural Investment principles. A specific tropical fruit project, running through 2027, will focus on processing, packaging, and marketing to create more local value addition.

Officials highlighted the need for investment beyond training to address critical issues like storage and processing, which currently lead to significant losses for farmers when produce floods the market. The projects aim to identify barriers for women and youth, provide technical assistance, improve market access, and promote dialogue among stakeholders to foster sustainable and inclusive food systems and economic independence.

Why it mattersYoung women and men in The Gambia will gain skills and support to establish and grow agribusinesses, improving their livelihoods and economic independence.

Businessman paid D600,000 for failed Hajj trip

A witness told the Banjul Magistrates’ Court that his stepfather collapsed after learning his planned Hajj trip had failed.

A businessman testified in court that he paid D600,000 for his stepfather's Hajj package, which ultimately failed. Mohammed Drammeh stated the payment was made at Kanimang Bajo's office in Banjul after being introduced by a trusted shop owner. The package included flights, accommodation, and a ram for Tobaski, along with a Guinea-Bissau passport arranged by Bajo.

Drammeh explained that a WhatsApp group was created for pilgrims, but concerns arose when Hajj visas were not issued. Bajo initially advised patience but later informed the group that visas were unavailable. Pilgrims were then offered the choice to defer their trips or seek refunds.

The witness recounted that his stepfather, who was excited about the trip, collapsed due to high blood pressure upon hearing the news and was hospitalized. Drammeh stated he has not received his D600,000 refund and that some community members blamed him for the situation. The prosecution has amended charges against Bajo and Ejabah Travel and Tours, raising the alleged amount to D35,941,640.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the case involves allegations of fraud totaling over D35 million related to travel arrangements, impacting business trust and financial dealings.

Government Reverses Health Fee Hike After Public Outcry

Charges at health facilities are reverted to previous rates following widespread objections and presidential engagement with citizens.

The Gambian government has reversed its decision to increase health facility fees, restoring them to their former levels after significant public backlash. Minister of Information and Broadcasting, Dr. Ismaila Ceesay, announced the change, stating it reflects the government's commitment to affordable and universal healthcare access for all citizens, regardless of their location within the country.

Dr. Ceesay confirmed the reversal during a media appearance, noting that the issue had become a recurring concern for the public. He linked the decision directly to feedback gathered during the President's nationwide tours, emphasizing that the President listened to Gambians' concerns and felt their pain. Antenatal care and healthcare for children under five will continue to be free under the National Health Insurance Scheme.

This reversal follows previous government attempts to justify the increment, but the charges have now been restored to pre-increase levels. The move is presented as a direct response to citizen concerns raised during the President's ongoing engagements, demonstrating the administration's responsiveness to public sentiment and its dedication to ensuring quality healthcare remains accessible to everyone.

Why it mattersCitizens are directly affected by the reversal of health facility fees, which ensures continued access to affordable healthcare services.

Trade Ministry Assesses Local Industries for Support

Government officials toured businesses to understand operations before offering incentives and financial aid.

The Ministry of Trade, Industry, Regional Integration and Employment, along with GIEPA, conducted an industrial tour to assess local businesses. The tour included stops at Applied Research, Innovation and Manufacturing in Yundum, Niro Company Limited near the airport, Pasma Company Limited in Jambanjelly, and Muhammed’s Poultry Farm in Gunjur. Minister Mod K. Ceesay stated the visits were crucial for the government to gain firsthand knowledge of business operations before providing support, emphasizing a move away from solely office-based document processing.

Minister Ceesay explained that the ministry has received applications for support, including special incentives, from various businesses. The tour aimed to verify project progress, owner investment, and the seriousness of these enterprises. He indicated that government support, which includes financial and technical assistance, would be processed only after satisfactory assessments, as the resources belong to the Gambian people. Measures are also being implemented to favor locally produced goods over imports.

During the tour, Applied Research, Innovation and Manufacturing’s CEO Wally Saine highlighted the company's need for financial aid, equipment, affordable raw materials, and facility expansion support. Pasma Company Limited’s CEO Pa Saikou Manneh detailed his company's construction of a temperature-controlled warehouse for storing dry commodities, onions, vegetables, and poultry products. These assessments are part of an effort to create opportunities and transform the economy through local industry.

Why it mattersGambian-owned businesses are affected by the government's assessment process for potential financial and technical support to foster growth.
