Gambia targets 3.21% GDP revenue gain by 2030

The nation aims to boost its tax-to-GDP ratio through reforms outlined at the African Tax Administration Forum in Cairo.

The Gambia aims to increase its domestic revenue by 3.21 percentage points of GDP by 2030 under its first Domestic Resource Mobilization Strategy (DRMS) for 2026–2030. Finance Minister Seedy K.M. Keita announced this goal on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, at the African Tax Administration Forum in Cairo, Egypt. He noted that The Gambia’s tax-to-GDP ratio rose from 9.8 per cent in 2020 to about 13 per cent by the end of 2025, but still lags the sub-Saharan African average.

Minister Keita stated that the Gambia Revenue Authority (GRA) collected over D3 billion in March 2026, a monthly record for the institution. The DRMS integrates tax policy, revenue administration, and non-tax measures to approach the sub-Saharan African average tax effort by 2030. Key reforms include investing in digital tax administration, improving tax policy, strengthening public trust, and ensuring national ownership of revenue mobilization.

The GRA is implementing an Integrated Tax Administration System, rolling out electronic VAT invoicing, and introducing digital compliance tools for rental income and the telecommunications sector. The ministry and GRA are also reviewing the Income Tax and Value Added Tax Acts to enhance taxation of digital services and transfer pricing. A standalone Tax Administration Act is also under development.

Why it mattersThe Gambia's new strategy aims to increase domestic revenue by 3.21 percentage points of GDP by 2030, affecting national development priorities.

EU deploys 16 observers for Gambia election

The European Union Election Observation Mission will monitor the December 5, 2026 presidential polls.

16 long-term observers from the European Union have been deployed across The Gambia to monitor the December 5, 2026 presidential election. The European Union Election Observation Mission (EU EOM) announced the deployment on Thursday, November 5, from its headquarters in Banjul. Chief Observer Reinhold Lopatka stated the mission aims to contribute to an inclusive, credible, and transparent electoral process.

The observers, from 15 EU member states and Switzerland, will visit different regions to assess the electoral environment. They will engage with election officials, candidates, political parties, civil society, and media. The mission’s core team arrived on October 21 to evaluate the political and legal framework. Lopatka noted that the EU does not certify election results, which remains the responsibility of The Gambia's Independent Electoral Commission.

A further 20 short-term observers and a seven-member European Parliament delegation will join before election day. The EU mission will stay until the process is complete, with Lopatka set to present preliminary findings two days after the vote. The EU EOM was invited by Gambian authorities to observe the polls.

Why it mattersGambian citizens will have international observers monitoring their presidential election on December 5, 2026.

Gambia's honorary consul receives Pushkin Medal

Momodou Lamin Manga awarded Russia's Pushkin Medal for promoting cultural ties and heritage.

Momodou Lamin Manga, the Honorary Consul of the Russian Federation in The Gambia, received the Pushkin Medal from Russian President Vladimir Putin. The award recognizes Manga's contributions to cultural promotion and the preservation of historical heritage.

Manga, a Gambian citizen, was honored at a Kremlin ceremony for his role in promoting Russian culture abroad and fostering closer relations between nationalities. He expressed gratitude to the Russian Government and the Gambian administrations for their support.

The Pushkin Medal is given for contributions to culture, arts, education, and humanities. Manga reaffirmed his commitment to strengthening diplomatic friendship and cultural cooperation between Russia and The Gambia.

Why it mattersGambian citizen Momodou Lamin Manga was awarded a Russian state honour for his work in promoting cultural relations.

Gambia election aspirants face nomination hurdles

Two presidential hopefuls encountered issues with expired IDs and dual citizenship requirements.

Presidential aspirants Alieu Sowe and Bakusa Dukureh faced eligibility questions as they submitted nomination papers for the 2026 election. Sowe, an independent candidate, presented his birth certificate and voter’s card but had an expired national identity card and outstanding financial and administrative requirements, including a D1 million deposit. Dukureh, who holds dual Gambian and American citizenship, submitted his papers after paying the deposit but was asked about renouncing his US nationality.

The Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) identified several missing items for Sowe, such as an income tax clearance certificate and campaign itinerary, and requested corrections to documents identifying him as a retired Major and listing a sponsoring organisation. Sowe stated he had not renewed his ID due to difficulties obtaining cards and affirmed he was Gambian by birth. Dukureh, 48, has lived in the United States for 26 years and also needed to submit an income tax clearance certificate and sign the Code of Election Campaign Ethics.

National Builders Party leader Ansu Sanneh also submitted his nomination papers seven weeks after his party's registration. Sanneh's agenda includes building a new city and establishing a regional airport. He stated his party has a clear development blueprint to build the country.

Why it mattersPresidential aspirants in Gambia are facing scrutiny over their eligibility and required documentation for the upcoming 2026 election.

Gambia councillors reject bid to remove Mayor Bensouda

15 councillors voted against the government's recommendation to remove the mayor of Kanifing Municipal Council.

Councillors at the Kanifing Municipal Council (KMC) voted on Thursday to reject the government's recommendation to remove Mayor Talib Ahmed Bensouda from office. During a council meeting, 15 councillors voted against the removal, two abstained, and one voted in favour. This decision follows deliberations on the government's proposed action against the mayor.

Mayor Bensouda stated that the dispute originated from allegations against a former KMC Chief Executive Officer in 2020 and 2021. He claimed a joint investigation by the Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Local Government vindicated the council's concerns, but alleged the government proceeded with a Presidential Commission of Inquiry, which he described as political retaliation.

The mayor questioned the timing and conclusions of the White Paper, noting the commission finished its work months before the report was released. He argued the commission identified broader administrative challenges, not personal liability, and renewed calls for decentralisation reforms to grant local governments more authority. Bensouda stressed KMC's autonomy as an elected institution.

Why it mattersThe elected representatives of the Kanifing Municipal Council have shown strong backing for Mayor Talib Ahmed Bensouda, rejecting a government recommendation for his removal.
