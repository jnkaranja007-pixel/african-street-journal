Gambia reports trade gains but potential untapped

The Gambia and LDC Group saw export earnings rise, but primary products still dominate.

The global trading system has delivered measurable gains for The Gambia, including market access, export earnings, economic activity, and jobs, according to Ambassador Muhammadou M.O. Kah. Speaking in Geneva on October 29, 2026, on behalf of Trade Minister Mod Ceesay, Kah stated that trade is a foreign-exchange lifeline for The Gambia, with merchandise exports reaching USD 407 million and commercial services exports USD 479 million in 2025.

Tourism and travel alone accounted for 91.6 per cent of The Gambia's services exports in 2024, highlighting the need for diversification and value addition. Ambassador Kah also noted that regional integration, through the OMVG interconnection, has provided The Gambia with lower-cost electricity from Guinea, saving its utility, NAWEC, an estimated 42 per cent and helping it return to profitability. For the wider LDC Group, merchandise exports rose to USD 313 billion in 2025, and commercial services exports reached USD 53.1 billion.

Despite these gains, primary products still accounted for 53 per cent of LDC merchandise exports in 2025, with 10 exporters generating 71 per cent of the total. Ambassador Kah stressed that market access alone is insufficient, and productive capacity, infrastructure, standards, and competitiveness are crucial. He outlined priorities for The Gambia and other LDCs, including value addition in agro-processing and fisheries, climate-resilient sectors, and green and digital industries.

Why it mattersThe Gambia's economy and job creation are directly impacted by its trade performance and efforts to diversify beyond primary products.

Dumbuya promises D2,000 rent if elected

Democratic Party leader Yusupha A. Dumbuya faced questions from the IEC over missing nomination documents.

Democratic Party leader Yusupha A. Dumbuya pledged to cap rent for a two-bedroom house at D2,000 if elected president. He also proposed D4,000 for a three-bedroom house, with government subsidies to support the plan. Dumbuya presented his nomination papers to the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) on Wednesday.

IEC Chairman Joseph Colley raised concerns about Dumbuya’s nomination, noting the absence of his voter’s card and Senior Secondary School Certificate. The commission also questioned the proof of payment for the D1 million nomination fee and inconsistencies in his name. Dumbuya’s nomination is pending verification of these requirements.

Dumbuya also criticized the government for failing to deliver a new constitution and promised to implement constitutional term limits. Former Vice President Dr. Isatou Touray also filed her nomination as an independent candidate for the December 5 presidential election.

Why it mattersGambia's presidential election candidates are making promises on housing costs and constitutional reform, affecting voters' future living expenses and governance.

Team Gambia Coalition names Lamin J. Darboe flagbearer

Dr. Lawyer Lamin J. Darboe will present his nomination papers to the IEC on November 11, 2026.

The Team Gambia Grand Coalition officially named Dr. Lawyer Lamin J. Darboe as its presidential candidate for the December 5, 2026 elections on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. The Coalition Executive Committee concluded its scrutiny of the election process, deeming the results unimpeachable.

The flagbearer selection process began on October 30, 2026, with aspirants attempting to reach a unanimous decision, which proved unsuccessful. The Good Governance Committee then conducted a vote in the presence of aspirants and their representatives, submitting its report on November 2, 2026.

Dr. Darboe is scheduled to submit his nomination documents to the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, between 10 and 11 am. The Coalition encourages supporters to attend this event to show unity and support for their chosen candidate.

Why it mattersGambia's presidential election on December 5, 2026, will feature Dr. Lawyer Lamin J. Darboe as the Team Gambia Grand Coalition's candidate.

SJI Academy opens with free enrollment for 50 students

The new school in Kartong offers Quranic and Arabic education, with plans for English-based learning and skills training.

Sadaka Jariyaa International (SJI) Academy opened last Saturday in Kartong, offering free enrollment to the first 50 students. The academy, located opposite the Military Camp, is admitting children aged five and above into its Quranic Memorization and Arabic Grade 1 to 12 programs.

School management plans to introduce Daycare, Nursery, and Lower and Upper Basic Education in the English system, alongside Skills Training in STEM, IT, Plumbing, Masonry, and Tiling. Proprietor Lamin Sillah stated plans are underway to develop the academy into a boarding school and teach English, French, and Portuguese.

Oustass Sheikh Joh, Principal of the Arabic section, urged parents to provide support for their children's confidence. Imam Abbass Manneh advised parents to ensure their children learn Islamic knowledge and thanked Lamin Sillah for the free education initiative for Kartong and surrounding villages.

Why it mattersChildren in Kartong and surrounding communities will benefit from free enrollment and a diverse curriculum at the new SJI Academy.

African universities strengthen child rights resolve

Gambia hosts meeting to boost research, policy, and collaboration for children's welfare across Africa.

African universities, government officials, and UN representatives convened in Banjul for the Second Meeting of the African Network of Universities for Child Rights. The gathering aimed to validate the network's governance, expand membership, and boost collaboration to advance children's rights across the continent.

University of The Gambia Vice Chancellor Professor Herbert Robinson called the meeting a historic milestone, highlighting the university's new Child Rights Centre, supported by UNICEF. He stressed that universities must lead research, policy influence, and evidence generation to improve children's lives, especially those in vulnerable communities.

The UN reaffirmed its commitment to the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child and the African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child. With less than four years to the 2030 Sustainable Development Goals, the meeting focused on generating locally owned, evidence-based policies and strengthening child rights education and advocacy.

Why it mattersChildren in Africa, particularly those in vulnerable and remote communities, will benefit from strengthened research, policy reforms, and educational initiatives focused on their rights.
