Gambia receives $2.5M forfeited Jammeh U.S. fund

The Reparations Commission confirmed receipt of the money from the sale of former President Yahya Jammeh’s Potomac property.

The Gambia Reparations Commission has received USD 2,573,414.10 from the forfeiture and sale of former President Yahya Jammeh’s property in the United States. The funds were deposited directly into the Reparations Fund account at the Central Bank of The Gambia. The commission stated these funds will be used exclusively for the payment of reparations to eligible victims.

This development is considered a significant step in implementing the commission's mandate under the Victims Reparations Act, 2023. The funds are intended to support swift and comprehensive reparations, which can include individual or collective restitution, compensation, and rehabilitation for victims and survivors.

The commission acknowledged the cooperation between the Gambian and United States governments in facilitating the transfer of these proceeds. It reaffirmed its commitment to transparency and responsible management of the funds, ensuring they are used solely for reparations and meet the needs of victims and survivors.

Why it mattersVictims and survivors of former President Yahya Jammeh's rule will receive reparations from these funds.

Gambia moves to ratify WIPO treaty protecting musicians

The National Assembly is considering a World Intellectual Property Organization treaty to safeguard artists' digital rights and earnings from piracy.

The Gambia is moving to strengthen protections for musicians and record producers against online piracy by ratifying the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) Performance and Phonograms Treaty. Attorney General Dawda A. Jallow presented the motion to the National Assembly, explaining that the treaty, adopted in 1996, aims to protect artists as music consumption shifts to digital platforms like streaming and social media.

Jallow told lawmakers that while Gambian musicians reach global audiences digitally, their work is vulnerable to unauthorized copying and sharing, leading to no financial return. He stated that piracy deprives the state of revenue and discourages investment in creative industries. Ratification would enable Gambian collecting societies to gather royalties from digital uses and update the Copyright Act of 2004.

Lawmakers supported the motion, with one member highlighting the increasing importance of protecting creative work due to artificial intelligence advancements. The treaty would grant performers and producers rights over recorded performances and online use, ensuring they are rewarded for their efforts and protecting their reputations from harmful alterations.

Why it mattersGambian musicians and record producers will benefit from stronger protections against online piracy, ensuring they receive financial returns for their digital work.

China Day celebrates Gambia-China ties with food and culture

The West Africa Food Festival featured a 'China Day' event to strengthen relations through culinary exchange and cultural performances.

Gambia's Tourism Minister Abdoulie Jobe described food as a bridge for strengthening relations between The Gambia and China during China Day at the 2026 West Africa Food Festival (WAFFEST). The event, held from October 15 to 21, gathered members of the Chinese community, the Chinese medical team, and chefs to celebrate Chinese cuisine and heritage. Jobe stated that culinary exchange serves as a tool for tourism, cultural understanding, and international cooperation, reinforcing longstanding ties and extending friendship to other participating nations.

China's Deputy Ambassador to The Gambia, Zhao Shangsen, praised the Chinese community for integrating with local communities and highlighted China Day as an opportunity for cultural exchange, including providing a meal for guests. The Chinese medical team also showcased traditional Chinese medicine and offered medicinal soup at a festival booth. Zhao thanked the organizers and the Tourism Ministry for their support, noting the community's contribution to strengthening cultural ties.

The celebration included cultural performances by Chinese community associations and showcased the skills of Chinese culinary professionals. China Day was part of WAFFEST 2026, which brought together participants from over 30 countries under the theme “Developing Skills, Changing Lives.” The festival aimed to celebrate culinary heritage, promote professional exchange, and explore tourism development and regional cooperation, adding an international dimension to the event.

Why it mattersThe event is for Gambians and visitors to experience Chinese culture and cuisine, fostering international understanding.

Gambia minister warns against treasonous march on State House

Information Minister Dr Ismaila Ceesay stated that marching towards the presidential residence could be interpreted as an attempt to commit treason.

Information Minister Dr Ismaila Ceesay has supported President Adama Barrow’s warning to a lawmaker against leading young people towards State House at night, describing the potential action as dangerous and reckless. Ceesay stated on a television program that such a march could be interpreted as an attempt to commit treason, a crime punishable by death in The Gambia. He emphasized that while citizens have the right to protest legally, approaching the highly secured presidential residence, especially with children reportedly as young as 10 to 15 years old, carries serious security implications.

The minister suggested that alternative public locations like McCarthy Square or the Arch would be more appropriate for expressing grievances. He clarified that the president's warning was directed at anyone considering similar actions, not just the specific lawmaker. Ceesay commended the security personnel at the Office of the President for their restraint during a related incident, noting that a different response could have escalated the situation.

Dr Ceesay asserted the government's responsibility to maintain law and order, cautioning citizens against actions that could jeopardize security around the presidential residence. He stressed that State House is the nation's most secure building and that organizing a march towards it under the described circumstances differs significantly from a lawful demonstration at a designated public space.

Why it mattersThe government's warning affects citizens who might consider protesting near the State House, as such actions could be interpreted as treason.

Coalition 2026 to launch manifesto in Banjul

The political group will unveil its vision and policy agenda for the nation on Thursday, October 22, 2026, at the Atlantic Hotel.

Coalition 2026, led by flagbearer Talib Ahmed Bensouda, will officially launch 'The People’s Manifesto' on Thursday, October 22, 2026, in Banjul. The event, scheduled from 9:00 a.m. to 12 p.m. at the Atlantic Hotel, signifies the coalition's commitment to outlining its future priorities and policy agenda for The Gambia.

Following the manifesto launch, a grand rally is planned at Arch 22. Supporters and the public are invited to attend this gathering, which aims to demonstrate unity and support for the coalition's vision for national progress. The coalition has extended an invitation to the media to cover both events.

Coalition 2026 seeks to engage directly with the public during these events to discuss its modernization plan for the country. The launch of 'The People’s Manifesto' is presented as a key step in this engagement process.

Why it mattersThe people of The Gambia are affected by the launch of a new political manifesto that outlines a vision and policy agenda for the country's future.
