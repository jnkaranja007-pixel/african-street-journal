Gambia university students protest campus conditions

Students at the University of The Gambia demanded urgent action on food, water, and transport despite government assurances.

Students at the University of The Gambia (UTG) protested on Monday at the Kanifing Campus over deteriorating conditions at the Faraba Campus. Demonstrators highlighted shortages of food, water, sanitation, and internet, alongside inadequate transportation, which they say disrupts academic life.

Protesters carried placards demanding better cafeteria services and clean facilities, stating that previous government assurances had not led to tangible improvements. Students' Union President Ismaila Fadera presented a petition to Higher Education Minister Professor Pierre Gomez, who visited the campus.

The government committed to restoring catering, improving water and internet access, and deploying 20 buses. However, students insisted that concrete action, not just pledges, was needed to address the critical situation affecting their ability to attend lectures and examinations.

Why it mattersStudents at the University of The Gambia are protesting poor living and learning conditions on campus.

National Builders Party unveils blueprint for Gambia

Party leader Ansu Sanneh presented proposals for security, agriculture, infrastructure, housing, and youth empowerment.

The National Builders Party (NBP) launched its national development plan on Saturday at Buffer Zone in Tallinding. Party leader Ansu Sanneh presented proposals for transforming security, agriculture, infrastructure, housing, and youth empowerment.

Sanneh pledged to modernize the police and security services with better training and equipment, promising farmers access to machinery, financing, and processing plants. The NBP also plans a new regional airport in Upper River Region and a new city near the River Gambia with integrated public services.

The blueprint includes affordable housing nationwide and support for women's businesses with financing and modern markets. The party aims to strengthen accountability and establish an independent anti-corruption commission before the December 5th election.

Why it mattersThe National Builders Party has presented a plan addressing security, economic opportunities, and public services for all Gambians.

Gambia hosts African geological assembly

Experts meet in Banjul to discuss managing continent's mineral resources for economic growth.

The Gambia is hosting the 18th General Assembly of the Organisation of African Geological Surveys (OAGS) for the first time. The three-day assembly, which opened on Monday, 26 October 2026, at the Sir Dawda Kairaba Jawara International Conference Centre, brings together geological experts, government officials, and international partners. The event aims to strengthen scientific cooperation and promote sustainable management of Africa’s mineral resources.

Organised by the Ministry of Petroleum, Energy and Mines, the gathering provides a platform for delegates from Africa and Europe to exchange knowledge and explore solutions to challenges facing geological surveys. Alieu Jawo, Director of the Geological Department, stated that the assembly is a vital opportunity to strengthen cooperation among geological institutions. Gabriel Data, OAGS President, highlighted the organisation’s role in advancing geological science and supporting Africa’s socio-economic development.

The assembly occurs as global demand for critical minerals surges, intensifying debate on how Africa can better assess, manage, and benefit from its resources. Minister of Petroleum, Energy and Mines, Hon. Nani Juwara, challenged African countries to end dependence on exporting raw minerals and importing finished products. For The Gambia, hosting the assembly is an opportunity to strengthen ties with regional and international partners while showcasing its geological potential.

Why it mattersAfrican nations are discussing how to better manage their mineral wealth to drive industrialization and job creation.

Business · 2026-10-28

Gambia government spent D827.59 million on fuel subsidies

The subsidies aimed to cushion consumers from rising international fuel prices following Middle East tensions.

The Gambia government spent D827.59 million on petrol and diesel subsidies between March and September 2026. Finance Minister Seedy Keita presented this figure in his 2027 budget speech to the National Assembly. The expenditure aimed to protect consumers from increasing global fuel costs.

Nearly 95 per cent of the total subsidy, D785.13 million, went to diesel, while D42.46 million covered petrol. Despite these subsidies, domestic retail prices for petrol rose by 33.1 per cent and diesel by 41.8 per cent from March to September 2026. The government stated these measures kept prices below what they would have been without intervention.

The government is targeting a tax-to-GDP ratio of 13.1 per cent in 2026, aiming for 13.5 per cent in 2027 through improved revenue collection. President Adama Barrow's administration also reported progress on several road projects inaugurated in the second and third quarters of 2026.

Why it mattersGambia's government spent over D827 million on fuel subsidies to protect citizens from rising international fuel prices.

Politics · 2026-10-28

Gambia moves to ratify domestic workers convention

Ministry workshop with unions and civil society charts roadmap for ILO Convention 189 ratification.

The Gambian government, through the Ministry of Trade, Regional Integration and Employment, held a two-day workshop in Bijilo to plan the ratification of the International Labour Organization (ILO) Convention 189 on Domestic Workers. The meeting, a collaboration with CREDESA, gathered government officials, trade unions, employers, and civil society to develop a national strategy for this ratification.

Fabba Jammeh, Director of Employment, stated that the workshop's attendance by workers, employers, and social partners demonstrated a shared commitment to progress. Deputy Permanent Secretary Mariama Fofana noted that 89.9% of domestic workers lack formal contracts and 86% have no medical insurance, leaving them vulnerable. She highlighted that the Labour Act 2023 now formally recognizes domestic work, and bilateral agreements exist with Saudi Arabia and the UAE for Gambian workers abroad.

The workshop aims to produce a national roadmap and the Banjul Declaration, outlining concrete steps for ratification. Dr. Fatima Diallo of CREDESA emphasized that harmonizing African legislation would create a legal framework for protecting domestic workers, particularly migrants, supporting African integration and rights beyond borders.

Why it mattersDomestic workers in Gambia, many of whom are migrants, will gain better legal protection and fairer working conditions.
