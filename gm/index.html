Gambia targets 3.21% GDP revenue gain by 2030

The nation aims to boost its tax-to-GDP ratio through reforms outlined at the African Tax Administration Forum in Cairo.

The Gambia aims to increase its domestic revenue by 3.21 percentage points of GDP by 2030 under its first Domestic Resource Mobilization Strategy (DRMS) for 2026–2030. Finance Minister Seedy K.M. Keita announced this goal on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, at the African Tax Administration Forum in Cairo, Egypt. He noted that The Gambia’s tax-to-GDP ratio rose from 9.8 per cent in 2020 to about 13 per cent by the end of 2025, but still lags the sub-Saharan African average.

Minister Keita stated that the Gambia Revenue Authority (GRA) collected over D3 billion in March 2026, a monthly record for the institution. The DRMS integrates tax policy, revenue administration, and non-tax measures to approach the sub-Saharan African average tax effort by 2030. Key reforms include investing in digital tax administration, improving tax policy, strengthening public trust, and ensuring national ownership of revenue mobilization.

The GRA is implementing an Integrated Tax Administration System, rolling out electronic VAT invoicing, and introducing digital compliance tools for rental income and the telecommunications sector. The ministry and GRA are also reviewing the Income Tax and Value Added Tax Acts to enhance taxation of digital services and transfer pricing. A standalone Tax Administration Act is also under development.

Why it mattersThe Gambia's new strategy aims to increase domestic revenue by 3.21 percentage points of GDP by 2030, affecting national development priorities.

EU deploys 16 observers for Gambia election

The European Union Election Observation Mission will monitor the December 5, 2026 presidential polls.

16 long-term observers from the European Union have been deployed across The Gambia to monitor the December 5, 2026 presidential election. The European Union Election Observation Mission (EU EOM) announced the deployment on Thursday, November 5, from its headquarters in Banjul. Chief Observer Reinhold Lopatka stated the mission aims to contribute to an inclusive, credible, and transparent electoral process.

The observers, from 15 EU member states and Switzerland, will visit different regions to assess the electoral environment. They will engage with election officials, candidates, political parties, civil society, and media. The mission’s core team arrived on October 21 to evaluate the political and legal framework. Lopatka noted that the EU does not certify election results, which remains the responsibility of The Gambia's Independent Electoral Commission.

A further 20 short-term observers and a seven-member European Parliament delegation will join before election day. The EU mission will stay until the process is complete, with Lopatka set to present preliminary findings two days after the vote. The EU EOM was invited by Gambian authorities to observe the polls.

Why it mattersGambian citizens will have international observers monitoring their presidential election on December 5, 2026.

Gambia opposition leader files for election despite abuse claims

Talib Ahmed Bensouda submitted his candidacy papers for the December 5 presidential election amid government accusations.

Gambia's opposition leader Talib Ahmed Bensouda filed his candidacy papers on Friday for the December 5 presidential election. Bensouda, leader of the UNITE-led coalition, submitted his papers despite a government report last week accusing him of financial improprieties and abuse of office during his eight years as mayor of Kanifing Municipality.

Bensouda's UNITE party dismissed the allegations as a political manoeuvre by the ruling National People's Party (NPP) government. Supporters celebrated Bensouda's move, highlighting his popularity gained through regular waste collection in Kanifing and his rising political profile amid prolonged electricity cuts affecting the nation.

President Adama Barrow is seeking a third term in the December 5 election. Leading opposition candidate Ousainou Darboe of the United Democratic Party also plans to run. If proven, the allegations against Bensouda could disqualify him from the race.

Gambia's honorary consul receives Russian Pushkin Medal

Momodou Lamin Manga awarded for promoting Russian culture and strengthening ties between nations.

Momodou Lamin Manga, the Honorary Consul of the Russian Federation in The Gambia, received the Pushkin Medal from Russian President Vladimir Putin. Manga, a Gambian citizen, was honoured for his work in promoting culture, preserving heritage, and building connections between countries during a Kremlin ceremony.

The Pushkin Medal recognises contributions to culture, arts, education, humanities, and fostering cultural ties. Manga was cited for promoting Russian culture abroad, preserving historical heritage, and bringing people of different nationalities closer. He expressed deep honour and thanked the Russian Government and Gambian administrations for their support.

Manga reaffirmed his commitment to strengthening diplomatic friendship, cultural relations, and cooperation between Russia and The Gambia. This recognition highlights longstanding bilateral cooperation in areas including diplomatic engagement, education, and culture, placing a Gambian national among foreign recipients of Russian state honours.

Why it mattersGambian citizen Momodou Lamin Manga received a Russian state honour for his work in promoting Russian culture and fostering international understanding.

Gambia election hopefuls face nomination questions

Two presidential aspirants, Alieu Sowe and Bakusa Dukureh, encountered issues with expired ID and dual citizenship.

Presidential hopefuls Alieu Sowe and Bakusa Dukureh faced eligibility questions when submitting nomination papers for the 2026 election. Independent candidate Sowe, a retired Major, submitted birth certificate and voter's card but owed the D1 million nomination deposit and lacked an income tax clearance certificate. He presented an expired national identity card, citing renewal difficulties, and affirmed Gambian birthright without other nationalities.

Bakusa Dukureh, 48, of the Gambia Labour Party, paid the D1 million deposit but faces scrutiny over dual Gambian and American citizenship, stating he would not renounce his US passport. Dukureh also needs an income tax clearance certificate and to sign the Code of Election Campaign Ethics. National Builders Party leader Ansu Sanneh submitted his papers seven weeks after his party's registration.

Sowe stated his candidacy aims to improve security and allow citizens to move freely with valuables. Sanneh's agenda includes building a new city near the River Gambia and establishing a regional airport in the Upper River Region. His party, registered on September 17, has a development blueprint.

Why it mattersGambian citizens seeking to elect a president in 2026 face questions about candidate eligibility and requirements.
