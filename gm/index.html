EFF challenges immigration over border refusal

The party disputes the immigration department's explanation for barring campaign technicians and vehicles.

The Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF) Gambia is challenging the Gambia Immigration Department's (GID) explanation for denying entry to campaign technicians and vehicles for presidential candidate Prince Ebrahim Sanyang. EFF argues that the GID's account is contradictory and lacks a legal basis for the refusal.

Immigration Director General Ebrima Mboob claimed the passengers stated they were joining another candidate, but EFF points to vehicles branded with EFF colours and Sanyang's portrait. The party insists six Senegalese technicians arrived in two vehicles, contradicting the GID's description of "a bus and a pickup truck" or "two buses." EFF plans to provide documentation to prove campaign ownership of ECOWAS-registered vehicles.

EFF criticized the GID's reliance on "movement control" without citing a specific published rule. Prince Sanyang submitted a clearance request on October 23, but no decision was communicated by October 27, leading EFF to argue the delay cannot justify refusal. The party demands proof that this requirement applies equally to all candidates ahead of the December 5 presidential election.

Why it mattersOpposition parties face restrictions on campaign movements and meetings ahead of the presidential election.

Gambia Press Union urges peaceful election campaigns

The union calls for issue-based campaigns and media safety ahead of December 5, 2026 presidential nominations.

The Gambia Press Union (GPU) president, Isatou Keita, has called for peace and tolerance from political parties, candidates, and citizens as Gambia prepares for the December 5, 2026 presidential election nominations. Keita stated that the nomination process is a significant milestone in the nation's democratic journey and electoral process.

The GPU urged political actors to focus campaigns on issues and ideas, warning against hostility, division, personal attacks, hate speech, misinformation, and inflammatory rhetoric. The union also stressed the media's role, advising journalists to prioritize accuracy, verification, fairness, independence, and public interest over speed and speculation.

Journalists were reminded to ensure their safety by staying aware of surroundings, identifying themselves, maintaining communication, and avoiding dangerous situations, with media houses urged to provide adequate preparation and support. The GPU also advised strengthening digital security against disinformation campaigns and called on all stakeholders to reject hate speech and attacks on journalists.

Why it mattersGambia's citizens and media professionals are directly affected by the call for peaceful campaigns and safety during the upcoming presidential election nominations.

Gambia suspends ex-CEO over D696,609 loss

Former KMC CEO Sainabou Martin Sonko faces criminal investigation for irregular payments and loan guarantees.

The Gambian government has suspended former KMC CEO Sainabou Martin Sonko and ordered a criminal investigation into her actions. The Local Government Commission found she authorised irregular payments and loan guarantees leading to a D696,609 loss for the Council and a D5 million disbursement to a non-operational transport company.

The Commission's White Paper, released on 29 October, detailed that Sonko breached procurement laws by paying contractors without competitive bidding or committee clearance. She also executed welfare loan guarantees without Council approval, including an AGIB guarantee of D800,000 and a Trust Bank loan that resulted in a direct debit from the Council's account. Sonko admitted receiving a D30,000 inducement from a contractor.

The government accepted the Commission's recommendation for Sonko's suspension and directed the Inspector General of Police to investigate the Mbalit Project and related payments. The Auditor General will conduct a forensic audit and surcharge to recover losses, including the D5 million paid to Kanifing Municipal Transport and staff welfare loans.

Why it mattersFormer KMC CEO Sainabou Martin Sonko faces suspension and criminal investigation due to alleged financial misconduct causing significant losses to the Council.
