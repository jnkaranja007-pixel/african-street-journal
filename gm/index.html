Gambia launches two projects to restore ecosystems

The Great Green Wall RECOSERV Gambia Project and FARM-Plus Project aim to combat land degradation and deforestation.

The Gambia launched two five-year projects on Tuesday to restore degraded ecosystems and promote sustainable agriculture. The Great Green Wall (GGW) RECOSERV Gambia Project and the FARM-Plus Project were initiated to combat climate change, deforestation, and land degradation. These initiatives are funded by the Global Environment Facility (GEF) with support from the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) and the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), and will be executed by the National Environment Agency (NEA).

The RECOSERV Gambia project aims to restore 43,000 hectares of forest and wetland ecosystems, improve management across 38,000 hectares of protected areas, and introduce sustainable practices on 36,000 hectares of rural landscapes. These interventions are expected to benefit approximately 795,000 people, primarily women. FAO Country Representative Moshibudi Rampedi stated that the projects will help farmers adopt alternatives that reduce harmful chemicals, conserve water, and minimize waste.

Minister of Agriculture Demba Sabally described the projects as a commitment to combating desertification and securing agricultural land productivity. The FARM-Plus project will focus on strengthening policies, improving access to finance and insurance, building technical capacity, and promoting sustainable value chains for climate-smart agriculture. Minister of Environment Rohey John Manjang emphasized that success will be measured by the survival of restored forests and tangible improvements in livelihoods.

Why it mattersThe projects aim to restore 43,000 hectares of degraded forest and wetland ecosystems and introduce sustainable practices on 36,000 hectares of rural landscapes, benefiting 795,000 people.

Gambia election nominations face early hurdles

One party withdrew while two aspirants encountered issues with documentation and fees on the first day of nominations.

The Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) began receiving presidential nominations for the December 5, 2026 election on Tuesday, November 3, in Kanifing. The All-Inclusive Party withdrew its aspirant from the scheduled nomination, and two other candidates faced immediate challenges. The nomination process, which continues until November 14, requires aspirants to deposit D1 million into an IEC-designated account and provide original citizenship documents.

Independent candidate Salieu Jallow could not complete his nomination after failing to meet the D1 million deposit requirement, misunderstanding the payment process. Modou Lamin Sima, presidential aspirant for the Alliance of Progressive Citizens, submitted photocopies of his citizenship documents instead of originals, risking rejection unless he provides the required originals before the window closes. IEC Chairman Joseph Colley stated that original documents are mandatory under the Elections Act 2025.

The tightened compliance regime under the Elections Act 2025, including the D1 million deposit, has sparked public debate. Proponents argue it ensures seriousness and covers electoral costs, while critics contend it creates barriers for independents and smaller parties. The election will be the first under this revised electoral framework, which raises nomination thresholds.

Why it mattersGambia's presidential election nominations are underway, with potential candidates facing new requirements for documentation and significant financial deposits.

UNITE accuses gov't of misrepresenting report

The party claims the government's White Paper distorts findings to disqualify presidential candidate Mayor Talib Ahmed Bensouda.

The UNITE coalition has accused the Gambian government of deliberately misrepresenting a Commission of Inquiry report to influence the upcoming elections and disqualify its presidential candidate, Mayor Talib Ahmed Bensouda. UNITE Secretary for Legal and Constitutional Affairs, Abdul Aziz Bensouda, stated at a press conference that the government's White Paper distorts the commission's findings.

Bensouda argued that the White Paper does not reflect the actual report, which he said contains no disqualifying findings against the mayor. He noted that an adverse finding served in May stated only that the mayor had "exercised political and executive influence in a manner capable of causing or sustaining irregular commitments, unauthorised expenditure, irregular project direction, and extra-statutory financial exposure," which he contended does not amount to corruption or misconduct.

UNITE also alleged that the report was altered after its May submission to the President, with metadata showing changes, and suggested parts may have been generated by artificial intelligence. The party claims the government is politicising the report's release ahead of nominations to use state resources to influence the 2026 elections and warned of rising political tensions.

Why it mattersThe UNITE coalition alleges the government is misrepresenting a commission report to disqualify their presidential candidate, Mayor Talib Ahmed Bensouda, potentially affecting his eligibility for public office.

NAWEC warns of fines for illegal electricity connections

The national water and electricity company identified 200 bypass connections and 400 faulty meters, with penalties set at D50,000 for domestic and D200,000 for business users.

The National Water and Electricity Company (NAWEC) is warning individuals involved in electricity theft through illegal connections and meter bypasses that they will face financial penalties and possible prosecution. NAWEC's Managing Director, Gallo Saidy, announced at a town hall meeting in MansaKunda that the company has identified approximately 200 electricity bypass connections and about 400 cases of faulty meters.

A task force has been established to conduct nationwide operations to identify these irregularities. Domestic users caught bypassing electricity will be charged around D50,000, while businesses face charges of approximately D200,000. Those found guilty will also face court action as NAWEC works to combat theft and protect its infrastructure, which has also suffered damage during recent demonstrations.

Mr. Saidy urged Gambians to protect NAWEC facilities, stating that vandalism and illegal connections hinder reliable service delivery. He also cautioned consumers against using substandard electrical materials, as poor wiring can cause safety risks and electricity problems. The company relies on consumer cooperation to report irregularities and improve services across the country.

Why it mattersConsumers face significant financial penalties and potential prosecution for illegal electricity connections and meter bypasses.
