Darboe links UDP history to call for unity and electoral reform

United Democratic Party leader Ousainou Darboe used the party's 30th anniversary rally to advocate for political change and national unity ahead of the December 5 presidential.

Ousainou Darboe, leader of the United Democratic Party (UDP), addressed thousands of supporters at the party’s 30th anniversary rally in Brikama, emphasizing the UDP’s history of sacrifices. He recalled instances of imprisonment, violence, and deaths endured by party members, particularly during former President Yahya Jammeh’s 22-year rule, to highlight the cost of challenging authoritarianism.

Darboe specifically mentioned the 1996 ambush at Denton Bridge, where several young members were tortured, and the arrest of Amadou Sanneh for refusing to support the APRC government. He also paid tribute to Solo Sandeng, a UDP activist who died in state custody in April 2016 after campaigning for electoral reforms, noting that some of these reforms, like the Public Order Act, remain unfinished nearly a decade later.

Looking forward to the upcoming presidential election, Darboe called for opposition unity and urged Gambians to reject ethnic divisions. He pledged that a UDP government would increase representation for persons with disabilities in senior government positions, enhance support for farmers, and prioritize agriculture, framing the anniversary as a reminder of ongoing political struggles.

Why it mattersThis story is important to Gambians because it outlines the political platform and historical grievances of a major opposition party leader ahead of a national election, potentially influencing future governance and policy.

Three survive Jimpex Road crash, one passenger seriously injured

A road accident at Jimpex today left one passenger seriously injured after becoming trapped inside a vehicle that collided with a parked car.

Three individuals, including a driver and two passengers, survived a road accident earlier today at Jimpex. One front-seat passenger sustained serious injuries after being pinned inside the wreckage, with his legs stuck following the impact.

Eyewitnesses reported that the car, traveling from Banjul, struck another vehicle parked along the roadside. While the driver and a rear-seat passenger escaped unharmed, rescue efforts were challenging for the trapped passenger.

Emergency responders eventually freed the injured passenger, who was then rushed to the hospital for treatment. An eyewitness credited the airbag and seatbelt for preventing more severe injuries, stating, “The airbag and the seat belt saved him. If not, his head would have hit the car.” No fatalities were recorded, though the exact circumstances remain unclear, highlighting ongoing road safety concerns in The Gambia.

Why it mattersThis incident affects motorists and passengers in The Gambia by underscoring the critical importance of consistent seatbelt use and adherence to traffic regulations to prevent serious injuries and fatalities.

Central Bank cuts policy rate to 14 percent amid economic growth

The Central Bank of The Gambia reduced its Monetary Policy Rate to 14 percent in February 2026, maintaining it through August, as the economy expanded by 5.9 percent in 2025.

The Central Bank of The Gambia has lowered its Monetary Policy Rate (MPR) from 17 percent to 14 percent by February 2026, holding it steady through August. This decision follows a reduction from 17 percent to 16 percent in December 2025, aiming to balance inflation, economic activity, and financial stability.

The rate cuts occur as The Gambia's economy shows broad-based growth. The World Bank estimates a 5.9 percent growth in 2025, building on 5.6 percent in 2024, with contributions from agriculture, industry, and services. Tourism has recovered, remittances remain significant, and inflation has returned to single-digit figures.

Despite positive economic indicators, including a projected 5.3 percent growth by the World Bank for 2026, concerns persist regarding the nation's domestic debt. A former banker and financial analyst noted that while borrowing can be beneficial for development, the distinction between borrowing for economic assets versus financing recurring fiscal needs is crucial for the Gambian debate.

Why it mattersThis story affects Gambian citizens because the Central Bank's policy rate influences the cost of borrowing and economic activity, impacting their financial stability and the nation's debt burden.

NPP Student Wing welcomes poll results showing Barrow support

The NPP student wing president, Yusuf Tunkara, stated that a recent Gambia Participates survey confirms growing support for President Barrow and the governing coalition.

Yusuf Tunkara, president of the NPP student wing, announced that a new poll from Gambia Participates indicates increasing public backing for President Barrow. Tunkara stated the survey's findings are a "clear sign" Gambians are supporting the incumbent's record on stability, infrastructure, and economic recovery, asserting that the poll reflects "the true mood" of voters in both urban and rural areas.

Tunkara praised the methodology and timing of the Gambia Participates poll, noting it aligns with feedback his team receives during campaigning. He claimed that young people, women, and small business owners believe in the direction set by President Barrow, who was first elected in 2016 and re-elected in 2021.

Despite opposition criticism regarding the cost of living and governance, Tunkara insisted that the president's achievements in infrastructure, public sector reform, and regional diplomacy resonate more with voters. He added that the student wing plans to mobilize support for Barrow and advocate for a transparent electoral process, urging undecided voters to consider continuity of development.

Why it mattersGambian citizens are affected by these political developments as they indicate potential continuity in leadership and policy, impacting daily life through infrastructure projects, economic recovery efforts, and public sector reforms.

Gambian Muslims commemorate Prophet Muhammad's birth with gamo gatherings

Muslims across The Gambia will gather today, August 25th, for gamo celebrations, with August 26th declared a public holiday to honor the birth of the Holy Prophet Muhammad.

Today, August 25th, Muslims in The Gambia are observing gamo to commemorate the birth of the Holy Prophet Muhammad. Homes, mosques, and communities are hosting recitations of the Holy Qur’an, salawaat, sermons, and khassaïdes. This celebration extends across the border to Tivaouane, Medina Baye, and Touba, which will also become centers of prayer and remembrance.

The commemoration emphasizes the Prophet Muhammad's teachings on faith, honesty, prayer, compassion, justice, and forgiveness. He is described in the Holy Qur’an as “a mercy to the worlds” and “an excellent example” for believers, known for his great moral character and care for the vulnerable.

The event also serves as a call for self-examination, particularly relevant as The Gambia approaches a presidential election. Concerns about divisive political and ethnic rhetoric highlight the importance of preserving national cohesion, encouraging citizens to maintain brotherhood despite differences, consistent with the Prophet's message against tribal superiority and injustice.

Why it mattersGambian Muslims are directly affected by the public holiday and the cultural and religious gatherings that promote community reflection on moral character and national cohesion.
