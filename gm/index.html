Darboe marks 30 years, calls for unity and reform

UDP leader Ousainou Darboe addressed thousands at the party's 30th anniversary, urging Gambians to unite, fight corruption, and demand accountability for past injustices.

The United Democratic Party (UDP) marked its 30th anniversary on Sunday at Brikama SSP, where party leader Ousainou Darboe called for national unity and political change. Addressing thousands of supporters, allied groups, and political leaders, Mr. Darboe emphasized that the party's history was built on sacrifices, including imprisonment, violence, and deaths, particularly during former President Yahya Jammeh’s 22-year rule.

Mr. Darboe recalled specific incidents, such as the 1996 ambush at Denton Bridge where several young members were tortured, and the arrest of Amadou Sanneh for refusing to support the APRC government. He highlighted the death of UDP activist Solo Sandeng in state custody in April 2016, who campaigned for electoral reforms. Mr. Darboe stated that these memories serve to educate younger Gambians on the price paid for challenging authoritarian rule.

With the December 5 presidential election approaching, Mr. Darboe linked the party’s history to its political ambitions, calling for opposition unity and urging Gambians to reject ethnic divisions. He pledged greater representation for persons with disabilities, increased support for farmers, and a stronger emphasis on agriculture if the UDP comes to power. He also criticized the Public Order Act, arguing it continues to restrict political rights and free electoral competition.

Why it mattersThis story is important for Gambians because it outlines the UDP's political platform and calls for unity ahead of the upcoming presidential election, directly impacting the country's future leadership and policy direction.

Three survive Jimpex Road crash, one passenger seriously injured

A road accident at Jimpex involved a car from Banjul colliding with a parked vehicle, leaving one front-seat passenger trapped and seriously injured.

Three people survived a road accident at Jimpex earlier today, though one passenger sustained serious injuries after becoming trapped inside the vehicle. The car, traveling from Banjul, collided with another vehicle parked along the roadside. The driver and a rear-seat passenger escaped unharmed, while the front-seat passenger was pinned inside the wreckage with his legs stuck.

Emergency responders managed to free the injured passenger after several attempts, and he was immediately rushed to the hospital for treatment. Eyewitnesses credited the car's airbag and seat belt for preventing more severe injuries, stating that without them, the passenger's head would have hit the car.

No fatalities were recorded in the incident, and the exact circumstances leading to the crash remain unclear. Authorities continue to urge motorists and passengers to exercise caution and adhere to traffic regulations, as road accidents remain a major public concern in The Gambia.

Why it mattersThis event affects Gambian residents and travelers by highlighting the ongoing risks of road accidents and the importance of safety measures like seat belts.

Central Bank cuts policy rate to 14 percent

The Central Bank of The Gambia reduced its Monetary Policy Rate to 14 percent in February 2026, maintaining it through August 2026 to balance inflation and economic activity.

The Central Bank of The Gambia lowered its Monetary Policy Rate from 17 percent in December 2025 to 14 percent by February 2026, a decision aimed at supporting economic activity. This reduction follows a previous cut from 17 percent to 16 percent in December 2025, and the rate has remained at 14 percent through May and August 2026.

This policy adjustment by Governor Buah Saidy and the Monetary Policy Committee seeks to lower the cost of money, stimulate borrowing, investment, and consumption. The move is intended to balance inflation, economic activity, liquidity, exchange rate stability, private sector credit, and overall financial stability within the country.

The Gambian economy grew by an estimated 5.9 percent in 2025, following 5.6 percent growth in 2024, with contributions from agriculture, industry, and services. The World Bank projects 5.3 percent growth for 2026, while the IMF is more conservative at 4.7 percent, alongside an average consumer price inflation projection of about 7.5 percent for 2026.

Why it mattersGambians may find it easier to borrow money for personal or business ventures due to the reduced policy rate, potentially stimulating local economic activity.

NPP Student Wing welcomes poll showing support for Barrow

The NPP student wing president, Yusuf Tunkara, stated that a recent survey indicates growing support for President Barrow and the governing coalition, reflecting voter desire for.

Yusuf Tunkara, president of the NPP student wing, announced that a recent Gambia Participates poll shows increasing support for President Barrow and the governing coalition. Tunkara said the findings are a “clear sign” Gambians are rallying behind the incumbent’s record on stability, infrastructure, and economic recovery, asserting that the poll confirms what his team observes daily on the streets and campuses.

Tunkara praised the poll's methodology and timing, stating it captured “the true mood” of voters across urban and rural areas. He added that the results align with feedback received during campaigning, noting that young people, women, and small business owners express belief in President Barrow’s direction.

Despite criticism from opposition parties regarding the cost of living and governance, Tunkara insisted that the president’s achievements in infrastructure, public sector reform, and regional diplomacy resonate more with voters. He also stated the student wing would mobilize support for Barrow and advocate for a transparent electoral process, urging undecided voters to consider continuity of development.

Why it mattersThis story affects Gambian citizens by indicating potential political stability and continuity of current government policies, which could influence daily life and future development projects.

Gambians commemorate Prophet Muhammad's birth with gamo gatherings

Muslims across The Gambia will gather for gamo today, August twenty-fifth, to commemorate the birth of the Holy Prophet Muhammad, with tomorrow, August twenty-sixth, declared a.

Homes, mosques, and communities in The Gambia are observing gamo today, August twenty-fifth, with recitations of the Holy Qur’an, salawaat, sermons, and khassaïdes. This commemoration of the Holy Prophet Muhammad's birth extends across the border to Tivaouane, Medina Baye, and Touba, which will also become centers of prayer and reflection.

The event, which includes a public holiday on Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, is seen by Abdoulie Mam Njie as a time for self-examination. Njie states that the teachings of the Holy Prophet Muhammad emphasize faith, honesty, prayer, compassion, justice, and forgiveness, and challenged tribal superiority and injustice.

Njie highlights the relevance of these teachings to The Gambia's current political climate, noting increasing intensity in political competition and public debate ahead of another presidential election. He stresses that preserving national cohesion is a shared responsibility amid concerns about divisive rhetoric.

Why it mattersGambians are directly affected by the public holiday and the call for national cohesion during a period of intense political competition.
