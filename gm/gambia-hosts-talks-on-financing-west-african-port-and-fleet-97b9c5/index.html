Gambia hosts talks on financing West African port and fleet projects

Ministers meeting in Banjul are working to make a regional maritime development bank operational, and say bottlenecks must be cleared first.

Sources: The Point and The Alkamba Times · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Ministers from West and Central Africa gathered in Banjul, The Gambia, for a two-day meeting that began on Monday, 16 November, to push forward the Regional Maritime Development Bank. The meeting was the 12th Ordinary Session of the Bureau of Ministers of the Maritime Organisation of West and Central Africa (MOWCA), which The Gambia chairs for 2026 and 2027.

Transport minister Ebrima Sillah said ministers examined a report from a Special Committee of Experts and legal documents prepared by Juris Law. Bottlenecks must be removed, he said, before financing can reach port infrastructure, fleet renewal and maritime commerce. Ministers also took up the Regional Maritime Fund, human resources and member states' 2027 contributions.

Ministers also reviewed the 2026 work programme and a roadmap for a first MOWCA Heads of State Summit to be hosted by Côte d'Ivoire. Talks with the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) and the West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU) over double taxation were also on the agenda.

Why it mattersShipping and port investment across West and Central Africa depends on the bank becoming operational, which hinges on clearing these obstacles and securing member contributions.
