Gambian columnist warns polygamy can spread sexually transmitted infections silently

Writing in The Alkamba Times, Dr. Mimi says multiple partners, weak testing and unequal decision-making can expose wives to serious health risks.

Source: The Alkamba Times · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Writing in The Alkamba Times, Dr. Mimi argues that polygamy does not automatically cause sexually transmitted infections, but that multiple sexual partnerships combined with inadequate testing, unprotected sex, secrecy and unequal decision-making can have devastating consequences for women. She says many women discover the truth only after the damage is done.

She illustrates the point with the case of Modou and Fatou, a couple married for nearly 18 years with three children. When Modou announced he would take a second wife, Fatou was devastated, and he dismissed her concerns by pointing to cultural and religious acceptance of polygamy in their community. The column says Fatou lost sleep and appetite, became withdrawn and anxious, and that her children noticed the change.

The column says the home became filled with tension and emotional distance, and that the eldest daughter asked why her mother cried at night while the youngest son wondered whether his father still loved the family. It frames the issue as a conversation too often overlooked across many African communities.

Why it mattersWives in polygamous marriages, and the children in those homes, face possible health risks from untested partners and unprotected sex, and the column urges women to insist on their right to informed decisions about their bodies.
