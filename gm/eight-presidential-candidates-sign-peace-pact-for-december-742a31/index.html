Eight presidential candidates sign peace pact for December election

The candidates pledged to reject violence, intimidation, and inflammatory rhetoric ahead of the December 5 vote.

Sources: The Alkamba Times and Kerr Fatou · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Eight presidential candidates in The Gambia signed an 11-article Peace Pact at the Sir Dawda Kairaba Jawara International Conference Center, committing to peaceful, issue-based campaigns before the December 5, 2026 election. The agreement, facilitated by the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) and International IDEA, was signed as the official campaign period began on November 17. Incumbent President Adama Barrow and his main challenger, A.N.M. Ousainou Darboe, were among the signatories.

The pact requires candidates to reject hate speech, tribal bigotry, and the irresponsible use of digital media, while respecting press freedom. IEC Chairman Joseph Colley reminded signatories that the Code of Election Campaign Ethics is legally binding. International IDEA Country Director Mrs Jainaba Faye stated the pact's value depends on candidates' conduct, emphasizing it remains "only paper until it is lived."

The National Elections Response Group Chairman David Darrow-Tommy urged candidates to prioritize national interests over personal ambitions, noting their words carry immense weight with supporters. Switzerland’s Ambassador to The Gambia, Pedro Mona, highlighted the importance of respecting democratic institutions and ensuring political opponents do not become enemies.

Why it mattersEight presidential candidates have committed to peaceful campaigning for the December 5 election, with the Independent Electoral Commission stating the code of ethics is legally binding.
