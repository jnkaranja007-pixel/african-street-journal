Gambia civic leaders urge tolerance before 2026 election

National Council for Civic Education warns 35 constituencies are high-risk for electoral violence.

Source: The Point · 2026-10-24 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Gambian civic leaders are calling for tolerance and unity ahead of the December 5, 2026 presidential election. At a workshop, Chief Civic Education Officer Yusupha Bojang warned against divisive language in political discourse, stating elections should be a marketplace of ideas, not a battleground for insults or polarization.

Bojang revealed that early warning analysis shows 35 of the country’s 53 constituencies are rated high or very high risk, a concern requiring vigilance from all stakeholders. He urged political leaders to publicly condemn hate speech, reminding them that rivals are not enemies.

The Inter-Party Committee (IPC) also reaffirmed its dedication to fostering peace, inclusivity, and mutual respect. Pamela Camara of Catholic Relief Services stressed that peace is nurtured daily, urging citizens to use their voices for healing and trust.

Why it mattersGambian citizens preparing for the 2026 presidential election are being urged to promote peace and unity to safeguard democracy.
