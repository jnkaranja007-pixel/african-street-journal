Burkina Faso reviews study of maternal and child mortality declines

Research partners presented findings in Ouagadougou after maternal mortality fell 47% and deaths of children under five fell 78% from 1998 to 2021.

Source: Lefaso.net · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Burkina Faso’s health ministry and research partners reviewed final findings on what contributed to falling maternal and child mortality at a two-day workshop in Ouagadougou on Nov. 24 and 25. The study, begun in 2024, was conducted with Northwestern University and the Institute for Disease Modeling.

The 2021 Demographic and Health Survey recorded a 47% reduction in maternal mortality and a 78% decline in deaths of children under five between 1998 and 2021, ministry technical adviser Estelle-Edith Dembélé/Dabiré said. She said the progress came amid security and humanitarian challenges, as well as limits on access to and availability of health services.

Participants discussed recommendations intended to strengthen the health system. The ministry said the research sought to identify which system factors and interventions contributed to the declines and help guide health policy.

Why it mattersThe health ministry and its research partners must use the study’s findings to shape recommendations for improving health-system performance.
