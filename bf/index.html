Burkina Faso launches public conferences on patriotism

Minister of Justice Edasso Rodrigue Bayala launched public talks on civics and patriotism across all 47 provinces.

Minister of Justice Edasso Rodrigue Bayala officially launched public conferences on civics, citizenship, patriotic engagement, and peace culture in the Kadiogo province on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. These conferences, an initiative of the ministry in charge of citizenship, aim to instill values of patriotism and civism in Burkinabè citizens across all 47 provinces. The talks also seek to bring populations closer to the ideals of the popular progressive revolution, focusing on sovereignty, freedom, dignity, integrity, and autonomy.

The conferences are designed to strengthen citizen engagement in the fight against imperialism and terrorism, while promoting community values and mobilizing people for national development initiatives. Minister Bayala stated that the primary goal is to encourage the populace's ongoing support for the popular progressive revolution and to foster an even stronger commitment. The events will cover sub-themes including civics, citizenship, patriotic engagement, peace culture, and the ideals of the popular progressive revolution.

During the launch, Minister Bayala conveyed a message from the Head of State, Captain Ibrahim Traoré, urging all Burkinabè to cultivate sincere patriotism and responsible citizenship. The President also called for the patriotic flame to be kept alive, respecting the ideals of the popular progressive revolution. The public conferences are organized in collaboration with the Presidential Initiative for Community Development and the National Bureau of Major Projects.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso citizens are being encouraged to strengthen their patriotic commitment and civic engagement through public conferences.

Burkina Faso and Türkiye discuss expanding partnership

The Turkish ambassador highlighted cooperation in health, defense, and education during a celebration in Ouagadougou.

Burkina Faso and Türkiye are strengthening their bilateral ties, with Ambassador Feriba Hokkaci Esirgen celebrating the 103rd anniversary of the Turkish Republic in Ouagadougou on November 2, 2026. The ambassador noted advancements in areas including political consultations, kidney transplant operations, defense, education, and investments, expressing a desire for further consolidation of this cooperation.

Ambassador Esirgen praised Burkina Faso's efforts towards economic and social sovereignty, including initiatives for energy independence and value addition. She affirmed Türkiye's commitment to supporting Burkina Faso's development and its fight against terrorism, aligning with shared goals for peace and prosperity.

The first political consultations between the two nations took place in Ankara on October 20, 2026. In the health sector, three kidney transplant operations have been successfully completed through collaboration between medical professionals from both countries, with a fourth mission planned before the end of 2026.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso's citizens will benefit from expanded cooperation in health services and potentially economic development initiatives.

Oumarou Boua earns doctorate for research on African Political Instability

His thesis links political unrest to reduced foreign investment and migrant remittances.

Oumarou Boua, an economics and development advisor, earned a doctorate from Université Thomas-Sankara in Ouagadougou on November 2, 2026. His thesis, "Essays on Political Instability and External Financing in Sub-Saharan African Countries," examined how political crises affect foreign investment, migrant transfers, and development aid.

Boua's research found that political instability significantly reduces foreign direct investment flows, impacting not only directly affected nations but also neighboring states due to contagion risks. He also noted that while some countries see migrant remittances increase during crises as families seek support, financial development can enhance this mobilization.

The study analyzed three areas: the influence of political instability on foreign direct investment, the role of financial development in migrant remittances amid instability, and the bidirectional relationship between political instability and official development assistance.

Why it mattersOumarou Boua's research highlights how political instability in sub-Saharan Africa can deter foreign investment and affect migrant remittances.

Algeria sends troops to Niger

Algeria formalised military support for Niger on October 21 with a cooperation protocol and parliamentary approval for troop deployment.

Algeria will send troops to Niger to support its neighbour against armed groups and destabilisers. This military cooperation follows a protocol signed on October 21 and parliamentary approval four days later. The deployment marks Algeria's first foreign troop commitment since the 1973 Arab-Israeli War.

The Algerian military aid, which includes fuel, aims to bolster Niger's government in its fight against terrorism. This support comes as Niger, along with Mali and Burkina Faso, forms the Confederation of Sahel States (AES), facing threats from armed groups and external destabilisation efforts.

Algeria's move signals a renewed strategic presence in the Sahel and could potentially extend to Mali and Burkina Faso. The country also aims to protect economic interests, including the Trans-Saharan gas pipeline project connecting Nigeria to Algeria via Niger.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso, as part of the Sahel States Confederation, faces similar threats and could potentially benefit from this military cooperation.

Burkina Faso soldiers train for farming careers

93 military and forestry agents began a three-week course on November 3, 2026, in Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso.

93 soldiers and forestry agents began a three-week training course on November 3, 2026, in Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso. The program prepares them for careers in farming and livestock as they approach retirement. Lieutenant Sompayiti Moctar Kaboré, three months from ending his military service, sees this as a way to support his family and contribute to Burkina Faso's food security.

The session includes 74 participants in Ouagadougou and 19 in Bobo-Dioulasso. Trainer Ibrahim Sory of CID Ingénierie emphasized that agriculture and livestock require skills, planning, and management. Training covers adapted production techniques, soil management, fertilizer use, water conservation, animal husbandry, and economic management.

Colonel-major Alassane Guiré, director of cabinet at the Ministry of Agriculture, donated 12 tonnes of agricultural inputs and equipment. The military's retraining policy, adopted in May 2008, aims to train 1,000 people in five years. A previous collaboration installed 100 fish farmers, with 15 harvests yielding encouraging results.

Why it mattersMilitary personnel are being trained in agriculture and livestock to support food security and create jobs upon retirement.
