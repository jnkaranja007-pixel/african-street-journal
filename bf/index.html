Burkina Faso, Turkey agree to boost education cooperation

Ankara talks between the two nations also covered increased scholarships for Burkinabe students.

Burkina Faso and Turkey held political consultations in Ankara on October 20-21, 2026, to strengthen cooperation. The Burkinabe delegation, led by Ambassador Hermann Yirigouin TOE, met with Turkish officials from various state structures, including the Maarif Foundation, TIKA, the Council of Higher Education, and the Ministry of Health.

Discussions with the Maarif Foundation focused on signing an agreement for school establishment and management, as the foundation already operates schools in Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso. The foundation agreed to increase scholarships for Burkinabe students and provide support for vocational and technical training.

TIKA, the Turkish development agency, committed to supporting Burkina Faso in vocational and technical training in 2027 and is considering opening an office in Ouagadougou. The Council of Higher Education also agreed to renew an academic cooperation accord and increase Master's and Doctorate scholarships for Burkinabe students.

Why it mattersBurkinabe students will benefit from increased scholarships and expanded vocational training opportunities in Turkey.

Business · 2026-10-24

Lomé port seeks stronger ties with Burkina Faso traders

Port officials met Ouagadougou business leaders on October 23, 2026, to discuss trade facilitation.

Officials from the Port of Lomé and the Alliance for the Promotion of the Port of Lomé (A2PL) met with Burkinabè economic operators in Ouagadougou on Friday, October 23, 2026. The meeting aimed to strengthen collaboration and ease the transit of goods to Burkina Faso. This event was part of a promotional mission from October 18 to 24, 2026, focused on improving trade with the Alliance of Sahel States.

During the session at the Burkinabè Shippers' Council, Lomé port representatives presented their services and addressed concerns from Burkinabè businesses. Awèmèwètou Haladoko, secretary general of A2PL, highlighted the port's performance, noting that in 2025 it handled nearly 36 million tonnes of cargo and processed about 2.5 million 20-foot equivalent containers. The average waiting time for container ships at anchor was reduced to 21 hours and 39 minutes.

The port also detailed its specialized handling capacities for strategic products, including 100 to 117 tonnes per hour for wheat and over 350 tonnes per hour for clinker. Efforts to streamline administrative procedures through digitalization, such as the One-Stop Shop for International Trade Formalities (GUFORD), were also discussed. A Task Force AES 2026 was established to address challenges faced by economic operators.

Why it mattersBurkinabè businesses will benefit from improved transit of goods through the Port of Lomé.

Health · 2026-10-24

IB Bank staff donate blood in Ouagadougou

The bank's staff mutual organised a blood drive to support health facilities facing high demand.

The staff mutual of IB Bank Burkina organised a blood donation drive on Friday at the bank's headquarters in Ouagadougou. The operation, in collaboration with the National Blood Transfusion Centre (CNTS), aimed to increase the supply of blood products for health facilities. This initiative mobilises bank workers for a gesture of solidarity to help those needing transfusions.

Moïse Kafando, a member of the mutual's bureau, stated that the organisation wants to contribute to public health and the population's well-being. He explained that blood donation was chosen to launch the mutual's activities due to the importance of health. Marguétou Derra/Mané, head of collection at the Ouagadougou blood transfusion centre, praised the initiative, noting high demand from maternity, pediatric, and security forces units.

Derra/Mané highlighted that blood is a precious resource not available through ordinary drug sales and urged the public to engage in voluntary donation. She stressed that meeting transfusion needs is a collective responsibility. The IB Bank staff mutual aims to promote mutual aid and civic responsibility, encouraging other companies to support public health actions.

Why it mattersThe blood donation drive by IB Bank staff in Ouagadougou will help provide blood products for patients in need at health facilities.

Anselme Traoré defends thesis on profit crime in Burkina Faso

Anselme Traoré was admitted with highest honors for his research on profit crime from 1987 to 2015.

Anselme Traoré successfully defended his doctoral thesis on profit crime in Burkina Faso between 1987 and 2015 at the Norbert Zongo University in Koudougou on Friday. He was awarded the highest honors for his work, which was examined by a jury led by Professor Salifou Idani.

Professor Idani noted that Traoré's analysis of societal components offered relevant solutions for authorities. Traoré stated his research shows a rise in profit-related offenses, some escalating from street crime to organized crime, driven by a risk-reward assessment. He suggested reducing contributing factors is key to combating these crimes.

Zara Dao, Traoré's thesis director, praised his contribution to Burkina Faso's economic and social history, highlighting the under-researched topic of profit crime. The jury also included Professor Mahamadou Oubda, with Master of Conferences Soualiouno Méité Ben from Ivory Coast and Research Master Wenceslas Sourbar Justin Hien serving as rapporteurs.

Why it mattersAnselme Traoré's thesis defense on profit crime affects the academic community and potentially government policy.

Tech · 2026-10-24

Burkinabè researcher develops cybersecurity tech in Canada

Dr Karim Ganamé, a Burkinabè computer scientist, is creating advanced cybersecurity solutions from Montreal, Canada.

Dr Karim Ganamé, a Burkinabè researcher based in Montreal, Canada, is developing advanced cybersecurity technologies. He works on detecting threats before they impact systems and also helps limit damage after attacks. Ganamé's work aims to protect sensitive digital systems from cyberattacks like ransomware.

Ganamé, who began his IT career at Burkina Faso's Office National des Télécommunications in 1999, holds a doctorate in computer security from the University of Franche-Comté in France. His expertise combines scientific research with entrepreneurship to create protective digital solutions.

His journey includes studies in communication networks and computer networks in France, followed by a master's in management and entrepreneurship. Ganamé's company develops solutions for environments where data and infrastructure protection are critical.

Why it mattersBurkinabè citizens are affected by the development of advanced cybersecurity technologies created by a fellow national working abroad.
