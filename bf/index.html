Burkina Faso plans 21st digital week for November 2026

The event will focus on technological sovereignty, secure digital transformation, and inclusion.

Burkina Faso will host the 21st edition of the Digital Week from November 24 to 27, 2026, at the Ouagadougou International Handicraft Fair (SIAO) site. The event's theme, "Technological Sovereignty: Accelerating Secure and Inclusive Digital Transformation," was announced at a press conference in Ouagadougou on November 9, 2026. This edition follows last year's focus on artificial intelligence and aims to address technology control, data security, and local solutions.

Organizers have planned several activities, including Conf'Days for discussions and experience sharing, and a Startup and Innovation Village to showcase entrepreneurial initiatives, particularly in AI and open-source solutions. Dr. Borlli Michel Jonas Somé, president of the organizing committee, confirmed preparations are on track. The program will also feature immersive experiences like robot demonstrations and the RoboKids robotics competition to engage youth in science and technology.

A five-week campaign of free training in information and communication technologies (ICT) will be offered, covering areas such as office automation, AI, cybersecurity, and web development. Special attention will be given to youth, women, internally displaced persons, and people with disabilities. Five competitions, including ethical hacking and a data challenge, will recognize outstanding digital initiatives, culminating in the Nuit des lauréats on November 27, 2026.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso's citizens will benefit from free ICT training and exposure to new technologies.

Health · 2026-11-10

Burkina radiologists discuss medical imaging innovations

Professionals met in Ouagadougou on November 6-7, 2026, to explore technological advancements for patient care and radiation safety.

Radiology professionals in Burkina Faso met in Ouagadougou on November 6 and 7, 2026, for scientific days focused on technological innovations. The Association of Professionals of Radiological Sciences and Medical Imaging of Burkina Faso (APSTRIM/BF) organized the event to discuss improving medical diagnosis, patient management, and controlling ionizing radiation doses.

Issaka Kaboré, vice-president of APSTRIM/BF, stated that medical imaging development relies on technological innovation, equipment quality, and radioprotection. He urged professionals to adopt new solutions for better diagnostics and patient care, emphasizing that modern equipment requires regular maintenance and control for reliable results. Kaboré also highlighted the health risks of ionizing radiation, calling for adherence to justification and optimization principles.

APSTRIM/BF President Alassane Guiro noted that the scientific days provided a platform to discuss challenges related to ionizing radiation and safety mechanisms for professionals. Discussions included artificial intelligence in radiology, dosimetry innovation, remote work, imaging's role in treating terrorism victims, and radiobiology, presented through three international scientific conferences and nine scientific communications.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso's health sector professionals are exploring new technologies to enhance patient diagnosis and safety in medical imaging procedures.

BAD trains Burkina Faso project managers on environmental rules

The African Development Bank is holding a five-day workshop in Ouagadougou for project staff.

The African Development Bank (BAD) is training project managers in Burkina Faso on new environmental and social safeguard rules. An information and capacity-building workshop is taking place in Ouagadougou from November 9 to 13, 2026. The training aims to equip staff involved in project preparation, execution, and monitoring with skills to manage environmental and social risks.

Daniel Ndoye, the BAD's country representative, stated that environmental and social safeguards improve project quality and prevent conflicts. The BAD revised its Integrated Safeguards System, effective May 31, 2024, to better identify and manage project risks. Participants will learn about impact assessments, resettlement plans, and stakeholder engagement.

The BAD supports projects in Burkina Faso across agriculture, energy, transport, water, and social development sectors. These projects face challenges related to land acquisition, resettlement, worker safety, and gender-based violence. The training is organized by the BAD's Department of Environmental and Social Safeguards and Compliance.

Why it mattersProject managers in Burkina Faso are being trained to better manage environmental and social risks in projects financed by the African Development Bank.

Two buses collide, killing six in Burkina Faso

A head-on collision between two passenger buses on the national road 1 near Ipendo killed six people.

A collision between two passenger buses on the national road 1 (RN1) in Burkina Faso resulted in six fatalities. The accident occurred in Ipendo, approximately seven kilometers from Sabou, during the night of Sunday, November 8, to Monday, November 9, 2026. One bus was traveling from Ouagadougou and the other from Bobo-Dioulasso when they crashed.

Six people died instantly in the crash, according to preliminary reports. Several other passengers sustained severe injuries and were rescued by firefighters from Koudougou. These injured individuals were then transported to health centers in Koudougou and Sabou for medical attention.

The accident took place on the RN1, a major transport artery. The specific cause of the collision was not detailed in the initial report.

Why it mattersSix people died and others were injured in a road accident involving two buses in Burkina Faso.

Education · 2026-11-10

Ex-soldiers share values with university students

Ouagadougou conference aimed to instill patriotism, discipline, and resilience in young people.

Former military officials shared their professional and human experiences with students at Université Thomas Sankara in Ouagadougou on November 9, 2026. The conference, organized by the Delegation for Veterans and Former Soldiers (DACAM), aimed to promote patriotism, discipline, resilience, and dedication to the nation among the youth. The event included a flag-raising ceremony, a baobab tree planting, and a panel discussion led by retired senior military officers.

Colonel William Aristide Nassidja Combari, delegate for veterans and former soldiers, stated that the initiative aims to leverage the experience of former military personnel to guide young people in their personal and professional lives. General Lougué Kouamé, with nearly 42 years of service, urged students to prioritize national interest over personal ambitions during the panel. The university president highlighted that the values of engagement, resilience, and patriotism are also guiding principles for university life, citing efforts to normalize academic years 2025-2026.

The DACAM, under the Ministry of War and Patriotic Defense, is responsible for managing veterans and former soldiers, defending their rights, and valuing their experience. Its motto, 'Honoring the past, illuminating the future,' inspired this intergenerational knowledge transfer. The university president also noted the contributions of teachers, administrative staff, and students to the institution's reputation and encouraged students to learn from the former soldiers' testimonies.

Why it mattersUniversity students in Ouagadougou heard from former military leaders about patriotism and national service.
