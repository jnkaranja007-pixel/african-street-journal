Burkina Faso and Mali agree to hold joint cooperation meeting

Mali's President Assimi Goïta visited Ouagadougou on November 5, 2026, to discuss bilateral ties and regional security.

Malian President Assimi Goïta visited Ouagadougou on November 5, 2026, meeting with Burkina Faso's Captain Ibrahim Traoré. The two leaders agreed to hold the 11th session of their joint cooperation commission in Ouagadougou to address shared challenges, including the fight against terrorism.

The visit aimed to strengthen fraternal ties and cooperation between Burkina Faso and Mali. Goïta and Traoré discussed bilateral, confederal, and international issues. They instructed their foreign ministers to arrange the joint commission meeting, which will focus on consolidating cooperation and finding common solutions to shared problems.

The leaders also reviewed progress within the Confederation of Sahel States (AES), particularly in defense, security, diplomacy, development, health, and education. Goïta invited Traoré for an official visit to Mali, with the date to be set diplomatically.

Why it mattersThe leaders of Burkina Faso and Mali met to strengthen bilateral cooperation and address shared security challenges.

Burkina Faso targets 90% financial inclusion by 2031

The nation aims to mobilize 44.2 billion FCFA to expand access to secure and adapted financial services.

Burkina Faso plans to reach 90% financial inclusion by 2031 through its second National Inclusive Finance Strategy (SNFI 2). This five-year plan, valued at 44.2 billion FCFA, aims to broaden access to financial services, accelerate digitalization, and enhance protection for vulnerable groups, including women and youth. Draft documents for the strategy and its three-year action plan were presented for validation at a national workshop in Ouagadougou on November 6, 2026.

Vieux Abdoul Rachid Soulama, Secretary General of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, stated that the strategy's core purpose is to bring financial services closer to excluded populations. He emphasized that inclusive finance should provide access to quality, secure services that are adapted to people's needs. The initiative seeks to ensure equitable access, considering disparities related to gender, age, social conditions, and vulnerability.

The SNFI 2 strategy builds on the success of the first strategy, which exceeded its target of 75% financial service usage between 2019 and 2023. The new plan aims to enable women to develop businesses, young people to fund entrepreneurial projects, and agricultural producers to secure resources for their campaigns.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso's citizens, particularly women, youth, and vulnerable populations, will gain better access to financial services like credit and savings accounts.

Burkina Faso bans unapproved dengue treatment

Faso FDA orders immediate removal of 'DENGUE- med' from sale due to lack of authorization and safety evaluation.

The Faso Food and Drug Administration (Faso FDA) has prohibited the sale and use of a product called 'DENGUE- med', marketed as a dengue treatment. The announcement on November 6, 2026, stated that the product currently circulating in Burkina Faso lacks market authorization and has not undergone any quality, efficacy, or safety assessments by a competent authority.

Health professionals and pharmacies must immediately remove 'DENGUE- med' from shelves, quarantine existing stock, and report quantities and supplier details to Faso FDA. Prescribers are asked to report any adverse effects observed in patients using the product, as inspection teams will recover affected stock and enforce administrative and criminal sanctions for non-compliance.

The public is advised not to purchase or use 'DENGUE- med'. For symptoms like fever, headache, or joint pain, individuals should seek care at a health center. The Faso FDA also warned against using aspirin and anti-inflammatories like ibuprofen for suspected dengue due to increased bleeding risks.

Why it mattersThe Faso FDA banned the sale and use of the unapproved 'DENGUE- med' product, impacting public health and patient treatment.

Soum and Bankui regions showcase artisanal crafts at SIAO

The 18th edition of the SIAO features traditional pottery from Bankui and woven goods from Soum.

The 18th edition of the SIAO features distinct displays from the Soum and Bankui regions. The Soum region's stand highlights traditional utilitarian objects and basketry, including a large collection of gourds and colorful woven straw mats. Visitors can also see straw shepherd hats, vibrant textiles, and paintings depicting local figures and Sahelian heritage.

The Bankui region's pavilion celebrates pottery with an impressive display of traditional black pottery and various sizes of terracotta jars. Their exhibit also includes bead jewelry, art objects, masks made from fibers, and Faso Dan fani traditional clothing. The juxtaposition of these regional exhibits demonstrates the complementary nature of African artisanal crafts.

The fine straw weaving from the Soum region and the terracotta pottery from the Bankui region exemplify the rich indigenous skills of Burkina Faso. These displays at the SIAO fair allow attendees to appreciate the depth and diversity of the nation's craft heritage.

Why it mattersArtisans and craftspeople from the Soum and Bankui regions are showcasing their traditional skills and products at the SIAO fair.

Business · 2026-11-07

Savane Médias marks 27 years with local product fair

The Ouagadougou media group celebrates anniversaries by promoting national production and local know-how.

Savane Médias is celebrating 27 years of Radio Savane FM and 8 years of Savane TV with a local product fair in Ouagadougou. The event, running from November 6 to November 14, aims to promote national production, showcase local skills, and encourage the consumption of Burkinabè products under the theme "Produce, transform and consume Burkinabè for true sovereignty."

Group General Manager Issaka Sourwèma stated that Savane Médias goes beyond information and communication, actively promoting economic and cultural actors through events. He highlighted the participation of listeners and viewers in the media group's work. The fair serves as a meeting point for producers, processors, and consumers, featuring local goods and artistic performances.

The organizing committee president, Yacouba Jacob Barry, emphasized the initiative's goal to promote Burkinabè self-sufficiency in production and transformation. The program includes regional days, a diaspora day, and a national day, along with a cycling race on November 15. Salifou Nikièma, president of the informal economy council, praised the fair for providing visibility and valuing local expertise, recalling the radio's historical role in information access for illiterate populations.

Why it mattersBurkinabè producers and consumers are directly affected by the fair promoting local goods and skills.
