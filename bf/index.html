Belgian Minister Visits Burkina Faso to Deepen Bilateral Ties

Belgium's Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Maxime Prévot arrived in Ouagadougou for a 72-hour visit aimed at strengthening cooperation based on sincerity and mutual.

Belgian Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Maxime Prévot arrived in Ouagadougou on August 26, 2026, for a 72-hour visit to reinforce bilateral cooperation with Burkina Faso. He was received by his Burkinabe counterpart, Karamoko Jean Marie Traore, and held discussions focused on the dynamism of their over 50-year-old partnership, largely managed through the Belgian development agency Enabel. Prévot emphasized his country's desire to continue cooperation without conditions, opting for direct engagement to understand the realities on the ground.

The Belgian minister stated his visit was a deliberate step to listen and gather information directly, rather than relying solely on reports from Brussels. This approach was welcomed by the Burkinabe Foreign Minister, who provided a detailed overview of the security crisis and the current government's efforts to address it through endogenous dynamics and sincere partnerships. Discussions also touched upon the AES Confederation and future cooperation with the European Union, headquartered in Brussels.

Concluding the talks, Prévot expressed his satisfaction and reaffirmed Belgium's commitment to investing "in Burkina Faso, with Burkina Faso, but not in place of Burkina Faso." This stance underscores a diplomatic relationship built on respect for sovereignty, strategic choices, sincerity, and transparency between the two nations. The visit signals a continued commitment to a partnership that prioritizes Burkina Faso's agency.

Why it mattersThe visit by Belgium's Foreign Minister aims to strengthen bilateral cooperation, impacting diplomatic relations and partnership frameworks between the two countries.

Burkina Faso Authority Sells 100 Tonnes Seized Rice

The Mobile Brigade for Economic Control and Fraud Repression will sell confiscated rice at a social price starting September 1, 2026, to eligible citizens who subscribe in advance.

The Mobile Brigade for Economic Control and Fraud Repression (BMCRF) announced it will sell 100 tonnes of seized rice at a social price. The sale begins September 1, 2026, and continues until the stock is depleted. Interested individuals must pre-register with the BMCRF and present valid identification.

This operation targets rice confiscated for clandestine storage, with sales organized by rice category. The BMCRF stated that controls are ongoing and violators face sanctions. Proceeds from the sale will be fully allocated to the Patriotic Support Fund (FSP).

The BMCRF is intensifying commercial inspections to clean up the market and protect consumers, working with agencies like ABNORM and the Laabal Brigade. These efforts aim to ensure compliance with regulations regarding pricing, speculation, and product quality, with potential penalties including business closures.

Why it mattersThis sale offers a chance for citizens to purchase rice at a reduced price, potentially easing household food costs.

Burkina Muslims Mark Prophet's Birth Amidst Peace Calls

The Maouloud celebration in Taslima saw increased attendance, signaling improved security and a focus on social cohesion and faith.

Muslims across Burkina Faso commemorated the Maouloud, the night of the birth of Prophet Mohammad, on August 25-26. The event, marked by Quranic readings, prayers, and public lectures, took place in Taslima, Zondoma province, with a notable increase in participation compared to previous years. This surge in attendance is attributed to an improvement in the security situation in the region, allowing for a more vibrant celebration.

Cheikh Abdoul Wahab Ouédraogo led the festivities in Taslima, urging attendees to deepen their faith and strengthen fraternal bonds as foundations for social cohesion and peace. He emphasized that Maouloud should inspire daily actions and teachings of the Prophet, rather than being a mere commemoration. Religious leaders highlighted the Prophet's life as an example of tolerance, forgiveness, and wisdom, advocating for his principles to guide societal conduct.

The celebration drew a large crowd of Muslim faithful, including administrative, customary, religious, and military officials, led by the high commissioner of Zondoma province. Scholars from within Burkina Faso and abroad participated, leading prayers and discussions focused on peace and unity. The event underscored a collective desire for faith to be a unifying force for peace, unity, and mutual support within the community.

Why it mattersMuslims in Burkina Faso are encouraged to draw inspiration from the Prophet's life for daily conduct and to foster peace and unity within their communities.

USFA Women's Team Leads CAF Champions League Group Ahead of Final Match

The Burkinabè team holds a strong position in Group A after a draw between rivals ASKO de Kara and Dynamique FC, with a decisive match against ASKO looming.

The USFA women's football team from Burkina Faso is poised to secure the top spot in Group A of the UFOA-B CAF Women's Champions League qualifiers. This follows a goalless draw between Togo's ASKO de Kara and Benin's Dynamique FC of Calavi on Wednesday evening in Ouagadougou. The result leaves USFA provisionally leading the group with three points and a +3 goal difference, having played one less match.

ASKO de Kara and Dynamique FC now both have one point each, trailing USFA by two points. The qualification for the semi-finals will be determined in the final group match on August 29th, where USFA will face ASKO de Kara. This encounter will decide which team advances alongside USFA, with the Togolese side needing a win to potentially overtake the Burkinabè leaders.

In Group B, the opening day saw Ghana's Ampem Darkoa Ladies defeat ASEC d'Abidjan 1-0, while Nigeria's Edo Queens secured a dominant 7-0 victory over Niger's AS Garde Nationale. Edo Queens leads Group B with three points and a +7 goal difference, followed by Ampem Darkoa Ladies with three points and a +1 difference.

Why it mattersThe USFA women's team's performance in the qualifiers directly impacts their potential progression in a continental championship, affecting national sporting pride and future opportunities.

Student Aids Mother in Sorghum Cultivation During University Break

Bagué Souleymane, a Modern Letters student, dedicates his university vacation to assisting his mother with farm work, focusing on sorghum production to support family needs.

Bagué Souleymane, a student of Modern Letters at the University Norbert-Zongo of Koudougou, is using his university break to help his mother with farm work. He is actively involved in cultivating sorghum with his younger siblings, aiming to contribute to the family's sustenance through agricultural production.

Souleymane explained that farm work is a significant activity for his family, but the start of the agricultural season is dependent on rainfall. He noted that the land is currently dry and they are eagerly awaiting rain to begin planting. Despite the challenges posed by the rainy season, the young student is committed to dedicating part of his vacation to agricultural activities alongside his family.

This involvement highlights the desire of some young people in rural areas to balance their studies with agricultural pursuits, particularly during university holidays. The effort underscores a commitment to family needs and rural livelihoods, even amidst academic responsibilities and environmental uncertainties.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected as the student's participation in sorghum cultivation highlights the reliance on rainfall for the agricultural season's effective start.
