Burkina Faso citizens rally for President Traoré

Hundreds gathered in Bobo-Dioulasso on October 31, 2026, to express support for the RPP and the president.

Hundreds of people gathered in Bobo-Dioulasso on Saturday, October 31, 2026, to show support for the Révolution progressiste populaire (RPP) and President Ibrahim Traoré. The Coordination nationale des associations de veille citoyenne (CNAVC), Houet section, organized the meeting at Tiéfo Amoro square, drawing diverse groups including students, farmers, and public servants.

Speakers representing various sectors voiced their backing for the government's initiatives in education, security, and agricultural development. Delegations from Tuy and Kénédougou specifically acknowledged the efforts of the defense forces and VDP volunteers. Lieutenant-colonel Lassané Porgo, speaking for the fighting forces, thanked the population for their support in combating terrorism and urged continued cohesion.

The CNAVC-Houet's general secretary, Moussa Traoré, read a motion reaffirming total adherence to the RPP's ideals of national sovereignty and endogenous development. The motion also expressed solidarity with those affected by the security crisis and encouraged responsible citizen mobilization against disinformation.

Why it mattersHundreds of citizens in Bobo-Dioulasso reaffirmed their support for President Ibrahim Traoré and the RPP, showing solidarity with national development and security efforts.

Burkina Faso army marks 66 years with 56 awards

The Minister of War praised soldiers fighting terrorism and honoured 56 individuals with distinctions.

Burkina Faso's National Armed Forces (FAN) celebrated their 66th anniversary on Sunday, November 1, 2026, with a ceremony in Ouagadougou. The event included a military parade and the awarding of honours to 56 individuals for their service. The FAN have existed from November 1, 1960, to November 1, 2026.

General Célestin Simporé, the Minister of War, commended the efforts of soldiers defending the nation and paid tribute to those who died fighting for territorial integrity. He expressed confidence in overcoming terrorism, stating that the armed forces have gained the upper hand against an enemy he described as being in disarray. Among those honoured was VDP Issaka Guira, who received a medal for his bravery.

Issaka Guira, a volunteer who joined in June 2023, was recognised for his active participation in counter-terrorism missions, including operations to liberate the Ouahigouya-Titao route. He sustained severe injuries, losing a leg and his left arm and suffering facial disfigurement, after encountering an improvised explosive device during a mission in Youba village.

Why it mattersThe 66th anniversary celebration and the awarding of 56 honours to military personnel and volunteers highlight the ongoing fight against terrorism.

Paul Daumont gains time in Tour du Faso

Burkinabè cyclist Paul Daumont is now 44 seconds behind the yellow jersey after the third stage.

Burkinabè cyclist Paul Daumont finished second in the 3rd stage of the 37th Tour du Faso on November 1, 2026, between Koudougou and Boromo. This performance allowed him to reduce his deficit to the yellow jersey leader to 44 seconds, from 50 seconds previously. The national team now holds third place overall in the general classification.

The Banque Commerciale du Burkina (BCB) sponsored this stage, with marketing director Nadège Traoré stating the bank supports national sport. Boromo branch manager Faye Manse expressed pride in Daumont's performance and the bank's visibility, noting the BCB is a patriotic institution supporting national engagement.

The Tour du Faso continues with a circuit race in Bobo-Dioulasso on November 2, 2026. Despite a crash involving the yellow jersey holder, Tom Wijfje, in the final meters, Paul Daumont confirmed his position as the top Burkinabè rider and also secured the green jersey for points classification.

Why it mattersBurkinabè cyclists are competing for the yellow jersey and other distinctions in the Tour du Faso, a national sporting event.

Man gets life for acid attack on partner in Dori

A Dori court sentenced a man to life in prison for brutally attacking his pregnant partner after she refused an abortion.

A man received a life sentence on October 30, 2026, for attacking his partner with a knife and acid in Dori. The court found him guilty of criminal aggravated assault after the woman refused to have an abortion.

The victim was lured to an isolated spot on the night of August 31, 2026, by her partner who wanted her to terminate her pregnancy. When she refused, he beat her, stabbed her back, and poured acid on her face, causing severe disfigurement. She was found unconscious the next morning in Torodi village, about 10 kilometers from Dori, and taken to the regional hospital.

Police informed the prosecutor on September 1, 2026, and an investigation began. The victim regained consciousness and identified her partner as the attacker. He was arrested on October 15, 2026, and placed in pre-trial detention before his trial on October 30.

Burkina Faso firms urged to secure digital identities

A digital lab in Ouagadougou highlighted risks to domain names and online branding for businesses.

50 participants gathered online and in Ouagadougou on October 29, 2026, for the first DNS & Brand Lab, urging Burkinabè companies to protect their domain names and digital identities. Organized by Digitales Solidaires with national and international partners, the event focused on domain management, cybersecurity, and brand protection.

Rodrigue Guiguemdé, president of Digitales Solidaires, stressed that domain names are strategic assets like brands, warning that companies risk losing website and email access if they don't control their domain registration. Izaï Toé of ABDI emphasized promoting the national ".bf" extension and digital sovereignty, while Abdoul Aziz Kabré of Internet Society Burkina Faso called for rigorous protection of digital assets.

The lab highlighted the importance of verifying domain ownership, controlling access, and monitoring renewal dates. Discussions also centered on the " .bf " national extension as a tool for Burkinabè businesses to assert their local identity and build a trusted online presence.

Why it mattersBurkinabè businesses face risks of losing control over their websites and professional emails if their domain names are not properly managed.
