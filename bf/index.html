Burkina Faso opens international craft fair

The Salon international de l'artisanat de Ouagadougou (SIAO) will run from October 30 to November 8, 2026.

The Salon international de l'artisanat de Ouagadougou (SIAO) officially opened its doors on Friday, October 30, 2026, in Ouagadougou. President Ibrahim Traoré presided over the opening ceremony for the event, which will run for 10 days until November 8, 2026. The fair aims to showcase African artisans and their creations, fostering discussions on the development and promotion of the artisanal sector.

Under the theme "African craftsmanship and industrialization: what synergy for better organization and valorization of local products and cultural heritage?", the SIAO seeks to provide a platform for artisans. President Traoré expressed his commitment to advancing African craftsmanship towards modernization and industrialization, emphasizing the importance of mastering materials and artistic skills. He encouraged artisans to be creative and persistent.

Minister of Industry, Commerce and Artisanship, Serge Gnaniodem Poda, read the president's speech, highlighting the vision of the Popular Progressive Revolution to make craftsmanship a tool for sovereignty and individual fulfillment. He noted that since its creation in 1988, SIAO has become a significant event for African creativity, representing the continent's intelligence, identity, and ability to transform materials into beauty and wealth.

Why it mattersBurkinabè artisans and their cultural heritage are directly affected by the opening of the SIAO, which aims to promote their work and foster industrialization.

Burkina Faso prime minister visits bronze art exhibition in Moscow

Rimtalba Jean Emmanuel Ouédraogo viewed 20 handcrafted pieces made using the ancestral lost-wax technique.

Burkina Faso's Prime Minister, Rimtalba Jean Emmanuel Ouédraogo, visited a bronze art exhibition in Moscow on Friday, October 30, 2026. The exhibition, held at the All-Russian Museum of Decorative Arts, showcased traditional Burkinabè bronze art as part of the third Russia-Africa Summit's cultural program. This initiative, conceived by President Ibrahim Traoré, aims to introduce Russian audiences to the work of Burkinabè bronze casters.

The exhibition features 20 contemporary Burkinabè sculptures, each handcrafted using the ancestral lost-wax technique. Dmitri Savelyev, Director General of the Center for Public Diplomacy, highlighted the project's importance for bilateral relations, stating that cultural understanding builds a foundation for closer cooperation. Prime Minister Ouédraogo thanked the organizers and museum for presenting Burkinabè cultural heritage, emphasizing the role of cultural exchange in strengthening ties beyond politics and diplomacy.

The exhibition previously toured Novossibirsk and Kazan before arriving in Moscow, attracting tens of thousands of Russian visitors. Prime Minister Ouédraogo noted that this visit coincides with the opening of the 18th International Crafts Fair in Ouagadougou, where Russia is a participant. He expressed confidence that such events foster stronger cultural and artistic connections between Burkina Faso and Russia.

Why it mattersBurkinabè cultural heritage is being presented to a Russian audience, strengthening diplomatic and artistic ties between the two nations.

Burkina Faso trains 40 specialists on strabismus care

The Burkinabe Society of Ophthalmology is conducting a training session in Ouagadougou to improve the management of eye misalignment.

40 specialists, including ophthalmologists and fourth-year medical students, began a training session on strabismus management on October 30, 2026, in Ouagadougou. The training, held at the CHU of Bogodogo and funded by the NGO Light for the World, will continue until November 3, 2026. Strabismus, an eye misalignment, can lead to amblyopia or a "lazy eye" if not treated early, especially in children.

The training aims to enhance participants' knowledge of strabismus diagnosis, evaluation, and treatment options, including the rehabilitation of strabismic amblyopia. Dr. Toé Roland, secretary-general of the Burkinabe Society of Ophthalmology, stated that the session addresses a real need in health facilities and will help practitioners deepen their understanding and correct misconceptions about strabismus treatment. The session is led by Dr. Amadou Alfa Bio Issifou, an associate professor from the University of Parakou, Benin.

Dr. Toé Roland also highlighted the issue of under-reporting strabismus cases, which hinders a full understanding of the problem's scale. Difficulties in early detection by some health professionals further complicate the situation. The training is expected to improve the identification of anomalies, selection of appropriate treatments, and patient follow-up.

Why it mattersChildren in Burkina Faso with strabismus risk reduced vision and developmental issues if the condition is not detected and treated early.

Manga schools receive garden fencing materials

10 schools in Manga received fencing materials to secure their school gardens and support canteen food supplies.

The special delegation of Manga commune provided fencing materials to 10 schools on October 28, 2026. This donation aims to help the schools establish their gardens and improve food self-sufficiency for school canteens. The materials include wire mesh, posts, and fasteners, with the goal of enabling local production for school meals.

Bérenger Bihoun, president of the special delegation, stated that this is the first step, with actual garden development, production, and harvesting to follow. He urged school officials to use the materials effectively to supply canteens with local produce. This initiative arose from a request made by the director of Communal School B in 2025, who highlighted difficulties in securing their school garden against animal damage.

Geneviève Kambiré, director of Communal School B, expressed her satisfaction, noting that the materials address a pressing need and will help organize gardens for better production benefiting students and staff. The delegation plans to follow up on the effective development and use of these school gardens.

Why it matters10 schools in Burkina Faso received fencing materials to secure their gardens, which will help supply school canteens with local produce.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Burkina Faso reinstates death penalty

The People's Legislative Assembly voted unanimously to bring back capital punishment for terrorism and treason.

Burkina Faso has reintroduced the death penalty for crimes including terrorism and treason. On October 20, the 71 unelected members of the People's Legislative Assembly voted unanimously to pass the bill, which was first adopted in December 2025.

Leader Ibrahim Traoré declared, "Sentimentalism is over," as the assembly approved the reinstatement of capital punishment. This decision aligns Burkina Faso with its allies in the Alliance des États du Sahel.

Non-governmental organizations have expressed concern that this move could threaten critical voices within the country. The death penalty was previously removed from the penal code but has now been reinstated.

Why it mattersCitizens in Burkina Faso now face capital punishment for offenses such as terrorism and treason.
