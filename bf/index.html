AES Parliament convenes first session in Niamey

The confederal parliament of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) officially began its operations on Monday in Niamey, Niger, with 45 parliamentarians from Burkina Faso, Mali, and.

The inaugural session of the confederal parliament of the AES took place at the Mahatma Gandhi International Conference Center in Niamey. The event was presided over by Niger's Prime Minister, Lamine Zeine Ali Mahaman, representing President General Abdourahamane Tiani. The session saw the installation of 45 confederal parliamentarians from the three member states.

Dr. Ousmane Bougouma, President of the Confederal Sessions of the AES Parliaments, opened the inaugural session and subsequently installed the two vice-presidents: Malick Diaw, President of Mali's National Transitional Council, and Dr. Mamoudou Harouna Djingarey, President of Niger's Consultative Council for Refoundation. The President of Faso, Captain Ibrahim Traoré, also sent a message that was read during the session.

The establishment of this parliamentary body marks a new phase for the AES, aiming to represent the voices of the people from Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger within the confederation. Presidents of the Parliaments of Chad and Togo were also present as guests, offering their congratulations and wishes for success and peace to the members.

Why it mattersThe establishment of the AES confederal parliament directly affects citizens of Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger by creating a new legislative body intended to represent their interests within the confederation.

Burkina Faso Muslims Celebrate Maouloud with Prayers for Peace

Muslim faithful across Burkina Faso, including in the Liptako region, observed Maouloud 2026 with religious teachings and collective invocations for national unity and social.

Muslim communities in Burkina Faso commemorated Maouloud 2026, the birth of Prophet Mohammad, on the night of August 25 to 26. In Dori, the capital of the Liptako region, many faithful gathered at the city's grand mosque for a vigil of religious instruction focused on the Prophet's life and work, according to the Agence d'Information du Burkina.

The celebration, marked by spiritual fervor, included individual and collective prayers for the return of peace, unity, and social cohesion in Burkina Faso. Imam Yaya Mamoudou Cissé led the ritual, emphasizing the Prophet's teachings on tolerance, understanding, and solidarity, and urging attendees to embody these values daily.

Religious leaders reminded the faithful that Maouloud is a night of gratitude and reflection on the Prophet's exemplary life, which serves as a model for all Muslims. While not obligatory like Tabaski or Ramadan, the observance is an opportunity to give thanks to God and honor Prophet Mohammad, as stated by Cheikh Abdoul Aziz Ouédraogo of Tansalgha.

Why it mattersThe event directly affects Muslims in Burkina Faso by providing a communal opportunity for spiritual reflection and collective prayer for peace and national unity.

President Traoré urges Sahel parliamentarians to prioritize regional interests

Captain Ibrahim Traoré, President of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES), called on confederate deputies to place the general interest of the AES above partisan and national.

The first session of the Parliament of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) opened this week, with President Ibrahim Traoré exhorting its 45 deputies to defend the superior interest of the people. Speaking through Ousmane Bougma, president of the Legislative Assembly of the People, Traoré emphasized unity and bold decisions for the newly formed confederation.

The session, running from August twenty-fourth to August twenty-ninth, 2026, aims to establish a framework for parliamentary cooperation among the three member states: Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger. These nations formed the AES in August 2024, seven months after their departure from ECOWAS in January 2024, to consolidate efforts against terrorism, defend sovereignty, and promote endogenous development.

The initial phase of the session, concluding on August twenty-fifth, focused on adopting the session's program and committee work. The ordinary session, beginning August twenty-sixth, will be presided over by General Abdourahamane Tiani, President of Niger, at the Mahatma Gandhi International Conference Center in Niamey.

Why it mattersThe formation of the AES Parliament directly affects citizens of Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger by creating a new legislative body intended to address regional security and development, potentially impacting their daily lives through new policies and cooperation.

ARSN inspects Clinique les Oliviers radiology facilities

The National Authority for Radioprotection and Nuclear Safety (ARSN) conducted a pre-authorization inspection of radiology facilities at Clinique les Oliviers in Ouagadougou.

A team from the National Authority for Radioprotection and Nuclear Safety (ARSN), led by National Director Belko Massi Barry, inspected the radiology facilities at Clinique les Oliviers in Ouagadougou on Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, 2026. This pre-authorization mission aims to verify the conformity of the installations, the radiology equipment, and protection devices before their authorization for use.

The inspection is part of ARSN's control missions and the application of law number zero-three-two-two-thousand-twelve-AN of June eighth, 2012, concerning nuclear safety, security, and guarantees. It follows an authorization request from Clinique les Oliviers to operate its standard radiology equipment. Inspectors examined the radiology room's conformity, collective protection barriers, individual protective equipment, and personnel dosimetric surveillance devices.

Quality control tests were also performed to verify the technical characteristics of the equipment and ensure its use complies with radioprotection requirements. The inspection team noted the presence of signage informing about the risks of ionizing radiation. The clinic's deputy general director, Tanga Ouédraogo, stated that this process will enhance patient and worker protection and contribute to public well-being.

Why it mattersPatients and medical workers in Burkina Faso are directly affected as this inspection ensures the safety of radiology equipment and procedures in a healthcare facility.

Red Cross Director Reaffirms Commitment to Burkina Faso Dialogue

The International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) regional director, Mamoudou Sow, visited Burkina Faso to strengthen collaboration with authorities and adapt cooperation to.

Mamoudou Sow, the new regional director for Africa of the International Committee of the Red Cross, chose Burkina Faso for his first field visit to understand the humanitarian situation and enhance cooperation. Mr. Sow stated the ICRC aims to introduce a new cooperation dynamic that respects Africa's increasing sovereignty and new perspectives, emphasizing consultation and equal relations.

Burkina Faso's Minister of Foreign Affairs, Karamoko Jean Marie Traoré, welcomed the ICRC's new approach, calling it realistic and appreciating the commitment to open and sincere dialogue. The minister acknowledged past challenges in mutual understanding and collaboration with humanitarian partners, outlining the government's updated requirements for international cooperation.

Mr. Traoré highlighted the ICRC's significant role in managing Burkina Faso's humanitarian crisis, noting the organization's inspiring work and its contribution to reassuring the population. He stressed the importance of maintaining the trust built with the ICRC to achieve shared objectives in the country.

Why it mattersThis development affects humanitarian aid recipients in Burkina Faso by potentially reshaping how international assistance is delivered and adjusted to local needs and government priorities.
