Burkina Faso hosts 4th Sino-Burkinabè Film Week

Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso will screen 14 films from November 12-15, 2026.

The fourth edition of the Sino-Burkinabè Film Week will take place in Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso from November 12 to 15, 2026. This cultural event, formerly known as the "Chinese Film Week," will now highlight the cultural cooperation between Burkina Faso and China. 14 films, seven from each country, will be shown across 12 screening sites, with nine in Ouagadougou and three in Bobo-Dioulasso.

Organizers announced the event's name change during a press conference in Ouagadougou on November 10, 2026. François Akonabou Adianga, head of the FESPACO department, stated the films will explore themes of bravery, the fight against terrorism, love, and integrity, with a new panel on cooperation. Entry to all screenings is free.

Zhao Shiqian, cultural cooperation attaché at the Chinese embassy, noted the event's expansion and announced a skills transfer seminar in China in 2027. The Film Week is jointly organized by the Chinese Embassy in Burkina Faso and the Burkina Faso Cinematography and Audiovisual Agency.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso citizens can attend free film screenings celebrating Sino-Burkinabè cultural ties.

Burkina Faso graduates 78 information professionals

Prime Minister urges new graduates to combat disinformation and uphold truth.

Ouagadougou on Tuesday welcomed 78 new graduates from the 38th class of the Institute of Information and Communication Technologies (ISTIC). The graduates, whose class is named "Dimaaku" meaning "integrity" in Fulfulde, were urged by Prime Minister Rimtalba Jean-Emmanuel Ouédraogo to prioritize fact-checking and truth in their professional work.

Presiding over the ceremony themed "Inform to liberate and communicate to build," the Prime Minister called on the new professionals to combat disinformation and seek truth amidst Burkina Faso's security challenges. He encouraged them to cultivate doubt, verify facts, and transmit information faithfully to enlighten the public and build trust.

The graduating class includes 32 at Level A (journalists, communication advisors, and IT engineers), 45 at Level B (journalism assistants, communication assistants, and IT technicians), and one at Level C (specialized communication agent). The Director General of ISTIC, Dr. Alizèta Ouoba, highlighted the institute's mission to train competent professionals for the information sector and national development.

Why it mattersThe graduation of 78 information professionals from ISTIC equips Burkina Faso with new personnel to counter disinformation and contribute to national development.

Agriculture · 2026-11-11

Burkina Faso stakeholders seek recognition for biopesticides

A two-day national workshop in Koudougou on November 10 and 11, 2026, will draft a multi-stakeholder roadmap on approving biopesticides.

Officials, researchers, private firms, farmer organisations and civil society met in Koudougou on November 10 and 11, 2026, to discuss how Burkina Faso can formally recognise and regulate biopesticides. The national workshop is organised by the National Council for Organic Agriculture (Cnabio) with the RADIUS project of Joseph Ki-Zerbo University and the RESI-2P and GP-SAEP programmes.

Seydou Savadogo, vice-president of Cnabio and head of a cooperative of agroecological input makers and suppliers, said the event follows about 15 years of campaigning by agroecology actors. He said that after the adoption of a regulatory framework for biofertilisers in November 2025, producers want a similar recognition for biopesticides, which they also call bioprotectors. Antoine Sanon, coordinator of the RADIUS project, said progress depends on closer ties between research, producers and public decision-makers.

Participants will review the pesticide control law, number 026-2017/AN, and take stock of how biopesticides are used, produced and distributed. They will also study approval practices in neighbouring countries and aim to produce a roadmap and the basis of a policy note to support advocacy for biopesticides.

Why it mattersMakers and suppliers of agroecological inputs, along with producers, want biopesticides officially recognised so they can be promoted and used in place of chemical products.

Health · 2026-11-11

470 women screened in Koper for cancers

A mobile clinic offered breast self-examination training, mammograms, and cervical cancer screenings.

A mobile clinic provided health services to 470 women in Koper on Thursday, October 22, 2026. The clinic offered training on breast self-examination and screenings for female cancers at the Babora health center. This initiative was part of the Djôrô mobile clinic's visit to the commune.

During the event, 87 women aged 40 and over received mammograms, and 197 women were screened for precancerous cervical lesions using visual inspection methods. Additionally, 38 pregnant women received free obstetric ultrasounds, and 30 women had gynecological consultations. Zakaria Dao, the special delegate president of Koper commune, welcomed the medical team and urged women to cooperate.

Dr. Lassina Traoré, a gynecologist coordinating the mobile clinic, praised the women's turnout. Prisca Somda, a pregnant beneficiary, expressed gratitude for the free obstetric ultrasound, stating it was previously unaffordable. Beli Benjamin Zongo, the chief health post nurse, also highlighted the value of on-site ultrasounds for pregnant women.

Why it mattersWomen in Koper received free health screenings and consultations, including mammograms and cervical cancer checks.
