Nuru Burkina Faso trains agricultural cooperative leaders in Kourwéogo

Cooperative leaders from Bazèga and Kourwéogo met in Tindatenga to swap methods on organic fertiliser and soybean growing and processing.

Nuru Burkina Faso (NBF) held a meeting on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, in Tindatenga, in the commune of Boussé, for the board members of agricultural cooperatives in its network. The event aimed to strengthen their organisational and operational capacities and the cohesion between the groups. Cooperative leaders came from Bazèga province and several communes of Kourwéogo province.

Sévérin Sama, a member of NBF's executive bureau, said the activity forms part of a continuing effort to improve the cohesion and professionalism of the network. He said some member cooperatives have proven practices in governance, stock management, loan recovery, resilience and the inclusion of women, and that less experienced groups could learn from them. Community agents, agricultural service representatives and partner associations also took part.

Kabré Zouma Sawadogo of the Wend la Songda cooperative described the benefits of organic manure for soil fertility and for sale as a natural fertiliser. Nabaloum Yacouba, from Kombissiri, shared his cooperative's experience growing soybeans, which can be sold raw or processed. Idrissa Compaoré presented options for adding value to soybeans through processing. The session was led by the activity coordinator, Renaud B. Nagalo.

Why it mattersCooperative members who attended can apply the soybean and organic fertiliser methods shared at the meeting to their own production and sales.

Sahel alliance ministers review second-year roadmap in Ouagadougou

Ministers from Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger met on 11 and 12 November to assess progress across defence, diplomacy and development.

Ministers from Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger met in Ouagadougou on 11 and 12 November 2026 for a session of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) council of ministers. The meeting, opened by Defence Minister Célestin Simporé, reviewed how the second-year roadmap of the confederation has been carried out.

Simporé called for more pooling of resources, including intelligence, and greater strategic autonomy for member states. On diplomacy, he said the three countries want to speak with one voice internationally while diversifying their partnerships. On development, he stressed economic integration, easier trade and the use of natural resources, which he said should create lasting opportunities for young people.

The ministers are due to produce a synthesis note on progress in each of the three pillars. Simporé described the session as a strategic review ahead of the next meeting of the confederation's heads of state. He also thanked Mali for its support over Burkinabè gold prospectors expelled from Guinea.

Why it mattersThe next heads of state meeting will decide the alliance's direction, and the synthesis note will set out what each member state has delivered so far.

Koubri communities prepare for first unity week

Residents of Koubri are mobilizing for a week of events from December 8-12 to strengthen fraternity and unity.

Communities in Koubri, Burkina Faso, are preparing for the first communal week of living together and social cohesion, scheduled for December 8 to 12, 2026. Residents, including Burkinabè and foreign nationals, met on November 6 to commit to making the event a platform for reinforcing fraternity and unity. The meeting, chaired by Koubri's Special Delegation President Samadé Léonard Gougou, included representatives from various community groups, youth and women's associations, traders, and local service chiefs.

During the information and exchange meeting, community representatives presented their cultural values, such as traditional attire, local dishes, dances, and storytelling, which they plan to showcase. These elements are seen as key to promoting solidarity, tolerance, and unity among the populations. The week's program includes a 'week of communities,' a day of excellence, and a day of accountability.

Proposals to enhance the event's visibility include an opening parade, traditional artist performances, and joking-kinship competitions. Leaders for each main activity have been designated to oversee preparations and coordination. Participants reaffirmed their commitment to making this inaugural edition a moment of meeting, sharing, and strengthening bonds between Koubri's diverse communities.

Why it mattersResidents of Koubri will participate in a week of cultural events from December 8-12 aimed at fostering unity and solidarity.
