Mali and Niger celebrate Sahelian fraternity at Ouagadougou craft fair

The 18th edition of the Salon international de l'artisanat de Ouagadougou dedicated a special day to Mali and Niger, celebrating shared heritage and economic ties.

Mali and Niger, special guests at the 18th Salon international de l'artisanat de Ouagadougou (SIAO), celebrated their dedicated day on Wednesday, November 4, 2026. Authorities and craft sector representatives from the three Alliance des États du Sahel (AES) countries reaffirmed their commitment to strengthening economic, cultural, and professional exchanges.

The event, focused on showcasing local products and awarding artisans, highlighted fraternity and integration within the AES. Alassane Ouédraogo, secretary general of Burkina Faso's Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Artisans, welcomed the Malian and Nigerien delegations, noting their participation gave the SIAO edition a special dimension.

Ouédraogo stated that Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger are linked by common history, traditions, and aspirations for development, with this fraternity now finding new momentum through the AES confederation. He urged concrete adoption of this vision by populations and economic actors, particularly through trade and private sector initiatives.

Why it mattersThe event directly affects artisans and craft sector representatives from Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger by promoting their work and fostering economic cooperation.

Burkina Faso not informed of Guinean artisanal mining expulsions

Thousands of Burkinabe artisanal miners were forced to leave Guinea's Siguiri region.

Thousands of young Burkinabe artisanal miners were forced to leave Guinea's Siguiri administrative region. Guinean authorities cited environmental reasons and illegal activity for suspending artisanal mining operations and expelling workers. Burkinabe Minister of Security Mahamadou Sana stated that Burkina Faso was not officially informed of the operation.

Guinean officials claim artisanal mining pollutes land and water and threatens local populations. However, the Burkinabe government believes a lack of official consultation between the two nations contributed to the crisis. This situation has sparked debate on social media, with differing views on artisanal mining.

The expulsion of Burkinabe citizens from Guinea highlights a lack of diplomatic tact, according to the report. A high-level meeting or official communication between the two countries could have provided a more humane resolution to the crisis.

Why it mattersThousands of Burkinabe citizens were expelled from Guinea, impacting their ability to work and live in the region.

Education · 2026-11-06

Burkina Faso school councils meet on education reforms

Bané school leaders gathered on November 4, 2026, to discuss their role in implementing educational changes.

School council presidents and headteachers in Bané, Burkina Faso, met on Tuesday, November 4, 2026, to discuss their role in implementing educational reforms. The meeting, led by the head of the Basic Education Circumscription (CEB) Théodore ZOUGMORE, took place at Bané Mixed School. ZOUGMORE credited the school councils' support for the 97% success rate in the 2025-2026 primary school certificate exams.

Primary education supervisor Bangré Locré urged council members to create action plans for their schools by November 15, 2026, and to manage finances transparently. He also called for their involvement in preventing and managing risks from terrorist incursions by sharing information. The reforms discussed included vocational training, civic education, bilingual teaching, and the responsible use of digital technologies.

The reforms aim to reveal students' aptitudes for future employment through practical activities like school gardens and livestock. Civic education will focus on respect for laws and national values, including wearing helmets and respecting national symbols. Bilingual education, using national languages, is intended to improve learning and preserve linguistic heritage, with councils encouraged to promote traditional attire.

Why it mattersSchool leaders and students in Bané are affected by new educational reforms focused on vocational training, civic education, and bilingual instruction.
