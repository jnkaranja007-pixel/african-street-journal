Burkina Faso and Turkey discuss defense pact

Officials from both nations met in Ankara to explore enhanced cooperation in security, health, and economic sectors.

Burkina Faso and Turkey are negotiating a defense and security agreement following the first session of political consultations held in Ankara on October 20, 2026. The discussions, led by Burkina Faso's Ambassador Hermann Yirigouin Toé and Turkey's Ambassador Volkan Isikci, covered a broad spectrum of bilateral cooperation, including education, health, agriculture, industrial development, mining, and energy.

In the health sector, Burkina Faso's delegation praised Turkey's support for national renal transplant training and requested a fast-track visa system for patients needing emergency medical evacuation. The aim is to reduce medical expatriation and strengthen domestic healthcare infrastructure. On regional security, Ambassador Toé highlighted the geopolitical shifts in the Sahel, including the formation of the Sahel States Confederation (AES), and urged Turkey to partner in combating terrorism.

Ambassador Isikci reaffirmed Turkey's commitment to supporting Burkina Faso's security, sovereignty, and territorial integrity. The Turkish delegation expressed a desire for a formal defense and security accord and proposed a partnership convention between the diplomatic institutes of both countries. Both sides also acknowledged the potential for increased economic and trade exchanges.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso's government is seeking to bolster its defense capabilities and improve healthcare access for its citizens through international agreements.

Burkina Faso police adapt to evolving threats

Ouagadougou hosts annual police commissioners' conference to address rising crime and digital offenses.

The sixth annual conference of police commissioners opened in Ouagadougou on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, under the theme "The Popular Progressive Revolution, a lever for reforming the National Police: adaptability, challenges and prospects." The meeting aims to assess the institution's achievements and formulate recommendations to enhance service efficiency.

National Police Director General Thierry Dofizouho Tuina stated that the conference is particularly important given the evolving threats, diversifying criminal phenomena, and the need to continuously adapt institutional responses to territorial realities. He emphasized that the meeting will critically examine the National Police's successes, shortcomings, and challenges, aligning with the ideals of the popular progressive revolution.

Minister of State for Territorial Administration, Émile Zerbo, representing the Prime Minister, highlighted that security is essential for institutional stability, population protection, and economic development. He stressed that the National Police's transformation must focus on adaptability to changing threats, performance in meeting public needs, and exemplary leadership.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso's police are adapting their strategies to counter increasing and diversifying criminal threats, including digital offenses.

Poura CEB teacher receives motorcycle for perfect exam results

Bouma Nebié was awarded a motorcycle and cash for achieving a 100% pass rate for his 59 students in the 2026 CEP exams.

The top-performing teacher in Poura's basic education district (CEB), Bouma Nebié, received a motorcycle and a financial envelope on October 17, 2026. Nebié, from Poura-Mine D school, achieved a 100% success rate for his 59 students in the 2026 Certificat d’études primaires (CEP) exams. The award ceremony, held during the CEB's excellence day, also recognized top students and other outstanding teachers.

Fatim Ouedraogo, from Poura-Mine « B » school, was named the best student in the CEB with an average of 9.06. Top students in CP1 through CM1 classes each received a school kit, while the top three CM2 students were awarded a bicycle and a school kit. Three internally displaced students were also encouraged with school kits, and intermediate class teachers received envelopes and congratulatory letters.

The best school award went to Poura-Mine D, which also achieved a 100% CEP pass rate under the leadership of Sibiri Zoma. The event was sponsored by local mining operator Kibsa Sawadogo and attended by Poura's special delegation president, Momini Sawadogo, who pledged continued support for educational goals. The ceremony featured student performances and a show by artist Desko 1er.

Why it mattersTeachers and students in Poura are directly affected by the recognition and rewards for academic performance.
