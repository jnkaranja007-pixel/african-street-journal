Agriculture · 2026-11-18

Coris Foundation gives agricultural VDPs 360 tricycles

The 500 million FCFA donation is intended to help the volunteers travel to fields and move harvests to storage sites.

The Coris Foundation handed 360 tricycles to agricultural Volunteers for the Defence of the Homeland (VDPs) at the National Office of Major Projects in Burkina Faso on Tuesday, 17 November 2026. The donation is valued at 500 million FCFA and supports the presidential initiative for agricultural production and food self-sufficiency.

Drissa Bassinga, the beneficiaries’ representative, said the vehicles would ease travel to fields and transport produce to storage sites. The tricycles will be distributed among cooperatives formed after the agricultural VDPs’ training, according to the report.

Emmanuel Sawadogo, president of the Coris Foundation, said the donation responds to the president’s call for support for his initiatives. Inoussa Ouédraogo, technical coordinator of the presidential initiative, welcomed private-sector involvement, saying it helps create jobs for young people.

Why it mattersThe agricultural VDPs are to receive the tricycles through their cooperatives, and Bassinga said they will use them to carry produce to storage sites.

Education · 2026-11-18

Burkina Faso trains 55 education workers in inclusive teaching and school safety

The four-day Ouagadougou course covers gender-sensitive teaching, preventing gender-based violence in schools and digital tools.

Burkina Faso has opened a four-day training in Ouagadougou for 55 education-sector workers on gender-sensitive teaching, safe learning environments and classroom technology. The course runs from 17 to 20 November 2026.

Participants from the National Institute for Training Education Personnel, the École normale supérieure and central education ministry bodies are studying gender-based violence prevention in schools alongside digital teaching tools. The initiative is supported by Japan’s government and delivered with the UNESCO International Institute for Capacity Building in Africa.

The course is part of a programme launched in March 2024 that covers Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Mali, Mauritania, Niger and Chad. Félicité Kou-nangue of the African Union’s International Centre for Girls’ and Women’s Education in Africa cited 2024 primary-completion rates of 59.9% for girls and 49.3% for boys.

Why it mattersThe 55 participating education workers are due to complete the course on 20 November, after training in safer and more inclusive teaching.

Business · 2026-11-18

Burkina Faso chamber visits sugar and flour plants in Banfora

SOSUCO's director asked the chamber to help find partners to modernise its factory and expand the land under cultivation.

A delegation from Burkina Faso's Chamber of Commerce and Industry, led by its president, Roland Achille Sow, visited the SOSUCO sugar company and the MINOFA flour mill in Banfora on Monday 16 November 2026. The visits were part of an industrial tour scheduled in Bobo-Dioulasso and Banfora.

SOSUCO director Djakaridja Hema Ouattara told the delegation the company needed to renovate its factory and increase the area available for cultivation. He asked the chamber to help it find partners to support modernisation. The delegation also discussed the plant's operations, development prospects and difficulties, then toured its sugar-production facilities.

Ouattara said SOSUCO wanted to raise annual sugar production from 20 000 tonnes to about 100 000 tonnes. He said the company employed people in the Tannounyan region and called for collective support to restore its production capacity.

Why it mattersSOSUCO is seeking partners to modernise its factory and expand cultivated land as it targets annual sugar production of about 100 000 tonnes.

Sport · 2026-11-18

Burkina Faso beat Mauritania 3-1 to qualify for CAN 2027

Aboubacar Camara, Franck Lassina Traoré and Elohim Kaboré scored in Nouakchott, securing qualification with two group matches left.

Burkina Faso beat Mauritania 3-1 in Nouakchott on Tuesday, 17 November, to qualify for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations with two rounds of group matches remaining. Aboubacar Camara opened the scoring in the 11th minute, and Franck Lassina Traoré made it 2-0 before the break.

Mauritania’s Mohamed Ali Diadié headed a goal in the 75th minute, but substitute Elohim Kaboré restored Burkina Faso’s two-goal lead in the 88th. Coach Amir Abdou’s team has 10 points after four matches and is guaranteed a top-two finish in Group F.

Burkina Faso is second behind Benin, also on 10 points, with Benin ahead on goal difference, +5 to +4. The Etalons are scheduled to visit Benin on 24 March 2027 and host the Central African Republic on 28 March, with a chance to finish top of the group.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso’s final two group fixtures are scheduled for 24 and 28 March 2027, against Benin and the Central African Republic.

Health · 2026-11-18

Sawadogo becomes Burkina Faso’s first cardiovascular surgery associate professor

The Burkinabè doctor passed three examinations at the CAMES health sciences competition in Bangui, qualifying to train future specialists.

Dr Adama Sawadogo has become Burkina Faso’s first associate professor in cardiovascular surgery after passing the 23rd health sciences aggregation competition of the African and Malagasy Council for Higher Education (CAMES), held in Bangui, Central African Republic.

Sawadogo passed three assessments: a presentation of his scientific work, a lecture testing his teaching ability and a clinical examination. Lefaso.net reported that his new rank will allow him to take a greater role in training and supervising future cardiovascular surgery specialists.

In 2024, Lefaso.net interviewed Sawadogo for a report on care for children with heart conditions at Tengandogo University Hospital. The outlet did not report when he will begin his expanded training role.

Why it mattersFuture cardiovascular surgery specialists in Burkina Faso will have Sawadogo as a trainer and supervisor under his new academic rank.
