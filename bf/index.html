AES ministers review second-year roadmap in Ouagadougou

Ministers from Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger are reviewing progress on the confederation's roadmap at a two-day session that ends with a synthesis note.

Ministers of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) confederation opened a two-day ministerial session in Ouagadougou on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, to review how the second-year roadmap is being carried out. Delegations from Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger are taking part, and the session is chaired by Burkina Faso's Minister of War and Patriotic Defence, General Célestin Simporé.

Simporé, who read the opening speech, said the meeting will assess activities across the three pillars of the confederation: defence and security, diplomacy and development. He called for better sharing of intelligence and stronger strategic autonomy, and for the three countries to speak with one voice internationally. The roadmap was finalised on February 26, 2026, in Ouagadougou.

The ministers are expected to produce a synthesis note on progress made during the confederation's second year, and to make recommendations ahead of the next meeting of the heads of state college. The session also addressed Burkinabè gold miners expelled from Guinea, and Simporé thanked the Malian authorities for their handling of the case.

Why it mattersBurkinabè readers will see whether the confederation's joint security, trade and resource plans move from paper to practice before the heads of state meet.

Burkina Faso and China open business forum in Ouagadougou

Chinese investors and Burkinabe operators met on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, to plan vehicle, glass, health and broadcast projects.

A Burkina Faso-China business forum opened on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, in Ouagadougou, under the theme of making Sino-Burkinabe cooperation a lever for industrial development. The event brings together a delegation of Chinese investors and economic operators led by Burkina Faso's ambassador to China, Daouda Bitié.

The projects discussed include production lines for vehicles, among them ambulances and security vehicles, a glass and glazing industrial complex, equipment for health centres and the modernisation of television studios. Bitié said the mission follows economic meetings held in Beijing in May 2026, which led to five projects signed between Chinese investors and Burkinabe companies.

China's ambassador to Burkina Faso, Zhao Deyong, welcomed the meeting and thanked the government for the conditions it offers Chinese companies, including security for their staff and projects. He urged investors to look more closely at opportunities in Burkina Faso.

Why it mattersBurkinabe companies and Chinese investors are set to work on five agreed projects, with vehicle, glass, health equipment and broadcast production lines under discussion.

Agriculture · 2026-11-12

Burkina Faso stakeholders seek recognition for biopesticides

A two-day national workshop in Koudougou on November 10 and 11, 2026, will draft a multi-stakeholder roadmap on approving biopesticides.

Officials, researchers, private firms, farmer organisations and civil society met in Koudougou on November 10 and 11, 2026, to discuss how Burkina Faso can formally recognise and regulate biopesticides. The national workshop is organised by the National Council for Organic Agriculture (Cnabio) with the RADIUS project of Joseph Ki-Zerbo University and the RESI-2P and GP-SAEP programmes.

Seydou Savadogo, vice-president of Cnabio and head of a cooperative of agroecological input makers and suppliers, said the event follows about 15 years of campaigning by agroecology actors. He said that after the adoption of a regulatory framework for biofertilisers in November 2025, producers want a similar recognition for biopesticides, which they also call bioprotectors. Antoine Sanon, coordinator of the RADIUS project, said progress depends on closer ties between research, producers and public decision-makers.

Participants will review the pesticide control law, number 026-2017/AN, and take stock of how biopesticides are used, produced and distributed. They will also study approval practices in neighbouring countries and aim to produce a roadmap and the basis of a policy note to support advocacy for biopesticides.

Why it mattersMakers and suppliers of agroecological inputs, along with producers, want biopesticides officially recognised so they can be promoted and used in place of chemical products.

Education · 2026-11-12

Burkina Faso weighs teaching intellectual property in schools

The national IP centre's workshop in Ouagadougou discussed adding the subject to general, technical, professional and university curricula.

The permanent secretariat of Burkina Faso's National Intellectual Property Centre, SP-CNPI, held a workshop in Ouagadougou on Wednesday on putting intellectual property into school and university programmes. The meeting, in the video room of the PSUT building at Joseph-Ki-Zerbo University, aimed to encourage creativity and innovation across the country.

Kohio Lodouba, head of prospective, training and cooperation, spoke for the centre's permanent secretary and said the aim was to build a culture of intellectual property. He said schools are an effective way to reach a wide public. The secretariat helps creators protect their works with the African Intellectual Property Organisation, OAPI, which gives the owner the exclusive right to authorise or forbid use.

Participants included teachers, researchers and education stakeholders. Lodouba also said national law recognises only a natural person as the author of a work, which raises open questions for artificial intelligence. The workshop is part of a specific activity programme of the secretariat validated by OAPI.
