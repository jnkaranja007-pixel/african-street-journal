Court rejects bail for real estate firm executives

Executives of SITRAF, a real estate company, will remain in detention as their trial for illegal land promotion activities is postponed.

The trial of the Société immobilière Traoré et Frères (SITRAF) executives was adjourned to September 18, 2026, by the High Court of Bobo-Dioulasso. The court denied provisional release requests from the defense, who argued there was no risk of witness tampering or public disorder. The prosecution, however, cited potential witness intimidation and the risk of flight, especially given the defendants' former ministerial status.

The case involves SITRAF, its manager and former Minister of National Education, Wendkouni Joël Lionel Bilgo, and executive director Vincent de Paul Kaboré. They are accused of illegal real estate development activities and advertising without prior authorization. The public prosecutor indicated that money laundering charges would be added, as more victims have come forward since the initial arrests.

The defense lawyers pleaded for their clients' release, emphasizing that account freezes were already in place and that the defendants intended to face justice. Former Minister Bilgo personally appealed to the court for provisional release, expressing distress over their detention. The prosecution countered that the prison infirmary could manage any health concerns and that sufficient guarantees for their appearance in court were lacking.

Why it mattersExecutives of a real estate company are facing charges for illegal activities, impacting investor confidence and regulatory oversight in the sector.

Burkina Faso Expands Kidney Care Centers to Twelve

The Ministry of Health announced the addition of seven new hemodialysis centers, increasing national capacity and reducing patient travel burdens.

Burkina Faso has increased its specialized renal care services by constructing and opening seven new hemodialysis centers between 2022 and 2026. This expansion brings the total number of kidney care centers in the country to twelve, as reported by the Ministry of Health on Friday, September 4, 2026. The goal of this national network enhancement is to bring care closer to patients and to lessen the frequent, costly, and taxing journeys required for regular dialysis sessions.

Previously, the nation had only five hemodialysis centers. The newly established facilities, including those in Banfora, Dori, Dédougou, and Fada N'Gourma, which were officially inaugurated on June 20, 2026, represent an investment exceeding 1.2 billion CFA francs. According to the Ministry of Health, these new infrastructures are expected to boost the national capacity for treatment by approximately forty-five thousand additional sessions annually.

Beyond the statistics, these developments signify a tangible improvement in healthcare access for individuals suffering from kidney failure. This progress is attributed to the integration of modern equipment, suitable infrastructure, and the deployment of qualified personnel. The Ministry of Health stated that these advancements translate into better care for affected citizens.

Why it mattersIndividuals suffering from kidney failure in Burkina Faso will benefit from improved access to specialized care closer to their homes.

Ex-Minister Bilgo's Property Case Delayed for More Evidence

The trial of former minister Wendkouni Joël Lionel Bilgo and a real estate director was postponed to September 18 for further investigation.

The trial of former minister Wendkouni Joël Lionel Bilgo and Vincent de Paul Kaboré, technical director of Société immobilière Traoré et Frères (SITRAF), began at the Bobo-Dioulasso High Court but was adjourned to September 18, 2026. The prosecution requested the delay, citing the need for further inquiries due to ongoing complaints being received. Both defendants, accused of illegal real estate promotion activities and unauthorized advertising, remain in detention.

Defense lawyers for Bilgo and Kaboré requested their clients' provisional release, arguing they are family men with fragile health and that continued detention could cause further harm. They emphasized their clients' voluntary surrender to the police and expressed a desire for the truth to emerge. The prosecution opposed the release, citing risks of witness tampering and insufficient guarantees of appearance, noting that former ministers have previously absconded.

The court rejected the request for provisional release and upheld the prosecution's motion for a postponement to September 18, 2026, for additional investigations. The case stems from allegations on August 19, 2026, that SITRAF was selling land plots without proper authorization. Both Bilgo and Kaboré stated they presented themselves voluntarily to investigators and expressed a strong desire for the case to be resolved quickly.

Why it mattersThe trial delay and continued detention of the former minister and real estate director affect investors by creating uncertainty around property development regulations and ongoing legal proceedings.

Salesman Shares 25 Years of Experience in New Book

Nouhoun Diallo's 105-page book, "Agent commercial: une expérience humaine," details the human side of sales and offers advice to young professionals.

Nouhoun Diallo launched his book, "Agent commercial: une expérience humaine," on September 3, 2026, sharing 25 years of observations and practical experience in sales. The 105-page work emphasizes the human dimension of the commercial agent profession, drawing from Diallo's background in medical, telecommunications, and insurance sectors. He highlights that commercial success hinges on understanding people and addresses common challenges like pressure, rejection, negotiation, and client relations.

Diallo aims to inspire younger generations with his insights, stating he is legitimate to share his knowledge after nearly 25 years in the field. He encourages aspiring sales professionals to learn from past experiences to avoid pitfalls. The book also explores emotional intelligence, stressing the need for sales agents to adapt their communication style to each person they interact with daily.

The author advises that passion is crucial for success in sales, suggesting that those without it should reconsider the profession. He notes that a passionate individual may earn less but find greater happiness. Thierry Millogo, CEO of Éditions Mercury, praised Diallo's book for credibly sharing a lifetime of experience as a commercial agent. The book is available for five thousand FCFA.

Why it mattersThe book offers practical advice and insights into the sales profession, potentially guiding young people and those seeking employment.

Burkina Faso, Turkey Agree to Boost Bilateral Cooperation

Ouagadougou and Ankara will hold political consultations to deepen dialogue and identify new cooperation prospects.

Burkina Faso's Minister Delegate to the Minister of Foreign Affairs, Bêbgnasgnan Stella Eldine Kabré, met with the Turkish Ambassador to Burkina Faso, Feriba Duygu Hokkacý Esirgen, on September 2, 2026. The two officials reviewed common interests in bilateral cooperation, emphasizing the need to strengthen their relationship. They also discussed mutual support within international forums, reflecting shared views on global issues.

Discussions focused on upcoming political consultations between Burkina Faso and Turkey. These meetings aim to enhance bilateral dialogue, explore new avenues for cooperation, and improve coordination between Ouagadougou and Ankara. The goal is to achieve concrete, mutually beneficial outcomes for the citizens of both nations.

Minister Delegate Kabré highlighted the importance of increased mutual support between Burkina Faso and Turkey. This collaboration is intended to translate shared commitments into tangible results that benefit both countries. The meeting underscored a mutual desire to invigorate the existing partnership.

Why it mattersThe agreement between Burkina Faso and Turkey aims to transform common commitments into concrete, mutually beneficial results for the peoples of both countries.
