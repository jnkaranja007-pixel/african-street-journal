Minister Simporé Honors Sankara, Urges Youth to Build Nation

The Minister of War and Patriotic Defense presided over the 11th monthly military ceremony honoring Thomas Sankara and his companions.

General of Division Célestin Simporé, Minister of War and Patriotic Defense, led the 11th monthly military ceremony at the Thomas Sankara Memorial on September 3, 2026. The event, attended by ministry personnel, honored President Thomas Sankara and his companions, emphasizing their ideals of dignity, sovereignty, and social justice. Simporé stated that Sankara's memory calls for reflection on collective responsibilities and the nation's destiny, free from dependency.

The ceremony, marked by military music and protocol, underscored Sankara's commitment to human rights, women's emancipation, education, health, and anti-corruption efforts. Minister Simporé highlighted that Sankara's values serve as a guide for public policy and daily life. He also acknowledged the dedication of all Burkinabè citizens, including defense and security forces, civil servants, educators, healthcare workers, farmers, and civil society members, recognizing their efforts as the nation's true strength.

Simporé specifically addressed the youth, emphasizing their crucial role in national construction. He urged them to embrace the ideals of the Revolution and actively participate in building the country. The event also included discussions in Passoré province on the ideological foundations of the Popular Progressive Revolution, leadership, and youth responsibilities in consolidating Sahel states, aiming to foster engagement and understanding among young people and the wider community.

Why it mattersThe Minister of War and Patriotic Defense's call for youth participation in nation-building directly affects young Burkinabè citizens by encouraging their engagement in the country's development.

Nahouri officials review agro-pastoral gains and challenges

Local leaders met in Pô to assess the 2026-2027 agricultural campaign and the 2023-2026 agro-pastoral offensive's progress.

Members of the technical committee and general assembly of the Provincial Council for Food Security (CT-CPSA) convened in Pô on September 3, 2026. The meeting focused on the 2026-2027 agro-pastoral campaign, the food and nutritional situation, and the 2023-2026 agro-pastoral and fishing offensive's contribution to food sovereignty in the Nahouri province.

The session, themed "From the 2023-2026 Agro-Pastoral and Fishing Offensive to Food Sovereignty in Nahouri Province: Gains, Challenges, and Prospects," aimed to review local progress and identify obstacles hindering production. Provincial Director of Agriculture for Nahouri, Aïcha Sanou/Zidwemba, highlighted the council as a vital tool for local governance, fostering dialogue and synergy among stakeholders for food and nutritional security.

Participants reaffirmed their commitment to national strategies for sustainable food self-sufficiency. Discussions covered advancements made within the province and pinpointed difficulties affecting agricultural output. The meeting underscored the importance of local collaboration in addressing food security and nutritional challenges.

Why it mattersFarmers in Nahouri province are directly affected by the review of agricultural gains and challenges, which informs future production strategies and food security efforts.

Youth Launch Resilience Tournament in Piéla

Six teams from Piéla are competing in a football tournament aimed at strengthening community ties and promoting peace.

The youth of Piéla officially launched the "Maracana of resilience and social cohesion" on Sunday, a competition featuring six teams representing different parts of the commune. The initiative, spearheaded by Biyourpo Lankoandé, aims to foster closer relationships among residents and promote peace, solidarity, and coexistence amidst national security challenges.

The tournament emphasizes fair play, respect, solidarity, and unity, serving as more than just a sporting event. It provides a platform for fraternity, dialogue, and connection among the commune's youth. The six participating teams draw from various segments of Piéla's population, reflecting the organizers' goal of creating an inclusive and unifying space. The opening day saw FC Dortmund defeat FC Jeune Talent 2-0, while FC Privée and FC Folbongou drew 0-0.

Organizers and participants view each match as an opportunity to reinforce bonds within the community and champion peace and social cohesion, transcending the purely sporting results. This event highlights the role of sport in bridging divides and fostering a sense of shared identity in the region.

Why it mattersThe event aims to strengthen community ties and promote peace and social cohesion among the youth of Piéla.

Culture professionals train with weapons at police academy

Twenty-one cultural professionals completed a voluntary patriotic immersion program that included firearms training.

Twenty-one cultural professionals participated in a voluntary patriotic immersion program, which included a live firing exercise at Badnoogo on Thursday, September 3, 2026. The participants familiarized themselves with handling weapons, including RPG7s, machine guns, and Kalashnikovs, at the National Police Special Intervention Group (GSIPN) training center. They received briefings on the assembly, disassembly, and operation of various military-grade firearms.

The GSIPN commander, Issa Zongo, commended the participants for their voluntary engagement and expressed pride in their willingness to learn about weaponry. The training, a partnership between the Ministry of Culture and the Ministry of Security, involved 25 cultural actors from August 20 to September 4, 2026, at the police academy. Marius Diessongo, representing the trainees, described the experience as one of stress management and self-transcendence, yielding satisfactory results.

The immersion program aimed to instill patriotic values and practical skills. Participants engaged in theoretical and practical sessions, culminating in the firearms training. This initiative is part of a broader effort to foster a new generation of integrated and patriotic Burkinabè citizens committed to national development and sovereignty.

Why it mattersCultural professionals are affected by participating in a patriotic immersion program that includes weapons training, potentially impacting their future roles and perspectives.

Amon Bazongo Builds AI Ecosystems Linking Burkina Faso and Asia

Technology entrepreneur Amon Bazongo is fostering innovation by connecting Burkina Faso with Asian tech hubs and developing AI applications.

Amon Bazongo, a technology entrepreneur and artificial intelligence consultant, is bridging Burkina Faso with Asian innovation hubs. Bazongo, who grew up with digital technology, sees AI, blockchain, and big data as new frontiers for learning and entrepreneurship. He believes technological revolutions only have real impact when an ecosystem forms around them, emphasizing training, connection, and community building in his work.

Bazongo's academic journey began in Burkina Faso with an electrical and electronics engineering degree from the University Polytechnique of Bobo-Dioulasso. He then pursued international studies in Taiwan, earning degrees in computer science and Big Data Management. His professional career started in Taiwan, developing marketing applications and chatbots. He later transitioned to AI consulting, notably working on inCare, an AI system for cardiology developed with a Kaohsiung hospital.

His work on inCare highlights his approach to AI as a practical tool for strategic sectors like healthcare. Bazongo's career trajectory is now increasingly focused on the digital transformation challenges facing the African continent. He is recognized as one of the 100 personalities shaping Burkina Faso's digital landscape, contributing to a generation that views emerging technologies as opportunities for development.
