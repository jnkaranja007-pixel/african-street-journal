Burkina Faso energy access hits 36% in 2025

National energy report shows primary energy supply up 6.61% and final consumption up 8.3% between 2024 and 2025.

Burkina Faso recorded a 36% national energy access rate in 2025, according to the national energy balance report validated on Friday, November 13, 2026, in Ouagadougou. The report, presented by Doulaye Sanou, Secretary-General of the Ministry of Energy, Mines and Quarries, detailed energy production, consumption, and exchanges within the West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA) information system format. Biomass remained the primary energy source at 61.58% in 2025, followed by petroleum products at 36% and electricity at 1.82%.

The total primary energy supply (ATEP) increased by 6.61% between 2024 and 2025, while total final consumption (CFT) rose by 8.3%. In the industrial sector, petroleum products dominated consumption at 55.17%, whereas biomass accounted for 78.90% of consumption in trade and public services. The rural access rate to energy increased from 7% to 11% in 2025, contrasting with 89% in urban areas for the same period.

Alidou Koutou, Director General of Energy, highlighted the progress in implementing energy policy and the importance of participant contributions to refining this reference tool. Yvon Polycarpe Dossa, head of the conventional energies division at the UEMOA Commission, reaffirmed the institution's commitment to enhancing energy integration, inter-state exchanges, and renewable energy development.

Why it mattersThe national energy access rate reached 36% in 2025, with rural access increasing to 11% while urban access stands at 89%.

EU project boosts goat farming in Burkina Faso's Koulsé region

A European Union-funded initiative provides livestock and training to displaced families, enabling income generation.

The OKDD project, funded by the European Union with over 32 billion FCFA, is changing lives in Burkina Faso's Koulsé region. On Thursday, November 12, 2026, an EU delegation visited the region to see the project's impact. Beneficiaries expressed satisfaction with the project's support for income-generating activities.

In Kotoul Yarcé village, families displaced by insecurity have returned and are now raising goats. In 2025, the Oxfam consortium, part of the OKDD project, supported 24 households with 4 goats and 1 buck each, along with animal feed. Beneficiaries received training in goat husbandry, and the initial 120 goats have grown to 186.

Kadidiata Ouédraogo, a resident of Kotoul Yarcé, shared her happiness with the project's support. The EU delegation, led by Inga Stefanowicz, head of EU partnerships in Burkina Faso, met with regional authorities, including Governor Bernadette Adenyo Sermé, before visiting project sites.

Why it mattersFamilies displaced by insecurity are rebuilding their livelihoods through goat farming, with 120 animals distributed and a herd now numbering 186.

Burkina Faso trains 35 young referees on updated laws of the game

The three-day session at the Focus Sport academy, which ends Monday, covers rule changes trialled at the last World Cup.

35 young referees in Burkina Faso, 25 referees and 10 assistants, are taking a three-day refresher course on the laws of the game at the Focus Sport academy. The session runs from November 14 to 16, 2026, and was organised by Suduu Andal Sport, with the central referees commission taking part.

Losseni Paré, president of the Commission centrale des arbitres (CCA), the body that oversees referees, said the ambassador for sport had offered the training and that the commission used the chance to select its young referees. Rosalie Tempa Ndah François, the course trainer, said changes tested at the most recent World Cup had sped up matches and improved the handling of time-wasting and feigned injuries.

The final day, on Monday, November 16, 2026, is open to everyone involved in the sport, including journalists and coaches.

Why it mattersReferees who complete the course will apply the updated rules in matches, and the organisers hope this will reduce disputes over decisions.

Tech · 2026-11-16

Le Faso Digital closes second edition with awards

The event celebrated laureates and partners, with a virtual platform remaining open until 2028.

The second edition of Le Faso Digital concluded with a closing dinner on Friday, November 13, 2026, in Ouagadougou. The event, which ran from October 17 to 24 under the theme "Inclusion, sovereignty, innovation: towards a digital Burkina for all," honored award recipients and recognized partners with certificates of appreciation.

Dr. Cyriaque Paré, promoter of Le Faso Digital, expressed gratitude to institutional and media partners for their support in making the second edition a success. He announced that the virtual platform will remain accessible until 2028, allowing participating digital solutions and companies to maintain visibility for the next 24 months.

42 laureates, representing the top three from each of the 14 competition categories, will receive six months of free subscription from Digitales Solidaires. Rodrigue Guiguemdé, president of Digitales Solidaires, stated that this partnership aims to promote young people, innovations, and digital solutions with a concrete societal impact.

Why it matters42 digital projects will receive six months of free subscription from Digitales Solidaires to support their development.
