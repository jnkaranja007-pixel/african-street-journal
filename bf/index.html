Business · 2026-08-05

State miner SOPAMIB wins permit for Bouboulou gold project as nationalisation push widens

Burkina Faso has awarded an industrial mining permit to state-owned miner SOPAMIB for the Bouboulou gold project, advancing the government's push for greater state control of the mining sector, according to Reuters. The project in the north-central Yako commune will require more than 32 billion CFA francs ($56.1 million) in investment and is expected to run for over 15 years; the mines minister called it a break from a model that left development largely to private operators.

Why it mattersExpanding state ownership of mines could reshape revenue flows and raise risk premiums for remaining foreign gold operators.

4.5 million people need urgent aid in Burkina Faso as conflict and climate shocks bite

As of early 2026, 4.5 million people, including 2.9 million children, require urgent humanitarian assistance in Burkina Faso, according to UNICEF, driven by displacement, food insecurity and recurrent climate shocks. Across the wider central Sahel, an estimated 11.4 million people need critical food assistance, with 401,000 children suffering severe acute malnutrition, OCHA reported.

Why it mattersPersistent food and nutrition gaps keep pressure on markets, aid budgets and rural livelihoods across the north and Sahel regions.

Business · 2026-08-05

Burkina Faso's gold output hits record as state deepens control of mining sector

Burkina Faso's gold production has climbed to a record level as the government's nationalization drive accelerates, according to Africa News Desk, with five mines already transferred to state control and plans for ten new semi-mechanized, locally led mines scheduled for 2026. Gold makes up more than 70% of the country's export revenue, and the sector has drawn scrutiny after Burkina Faso nationalized the Boungou and Wahgnion mines for about $80 million, a fraction of their earlier $300 million valuation.

Why it mattersRecord output plus state control could boost government revenue but adds uncertainty for remaining foreign mining investors.
