Minister Launches Road Safety Brigade in Ouagadougou

A new unit, BRICIR, will enforce traffic laws and collect unpaid fines, shifting from prevention to rigorous action.

The Minister of Security, Mahamadou Sana, officially launched the Brigade for Research and Intervention Against Road Incivism (BRICIR) in Ouagadougou on September 2, 2026. This specialized, motorized unit of the National Police will operate under the Kadiogo regional traffic and road safety service. BRICIR aims to ensure tight and responsive public road coverage, intervening rapidly against urban emergencies and enforcing payment of electronic fines.

BRICIR's core missions include identifying, intercepting, and compelling road users who evade fines issued through video-ticketing systems. The brigade will also actively target violations such as running red lights, speeding, dangerous overtaking, and failure to wear helmets, which is identified as a major factor in serious accidents. Additionally, it will address illegal parking and other forms of incivism that obstruct traffic flow.

Minister Sana stated that the phase of awareness is over, and the government is now entering a period of rigor and direct action against dangerous behaviors that cause daily fatalities. He emphasized that road incivism will no longer be tolerated and the law will be strictly applied to all offenders. The Director General of the National Police, Thierry Dofizouho Tuina, highlighted the tactical reorganization needed to address urban traffic security challenges.

Why it mattersRoad users in Ouagadougou face stricter enforcement and penalties for traffic violations, including unpaid fines.

Burkina Faso, Egypt Boost Economic Ties

Ministers from Burkina Faso and Egypt reaffirmed their commitment to strengthening economic cooperation and investment opportunities.

Burkina Faso and Egypt have agreed to enhance their economic partnership, focusing on increasing trade and investment. This renewed commitment was stated by the Foreign Ministers of both nations during a meeting in Cairo on September 1, 2026. The discussions aimed to review existing collaborations and identify new avenues for development, signaling a desire to inject new dynamism into their economic relationship.

The two countries are keen to explore investment opportunities, evidenced by the organization of a second forum for Egyptian and Burkinabe business leaders in Cairo, following a successful first forum held in Burkina Faso in 2025. This initiative reflects a shared interest in fostering concrete projects and promoting investment between the two nations, with Burkina Faso's Foreign Minister encouraging more such economic exchanges.

Both nations emphasized the importance of leveraging Africa's economic potential, with Burkina Faso's minister stating that African countries must be both actors and beneficiaries of this potential. They also called for greater utilization of African economic resources, tools, and space, underscoring a collective ambition to build a stronger African economic landscape through increased intra-continental collaboration.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the reaffirmed commitment to strengthening economic cooperation and exploring new investment opportunities between Burkina Faso and Egypt.

Army Health Staff Trained on HIV/AIDS Care

Ouagadougou hosts a training session for 21 military health personnel on differentiated service approaches and PrEP for HIV/AIDS management.

The Ministry of War and Patriotic Defense, in partnership with the association REV+, organized a capacity-building session in Ouagadougou from August 31 to September 4, 2026. The training focused on "Differentiated Service Approaches (ADS) and PrEP in HIV/AIDS Care" for military health service personnel. This initiative aims to address the health risks associated with the high mobility of combat forces due to the war on terrorism, ensuring their well-being for battlefield effectiveness.

According to Colonel Steevens Ouédraogo, ministerial coordinator for the fight against HIV and STIs, the training is an investment in the health, security, and resilience of the forces. The acquired knowledge will enable the 21 beneficiaries to better understand prevention, advise colleagues, and reduce risks. PrEP, or pre-exposure prophylaxis, is highlighted as a tool to protect combat forces from HIV/AIDS before exposure.

The differentiated service approaches are presented as a new tool to decentralize HIV/AIDS care across Burkina Faso. Participants, drawn from various regions, will implement these acquired skills in their home areas. The association REV+ has identified combat forces as a priority in its national strategic plan for HIV/AIDS control from 2026 to 2030, underscoring the importance of this training.

Why it mattersMilitary health personnel are being trained to better manage HIV/AIDS care and prevention, particularly for combat forces facing increased health risks.

BBDA Educates Cultural Figures on Rights and Obligations

The Burkinabè Copyright Office and the Directorate General of Culture and Arts held a conference for cultural professionals participating in a voluntary patriotic immersion.

The Burkinabè Copyright Office (BBDA) and the Directorate General of Culture and Arts hosted a conference for cultural actors engaged in a voluntary patriotic immersion. The session, held at the Pabré Police Academy, focused on their rights, obligations, and social responsibilities, particularly concerning the law on the status of artists. This legislation outlines various rights and mandates social protection measures for artists.

Zita Daniel Bonzi, Director General of the BBDA, highlighted the institution's ongoing efforts in social protection for cultural professionals. He commended the voluntary and humble participation of artists, creators, and cultural journalists in the immersion program. Bonzi also reiterated the BBDA's role in managing collective copyright and neighboring rights, expressing hope that the immersion would foster patriotism and enhance the quality of artistic productions.

Cultural journalist Marie Dossongo described the initiative as "salutary," noting that the conference clarified previously unknown aspects of the artist status law, including social actions like mutual aid and support for elderly members. She shared her immersion experience, emphasizing self-transcendence, defense and security concepts, and solidarity among participants, encouraging others to join future programs.

Why it mattersCultural professionals are affected by learning about their rights and obligations, including social protection measures and copyright management.

Nakambé Conference Formalizes Informal Economy

Regional officials and business leaders met in Tenkodogo to discuss strategies for integrating informal economic units and jobs into the formal sector.

A regional conference on the informal economy opened in Tenkodogo, Burkina Faso, focusing on the formalization of economic units and jobs. Noël Kéré, technical advisor to the governor of the Nakambé region, presided over the event, which was organized by the Ministry of Sports, Youth and Employment. The conference theme, "The formalization of economic units: a lever for the economic sovereignty of Burkina Faso," highlights the government's strategic focus on this sector.

The governor of the Nakambé region, represented at the opening, acknowledged the informal economy's significant role in job and income creation for many households. However, he noted persistent challenges such as limited access to finance, social protection, public procurement, and support services. To address these issues, the government has prioritized formalization within its RELANCE 2026-2030 plan and the National Employment Strategy.

The conference aims to provide a platform for informal economy actors to learn about the benefits and opportunities of formalizing their activities. This process is expected to enhance business security, improve competitiveness, build partner confidence, and facilitate access to funding and new markets. Officials urged participants to engage actively and called for strengthened local outreach and support to make formalization accessible to all entrepreneurs, especially in rural areas.

Why it mattersActors in the informal economy are affected by a lack of access to finance, social protection, and new markets, which formalization aims to improve.
