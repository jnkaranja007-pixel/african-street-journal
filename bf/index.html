Burkina Faso seeks Indonesian partnerships for resource transformation

Over 35 Burkinabe business people attended the Trade Expo Indonesia 2026 in Jakarta to explore trade and investment opportunities.

Burkina Faso's Honorary Consul in Indonesia, Dr Zongo Fabiola, presented business opportunities at the Trade Expo Indonesia (TEI) 2026 in Jakarta. The country aims to transform local resources, boost industrial capacity, and create jobs, focusing on sectors like agriculture, mining, construction, and energy. Indonesian technology and expertise could support this transformation.

More than 35 Burkinabe business people attended the expo to meet Indonesian counterparts, identify suppliers, and explore partnerships. This engagement follows the September 2026 signing of a framework cooperation agreement and a visa exemption accord for diplomatic and service passport holders.

The TEI 2026 serves as a platform to connect businesses and convert identified opportunities into concrete projects. The goal is to leverage Indonesia's industrial capabilities and Burkina Faso's economic transformation objectives for sustainable partnerships. The next TEI event is scheduled for 2027.

Why it mattersBurkinabe businesses are seeking new partnerships and investment opportunities in Indonesia to drive the country's economic development and job creation.

African cardiologists meet in Ouagadougou

The 9th International Congress of the Burkina Faso Cardiology Society and the 4th International Congress of the Association of Cardiology Societies of West and Central Africa are.

Ouagadougou is hosting the 9th International Congress of the Burkina Faso Cardiology Society and the 4th International Congress of the Association of Cardiology Societies of West and Central Africa from October 28 to 30, 2026. The dual scientific meeting, themed "Heart and Nutrition," brings together specialists from across Africa to discuss strategies for preventing and managing cardiovascular diseases. A key message from the experts is that heart health is built not only in hospitals but also in kitchens and through daily eating habits.

The congress aims to bridge scientific knowledge with the realities of African populations, focusing on how to reconcile socio-economic conditions and dietary traditions with effective cardiovascular prevention. According to the President of the Burkina Faso Cardiology Society, Pr Nobila Valentin Yaméogo, nutritional advice must be adapted to patients' daily lives and financial capacities. He stressed that effective prevention requires considering dietary habits and purchasing power.

Pr Nobila Valentin Yaméogo called for concrete proposals from the scientific exchanges to guide public policies and strengthen awareness campaigns, emphasizing the need to connect science with people and promote early nutritional education. Excessive salt consumption was identified as a primary risk factor at the congress opening.

Why it mattersCardiologists and health professionals are meeting in Ouagadougou to discuss cardiovascular disease prevention and management, focusing on the link between diet and heart health.

Ouagadougou hosts 18th international crafts fair

The 18th edition of the Salon international de l’artisanat de Ouagadougou (SIAO) will run from October 30 to November 8, 2026.

Ouagadougou launched the 18th edition of the Salon international de l’artisanat de Ouagadougou (SIAO) with a popular run on Thursday evening, October 29, 2026. The event, which officially opens on Friday, October 30, saw many participants gather around the SIAO site in the Zone d’activités diverses (ZAD). Governor Abdoulaye Bassinga of the Kadiogo region and SIAO Director General Éric Bassolé led the approximately four-kilometer route.

Following the run, participants engaged in a collective aerobic session at the SIAO grounds. Governor Bassinga praised the high turnout and thanked all involved. Director General Bassolé emphasized the significance of this 18th edition and assured attendees that organizational teams were fully prepared to ensure its success.

The SIAO 2026, themed « African craftsmanship and industrialization: what synergy for better organization and valorization of local products and cultural heritage? », is scheduled to run until November 8, 2026. This major gathering aims to highlight opportunities for transforming and enhancing local skills and cultural heritage.

Why it mattersBurkinabe artisans and cultural heritage are showcased at the 18th Salon international de l’artisanat de Ouagadougou.

Ouagadougou hosts tribute to Professor Idrissa Ouédraogo

Academics from Mali, Niger, Senegal, and Canada will join Burkinabe scholars to discuss informal sector development.

Universities Thomas-Sankara and Aube-Nouvelle, along with the Centre for Economic Integration Studies in Africa, are hosting an international conference in Ouagadougou from October 29-31, 2026, to honor Professor Idrissa Mohamed Ouédraogo. The event celebrates his career, which includes supervising 47 doctoral theses and training hundreds of professionals now working in public administration, banking, insurance, and regional organizations. The conference theme is 'Informal Sector and Development in Africa: Theoretical Issues and Action Perspectives'.

The gathering, under the patronage of Prime Minister Rimtalba Jean Emmanuel Ouédraogo and sponsored by Minister Mathias Traoré and Isidore Kini, aims to recognize Professor Ouédraogo's over three decades of dedication to research and teaching. Researchers and practitioners from Mali, Niger, Senegal, and Canada will present scientific communications on topics such as formalizing the informal sector, mobile money's impact on poverty, and the effect of foreign aid on African economies.

The conference also features discussions on public borrowing and informal sector credit access within the UEMOA region. Professor Didier Zoungrana, president of Thomas-Sankara University, praised the initiative to honor Ouédraogo's contributions to human capital development and knowledge transmission. The organizing committee president, Professor Mahamadou Diarra, described the event as a way for students to repay their master's debt through scientific debate.

Why it mattersThe conference honors Professor Idrissa Ouédraogo's extensive academic work and contributions to training professionals in Burkina Faso and beyond.

Burkina Faso explores nuclear tech for farming

Scientists and officials met in Ouagadougou to discuss how nuclear techniques can boost crop yields and improve soil and water management.

The Atomic Energy Agency of Burkina Faso (ABEA), with the Health Sciences Research Institute, held a conference in Ouagadougou on Wednesday about using nuclear technologies for productive and resilient agriculture. ABEA Director General Harouna Sawadogo stated the meeting aimed to share knowledge on how nuclear techniques can address current agricultural challenges.

Sawadogo explained that nuclear and isotopic techniques help understand soils, manage water and fertilizers better, and select higher-yielding crop varieties suited to Burkina Faso's climate. These technologies also offer solutions for pest control with reduced environmental impact and improve crop conservation and product quality.

University professor Ismaël Henry Bassolé presented how national yields could double or triple, enabling exports, and how specific rice varieties, like the local 'moui kolgho', can be developed for different dietary needs. He clarified that the process involves exposing seeds to energy, similar to medical X-rays, to modify genetic material.

Why it mattersFarmers in Burkina Faso could benefit from improved crop yields, better soil and water management, and pest control methods through the application of nuclear technologies.
