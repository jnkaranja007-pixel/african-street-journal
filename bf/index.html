Transport Union Asks Members to Lower Fares After New Penalties

The National Federation of Road Transport Actors called for a return to pre-increase tariffs effective August 29, 2026.

The National Federation of Road Transport Actors (FENAT) has instructed its members to revert to the tariffs in effect before the recent price adjustment. This decision, made on Saturday, August 29, 2026, follows an extraordinary session of the organization's national bureau in Ouagadougou. The move comes after the joint order n° 2026-00456/MATM/MEF was issued, establishing penalties for bus and passenger transport companies.

FENAT stated in a release that it examined the situation and, in a spirit of responsibility, decided to implement precautionary measures to ensure the continuity of public transport services and protect users' purchasing power. The organization urges all members to reinstate the tariffs that were in place before the latest adjustment, starting from midnight on August 29, 2026. Concurrently, FENAT's national bureau has requested its officials to initiate discussions with relevant authorities promptly.

The stated objective is to establish a dialogue framework for a consensual and lasting solution that considers the interests of transporters, public authorities, and users. FENAT also affirmed its commitment to continued collaboration with authorities to foster a viable and regulation-compliant road transport sector. This action occurs amid ongoing discussions about road transport pricing and new regulatory measures targeting passenger transport companies.

Why it mattersTransport users are directly affected by the call to lower fares, aiming to preserve their purchasing power.

Association Donates 35 Million CFA Dispensary to Wa Health Center

The Association of Islamic Charity and Social Service provided a new health facility, including six observation rooms and medical supplies, to improve local healthcare access.

The Centre de santé et de promotion sociale (CSPS) of Wa, in the commune of Niou, inaugurated a new dispensary on August 27, 2026, funded by the Association of Islamic Charity and Social Service (ABISS) at a cost of 35 million CFA francs. The facility includes six observation rooms, a consultation room, a pharmacy, a waiting area, and sanitation facilities. ABISS also donated over one million CFA francs worth of medicines and medical equipment and committed to covering cleaning costs for one year.

This dispensary is the twelfth built by ABISS nationally and the sixth in the commune of Niou, aiming to support state efforts in expanding health coverage. The initiative is described as an act of compassion aligned with ABISS's humanitarian mission. Local authorities and health officials expressed gratitude, emphasizing the new facility's role in enhancing patient capacity and care, and urged the community to maintain the infrastructure.

The new facility brings relief to the community as the main CSPS building, functional since 2005, had been in disrepair, forcing services into the maternity ward and causing difficulties for both patients and staff. The improved infrastructure is expected to significantly benefit the populations of Wa and surrounding villages by providing better access to healthcare services.

Why it mattersThe populations of Wa and surrounding villages will benefit from improved healthcare access and capacity with the inauguration of the new dispensary.

TV Station Launches Road Safety Campaign in Bobo-Dioulasso

BF1 Television organized a caravan to promote helmet use and civic responsibility on the roads.

The regional office of BF1 Television in Bobo-Dioulasso launched a road safety awareness campaign on August 29, 2026, as part of the fourth BF1 Foot tournament. The initiative aimed to encourage the mandatory wearing of helmets and promote road safety and civic behavior. A motorized caravan traveled through the city's main streets, starting from the BF1 Ouest headquarters, to spread the message.

Jacob Zongo, commercial manager for BF1 Ouest, conveyed the station's appreciation to law enforcement, partners, and participants. He emphasized that the BF1 Foot tournament serves as a platform for civic engagement, focusing on safety and coexistence. Zongo advised the awareness teams to maintain exemplary conduct, urging them to remain calm and avoid provocation while on duty.

During the caravan, awareness teams engaged with citizens at intersections and along roadsides to discuss the critical importance of wearing helmets. To reward good practices, BF1 distributed gadgets and gifts to road users seen wearing helmets, a festive way to acknowledge and encourage road safety. This campaign, linking sport with road safety, underscores BF1's commitment to the residents of Bobo-Dioulasso.

Why it mattersMotorists and passengers in Bobo-Dioulasso are affected by the campaign promoting mandatory helmet use to improve road safety.

CNSS Plants Over 300 Trees at Water Museum

The National Social Security Fund's reforestation effort at the Water Museum combines social protection with environmental responsibility.

The National Social Security Fund (CNSS) has planted more than 300 trees at the Water Museum as part of a civic initiative. This action links social protection, environmental responsibility, and the enhancement of a heritage site where water is central. The effort aligns with the national goal of planting five million trees in 2026 and the theme "My concession, my tree."

Site promoter Alassane Samoura praised the initiative, noting its coherence with current challenges and the CNSS's ability to connect water, environment, and social security. CNSS board president Richard Tiendrebeogo stated the action responds to a national call to reforest the country and extends the institution's civic mission, emphasizing that protecting people requires them to be alive, linking environmental action to the institution's social purpose.

CNSS Director General Yacouba Nacambo highlighted the initiative as reflecting a broader vision of public institutions' roles in addressing climate change. The planting of fruit-bearing and local species aims to provide shade, beautify the space, and foster a more resilient environment, forging a lasting alliance between the CNSS and the Water Museum to create an eco-civic space where water, trees, and social life intersect.

Why it mattersThe CNSS is undertaking a reforestation project at the Water Museum, planting over 300 trees to promote environmental responsibility and social well-being.
