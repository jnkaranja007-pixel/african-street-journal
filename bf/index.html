Le Faso Digital attracts 201 digital innovation entries

The virtual fair in Ouagadougou concluded with plans for a 2028 international conference on West African online media.

The second edition of Le Faso Digital, a virtual fair for digital solutions, officially closed on Saturday, October 24, 2026, in Ouagadougou. Organized by Lefaso.net from October 17 to 24, the event saw 201 national digital innovation competition entries validated. Innovators, entrepreneurs, companies, institutions, and citizens engaged in discussions on digital inclusion, sovereignty, and innovation over eight days.

Dr. Cyriaque Paré, president of the organizing committee, expressed satisfaction with the event's progress, noting a significant increase from the 34 candidate solutions in 2025 to 208 registered this year, with 201 validated. These innovations span critical sectors including commerce, education, health, agriculture, finance, governance, environment, and digital infrastructure, demonstrating Burkina Faso's local talent in developing tailored digital solutions.

Following this edition, organizers will take a break in 2027 to prepare for an international conference in 2028. This conference will mark 30 years of online press in West Africa and coincide with the 25th anniversary of Lefaso.net. Discussions during the fair covered artificial intelligence, digital sovereignty, cybersecurity, regulation, digital financial services, national languages, and business digital transformation.

Why it mattersInnovations in commerce, education, health, agriculture, and finance were showcased, directly impacting these sectors in Burkina Faso.

Burkina Faso minister unveils Sankara statue in Rome

A two-meter steel sculpture of Thomas Sankara was inaugurated in a park named after him in Rome, Italy.

Burkina Faso's Minister of Culture, Pingdwendé Gilbert Ouédraogo, inaugurated a two-meter steel sculpture of Captain Thomas Sankara on Saturday, October 24, 2026. The artwork, located in the Thomas Sankara park in Rome's 3rd arrondissement, was created by artist Hamed Sékou Ouattara and gifted by Madi Sakandé. This sculpture symbolizes the friendship between the Burkinabè and Italian peoples.

The ceremony, attended by Burkina Faso's ambassadors to Rome and the Vatican, diplomatic representatives from Mali and Niger, and members of the Burkinabè diaspora, took place in the park officially named on September 17, 2025. Rome's 3rd arrondissement president, Paolo Emilio Marchionne, praised the initiative for perpetuating Sankara's ideals, while Minister Ouédraogo highlighted the universal reach of his legacy.

Minister Ouédraogo also launched the international fundraising campaign 'A brick for Sankara' during the event, aimed at supporting the construction of a Thomas Sankara memorial. The park itself is intended as a symbol of fraternity, justice, and dialogue between peoples.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso's culture minister inaugurated a statue and launched a fundraising campaign for a memorial in Rome, Italy.

Burkina Faso police seize nearly 400 motorbikes in three hours

Ouagadougou police confiscated motorbikes during a night operation targeting mandatory helmet use.

Burkina Faso's national police seized nearly 400 motorbikes in Ouagadougou during a three-hour night operation on Friday, October 23, 2026. The action targeted mandatory helmet use, with police confiscating vehicles from riders not wearing the required safety gear after dark.

The operation, led by Kadiogo regional police director Aguima Abdoulaziz Yonli, aimed to enforce road safety and protect riders, especially at night when helmet use reportedly declines. Police officials stated that the seizures highlight continued non-compliance with safety regulations despite awareness campaigns.

Director Yonli urged motorcyclists to make helmet use a permanent habit for their own safety and their families' well-being. The police initiative, named "Bouclier tête durcie" (Hardened Head Shield), aims to reinforce that wearing a helmet is essential for preserving lives on the road.

Why it mattersMotorcyclists in Ouagadougou face motorbike seizures if they do not wear helmets.

Burkina Faso launches sovereignty football league in 2027

The new competition aims to professionalize football and create 740 direct jobs across the country.

Burkina Faso will launch the first "Ligue de la Souveraineté" in January 2027, a new football competition initiated by Captain Ibrahim Traoré. Officially presented on October 24, 2026, in Ouagadougou, the league aims to promote local talent and professionalize Burkinabè football. The pilot phase, running from January to June 2027, will feature 20 teams divided into two groups based in Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso.

According to Adama Guira, the executive head of the initiative, each team will have 30 players and seven staff, creating an estimated 740 direct jobs. The league requires at least 50% local players among starters, with a six-month residency condition. Sports Minister Annick Lydie Djouma Pikbougoum/Zingué Ouattara highlighted the reform's importance for modernizing national football.

Adama Guira clarified that the Ligue de la Souveraineté will complement, not replace, the national championship, offering greater visibility to regional talents. The initiative is part of the Presidential Initiative for the Development and Promotion of Sporting Excellence (IP-RELIS) and involves all 17 regions of Burkina Faso.

Why it mattersThe new football league will create 740 direct jobs and promote local talent for players and staff in Burkina Faso.

Moov Africa-ONATEL donates 16.5 million FCFA to Burkina Faso schools

The telecommunications operator provided 25 computers and 100 scholarships to support students in scientific and professional high schools.

Moov Africa-ONATEL invested 16.5 million FCFA in Burkina Faso's education sector, donating 25 computers and 100 scholarships. The telecommunications operator delivered these resources to three high schools on October 21, 2026, in Ouagadougou. This initiative aims to improve learning conditions and promote academic excellence.

The donation includes 25 new computers for the Lycée scientifique national de Ouagadougou, the Lycée professionnel régional du Centre, and the Lycée professionnel régional Guimbi Ouattara in Bobo-Dioulasso. Additionally, 100 scholarships valued at 5 million FCFA will help reduce family financial burdens. Moov Africa-ONATEL's Director General, Mohamed Karim, stated the company is responding to a national call to promote scientific and technological disciplines.

Minister of Secondary Education and Vocational Training, Pr Moumouni Zoungrana, praised the initiative for bolstering the education system. Karim also encouraged students to use digital tools constructively for learning, not distraction. The total value of the support provided by Moov Africa-ONATEL amounts to 16.5 million FCFA.

Why it mattersStudents in Burkina Faso's scientific and professional high schools will benefit from new computers and scholarships to improve their learning and academic success.
