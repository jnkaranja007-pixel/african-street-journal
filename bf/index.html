3,400 Burkinabè return from Guinea

A government delegation welcomed the first wave of 3,400 citizens expelled from Guinean gold sites at Bobo-Dioulasso stadium.

A first group of 3,400 Burkinabè, expelled from Guinea and transiting through Mali, arrived in Bobo-Dioulasso on the night of November 6-7, 2026. They were met by a government delegation at the Bobo-Dioulasso sports stadium. This arrival marks the return of citizens who left Guinean artisanal gold mining sites following that country's decision to suspend such activities.

The group arrived in 48 buses from Mali. Minister of Family and Solidarity, Lieutenant-Colonel Passowindé Pélagie Kaboré/Kabre, stated that the government would provide health, psychosocial, and socio-professional reintegration support. A security and health check, followed by psychosocial profiling, was initiated upon arrival. Minors will be housed, while adults will be registered and can rejoin their families.

The government plans to offer vocational training and educational opportunities, including university access for BAC holders, as stated by the solidarity minister. Minister of Secondary Education, Vocational Training and Technical Education, Moumouni Zoungrana, encouraged the returnees to pursue training in fields like agriculture, livestock, and welding. Another 199 buses are en route to bring back more citizens.

Why it mattersBurkinabè citizens expelled from Guinean gold sites are returning home and will receive government support for reintegration.

Nahouri leaders promote patriotism and civic duty

Provincial officials in Pô led a conference on citizenship, peace, and the ideals of the popular progressive revolution.

The Haut-commissaire of Nahouri province, Sié Aristide Mohamed Kam, presided over a public conference in Pô on November 6, 2026. The event focused on civics, citizenship, patriotic engagement, and the ideals of the popular progressive revolution (RPP). This initiative, organized by the National Bureau of Major Projects, aimed to foster these values among the vital forces of the province. The conference included speeches, thematic presentations, and artistic performances.

Ilassa Dianda, President of the Special Delegation of Pô commune, praised the development actions in the area and urged residents to embrace the RPP ideals. The Haut-commissaire conveyed a message from the Head of State, honoring those working to sustain Burkina Faso and expressing satisfaction with public engagement in presidential initiatives. Following a film on government achievements, experts discussed human rights and citizenship.

Representatives from traditional and religious authorities, women, youth, merchants, people with disabilities, students, and security forces shared their satisfaction with government actions and signed the Citizen Pact of Nahouri's vital forces. Benjamin Sow, Director General of Citizenship and Peace Promotion, highlighted the RPP as a path to hope requiring citizen involvement. The ceremony concluded with the distribution of the RPP Manifesto and the symbolic planting of the 'Citizenship Tree'.

Why it mattersResidents of Nahouri province are being encouraged to engage in civic duties and embrace national ideals through government-led initiatives.

Burkina Faso targets 90% financial inclusion by 2031

The nation plans to mobilize 44.2 billion FCFA for its second inclusive finance strategy covering 2027-2031.

Burkina Faso aims to reach 90% financial inclusion by 2031 through its second national inclusive finance strategy (SNFI 2), set to run from 2027 to 2031. This initiative, valued at 44.2 billion FCFA over five years, seeks to expand access to financial services, accelerate digitalization, and enhance protection for vulnerable groups, including women and youth. Draft documents for the strategy and its three-year action plan were presented for validation at a national workshop in Ouagadougou on November 6, 2026.

The SNFI 2 strategy aims to bring financial services closer to populations traditionally excluded from them, ensuring access to quality and secure services. Vieux Abdoul Rachid Soulama, Secretary General of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, stated that the goal is not just to increase account openings or credit access, but to ensure equitable access considering gender, age, social conditions, and vulnerability. The strategy builds on exceeding the previous target of 75% financial service usage between 2019 and 2023.

The strategy's success is intended to enable women to develop businesses, young people to fund entrepreneurial projects, and agricultural producers to secure resources for their campaigns. This inclusive finance approach aims to provide practical support for individuals and communities across Burkina Faso. The national workshop in Ouagadougou marked an important step in renewing the framework for inclusive finance interventions.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso's citizens, particularly women, youth, and vulnerable populations, will gain improved access to financial services like bank accounts and loans.

Birds destroy Burkina Faso millet crops

Farmers in Bourasso commune face significant losses as granivorous birds attack fields before harvest.

Producers in Burkina Faso's Bourasso commune are battling granivorous birds that are destroying cereal crops, particularly millet. The attacks, occurring in villages like Bourasso, Sikoro, Kamiankoro, and Kodougou, compound existing challenges from poor rainfall. Farmers are spending long hours in fields trying to protect their harvests.

Joel Coulibaly, a producer from Kamiankoro, reported that one hectare of his millet crop has already been devastated. He expressed fears of famine, stating that most producers in his village face similar destruction. Blaise Traoré, a producer and VDP volunteer in Sikoro, noted that farmers are forced to harvest prematurely to salvage any grain, leading to substantial losses.

The producers of Bourasso commune are appealing to authorities for urgent measures to combat the granivorous birds. This new threat follows a difficult agricultural season marked by late and uneven rainfall, and an early end to precipitation. The birds are targeting crops before they fully mature, jeopardizing food security for households.

Why it mattersFarmers in Bourasso commune are facing potential famine due to bird attacks on their millet crops, adding to difficulties from poor rainfall.

Germany reaffirms aid to Burkina Faso

German ambassador highlights shared values and cooperation in Ouagadougou on reunification anniversary.

Germany celebrated the 36th anniversary of its reunification on Friday, November 6, 2026, at the German ambassador's residence in Ouagadougou. The event brought together political figures, partners, and friends of Germany to commemorate the historic occasion.

German Ambassador Dietrich Becker stated that reunification teaches essential lessons about peaceful coexistence and respect for international law, which are vital for peace, progress, and freedom. He also acknowledged Burkina Faso's efforts to strengthen its sovereignty and the cooperation within the Alliance of Sahel States (AES).

Ambassador Becker reiterated Germany's commitment to supporting Burkina Faso through the RELANCE program, focusing on water supply, agricultural processing, and sustainable industrialization. He also announced continued efforts in cultural and scientific exchanges, including training opportunities for young Burkinabè in Germany and expanding the Goethe-Institut's presence.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso will receive German support in water supply, agricultural processing, and industrialization through the RELANCE program.
