Association Nodde Nooto marks 30 years in Ouagadougou

The organisation honoured five partners for their contributions to development and humanitarian aid.

The Association Nodde Nooto (A2N) celebrated 30 years of its existence on Saturday, November 7, 2026, in Ouagadougou. The event recognised the organisation's pioneers and presented awards to five partners for their support of development and humanitarian actions benefiting communities.

A2N, founded in Dori, brought together partners, former collaborators, staff, and officials to commemorate its journey and achievements. The five distinguished partners are Action de Carême Suisse, Vétérinaires Sans Frontières Belgique, the CCBE-BF, the SPONG, and the Danish Refugee Council, all acknowledged for their assistance in local development, food security, education, and humanitarian aid.

A film showcasing A2N's 30-year activities was screened, and former collaborators shared memories of the association's founding and challenges. Clarisse Honadia Kambou, president of A2N's board, paid tribute to all who contributed to the organisation's growth, including founders, administrators, staff, and partners.

Burkina Faso villagers repair Saye-Gourcy road

Residents of Saye in Zondoma province used shovels and wheelbarrows to fill potholes on a vital route.

Residents of Saye village in Burkina Faso's Zondoma province, specifically women and the local citizen watch, worked on November 6, 2026, to manually repair the road connecting their village to Gourcy. This second phase of community work followed an initial effort on October 23, aiming to restore the road's usability for agricultural transport and access to Gourcy's social and health facilities.

Equipped with tools like hoes, shovels, and wheelbarrows, the women and men collected earth to fill potholes, leveled the road surface, and cleared the verges. Safiata Ouédraogo, the women's leader, stated the initiative provides relief, especially for emergency medical evacuations. Alassane Zallé, representing the citizen watch, noted it will improve year-round traffic and economic exchanges.

The village development councilor, Amadé Ouédraogo, praised the solidarity, saying it will improve travel safety and speed up rescue responses. This community action demonstrates Saye's commitment to improving their living conditions through self-effort.

Why it mattersVillagers in Saye, Zondoma province, repaired a road to improve access for agricultural products and emergency services.

Burkina Faso receives 200 tractors for farming

The tractors, with ploughs and harrows, will support agricultural volunteers and other producers.

The National Bureau of Major Projects of Burkina (BN-GPB) handed over 200 tractors to the Ministry of Agriculture on Friday, November 6, 2026, in Ouagadougou. The initiative, part of the Presidential Initiative for Agricultural Production and Food Self-Sufficiency (IP-P3A), aims to boost farm mechanization and national production capacity. The tractors are 40 horsepower and include ploughs and harrows.

These 200 tractors are primarily designated for agricultural Volunteers for the Defense of the Fatherland (VDP) and will also be available to other producers across Burkina Faso. BN-GPB Executive Director Pr Hamidou Sawadogo urged beneficiaries to use the equipment responsibly and asked mechanization brigades to support the VDP. Minister of Agriculture, Water, and Animal and Fisheries Resources, Ismaël Sombié, assured the prompt deployment of the machinery.

Under Captain Ibrahim Traoré, Burkina Faso is implementing initiatives like the Presidential Initiative for Food Self-Sufficiency and the 2023-2028 Agro-Pastoral, Hydraulic, and Fisheries Offensive. The minister stated that these combined efforts helped Burkina Faso reach a cereal needs coverage rate of 124.6 in 2025, an unprecedented figure for the country, moving towards food security.

Why it mattersFarmers in Burkina Faso will benefit from new tractors to improve their work and increase cultivated areas.

Sourgou residents clean health centre

Volunteers cleared debris and vegetation from the local medical centre on Sunday.

Residents of Sourgou in Burkina Faso mobilized on Sunday, November 8, 2026, to clean their local medical centre. The effort took place as part of the National Vector Control Day.

Armed with brooms, machetes, and rakes, the inhabitants worked to clear the health facility and its surroundings. Dr. Donald Ouédraogo, the chief medical officer, praised the community's commitment and expressed his satisfaction with the improved appearance of the premises.

Dr. Ouédraogo wished all participants a good return after the cleanup operation.
