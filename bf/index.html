Burkina Faso and Türkiye agree education and health pact

Ankara talks between the two nations also explored economic and security cooperation.

Burkina Faso and Türkiye explored new avenues for cooperation in education, health, and the economy during high-level talks in Ankara on October 20 and 21, 2026. A Burkinabè delegation, led by Foreign Affairs Ministry Secretary General Hermann Yirigouin Toé, met with Turkish officials from institutions including the Maarif Foundation, TIKA, and the Ministry of Health. The discussions aimed to strengthen bilateral ties and expand existing partnerships.

The Maarif Foundation agreed to increase scholarships for Burkinabè students and support vocational and technical training, building on its existing schools in Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso. TIKA committed to supporting emergency medical training and re-examining a project for emergency medicine development. Both sides also agreed to renew a 2024 academic cooperation accord, with the Turkish Higher Education Council promising more master's and doctoral scholarships for Burkinabè students.

Security cooperation was also a key focus, with Burkina Faso inviting Türkiye to partner with the Sahel States Confederation (AES) in combating terrorism. Türkiye reaffirmed its commitment to supporting Burkina Faso's security and territorial integrity, expressing a desire for a defense and security agreement. The two nations also plan to hold their fourth joint cooperation commission in Ouagadougou in 2027.

Why it mattersBurkinabè students will benefit from increased scholarships and expanded vocational training opportunities in Türkiye.

Burkinabè researcher develops cybersecurity tech in Canada

Dr Karim Ganamé, a Burkinabè scientist based in Montreal, Canada, is creating advanced cybersecurity solutions.

Dr Karim Ganamé, a Burkinabè researcher, is developing advanced cybersecurity technologies from his base in Montreal, Canada. He holds a doctorate in computer security and founded a company that creates solutions for sensitive data protection environments. Ganamé's work aims to detect threats before they cause damage, addressing the growing risk of cyberattacks on organizations.

His career began in Burkina Faso in 1999 with an analyst-programmer qualification from the ESI. Ganamé then pursued higher education in France, earning degrees in communication systems, computer networks, and management from institutions including INSA Lyon and the University of Clermont-Ferrand. He completed his cybersecurity doctorate at the University of Franche-Comté in 2007.

Ganamé's expertise combines scientific research with entrepreneurial ventures, focusing on protecting digital systems from threats like ransomware. His company's solutions are designed for environments where data and infrastructure security are critical, helping organizations avoid disruptions and data loss.

Why it mattersBurkinabè citizens living abroad are contributing to technological advancements that protect digital systems.

Burkina Faso prepares 40 local dishes for tourism

A workshop validated a collection of traditional recipes aimed at preserving culinary heritage and boosting gastronomic tourism.

Burkina Faso is preparing a collection of 40 local dishes to promote gastronomic tourism and preserve its culinary heritage. The Ministry of Culture, represented by the Director General of Tourism, Monique Ouédraogo/Ilboudou, opened a validation workshop in Ouagadougou on Thursday, October 22, 2026. This initiative aims to document traditional recipes, including dishes like kazanga, zamné, babenda, tô with various sauces, and local drinks such as dolo and zoom-koom.

The Director General stated that gastronomy is a vital part of national cultural heritage, reflecting community history, traditions, and values. She emphasized that many culinary knowledge traditions are passed down orally, making documentation necessary for future generations. Participants are reviewing the accuracy and authenticity of information on dish names, origins, ingredients, preparation methods, and their cultural significance.

This collection is also intended to diversify national tourism offerings and encourage the consumption of local products. The initiative can create economic opportunities for producers, processors, and restaurateurs. The validation process is seen as a concrete contribution to safeguarding collective memory and recognizing community knowledge, rather than a mere administrative step.

Why it mattersBurkina Faso's culinary heritage is being documented and promoted to boost tourism and create economic opportunities for local food producers and businesses.

Business · 2026-10-23

Vista Bank launches French subsidiary

The pan-African bank, led by Burkinabè Simon Tiemtoré, opens its first European branch with 50 million euros capital.

Vista Bank, the pan-African banking group chaired by Burkinabè national Simon Tiemtoré, has officially launched Vista Bank France. This new entity, announced in Bangkok during International Monetary Fund and World Bank meetings, marks the group's first banking presence outside of Africa. The French subsidiary begins operations with an approved capital of 50 million euros.

Vista Bank France aims to facilitate financial exchanges between the group's African subsidiaries and international markets. Services offered will include foreign trade financing, corporate banking, correspondent banking, foreign exchange and treasury operations, factoring, and advisory services. The group intends to reduce transaction times and costs for its African clients.

Simon Tiemtoré stated that Vista Bank France expresses the vision of building a pan-African group that sustainably connects Africa to the world. The European expansion is part of Vista Bank's growth strategy, which has previously involved acquiring banking assets from international groups in Africa.

Why it mattersBurkinabè national Simon Tiemtoré's banking group has expanded its international reach by opening a new subsidiary in France.
