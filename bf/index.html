Military Team Defeats Civilians in Ténado Football Match

A football game organized by the Ministry of War and Patriotic Defense aimed to strengthen ties between the armed forces and civilians.

The military forces of Ténado defeated the local youth team 2-1 in a football match on Saturday, August 29, 2026. The game, played at the Saint-Augustin high school field, was part of an initiative by the Ministry of War and Patriotic Defense to foster cohesion between the army and the nation. The match saw the military team score the winning goal in the 37th minute after a first-half tie.

Minister of State, Minister of War and Patriotic Defense, General Célestin Simporé, attended the event and emphasized that the outcome was secondary to the goal of strengthening military-civilian relations. He stated, "There was no winner. Ténado won, the Nando region won, because the objective has been achieved." He praised the fair play and positive atmosphere, urging both soldiers and civilians to work together for community safety.

The military team received a trophy, medals, balls, and a jersey set, while the youth team also received medals, jerseys, and a ball. Kiswendsida Nikiema, captain of the winning military team, highlighted the importance of the victory for morale and thanked the authorities for organizing the event. Eugène Bationo, captain of the Ténado youth team, acknowledged his team's shortcomings but expressed determination to learn from the experience for future matches.

Why it mattersThe event directly involved military personnel and local civilians in a shared activity to improve their relationship.

Burkina Forces Confirm Superiority Over Terrorists in July Operations

Government forces conducted offensive patrols, ambushes, and airstrikes across multiple regions, eliminating numerous militants and disrupting enemy logistics.

Burkinabe combat forces asserted their dominance over terrorist elements during the latter half of July, engaging in offensive reconnaissance, ambushes, and coordinated airstrikes. These actions, spanning areas from Tougouri to Sebba, including Yaadga and Soum, resulted in the elimination of many militants and forced others to flee, according to security sources. The period concluded with a clear demonstration of superior firepower against the enemy and its supporters.

Key engagements included the neutralization of several militants during Operation Toodima 4 in Sebba on July 31, and another group on July 30 by a patrol from Pensa. Earlier, on July 25, Operation FORSATEC Horonya concluded in Kinséré with militants subdued and logistics seized. Terrorist supply routes were also disrupted on July 23 near Bangossogo. A significant encounter on July 21 near Toulfé saw combatants, supported by aerial vectors, neutralize militants, including a terrorist leader identified as TALL Khalidou, alias Yéro, alias Wahilou.

Further actions involved the neutralization of an enemy spotter with communication equipment on July 20 near Gomboro, and repelled surprise attacks on friendly positions north of Djibo on the same night. These events followed successful ambushes by the 7th BIR in Kiembara on July 18 and the preceding day. The month began with forces repelling an enemy attempt to attack Tougouri on July 16, forcing a hasty retreat.

Why it mattersThe operations directly affect the safety and security of civilians in the affected regions by reducing militant presence and disrupting their activities.

Azize Ouédraogo Builds Digital Transformation in Burkina Faso

An IT engineer and entrepreneur, Ouédraogo has spent over two decades shaping West Africa's digital landscape.

Abdel Azize Ouédraogo, an IT engineer and entrepreneur, is a key figure in Burkina Faso's digital transformation efforts. With over twenty years of experience in information technology across West Africa and Europe, Ouédraogo has witnessed and influenced the evolution of the internet, from its early days before social media and smartphones to the current era of cloud computing and artificial intelligence. His career has spanned roles from developer and project manager to digital governance advisor, consistently focusing on leveraging technology for organizational and user benefit.

Ouédraogo's technical expertise was instrumental in the early development of Lefaso.net, one of Burkina Faso's first major online news portals, which he helped launch in 2003. This pioneering work established a platform for Burkinabe citizens, both domestically and in the diaspora, to access national information daily. His journey reflects the broader shift of traditional media towards the internet and the creation of new digital public spaces.

Educated in computer science in Yamoussoukro and later as an IT engineer specializing in new technologies, systems, and networks in Paris, Ouédraogo's foundation in software engineering in France provided him with a deep technical understanding. This technical mastery has remained a core element of his career, even as he transitioned into strategic, management, and governance roles, guiding technological advancements and digital infrastructure projects.

Why it mattersAzize Ouédraogo's work in digital transformation and information technology directly impacts businesses and the development of digital infrastructure in Burkina Faso.

Mandatory Helmet Rule Fuels Helmet Sales for Merchants

Increased demand for helmets in Ouagadougou has boosted merchant revenues following strict enforcement of the mandatory helmet law.

Burkina Faso's Ministry of Security has enforced a mandatory helmet-wearing policy, creating a thriving business for helmet vendors. With individuals being systematically turned away for not wearing helmets, demand has surged, leading to increased sales and profits for merchants. This new reality has transformed the helmet into a sought-after commodity, particularly in popular areas like Paspanga in Ouagadougou.

Vendors like Issouf Zoundi, who sells fuel and helmets, report significant increases in their sales since the government mandated helmet use. He notes that beyond protecting road users, the policy has become a lucrative business. Djibril Ouattara, another helmet seller, has also seen a record number of sales, attributing the rise to police turning away unhelmeted riders. He expressed satisfaction with his turnover, though he did not specify sales figures.

However, the increased demand has led to price hikes, with one customer, Zakaria Derra, complaining that a helmet previously costing six thousand F CFA now sells for ten thousand F CFA. He urged vendors not to exploit the executive's decision and to sell helmets at fair prices. Another customer, Tasseré Ouédraogo, purchased a helmet for personal protection and to comply with regulations, commending the authorities for their strictness in potentially reducing road fatalities.

Why it mattersHelmet merchants in Ouagadougou are experiencing increased sales and profits due to the mandatory helmet law, while consumers face higher prices.

World Vision Project Ends, Boosting Resilience in Bankui and Sourou

The SAVE Plus project, funded by the Regional Humanitarian Fund, concluded with an assessment workshop in Dédougou.

World Vision International Burkina Faso has concluded its SAVE Plus project, aimed at reinforcing integrated multisectoral assistance to vulnerable populations in emergency situations. The project, implemented over nearly twelve months in the Bankui and Sourou regions, focused on nutrition, protection, water, hygiene, and sanitation. A final assessment workshop was held in Dédougou on August 28, 2026, to review activities and impact.

The initiative was supported financially by the Regional Humanitarian Fund for West and Central Africa (FHRAOC), which contributed approximately 500 million FCFA, and World Vision, which added 150 million FCFA, totaling around 650 million FCFA. Partners included ANADI-Burkina Faso, All for peace and dignity (APADE), and SOS jeunesse et défis. The project's interventions spanned communes such as Dédougou, Tougan, Nouna, Solenzo, and Djibasso.

Key achievements highlighted included the construction of five human-powered water pumps, improved nutritional support for children in Tougan, and the establishment of child protection frameworks and 'child-friendly spaces' for the psychosocial care of approximately 710 children. Local authorities emphasized the importance of sustaining the project's gains to benefit the communities affected by the security crisis.

Why it mattersVulnerable populations in the Bankui and Sourou regions benefited from improved access to water, nutrition, and psychosocial support.
