Burkina Faso thanks Mali for hosting expelled gold miners

Minister Mahamadou Sana conveyed gratitude to Mali for assisting Burkinabè citizens expelled from Guinea.

Burkina Faso's Security Minister Mahamadou Sana expressed gratitude to Mali on Wednesday in Bamako for hosting Burkinabè gold miners expelled from Guinea. The minister, acting on behalf of Captain Ibrahim Traoré, met with his Malian counterpart, General Daoud Aly Mohammedine, to discuss the situation of these young miners. Sana thanked the Malian people and government for their support and care for the Burkinabè citizens.

The visit aimed to assess the condition of the expelled miners and arrange their safe and dignified return to Burkina Faso. Minister Sana conveyed the high authorities' concern for their citizens. Both ministers discussed measures to assist the miners and organize their secure repatriation to their families. This initiative highlights the solidarity between Burkina Faso and Mali, both members of the Sahel States Confederation (AES).

Around 200 buses have been mobilized in Ouagadougou to facilitate the return of these Burkinabè nationals. Transport companies have shown significant engagement, with many vehicles already en route or preparing to depart towards the Mali-Guinea border. This collective effort underscores the government's commitment to ensuring the dignity of its citizens abroad.

Why it mattersBurkinabè citizens expelled from Guinea are being assisted and repatriated with the help of Mali and transport companies.

Prosecutor seeks 24-month jail term for lawyer

Ouagadougou prosecutor requests 2 million CFA francs fine for lawyer Ini Esther Doli for alleged defamation.

In Ouagadougou, prosecutors on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, requested a 24-month prison sentence and a 2 million CFA francs fine, all to be served strictly, against lawyer Ini Esther Doli. She is accused of alleged defamation against the municipal police of Ouagadougou in the case concerning the eviction of the "Allah Ti Kouma" establishment.

The proceedings were initiated by the commune of Ouagadougou following two Facebook posts by the lawyer on August 15, 2026. The commune's special delegation stated that her comments, suggesting that individuals involved in the eviction might be current or former clients, harmed the honor of municipal police officers. Doli denied targeting the police, stating her remarks were part of a broader reflection on prostitution.

The civil party requested a symbolic one CFA franc for damages, while the defense argued for acquittal, citing a lack of intent and connection. The court has set November 25, 2026, for its deliberation. The lawyer had previously been granted provisional liberty on September 30, 2026.

Why it mattersLawyer Ini Esther Doli faces a prison sentence and a fine for alleged defamation related to comments about a police operation.

Burkina Faso and Türkiye boost partnership

Ambassador Feriba Hokkaci Esirgen noted progress in defense, education, and health cooperation.

Burkina Faso and Türkiye are deepening their bilateral relationship, with Ambassador Feriba Hokkaci Esirgen celebrating the 103rd anniversary of the Republic of Türkiye in Ouagadougou on November 2, 2026. Esirgen praised Burkina Faso's efforts towards economic and social sovereignty, including energy independence and national self-sufficiency initiatives.

The ambassador highlighted the dynamic relationship, citing the first political consultations held in Ankara on October 20, 2026, as a key development. Health cooperation has also advanced, with three kidney transplant operations completed and a fourth mission scheduled before the end of 2026.

Türkiye intends to continue supporting Burkina Faso's development and its fight against terrorism. Ambassador Esirgen affirmed her country's commitment to backing Ouagadougou's strategies for peace and prosperity.

Why it mattersThe partnership between Burkina Faso and Türkiye is expanding to include defense, education, and health, potentially impacting the lives of Burkinabe citizens.

Burkina Faso launches patriotism conferences nationwide

Justice Minister Edasso Rodrigue Bayala launched public talks on civics and patriotism in Kadiogo province on November 3, 2026.

Justice Minister Edasso Rodrigue Bayala officially launched public conferences on civics, citizenship, patriotic engagement, and peace culture in Burkina Faso's Kadiogo province on November 3, 2026. These conferences, an initiative of the ministry in charge of citizenship, aim to instill patriotism and civics in all 47 provinces. The talks also seek to bring populations closer to the ideals of the popular progressive revolution, including sovereignty and autonomy.

Minister Bayala delivered a message from President Ibrahim Traoré, urging citizens to cultivate sincere patriotism and responsible citizenship. He stated the conferences will reinforce citizen engagement against imperialism and terrorism, and promote community values for national development. The minister expressed a desire to see a "new Burkinabè" who loves their country and supports development initiatives.

The initiative is a collaboration between the ministry, the Presidential Initiative for Community Development, and the National Bureau of Major Projects. Similar events are occurring across the country, including the launch of the "Ligue de la souveraineté" in Fada N’Gourma on November 3, 2026, which aims to use football to develop young talent and promote social cohesion.

Why it mattersThe conferences aim to foster patriotism and civic engagement among Burkinabè citizens, particularly in the context of fighting imperialism and terrorism.

Burkina journalists trained on terrorism finance investigations

30 journalists learned techniques for investigating illicit trafficking and funding of terrorism in Burkina Faso and the Sahel.

30 Burkinabè journalists participated in a training workshop in Koudougou from October 27 to 30, 2026. The event, organized by the Norbert-Zongo National Press Cell (CENOZO) with support from the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), focused on investigating illicit trafficking and the financing of terrorism.

The training aimed to enhance journalists' understanding of terrorism financing mechanisms in Burkina Faso and the Sahel, improve their investigative skills for illicit trades, and bolster their cybersecurity and source protection capabilities. Arnaud Ouédraogo, CENOZO coordinator, stated the goal was to equip journalists to better understand illicit economies fueling terrorist groups and to raise public awareness.

Participants learned about open-source intelligence tools, including satellite imagery analysis, and financial data collection techniques. Trainer Elza Sandrine Sawadogo emphasized the importance of identifying company ownership and real beneficiaries. Ignace Sossou covered cyberattack typologies and digital security for journalists.

Why it mattersBurkinabè journalists received training to investigate the illicit economies that fund terrorist groups, aiming to improve public awareness and inform authorities.
