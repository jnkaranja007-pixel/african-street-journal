Rwanda and Uganda launch three-year police plan against cross-border crime

The programme sets out joint investigations, intelligence-sharing and coordinated operations against trafficking, terrorism, smuggling and cybercrime.

Sources: Rwanda Dispatch and KT Press · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Rwanda and Uganda launched a three-year programme to strengthen police cooperation against cross-border crime, agreeing the plan on November 17 during talks in Kigali. The two forces plan joint investigations, intelligence-sharing, training and coordinated security operations.

Rwanda National Police chief Felix Namuhoranye said the partnership's effectiveness depended on implementing the commitments. Uganda Police Force chief Abbas Byakagaba said the plan would help the countries tackle crimes that individual institutions cannot address alone. The programme also covers drug trafficking and the exchange of instructors and security specialists.

The agreement builds on a framework the forces signed on August 22, 2025, for crime prevention, investigations, intelligence-sharing and professional development. The new programme runs for three years.

Why it mattersOfficers in both forces will be expected to carry out joint investigations, exchange instructors and run coordinated operations over the next three years.
