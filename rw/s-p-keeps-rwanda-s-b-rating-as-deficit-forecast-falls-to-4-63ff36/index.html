S&P keeps Rwanda's B+ rating as deficit forecast falls to 4.9% of GDP

The agency cites growth and concessional borrowing, and the airport's financing arrangement cut second-phase spending by Rwf168.2 billion in 2026.

Source: Taarifa Rwanda · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Standard & Poor's (S&P) Global Ratings maintained Rwanda's B+ sovereign credit rating, citing economic growth, improved public financial management and the structure of external debt. The agency expects the fiscal deficit for the 2025/26 financial year to reach about 4.9% of gross domestic product, below the initially planned 5.7%, because tax revenues beat expectations and spending fell.

S&P said the economy grew 9.3% in 2025 and about 10% year on year in the first half of 2026, led by manufacturing and services. The Ministry of Finance reported public and publicly guaranteed debt of Rwf17.85 trillion by June, equal to 69.1% of GDP, down from 74.8% a year earlier. More than 87% of external debt consists of concessional loans, which carry lower interest rates and longer repayment periods.

The agency projects 7.5% growth in 2026 and an average of about 7% a year from 2027 to 2029. Financing involving the World Bank Group's International Development Association and Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency reduced spending on the New Kigali International Airport's second phase by Rwf168.2 billion in 2026, and full commercial operations are expected around mid-2028.

Why it mattersLower borrowing costs on most external debt give the government more room to fund infrastructure, and the airport's cheaper financing frees public money that would otherwise go to construction.
