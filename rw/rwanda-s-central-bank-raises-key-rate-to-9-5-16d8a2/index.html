Rwanda's central bank raises key rate to 9.5%

The 75-basis-point increase follows October inflation of 19.5%, driven in part by fresh food prices up 39.9% from a year earlier.

Sources: KT Press and Rwanda Dispatch · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

The National Bank of Rwanda raised its Central Bank Rate to 9.5% from 8.75% on Thursday, November 26, after a meeting of its Monetary Policy Committee. Governor Soraya Hakuziyaremye announced the 75-basis-point increase as the bank sought to contain rising inflation.

Annual headline inflation averaged 15.8% in the third quarter of 2026, compared with 13.2% in the previous quarter, then reached 19.5% in October, according to the National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda. Fresh food prices were 39.9% higher than a year earlier, while food and non-alcoholic beverages rose 28.9%. Core inflation, which excludes fresh food and energy, was 11.5%.

The bank targets inflation of 2% to 8% and has a medium-term objective of 5%.

Why it mattersRwandan households face higher food costs, with fresh food prices 39.9% above their level a year earlier in October.
