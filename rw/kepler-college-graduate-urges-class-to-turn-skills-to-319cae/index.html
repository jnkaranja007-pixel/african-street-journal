Kepler College graduate urges class to turn skills to community problems

Oceane Kamikazi told the college's second graduating class that they were trained to create solutions, not wait for them, and that the discipline they built should be used across.

Sources: KT Press and Taarifa Rwanda · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Oceane Kamikazi, speaking for the Class of 2026 at Kepler College in Kigali, urged graduates to use what they learned to address challenges in their communities and across Africa. She said the three-year course had tested students with deadlines, presentations and demanding group assignments.

Kamikazi, who graduated with a Bachelor of Arts in Project Management, recalled struggling to reach a typing speed of 50 words per minute in her first trimester. After scores of 44, 42 and 45 words per minute, she made one final attempt and reached the target. She said the experience taught her staying power, and that classmates learned to help one another when someone fell behind.

The graduation was the college's second and the 11th cohort to graduate through its partnership with Southern New Hampshire University. At the same ceremony, Minister of Sports Nelly Mukazayire urged the 322 graduates from eight African countries to stay teachable and keep up regular exercise.

Why it mattersGraduates from eight African countries leave with a qualification their college says is meant to be used on problems in their own communities.
