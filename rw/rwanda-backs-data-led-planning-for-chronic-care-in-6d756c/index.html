Rwanda backs data-led planning for chronic care in community clinics

Health Minister of State Yvan Butera says reliable data should guide resources as Rwanda brings non-communicable disease services closer to patients.

Source: Taarifa Rwanda · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Rwanda is strengthening the use of health data to guide planning and bring care for non-communicable diseases and other chronic conditions into community-based primary healthcare. Health Minister of State Dr Yvan Butera set out the approach at a panel on data and decision-making during the 2026 International Conference on Public Health in Africa in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia.

Butera said reliable data helps identify population health needs and direct resources. He said community services should offer prevention, screening, diagnosis and treatment for chronic conditions, including cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, cancer and chronic respiratory diseases. The approach is intended to bring those services closer to where patients live.

The conference, convened by the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, brought together policymakers, researchers and public health experts to discuss the continent’s health priorities. Its 2026 discussions focused on using data and research to shape policy and direct limited resources.

Why it mattersRwanda’s health planners must use reliable data to allocate resources and bring prevention, screening, diagnosis and treatment for chronic conditions into community services.
