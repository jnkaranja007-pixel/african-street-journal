Rwanda expands HPV vaccine to women up to 45

The country aims to eliminate cervical cancer as a public health problem by 2027, adding a new preventive measure.

Source: Rwanda Dispatch · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Free human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination is now open to eligible women and girls aged 30 to 45 in Rwanda, a step that widens access to cervical cancer prevention. Recipients can get the shot at health centres nationwide from Monday, November 16. Two doses are given, the second six months after the first, and applicants must show a national identity card or another recognised document to prove their age.

The expansion complements Rwanda's routine HPV campaign for 12-year-old girls and its cervical cancer screening and treatment services, according to the Ministry of Health and the Rwanda Biomedical Centre (RBC). Vaccination coverage among girls has stayed above 90 per cent, but RBC reported in February that only 31 per cent of women aged 30 to 49 had been screened with high-performance tests.

Under Mission 2027, the accelerated plan to eliminate cervical cancer, Rwanda aims to screen 70 per cent of women aged 30 to 49 and ensure 90 per cent of women diagnosed with precancerous lesions or invasive cancer receive timely treatment. The disease was the second most common cancer among Rwandan women in 2022, with 866 new cases and 609 deaths.
