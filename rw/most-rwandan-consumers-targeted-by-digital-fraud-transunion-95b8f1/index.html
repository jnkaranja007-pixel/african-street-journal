Most Rwandan consumers targeted by digital fraud, TransUnion survey finds

Money and gift card scams were the most reported schemes, while fears of identity misuse topped consumers' concerns.

Source: Taarifa Rwanda · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

A TransUnion survey found that 62% of Rwandans surveyed had been targeted by email, online, phone or text-message fraud during a recent three-month period. The findings appear in the company's H2 2026 update to its Top Fraud Trends Report.

Among those targeted, 43% reported money or gift card scams, 30% reported fraudulent calls designed to obtain personal information, and 29% reported scams by third-party sellers on legitimate e-commerce sites. Asked about consequences beyond losing access to accounts, 62% in a separate Q1 2026 study said they were most concerned that someone might use their identity to commit a crime, and 45% cited access to workplace systems.

TransUnion Rwanda chief executive Didier Mutabazi said criminals were combining stolen identities, compromised credentials and AI-powered deception to target consumers at scale. Consumers also named fake social media profiles, cited by 52%, and email phishing, cited by 51%, as the attack methods that worried them most.

Why it mattersMobile money and online banking users are the ones handing over the credentials that fraudsters are now chasing, according to the survey.
