Rwanda’s health leaders hear youth demands for accessible adolescent services

Young speakers say invitations to meetings are not enough; they want their ideas to shape decisions and care at health facilities.

Source: Rwanda Dispatch · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Young people urged Rwanda’s health decision-makers to let adolescents shape reproductive health policies and services at the country’s seventh annual adolescent sexual and reproductive health conference, which opened in Kigali on Thursday. The two-day meeting, organised by the Health Development Initiative and partners, ended on Friday, 20 November.

Ornella Imena of the initiative’s Youth Advisory Committee said participation means young people’s ideas influence decisions and service delivery. She called for reliable information, youth-friendly services and safe places to seek help. Speakers also described their experiences obtaining contraceptives, including where they went and how health workers treated them. Rwanda’s 2025 health services law lowered the age of consent for reproductive health care from 18 to 15, allowing those aged 15 and above to seek contraception without parental consent.

Rwanda Biomedical Centre representative Dr Aline Uwimana said services close to young people must be maintained as financing priorities change. The UN Population Fund’s Rwanda representative said more than 800 teachers at technical and vocational education institutions had been trained, alongside the introduction of comprehensive sexuality education in schools.

Why it mattersAdolescents aged 15 and above can seek reproductive health services, including contraception, without parental consent under the 2025 law, while young speakers are pressing for care shaped by their input.
