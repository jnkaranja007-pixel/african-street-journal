IER and MUA partner on engineering insurance

The Institute of Engineers Rwanda and MUA Insurance will offer tailored products and training to over 5,000 members.

The Institute of Engineers Rwanda (IER) and MUA Insurance signed a partnership agreement on Wednesday, October 28, at MUA's headquarters. This collaboration aims to provide Rwanda's Engineering Practitioners with specialized insurance products, reduced rates, and risk-management training. The agreement covers engineers, technologists, technicians, and consulting firms represented by IER.

IER President Eng. Philbert Mbanza stated the partnership will help members secure cover for risks in design, construction, and on-site work, making it more profitable for both parties. MUA's Konde Bugingingo added that the insurer was chosen for its strong interest and broad cover options, and that pooling members could lower costs compared to individual policies.

The deal is expected to boost IER membership, which has over 5,000 members and grows by 25% annually. Professional indemnity insurance, though not mandatory, is being discussed as a key offering. MUA plans to develop services based on risk data to improve safety and expects the partnership to increase public confidence in infrastructure projects.

Why it mattersEngineering Practitioners in Rwanda will gain access to specialized insurance and risk management training, potentially lowering costs and increasing confidence in their work.

Rwanda seeks $386 million from South Korean firm

The government filed a counterclaim against KT Corporation over a failed 4G network project at the Permanent Court of Arbitration.

Rwanda has filed a $386 million counterclaim against South Korean telecommunications company KT Corporation over a failed 4G network project. The government, along with the Rwanda Development Board, Rwanda Social Security Board, and Agaciro Development Fund, filed the claim on October 20, 2026, with the Permanent Court of Arbitration.

KT Corporation, which owns 51% of the joint venture KT Rwanda Networks, disclosed the filing on October 23, stating it would challenge Rwanda's damage calculations. The dispute centres on the 2013 joint venture established to build and operate Rwanda’s 4G LTE broadband infrastructure.

The $386 million claim is more than 1.4 times Rwanda's agriculture and livestock budget for 2026/27 and represents about 7.3% of the national budget for the same fiscal year. The arbitration process will determine the outcome of Rwanda's financial claim.

Why it mattersRwanda is seeking $386 million from KT Corporation in an international arbitration over a failed 4G network project.

New Auditor General urged to improve audit compliance

Claudette Mukashyaka faces challenge of ensuring public institutions implement audit recommendations.

Rwanda’s new Auditor General of State Finances, Claudette Mukashyaka, has been tasked with investigating why public institutions fail to implement audit recommendations. The Senate’s Committee on Economic Affairs and Finance raised the issue during its review of Mukashyaka’s appointment on October 29, 2026. Senators also called for annual audits of all institutions receiving public funds.

Senator Dr Frank Habineza stated that Mukashyaka has the necessary academic and professional background for the role, having worked at the Office of the Auditor General for four years. He noted that while Mukashyaka intends to improve audit quality, senators stressed the need for institutions to act on audit findings. The latest annual report showed that 65 per cent of audit recommendations were implemented in 2025, an increase from 60 per cent in 2024.

The report covered 96 per cent of government expenditure across 252 public entities. In 2025, 97 per cent of public entities received unqualified audit opinions, up from 94 per cent in 2024. The proportion receiving unqualified conclusions on compliance rose from 75 per cent to 83 per cent. Mukashyaka was appointed by the Cabinet on October 26, 2026, succeeding Alexis Kamuhire.

Why it mattersThe new Auditor General, Claudette Mukashyaka, must address the failure of public institutions to implement audit recommendations, impacting the proper management of public assets and finances.

Rwanda police add 1,652 officers to prevent crime

Interior Minister Dr. Vincent Biruta urged new recruits to anticipate threats and work with citizens to stop crime before it happens.

Rwanda's police force has added 1,652 new officers, including 337 women, to better combat crime. The graduates from Gishari Police Training School were challenged to prevent offenses before they occur.

Interior Minister Dr. Vincent Biruta told the new officers that modern policing requires anticipating threats, using technology, and cooperating with citizens. He stressed the need for a force that prevents crime rather than just responding after the fact.

The police will confront a range of crimes, from theft and drug abuse to cybercrime, human trafficking, and terrorism. Minister Biruta stated that officers must develop their capabilities to address these evolving security challenges.

Why it mattersThe addition of new police officers and the directive to proactively prevent crime directly impacts the safety and security of Rwandan citizens.

Rwanda PM tells Russian forum youth can rebuild nations

Justin Nsengiyumva warned that young people's energy can be manipulated to fuel violence, citing the 1994 genocide.

Rwanda’s Prime Minister Justin Nsengiyumva told a Russia–Africa youth forum in Moscow on October 29 that the country's post-genocide recovery offers lessons for young people. Nsengiyumva stated that Rwanda's experience showed how youth can rebuild a nation but also how their energy can be used to fuel violence.

Speaking at MGIMO University, Nsengiyumva said that over one million people died in the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi in 100 days. He explained that young people were manipulated by the regime responsible for the atrocities, but others fought to end the tragedy. The Prime Minister highlighted the role of the Rwanda Patriotic Army, led by Paul Kagame, in securing the country and choosing reconciliation over revenge.

Nsengiyumva outlined Rwanda’s recovery strategies, including fighting corruption, progressive business rules, and investments in infrastructure, digital transformation, and skills. He also mentioned Rwanda’s visa-free policy for all African citizens, which he said removes barriers for economic integration and youth opportunities. The Prime Minister asserted that Africa's future depends on countries working together to enable young people to move, exchange ideas, and build businesses.

Why it mattersYoung Africans can learn from Rwanda's post-genocide recovery on how to rebuild their nations and avoid manipulation.
