Singapore approves Rwanda water filter project

The carbon-financed initiative will provide 1.8 million Rwandans with safe drinking water and reduce emissions.

Singapore's National Environment Agency (NEA) approved a carbon-financed water project for Rwanda on November 14, which will distribute 300,000 ceramic filters to provide safe drinking water for about 1.8 million people. This is the first project approved under the Singapore-Rwanda carbon market agreement, designed to mobilize financing for emissions reductions and sustainable development.

Developed by Climate Bridge International, the Safe Water Supply Project will distribute filters to households and early childhood development centres, particularly in the Western Province, eliminating the need to boil water with firewood or charcoal. The NEA stated this will reduce fuel consumption, emissions, and indoor air pollution, with an initial 10,000 filters distributed in June 2026 and the rest in 2027.

The project, part of a May 2025 agreement under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, is estimated to reduce emissions by 780,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent between 2026 and 2030. Singapore is assessing over 20 other projects under the agreement, which allows carbon credit projects in Rwanda to attract financing linked to emissions reductions, with credits potentially transferable to Singapore for its climate targets.

Why it mattersThe project will provide 1.8 million people with access to safe drinking water by distributing 300,000 ceramic filters, with an initial rollout in June 2026.

Business · 2026-11-15

Rwanda and Cambodia sign three deals to expand investment and trade

The agreements cover investment promotion, tourism and air transport, and were signed during President Paul Kagame's state visit to Phnom Penh.

Rwanda and Cambodia have signed three cooperation agreements to expand economic ties, covering investment promotion, tourism and air transport. The agreements were exchanged on Friday, November 13, at the Peace Palace in Phnom Penh, in the presence of President Paul Kagame and Cambodian Prime Minister Hun Manet.

The first is a memorandum of understanding between the Rwanda Development Board (RDB) and Cambodia's Council for the Development of Cambodia (CDC) on investment promotion. A second memorandum covers tourism, and a third, an Air Services Agreement, sets a framework for air transport cooperation. The air services agreement does not by itself introduce direct flights between Kigali and Phnom Penh.

During their talks, the two leaders discussed expanding trade, investment and tourism, and cooperating against transnational crime, including technology-based scams. Rwanda could serve as an entry point for Cambodian products into African markets, while Cambodia could give Rwandan businesses a gateway to the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) and wider Asian markets.

Why it mattersRwandan businesses gain a framework for investment and air links with Cambodia, though no direct flights have yet been introduced.

Politics · 2026-11-15

Rwanda questions DRC's Francophonie candidate choice

Kinshasa withdraws its candidate but backs a Romanian over another African contender for OIF top job.

Rwanda has questioned the Democratic Republic of Congo's (DRC) decision to withdraw its candidate for the International Organisation of La Francophonie (OIF) Secretary-General position and instead support a Romanian contender. The DRC announced on November 14 that it had withdrawn Juliana Amato Lumumba to foster consensus within the OIF.

Rwanda's Foreign Affairs Minister Olivier Nduhungirehe welcomed Lumumba's withdrawal, stating it could help build consensus for Rwanda's candidate, Louise Mushikiwabo, who is seeking re-election. Nduhungirehe also noted that the withdrawal weakened arguments about a political confrontation between Rwanda and the DRC. However, he questioned the DRC's choice to back a Romanian candidate over other African contenders.

The DRC stated its decision to support Romanian candidate Dacian Cioloș followed consultations with Romania, aiming to unite member states around a common candidate. The OIF Ministerial Conference took place in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, where Lumumba's withdrawal was announced.

Why it mattersRwanda's foreign minister has questioned the Democratic Republic of Congo's decision to back a Romanian candidate for the OIF leadership over an African one.

Sport · 2026-11-15

Mali beats Rwanda 3-2 in Bamako after Amavubi twice led

Mamadou Camara headed in the winner six minutes from time, ending Rwanda's unbeaten start in Group K of 2027 AFCON qualifying.

Mali came from behind twice to beat Rwanda 3-2 in Bamako on Friday, November 13, in the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) qualifier. Amavubi had taken the lead through Jojea Kwizera in the 28th minute and again through Phanuel Kavita, who rose above the Mali defenders to head home a corner in the 53rd minute.

Mali restored parity before halftime in the 42nd minute. Substitute Mamadou Camara then headed in a corner six minutes from time at Stade du 26 Mars, giving the hosts all three points. Rwanda lost for the first time in Group K.

The defeat ended Rwanda's perfect start to the campaign.

Why it mattersRwanda drops its first points in Group K, so its place in the qualifying table is now at risk.
