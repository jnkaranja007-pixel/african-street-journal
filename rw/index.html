Rwanda asks churches to help families

Prime Minister Nsengiyumva urged religious leaders to strengthen marriage preparation and support household finances.

Prime Minister Justin Nsengiyumva has asked churches in Rwanda to help address family breakdown, shrinking household finances, and rising social problems. Speaking on Sunday, October 25, Nsengiyumva stated that unstable families contribute to crime and conflict.

Nsengiyumva, representing President Paul Kagame, called on religious institutions to use their influence to improve people's lives and strengthen families. He urged the Anglican Church and others to enhance marriage preparation programs and work with the government to build resilient families.

The government seeks stronger partnerships with religious institutions to promote responsible behavior and ensure families are grounded in values that support their well-being and national progress. The objective is to build strong families whose members uphold positive values for the country's development.

Rusizi hotel closed over Rwf7.2 million renovation penalty

Hotel du Lake remains shut after owners appealed a fine for alleged construction violations during renovation.

A family-owned hotel valued at over Rwf400 million has been closed for months in Rusizi District following a Rwf7.2 million penalty imposed by local authorities for alleged construction violations during renovation works.

Hotel du Lake, built in the 1980s, has not reopened as its owners appeal the penalty and seek resolution with the district, stating they followed initial instructions for renovation but were surprised by the February 2026 inspection findings.

The owners appealed the penalty on October 21, requesting reconsideration and an amicable resolution to allow the hotel to reopen, but meetings with the district mayor and Vice Mayor Alfred Habimana have not yet resolved the dispute.

Why it mattersThe closure of Hotel du Lake affects the owners' investment and prevents the business from resuming operations.

Rwanda launches AI agency for Korean tech firms

The government aims to make Rwanda a testing ground for artificial intelligence products before they enter other African markets.

Rwanda is establishing a government artificial intelligence agency to help South Korean technology companies test and expand their products within the country. Minister of State for ICT and Innovation Yves Iradukunda announced the initiative, which aims to position Rwanda as a launchpad for these firms into wider African markets. The agency, operational by the end of 2026, will serve as a central hub for private sector collaboration, facilitating access to data, computing power, and AI safety testing.

The agency's technical arm, the AI Factory, will collaborate with companies to build and test applications. Potential applications for AI tools are being explored in healthcare for patient assessment and fetal development monitoring, as well as in agriculture, education, and financial technology. Minister Iradukunda stated that Rwanda offers a favourable entry point for Korean companies seeking to grow their presence across the continent. Initial funding will come from development partners, with plans to recoup costs through paid services.

During his visit to South Korea, Iradukunda met with officials and technology firms to discuss cooperation on AI, digital infrastructure, and smart cities. He also explored opportunities for Korean investment in data centres, suggesting regional resource pooling for such facilities. The government plans to recover some operational costs through paid AI development, training, advisory services, and computing access.

Why it mattersRwanda's new AI agency will help Korean tech firms test and expand their products, impacting the country's technological development and economic growth.

Four recover from Ebola in North Kivu

The Matanda Ebola Treatment Center released four adults, bringing total recoveries to 29 since September 23.

Four patients have recovered from Ebola disease and been released from the Ebola Treatment Center (ETC) at Matanda Secondary Referral Hospital in North Kivu province. The discharged patients, two women and two men, are all adults. Their release on Sunday brings the total number of recovered patients treated at the facility to 29 since its opening on September 23.

Among the survivors is Kyakimwa Kavutirwaki Micheline, a clinical psychologist who contracted the disease while caring for patients at her health facility. After experiencing symptoms, she was treated at the Matanda ETC for 24 days. Dr. Nzanzu Furaha Blaise Pascal, an attending physician, confirmed the total recoveries at the facility now stand at 29.

The Katwa Ebola Treatment Center in Butembo City also discharged six cured patients between October 19 and October 23. During the same week, three other patients were declared non-cases. The hospital management thanked patients for trusting the treatment centers.

Rwanda's liberation movement compared to China's Long March

A Rwandan journalist in Beijing reflects on the 90th anniversary of China's Long March and its parallels to Rwanda's own liberation struggle.

The 90th anniversary of China's Long March in 2026 is prompting reflection on the historical journey of the Red Army and its impact on the nation. Chinese citizens are revisiting this history through documentaries, exhibitions, and discussions.

A Rwandan journalist based in Beijing sees parallels between the Long March and the Rwandan Patriotic Front's liberation movement, which began its armed campaign on October 1, 1990. While acknowledging different circumstances and adversaries, the journalist questions how a movement's determination shapes the nation it helps to build.

This comparison is relevant as China pursues technological advancement and Rwanda focuses on post-genocide reconstruction. The experiences suggest resilience can be a national resource, but the true measure of a movement is what it builds after the struggle concludes.
