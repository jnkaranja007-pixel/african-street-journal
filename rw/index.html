Rwanda says DRC unmet security commitments

Kigali accuses Kinshasa of failing to neutralize the FDLR militia group as per Washington Accords.

Rwanda has accused the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) of failing to meet its security commitments under the Washington Accords, stating the continued presence of the FDLR militia remains a major concern. Rwandan government spokesperson Yolande Makolo told the BBC that the accords contain clear commitments regarding Rwanda’s security, specifically the neutralization of the DR Congo-sponsored FDLR genocidal militia, which are wholly unmet, and with no accountability.

Makolo stated that Rwanda remains prepared to implement the agreements but insisted that progress depends on reciprocal action by the DRC and accountability from the mediator. The Washington Accords were intended to address tensions between Rwanda and the DRC, including security concerns in eastern Congo where fighting involving armed groups has persisted.

At the centre of Rwanda’s concerns is the Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda (FDLR), a militia group linked to perpetrators of the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi. Kigali considers the group an existential security threat and has repeatedly called for its neutralisation. The latest US sanctions add pressure to the regional peace process.

New Auditor General urged to close Rwanda audit gap

Senators want new Auditor General to investigate why public institutions ignore audit recommendations.

Rwanda’s new Auditor General of State Finances, Claudette Mukashyaka, was urged by the Senate’s Committee on Economic Affairs and Finance to investigate why some public institutions fail to implement audit recommendations. Senators also called for annual audits of all institutions receiving public funds. Mukashyaka, appointed on October 26, 2026, previously served as chief audit executive at I&M Bank Rwanda.

Presenting the committee’s report, Senator Dr Frank Habineza stated that Mukashyaka has the academic qualifications and professional experience for the role. He noted her four years at the institution and understanding of its operations. Mukashyaka intends to improve audit quality and ensure work meets international standards, but senators stressed that findings must lead to action by the institutions concerned.

The Office of the Auditor General’s latest annual report showed that 65 per cent of audit recommendations were implemented in 2025, an increase from 60 per cent in 2024, covering 96 per cent of government expenditure across 252 public entities. In 2025, 97 per cent of public entities received unqualified audit opinions, up from 94 per cent in 2024, and unqualified conclusions on compliance rose from 75 per cent to 83 per cent.

Why it mattersThe new Auditor General must ensure public institutions implement audit recommendations to improve the management of government finances and assets.

Politics · 2026-10-31

RPF youth meet in Poland to discuss development

RPF Secretary General Christophe Bazivamo urged young members in Europe to take practical action for Rwanda's development.

Young members of Rwanda's ruling RPF-Inkotanyi party living in Europe convened in Warsaw, Poland, for a retreat focused on their identity and contribution to Rwanda’s development agenda. The meeting brought together party members from across Europe to discuss guiding values and methods for contributing to the country's transformation.

RPF Secretary General Christophe Bazivamo opened the retreat, conveying greetings from President Paul Kagame, who also chairs the party. Bazivamo thanked attendees for their involvement in Rwanda’s development efforts and challenged them to think ambitiously and set examples in their communities.

The retreat provided an opportunity for participants to reflect on their beliefs and renew their commitment to the country’s development goals. Bazivamo stated that RPF members are committed to leading the country's rebuilding and development regardless of their location.

Why it mattersYoung Rwandan RPF party members living in Europe are discussing their role in the country's development.

African Cup of Nations reverts to two-year cycle

Confederation of African Football reverses decision to hold tournament every four years.

The Confederation of African Football (CAF) has decided to return the African Cup of Nations to its traditional two-year cycle. This reverses a December decision to hold the tournament every four years. CAF president Patrice Motsepe stated the biennial Afcon aims to advance African football and its 54 member associations.

The governing body had planned an African Nations League to replace the biennial Afcon but acknowledged it would take years to generate sufficient revenue. The previous four-year plan was impacted by FIFA's expanded 32-team Club World Cup held in June and July. The next Afcon will be hosted by Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda in 2027.

Future tournaments are scheduled for 2028, 2031, and 2033. There will be no tournament in 2030 due to the World Cup. The biennial hosting of Afcon has often caused issues with the European club football calendar, with many recent tournaments held mid-season.
