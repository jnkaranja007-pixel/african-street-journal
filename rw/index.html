Africa CDC chief cites Rwanda and Uganda as Ebola response models

Dr Jean Kaseya made the call at a virtual briefing marking six months since the Ebola outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Africa CDC Director General Dr Jean Kaseya has named Rwanda and Uganda as examples of countries that prepare for and manage public health emergencies well. He spoke at a virtual media briefing marking six months since the Ebola outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, which is still fighting the disease.

Kaseya said controlling an outbreak needs more than medical supplies and treatment facilities. He called for stronger government leadership, greater community involvement and closer cooperation between African countries, and said governments must invest in preparedness, train health workers and strengthen public health systems.

International partners, he added, should coordinate their support under national government leadership.

Why it mattersGovernments that act on his advice would commit money to training health workers and strengthening clinics before the next outbreak arrives.

Rwanda and DRC put rival candidates forward for OIF Secretary General

The contest for the francophone body's top post is being held at its 20th summit in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, while the two countries remain in conflict over eastern DRC.

Rwanda's candidate Louise Mushikiwabo and Congolese candidate Juliana Lumumba are competing for the Secretary General post of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF), the body of French-speaking countries. The vote is to be held at the organisation's 20th summit in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, where 53 heads of state and government will choose the new Secretary General. Lumumba is a former minister and the daughter of DRC independence leader Patrice Lumumba.

The two countries are indirectly at war through the rebellion in eastern DRC, where the Front for Refoundation of the Nation (FRN) rebels have seized large parts of the region, including the provincial capital Goma. Kinshasa accuses Rwanda of backing the FRN, while Kigali questions why Kinshasa has deployed the FDLR militia, a group Washington has designated as a terrorist organisation and which is made up of perpetrators of the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi.

Kinshasa banned Mushikiwabo from Congolese soil after she was elected to the post, and has threatened to leave the OIF if she is re-elected. Kinshasa had also proposed dropping Lumumba's candidacy in favour of a Romanian candidate, an offer Kigali refused, saying no member state should dictate another's choice of candidate.

Why it mattersIf the DRC carries out its threat, it would leave the organisation whose 53 members choose this Secretary General.

Politics · 2026-11-16

Burundi minister attacks Rwanda's OIF candidate

Burundi's foreign minister threatened withdrawal from the OIF if Rwanda's candidate is re-elected.

Burundi's Foreign Affairs Minister Edouard Bizimana attacked Rwanda's candidature for the Secretary General of the Francophonie organisation, stating that a third term for the current leader would "bury" the International Organisation of La Francophonie (OIF). Bizimana claimed the attempt to impose a candidacy served particular interests and threatened that Burundi would withdraw from the organisation if Rwanda's candidate, Louise Mushikiwabo, is re-elected. The announcement came as the OIF prepares to announce its next Secretary General tomorrow morning.

Bizimana's comments come amid frosty relations between Burundi and Rwanda, with Burundi having closed its doors since 2015 and accusing its northern neighbour of interference. Political pundits suggest the contest in Cambodia may mirror a 2025 precedent where Rwanda withdrew from the Economic Community of Central African States (ECCAS) after opposition blocked its expected rotating presidency. Insiders claim French leader Emmanuel Macron urged Rwanda to withdraw Mushikiwabo's candidacy, an attempt described by a commentator as blackmail.

An African Intelligence source claims the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) President Felix Tshisekedi has instructed his information minister to prepare a letter announcing the DRC's withdrawal from the OIF should Mushikiwabo win. This threat has reportedly been presented to several African ministers in Phnom Penh to underscore the DRC's intent. Rwanda has stated it will not withdraw Mushikiwabo's candidature.

Rwanda votes for UN draft on mercenaries

A UN committee approved the Cuban-led text on November 13, with 117 votes in favour, 54 against and seven abstentions.

Rwanda voted in favour of a draft United Nations resolution on the use of mercenaries to violate human rights and obstruct peoples' right to self-determination. The UN General Assembly's Third Committee, which handles social, humanitarian and cultural issues including human rights, approved the text on November 13.

The draft, A/C.3/81/L.35, was presented by Cuba and received 117 votes in favour, 54 against and seven abstentions. It calls on states to prevent their territories from being used to recruit, finance or protect mercenaries involved in efforts to destabilise governments or undermine peoples' right to determine their political future.

The committee's vote approves the draft but is not a final General Assembly adoption; the resolution will now be considered in the Assembly's plenary process. Rwanda's vote matches its stated opposition to mercenaries and its commitment to UN and African Union principles.

Why it mattersGovernments that host or finance private fighters would be required to stop them, and the resolution now moves to the General Assembly plenary.
