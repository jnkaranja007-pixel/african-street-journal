Police Find 50 Teenagers Drugged at Kicukiro Party

Authorities intervened at a gathering where minors aged 15 were found intoxicated and ill from alcohol and vaping.

Police in Kicukiro intervened at a party where over 50 young people, some as young as 15, were found heavily intoxicated and ill from alcohol and vaping. The gathering, which began in the afternoon, was still ongoing late into the morning when officers arrived. Some individuals required hospital treatment due to their condition, prompting police to question parental awareness of their children's whereabouts and activities.

Inspector General of Police Felix Namuhoranye highlighted the incident during Umuganda, directing scrutiny towards parents' oversight. He questioned how teenagers could spend hours consuming harmful substances without parental knowledge, challenging those who go to bed without knowing their children's locations or activities. This comes amid a broader alcohol crisis in Rwanda, with over 500 people treated for consuming unsafe beverages and approximately 50 deaths reported in the first half of 2026.

President Paul Kagame has previously addressed the alcohol crisis, emphasizing that public life must take precedence over business losses. He also questioned the effectiveness of regulatory bodies in preventing dangerous products from reaching consumers. Namuhoranye expanded the "Hehe n’Icyuma" campaign to "Hehe n’imico mibi," targeting destructive behavior beyond just illicit alcohol, acknowledging that enforcement alone has limitations.

Why it mattersTeenagers aged 15 and older are affected by being found intoxicated and ill at parties, requiring hospital treatment and raising concerns about parental supervision.

Rwanda Urges African Union to Fund Peace Efforts

Prime Minister Nsengiyumva told the AU summit that predictable African financing is crucial for implementing peace decisions.

Rwanda's Prime Minister, Dr. Justin Nsengiyumva, has urged African leaders to move beyond adopting peace and security decisions without securing predictable African resources for their implementation. Speaking at the 21st African Union Extraordinary Session in Luanda, Angola, Nsengiyumva, representing President Paul Kagame, stated that the continent cannot achieve lasting peace while relying on external partners for conflict response and mediation.

Nsengiyumva emphasized that the effectiveness of summits will be measured by the collective ability to implement decisions with the necessary discipline and predictable financing. Africa faces increasingly prolonged and complex conflicts, straining governments and regional organizations. The continent's capacity to finance its own peace and security architecture remains a significant challenge, making the African Union Peace Fund a critical component of the solution for Rwanda.

The Prime Minister's remarks highlight a persistent issue for the African Union: the gap between adopting resolutions and having the financial means to enact them. This reliance on external funding can hinder the continent's autonomy in addressing its security challenges and achieving sustainable peace.

Why it mattersAfrican leaders are affected by the challenge of financing peace efforts, as their ability to implement decisions and address conflicts depends on predictable African resources.

Rwanda Volleyball Team Advances Unbeaten to Championship Quarterfinals

The national women's volleyball team secured a perfect group stage record, advancing to the CAVB African Nations Championship quarterfinals without dropping a set.

Rwanda's national women's volleyball team has stormed into the quarterfinals of the CAVB African Nations Championship in Kenya, maintaining a flawless record through the group stage. The team secured their spot in the last eight with a decisive 3-0 victory over Burkina Faso, winning sets with scores of 25-19, 25-16, and 25-8.

Throughout the match, Rwanda demonstrated clear dominance, with increasing set margins highlighting their control over Burkina Faso. The opposing team struggled to counter Rwanda's pressure, particularly in the critical third set. This victory concludes an impressive group stage performance where Rwanda won every match without losing a single set.

The team's unbeaten run now positions them for a more challenging encounter in the knockout stage as they continue their pursuit of a strong performance in the continental championship. Their consistent success sets a high bar for their upcoming matches.

Why it mattersThe Rwandan women's volleyball team's success in the African Championship provides national pride and highlights athletic achievement for Rwandans.

Alliance Launches Strategy for Africa's Food Systems

The Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT will unveil its 2026-2030 strategy in Kigali, focusing on science, investment, and partnerships to enhance food and land systems.

The Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT is set to launch its 2026-2030 strategy for Africa in Kigali next week. The initiative places science, investment, and partnerships at the core of efforts to build more resilient and sustainable food and land systems across the continent. This strategy aims to translate scientific evidence and innovation into practical solutions for farmers, communities, policymakers, and businesses.

The strategy, which follows the Alliance's global launch in July, will be unveiled on September 1 during a high-level event at the Africa Food Systems Forum at the Kigali Convention Centre. It identifies four key priorities: linking science with sustainability, fostering inclusive food systems, strengthening partnerships, and mobilizing finance to scale up proven solutions. The Alliance emphasizes that transforming food systems requires greater access to evidence and tools for informed decision-making by all stakeholders.

Investment is highlighted as a critical component of the Kigali launch, with the Alliance noting that financial resources are essential for accelerating the adoption of effective solutions. The event will convene representatives from the science and investment sectors to explore how evidence can guide capital towards initiatives that bolster food security, create jobs, protect ecosystems, and improve livelihoods. The strategy aligns with the global theme 'Resilient Futures: Urgent Science for Food and Land Systems Transformation.'

Why it mattersFarmers and investors are directly affected by the strategy's focus on practical scientific solutions and mobilizing finance for food and land systems.

Rwanda Schools Impose Varied, Extensive Requirements on Parents

Parents face significant financial and material demands beyond tuition as the new academic year begins, with school requirements varying widely.

As Rwanda's 2026-2027 academic year approaches, parents are bracing for the "Babyeyi," lists of fees, uniforms, books, and other supplies mandated by schools. An investigation into documents from 25 Rwandan schools revealed substantial disparities in these requirements, which extend beyond tuition and include items like toilet paper and bedding.

These findings echo concerns raised in 2020 by then-Minister of State for Primary and Secondary Education, Dr. Isaac Munyakazi, who warned against excessive school demands. Despite his statement, "We will not tolerate this," the current school term's requirements suggest the issue persists, with some schools demanding extensive provisions from families.

For instance, Flair Nursery and Day Care Center lists nursery tuition at 900,000 RWF per term. Additional costs include optional swimming lessons at 70,000 RWF per term and annual playing materials worth 30,000 RWF. Enrollment also necessitates passport photographs for both the child and parents.

Why it mattersParents are directly affected by the wide range of mandatory expenses and material provisions required by schools for their children's education.
