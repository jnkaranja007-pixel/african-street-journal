Mushikiwabo seeks third term leading Francophonie

Rwandan Louise Mushikiwabo is running for re-election as head of the international organisation on November 16.

Rwandan national Louise Mushikiwabo is seeking a third term as the leader of La Francophonie, with elections scheduled for November 16 in Phnom Penh. She faces competition from Juliana Amato Lumumba of Congo, Coumba Bâ of Mauritania, and Dacian Cioloș of Romania. Kinshasa has accused Kigali of aiding rebels, a claim Rwanda denies, while Kigali accuses Kinshasa of supporting FDLR rebels.

Mushikiwabo, who has led the organisation since 2018 and was re-elected in 2022, benefits from incumbency and familiarity with the organisation's mechanisms. French President Emmanuel Macron previously backed her candidacy, stating she has "all the skills for the job." Rwandan President Paul Kagame has actively lobbied African leaders and OIF member states to support her bid for re-election.

Congolese candidate Juliana Amato Lumumba's bid aims to leverage the DRC's demographic weight for institutional influence. Her candidacy raises questions about the Francophonie's focus on major demographic and cultural hubs. The DRC views its candidate's challenge as a stance against Rwanda, potentially complicating Lumumba's efforts to gain broader support beyond Kinshasa's allies.

Kagame, Antigua PM seek expanded Africa-Caribbean trade

Rwandan president and Antigua and Barbuda prime minister discussed boosting commercial ties between the regions.

President Paul Kagame met with Antigua and Barbuda Prime Minister Gaston Browne on November 3 to discuss expanding trade and investment between Africa and the Caribbean. The leaders held discussions during the 2026 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting on Jumby Bay Island.

Browne encouraged greater engagement between African and Caribbean businesses, seeking mutually beneficial commercial opportunities. He stated that current global challenges offer chances for Global South countries to deepen cooperation and build stronger economic partnerships. Browne affirmed Antigua and Barbuda’s support for Rwanda on matters of mutual interest.

Kagame congratulated Browne on Antigua and Barbuda hosting CHOGM 2026 and expressed Rwanda's interest in closer relations with the country and the wider Commonwealth. Browne also serves as Commonwealth Chair-in-Office.

Why it mattersRwandan businesses and investors may find new opportunities for trade and investment in the Caribbean region.

Rwanda showcases dried fruits and chili at China expo

The nation aims to boost exports to China, which reached $227 million in 2025.

Rwanda is exhibiting coffee, tea, honey, chili, dried fruits, and macadamia nuts at the ninth China International Import Expo in Shanghai, which began on November 5 and runs until November 10. The country is using the six-day event to seek increased exports to the Chinese market and promote investment, tourism, and cultural exchange.

Rwanda’s Ambassador to China, Lt Gen (Rtd) Emmanuel Karenzi Karake, stated that the expo offers a significant platform for Rwandan businesses, including smallholder farmers and women-led cooperatives, to connect with Chinese consumers. This participation coincides with 55 years of diplomatic ties between Rwanda and China.

Bilateral trade between the two nations reached approximately $849 million in 2025, a substantial increase from $300 million in 2019. Rwandan exports to China alone grew by 549% from $35 million in 2019 to $227 million in 2025, while Chinese investment in Rwanda rose by 19% to $548.5 million in 2025.

Why it mattersRwandan farmers and cooperatives can reach Chinese consumers and establish commercial partnerships through the expo.

Equity Bank Rwanda launches EMMA wallet app

The digital wallet allows users to make payments, send and receive money, and save towards personal goals.

Equity Bank Rwanda has introduced EMMA, a new digital wallet app designed to bring banking services closer to Rwandans. The app allows users to make payments, send and receive money, save for personal goals, and access credit if eligible. EMMA connects to Rwanda’s national payment network, including eKash, MTN MoMo, Airtel Money, and other banks.

Customers can register for EMMA using an active mobile number and accepted identification, without needing an existing Equity Bank account. The app is accessible via a mobile number or by dialing *555*6# on supported networks. Many everyday transactions, such as transfers between EMMA users or to Equity accounts and payments at PWE tills, are free, while transfers to other banks cost RWF 20.

At the November 5 launch in Kigali, Managing Director Hannington Namara stated that while Rwanda has progressed in money movement, the next step is ensuring access improves lives. He noted FinScope 2024 data showing 96% of Rwandan adults are financially included, with 22% holding bank accounts. Namara emphasized EMMA's success will be measured by active use and saving habits, not just downloads.

Why it mattersThe EMMA wallet app aims to improve financial access and convenience for Rwandan adults, including small business owners like Diane Uwase.

Earthquake shakes homes across Rwanda

Tremors felt in Kayonza, Nyagatare, Rwamagana, Ngoma and Kirehe districts after 5.1 magnitude quake

Residents in several parts of Rwanda felt their houses shaking on Thursday, November 5, following an earthquake with a magnitude of 5.1. The European-Mediterranean Seismological Centre (EMSC) reported the epicentre was near Ushirombo, Tanzania. Tremors were felt across the region, including in Kigali.

Five residents confirmed experiencing the tremors, with some reporting their motorcycles and market stalls shaking. Baraka Murenzi, a motorcycle taxi operator in Kayonza District, Eastern Province, described feeling his motorcycle shake while receiving payment. Mukamurenzi Beatrice in Rwamagana said market stalls shook beneath people.

The Rwanda Seismic Monitor (RMB) stated that no damage had been reported following the earthquake. Other residents in Kayonza, Nyagatare, Rwamagana, Ngoma, and Kirehe also reported experiencing shaking inside their homes.

Why it mattersResidents in multiple Rwandan districts experienced shaking homes and surroundings due to an earthquake originating in Tanzania.
