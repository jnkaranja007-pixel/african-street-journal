Rwanda Schools Impose Varied, Extensive Requirements on Parents

An investigation into school requirement lists, known as 'Babyeyi,' reveals significant disparities in costs and items demanded from parents across Rwanda.

As Rwanda's 2026-2027 academic year approaches, thousands of parents face the 'Babyeyi,' or school requirement lists, which include fees, uniforms, and supplies beyond tuition. An investigation by Kigali Today Ltd, examining documents from 25 schools, found striking differences in what institutions demand. These findings echo concerns raised in 2020 by Dr. Isaac Munyakazi, then State Minister for primary and secondary education, who warned against excessive school requirements.

The requirements for the upcoming school term include a wide range of items, from standard school supplies to more specific needs. For instance, Flair Nursery and Day Care Center lists nursery tuition at 900,000 RWF per term. Additionally, parents must provide playing materials worth 30,000 RWF annually and may opt for optional swimming lessons at 70,000 RWF per term. Enrollment also necessitates two passport photographs of the child and one for each parent.

The disparities in 'Babyeyi' lists suggest that schools are setting different financial and material burdens on families. While tuition is a primary concern, the additional costs for uniforms, books, toiletries, and other supplies can significantly increase the overall expense for parents. This situation highlights ongoing challenges in standardizing educational costs and ensuring equitable demands across different institutions.

Why it mattersParents are affected by the significant financial and material burdens imposed by schools through varied and extensive 'Babyeyi' requirement lists.

Police Find Over 50 Young People Drugged at House Party

Inspector General of Police CG Felix Namuhoranye revealed the discovery of intoxicated minors and young adults during a nationwide campaign against substance abuse.

Rwanda launched a nationwide campaign against alcohol and drug abuse on Saturday, coinciding with the monthly community work, Umuganda. The initiative targets illicit and dangerous alcoholic drinks, some of which are believed to be imported. Senior government and security officials joined hundreds of residents across the country to raise awareness about the dangers of substance abuse.

During the campaign in Kicukiro, Inspector General of Police CG Felix Namuhoranye disclosed that police recently found over 50 young people, including minors, drugged and using substances in a single house. The affected individuals, aged from 15 to 28, were found in various states of intoxication, with some vomiting and others requiring hospitalization after falling into comas. They were later released to their parents.

Namuhoranye stated that the incident occurred in Kicukiro approximately two weeks prior. He noted that some of the young people had never consumed alcohol before, while others were already heavily intoxicated or smoking marijuana. The police chief highlighted that the group included both boys and girls, with ages ranging from 15 to 28 years old.

Young Farmer Gains Land and Independence Through Agricultural Support

Adeline Gwizimpundu transitioned from renting land to owning it after accessing credit for seeds and fertilizer from One Acre Fund.

Adeline Gwizimpundu, a 28-year-old farmer from Kayonza district, Rwanda, has transformed her agricultural career by securing land and becoming a full-time farmer. Initially renting one hectare to grow beans, maize, cassava, and Irish potatoes, she found that renting alone was insufficient to cover essential farming costs like seeds and fertilizer. Her situation changed when she joined One Acre Fund, an organization that provided her with access to quality farm inputs on credit.

Through this support, Adeline was able to progressively increase her harvests, build a more sustainable livelihood, and eventually earn enough income to purchase her own land. She now aims for her children to view farming with the same professional respect as careers like doctors or engineers. This journey challenges the notion that young people are disinterested in agriculture, suggesting instead that they seek opportunities for wealth creation and entrepreneurship within the sector.

Rwanda's demographic, with over 70% of its population under 35, presents a significant opportunity to engage a dynamic generation in agriculture. Organizations like One Acre Fund are adapting their support systems to meet the varied needs of young farmers, addressing challenges such as access to land, affordable inputs, finance, and markets. For those like Adeline, who has achieved success, the focus shifts to market access, while new farmers may require different forms of assistance.

Why it mattersYoung farmers like Adeline Gwizimpundu are affected by their ability to access credit for inputs and gain market access, which enables them to build wealth and establish farming as a viable profession.
