Kagame Urges Youth to See Food as Life's Foundation

President Paul Kagame told young African entrepreneurs that food systems are essential for survival, health, and the continent's future development.

President Paul Kagame has urged young Africans to view food not just as a right, but as the foundation of life and development. Speaking at the Africa Food Systems Forum 2026 in Kigali, he emphasized that securing adequate and nutritious food is directly linked to the continent's broader economic ambitions. The dialogue with entrepreneurs highlighted the growing role of youth in transforming Africa's agricultural sector, moving beyond subsistence farming towards a more productive and commercially viable industry.

The forum, themed "Investing in Africa’s Agri-Food Systems: Nourishing Nations, Growing Jobs, Building Resilience," brought together thousands to discuss attracting investment, strengthening food value chains, and improving nutrition. Kagame stressed the need for integrated systems connecting farmers with technology, financing, markets, and consumers. He pointed out that opportunities exist across the entire food value chain, from production to the final consumer, creating potential for jobs and entrepreneurship.

Entrepreneurs like Fareeda Mustapha from Ghana, Musa Mishamo from Tanzania, Imungana Malikana from Zambia, and Lydia Murekatete from Rwanda participated in the dialogue. Their involvement underscored the forum's focus on practical solutions for agricultural challenges. The discussions aim to promote innovation and private-sector investment to build resilient economies and improve livelihoods across Africa.

Why it mattersYoung African entrepreneurs are affected by President Kagame's call to explore opportunities across the entire food value chain, which he presented as essential for life and development.

IFAD, Bank of Kigali inject 21M USD into Rwandan agriculture

The financing aims to boost agricultural productivity and food security by expanding access to finance, technology, and technical support for small and medium-scale farmers.

A $21 million financing injection from the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD), in partnership with Bank of Kigali, will expand access to finance, technology, and technical support for small and medium-scale farmers. This initiative is designed to overcome obstacles like limited access to affordable credit and modern agricultural technology, enabling farmers to invest in productive assets and activities across various value chains.

The program will provide technical assistance to help farmers adopt improved farming practices and more efficient production methods. IFAD Vice-President Dr. Gérardine Mukeshimana stated that agricultural investment is critical for building resilient food systems and improving livelihoods. The partnership with Bank of Kigali is expected to widen financial opportunities for farmers who might not meet conventional lending requirements.

This $21 million investment supports modernizing agriculture through technology adoption and improved techniques, potentially increasing productivity and commercial viability. For Rwanda, where agriculture is a major source of employment and income, stronger capital access could stimulate job creation and strengthen agricultural value chains from production to markets, transforming the sector into a more productive and market-oriented contributor to economic growth.

Why it mattersSmall and medium-scale farmers in Rwanda are affected by receiving expanded access to finance, technology, and technical support to boost their agricultural productivity and incomes.

Evra Honors Genocide Victims Before Gorilla Naming Ceremony

Former footballer Patrice Evra visited the Kigali Genocide Memorial ahead of Rwanda's annual Kwita Izina gorilla naming event.

Former Manchester United player Patrice Evra paid tribute to victims of the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi during a visit to the Kigali Genocide Memorial. Evra is in Rwanda to participate in the 21st edition of Kwita Izina, the country's annual gorilla naming ceremony, scheduled for September 4, 2026, in Kinigi. The memorial serves as a site for remembrance and education, preserving the memory of over one million people killed during the genocide.

Evra is among several international figures invited to this year's Kwita Izina, where he is expected to name one of 22 baby mountain gorillas. The ceremony in Musanze District is a prominent conservation event that draws global attention to Rwanda's efforts in protecting endangered mountain gorillas. His participation, due to his high profile, is anticipated to provide additional international spotlight on the event.

The Kwita Izina celebrations will be followed by a concert in Kigali featuring Afrobeats star Rema and Rwandan artist Kenny Sol. This event aims to celebrate the conclusion of the gorilla naming festivities through music. The combined activities, from remembrance to conservation and entertainment, highlight Rwanda's efforts in rebuilding and promoting its national story on an international stage.

Why it mattersInternational personalities like Patrice Evra are drawn to Rwanda's conservation events, boosting the profile of tourism and national heritage.

Rwanda Deputy Governor Urges Action on AML Rules

Nick Barigye stated that anti-money laundering systems must demonstrate measurable results in disrupting financial crime, not just compliance.

Countries in Eastern and Southern Africa are being urged to move beyond simply meeting regulatory requirements for combating money laundering and instead focus on achieving tangible results against financial crime. This call came during the 2026 Annual Meetings of the Eastern and Southern Africa Anti-Money Laundering Group (ESAAMLG), attended by representatives from 22 member states and international bodies like the FATF, World Bank, and IMF.

Rwanda’s central bank Deputy Governor, Nick Barigye, emphasized that the true measure of anti-money laundering systems lies in their effectiveness at disrupting illicit financial flows, recovering criminal assets, and dismantling networks. He highlighted the increasing cross-jurisdictional nature of financial crime, stressing the critical need for enhanced international cooperation and faster exchange of financial intelligence to track and recover proceeds hidden through complex corporate structures.

The challenge is substantial across the region, with Rwanda's Financial Intelligence Centre reporting fraud, embezzlement, human trafficking, illegal foreign exchange, and tax evasion as frequent crimes between 2019 and 2024. Officials noted that identifying money movement is insufficient; authorities must also uncover beneficial ownership to link suspicious transactions to individuals. Delays in information exchange remain a major concern, allowing criminals to move assets, underscoring the need for closer collaboration among various national and international agencies.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the call for stronger anti-money laundering measures and beneficial ownership transparency aims to disrupt illicit financial flows and recover criminal assets, potentially improving the integrity of the financial system.
