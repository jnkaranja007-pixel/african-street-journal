Rwanda seeks hundreds of millions in investment with Raffles Family Office

The Asian wealth management firm will connect international investors with priority projects including Formula 1.

Rwanda has partnered with Raffles Family Office, an Asian wealth management firm, to attract hundreds of millions of dollars in private investment. The agreement with the Rwanda Development Board (RDB), announced Oct. 22, 2026, aims to connect international investors with priority economic development projects. Raffles Family Office will promote opportunities in aviation, tourism, real estate, and infrastructure, including developments for the country’s planned Formula 1 Grand Prix.

The partnership seeks to connect wealthy individuals and families through Raffles Family Office’s network with projects like Kigali International Airport in Bugesera and Airport City. RDB Chief Executive Jean-Guy Afrika stated that private-sector participation is important for realizing investment opportunities associated with Formula 1, which is expected to catalyze business development and job creation.

Raffles Family Office Chief Executive Chi Man Kwan highlighted the potential for international capital to support long-term investment in Rwanda and Africa. The firm appointed Jean de Dieu Uwihanganye, a former Rwandan minister of state, as principal for emerging markets in September 2026 to expand its engagement across the continent.

Why it mattersRwanda seeks to attract hundreds of millions of dollars in private investment for national development projects.

Rwanda targets 45% adult insurance cover by 2035

The National Insurance Strategy aims to boost penetration from 1.9% to 5% of GDP, covering 2.1 million adults.

Rwanda's insurance sector assets have doubled to Rwf1.14 trillion, with 18 licensed insurers now operating. The National Bank of Rwanda recorded Rwf1.141 trillion in assets by December 2024, with public medical insurers holding over 61.8% of this total. Currently, 27% of Rwandan adults, approximately 2.1 million people, use insurance, a rise from 1.2 million in 2020.

Herbert Asiimwe, Head of Financial Sector Development at the Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning, presented these figures at the International Conference on Inclusive Insurance in Kigali on October 20. The National Insurance Strategy aims to increase insurance penetration to 5% of GDP by 2035 and targets 45% adult coverage by the same year. Private policy ownership stands at 7.5%, with a goal of 15%.

The strategy also targets increasing insurance density to Rwf40,000 per person by 2035, up from Rwf25,000. Specific focus is placed on farmers and SACCO members, with a goal to cover one million farmers by 2030 under the National Agricultural Insurance Scheme, where the government subsidizes 40% of premiums. A proposed rainfall-based policy for mine workers aims to trigger payments without individual claims.

Why it mattersRwanda's insurance strategy aims to increase coverage for adults and specific groups like farmers and mine workers.

Kagame urges youth to link discipline to societal conduct

President Paul Kagame told over 10,000 young Rwandans at BK Arena that individual choices impact families and communities.

President Paul Kagame met over 10,000 young Rwandans at BK Arena on Wednesday, October 21, to discuss societal challenges and shared responsibility. The dialogue focused on issues like dressing, alcohol abuse, and public service delivery, prompting discussions on discipline and cultural values.

During the meeting, Kagame emphasized that discipline should extend beyond the individual to encompass how people interact with others, stating that individual choices affect families, communities, and the public. He also highlighted the importance of education on the dangers of substance abuse, drawing on the experiences of those affected.

The president challenged officials to act on poor service delivery, a concern raised by a young attendee about public institutions. The effectiveness of the message delivered at BK Arena in fostering a genuine shift in attitudes and behavior remains a key question.

Why it mattersYoung Rwandans are directly addressed by their president on issues of personal conduct and its societal impact.

NBA Africa convenes leaders in Nairobi

The Basketball Africa League has contributed over US$250 million to Africa’s GDP since 2021.

NBA Africa gathered leaders and stakeholders in Nairobi on Thursday, October 22, to discuss sport’s capacity to foster trust, strengthen communities, and create opportunities across the continent. CEO Clare Akamanzi stated the organisation seeks input from industry leaders to shape future development.

Oliver Wyman estimates Africa’s sports market is valued at over US$12 billion and could reach more than US$20 billion by 2035, supporting tourism, investment, infrastructure, and jobs. The Basketball Africa League alone has generated over US$250 million for Africa’s GDP since 2021, creating nearly 37,000 jobs over its first four seasons.

In Kenya, the NBA Africa M-PESA Jr. NBA programme is set to engage over 10,000 young people in basketball and financial literacy. Akamanzi stressed at the Global Trust Summit in Nairobi that public belief in delivered promises is key.

Why it mattersRwanda's CEO Clare Akamanzi participated in discussions about how sport can build trust and create opportunities across Africa.

Agasaro wins silver at cycling championships

Gloria Agasaro finished second in the 38.2km cadet girls road race at the CAC African Cycling Championships.

Gloria Agasaro of Rwanda secured a silver medal in the cadet girls road race at the CAC African Cycling Championships on Thursday. She finished the 38.2km race just one second behind Zimbabwe's Beamish Beamish, who won gold. Agasaro's compatriot Liza Jehovaire took the bronze medal, giving Rwanda two of the three podium spots.

This was Agasaro's first appearance at the championships, and she expressed satisfaction with her performance, stating it meant a lot to her. Beamish completed the race in 1 hour and 18 minutes. Rwanda's Cynthia Mwamikazi did not finish the race.

Rwanda currently leads the medal table with five medals, including three gold, one silver, and one bronze. South Africa also has five medals but fewer golds. In other events, Rwanda's Byirigiro Moubarak finished fourth in the men's youth road race.
