Kagame defends alcohol ban, links DRC involvement to FDLR presence

President Paul Kagame addressed journalists in Kigali, explaining the government's crackdown on substandard alcohol and reiterating Rwanda's security concerns regarding the FDLR.

President Paul Kagame defended Rwanda's recent ban on locally produced and imported liquors, stating that substandard alcohol has caused deaths and health issues among Rwandans. He emphasized that the government's primary responsibility is to protect its citizens, even if it means questioning factory owners responsible for producing harmful products.

Kagame clarified that the ban includes some imported liquors found to lack standards and be unfit for human consumption. He asserted that the fight against illicit alcohol would not tolerate such production, regardless of practices in other countries, and suggested factory owners should be held accountable by courts.

Additionally, President Kagame stated that Rwanda will remain involved in the conflict in the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo as long as the Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda (FDLR) remains active there. He stressed that the FDLR's continued presence across Rwanda's border constitutes a direct security threat that Kigali cannot ignore.

Why it mattersRwandan citizens are directly affected by the government's ban on certain alcohol products and by the ongoing security situation in the eastern DRC.

RMB Staff Depart for Russia to Study Mining

Five employees from the Rwanda Mines, Petroleum and Gas Board (RMB) have traveled to Russia to pursue Master’s studies, aiming to enhance expertise in Rwanda’s extractive sectors.

Five employees of the Rwanda Mines, Petroleum and Gas Board (RMB) have departed for Russia to begin Master’s studies at Empress Catherine II Saint Petersburg Mining University. This initiative is part of a broader effort to strengthen professional expertise within Rwanda’s mining, petroleum, and gas sectors, according to Taarifa Rwanda.

These five officials are among 60 Rwandan beneficiaries of scholarships provided by the Russian government this year. The program seeks to support Rwandans in advancing their education in various fields, with a focus on specialized skills for managing and developing the country’s mineral and energy resources.

The scholarships are expected to provide advanced knowledge and international exposure to the RMB employees, which can then be applied to Rwanda’s extractive industries. This move also highlights growing educational cooperation between Rwanda and Russia, particularly in technical and specialized fields like mineral resource management.

Why it mattersRwandan extractive industries will gain enhanced technical and managerial capabilities from the returning staff, potentially leading to more efficient resource management and greater economic value.

Rwanda creates rules for Sudanese clubs in national football league

Rwanda has established special regulations for Sudanese guest clubs Al Hilal SC and Al Merrikh SC participating in its BK Pro League for the 2026–27 season.

Rwanda’s BK Pro League will again host Sudanese football giants Al Hilal SC and Al Merrikh SC for a second consecutive season, operating under special regulations. These rules address the unusual situation of foreign clubs competing for the top position in a national league, as Al Hilal won the 2025–26 Rwanda Premier League.

The Sudanese clubs moved their domestic operations to Rwanda after the war in Sudan, which began in April 2023, disrupted their home league. They previously played in Mauritania during the 2024–25 season before joining Rwanda’s league for the 2025–26 season.

Under the new arrangement, if a Sudanese club finishes first in the league, it will receive an honorary trophy. However, the official BK Pro League championship trophy will be awarded to the highest-ranked Rwandan club, ensuring the national championship remains with a Rwandan team.

Why it mattersSudanese football fans are affected as their clubs continue to play in a foreign league, while Rwandan football stakeholders must adapt to new rules regarding championship titles.

Rwanda backs 249 UN human rights recommendations, notes 37 others

Rwanda has supported 249 of 286 recommendations from its fourth Universal Periodic Review, indicating a commitment to strengthen human rights protections.

Rwanda has formally supported 249 of the 286 human rights recommendations presented during its fourth Universal Periodic Review (UPR) at the United Nations Human Rights Council on January twenty-first, two thousand twenty-six. The government also noted 37 other recommendations, following its examination of all proposals.

The supported recommendations include measures to improve prison conditions, expand healthcare and legal aid access, and strengthen rehabilitation for detainees. Rwanda also backed calls for independent investigations into allegations of torture, arbitrary detention, and extrajudicial killings, as well as prosecution of those responsible.

Additionally, the government supported recommendations on freedom of expression, media protection, and reviewing the 2024 law governing non-governmental organizations to ensure it does not restrict civil society activities. Recommendations on political participation, social and economic rights, gender equality, and environmental protection also received support.

Why it mattersThis development affects Rwandan citizens by potentially improving their access to justice, healthcare, education, and strengthening protections against various forms of violence and discrimination.

KoraLink empowers youth with health product sales in rural Rwanda

A new initiative is creating income opportunities for young people, particularly women, by enabling them to sell essential health products in underserved communities.

KoraLink is expanding access to essential health products in rural Rwanda by training and supporting young entrepreneurs. The program provides financially disadvantaged youth, aged 18 to 35, with product knowledge, business management skills, and an interest-free investment of Rwf360,000 in installments. This capital helps them establish businesses selling items like malaria prevention products, HIV and STI prevention products, sanitary pads, and water-treatment products.

The initiative, a collaboration between the Mastercard Foundation, The Commons Project Foundation Africa (TCP Africa), Society for Family Health Rwanda (SFH Rwanda), and ICT Chamber, aims to overcome challenges of long travel distances and infrequent market days for rural residents. Dr. Richard Gakuba, Executive Secretary of TCP Africa, stated that the program supports youth, especially women and those in rural areas, to earn livelihoods.

Agents like Bamurere Sylvie, 27, from Ngarama Sector, reported earning Rwf58,000 in profit within her first three months, with aspirations to become a livestock farmer. Niyokwizera Isaac, 26, from Rugarama Sector, grew his initial Rwf50,000 capital to order stock worth up to Rwf200,000 and invested in farmland and fertilizer for maize. Shakira Mwiza, 25, used profits to buy a computer and offer digital services, receiving working space from the Rwimiyaga Sector administration.

Why it mattersThis initiative directly affects young, financially disadvantaged individuals in rural Rwanda by providing them with business opportunities and access to essential health products, improving their economic standing and community well-being.
