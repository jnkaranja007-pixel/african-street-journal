Rwanda Central Bank Hikes Rate to 8.75% as Inflation Reaches 14.5%

The National Bank of Rwanda increased its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to combat accelerating inflation, which has surpassed the target range and reached 14.5%.

Rwanda's central bank has raised its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to 8.75%, a move aimed at curbing escalating inflation that reached 14.5% in July. The Monetary Policy Committee announced the decision on August 26, citing elevated price pressures and increased economic risks. This marks a continuation of the monetary tightening cycle that began in late 2025, as inflation significantly exceeds the bank's target range of 2%-8%.

Headline inflation surged from 9.1% in the first quarter of 2026 to 13.2% in the second quarter, culminating in the 14.5% figure for July. The National Bank of Rwanda projects inflation to average 13.1% for 2026, a slight decrease from the prior forecast of 13.9%. Increases in core, fresh-food, and energy prices are driving this trend, with energy inflation notably surging to 45.7%.

Despite inflationary challenges, Rwanda's economy demonstrated strong growth, expanding by 10% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026. However, the central bank anticipates inflation will remain above its target throughout 2026, with a gradual return expected in the latter half of 2027. Risks such as the El Niño climate pattern's impact on food prices and geopolitical tensions contributing to elevated global commodity prices were also identified.

Why it mattersInvestors face higher borrowing costs and potentially slower economic growth due to the central bank's decision to increase interest rates.

RSSB Acquires Full Ownership of Inyange Industries and Ruliba Clays

The Rwanda Social Security Board has purchased the remaining shares in the food processing and construction materials companies from Crystal Ventures Limited, gaining complete.

The Rwanda Social Security Board (RSSB) now holds full ownership of Inyange Industries Ltd and Ruliba Clays Ltd, having acquired the remaining shares from Crystal Ventures Limited (CVL). Previously, RSSB held a 40 percent stake in Inyange Industries, a major food and beverage producer, and 50 percent in Ruliba Clays, which manufactures clay-based building products like bricks and tiles. This acquisition expands RSSB's direct investment portfolio and grants it greater control over the strategic direction and future expansion of both companies.

The transaction signifies a significant shift in the ownership structure of these two prominent Rwandan businesses. Inyange Industries is known for its dairy products and beverages, while Ruliba Clays' products are certified under national standards. Crystal Ventures, which rebranded from Tri-Star Investments in 2009, has divested these assets as part of its evolving investment portfolio. Details regarding the transaction's value and terms were not immediately disclosed.

This move positions RSSB as a more dominant institutional investor, with the potential for increased returns to support its managed social security schemes. The acquisition allows RSSB to directly influence investment decisions and growth strategies for both Inyange Industries and Ruliba Clays, integrating them more fully into its broader investment objectives. The full ownership is expected to streamline operations and enhance the long-term financial sustainability of the social security funds.

Why it mattersThis acquisition affects investors by granting the Rwanda Social Security Board full control over two key Rwandan companies, potentially influencing their strategic direction and future expansion.

Ferwafa Boss Faces 30-Day Detention Over $600,000 Unfunded Cheques

Prosecutors allege Fabrice Shema issued cheques totaling US$600,000 without sufficient funds, while Shema claims the issue is a commercial dispute over payment arrangements.

Prosecutors are seeking a 30-day detention for businessman and FERWAFA President Fabrice Shema as investigations continue into two cheques totaling US$600,000, approximately Rwf883.5 million, which authorities claim were issued without adequate funds. Shema, arrested on August 11, 2026, denies charges of issuing unfunded cheques and fraudulent acquisition of property, asserting the case arises from a commercial disagreement with a foreign partner.

The dispute centers on a commercial agreement worth over €1 million between Shema’s company, Africa Medical Supply, and UAE-based ABZL International General Trading LLC. Prosecutors stated that Africa Medical Supply failed to settle the amount within 60 days of a January 23, 2024 agreement. Following payment demands, Shema allegedly issued two cheques on August 5, 2025, for US$400,000 and US$200,000. Bank inquiries reportedly revealed less than US$1,000 in the account.

Shema's defense contends that the case stems from payment difficulties, not fraud. He stated that his company has a history of successful transactions with ABZL and attempted a US$400,000 payment in December 2024, which failed due to account issues. He argued that the contract required ABZL to provide an alternative account, and the cheques were issued as part of efforts to resolve the payment problem while a new account was identified. One cheque was allegedly undated.

Why it mattersInvestors face uncertainty regarding the financial dealings and potential fraud allegations involving a prominent business figure and a significant commercial agreement.

Kenya, Rwanda Deepen Trade and Investment Ties

Bilateral trade has nearly doubled in five years, approaching half a billion dollars, as Kenyan firms invest in Rwanda's financial and education sectors.

Kenya and Rwanda are strengthening their strategic partnership through increased trade, investment, security cooperation, and people-to-people exchanges, according to Kenya’s Principal Secretary for Foreign Affairs, Dr. Korir Sing’Oei. He stated that bilateral trade has nearly doubled over the past five years, now approaching half a billion dollars, highlighting concrete economic interests driving the relationship. Kenyan companies like Equity Bank and KCB are significant investors in Rwanda's financial sector, with additional investments in education and other areas.

Rwanda is also investing in Kenya's strategic infrastructure, including the Kenya Pipeline Company, demonstrating a reciprocal economic engagement beyond diplomatic ties. Sing’Oei described the relationship as "brotherly and sisterly," noting its expansion driven by shared economic and strategic interests and built on mutual trust. Security cooperation, including military collaboration and intelligence sharing, remains a key pillar of this deepening alliance.

The strong people-to-people connections are evident, with Rwandans living, working, and studying in Kenya, and approximately ten thousand Kenyans residing in Rwanda for work, study, and business. Sing’Oei also addressed the Africa Mindset Reset Forum, urging African leaders to overcome the fear of failure to seize opportunities and achieve objectives, emphasizing learning from mistakes to drive progress.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the near doubling of bilateral trade and growing investment links between Kenya and Rwanda, indicating expanding economic opportunities.

Rwanda Financial Sector Assets Reach Rwf17.5 Trillion

The National Bank of Rwanda reported a 23% annual expansion in the financial sector, with lending to the economy increasing by 22% to Rwf7 trillion.

Rwanda's financial system has expanded to Rwf17.5 trillion in assets, with banks, insurers, and pension funds increasing their economic investments. The National Bank of Rwanda (BNR) announced the sector grew by 23% in the past year, while overall lending to the economy rose 22% to approximately Rwf7 trillion.

Agriculture, a sector previously facing challenges in securing formal financing, saw a significant 177% surge in new lending during the first half of 2026, totaling Rwf57 billion. Construction remains the largest recipient of credit, increasing its share of outstanding loans to 23% from 19% in the previous year, with trade, households, and manufacturing also receiving substantial funding.

BNR Governor Soraya Hakuziyaremye stated that while the economy grew by 10% in the first quarter, some sectors receive less financing due to the need to balance rapid expansion with resilience against inflation and global uncertainty. She emphasized that financial institutions must lend prudently, addressing challenges like corporate governance and transparency to improve financing access.

Why it mattersFarmers may benefit from increased agricultural lending, which saw a 177% jump, potentially improving access to capital for agricultural activities.
