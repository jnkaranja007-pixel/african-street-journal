Rwanda Pension Fund Buys Full Control of Three Companies

The Rwanda Social Security Board acquired remaining shares in two firms and completed a purchase in a third, signaling an aggressive investment strategy.

The Rwanda Social Security Board (RSSB) has completed a five-day acquisition spree, taking full ownership of three major Rwandan companies: Inyange Industries, Ruliba Clays, and BK General Insurance. The transactions, which included purchasing the remaining 60% of Inyange and 50% of Ruliba from Crystal Ventures Limited, and a Rwf31.7 billion deal for BK General Insurance, mark a significant shift towards direct investment in established businesses.

These acquisitions position RSSB as the sole owner of these companies and reflect its growing financial capacity, with assets under management reaching Rwf3.9 trillion. RSSB Chief Executive Regis Rugemanshuro stated the move is part of a new five-year strategy to generate sustainable, long-term returns for pension members, moving beyond traditional securities.

The targeted companies show strong performance metrics. Inyange Industries, Rwanda’s largest food and beverage producer, saw its revenue climb from Rwf28 billion in 2021 to Rwf68.9 billion in 2025, with a profit after tax of Rwf1.35 billion last year. This financial strength underpins RSSB's investment strategy.

Why it mattersFarmers are indirectly affected as the largest food and beverage company in Rwanda is now fully owned by the national pension fund, potentially influencing its production and market strategies.

Rwanda, GGGI Seek Investment to Turn Waste Into Resources

Minister of Environment Dr. Bernadette Arakwiye and Global Green Growth Institute Deputy Director-General Dr.

Rwanda is actively seeking to transform waste into an economic and environmental asset, bolstering efforts in sustainable waste management and climate-resilient infrastructure. Environment Minister Dr. Bernadette Arakwiye met with Global Green Growth Institute (GGGI) Deputy Director-General Dr. Mallé Fofana to explore avenues for increased investment in waste management and the development of infrastructure supporting the nation's green-growth objectives.

The discussions centered on attracting capital for climate-resilient infrastructure, enhancing waste management systems, and fostering a circular economy where waste is recovered and repurposed. Minister Arakwiye highlighted waste as a source of innovation, investment, and job creation, stating, “By converting waste into useful resources, we are not only reducing environmental impact and mitigating emissions, but we are also creating new economic opportunities.” This aligns with Rwanda's goal of a zero-waste circular economy by 2035.

Current initiatives, like the Waste to Resources Project at the Nduba landfill, include facilities for sorting recyclables and processing organic waste into fertilizer. The sorting facility handles up to 100 tonnes daily, while the biowaste treatment plant processes approximately five tonnes per day. Rwanda is seeking technologies, financing, and partnerships to improve waste collection, sorting, recycling, and treatment, aiming to create economic value and reduce landfill pressure.

Why it mattersRwanda is seeking investment and partnerships to improve waste management, viewing waste as a resource for economic growth, job creation, and climate resilience.

Rwanda Faces Below-Average Rains and Strong Winds in September

Meteo Rwanda forecasts lower than usual rainfall and increased wind speeds across the country for the upcoming month.

Rwanda is bracing for a drier than average September, with rainfall expected to be between 20mm and 120mm, falling short of the typical 35mm to 213mm for the month, according to Meteo Rwanda. The first ten days are predicted to be mostly dry with sunny periods, receiving less than 5mm to 30mm of rain. However, certain areas, including parts of Musanze, Rubavu, and western and northern Burera and Nyabihu, could see higher amounts ranging from 80mm to 120mm.

Temperatures are also anticipated to be slightly above the long-term September average, with daytime highs reaching 22°C to 33°C and nighttime lows between 10°C and 20°C. The hottest conditions, 31°C to 33°C, are expected in Bugesera, parts of Kigali, Nyagatare, and other eastern and southern regions. Conversely, cooler nights, 10°C to 12°C, are forecast for higher altitude areas in Musanze and Nyaruguru.

The weather agency also predicts moderate to strong winds across Rwanda, with speeds ranging from 4 to 12 metres per second. Specific areas like Rubavu, Rutsiro, Karongi, and Nyamasheke are likely to experience the higher end of wind speeds, between 8 to 12 metres per second. Residents in these vulnerable districts have been advised to implement preventive measures against potential strong wind impacts.

Why it mattersFarmers and residents in specific districts face potential impacts from reduced rainfall and strong winds, affecting crop yields and infrastructure.

Rwanda Receives Over 3,000 Asylum Seekers Evacuated From Libya

Since 2019, Rwanda has provided refuge to vulnerable individuals fleeing dangerous conditions in Libya through the Emergency Transit Mechanism program.

Rwanda has received more than 3,000 refugees and asylum seekers evacuated from Libya since 2019. This initiative, known as the Emergency Transit Mechanism (ETM), was established through a partnership between Rwanda, the UNHCR, and the African Union to offer a safe route for vulnerable individuals stranded in Libyan detention centers.

The program remains active in 2026, with recent arrivals in June and August. In June, 173 individuals arrived, including Sudanese, Eritreans, South Sudanese, Ethiopians, and Somalis. August saw another 177 arrivals, with a similar breakdown of nationalities. These evacuees are currently housed at the Gashora Emergency Transit Centre in Bugesera District.

The ETM aims to provide immediate safety and arrange longer-term solutions for those seeking refuge. The program was created to remove vulnerable Africans from dangerous conditions in Libya, offering them a pathway to safety and potential resettlement or onward journeys.

Why it mattersOver 3,000 asylum seekers are provided with safety and temporary shelter in Rwanda, escaping dangerous conditions in Libya.

Family Values Push Threatens Women's Rights Globally

Experts at the UN General Assembly warn that narratives of family, culture, and religion are being used to undermine gender equality and reproductive health.

Hard-won women's rights face a new threat from increased demands for the restoration of family values, according to rights experts and civil society groups meeting at the 81st United Nations General Assembly. The discussions focused on growing anti-rights mobilization and its potential impact on gender equality, sexual and reproductive health, and human rights accountability. Sessions highlighted the exploitation of family, cultural, and religious narratives in political and legal debates.

Dr. Robert Eno of the African Court on Human and Peoples’ Rights noted that a proposed African Charter on "family sovereignty and values" is not currently scheduled for AU Assembly consideration and faces a lengthy institutional process. However, he cautioned against complacency, stating the broader contest is about how societies define family, culture, religion, equality, and human dignity, citing ongoing issues like unequal inheritance rights and restrictions on divorce.

Rehema Namukose of Musawah explained that anti-rights actors are increasingly using the language of family protection to delay reforms affecting women, citing examples in Ghana, Kenya, and Uganda where "family protection" bills gain traction while legislation for women's rights faces delays. She linked this framing to practices like child marriage and female genital mutilation, and to economic disadvantages for women, arguing that such language can preserve patriarchal power rather than protect families.

Why it mattersWomen and girls are affected by the potential rollback of rights related to marriage, divorce, custody, bodily autonomy, and economic equality through the exploitation of 'family values' narratives.
