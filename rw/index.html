China Expands Rwanda Hospital With Rwf85 Billion Investment

The Masaka Hospital expansion, funded by China, has increased bed capacity sevenfold and added advanced medical facilities to serve Kigali and beyond.

China has officially handed over the expanded Masaka Hospital to Rwanda, a project costing approximately Rwf85 billion. The four-year initiative transformed the facility from 6,398 square metres to 56,957 square metres, increasing its bed capacity from 127 to 837. This expansion is China's largest grant-funded health infrastructure project in Rwanda to date, aiming to significantly boost healthcare access in Kigali and surrounding areas.

The upgraded hospital now features 18 operating theatres, modern diagnostic facilities including MRI services, four X-ray machines, and specialized treatment infrastructure. It also includes critical-care systems like a medical-gas generator, backup power, and a helicopter landing pad, enhancing its capacity for emergencies and complex cases. The University Teaching Hospital of Kigali (CHUK) is set to move its services to this expanded facility in phases.

Rwanda's Minister of Health, Dr. Sabin Nsanzimana, stated that the hospital is an investment in the nation's health, enabling more specialized services closer to home. This development strengthens Rwanda's strategy for specialized healthcare centers and local capacity for complex conditions, building on previous cooperation with Chinese medical teams. The expanded Masaka Hospital is poised to become a major referral and specialized-care center.

Why it mattersThe expansion of Masaka Hospital directly increases Rwanda's capacity to provide specialized medical treatment and care for its citizens.

Rwanda Central Bank Hikes Rate to 8.75% as Inflation Reaches 14.5%

The National Bank of Rwanda tightened monetary policy for the second quarter, raising its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to combat accelerating inflation.

KIGALI – Rwanda’s central bank has increased its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to 8.75%, signaling a further tightening of monetary policy. The decision, announced by the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) on August 26, comes as inflation surged to 14.5% in July, significantly exceeding the bank’s target range of 2%-8%. This move extends the monetary tightening cycle that commenced in late 2025.

Headline inflation has climbed from 9.1% in the first quarter of 2026 to 13.2% in the second quarter, culminating in the 14.5% figure for July. The National Bank of Rwanda (BNR) now projects inflation to average 13.1% for 2026, a slight downward revision from the previous forecast of 13.9%. The MPC attributed the persistent inflation to rising costs in core goods, fresh food, and energy, with energy inflation alone surging to 45.7%.

The BNR anticipates inflation will remain above the target range throughout 2026, with a gradual return expected in the latter half of 2027, forecasting 7.9% for that year. Potential risks include the El Niño climate pattern affecting food prices and ongoing Middle East tensions impacting global commodity costs. Despite these pressures, Rwanda’s economy demonstrated strong growth, expanding by 10% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the central bank raises interest rates to combat inflation, potentially impacting borrowing costs and economic growth.

Rwanda Selects Top Coffees for October Auction

Twenty-five award-winning coffees will be offered to international buyers in an online auction on October 21, aiming to boost export value.

Rwanda has chosen 25 of its finest coffees for an international auction scheduled for October 21. This initiative, following the Best of Rwanda National Specialty Coffee Competition, aims to increase the value of the country's coffee exports and solidify its standing in the global specialty market. The winning lots were recognized on August 27 in Kigali after being selected from 320 samples submitted by cooperatives and processing companies nationwide.

The competition, organized by the National Agricultural Export Development Board (NAEB), identifies exceptional coffees and connects producers with specialty market buyers, where higher prices can be achieved. K-Organics coffee from Huye District won the Anaerobic Natural category, while Great Coffee Farmers Ltd’s Cyeza Coffee Washing Station in Muhanga District secured first place in the Fully Washed category. Last year, K-Organics' winning coffee sold for $88 per kilogram, an increase from previous years.

NAEB Director General Claude Bizimana stated the competition's goal is to promote Rwandan coffee internationally, expand markets, and increase export earnings. Agriculture Minister Telesphore Ndabamenye emphasized coffee's economic importance and called for improved quality and value across the coffee chain. The 25 selected coffees will be available through an online auction, offering producers a chance to translate quality recognition into greater financial returns.

Why it mattersTwenty-five Rwandan coffee producers and cooperatives will have their premium coffees auctioned internationally, potentially leading to higher prices and increased export earnings.

RSSB Acquires Full Control of Inyange and Ruliba Clays

The Rwanda Social Security Board now owns 100% of two companies after purchasing remaining shares from Crystal Ventures Limited.

The Rwanda Social Security Board (RSSB) has acquired the remaining shares in Inyange Industries and Ruliba Clays from Crystal Ventures Limited (CVL), securing full ownership of both companies. This transaction grants RSSB complete control over the future expansion plans for these two entities. The deal was announced on Thursday, August 27, marking the end of a long-standing arrangement.

While RSSB has a history of successful investments, the specifics of this acquisition remain undisclosed, raising questions about accountability. Information regarding the purchase price for each company, their independent valuations, profitability, liabilities, and expected returns for pensioners has not been provided by RSSB officials despite repeated requests. This lack of transparency is particularly notable given CVL's association with the ruling RPF.

RSSB officials have been criticized for their response to inquiries, with their perceived arrogance and disrespect fostering an impression of a family-run business rather than a statutory institution. While the transaction itself is not inherently improper and may prove to be a sound investment, the close relationship between the buyer and seller necessitates a higher standard of transparency and clear disclosure of financial details and due diligence processes.

Why it mattersPensioners are affected because the Rwanda Social Security Board has not disclosed the financial details of its acquisition of Inyange Industries and Ruliba Clays, leaving them uncertain about the returns on their pension assets.

Rwanda C-Section Rate Doubles to 24%, Prompting Health Debate

The nation's surgical birth rate has more than doubled since 2000, reaching 24% and sparking questions about medical necessity versus other contributing factors.

Rwanda's Caesarean section rate has climbed to 24%, according to the latest Rwanda Demographic and Health Survey (RDHS7). This significant increase, more than double the World Health Organization's 10% benchmark for population-level impact, is prompting renewed debate about the drivers behind the growing number of surgical births.

The current rate represents a substantial rise from 15.6% in 2019-20 and a dramatic jump from 2.2% in 2000. While C-sections can be life-saving when medically necessary, experts question whether the current trend is solely driven by complications or influenced by other factors, such as higher rates in private facilities which previously stood at 60.6% compared to 15.4% in public ones.

This rise in surgical births occurs alongside progress in reducing maternal deaths, with the ratio declining from 203 per 100,000 live births in 2020 to 149 in 2025. However, health experts emphasize that the priority is ensuring C-sections are performed when medically required, while avoiding unnecessary procedures, making understanding the root causes of the increase critical for public health.

Why it mattersThe rising C-section rate affects mothers and newborns by potentially increasing unnecessary surgical procedures, while also raising questions about the quality and necessity of maternal healthcare services.
