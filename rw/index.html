Rwanda arrests 11 over cable theft linked to power cuts

Two Ugandan nationals are among those detained for stealing electricity infrastructure worth an estimated Rwf20 million.

Rwanda Investigation Bureau (RIB) arrested 11 people, including two Ugandans, for stealing and destroying electricity cables. The criminal operation is linked to ongoing power outages across the country. The suspects are accused of theft, damaging infrastructure, and forming a criminal gang. The value of the stolen property is estimated at Rwf20 million.

RIB spokesperson Dr. Thierry Murangira B. stated the suspects operated as an organized network, with specific roles in stealing, transporting, and selling the cables. Nuwagaba Isaac and Mugisha Fred, the two Ugandan nationals, are accused of buying stolen cables and arranging their sale outside Rwanda. Motorcycle taxi operators and drivers allegedly assisted in transporting the stolen materials.

The recovered stolen cables weigh over one tonne. The suspects are currently detained at RIB stations in Nyarugenge and Kicukiro as investigations continue. This operation is part of RIB's efforts to tackle economic crimes and protect public infrastructure.

Why it matters11 people, including two Ugandans, were arrested in Rwanda for stealing electricity cables, which has been linked to power cuts affecting residents.

Kagame seeks Caribbean trade and investment partnerships

Rwandan president met leaders from four Caribbean nations on the sidelines of the Commonwealth summit.

President Paul Kagame met leaders from four Caribbean nations to explore expanding Rwanda's cooperation in trade, investment, and tourism. The discussions occurred on November 2 and 3 in Antigua and Barbuda during the 28th Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting.

Kagame discussed strengthening cooperation in health, defence, and tourism with Jamaican Prime Minister Andrew Holness. He also met with Prime Ministers Gaston Browne of Antigua and Barbuda, Philip Davis of the Bahamas, and Godwin Friday of St Vincent and the Grenadines to discuss bilateral relations and economic cooperation.

No specific agreements or investment commitments were announced from these Caribbean meetings. Kagame also used the summit to challenge perceptions that investing in developing economies carries greater risks than in developed markets.

Why it mattersRwanda is seeking to expand its trade, investment, and tourism cooperation with Caribbean nations.

Rwanda trials new long-acting malaria injection

About 80 healthy participants aged 12 to 50 are involved in the Phase 1b study in Kigali.

Rwanda has started a clinical trial for MMV371, an experimental malaria-prevention injection designed to offer protection for up to three months. Medicines for Malaria Venture (MMV) and Rwandan health research organisation Rinda Ubuzima announced the Phase 1b trial in Kigali on November 2, 2026. The injection, which releases atovaquone gradually, aims to reduce the need for frequent preventive medicine doses.

The trial will assess the safety and behaviour of MMV371 in about 80 healthy participants aged between 12 and 50, with three-quarters receiving the drug and the rest a placebo. Participants will be monitored for 24 weeks, including overnight stays and follow-up visits, to check for side effects and malaria infections. Marie Michele Umulisa of Rinda Ubuzima stated that conducting research in Rwanda allows African institutions to develop responses to local health challenges.

Malaria incidence in Rwanda was 72 cases per 1,000 people in the 2025/2026 financial year, a slight decrease from the previous year but still above 2023/2024 figures. Dr Joyce Eugénie noted that people living far from health centres could benefit from a long-acting preventive medicine. The cost of MMV371 is still under consideration as development continues.

Why it mattersThe trial aims to provide a new, potentially longer-lasting prevention method for malaria, a disease that remains a public health concern in Rwanda.

Rwanda PM urges small businesses to target East African market

Prime Minister Nsengiyumva sees a gap in regional shoe production as an opportunity for local firms to expand.

Prime Minister Justin Nsengiyumva visited the 26th East African Community (EAC) Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) Trade Fair in Kigali, where he encouraged small businesses to aim for larger production capacities. East Africa requires nearly 290 million pairs of shoes annually, but regional manufacturers currently produce only around 17 million pairs, presenting a significant growth opportunity.

Nsengiyumva addressed exhibitors at the fair, stating that government institutions and financial partners are ready to support small enterprises in expanding their capacity to serve the wider regional market. He cited a Rwandan shoe manufacturer with a capacity of 6,000 pairs per year as an example of a business that could grow with the right assistance.

The fair, known as the Jua Kali / Nguvu Kazi Exhibition, runs until November 8 and features 1,500 exhibitors from across East Africa. Its focus on leather and horticulture sectors aims to create jobs, strengthen regional production, and reduce reliance on imported finished goods.

Why it mattersSmall businesses in Rwanda can grow by accessing the larger East African market, particularly in sectors like leather and horticulture.

Rotary Club seeks community partnerships for youth opportunities

Rwanda Automobile Club president Christian Gakwaya sees motorsport as an industry creating jobs for engineers and mechanics.

The Rotary Club is seeking community partnerships to support local needs, empower vulnerable groups, and create youth opportunities as Rwanda aims for a larger role in international motorsport. Charles Habba of the Rotary Club stated the organisation wants to use its network for practical projects, especially for children, people with disabilities, and vulnerable individuals.

Habba explained the club identifies community challenges and mobilises resources, noting initiatives supporting children with disabilities and cancer patients. He also mentioned a potential collaboration for piped water access in Bugesera as an example of improving living conditions. The Rwanda Automobile Club President, Christian Gakwaya, views motorsport as an industry creating jobs for engineers, mechanics, and technicians.

Gakwaya emphasised preparing young Rwandans for motorsport careers through specialised training and partnerships with educational institutions. He highlighted potential local vehicle manufacturing and automotive innovation, aiming for domestic technical capacity rather than imported expertise. Achieving these ambitions requires investment in infrastructure, skills, and a supportive automotive industry environment.

Why it mattersThe Rotary Club aims to improve living conditions through community projects, while Rwanda's motorsport ambitions could create jobs for young people in technical fields.
