Rwanda seeks private cash for electricity access

Officials in Kigali urged governments to attract investors and ease financing for energy projects to reach millions without power.

Rwanda's Minister of Infrastructure, Zingiro Armand, stated on October 27 that African governments must attract more private investment to extend electricity to millions lacking power. Speaking at the Global Off-Grid Solar Forum & Expo 2026 in Kigali, he stressed the need to move beyond targets and deliver reliable, affordable electricity that supports economic activity. About 600 million people globally, mostly in sub-Saharan Africa, still lack electricity access.

Zingiro urged governments to develop projects that attract private investors, improve credit access, share investment risks, and maintain predictable policies. He noted that public funds should strategically encourage private investment, ensuring no communities are left behind. Off-grid solar, including for irrigation and cold storage, can support farmers' production and reduce losses, according to the minister. Rwanda aims for universal electricity access by 2029, combining grid expansion with off-grid solar and renewables.

Ministry of Infrastructure data shows 84.6 per cent of Rwandan households had electricity access by July 2025, with 59.6 per cent on the national grid and 25 per cent using off-grid sources. African Development Bank's Dr. Daniel Alexander Schroth confirmed the bank will support energy projects by reducing investment risks and expanding local currency lending. The forum, running until October 29, gathers government officials, investors, and businesses from 24 African countries.

Why it mattersRwanda's efforts to provide electricity access to all citizens by 2029 are dependent on attracting private investment and improving energy project financing.

Rwanda, DRC military planners meet in Germany on FDLR

Military planners from Rwanda and the DRC met in Germany to finalise a plan to neutralise the FDLR.

Military planners from Rwanda and the Democratic Republic of Congo convened in Garmisch, Germany, to brainstorm on neutralising the FDLR. The United States organised the meeting as part of the Joint Security Coordination Mechanism (JSCM) to implement security commitments from the Washington Accords for Peace and Prosperity. The African Union and Qatar also participated.

Discussions focused on a draft operations order intended to guide the DRC in neutralising the FDLR, alongside the disengagement of Rwandan forces and the lifting of their defensive measures. A U.S. statement indicated this plan requires approval before guiding implementation.

The draft operations order still needs approval before guiding the implementation of these security commitments.

Rwanda coffee sells for record $452 per kilogram

A K-Organics lot fetched a record price at the Best of Rwanda auction, highlighting value chain transformation.

A kilogram of coffee from K-Organics in Huye District sold for a record $452, about Rwf 670,000, at the Best of Rwanda auction on October 21. This sale demonstrates how Rwanda’s coffee sector has transformed from producing low-grade commodity coffee in the 1990s to specialty coffee commanding premium prices.

The transformation involved export reforms, private investment, washing stations, cooperatives, quality standards, and international buyer relationships, reshaping the value chain under the National Agricultural Export Development Board (NAEB). Dr Agnes Kalibata, former Minister of Agriculture, welcomed the record price but urged fair rewards for farmers, who are allocated 75 per cent of auction revenues.

This success builds on systems thinking, where the entire market system connecting farmers to consumers is considered, not just production methods. A past project introducing greenhouse tomatoes and peppers in Rwanda nearly failed due to insufficient attention to market systems, demonstrating that improving production alone is not enough.

Why it mattersFarmers who supply cherries to processors are allocated 75 per cent of Best of Rwanda auction revenues.

Rwanda fire displaces 1,100 traders

Only 17 traders at GOICO Plaza had insurance after a fire destroyed goods valued at over Rwf30 billion.

Around 1,100 traders displaced by a fire at GOICO Plaza in Rwanda's Northern Province have found new places to operate. The fire, which broke out on October 10, affected over 2,000 traders, with preliminary losses of goods estimated at more than Rwf30 billion.

Northern Province Governor Mugabowagahunde Maurice stated that only 17 traders had insurance for their lost goods, and these individuals are expected to receive compensation this week. Rwanda Revenue Authority Commissioner General Ronald Niwenshuti expressed sympathy and pledged continued support for the affected businesses.

Authorities identified 635 alternative trading spaces in Musanze, with the 1,100 traders finding new locations marking a step towards restoring business activity. Traders have raised concerns about rent costs and loans, while the low insurance penetration, around 2 per cent of GDP, highlights a wider challenge for Rwandan businesses.

Why it mattersOver 2,000 traders lost goods valued at over Rwf30 billion in a fire, with only 17 having insurance.

Rusizi Port nears completion to cut Rwanda transport costs

The new port on Lake Kivu is 97% complete and expected to be operational by the end of 2026.

Rwanda is nearing the completion of a modern port in Rusizi District on Lake Kivu, a project expected to reduce transport costs and boost cross-border trade with the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Local authorities report that construction is 97% complete, with final work focusing on road paving and cargo loading equipment, aiming for an operational launch before the end of 2026.

This development is part of a government strategy to transform Lake Kivu into an efficient transport corridor, offering an alternative to road transport for moving goods.

Why it mattersThe new port aims to lower the cost of moving goods and strengthen cross-border trade for Rwanda.
