Rwanda expands HPV vaccine to women up to 45

The country aims to eliminate cervical cancer as a public health problem by 2027, adding a new preventive measure.

Free human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination is now open to eligible women and girls aged 30 to 45 in Rwanda, a step that widens access to cervical cancer prevention. Recipients can get the shot at health centres nationwide from Monday, November 16. Two doses are given, the second six months after the first, and applicants must show a national identity card or another recognised document to prove their age.

The expansion complements Rwanda's routine HPV campaign for 12-year-old girls and its cervical cancer screening and treatment services, according to the Ministry of Health and the Rwanda Biomedical Centre (RBC). Vaccination coverage among girls has stayed above 90 per cent, but RBC reported in February that only 31 per cent of women aged 30 to 49 had been screened with high-performance tests.

Under Mission 2027, the accelerated plan to eliminate cervical cancer, Rwanda aims to screen 70 per cent of women aged 30 to 49 and ensure 90 per cent of women diagnosed with precancerous lesions or invasive cancer receive timely treatment. The disease was the second most common cancer among Rwandan women in 2022, with 866 new cases and 609 deaths.

Why it mattersWomen aged 30 to 45 can now receive free HPV vaccinations, a measure aimed at eliminating cervical cancer by 2027.

Rwanda to host EU migrant return hub

Five European nations will send rejected asylum seekers to Rwanda starting in 2027.

Five European Union countries will establish a migrant deportation centre in Rwanda to house asylum seekers whose applications have been rejected. Germany, Austria, Denmark, Greece, and the Netherlands announced the plan on Monday, November 16, 2026, in Vienna, Austria. The proposed facility, a return hub, is expected to be operational in 2027 and initially accommodate about 100 people.

Rwanda's Interior Minister Vincent Biruta stated the initiative offers a second chance to individuals who can no longer legally remain in their European host countries. Unlike a previous UK agreement, this centre will be an open reception facility, not a detention centre. Those transferred will receive residence rights, healthcare, education, and employment opportunities to integrate into Rwandan society.

The five European governments will cover the costs of transfer, accommodation, and integration services, though the financial value of the arrangement has not been disclosed. Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen described the initiative as a major shift in European migration management, aimed at regaining border control and discouraging irregular migration.

Why it mattersFive European countries will transfer rejected asylum seekers to Rwanda starting in 2027, offering them integration into Rwandan society.

Rwanda minister accuses DRC counterpart of lying

Rwanda's Foreign Affairs Minister Olivier Nduhungirehe stated that DRC's Wagner Kayikwamba spread disinformation regarding Louise Mushikiwabo's mandate at La Francophonie.

Rwanda's Foreign Affairs Minister Olivier Nduhungirehe accused his Congolese counterpart Wagner Kayikwamba of lying about closed-door discussions during elections for the next La Francophonie Secretary General in Cambodia. Nduhungirehe stated early Tuesday that Kayikwamba should avoid lying to the public about Mushikiwabo's interim role and mandate for electoral reform.

Nduhungirehe called Kayikwamba's claims of Mushikiwabo handling only day-to-day affairs and enabling electoral reform a "blatant disinformation." He explained that the compromise, negotiated with presidents of Senegal, Togo, and Benin, and reluctantly agreed to by President Tshisekedi, did not contain these specific words. The DRC president had initially sought a short transition without Mushikiwabo but with the OIF Administrator.

Rwanda's Chief diplomat clarified that Mushikiwabo's mandate runs until December 31, 2027, and includes full authority, with reforming the electoral process as a main objective. Nduhungirehe emphasized that Rwanda pledged not to put forward a candidate to succeed Mushikiwabo, a common practice in international organizations, and that the DRC should not claim credit for this.

Rwanda's Mushikiwabo gets one-year OIF extension

Diplomatic divisions prevent agreement on successor, delaying leadership vote.

Rwanda's Louise Mushikiwabo will serve another year as Secretary-General of the International Organization of La Francophonie (OIF) after member states failed to agree on her successor at the summit in Phnom Penh. The leadership contest exposed deep diplomatic divisions within the organization, which aims to unite countries through shared language and values.

The failure to select a new leader reflects disagreements over the selection process, with some states pushing for an election and others favoring consensus. The OIF recently amended its rules to prioritize elections, where each member state holds one vote, a change from the previous system that gave significant influence to major financial contributors like Canada and France.

The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) had put forward former Culture Minister Juliana Amato Lumumba as its candidate, presenting the strongest opposition to Mushikiwabo's re-election. The DRC's Foreign Affairs Minister stated that at the end of this 12-month transition period, Mushikiwabo will not be able to run again, and Rwanda will not be able to put forward a candidate.

Why it mattersHeads of state and government failed to reach an agreement on the next Secretary-General, granting a one-year extension to Rwanda's Louise Mushikiwabo.

Business · 2026-11-17

Rwanda courts South African mining investors

Ambassador Emmanuel Hategeka invited investors to a Kigali conference in December.

On Monday, November 16, 2026, Rwanda's ambassador to South Africa, Emmanuel Hategeka, met South African mining investors to pitch Rwanda as a destination for more of their capital. Among those present were members of the Young Presidents' Organization (YPO), a global network of business leaders.

Hategeka pointed out openings in Rwanda's mining sector and urged South African firms to look at business prospects there and consider expanding into the country. The investors were also asked to join an international mining conference set for Kigali, running from December 9 to 11, 2026.

The push aims to deepen economic ties with South Africa, draw in foreign direct investment and encourage joint ventures in mineral exploration, extraction, processing and value addition. The Kigali conference is meant to bring together investors, policymakers and mining companies to discuss those partnerships.

Why it mattersMining companies from South Africa now have a concrete invitation to weigh Rwandan projects before the Kigali conference on December 9 to 11, 2026.
