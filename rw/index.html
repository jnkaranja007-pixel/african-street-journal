Rwanda invests in connectivity to overcome landlocked trade costs

The country aims to boost competitiveness by focusing on innovation and high-value sectors like tourism.

Rwanda is investing in connectivity, regional integration, innovation, and high-value sectors to overcome the economic challenges of being a landlocked country, according to Jean-Guy Afrika, Chief Executive Officer of the Rwanda Development Board (RDB). Afrika spoke on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, to students at the African School of Governance in Kigali.

Landlocked developing countries face trade costs 1.4 times higher than coastal economies, Afrika stated. Rwanda is therefore prioritizing sectors where competitiveness relies on skills, quality, innovation, and efficiency, such as tourism and services, to create economic value and strengthen its global market position.

The RDB CEO highlighted luxury tourism and the "Visit Rwanda" initiative as key strategies for attracting international visitors and supporting economic growth. The discussion explored how Rwanda identifies global economic trends and translates long-term planning into practical development strategies.

Why it mattersRwanda's economic strategy aims to increase competitiveness and attract international visitors through investments in connectivity and high-value sectors.

Zipline's valuation triples to US$21.5 billion after Rwanda partnership

The US drone-delivery company raised US$1.5 billion in its latest funding round, with Walmart, Uber and JPMorgan among the investors.

Zipline, an American drone-delivery company, has seen its value triple to US$21.5 billion in its latest funding round, which raised US$1.5 billion. The round was led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management and Layer Global, and Uber, JPMorgan, Fidelity Management & Research, Tiger Global, ARK Invest and Salesforce Ventures also took part.

Zipline began operating in Rwanda in October 2016, when it made its first commercial delivery of blood and other essential medical products to hospitals that were hard to reach quickly by road. Rwanda was the first country to run Zipline's national autonomous delivery service, and the company later expanded into Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya and Côte d'Ivoire. Zipline is now moving from medical goods into food, retail goods and other consumer purchases.

In February 2026, Rwanda expanded the partnership, aiming to become the first country with nationwide autonomous logistics coverage and Africa's first urban drone-delivery network. Walmart and Zipline have agreed to add 300 drone-delivery sites across the United States under an expanded multiyear partnership. Uber has agreed to offer Zipline's service to Uber Eats customers and is targeting one million Zipline-supported deliveries daily by 2029, according to Zipline.

Why it mattersZipline's expansion gives Rwandan hospitals and communities a service that has grown from medical deliveries into wider consumer goods, backed now by larger investors.

Kigali and St. Louis sign trade partnership

The agreement aims to boost investment and cooperation in sectors from agriculture to technology.

The City of Kigali and the US city of St. Louis signed a sister-city agreement on Tuesday, November 10, to strengthen economic ties and attract investment. The partnership framework covers sectors including agriculture, healthcare, technology, biosciences, education, and urban development.

A delegation of over 40 business leaders and officials from St. Louis, led by Mayor Cara Spencer and County Executive Dr. Sam Page, visited Kigali for the signing. Fulgence Dusabimana, Kigali's Deputy Mayor for Urbanisation and Infrastructure, stated that the partnership's success will be measured by the benefits it brings to residents, aiming to connect companies, encourage exploration of new markets, and promote technology transfer.

St. Louis offers expertise in agriculture, research, healthcare, and innovation, while Kigali's infrastructure and development initiatives were praised by Mayor Spencer. The agreement also facilitates educational and cultural exchanges, with St. Louis Economic Development Partnership noting the goal of developing economic, educational, and cultural connections.

Why it mattersThe partnership between Kigali and St. Louis aims to create new trade and investment opportunities for businesses and institutions in both cities.

Rwanda pitches green industry investment at COP31 in Antalya

Prime Minister Dr Justin Nsengiyumva told the Antalya summit that green industrialisation can cut emissions while lifting growth and jobs.

At COP31 on Wednesday evening, Prime Minister Dr Justin Nsengiyumva argued that green industrialisation gives Rwanda a way to fight climate change while speeding up its economic transformation. He made the case at a high-level dialogue titled "Seizing the Green Industrial Opportunity: Investment, Jobs and Growth for Every Economy", delivering his remarks on behalf of President Paul Kagame.

To illustrate the point, Nsengiyumva cited a national programme that saves more than 30,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide emissions a year, the equivalent of taking about 6,500 cars off the road. He told development partners and financial institutions that practical partnerships sharing investment risks should replace pledges that go no further.

Rwanda's delegation to COP31 includes Environment Minister Bernadette Arakwiye, envoy Lt Gen Rtd Charles Kayonga and Teddy Mugabo, chief executive of the Rwanda Green Fund.

Why it mattersDevelopment partners and financial institutions are being asked to share investment risks in Rwanda's green industries.

Veteran journalist Museminari Muyango seeks Nyagatare council seat

The 68-year-old, who lives in Humure village, says he will consult residents and push for bank loans for farmers in the district.

Museminari Marcel Muyango, a veteran journalist aged 68, is seeking election as General Councillor for Nyagatare district and has told Taarifa Rwanda he wants to bring tangible change to its management. He lives in Humure village, Rwisirabo cell, Karangazi sector, and says leadership is about responsibility rather than prestige.

He says he will consult residents, listen to them and stay close to the people. Nyagatare is a border district that hosts one of Rwanda's fast-growing secondary cities and is known for large-scale animal husbandry and irrigation farming. Museminari says he wants modern agriculture with irrigation, processing and markets, and promises to lobby for farmers to access bank loans to harvest crops or when dairy milk payments are delayed.

His manifesto also commits to training youth and women, zero tolerance for corruption, simpler services and good schools, health centres, clean water, electricity and roads. He dismissed suggestions that his promises are mere campaign rhetoric, calling them a promise to work.

Why it mattersFarmers in Nyagatare, as Museminari describes it, would gain help from lobbying for bank loans and delayed dairy milk payments.
