Kagame Demands Rwanda Investment Board Deliver Tangible Results

President Paul Kagame urged the Rwanda Development Board to focus on attracting private capital, expanding exports and tourism, and boosting economic competitiveness.

President Paul Kagame has directed Rwanda's investment agency to prioritize tangible outcomes in its efforts to attract private capital, grow exports and tourism, and enhance the nation's economic competitiveness. Speaking at a meeting with the Rwanda Development Board (RDB) directors in Kigali, Kagame emphasized that attracting investors is only one part of the equation; making it easier for businesses to operate and succeed is crucial.

The RDB, responsible for investment promotion, export development, and tourism, is central to the government's strategy of shifting economic expansion towards the private sector. Rwanda has invested significantly in infrastructure and reforms to position itself as a regional hub, but the challenge now lies in converting this into increased private investment, stronger exports, and more job creation.

Kagame stressed the importance of delivery, accountability, and continuous improvement of the business environment. This directive comes as African economies increasingly compete for global capital, making Rwanda's reputation for efficient public administration and a business-friendly regulatory framework a key factor in attracting the necessary investment.

Why it mattersFarmers may benefit if increased private investment leads to better infrastructure and market access for agricultural products.

Rwanda Minister Proposes AI for 2.5 Million Farmers

Minister Paula Ingabire stated that artificial intelligence can provide farmers with timely, expert advice on planting, fertilizing, and harvesting.

Rwanda's Minister of ICT and Innovation, Paula Ingabire, has outlined a plan to integrate artificial intelligence into agricultural practices, aiming to support 2.5 million farmers across the region. Speaking at the Africa Food Systems Forum in Kigali, Ingabire highlighted the critical need to leverage AI to address information gaps that currently hinder farmers' decision-making processes.

The minister emphasized that AI is not intended to replace farmers or agronomists but rather to enhance their capabilities. The goal is to provide them with immediate access to expert knowledge, including agronomic, weather, and financial advice, delivered in languages they understand. This initiative seeks to transform the agricultural ecosystem by putting better intelligence directly into the hands of those who need it most.

Ingabire's proposal comes at a time when agriculture remains a significant source of employment in Sub-Saharan Africa, yet millions of farmers operate with limited or delayed information. The proposed AI intervention aims to empower these farmers to make more informed economic decisions regarding their crops and livelihoods.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by this initiative as it aims to provide them with better intelligence and expert advice to improve their decision-making regarding planting, fertilizing, and harvesting.

Producer Prices Jump 20.8% as Mining Costs Soar 77%

Rwanda's producer prices rose significantly in July, driven by sharp increases in mining, manufacturing, and electricity costs, impacting businesses.

Producer prices in Rwanda increased by 20.8% in July compared to the previous year, according to the National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda. This rise was primarily fueled by a substantial 77.1% jump in mining costs, alongside a 34.8% increase in electricity prices and a 17.8% rise in manufacturing prices. While the annual increase slowed from June's 22.3%, the escalating costs at the producer level signal ongoing pressure on businesses.

The Producer Price Index (PPI) figures reflect costs earlier in the supply chain, meaning these increases are likely to affect the final prices of goods for consumers. Mining is a critical sector for Rwanda's economy, as minerals constitute a significant portion of the country's exports, making the surge in mining costs particularly noteworthy.

On a monthly basis, producer prices saw a more modest increase of 0.5% from June to July. The National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda compiles these figures, providing insight into the inflationary pressures faced by domestic producers and their potential impact on the broader economic expansion.

Why it mattersBusinesses are affected by rising producer prices, particularly those in mining, manufacturing, and electricity sectors, due to increased costs in their supply chains.

Rwanda Receives Over 3,000 Evacuees From Libya Since 2019

The Emergency Transit Mechanism has housed vulnerable Africans, including recent arrivals from Sudan and Eritrea, at a center in Bugesera District.

Rwanda has provided refuge to more than 3,000 asylum seekers and refugees evacuated from Libya since 2019. This program, known as the Emergency Transit Mechanism (ETM), aims to offer safety to vulnerable individuals who were held in detention or facing dangerous conditions in North Africa. The latest arrivals in June and August 2026, comprising nationalities such as Sudanese, Eritreans, South Sudanese, Ethiopians, and Somalis, demonstrate the program's ongoing activity.

These evacuees are currently accommodated at the Gashora Emergency Transit Centre, located in Bugesera District, southeast of Kigali. The center serves as a temporary holding facility while longer-term solutions are being arranged for the individuals. The ETM was established in September 2019 through a collaborative effort between Rwanda, the United Nations refugee agency (UNHCR), and the African Union.

The primary objective of the ETM is to create an escape route for refugees and asylum seekers who find themselves stranded in Libya, often after attempting to reach Europe. Many of these individuals have experienced detention in Libya, and the mechanism provides them with a pathway to safety and potential resettlement or onward travel.

Why it mattersThe program affects asylum seekers and refugees evacuated from Libya by providing them with a safe transit and temporary housing in Rwanda.
