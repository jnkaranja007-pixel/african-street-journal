Rwanda coffee fetches record US$452 per kilogramme

K-Organics Ltd's specialty coffee sold for five times the previous year's price at the Best of Rwanda auction.

Rwanda's specialty coffee reached a record US$452 per kilogramme at the Best of Rwanda 2026 online auction on October 21. This price, equivalent to about Rwf670,000, was paid for coffee produced by K-Organics Ltd.

The winning bid was five times higher than the Rwf130,000 achieved by the company in 2025, and about 66 times the international market average of US$6.90 per kilogramme, according to the National Agricultural Export Development Board (NAEB). NAEB chief executive Claude Bizimana called the result a milestone for the country's coffee industry.

Bizimana attributed the record price to farmers' adoption of good agricultural practices, supply-chain contributions, and suitable coffee varieties. Farmers benefit from a 75% profit-sharing arrangement from coffee sales, with processing and exporting companies receiving the remaining 25%.

Why it mattersFarmers in Rwanda will receive 75% of profits from the record-breaking coffee sales.

Rwanda begins month-long local elections

Voters will choose village leaders and representatives for district and national councils.

Rwanda's month-long local elections started on Saturday, October 24, 2026, with voters selecting village leaders and representatives for local councils. The voting process will continue through November 2026, ultimately electing new district leaders across the country.

The National Electoral Commission (NEC) has utilized social media, including the hashtag #Amatora2026, to inform voters and explain the electoral process. Official accounts from NEC and the Ministry of Local Government have shared reminders and graphics to encourage participation in choosing leaders who will serve five-year terms.

The election campaign has focused on voter participation and civic responsibility, with officials urging residents to vote early. Messages, some featuring Minister Dominique Habimana and musician Bruce Melodie, have been amplified by government accounts and local media to reach various groups, including youth and women.

Why it mattersRwandan citizens will elect local leaders who will serve for five-year terms.

Rwanda to host African Grand Prix in early 2030s

Formula 1 signed a 10-year agreement for a new circuit in Bugesera, south of Kigali.

Formula 1 will bring the African Grand Prix to Rwanda in the early 2030s, following a 10-year agreement. A purpose-built circuit is planned for Bugesera, approximately 40 kilometres south of Kigali. The Rwanda Development Board is overseeing a wider project for sports, tourism, and infrastructure development.

African Union Commission Chairperson Mahmoud Ali Youssouf stated that the deal should help young Africans gain skills and find work in the global sports industry. He described the agreement as a milestone for African sport, noting that major international events can attract investment and tourism. Formula 1 says over 100 million people across Africa follow the sport, with nearly 60 percent of fans in key sub-Saharan markets under 35.

The race marks Formula 1’s return to Africa for the first time since the 1993 South African Grand Prix. The new venue is intended to host events throughout the year, not just race weekends. Youssouf said the initiative aligns with the African Union’s Agenda 2063 vision for an integrated Africa.

Why it mattersYoung Africans may gain skills, find work, and build businesses in the global sports industry.

Rwanda partners with Asian firm to attract investment

Raffles Family Office will promote projects including aviation, tourism, and real estate to wealthy clients.

Rwanda has partnered with Asian wealth management firm Raffles Family Office to attract hundreds of millions of dollars in private investment for projects in aviation, tourism, real estate, and infrastructure. The agreement with the Rwanda Development Board (RDB), announced on October 22, 2026, aims to connect international investors with priority economic development projects.

Raffles Family Office will leverage its network of wealthy individuals and international investors to promote opportunities in Rwanda, including developments for the planned Formula 1 Grand Prix. Projects highlighted include Kigali International Airport in Bugesera, Airport City, hotels, tourism facilities, and entertainment and commercial real estate. RDB Chief Executive Jean-Guy Afrika said private-sector participation would be important to realizing these opportunities.

The partnership aims to attract hundreds of millions of dollars over the coming years, with the first Formula 1 race expected in the early 2030s after circuit construction. Raffles Family Office Chief Executive Chi Man Kwan said the agreement would connect clients with prioritized sectors and support long-term investment in Rwanda and Africa. Jean de Dieu Uwihanganye, appointed principal for emerging markets, previously served as a Rwandan minister of state.

Why it mattersRwanda seeks foreign investment for economic development projects, including those linked to the Formula 1 Grand Prix.

Rwandans report lower satisfaction with public services

Security remains the highest-rated governance area in the 13th Rwanda Governance Scorecard released Thursday.

Satisfaction with public services in Rwanda declined to 68.48% in the past year, according to the 13th Rwanda Governance Scorecard released Thursday by the Rwanda Governance Board (RGB). Security and public order scored 90.15%, an increase from the previous year, making it the country's highest-rated governance area. The annual assessment, published Oct. 22, 2026, showed service delivery as the lowest of eight categories.

The scorecard assesses governance through 35 indicators, drawing on citizen surveys and administrative data. While overall national security satisfaction stood at 97.7%, personal and property safety declined by 3.32 points. Anti-corruption, transparency and accountability rose to 85.80%, and rule of law scored 85.47%.

RGB Chief Executive Dr. Doris Uwicyeza Picard stated that the findings should inform policy and public spending. Digital public services scored 60.98%, a decline from the previous assessment, with electronic court filing services at 65.40%. Agricultural services satisfaction was 53.70%.

Why it mattersRwandan citizens are experiencing lower satisfaction with the public services they receive from government institutions and other providers.
