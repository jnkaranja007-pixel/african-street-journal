Kagame defends alcohol ban, cites health risks to Rwandans

President Paul Kagame stated that the recent ban on locally produced and imported liquors was necessary due to their destructive impact on the lives and health of Rwandans.

President Kagame addressed the ban on substandard alcoholic drinks, stating that many Rwandans have died from consuming these products. He clarified that both local and some imported liquors were found to lack standards and were unfit for human consumption, according to Taarifa Rwanda.

Kagame questioned why factories producing such alcohols should be allowed to continue operating, suggesting that owners of these factories are intentionally responsible and should be questioned by courts. He emphasized that the fight against illicit alcohol would not tolerate such production, even if permitted in other countries.

The President made these remarks on Monday, August 24, 2026, during a press conference in Kigali, where he also discussed Rwanda’s involvement in the conflict in the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, as reported by KT Press and Rwanda Dispatch.

Why it mattersRwandan consumers are directly affected by the removal of certain alcohol products from the market, impacting their choices and health, while businesses involved in alcohol production face legal scrutiny and operational changes.

RMB Staff Gain Russian Scholarships for Mining Studies

Five employees from the Rwanda Mines, Petroleum and Gas Board (RMB) are pursuing Master’s degrees in Russia, aiming to boost expertise in Rwanda’s extractive sectors.

Five employees from the Rwanda Mines, Petroleum and Gas Board (RMB) have traveled to Russia to begin Master’s studies at Empress Catherine II Saint Petersburg Mining University. This initiative is part of Rwanda’s focus on developing specialized skills for the sustainable management of its mineral and energy resources, according to Taarifa Rwanda.

The five officials are among 60 Rwandan beneficiaries of scholarships offered by the Russian government this year. Their studies at the university, known for its engineering and mineral resources programs, are expected to provide advanced knowledge and international exposure applicable to Rwanda’s extractive industries.

This scholarship program highlights growing educational cooperation between Rwanda and Russia, particularly in technical fields. The RMB staff are anticipated to return with enhanced technical and managerial capabilities, contributing to Rwanda’s goal of making better use of its mineral, petroleum, and gas resources and strengthening institutional capacity.

Why it mattersRwandan mining, petroleum, and gas sectors are affected by gaining five highly trained professionals who can improve resource management and value creation.

East African Nations Seek Investment to Boost Agricultural Output

Eight East African countries are working to attract more investment into agriculture and food processing to create jobs and economic growth.

Representatives from Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, Somalia, South Sudan, and Uganda gathered in Kigali on Monday for the Hand-in-Hand Investment Forum for Eastern Africa. Organized by the U.N. Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), the forum aims to identify projects, mobilize capital, and strengthen trade and investment across the region’s food systems.

Mohamed Aw Dahir, FAO representative in Rwanda, stated that the forum's success would be measured by whether discussions lead to investments benefiting farmers, businesses, and rural communities. Rwanda's Agriculture and Animal Resources Minister, Dr. Telesphore Ndabamenye, noted that the region possesses significant investment opportunities, but these need development into projects that can attract capital and generate returns.

Rwanda is collaborating with the FAO to identify agricultural investment opportunities and develop projects connecting investors with financing, technology, and expertise. Potential areas include coffee, livestock, dairy, fisheries, horticulture, cereals, and oilseed production. However, climate change, inadequate infrastructure, high transportation costs, and limited access to finance remain major barriers, according to Farayi Zimudzi, FAO’s regional coordinator for Eastern Africa.

Why it mattersThe forum directly affects investors by identifying specific agricultural sectors for capital injection and farmers by aiming to generate investments that benefit them and rural communities.

Rwanda Modifies League Rules for Sudanese Football Clubs

Rwanda's BK Pro League has established special regulations for Sudanese guest clubs Al Hilal SC and Al Merrikh SC, allowing them to compete for an honorary trophy but reserving.

Sudanese football giants Al Hilal SC and Al Merrikh SC will play in Rwanda’s BK Pro League for a second consecutive season under special rules. These regulations permit the guest clubs to participate and contend for an honorary trophy, but the official national championship will be awarded to the highest-ranked Rwandan club. This arrangement follows Al Hilal SC winning the 2025–26 Rwanda Premier League, creating an unusual situation where a foreign club claimed the top position.

The two clubs relocated to Rwanda after the suspension of the Sudan Premier League due to the war that began in April 2023. They previously competed in Mauritania during the 2024–25 season before moving their domestic operations to Rwanda for the 2025–26 season. Both Al Hilal SC and Al Merrikh SC performed strongly, with Al Hilal SC becoming the first Sudanese club to win Rwanda’s top-flight competition.

The new rules ensure that while Sudanese clubs can continue to provide active competition for their players, the integrity of Rwanda's national championship remains with its domestic teams. This same arrangement will be in effect for the 2026–27 season, allowing for continued participation while clarifying championship distinctions.

Why it mattersSudanese football players and club management are directly affected as they gain continued access to competitive play in Rwanda's top league, while Rwandan clubs maintain their exclusive claim to the national championship.

Research links Rastafari roots to Rwandan-Ugandan Nyabingi tradition

New historical research traces the origins of the Rastafari movement's Nyabinghi tradition back to the 16th-century Rwandan Princess Nyabyinshi.

Historical research indicates that the Nyabingi tradition, originating among the Kiga/Bakiga and Banyarwanda people in southwestern Uganda and northern Rwanda, predates the Rastafari movement by roughly two centuries. This tradition revered Nyabingi as a powerful female spiritual presence associated with protection, fertility, healing, and authority, mediated by spiritual practitioners called bagirwa.

The figure of Princess Nyabyinshi, known as the “Conqueror of Nations,” evolved into a goddess of abundance, justice, and defiance against colonial oppression in Rwanda, Uganda, and Tanzania. Her story became intertwined with political resistance, notably through Muhumuza, a key Nyabingi figure who resisted colonial authority in the Great Lakes region.

When Rastafari emerged in Jamaica during the 1930s, the Nyabinghi tradition was adopted and transformed within the movement, becoming a major tradition known as the House of Nyabinghi. This adoption highlights a significant African thread in Rastafari, distinct from its Jamaican development influenced by Ethiopianism and Marcus Garvey’s Pan African thought.

Why it mattersThis research provides a deeper understanding of the cultural and spiritual heritage connecting East Africa to the Caribbean, enriching the historical narrative for those interested in their ancestral roots.
