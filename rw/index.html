Rwanda pilots centralised school meals

A new system prepares food in a central kitchen and delivers it to 266 teachers and 15,014 students across five schools.

The Rwandan government has started a pilot programme for centralised school meals, preparing food in bulk and delivering it to schools, a method similar to systems in Japan and South Korea. The education ministry says the shift is also aimed at reducing reliance on firewood, improving nutrition and lowering the cost of school feeding. The pilot launched on Friday and serves 15,014 students and 266 teachers across five public schools in Rwamagana District.

Meals are cooked at a central kitchen at GS Bujyujyu in Muyumbu Sector and transported to the schools in electric vehicles, so each school no longer has to cook its own food. The facility was inaugurated on Friday, November 13, by Minister of State for Education Claudette Irere, alongside representatives of Solid'Africa, the local social enterprise implementing the project.

The arrangement follows a hub-and-spoke model also used in Japan, where municipal school lunch centres, known as kyushoku centres, prepare meals for several schools. Japan runs this system alongside school-based kitchens as part of a nationwide school feeding programme.

Why it mattersPupils in the five Rwamagana schools will receive meals cooked off-site and delivered by electric vehicle instead of prepared in their own kitchens.

Rwanda seeks $3.05bn from investors for power grid and climate projects

The government is pitching its electricity and urban resilience needs to international lenders at the COP31 climate conference.

Rwanda is seeking US$3.05 billion in international investment for its electricity ambitions, including US$1.3 billion for grid infrastructure. Prime Minister Justin Nsengiyumva presented the country's priorities at the COP31 climate conference, where Rwanda called for stronger partnerships with multilateral development banks and development finance institutions.

On November 12 Nsengiyumva attended a high-level event on the Global Grids Accelerator, presided over by United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres. Rwanda said it needs to expand transmission capacity by 70 per cent and raise regional electricity trade from 100 megawatts to 200 megawatts.

The country also highlighted renewable energy, clean cooking, electric mobility, sustainable agriculture and the circular economy. The discussions included the Mission 300 initiative, which aims to connect 300 million people in Africa to electricity by 2030.

Why it mattersLenders who fund the grid expansion would finance transmission capacity that Rwanda says must grow by 70 per cent.

Sport · 2026-11-14

Mali beats Rwanda 3-2 in Bamako after Amavubi twice led

Mamadou Camara headed in the winner six minutes from time, ending Rwanda's unbeaten start in Group K of 2027 AFCON qualifying.

Mali came from behind twice to beat Rwanda 3-2 in Bamako on Friday, November 13, in the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) qualifier. Amavubi had taken the lead through Jojea Kwizera in the 28th minute and again through Phanuel Kavita, who rose above the Mali defenders to head home a corner in the 53rd minute.

Mali restored parity before halftime in the 42nd minute. Substitute Mamadou Camara then headed in a corner six minutes from time at Stade du 26 Mars, giving the hosts all three points. Rwanda lost for the first time in Group K.

The defeat ended Rwanda's perfect start to the campaign.

Why it mattersRwanda drops its first points in Group K, so its place in the qualifying table is now at risk.

Politics · 2026-11-14

Kagame begins Cambodia state visit with royal welcome

Rwanda's president was received by Cambodia's King Norodom Sihamoni in Phnom Penh before a week of talks and signings.

Rwandan President Paul Kagame and First Lady Jeannette Kagame were received by Cambodia's King Norodom Sihamoni at the Royal Palace in Phnom Penh on Friday, 13 November, in a formal welcome that opened the state visit. A private audience between the King and Rwanda's leader followed the ceremony.

Cambodia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs says the trip will bring talks with the country's senior leaders, and agreements on trade, investment, aviation and tourism are due to be signed. Kagame is scheduled to meet Senate President Hun Sen, National Assembly President Khuon Sudary and Prime Minister Hun Manet, and is also expected to visit the Independence Monument.

After the visit, Kagame will attend the 20th Summit of the International Organisation of La Francophonie (OIF) in Phnom Penh on 15 and 16 November. Rwanda's Secretary-General, Louise Mushikiwabo, is seeking another term there, against three challengers, in a vote on 16 November.

Why it mattersRwandan and Cambodian businesses wanting to trade, invest or fly between the two countries will see agreements on trade, investment, aviation and tourism signed during the visit.

Politics · 2026-11-14

Foreign ministers gather in Phnom Penh to prepare Francophonie summit texts

Ministers will finalise the Phnom Penh Declaration and a peacebuilding resolution before heads of state meet at the 20th Francophonie Summit.

Foreign ministers and government representatives from member states of La Francophonie, the International Organisation of La Francophonie (OIF), will hold the 48th Ministerial Conference on November 14 at the Sokha Phnom Penh Hotel in Phnom Penh, Cambodia. Rwanda is among the countries taking part. The organisers say the meeting prepares recommendations for heads of state and government attending the 20th Francophonie Summit.

Ministers were due to finalise core texts for summit adoption, including the Phnom Penh Declaration, a resolution on peacebuilding and crisis resolution, and the Phnom Penh Appeal on cultural heritage and diversity. The conference will also hear candidate presentations for the OIF Secretary-General, followed by closed-door deliberations, and review institutional and financial governance.

The debate centres on the theme "Reinvesting in Peace for Shared Prosperity and Sustainable Stability". Ministers will also coordinate economic initiatives alongside FrancoTech 2026, which covers AI, digital governance, fintech and food security, and the Francophone Entrepreneurs Forum.

Why it mattersFrancophone states will decide on the texts and the next Secretary-General at the summit that follows this meeting.
