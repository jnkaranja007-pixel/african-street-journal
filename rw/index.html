Climate · 2026-11-08

Kigali plants 2,238 trees to start forestry season

More than 5,000 residents joined the Rwanda Forestry Authority and Ministry of Environment on Saturday, November 7, to plant seedlings.

Kigali began its 2026/2027 tree-planting season on Saturday, November 7, by planting 2,238 seedlings across eight hectares in Gahanga Sector. The community work programme in Nyabigugu Village involved over 5,000 residents, security personnel, and partners.

The campaign, themed “My Tree, My Legacy,” aims to foster community responsibility for tree maintenance and protection, according to Fidèle Bingwa, Permanent Secretary at the Ministry of Environment. Concorde Nsengumuremyi, Director General of the Rwanda Forestry Authority, stated that expanding forest cover is important for addressing soil erosion and land degradation.

The planted species, including Imisave and Jacaranda, are expected to aid soil conservation, enhance hillside appearance, and support biodiversity near the Nyabarongo River wetland. This effort is part of Rwanda's wider strategy to increase forest cover and build resilience to climate change.

Why it mattersResidents of Gahanga Sector will benefit from trees planted to protect agricultural land, reduce soil erosion, and regulate rainfall.

Business · 2026-11-08

Rwanda awards 31 journalists for development reporting

Media's role in advancing African trade, including Rwanda's tea and coffee shipments, was a key discussion at the 13th Development Journalism Awards.

31 journalists in Rwanda were recognised on Friday for their reporting on development issues at the 13th Development Journalism Awards ceremony held at Camp Kigali.

The event, which also featured a discussion on the media's role in promoting African trade, brought together media professionals, government officials, and development partners.

Panelists discussed how African media can better explain opportunities and challenges within the African Continental Free Trade Area, citing Rwanda's tea and coffee shipments to Ghana as an example of intra-African trade.

Why it mattersJournalists were awarded for reporting on development, and discussions focused on how media can support African trade and entrepreneurs.

Agriculture · 2026-11-08

Rwandan farmer boosts maize harvest to 35 bags

Anatholie Ayinkamiye gained access to financing, improved seeds, and training, increasing her yield from four bags.

Anatholie Ayinkamiye, a 39-year-old mother of three from Nyagatare District, transformed her maize harvest from four bags to 35 bags after joining Tubura. Previously, she farmed one hectare under the traditional Tugabane arrangement, yielding only two bags after sharing with landless members. She had to sell one bag to afford seeds for the next season, eating the remaining one.

Ayinkamiye's success came after gaining access to financing, improved seeds, quality farm inputs, and agronomic training through Tubura. This support enabled her to complete a loan for a house. Tubura works with farmers across Rwanda through over 1,800 field officers, providing inputs and training within walking distance.

The Fifth Strategic Plan for Agriculture Transformation (PSTA 5) aims to modernize Rwanda's food system. Tubura has distributed over 145 million trees since 2016 to build farm resilience. Last year, the organization aggregated and exported more than 378 metric tonnes of produce, and established Avocado Oil Industries to process over 200 metric tons of avocados weekly.

Why it mattersRwandan smallholder farmers are improving their yields, financial security, and ability to invest in assets like housing and livestock through access to better inputs and training.

Politics · 2026-11-08

DRC threatens to quit Francophonie over Rwanda's Mushikiwabo

Kinshasa seeks to block Rwandan incumbent Louise Mushikiwabo from a third term as OIF Secretary-General.

The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) has threatened to withdraw from the International Organisation of La Francophonie (OIF) if Rwanda's Louise Mushikiwabo wins a third term as Secretary-General. This threat emerges as Kinshasa actively campaigns against Mushikiwabo's re-election and supports its own candidate, Juliana Amato Lumumba. The OIF summit is scheduled to take place in Cambodia.

Mushikiwabo, who has served as OIF leader since January 2019, is seeking another four-year mandate, with Rwanda officially endorsing her bid based on her record of institutional reforms. Congolese authorities are reportedly considering leaving the organisation if the Rwandan candidate secures re-election, according to reports from Jeune Afrique and Congolese media, though no formal announcement has been made.

The diplomatic tension coincides with strained relations between Kigali and Kinshasa over the conflict in eastern Congo, where the DRC accuses Rwanda of backing the M23/AFC armed group, an accusation Rwanda denies. Rwanda has also voiced concerns about armed groups like the FDLR operating in eastern Congo.

Why it mattersThe DRC's potential withdrawal from the OIF could impact diplomatic relations and the organisation's future leadership.
