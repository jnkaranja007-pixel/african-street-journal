Rwanda Central Bank Hikes Rate to 8.75% as Inflation Hits 14.5%

The National Bank of Rwanda increased its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to 8.75% to combat accelerating inflation, which reached 14.5% in July.

The National Bank of Rwanda (BNR) raised its key interest rate to 8.75% from 8.25% on Thursday, continuing a monetary tightening cycle that began in late 2025. This decision by the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) aims to address inflation that has climbed to 14.5% in July, significantly exceeding the bank's target range of 2% to 8%.

Inflation rose from 9.1% in the first quarter of 2026 to 13.2% in the second quarter, driven by increases in core, fresh-food, and energy prices. Energy inflation saw a substantial surge to 45.7%, reflecting higher costs for solid and liquid fuels, as well as cooking gas. The BNR projects inflation to average 13.1% for 2026.

Despite these inflationary pressures, Rwanda's economy expanded by 10% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026, with growth across various sectors. The central bank anticipates inflation will remain above its target throughout 2026, gradually returning to the desired range in the second half of 2027, with a forecast of 7.9% for that year.

Why it mattersRwandans will find their borrowing costs higher and the prices of goods and services continuing to rise, impacting their purchasing power.

Kenya, Rwanda Double Trade to Nearly Half a Billion Dollars

Kenya and Rwanda have significantly expanded their strategic relationship, nearly doubling bilateral trade over the past five years to approach half a billion dollars.

Trade between Kenya and Rwanda has nearly doubled in the last five years, now approaching half a billion dollars, according to Kenya’s Principal Secretary for Foreign Affairs, Dr. Korir Sing’Oei. Speaking at the Africa Mindset Reset Forum in Kigali, Sing’Oei stated that the relationship between the two countries has expanded significantly, driven by shared economic and strategic interests, and is built on mutual trust.

The deepening ties include growing investment links, with Kenyan companies like Equity Bank and KCB investing in Rwanda’s financial sector, as well as in education. Rwanda has also invested in Kenya’s strategic infrastructure, including the Kenya Pipeline Company, demonstrating concrete economic interests beyond diplomacy, Sing’Oei noted.

Security cooperation is another key pillar, with both militaries working together and sharing intelligence. Additionally, strong people-to-people ties exist, with Rwandans living, working, and studying in Kenya, and approximately 10,000 Kenyans residing in Rwanda for work, study, and business.

Why it mattersBusinesses and individuals in both Kenya and Rwanda are directly affected by increased trade and investment opportunities, as well as enhanced security cooperation and easier cross-border movement for work and study.

Prosecution seeks 30-day detention for Ferwafa boss Shema over alleged fraud

Prosecutors requested a 30-day remand for Fabrice Shema, president of the Rwanda Football Federation (FERWAFA), in connection with two unfunded cheques totaling US$600,000.

Fabrice Shema, a businessman and the president of FERWAFA, faces a request for 30 days of provisional detention from prosecutors at the Nyarugenge Primary Court. The request follows his August 11, 2026 arrest by the Rwanda Investigation Bureau (RIB) on charges of issuing unfunded cheques and allegedly obtaining property through fraudulent means. The two cheques, issued on August 5, 2025, were for US$400,000 and US$200,000, amounting to approximately Rwf883.5 million.

The charges stem from a commercial agreement worth over €1 million between Shema's company, Africa Medical Supply, and ABZL International General Trading LLC, a UAE-based company. Prosecutors claim the payment deadline of 60 days, following the January 23, 2024 agreement, passed without full settlement. Bank information obtained by investigators indicated the relevant account held less than US$1,000 when the cheques were issued.

Shema denies the accusations, stating the case is a commercial dispute. He argued that his company had a prior successful business relationship with ABZL and that the cheques were part of efforts to resolve a payment issue after a previous US$400,000 transfer failed due to problems with ABZL's receiving account. He also claimed one of the cheques was undated.

Why it mattersBusiness owners and individuals involved in commercial transactions could face legal action and detention if they issue cheques without sufficient funds, impacting their operations and personal liberty.

Kagame Meets AfCFTA Chief, Pushes for Stronger Intra-African Trade

President Paul Kagame met with the AfCFTA Secretary-General to discuss implementing the agreement and expanding trade across the continent, according to a statement.

President Paul Kagame met Wednesday with Wamkele Mene, secretary-general of the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) Secretariat, at Village Urugwiro. The discussions focused on steps African countries need to take to implement their commitments under the agreement to increase trade between countries on the continent.

Rwanda has actively participated in the AfCFTA, being one of seven countries in the initial Guided Trade Initiative launched in 2022 to test preferential trade. The U.S. Commerce Department reports that Rwanda is already using the framework to dispatch consolidated shipments of value-added agricultural products to regional markets.

The AfCFTA agreement entered into force in May 2019, with trading beginning on January one, 2021. As of October 2025, 50 of the 54 signatory countries had ratified the agreement, according to the U.S. Commerce Department.

Why it mattersRwandan businesses and consumers are directly affected by efforts to expand intra-African trade, potentially leading to new markets for exports and a wider variety of goods available domestically.

Rwanda discusses stake in Dangote's $16 billion Lamu refinery

Rwanda is in preliminary discussions to acquire a stake in Aliko Dangote’s planned $16 billion oil refinery in Kenya, aiming to strengthen its energy security.

Rwanda has confirmed it is in preliminary discussions to invest in Aliko Dangote’s planned $16 billion oil refinery in Lamu, Kenya. President Paul Kagame stated Monday that talks are underway, though no final agreement has been reached. Rwanda would welcome the opportunity to participate in the investment, which is still at an early stage.

The proposed refinery, with a processing capacity of up to 700,000 barrels of crude oil per day, is expected to become a major source of refined petroleum products for East African markets. Kenya’s economic adviser David Ndii indicated that Kenya was offered a 10 percent stake, valued at about $500 million, and that Ethiopia and Rwanda were also invited to participate in a broader regional investment package.

This regional package could see governments collectively hold about 30 percent of the refinery, valued at approximately $1.5 billion. For Rwanda, participation could strengthen its energy security and provide greater access to refined petroleum products, reducing its dependence on imported fuel.

Why it mattersRwandan citizens and businesses could benefit from increased energy security and more reliable access to refined petroleum products, potentially stabilizing fuel prices.
