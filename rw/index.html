Kagame to attend Francophonie summit for top job election

Rwanda's candidate Louise Mushikiwabo seeks a third term as OIF Secretary-General against DRC's Juliana Lumumba.

President Paul Kagame is expected to attend the 20th Francophonie Summit in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, on November 15 and 16. The meeting will see Rwanda compete for the top office of the International Organisation of La Francophonie for the 2027 to 2030 term. Rwanda's current leader, Louise Mushikiwabo, who has held the position since 2019, is seeking re-election.

Mushikiwabo faces competition from Juliana Lumumba of the Democratic Republic of Congo, Mauritania’s Coumba Ba, and former Romanian Prime Minister Dacian Ciolos. The DRC is campaigning on its large French-speaking population, while Rwanda highlights Mushikiwabo's record on institutional reform and youth development. Kagame's presence aims to bolster Rwanda's diplomatic efforts during negotiations among the OIF's 53 full member states.

The summit, themed “Reinvest in Peace for Shared Prosperity and Lasting Stability,” will also discuss international cooperation, education, culture, digital innovation, governance, and sustainable development. This is Cambodia's first time hosting the summit, and it is only the second to be held in Asia. FrancoTech 2026, a related event, is expected to draw over 5,000 participants and more than 110 exhibition booths.

Why it mattersPresident Kagame's attendance at the summit is to support Rwanda's bid for the leadership of the Francophonie organisation.

Rwanda hosts climate talks marking Kigali Amendment anniversary

Experts and officials gather in Kigali to assess progress and plan future actions for ozone protection and greenhouse gas reduction.

Rwanda is hosting a technical workshop in Kigali to mark the 10th anniversary of the Kigali Amendment, bringing together government officials, climate experts, and international partners. The meeting, which runs alongside the 38th Meeting of the Parties to the Montreal Protocol (MOP38) from November 2 to 6, 2026, focuses on strengthening global efforts to protect the ozone layer and reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

Environment Minister Dr. Bernadette Arakwiye emphasized the need to translate commitments into practical climate action, stating, "Rwanda is proud to welcome the world back to Kigali, 10 years after countries came together here to adopt the Kigali Amendment." The Kigali Amendment, adopted on October 15, 2016, aims to gradually reduce the production and consumption of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs), which contribute significantly to global warming.

Full implementation of the amendment is projected to prevent up to 0.5 degrees Celsius of global warming by the end of the century. Discussions at the workshop are expected to lead to stronger implementation strategies, increased investment in climate-friendly cooling technologies, and improved refrigerant management, potentially benefiting food preservation, healthcare, and creating new economic opportunities through initiatives like the African Centre of Excellence for Sustainable Cooling and Cold-chain (ACES).

Why it mattersRwanda is hosting international discussions on climate action and the reduction of greenhouse gases, directly involving its environment minister and local institutions.

Rwanda seeks solar energy partnerships in India

A Rwandan delegation is in New Delhi for the International Solar Alliance assembly to attract investment and expertise.

Rwanda is pursuing new solar energy partnerships at the 9th General Assembly of the International Solar Alliance in New Delhi, India. State Minister for Infrastructure Armand Zingiro is leading a delegation, including Ambassador to India Jacqueline Mukangira, to the November 2–5 meeting. They aim to expand access to affordable and sustainable solar power.

The assembly offers a platform to discuss challenges in solar technology deployment, such as financing and infrastructure. Rwanda seeks to attract investment and gain technical expertise to accelerate solar projects and increase electricity access. Discussions also cover mobilising funds and encouraging private sector involvement.

The event runs alongside the Bharat Renewable Energy Summit & Expo and the Green Hydrogen Conference. These parallel events could offer Rwanda opportunities to engage investors and technology providers. Potential outcomes include new cooperation, investment prospects, and knowledge sharing to diversify Rwanda's energy mix.

Three bodies recovered from disused Ruhango mine

Rwanda Investigation Bureau probes deaths after men entered abandoned Big Mining shaft without authorisation.

Three bodies were recovered from a disused underground section of Big Mining in Ruhango District, Rwanda, eight days after the men entered the site. The deceased were aged 29, 34, and 36.

The Rwanda National Police Fire and Rescue Brigade retrieved the bodies, which were taken for examination. CIP Hassan Kamanzi, police spokesperson for Southern Province, stated the men entered without authorization and that the guard responsible for the site has been arrested for investigation by the Rwanda Investigation Bureau (RIB).

Big Mining Managing Director Eugène Shumbusho confirmed the men were not employees and alleged they were stealing minerals, adding the company is assisting with burial arrangements. Shumbusho noted the incident highlights security lapses and that Big Mining will hire more qualified guards and provide additional training.

Why it mattersThree men died after entering a disused mine shaft, raising safety concerns for abandoned mining sites.

Rwanda secures $4.97M for climate adaptation

The funds will support smallholder farmers in Gatsibo, Nyagatare, and Kayonza districts with climate-smart technologies.

Rwanda has secured U$4.97 million from the Adaptation Fund to strengthen climate resilience among smallholder farmers in Kayonza, Gatsibo, and Nyagatare districts. This UNDP-Ministry of Agriculture project aims to improve access to climate information and climate-smart farming technologies for at least 10,000 farmers. The initiative will promote climate-smart agriculture, enhance advisory services, and reduce losses from drought and erratic rainfall.

The Adaptation Fund has approved nearly U$35 million for climate resilience in Rwanda and the Horn of Africa, with U$4.97 million allocated to Rwanda and U$30 million for drought-affected communities in Kenya, Ethiopia, and Somalia. The Adaptation Fund finances projects that help vulnerable communities in developing countries adapt to climate change, based on country needs and priorities.

Since 2010, the Adaptation Fund has committed about U$1.6 billion for climate adaptation and resilience projects, including 226 localized projects benefiting about 120 million people globally. It has also pioneered Direct Access and Enhanced Direct Access, empowering countries to access funding and develop local projects through accredited national implementing entities.
