Rwanda arrests 11 over cable theft linked to power cuts

Two Ugandan nationals are among those detained for stealing electricity infrastructure worth an estimated Rwf20 million.

Rwanda Investigation Bureau (RIB) arrested 11 people, including two Ugandans, for stealing and destroying electricity cables. The criminal operation is linked to ongoing power outages across the country. The suspects are accused of theft, damaging infrastructure, and forming a criminal gang. The value of the stolen property is estimated at Rwf20 million.

RIB spokesperson Dr. Thierry Murangira B. stated the suspects operated as an organized network, with specific roles in stealing, transporting, and selling the cables. Nuwagaba Isaac and Mugisha Fred, the two Ugandan nationals, are accused of buying stolen cables and arranging their sale outside Rwanda. Motorcycle taxi operators and drivers allegedly assisted in transporting the stolen materials.

The recovered stolen cables weigh over one tonne. The suspects are currently detained at RIB stations in Nyarugenge and Kicukiro as investigations continue. This operation is part of RIB's efforts to tackle economic crimes and protect public infrastructure.

Why it matters11 people, including two Ugandans, were arrested in Rwanda for stealing electricity cables, which has been linked to power cuts affecting residents.

Rwanda secures $4.97 million for climate adaptation

The funds will strengthen climate resilience for 10,000 smallholder farmers in Gatsibo, Nyagatare, and Kayonza districts.

Rwanda has secured U$4.97 million from the Adaptation Fund to boost climate resilience for smallholder farmers in Gatsibo, Nyagatare, and Kayonza districts. This project, a collaboration between the UNDP and the Ministry of Agriculture, aims to improve access to climate information and climate-smart farming technologies for at least 10,000 farmers.

The initiative will promote climate-smart agriculture, enhance advisory services, and reduce crop losses caused by drought and unpredictable rainfall. The Adaptation Fund, which finances projects for vulnerable communities in developing countries, has approved nearly U$35 million for climate resilience across Rwanda and the Horn of Africa.

This funding is part of the Adaptation Fund's commitment to climate adaptation and resilience projects since 2010, which has seen it commit about U$1.6 billion. The fund empowers countries to access funding directly through accredited national implementing entities, supporting local projects for approximately 120 million beneficiaries globally.

Why it mattersSmallholder farmers in Gatsibo, Nyagatare, and Kayonza districts will benefit from improved access to climate information and technologies to combat drought and erratic rainfall.

Kagame meets Jamaican PM, WTO chief on trade

Rwanda's president discussed cooperation in health, defence, and tourism with Jamaica's leader.

President Paul Kagame met with Jamaican Prime Minister Andrew Holness and World Trade Organization Director-General Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala in Antigua and Barbuda. The meetings, held on the sidelines of the 28th Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting, focused on strengthening bilateral relations, expanding trade, and attracting investment.

With Prime Minister Holness, Kagame explored ways to expand collaboration in health, defence, and tourism, aiming to support economic development and improve public services. Discussions with Dr. Okonjo-Iweala centred on Rwanda’s partnership with the WTO and the importance of strengthening regional trade and investment for market access.

These engagements reflect Rwanda’s efforts to deepen international partnerships and develop practical cooperation with countries sharing development priorities. CHOGM 2026 brings together leaders to discuss shared challenges and explore strategies for promoting inclusive growth.

Why it mattersPresident Kagame's meetings aim to advance economic partnerships that can deliver tangible benefits in health, tourism, trade, and investment for Rwanda.

NBA confirms it follows US policy on Rwanda deal

The basketball league told US senators it will adjust its business ties based on American government actions.

The NBA will continue its partnership with Rwanda, stating its international business approach mirrors US government policy. In a letter to two US senators, the league's deputy commissioner Mark Tatum confirmed this stance, noting that the NBA adjusts its operations when American policy shifts. For example, the military-funded Armée Patriotique Rwandaise (APR) was withdrawn from the Basketball Africa League (BAL) after Washington imposed economic sanctions on the Rwanda Defence Force.

Senators Jeff Merkley and Marsha Blackburn had expressed alarm over the NBA's deepening ties with Kigali, questioning the league's financial dealings with governments accused of human rights abuses. Tatum did not directly answer these questions but pointed to the APR's removal as proof the NBA follows US policy, stating that the league condemns human rights violations globally and will adapt as policy demands.

This exchange follows previous pressure on Rwanda's sporting partnerships, including a call from the DRC last year for sports bodies to cut ties with Kigali. While some European football clubs ended sponsorship deals, the NBA has maintained its plans, including for the 10,000-seat BK arena.

Why it mattersThe NBA's continued partnership with Rwanda affects the country's sports development and international image.

Rwanda seeks solar energy deals in India

A Rwandan delegation is in New Delhi for the International Solar Alliance assembly to find new partnerships and investments.

Rwanda is attending the 9th General Assembly of the International Solar Alliance in New Delhi, India, from November 2-5 to boost solar energy partnerships. State Minister for Infrastructure Armand Zingiro leads the delegation, which includes Ambassador Jacqueline Mukangira, aiming to expand access to affordable and sustainable solar power.

The assembly offers a platform to review global solar progress, strengthen cooperation, and find solutions for financing and infrastructure challenges. Rwanda seeks to attract investment and gain technical expertise to accelerate solar projects and increase electricity access in underserved areas.

Discussions will also cover mobilizing funds for solar projects, encouraging private investment, and promoting technology transfer. The event runs alongside renewable energy and green hydrogen conferences, potentially offering Rwanda opportunities to engage investors and technology providers for its energy transition.

Why it mattersRwanda's participation in the International Solar Alliance assembly aims to attract investment and technical expertise for its renewable energy ambitions.
