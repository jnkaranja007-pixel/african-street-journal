S&P keeps Rwanda's B+ rating as deficit forecast falls to 4.9% of GDP

The agency cites growth and concessional borrowing, and the airport's financing arrangement cut second-phase spending by Rwf168.2 billion in 2026.

Standard & Poor's (S&P) Global Ratings maintained Rwanda's B+ sovereign credit rating, citing economic growth, improved public financial management and the structure of external debt. The agency expects the fiscal deficit for the 2025/26 financial year to reach about 4.9% of gross domestic product, below the initially planned 5.7%, because tax revenues beat expectations and spending fell.

S&P said the economy grew 9.3% in 2025 and about 10% year on year in the first half of 2026, led by manufacturing and services. The Ministry of Finance reported public and publicly guaranteed debt of Rwf17.85 trillion by June, equal to 69.1% of GDP, down from 74.8% a year earlier. More than 87% of external debt consists of concessional loans, which carry lower interest rates and longer repayment periods.

The agency projects 7.5% growth in 2026 and an average of about 7% a year from 2027 to 2029. Financing involving the World Bank Group's International Development Association and Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency reduced spending on the New Kigali International Airport's second phase by Rwf168.2 billion in 2026, and full commercial operations are expected around mid-2028.

Why it mattersLower borrowing costs on most external debt give the government more room to fund infrastructure, and the airport's cheaper financing frees public money that would otherwise go to construction.

Rwanda central bank sets franc rates against dollar, euro and regional currencies

The National Bank of Rwanda quoted the dollar at Rwf1,472.39 to buy and Rwf1,482.39 to sell on Tuesday, November 17.

The Rwandan franc traded at rates published by the National Bank of Rwanda (BNR) on Tuesday, November 17, 2026. The dollar was quoted at Rwf1,472.39 to buy and Rwf1,482.39 to sell; the euro at Rwf1,620.21 and Rwf1,631.22, respectively; and the British pound at Rwf1,942.29 and Rwf1,955.49.

Among regional currencies, the Kenyan shilling was Rwf11.32 to buy and Rwf11.39 to sell. The Ugandan shilling stood at Rwf0.36 for both transactions, while the Tanzanian shilling was Rwf0.56 for both, according to BNR’s figures.

The central bank said the rates provide guidance to financial institutions and the wider economy. It noted that commercial banks and foreign-exchange bureaus may offer different rates, advising customers to check their quotes before completing a transaction. The published rates took effect on Tuesday.

Why it mattersBusinesses settling import payments and exporters converting foreign-currency earnings use the published rates as a reference, while commercial-bank and bureau quotes can differ.

Rwanda court sets December 8 resumption for Ingabire trial

The High Court in Kigali gave the defence until November 23 to file written responses to the prosecution’s submissions.

Rwanda’s High Court in Kigali will resume proceedings on December 8, 2026, in the case involving opposition figure Ingabire Victoire and several co-accused. The court kept that date after their defence lawyers asked for more time to answer the prosecution’s submissions.

The court granted the defence until November 23, 2026, to submit written responses. Defence lawyer Me Gatera Gashabana asked the court to reconsider the hearing date, citing a planned trip that could affect his attendance, but the court declined to change it.

The defence is expected to file its responses before the next session in December, when the case returns to court on the date originally scheduled.

Why it mattersIngabire's defence team must file its written answers to the prosecution by November 23, ahead of the December 8 session.

Security · 2026-11-18

Rwanda and Uganda sign police cooperation framework in Kigali

The framework will guide the two forces in carrying out a memorandum of understanding they signed last year.

Rwanda and Uganda's police chiefs signed an implementation framework on Tuesday, November 17, during a bilateral meeting at Rwanda National Police headquarters in Kacyiru, Kigali. The framework is aimed at strengthening cooperation between the two countries' law enforcement institutions.

Rwanda's Inspector General of Police, CG Felix Namuhoranye, received Uganda's IGP Abbas Byakagaba and his delegation for talks on strengthening the existing relationship between the two police institutions. The discussions focused on cooperation for the mutual benefit of both countries.

The framework will guide implementation of a memorandum of understanding the two institutions signed last year. The Rwanda National Police and the Uganda Police Force said the meeting was part of efforts to deepen their institutional ties in areas of mutual interest.

Why it mattersPolice officers in both countries will work under a shared framework that sets out how last year's memorandum is put into practice.
