IER and MUA partner on engineering insurance

The Institute of Engineers Rwanda and MUA Insurance will offer tailored products and training to over 5,000 members.

The Institute of Engineers Rwanda (IER) and MUA Insurance signed a partnership agreement on Wednesday, October 28, at MUA's headquarters. This deal gives Rwanda's Engineering Practitioners access to specialized insurance products, reduced rates, and practical risk-management training. IER President Eng. Philbert Mbanza and MUA's Konde Bugingingo signed the accord, which covers engineers, technologists, technicians, and consulting firms.

Mbanza stated the partnership aims to secure cover suited to risks in design, construction, and on-site work, noting that a single insurer can offer better terms through pooled numbers. He also suggested the agreement could encourage more practitioners to join the IER, which has over 5,000 members and is growing by 25% annually. Professional indemnity insurance is among the products discussed, which Mbanza believes should be a necessity for practitioners.

Bugingingo explained that MUA will develop products for the distinct needs of engineering professionals, using risk data to improve safety and make their work easier. He added that pooling members could make insurance more affordable than individual purchases. MUA plans to tailor services for different roles, acknowledging varied risks. The collaboration also includes continuous professional development and aims to increase public confidence in infrastructure projects by ensuring risks are covered.

Why it mattersEngineering practitioners in Rwanda will gain access to specialized insurance and risk management training.

Rwanda PM warns youth forum on manipulation

Prime Minister Justin Nsengiyumva told a Moscow forum that young people can rebuild or fuel violence.

Rwanda’s Prime Minister Justin Nsengiyumva told a Russia–Africa youth forum in Moscow on October 29 that the country’s post-1994 Genocide recovery offers lessons on how young people can rebuild a nation.

Nsengiyumva stated that the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi killed over one million people in 100 days, and that the regime manipulated youth to fuel atrocities, while others fought to end the tragedy. He highlighted the Rwanda Patriotic Army’s choice of reconciliation over revenge after securing the country.

The Prime Minister urged young participants to use their skills for cross-border partnerships and noted Rwanda’s visa-free policy for African citizens as key for economic integration and shared prosperity.

Why it mattersYoung Africans attending a forum in Moscow heard about Rwanda's experience with youth manipulation and opportunities for development.

Rwanda prime minister attends Russia-Africa summit

Dr Justin Nsengiyumva represented President Kagame at the Moscow gathering focused on expanding cooperation.

Rwanda's Prime Minister, Dr Justin Nsengiyumva, represented President Paul Kagame at the third Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow on October 28, 2026. The summit, which opened on October 28, aims to expand cooperation between Russia and African nations across various sectors. Nsengiyumva also spoke at the Russia-Africa Youth Forum at the Moscow State Institute of International Relations (MGIMO) on October 28, sharing Rwanda’s development experience.

The summit agenda includes discussions on trade, investment, infrastructure, energy, agriculture, healthcare, and technology, with participants also set to explore cooperation in artificial intelligence and nuclear technology. The Prime Minister’s Office confirmed Nsengiyumva attended a reception hosted by Russian President Vladimir Putin on October 28. Rwanda and Russia established diplomatic relations in 1963.

This meeting is part of a series of Russia-Africa summits that began in 2019, with previous editions held in Sochi in 2019 and Saint Petersburg in July 2023. The 2023 summit produced cooperation documents including an action plan for 2023–2026 and agreements on counterterrorism and information security. Nsengiyumva arrived in Moscow on October 27, 2026, for the event.

Rwanda seeks $386 million from South Korean firm

The government filed a counterclaim at the Permanent Court of Arbitration over a failed 4G network project.

Rwanda has filed a $386 million counterclaim against South Korean telecommunications company KT Corporation over a failed 4G network project. The government, the Rwanda Development Board, the Rwanda Social Security Board, and the Agaciro Development Fund filed the claim on October 20, 2026, with the Permanent Court of Arbitration.

KT Corporation, which owns 51% of the joint venture KT Rwanda Networks (KTRN), disclosed the filing on October 23, stating it would challenge Rwanda's damage calculations. The dispute centres on the 2013 joint venture established to build and operate Rwanda’s 4G LTE broadband infrastructure.

The $386 million claim is equivalent to about Rwf 574 billion, more than 1.4 times Rwanda’s agriculture and livestock budget for 2026/27. It also represents about 7.3% of the nation's Rwf 7.796 trillion budget for the same fiscal year.

New Auditor General faces audit accountability gap

Claudette Mukashyaka urged to investigate why public institutions fail to implement audit recommendations.

Rwanda’s new Auditor General of State Finances, Claudette Mukashyaka, faces the challenge of improving the implementation of audit recommendations by public institutions. The Senate’s Committee on Economic Affairs and Finance raised this issue during her appointment review, also calling for annual audits of all entities receiving public funds.

Senator Dr Frank Habineza stated that Mukashyaka possesses the necessary academic and professional qualifications for the role, having worked at the Office of the Auditor General for four years. He noted her intention to enhance audit quality and meet international standards, but stressed the importance of institutions acting on audit findings. The latest annual report indicated that 65 per cent of audit recommendations were implemented in 2025, an increase from 60 per cent in 2024, covering 96 per cent of government expenditure across 252 public entities.

The report also showed improvements in financial management, with 97 per cent of public entities receiving unqualified audit opinions in 2025, up from 94 per cent in 2024. Compliance with laws and regulations saw an increase from 75 per cent to 83 per cent. Mukashyaka was appointed by the Cabinet on October 26, 2026, succeeding Alexis Kamuhire. Gratien Kubwimana was approved as Deputy Auditor General.
