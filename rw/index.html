Rwanda explores combining health insurance with farm and business cover

The government is investigating how its existing health insurance scheme could form the basis for wider financial protection against climate shocks and business risks.

Rwanda is considering expanding its community-based health insurance, Mutuelle de Santé, to include coverage for agricultural losses and small business risks. The Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning (MINECOFIN) is exploring this potential to create a broader financial protection model for households.

Herbert Asiimwe, Head of Financial Sector Development at MINECOFIN, stated that collaboration with the Rwanda Social Security Board (RSSB) and the insurance industry could lead to bundled products. This initiative aims to offer greater impact and benefits by potentially allowing households to afford multiple policies under one framework.

The proposal, discussed at the International Conference on Inclusive Insurance 2026 in Kigali, seeks to leverage the success of Mutuelle de Santé. By pooling contributions for healthcare, Rwanda hopes to replicate similar approaches to protect families against diverse financial shocks, such as illness, crop failure due to climate events, or business damage.

Why it mattersFarmers and small business owners could gain broader financial protection against diverse risks like crop failure, climate shocks, and business damage.

Kagame urges youth to prioritize health for future success

President Paul Kagame told over 10,000 young people that personal responsibility for health is key to achieving ambitions.

President Paul Kagame has challenged Rwanda’s youth to prioritize their health, stating that personal well-being is fundamental to achieving future aspirations. Speaking at the MeetThePresident event at BK Arena on October 21, attended by over 10,000 young individuals, Kagame emphasized that harmful habits like alcohol and drug abuse can derail dreams before they are realized. He argued that lasting change stems from individual willingness rather than external imposition.

Kagame linked individual choices and social pressures to the normalization of unhealthy behaviors, which he warned can undermine potential. He stressed that discipline cannot be forced, but rather learned and adopted through a conscious effort to do what is right. The president encouraged young people to use their time productively to gain knowledge and skills that can improve their lives, cautioning against blindly following societal norms.

The president also challenged officials to address poor public service delivery, a concern raised by a young person during the dialogue. Kagame stated that problems require action rather than mere discussion, highlighting the need for improved quality of services in public institutions. His message underscored the importance of personal responsibility in health and the need for effective governance in public services.

Why it mattersYoung Rwandans are directly addressed by President Kagame on personal choices affecting their future and are also impacted by the call for improved public services.

Rwanda targets medals at African Cycling Championships

A squad of 29 Rwandan riders will compete in the five-day event in Kisumu, Kenya, aiming to improve on last year's performance.

Rwanda's 29 cyclists began their campaign at the African Cycling Championships in Kisumu, Kenya, on October 21, with the individual time trials. The team, comprising riders from junior to elite categories, aims to secure medals against strong competition from South Africa, Eritrea, Algeria, and Morocco. This year's championships feature 282 riders from 33 countries across various age groups and race formats.

Coach Félix Ssempoma stated that the large contingent was a strategic choice to increase medal chances. "Our goal is to win medals. We selected a large number of riders because having more competitors gives us a better chance of winning," he said. Defending junior women's champion Akimana Donatha, a multiple gold medalist, leads the Rwandan challenge, supported by last year's bronze medalist Yvonne Masengesho. In the elite men's road race, Moise Mugisha, who finished third last year, will compete against defending champion Merhawi Kudus of Eritrea.

The championships, running until October 25, include mixed team relays and road races. The hot lakeside conditions in Kisumu are expected to be a factor, particularly in the longer road races, with organizers emphasizing hydration and providing medical support. Rwanda's participation follows their trip to the 2026 UCI Road World Championships in Montréal, where results indicated the challenge of competing at the highest international level.

Why it mattersRwandan cyclists are competing for medals at the African Cycling Championships, aiming for podium finishes.

El Salvador restricts Rwandan travelers over Ebola fears

The Central American nation has banned nationals from eight African countries, including Rwanda, despite no reported cases of the disease.

El Salvador has imposed visa restrictions on nationals from Rwanda and seven other African countries due to Ebola concerns, according to an official letter from the Ministry of Health. The directive, dated Tuesday, declares nationals and travelers from Angola, Burundi, the Central African Republic, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, and Uganda inadmissible. This move has drawn scrutiny as it includes countries, like Rwanda, with no reported cases of the Ebola virus.

The Rwandan Ministry of Health and the Rwanda Biomedical Centre have stated that there are no reported Ebola cases within the country. The restrictions gained wider attention after a Kenyan journalist reported his visa application denial, citing El Salvador's directive. He questioned the inclusion of countries without reported cases, noting Kenya had reported eight cases, though authorities maintained there was no widespread outbreak.

El Salvador's decision comes amid regional concerns about Ebola spread, with Kenya reporting eight cases and Nigeria confirming its first case from a traveler arriving from Uganda. Rwandan health authorities have stated the country remains on high alert and is monitoring the situation, coordinating with international health bodies for preparedness and surveillance, particularly due to cross-border transmission risks from the eastern DRC.

Why it mattersRwandan nationals are affected by El Salvador's new entry restrictions, which are based on Ebola concerns despite no reported cases in Rwanda.
