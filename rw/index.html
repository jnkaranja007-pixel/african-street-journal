Rwanda Names 22 Gorillas at Global Conservation Event

The 21st Kwita Izina ceremony blended conservation, culture, and entertainment, drawing international figures to Kinigi.

Kinigi, Rwanda – Twenty-two baby mountain gorillas received names on Friday during the 21st Kwita Izina ceremony, an event that combined conservation efforts with global recognition, entertainment, and cultural celebration. Senior leaders, celebrities, conservationists, and international guests gathered in the foothills of Volcanoes National Park for the occasion. Prime Minister Dr. Justin Nsengiyumva attended as Guest of Honour, alongside government officials and prominent figures from business, sport, and international organizations.

High-profile individuals, including Eng. Ahmed Sadek Elsewedy of Elsewedy Electric, former Miss Belgium Keza Johanna Ameloot, CAA Sports President Paul Danforth, and former France international footballer Mamadou Sakho, were among those who bestowed names upon the newborn gorillas. The names chosen often reflected significant cultural values. Rwandan musicians provided energetic performances, transforming the ceremony into a lively celebration of music and culture.

The event also highlighted Rwanda's commitment to conservation and community development. Prime Minister Nsengiyumva announced plans to expand Volcanoes National Park, aiming to provide more space for the growing mountain gorilla population while ensuring that local communities affected by the expansion benefit from conservation initiatives. The Kwita Izina ceremony has evolved into a significant global event showcasing Rwanda's natural heritage and conservation successes.

Why it mattersThe event highlights Rwanda's conservation success and its expansion of protected areas, which could impact land use and community benefits.

Africa Loses Up to $90 Million Annually to Financial Crime

Ministers and experts gather in Kigali to strengthen regional efforts against money laundering and terrorist financing amidst evolving digital threats.

Eastern and Southern African nations are losing an estimated $88–90 million annually to illicit financial flows, a challenge highlighted as the 26th Council of Ministers meeting of the Eastern and Southern Africa Anti-Money Laundering Group (ESAAMLG) convenes in Kigali. The gathering brings together ministers, senior government officials, and financial crime experts to bolster regional cooperation against money laundering, terrorist financing, and the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction.

The meeting occurs as financial systems rapidly evolve, with criminals increasingly leveraging digital services, cross-border transactions, and complex corporate structures to conceal illicit funds. President of the Senate Dr. François-Xavier Kalinda noted that the same financial systems driving economic growth can be exploited by criminal networks, emphasizing the need for stronger regional cooperation as countries acting independently cannot effectively combat sophisticated financial crime.

Rwanda's Minister of Finance and Economic Planning, Yusuf Murangwa, stated that while Rwanda has improved its anti-money laundering systems, challenges persist. Outgoing Director General of Rwanda’s Financial Intelligence Centre, Gashumba Jeanne Pauline, urged a shift from mere technical compliance to demonstrable outcomes, such as successful prosecutions and asset recovery, stressing that responses must evolve with the changing threats.

Why it mattersGovernments and financial institutions across Eastern and Southern Africa are affected by the need to strengthen anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing systems to combat significant annual financial losses.

BPR Bank Funds 50 Students Through Secondary School

The BPR Bank Foundation will cover school requirements and provide mentorship for 50 underprivileged students over six years.

BPR Bank Rwanda has initiated a six-year scholarship program, the BPR Scholars Programme, to support 50 students from disadvantaged backgrounds through secondary education. The initiative, launched on Friday, September 4, will provide scholarships to 25 boys and 25 girls selected from various districts across Rwanda. The bank will cover all essential school expenses from Senior One to Senior Six, aiming to ensure these students can complete their education.

In collaboration with the Ministry of Education, BPR Bank will identify and select eligible students, ensuring the program aligns with national educational goals. The bank stated the program is designed to overcome financial and socio-economic obstacles that hinder talented students from enrolling, staying in, or finishing secondary school. Beyond covering costs, beneficiaries will participate in a structured mentorship program focused on academic and personal growth.

The mentorship component will address areas such as goal setting, life skills development, confidence building, and career planning, while also offering guidance on future educational and employment opportunities. Vincent Ngirikiringo, Chief Financial Officer of BPR Bank Rwanda, emphasized the bank's intention to make a lasting commitment to the students' development rather than offering only short-term financial aid.

Why it mattersFifty underprivileged students in Rwanda will receive financial support and mentorship to complete their secondary education, addressing socio-economic barriers.

Kagame Urges Youth to See Food as Life's Foundation

President Paul Kagame told young African entrepreneurs that food systems are essential for development and economic growth, not just a basic right.

President Paul Kagame has urged young Africans to view food not merely as a right, but as the fundamental basis for life and development. Speaking at the Africa Food Systems Forum 2026 in Kigali, Kagame emphasized that the continent's ability to secure adequate nutrition is directly tied to its broader development ambitions. He highlighted the growing role of youth in transforming agriculture and the food value chain, encouraging them to explore opportunities beyond farming itself.

The dialogue included young entrepreneurs such as Fareeda Mustapha from Ghana, Musa Mishamo from Tanzania, Imungana Malikana from Zambia, and Rwanda's Lydia Murekatete. Their participation underscored the forum's focus on empowering young Africans to develop practical solutions for agricultural challenges. The forum, themed 'Investing in Africa’s Agri-Food Systems,' aims to attract investment, strengthen food value chains, improve nutrition, and build resilience against climate change.

Kagame stressed that transforming Africa's agriculture from subsistence to a commercially viable sector can create significant employment and entrepreneurship opportunities. He advocated for an integrated system connecting farmers with technology, financing, markets, and consumers, asserting that food systems are critical for the survival, health, and future of Africa's people.

Why it mattersYoung African entrepreneurs are encouraged to explore diverse business and job opportunities within the food value chain, from production to consumer access.

Youth prosecutions drop to 40% in Rwanda

The proportion of young people prosecuted in Rwanda decreased to 40% in the 2025-26 judicial year, down from 51% the previous year.

Rwanda's Prosecutor General, Habyarimana Angélique, announced that 32,231 individuals aged 18 to 30 were prosecuted during the 2025-26 judicial year. This figure represents 40% of all prosecutions, a notable decrease from the 51% recorded in the preceding year. The announcement was made at the opening ceremony for the 2026-27 judicial year in Kigali, attended by senior government officials.

Habyarimana stated that the prosecution service plans to enhance measures aimed at preventing youth involvement in criminal activities, emphasizing their significance to the nation's future. Efforts to curb the distribution of substandard alcoholic beverages were highlighted as a contributing factor to a reduction in crimes associated with their consumption. Theft and assault causing bodily harm were identified as the most prevalent offenses, making up 49% of all case files.

The prosecution service reported a 98% success rate in drug-related cases, noting that drug use can fuel other criminal activities. Overall, the Office of the Prosecutor General processed 68,232 out of 71,276 received case files. The service also plans to continue promoting alternative dispute resolution mechanisms to expedite justice and place citizens at the center of legal processes.

Why it mattersYoung people aged 18 to 30 are directly affected by the decrease in prosecutions, with authorities aiming to reduce their involvement in crime.
