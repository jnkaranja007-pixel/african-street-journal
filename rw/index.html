Rwanda's economy grows 10.6% as reserves reach $2.27 billion

The National Bank of Rwanda says the economy expanded faster in 2025/26, while the central bank raised its policy rate to 8.25%.

Rwanda's economy grew 10.6% in 2025/26, up from 5.7% a year earlier, according to the National Bank of Rwanda's Annual Integrated Report for 2025/26. Foreign exchange reserves stood at $2,272.79 million over the same period.

The report also shows average annual inflation rising from 5.7% to 9.2%, and the Rwandan franc losing 2.28% against the US dollar during the year. The international trade deficit narrowed by 2.8%.

The central bank raised its policy rate from 6.5% to 8.25%. The share of adults borrowing through financial institutions rose from 44% to 48.01%. Banks' capital adequacy ratio edged up to 22%, against a 15% minimum.

Why it mattersAdults borrowing through financial institutions rose from 44% to 48.01%, while the policy rate rose from 6.5% to 8.25%, which affects the cost of new loans.

Rwanda takes custody of genocide suspect Jean Leonard Teganya from US

Teganya, a former National University of Rwanda medical student, was flown home after a US conviction for immigration fraud and perjury.

Rwanda received Jean Leonard Teganya on Monday, November 9, 2026, after the United States deported him. The chartered plane touched down at Kigali International Airport around 7pm local time, although the arrival had first been scheduled for 11am. Police agents led Teganya down the stairs and put him straight into a waiting RIB detainee van.

Teganya is accused of involvement in the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi. He is a former medical student at the National University of Rwanda, and a US court convicted him of immigration fraud and perjury for hiding information about his alleged activities during the genocide. Rwanda's National Public Prosecution Authority took custody of him on arrival.

The return ends a decades-long run from justice for Teganya. Rwandan authorities now hold him in detention to face the genocide allegations against him.

Why it mattersTeganya, accused of genocide involvement, is now in Rwandan custody after a deportation from the United States.

Kigali hosts Africa's Business Heroes Summit

10 finalists will pitch for $1.5 million in grant funding at the December 1-2 event.

Rwanda's capital, Kigali, will host the 8th Africa's Business Heroes (ABH) Summit and Grand Finale on December 1-2. Organisers received over 24,000 applications from across Africa for the competition.

10 finalists, representing eight countries and including four women, will present their established businesses to judges for a share of $1.5 million in grant funding. The overall winner will receive $300,000.

The summit's first day will focus on "Defining Africa’s Intelligence," exploring AI and innovation, followed by the live pitches on the second day. Judges include Dr. Diane Karusisi, CEO of Bank of Kigali, and Shola Akinlade, Co-founder & CEO of Paystack.

Why it mattersRwanda is hosting a continental business competition that will award $1.5 million in grants to entrepreneurs.

Rwanda police launch nationwide fire safety training after recent blazes

The Fire and Rescue Brigade will train residents to use extinguishers and equipment before firefighters arrive, starting in November.

Rwanda National Police has launched a nationwide campaign to teach communities how to prevent fires and respond before firefighters arrive. The campaign began in November and will run in districts across the country, with training sessions planned at bus stations, churches, hotels, markets, garages and large buildings.

Assistant Commissioner of Police Paul Gatambira, head of the Fire and Rescue Brigade, said the training will cover how fires start and spread, how to prevent them, and how to use available equipment. He said information often reaches police late, and that people trained to use extinguishers could take first steps while firefighters are on their way. Police spokesperson ACP Boniface Rutikanga told RBA that firefighters can respond only after a fire has broken out.

The campaign follows several major fires since mid-October. The most destructive was at GOICO market in Muhoza Sector, Musanze District, where goods valued at about Rwf 30 billion were destroyed, according to an assessment. Other recent fires in Kigali damaged factories in the city's industrial zone. Police are urging welders to keep extinguishers within reach, and asking developers and managers of commercial buildings to install water supplies and piping for fighting fires.

Why it mattersManagers of commercial buildings, hotels, markets and garages are asked to install water supplies and piping and to check that extinguishers work and are sufficient in number.

Climate · 2026-11-11

UK spotlights Rwanda climate partnership ahead of COP31

The British High Commission in Rwanda is promoting UK-funded cooling and wetland projects while climate negotiators meet in Türkiye.

The United Kingdom is putting its climate partnership with Rwanda in the spotlight as world leaders and climate negotiators gather in Türkiye for COP31. The British High Commission in Rwanda has been posting videos, photographs and other content over recent weeks to showcase projects ranging from sustainable cooling to wetland restoration and climate finance.

Much of the attention has centred on the 10th anniversary of the Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol and the 38th Meeting of the Parties to the Montreal Protocol, which was held in Kigali earlier this month. The UK has singled out the Africa Centre of Excellence for Sustainable Cooling and Cold Chain, known as ACES, as one of the clearest examples of the amendment's practical results. ACES is a UK-funded partnership with the Rwandan government focused on cleaner and more efficient cooling and cold-chain systems.

The case for such work rests on rising demand for refrigeration and air conditioning as temperatures climb. Conventional cooling equipment can consume large amounts of energy and rely on refrigerants with high global warming potential.

Why it mattersThe UK-funded ACES partnership with the Rwandan government targets cleaner, more efficient cooling and cold-chain systems, and the packet describes it as a Rwandan project.
