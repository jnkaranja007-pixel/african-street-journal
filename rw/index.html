Rwanda Volleyball Team Wins All Group Matches Unbeaten

Rwanda's national women's volleyball team secured a perfect group stage record, advancing to the CAVB African Nations Championship quarterfinals without dropping a single set.

Rwanda has advanced to the quarterfinals of the CAVB Women’s African Nations Championship in Kenya after completing the group stage with a perfect record. The national women’s volleyball team sealed their place in the last eight with an emphatic 3-0 victory over Burkina Faso, winning the sets 25-19, 25-16 and 25-8. Rwanda controlled the match throughout, with the widening margins underlining their dominance as Burkina Faso struggled to contain the pressure, particularly in the decisive third set.

The victory capped an impressive run for Rwanda, who won every match without dropping a single set on their way to the quarterfinals. Their unbeaten campaign now sets up a potentially tougher test in the knockout stage as Rwanda continue their push for a strong finish at the continental championship.

The team's performance demonstrates strong national athletic representation on the continental stage. Their success in the group phase, marked by decisive victories, positions them as strong contenders in the upcoming knockout rounds of the championship.

Africa's Food Systems Connect to Address Shortages

Experts at a Kigali pre-summit debated how to link farm production to consumers amid global crises.

Delegates at the Africa Food Systems Forum pre-summit in Kigali on Monday discussed the continent's persistent food crisis. Despite possessing ample land, farmers, crops, and agricultural knowledge, millions still face food insecurity. The central theme revolved around improving connectivity between disparate parts of the food system to ensure production translates into reliable food security.

Recent global events, including the COVID-19 pandemic and the Russia-Ukraine war, have exposed significant weaknesses. The pandemic highlighted inadequate storage and transport networks, while the conflict revealed a heavy reliance on imported fertilizers. Climate shocks, conflict, and energy disruptions further threaten food production and affordability.

Dr. Agnes Kalibata, Founder and Chair of C4Impact Advisory Group, emphasized that these crises revealed systemic failures rather than production shortfalls. She noted that during the pandemic, insufficient food reserves and poor infrastructure prevented food distribution from surplus areas to those in need, underscoring a failure of system connectivity.

Why it mattersAfrica's food systems are being examined to improve connectivity and ensure production meets the needs of food-insecure populations.

Rwanda Demands African Funding for Continent's Peace Efforts

Prime Minister Nsengiyumva urged African leaders at the AU summit to finance peace operations with predictable resources, rather than relying on external partners.

Rwanda, represented by Prime Minister Dr. Justin Nsengiyumva, has called for greater financial self-sufficiency in addressing African conflicts. Speaking at the 21st African Union Extraordinary Session in Luanda, Angola, Nsengiyumva emphasized that diplomatic decisions alone are insufficient without dedicated, predictable funding from the continent itself. He argued that dependence on external actors for conflict resolution undermines Africa's ability to build lasting peace and security.

The Prime Minister, acting on behalf of President Paul Kagame, highlighted the persistent challenge of financing Africa's peace and security architecture. He stated that the continent's ongoing struggles with increasingly complex and prolonged conflicts place significant demands on governments and regional bodies. The African Union Peace Fund, Rwanda believes, is a crucial component for achieving this financial independence and ensuring the effective implementation of peace decisions.

Nsengiyumva's remarks underscore a broader concern within the African Union regarding the sustainability of peace operations. The summit aimed to address how to move beyond adopting resolutions to actively implementing them through disciplined and reliable financial commitments. This push for internal financing is seen as vital for Africa to manage its own security challenges effectively and reduce reliance on outside intervention.

Why it mattersAfrican leaders are affected by the need to secure predictable funding for peace operations to ensure the continent's stability and reduce reliance on external partners.

Author Connects Stoicism to Rwandan Home Lessons

Joy Uwanziga's new book finds ancient philosophy echoed in local values of restraint and patience.

Author Joy Uwanziga launched her book, "Rising with Dignity: The African Stoic, The Rwandan Way," in Kigali, exploring the intersections of ancient stoic philosophy and Rwandan traditions. Uwanziga discovered that core stoic principles, such as self-control and mastering reactions, resonated with lessons she learned at home from her grandparents and elders.

The book positions stoicism not as an imported concept but as a philosophy whose principles align with existing African values like dignity, self-mastery, responsibility, and resilience. Uwanziga highlights the African understanding that individual well-being is linked to the welfare of the community, offering these insights as tools for navigating modern life's pressures.

At the book launch, Uwanziga discussed how her research revealed familiar wisdom within Rwandan homes, suggesting that African communities already possess profound philosophical resources. The work aims to demonstrate how these deeply ingrained values can help individuals face contemporary challenges with greater fortitude and understanding.

Why it mattersRwandan author Joy Uwanziga's book connects ancient stoic philosophy with local values, offering insights into navigating modern life through dignity and self-mastery.
