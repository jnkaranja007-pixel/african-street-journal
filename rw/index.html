Rwanda Central Bank Hikes Rate to 8.75% as Inflation Reaches 14.5%

The National Bank of Rwanda increased its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to combat accelerating inflation, which has surpassed the target range and reached 14.5%.

Rwanda's central bank has raised its benchmark interest rate by 50 basis points to 8.75%, a move aimed at curbing escalating inflation that reached 14.5% in July. The Monetary Policy Committee announced the decision on August 26, citing elevated price pressures and increased economic risks. This marks a continuation of the monetary tightening cycle that began in late 2025, as inflation significantly exceeds the bank's target range of 2%-8%.

Headline inflation surged from 9.1% in the first quarter of 2026 to 13.2% in the second quarter, culminating in the 14.5% figure for July. The National Bank of Rwanda projects inflation to average 13.1% for 2026, a slight decrease from the prior forecast of 13.9%. Increases in core, fresh-food, and energy prices are driving this trend, with energy inflation notably surging to 45.7%.

Despite inflationary challenges, Rwanda's economy demonstrated strong growth, expanding by 10% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026. However, the central bank anticipates inflation will remain above its target throughout 2026, with a gradual return expected in the latter half of 2027. Risks such as the El Niño climate pattern's impact on food prices and geopolitical tensions contributing to elevated global commodity prices were also identified.

Why it mattersInvestors face higher borrowing costs and potentially slower economic growth due to the central bank's decision to increase interest rates.

Rwanda's Nyabarongo II Dam at 75% Completion

The multipurpose dam, expected to finish by end-2027, will add 43.5 megawatts and improve water management, irrigation, and flood control.

Construction of Rwanda’s Nyabarongo II Multipurpose Dam has reached approximately 75% completion, with the Rwanda Energy Group (REG) targeting an end-2027 finish. Located between Gakenke and Kamonyi districts, the project is set to add 43.5 megawatts of renewable electricity to the national grid, bolstering domestic generation capacity.

Beyond power generation, the dam's reservoir will regulate the Nyabarongo River's flow, supporting downstream irrigation to expand agricultural production and enhance farming reliability, especially during dry periods. REG indicates the project could help reclaim land for agricultural use and significantly improve flood management, mitigating impacts on communities and infrastructure.

The multipurpose infrastructure is also anticipated to create opportunities for inland water transport, tourism, and other economic activities. The government is exploring the possibility of accommodating floating solar installations on the reservoir. This project is a key part of Rwanda's strategy to increase renewable energy and reduce reliance on non-renewable sources.

Why it mattersFarmers will benefit from expanded irrigation and reclaimed land, while businesses can anticipate more reliable electricity and new transport opportunities.

Rwanda C-Section Rate Climbs to 24%, Exceeding WHO Guidance

The latest Rwanda Demographic and Health Survey reveals a significant rise in surgical births, prompting questions about the drivers behind the trend and its implications.

Rwanda's Caesarean section rate has reached 24%, more than double the 10% level recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO) for population-level impact on mortality. This increase, detailed in the latest Rwanda Demographic and Health Survey (RDHS7), is prompting renewed discussion on whether the growing number of surgical births is primarily due to medical necessity or other contributing factors.

The rate represents a substantial jump from previous years, with data from the 2019–20 DHS showing a rate of 15.6%, up from 2.2% in 2000. While Rwanda has seen progress in reducing maternal deaths, with the ratio declining from 203 per 100,000 live births in 2020 to 149 in 2025, health experts emphasize that the increase in C-sections alone does not explain this decline. Factors like skilled birth attendants and emergency obstetric services also play a crucial role.

Previous studies have highlighted disparities in C-section rates, with private facilities reporting 60.6% in 2019–20 compared to 15.4% in public facilities. Health experts stress the importance of ensuring C-sections are performed when medically required while avoiding unnecessary procedures. Understanding the specific reasons for the rise in Rwanda is critical for maintaining the procedure's life-saving role and protecting women from avoidable surgeries.

Why it mattersThe rising C-section rate affects women undergoing childbirth, raising concerns about medical necessity versus other factors influencing surgical birth rates.

RSSB Acquires Full Control of Inyange, Ruliba from Crystal Ventures

The Rwanda Social Security Board now holds 100% ownership of Inyange Industries and Ruliba Clays, following the acquisition of remaining shares from Crystal Ventures Limited.

The Rwanda Social Security Board (RSSB) has acquired the remaining shares in Inyange Industries and Ruliba Clays from Crystal Ventures Limited (CVL), securing full ownership of both companies. This transaction grants RSSB complete control over the future expansion plans for these two entities. The deal was announced on Thursday, August 27, concluding a long-standing period of shared interests.

While RSSB has previously reported strong overall investment performance, questions are being raised about the transparency of this specific transaction. Basic financial details such as the purchase price for each company, their independent valuations, profitability, liabilities, and expected returns for pensioners have not been disclosed by RSSB officials, despite repeated requests over two days.

The acquisition is particularly sensitive as Crystal Ventures Limited is widely identified as the investment arm of the ruling RPF. This raises governance questions about whether the transaction was conducted at arm's length on purely commercial merit, especially given the association between the buyer and seller. RSSB has stated that it has made many successful investments, but each major transaction should be evaluated on its own financial merits.

Why it mattersPensioners are affected because the lack of transparency surrounding the acquisition of Inyange Industries and Ruliba Clays by RSSB prevents them from understanding the financial merits and potential returns of this significant investment of their funds.

Young Farmer Transitions to Full-Time Agriculture With Input Support

Adeline Gwizimpundu, a 28-year-old farmer, leveraged credit for seeds and fertilizer from One Acre Fund to grow her harvests and become a full-time farmer.

Adeline Gwizimpundu, a 28-year-old farmer in Rwanda's Kayonza District, has transitioned to full-time farming after receiving crucial support from One Acre Fund. Initially renting one hectare of land to grow beans, maize, cassava, and Irish potatoes, she found that renting alone was insufficient to cover the costs of essential seeds and fertilizer. This is where One Acre Fund provided the necessary credit, enabling her to increase her harvests and build a more sustainable livelihood.

Her journey challenges the notion that young people are uninterested in agriculture. Gwizimpundu, who was a teacher before dedicating herself to farming, now owns land earned through her agricultural income and aims for her children to view farming with the same respect as other professions. The organization she works with, One Acre Fund/Tubura, provides access to quality farm inputs on credit, training, and supports initiatives like Kwiharika, where parents share land with their children.

Rwanda has a young population, with over 70% under 35, presenting an opportunity to engage this demographic in agriculture as a source of income and entrepreneurship. While some young farmers face challenges with land access, others like Gwizimpundu, after initial success, require better market access. The organization advocates for agricultural systems designed around the lived realities of young farmers, acknowledging their diverse needs and aspirations for wealth creation and community opportunity.

Why it mattersYoung farmers like Adeline Gwizimpundu are directly affected by access to credit for farm inputs, which enables them to increase harvests and transition to full-time farming.
