Three Rwandan tea factories certify 2025 production as carbon neutral

Mulindi, Shagasha and Nshili Kivu recorded about 32,926 tonnes of carbon removals beyond assessed emissions, according to the certification.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Black tea produced in 2025 by Rwanda's Mulindi, Shagasha and Nshili Kivu factories has received independent carbon-neutrality certification, The Wood Foundation Africa announced on November 24. The certification applies only to that year's production, not to future output or the tea's entire journey to consumers.

The assessment counted 24,076 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent in emissions against approximately 57,002 tonnes of recognised removals, leaving removals about 32,926 tonnes higher. Earthood Services Limited issued the certificate on October 30, verifying the data against two international standards for carbon footprints and greenhouse-gas inventories: ISO 14068-1:2023 (the international standard for carbon neutrality) and ISO 14064-3:2019 (the standard for verifying greenhouse-gas claims). Recognised removals came from plantations and shade trees within the assessment's boundaries.

The factories received 33 million kilogrammes of green leaf from 14,000 smallholder farmers in 2025, valued at about $9 million, and produced 7.6 million kilogrammes of tea worth $19 million. Rwanda is working on a national tea-sector strategy focused on branding, market expansion and competitiveness.

Why it mattersThe 14,000 smallholder farmers who supply the factories were paid about $9 million for their green leaf in 2025, and the certification is expected to help the factories reach international buyers seeking verified sustainability credentials.
