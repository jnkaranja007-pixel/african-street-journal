Rwanda’s chief justice demands measurable results from justice institutions

Domitilla Mukantaganzwa urged closer coordination as Rwanda works to deliver on 249 human-rights recommendations it accepted in 2026.

Source: Rwanda Dispatch · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Rwanda’s Chief Justice, Domitilla Mukantaganzwa, told justice institutions on Thursday, 26 November, to measure success by changes in people’s lives, not by policies adopted. She spoke at the opening of the 11th Justice, Reconciliation, Law and Order Sector peer-review and Universal Periodic Review retreat in Nyagatare.

Mukantaganzwa called for institutions to coordinate across investigations, prosecutions, court hearings, legal aid and enforcement, saying delays at one stage can weaken the rest. Rwanda’s Rule of Law score in the Rwanda Governance Scorecard rose to 85.47% in 2026 from 81.63% in 2025, a gain of 3.84 percentage points.

Rwanda accepted 249 of 286 recommendations at its fourth Universal Periodic Review, held in January, and took note of the other 37. The justice sector is also working toward halving case backlogs under the second National Strategy for Transformation.

Why it mattersJustice institutions must coordinate across the stages of a case, while the sector works toward halving its backlog under the second National Strategy for Transformation.
