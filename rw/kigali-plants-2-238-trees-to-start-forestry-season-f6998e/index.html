Kigali plants 2,238 trees to start forestry season

More than 5,000 residents joined the Rwanda Forestry Authority and Ministry of Environment on Saturday, November 7, to plant seedlings.

Source: Rwanda Dispatch · 2026-11-08 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Kigali began its 2026/2027 tree-planting season on Saturday, November 7, by planting 2,238 seedlings across eight hectares in Gahanga Sector. The community work programme in Nyabigugu Village involved over 5,000 residents, security personnel, and partners.

The campaign, themed “My Tree, My Legacy,” aims to foster community responsibility for tree maintenance and protection, according to Fidèle Bingwa, Permanent Secretary at the Ministry of Environment. Concorde Nsengumuremyi, Director General of the Rwanda Forestry Authority, stated that expanding forest cover is important for addressing soil erosion and land degradation.

The planted species, including Imisave and Jacaranda, are expected to aid soil conservation, enhance hillside appearance, and support biodiversity near the Nyabarongo River wetland. This effort is part of Rwanda's wider strategy to increase forest cover and build resilience to climate change.

Why it mattersResidents of Gahanga Sector will benefit from trees planted to protect agricultural land, reduce soil erosion, and regulate rainfall.
