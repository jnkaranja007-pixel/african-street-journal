NBA confirms it follows US policy on Rwanda deal

The basketball league told US senators it will adjust its business ties based on American government actions.

Source: Rwanda Dispatch · 2026-11-04 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The NBA will continue its partnership with Rwanda, stating its international business approach mirrors US government policy. In a letter to two US senators, the league's deputy commissioner Mark Tatum confirmed this stance, noting that the NBA adjusts its operations when American policy shifts. For example, the military-funded Armée Patriotique Rwandaise (APR) was withdrawn from the Basketball Africa League (BAL) after Washington imposed economic sanctions on the Rwanda Defence Force.

Senators Jeff Merkley and Marsha Blackburn had expressed alarm over the NBA's deepening ties with Kigali, questioning the league's financial dealings with governments accused of human rights abuses. Tatum did not directly answer these questions but pointed to the APR's removal as proof the NBA follows US policy, stating that the league condemns human rights violations globally and will adapt as policy demands.

This exchange follows previous pressure on Rwanda's sporting partnerships, including a call from the DRC last year for sports bodies to cut ties with Kigali. While some European football clubs ended sponsorship deals, the NBA has maintained its plans, including for the 10,000-seat BK arena.

Why it mattersThe NBA's continued partnership with Rwanda affects the country's sports development and international image.
