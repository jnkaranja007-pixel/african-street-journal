Rwanda's EAX builds storage and quality systems to cut crop losses

The Kigali-based exchange has added warehouses, dryers and farmer training to help produce meet market standards and reach buyers.

Source: Rwanda Dispatch · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

The East Africa Exchange (EAX), a commodities marketplace headquartered in Kigali, has invested in certified warehouses, silos and mechanical dryers while training Rwandan farmers and cooperatives in storage, handling and quality. The exchange was established in 2013 to address problems including fragmented markets, post-harvest losses and inconsistent standards.

EAX Chief Executive Clement Kayitakire said maize production lost up to 30% after harvest in the exchange’s early years. Field officers now test produce for moisture and aflatoxin, help grade it and train farmers in drying, cleaning and storage. EAX says trucks rejected at its facilities for poor quality have fallen significantly as farmers learn market requirements.

At its facilities, the exchange inspects deliveries and can dry, clean, sort or re-bag commodities that need corrective work. It monitors stored stocks for moisture changes and infestation, and isolates produce with uncorrectable problems or food-safety risks.

Why it mattersRwandan farmers and cooperatives receive training in drying and storage as EAX works to reduce post-harvest losses that once reached up to 30% for maize.
