Mozambique fuel retailers launch digital platform

ARCOMOC's new system connects operators nationwide to simplify information access and reduce bureaucracy.

The Association of Roadside Fuel Operators of Mozambique (ARCOMOC) launched a new digital platform in Maputo on May 11, 2026, to connect fuel retailers across all provinces in real time. The platform aims to simplify information access, reduce bureaucracy, and improve coordination with partners for a more efficient sector.

ARCOMOC President Luís Pereira stated the platform is a strategic tool to strengthen the association and enhance member value, focusing on innovation, commercial margins, supply security, and economic sustainability. BCI's Director of Public Relations, Heisler Castelo David, highlighted digital transformation as a driver of economic development, aligning with the bank's vision for Mozambique's progress.

The Deputy National Director for Hydrocarbons and Fuels, José Muhai, confirmed authorities are improving the regulatory environment with reforms to the legal framework. CTA Vice-President Amâncio Gume praised the initiative for its strategic vision and organizational capacity in an increasingly digital business world.

Mozambique releases $50 million for fuel imports

Minister Estevão Pale announced measures to ease fuel supply constraints following international market disruptions.

Mozambique's government has made US$50 million available to address ongoing fuel supply constraints, Minister Estevão Pale announced in Parliament. The country faces shortages due to a combination of internal and external factors, including reliance on imports and international market disruptions.

Pale explained that approximately 80% of Mozambique's petroleum products are imported from the Middle East, which experienced escalated conflict and supply chain disruptions in March 2026. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz further increased costs and logistical challenges, while difficulties in securing import financing also impacted distribution companies' capacity.

Cross-border demand also contributes to the shortage, as Mozambique's lower fuel prices encourage smuggling into neighbouring countries. Some fuel unloaded at ports remains under financial retention, with ownership held by suppliers until payments are regularised.

Why it mattersMozambicans face fuel shortages due to import issues and cross-border smuggling.

Frelimo pledges funds for Maputo oncology unit repair

The ruling party announced a contribution to restore the hospital unit damaged by fire on Sunday.

The Frelimo Political Commission announced it will contribute to the restoration of the Oncology Unit at the Central Hospital of Maputo (HCM). The unit was affected by a fire last Sunday.

The party organ is calling on its members, sympathizers, and the general public to join this solidarity initiative. The goal is to ensure the recovery of this vital hospital service.

The decision was announced on Monday, May 11, 2026. The Frelimo commission seeks to gather support for the damaged facility.

Why it mattersMozambicans needing cancer treatment at the Central Hospital of Maputo will benefit from the restoration of the Oncology Unit.

Mozambican engineers to drill Coral North wells

Empresa Nacional de Hidrocarbonetos will gain technical experience through a 10-month campaign to complete six wells.

Mozambican engineers and specialists will participate in drilling and completing six wells for the Coral North gas project over 10 months. Empresa Nacional de Hidrocarbonetos (ENH), through ENH Serviços and its joint venture with Saipem, is moving beyond its traditional role as a statutory shareholder.

ENH has stated ambitions to undertake technical work and compete for contracts, rather than solely holding state interests in petroleum projects. This campaign offers an opportunity to begin demonstrating that shift, providing ENH personnel with practical knowledge beyond reports or training.

The company has spent decades holding state interests in projects operated by international companies. These stakes provide Mozambique with a claim on future profits and a seat in project structures, but have not necessarily built the technical capacity to develop the country’s resources.

Why it mattersMozambican engineers will gain practical experience in a technically demanding deep-water campaign.
