Maputo hospital fire destroys oncology service

Staff evacuated 22 patients safely from Maputo Central Hospital's oncology unit after a fire broke out Sunday morning.

A fire severely damaged Maputo Central Hospital’s oncology service on Sunday morning, forcing the evacuation of 22 patients. Hospital staff successfully moved all patients to safety despite smoke, flames, and reported explosions within the building. The fire destroyed much of the recently renovated facility, which provides scarce medical services.

The immediate cause of the fire remains unknown, and investigators are looking into whether automatic detectors and alarms were operational. They will also examine the electrical system's inspection status, the effectiveness of fire doors, and the availability of functioning response equipment for staff. A nurse first reported smelling smoke around 07:10.

The incident raises questions about the systems in place to detect and contain fires at the hospital. The investigation aims to establish how the fire started and assess the preventative measures that were in place.

Maputo Central Hospital resumes cancer treatment after fire

95 percent of patient records were saved from the blaze that damaged the oncology unit.

The Central Hospital of Maputo, Mozambique's largest health facility, has resumed outpatient oncology services in the Dermatology Department's building after a fire on Sunday. The hospital's Clinical Director, Dércio Fernandes, stated that 95% of patient records were preserved in the building's basement, unaffected by the flames or firefighting water. Teams are now working to reconstruct damaged records to restart chemotherapy administration as soon as possible.

Fernandes reported that the 22 patients admitted to the oncology ward before the fire were safely transferred, with 10 moved to the Dermatology ward. On Monday, the service saw 60 patients for consultations, 12 for emergencies, and 20 for outpatient chemotherapy. However, these chemotherapy patients had to wait for new records to be created as their scheduled documents were destroyed.

The hospital aims to quickly restore daily treatments for the 30 to 40 patients who previously received day-hospital care. Specialized chemotherapy chairs have been moved to the Dermatology building to facilitate this. Despite challenges from the relocation and service reorganization, nursing, psychology, and medical teams are mobilized to ensure patient care continues.

Why it mattersCancer patients in Mozambique can now access chemotherapy and other oncology services at the Central Hospital of Maputo following a temporary relocation of services.

Mozambique's former defence minister Joaquim Tobias Dai dies

Dai served as minister from 2000 to 2008 and was a veteran of the National Liberation Struggle.

Former Mozambique Defence Minister Joaquim Tobias Dai died on Monday at the age of 75, reportedly from illness. Dai, a veteran of the National Liberation Struggle, served as Minister of National Defence from January 2000 to March 2008.

He held the defence portfolio under Presidents Joaquim Chissano and Armando Guebuza, who was his brother-in-law. Dai also served as a member of parliament during his career in public service. The President of the Republic issued a condolence message.

President Daniel Chapo highlighted Dai’s contributions to national sovereignty and independence in his message. The presidency stated that Dai dedicated part of his life to serving the nation. Condolences were extended to his family, friends, and comrades.

Why it mattersThe death of Joaquim Tobias Dai affects his family, friends, and former colleagues who are offered condolences.

Mozambique plans $189.8 million agricultural campaign

The 2026/27 plan includes emergency support for producers affected by El Niño and investment in agribusiness.

Mozambique's government has presented a US$189.8 million plan for the 2026/27 agricultural campaign. Minister Roberto Mito Albino chaired a meeting in Maputo where the plan was detailed to ministry officials and provincial directors.

The Agricultural Transformation Programme, funded by the government, World Bank, African Development Bank, and IFAD, aims to strengthen productive capacity, food security, and agricultural value chains. Around US$94.7 million will support producers at risk from El Niño with agricultural kits and other aid.

The remaining US$95.1 million will fund agribusiness development, including credit lines and institutional support for seeds, mechanisation, and livestock. The plan also includes the provision of 905 motor cultivators, 251 tractors, and 3,363 motor pumps.

Why it mattersFarmers in Mozambique will receive agricultural kits, irrigation systems, and other support to combat the effects of El Niño and improve production.

Politics · 2026-11-03

Mozambique government to brief parliament on health, roads, and climate

Parliamentary groups demand details on El Niño impact, National Road 1 repairs, and Nampula General Hospital construction delays.

The Mozambican government will appear before the Assembly of the Republic on Wednesday and Thursday to provide information to deputies. The executive will report on actions in health, social protection, and road infrastructure. The Frelimo party bloc specifically requested details on measures to prevent and mitigate the impacts of floods and El Niño, expected during the 2026/2027 rainy season.

The PODEMOS bloc wants a full account of spending on National Road 1 between 2015 and October 2026, including details on funded sections, investment years, and financing sources. The Renamo bloc is demanding an end date for the Nampula General Hospital construction, which began in 2017 and was due for completion in 2019. They also seek information on efforts to combat corruption and medicine diversion within the National Health System.

Renamo stated that despite 51 years of independence, many health units lack basic medicines and equipment. They also noted that essential medicines and hospital equipment are openly for sale. The Frelimo bloc highlighted that predicted rainfall deficits between January and March 2027 could affect districts in Tete, Sofala, Manica, Inhambane, Gaza, Maputo, Cabo Delgado, and Nampula provinces.

Why it mattersMozambicans will receive updates on government actions concerning health services, road infrastructure, and potential impacts of climate events like El Niño.
