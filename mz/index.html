Chapo Demands Central Bank Address Foreign Currency Access Issues

President Daniel Chapo urged the new governor of the Bank of Mozambique to tackle economic agents' concerns over accessing foreign currency.

President Daniel Chapo has directed the new governor of the Bank of Mozambique, Felisberto Navalha, to address the significant challenges companies face in accessing foreign currency. Navalha, who took office on Wednesday in Maputo, replacing Rogério Zandamela, was tasked by Chapo to investigate these concerns with "rigor, responsibility and transparency" and find sustainable solutions. Businesses have reported difficulties in obtaining the necessary foreign exchange for importing raw materials, equipment, and financing essential economic activities.

Chapo emphasized that while the central bank must pay attention to these issues, the scarcity of foreign currency is not solely a monetary policy problem or the exclusive responsibility of the Bank of Mozambique. He stated that a structural solution requires increasing production and exports, diversifying export products, processing local resources, and attracting productive investment. The President also called for continuous coordination between monetary, financial, and fiscal policy leaders.

Felisberto Navalha brings extensive experience to the role, having served 28 years at the Bank of Mozambique in various capacities, including director of Economic Studies and Statistics, administrator, and board member. He also has a background as a consultant for the Tax Authority and as a lecturer at the Faculty of Economics at Eduardo Mondlane University. President Chapo's remarks were made during Navalha's inauguration ceremony.

Why it mattersBusinesses and economic agents are directly affected by the difficulties in accessing foreign currency, which impacts their ability to import necessary goods and finance operations.

Gaza athletes disqualified for age falsification in school games

Fourteen out of 31 detected cases of age falsification at the 16th National School Sports Festival involve athletes from Gaza province.

The province of Gaza leads in age falsification cases at the 16th National School Sports Festival, currently underway in Inhambane. Fourteen of the 31 athletes caught competing with falsified ages were from Gaza. Some participants were found to be 18, 19, and even 21 years old, competing in an event intended for students aged 11 to 16.

As a result of these irregularities, the Gaza delegation has been disqualified from the handball competition, mirroring the earlier disqualification of their male volleyball team. An organization source confirmed that the handball team was removed due to documentation issues regarding athlete ages. This marks the second instance of age falsification involving Gaza in this edition of the games, raising questions about documentation management.

The 16th National School Sports Festival brings together over 1,400 students from across Mozambique in eight sports, including athletics, handball, basketball, and football. The competition is scheduled to conclude on September 6th. Organizers stated they will continue to verify participant documentation and eligibility throughout the event to ensure adherence to established rules.

Why it mattersYoung athletes from Gaza are affected by disqualifications from sporting events due to age falsification, impacting their participation and the integrity of the competition.

President Revokes Central Bank Governor Appointment

Waldemar de Sousa's appointment as governor of the Bank of Mozambique was revoked less than 24 hours after being named, citing unspecified 'supervening issues'.

President Daniel Chapo has revoked the appointment of Waldemar de Sousa as governor of the Bank of Mozambique, less than a day after naming him to the post. The Presidency cited "supervening issues" for the reversal, which occurred after protests emerged regarding accusations against Sousa related to administrative authorisations connected to the country's hidden debts. The timing of the revocation has led to speculation that the decision is linked to a criminal case reportedly opened against Sousa in 2020.

The accusations against Sousa, which surfaced publicly six years prior to his appointment, raise questions about the vetting process for high-level state positions. It remains unclear whether the Presidency was aware of the unresolved case when initially appointing Sousa, or if advisors failed to identify publicly available information. The security services, responsible for examining the records of potential appointees, also face scrutiny regarding their role in the vetting process.

The rapid reversal of Sousa's appointment, from nomination one evening to revocation the following day, suggests a lack of thorough preparation in the decision-making process. This situation highlights potential weaknesses in the integrity checks applied to presidential appointments, particularly for sensitive roles such as the central bank governor, where due diligence is critical.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the instability and potential lack of thorough vetting in high-level financial appointments, which can impact confidence in the banking sector.

Economist Urges Equal Support for Small Businesses

Economist José Sulemane advocates for applying the same institutional, legal, and political mobilization to SMEs as to megaprojects to foster job creation and economic growth.

Economist José Sulemane stated that Mozambique should extend the same institutional, legal, and political support given to megaprojects to Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs). He argued that SMEs are crucial for generating employment and stimulating the national economy. Sulemane emphasized the need for a legal and public policy framework that simplifies the establishment and operation of these businesses, noting that while megaprojects are strategically important, their job creation capacity is limited compared to the potential of SMEs.

Speaking at an open lecture at Pedagogical University in Maputo, Sulemane, a former National Director of Planning and recently retired from the International Monetary Fund (IMF), stressed the country's need to create conditions for more national companies to open, operate, and grow. He pointed out that Sub-Saharan Africa requires approximately twenty million new jobs annually to accommodate population growth, making the focus on SMEs particularly relevant.

Sulemane also highlighted a significant challenge in Mozambique: the existence of multiple sectoral policies that lack alignment with a unified national development objective. He advocated for a clear sequence of reforms, suggesting that not all changes can be implemented simultaneously. Drawing a parallel with Vietnam's success in transforming sectors like coffee through coordinated public policies, production, and economic integration, he stressed that national growth must translate into improved living conditions and per capita income.

Why it mattersSmall and medium-sized enterprises are affected by a lack of integrated national development strategy and require legal and policy infrastructure to facilitate their opening and growth.

KARMOL equips Nacala fishermen to boost productivity

The fishing association ASSOPENA received nets, diving gear, and storage containers to improve livelihoods and food security.

KARMOL Moçambique has provided fishing equipment and materials to the Naherenque Fishermen's Association (ASSOPENA) in Nacala, aiming to enhance the productive capacity of artisanal fishermen and improve their livelihoods. The donation includes fishing nets, ropes, floats, fishing lines, diving equipment, and containers for fish preservation. This initiative seeks to support local fishing communities by promoting safer and more sustainable fishing practices and fostering economic development linked to the fishing sector.

Bulut Karadag, Director of NEZIH BEY, KARMOL's Floating Platform in Nacala, stated that the delivery of equipment is a contribution to strengthening local fishermen's productive capacity, supporting their families, and creating better conditions for an activity vital to the community's economy and food security. Calibo Buananli, Secretary of ASSOPENA, expressed gratitude for the partnership and KARMOL's commitment to community development and economic opportunities, noting the support's importance for strengthening fishing activities and improving community life.

Gulssumo Mutarupa, a representative from Electricidade de Moçambique (EDM), KARMOL's energy partner, highlighted the company's social responsibility and the direct benefit to a community engaged in fishing activities. Municipal authorities, represented by Tuhane Amade, Councillor for Agriculture and Environment, urged beneficiaries to use the equipment responsibly for the development of fishing activities and to ensure the support reaches families and the community.

Why it mattersArtisanal fishermen in Nacala are directly benefiting from new equipment that improves their ability to fish and preserve their catch, impacting their income and food security.
