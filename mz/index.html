Frelimo Congress Set for Nampula, Chapo Consolidates Power

The ruling party's upcoming congress will elect new leadership, potentially shifting the balance of power within Mozambique's most influential political body.

Frelimo has announced its thirteenth congress will convene in Nampula from July 20 to 25 next year, marking the first under President Daniel Chapo's leadership. While the Central Committee meeting in Chimoio appeared routine, it signals the start of a year-long internal struggle for party control. Delegates will elect a new Central Committee, which subsequently selects the Political Commission, Frelimo's primary executive body whose decisions frequently guide government actions.

Traditionally, a strong party leader uses these elections to solidify their authority. Chapo, however, inherited party structures established before his presidency and has yet to build a political machine comparable to those previously commanded by Armando Guebuza and Filipe Nyusi. The congress in Nampula presents an opportunity for him to either consolidate power by installing loyalists or govern through existing factions with competing interests.

The congress outcomes will also address the ongoing debate about the extent to which the president should adhere to party body decisions, given the Political Commission lacks constitutional authority over the state. Some party members have questioned the current commission's alignment with Chapo, though evidence suggests the body aims for collective decisions and has not obstructed his governance. Chapo faces a choice between reinforcing his control with loyalists or embracing internal diversity.

Why it mattersThe election of a new Central Committee and Political Commission will determine who holds influence within Frelimo, potentially affecting government policy and patronage networks.

EDM Marks 49 Years, Faces Climate Challenges

Mozambique's electricity company, Electricidade de Moçambique, celebrated its 49th anniversary with a workshop focused on adapting to climate change impacts.

Electricidade de Moçambique (EDM) marked its 49th anniversary today, using the occasion to launch its golden jubilee celebrations. The company hosted a workshop to reflect on its achievements and was challenged to implement measures addressing climate-related issues. Established in 1977 during a period of conflict, EDM is now looking towards its next fifty years.

The workshop served as a platform for EDM to assess its progress and consider future strategies. A key outcome was the company's recognition of the need to adapt its operations in response to the evolving climate landscape. This focus on climate resilience signals a strategic shift for the national electricity provider.

As EDM commemorates nearly five decades of service, its leadership is tasked with navigating the significant environmental challenges ahead. The company's commitment to addressing climate impacts is crucial for its long-term sustainability and its role in Mozambique's development.

Why it mattersEDM's focus on climate adaptation affects its operational stability and infrastructure planning, which are critical for reliable electricity supply.

CTA Sees Food Security as Economic Opportunity

Mozambique's private sector confederation advocates for integrating food security and nutrition into economic development strategies, urging greater private sector involvement.

The Confederation of Economic Associations of Mozambique (CTA) has declared that food security and nutrition are not solely social and humanitarian issues, but also significant economic and development opportunities for the nation. CTA President Álvaro Massingue stated on Tuesday that the organization is committed to mobilizing businesses for a more active role in improving national nutrition.

Massingue, who also serves as the Private Sector Champion for Nutrition in Mozambique, emphasized the goal of promoting private sector engagement throughout the entire food value chain. This includes production, processing, fortification, and distribution of nutritious, safe, and quality foods at accessible prices for the population. Key areas for partnership with the World Food Programme (PMA) include promoting local procurement, developing value chains, and enhancing processing and fortification capacities.

The CTA offered to act as a liaison between the PMA and national businesses, facilitating dialogue, mobilizing companies, and fostering investment opportunities. The PMA's National Director and Representative in Mozambique acknowledged the country's agricultural potential but noted financial limitations hindering its full realization. She highlighted that malnutrition impacts health, productivity, and economic growth, underscoring the private sector's crucial role in combating undernutrition and building resilient food systems.

Why it mattersThe private sector is being called to actively participate in the food value chain to improve nutrition and create economic opportunities through production, investment, and job creation.

HCM records over three thousand traffic accident victims in first semester

The Central Hospital of Maputo registered 3,014 victims of road accidents between January and June 2026, a slight decrease from the previous year.

The Central Hospital of Maputo (HCM) recorded 3,014 victims of road accidents in the first six months of 2026. This figure represents a slight reduction compared to the same period last year, when 3,124 cases were reported. The hospital's spokesperson, Dino Lopes, stated that the monthly average of victims was 502 cases this year, down from 514 in the first half of 2025. Consequently, the number of fatalities also decreased, with 10 deaths recorded this year compared to 13 in the same period of 2025.

Many victims arrived at the hospital with severe injuries, including multiple fractures and head trauma, with some in hemorrhagic shock despite medical efforts. The high number of cases places significant pressure on the hospital's neurosurgery, orthopedics, and traumatology departments, often requiring prolonged hospital stays and specialized treatment. The hospital also receives patients transferred from other provinces for serious injuries resulting from various accidents.

The increasing number of accidents involving motorcycles, particularly among individuals aged sixteen to forty, is a growing concern. These accidents have significant social and economic consequences, potentially leading to temporary or permanent disability that impacts family livelihoods and overall productivity. The HCM emphasizes the need for a public health approach involving multiple sectors, including those responsible for vehicle licensing and traffic enforcement.

Why it mattersThe Central Hospital of Maputo's services are under pressure due to a high volume of traffic accident victims, impacting its capacity to treat patients and leading to prolonged hospital stays.
