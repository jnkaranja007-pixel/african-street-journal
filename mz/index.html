South Africa's IDC Eyes Mozal Aluminum Smelter Revival

The Industrial Development Corporation is in high-level talks to buy South32's stake in the Mozal smelter, aiming to restart operations by year-end.

South Africa's Industrial Development Corporation (IDC) is negotiating to acquire South32's 63.7% stake in the Mozal aluminum smelter, with the goal of reactivating the facility before the end of the year. The smelter, which represents 3% of Mozambique's economy and is the country's largest employer, was idled in March after failing to secure a tariff relief contract with Eskom. The IDC, which currently holds a 31.4% stake, is evaluating all options to revitalize the plant.

IDC Chief Executive Officer Mmakgoshi Lekhethe confirmed significant interest in purchasing South32's share, noting that potential investors, including former Mozal shareholders, see this as a opportune moment to re-enter the aluminum sector. The IDC has engaged transaction consultants to conduct due diligence and explore three scenarios: acquiring South32's shares, facilitating an alternative ownership structure with partners, or selling the IDC's own stake. BHP executive Catherine Raw's recent visit to South Africa, where she oversees capital allocation, adds to the ongoing discussions.

The Mozal smelter, located near Maputo, was shut down by South32 due to escalating energy costs impacting its business viability. The IDC is actively seeking strategic capital partners to collaborate on the acquisition and long-term operation of the facility. The outcome of these negotiations will determine the future of the smelter and its significant contribution to the Mozambican economy.

Why it mattersSouth32 is considering selling its stake in Mozal, and the IDC is negotiating to acquire it, potentially reactivating the smelter.

Libyan Rivals Agree on Elections

Rival administrations in Libya have reached an agreement to hold elections within the next two years, aiming to end political division.

Libya's rival administrations have agreed to hold elections within the next two years. This understanding, reached on Sunday, is an attempt to end the country's political division and reunify the nation. The agreement follows months of negotiations facilitated by the United Nations.

The country has been mired in a prolonged crisis since the fall of Muammar Kadhafi in 2011. The UN-led talks aimed to bridge the gap between the competing political factions that have emerged since that time.

This accord represents a significant step towards resolving the ongoing instability. The successful implementation of elections could pave the way for a more unified and stable government in Libya.

Why it mattersLibyan citizens are affected by the agreement as it aims to end political division and reunify the country after years of crisis.

Guinea-Bissau Voters Consider Presidential Power Boost

Citizens cast ballots in a referendum on a new constitution that could shift the nation from a parliamentary to a presidential system.

Voters in Guinea-Bissau went to the polls on Sunday to decide on a new constitution. The proposed changes could transform the country's current parliamentary system into a presidential regime, significantly increasing the powers of the head of state. This referendum follows a military coup that occurred less than ten months prior.

The outcome of the vote will determine the future structure of governance in Guinea-Bissau. A shift to a presidential system would concentrate more authority in the hands of the president, altering the balance of power within the government. The military's intervention preceding this vote adds a layer of political sensitivity to the proceedings.

The referendum presents a critical juncture for Guinea-Bissau's political landscape. The decision by the electorate will shape the nation's governance framework for years to come, potentially ushering in an era of stronger executive leadership. The context of the recent coup underscores the significance of this constitutional review.

Why it mattersThe referendum on constitutional changes that could increase presidential powers directly affects the political stability and governance structure of Guinea-Bissau.

Mozambique Economy Grows 1.7% as Manufacturing Sector Contracts

The national economy expanded by 1.7% in the second quarter, driven by services, while industrial activity declined significantly.

Mozambique's economy continued its positive trajectory, growing by 1.7% in the second quarter of this year. This expansion, which spans from April to June, extends the recovery trend that began at the end of 2025. The services sector showed advancement, contributing to the overall growth, but this was offset by a notable downturn in industrial activities.

Among the industrial sectors, manufacturing registered one of the most significant contractions. This decline in manufacturing activity presents a mixed picture for the national economic performance during the period. The data indicates a divergence between the services and industrial components of the economy.

The overall economic growth demonstrates resilience, maintaining positive territory for the second consecutive quarter. However, the sharp decline in the manufacturing industry signals potential challenges within the production sector that warrant further attention.

Why it mattersThe national economy grew by 1.7% in the second quarter, with the services sector advancing while manufacturing declined.
