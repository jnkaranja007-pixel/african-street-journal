Mozambique finance minister meets private sector leaders

Carla Loveira discussed economic policy and growth challenges with business and financial representatives in Maputo.

Mozambique's Finance Minister Carla Loveira met with private sector leaders on Friday, October 30, in Maputo. The meeting, organised by the Confederation of Economic Associations of Mozambique (CTA), focused on economic policy challenges and stimulating economic activity.

Minister Loveira highlighted the importance of trust between the state and businesses, noting that public finances and the private sector are interdependent for national development. Mozambique's economy grew by 1.7% in the second quarter of 2026, with inflation falling to around 6% between August and September.

Revenues from oil and gas projects reached US$334.35 million. The government is implementing a Fiscal Consolidation Plan and has introduced measures like structuring the Development Bank of Mozambique and preparing a VAT Refund Strategy to improve company liquidity.

Why it mattersBusiness leaders and financial institutions in Mozambique are affected by discussions on economic policy and new strategies aimed at improving company liquidity and stimulating business activity.

Mozambique champions get Gender training kits

Coastal communities in Moma, Ilha de Moçambique and Mossuril receive bicycles, smartphones and manuals.

Gender Action Learning System (GALS) champions in Mozambique's Moma, Ilha de Moçambique, and Mossuril districts received essential kits last week. The training aims to equip them with socially accepted tools for their families. The kits included bicycles, backpacks, T-shirts, caps, smartphones, GALS manuals, notebooks, and pens to help them continue disseminating GALS in their communities.

District officials, including Moma District Permanent Secretary Albino Mucuna and Ilha de Moçambique Administrator Josina Taipo, attended the handover ceremonies. They expressed their commitment to continued collaboration with coastal communities to promote gender-based practices. Champion Atija Momade Alfane spoke for the recipients, emphasizing the importance of sharing and unity within families based on GALS lessons.

Champion Age Janfar shared how GALS training transformed his life, enabling him to manage his fish sales income more effectively. He reported that he now owns a house and can provide for his family, which was not possible before. The Ministry of Agriculture, Environment, and Fisheries provided the kits.

Why it mattersCoastal community members in Mozambique are receiving training and tools to improve gender-based practices within their families and manage resources more effectively.

Maputo hospital fire prompts urgent investigation

Prime Minister Benvinda Levi calls for swift action on the blaze at the Central Hospital of Maputo's oncology services.

Mozambique's Prime Minister Benvinda Levi has called for a rapid investigation into the fire that affected the oncology department at the Central Hospital of Maputo. The Prime Minister also appealed for solidarity from Mozambicans and international partners to aid in the recovery of the affected services. The Minister of Health, Ussene Isse, stated that a multisectoral team is assessing the damage to sensitive equipment.

Minister Isse confirmed that the fire did not cause any fatalities or injuries, with all 22 patients in the oncology ward evacuated safely. 12 patients were discharged, and the remaining 10 were transferred to the Dermatology ward for continued care. A special unit has been established at the hospital to ensure cancer patients receive necessary treatment without interruption, accommodating both inpatients and outpatients.

The Minister assured that the cobalt capsule used in radiotherapy was safely removed and stored, with no risk of radioactive contamination. Several public institutions, including the National Service for Public Rescue, firefighters, and the National Atomic Energy Agency, were instrumental in preventing more severe consequences. The technical evaluation of the specialized radiotherapy equipment is ongoing, as its sensitivity to heat requires careful assessment before determining operational status.

Why it mattersCancer patients in Maputo continue to receive care through a special unit established after a fire in the oncology unit.

Mozambique bridge closed for repairs

Traffic will halt on the Natete River bridge between Ribáuè and Malema on November 5 and 6.

Traffic on the Natete River bridge connecting Ribáuè and Malema in Mozambique will be suspended for two days. The National Roads Administration (ANE) in Nampula announced the closure for November 5 and 6, from 6 a.m. to 5 p.m. daily. Damaged panels and the bridge decking will be replaced, and new safety features installed.

An alternative route is available via the Namigonha junction, passing through Iapala, and rejoining the N13 road near the Thi Thi River bridge. ANE is appealing to road users for cooperation and urges strict adherence to load limits, temporary signs, and official instructions to ensure safety and preserve infrastructure.

The repairs aim to maintain the integrity of the bridge and ensure the safety of all who use it. Drivers are advised to plan their journeys accordingly to avoid disruption during the scheduled work.

Why it mattersRoad users travelling between Ribáuè and Malema will face traffic disruptions and need to use an alternative route.

Mozambique plans private rail operators

State-owned CFM seeks private firms to run sections of national rail network to boost freight.

Ports and Railways of Mozambique (CFM) is preparing tenders to allow private companies to operate parts of the national rail network. This move aims to attract more freight traffic and strengthen Mozambique's role as a transit hub for neighbouring countries.

CFM, which currently holds a monopoly, will remain the infrastructure manager while private firms operate services under concession. Mozambique's railways carried 3.6 million metric tons of goods in the first quarter of 2026, an increase of 14.9% from the previous year.

The plan follows a 14-billion-metical investment programme announced in April 2025 to upgrade infrastructure, including new locomotives and freight cars. In July 2025, Mozambique secured €144 million to improve the railway linking Maputo to the South African border, aiming to increase freight capacity to 19 million metric tons annually.

Why it mattersImporters and exporters will have more options for moving goods through Mozambique's rail network.
