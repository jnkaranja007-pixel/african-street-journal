Mozambique president meets Putin in Moscow

Daniel Chapo and Vladimir Putin discussed strengthening ties and potential new agreements.

Mozambican President Daniel Chapo met with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on October 28, 2026, to discuss strengthening bilateral relations.

Putin noted that Mozambique was represented at a high level in previous Russia-Africa forums, which he believes laid a foundation for developing ties with the continent. He also mentioned that trade between the two countries nearly doubled from January to August, and an intergovernmental commission had its fourth meeting on October 1, signing a protocol to promote cooperation in areas including geology, energy, education, and agriculture.

Mozambique's Foreign Minister Maria Manuela Lucas stated that negotiations are underway for strategic agreements, with transport and economic cooperation being key areas. This engagement aims to secure economic and military benefits for Mozambique, while Russia seeks to maintain diplomatic connections amid international isolation.

Why it mattersMozambique's president met with Russia's president to discuss strengthening ties and potential new agreements.

Mozambican youth turns university rejection into shop

Margarida Pirilão opened a baking supply store near Maputo after failing to get into university.

Margarida Pirilão, 19, opened a shop selling baking supplies on the outskirts of Maputo after being rejected from university. The Portuguese organisation Helpo supported her venture in the Possulane neighbourhood of Marracuene, about 30 kilometres from the capital.

Pirilão aims to achieve her dream of being the first university graduate in her family, a goal postponed when she did not gain admission after finishing 12th grade in 2024. She recalled the sadness of seeing friends admitted while she remained home, fearing a wasted year.

Thousands of young Mozambicans face similar challenges annually due to limited places at public universities, often leaving them without studies or work for extended periods.

Why it mattersYoung Mozambicans are unable to pursue higher education due to limited university places.

Mozambique ruby auction raises $214,000

The first domestic ruby sale attracted 12 companies and concluded with four bids at Nacala International Airport.

Mozambique's first domestic ruby auction generated just over $214,000 from the sale of two rough ruby lots. The event, held at Nacala International Airport under police guard, saw 12 companies inspect the stones, with four submitting bids. Zambrock Mining and Exploration, owned by Belmiro Mize Lampiao, secured the two ruby lots after their bids surpassed initial offers.

Two corundum lots failed to sell as bids fell below their reserve prices and will be offered again at a later date. This auction, though modest compared to international sales, demonstrated Mozambique's capability to host such events with necessary security and procedures. Ethiopian Airlines is set to begin serving Nacala three times weekly from tomorrow, aiming to connect buyers to the region.

The auction occurred during the CAVAME-d conference, which the government intends to develop into an annual Mozambique Mining and Energy Week. The conference announced $4.2bn in investment, which will be assessed based on completed transactions. Next year's gathering will track the progress of these proposals.

Why it mattersThe auction directly affects Mozambican mining companies and the local economy through business transactions.

US boosts investment firepower to $205 billion

Washington targets African minerals, including Mozambique's graphite, to challenge China's supply chain dominance.

The United States International Development Finance Corporation (DFC) can now support up to $205 billion in global investments, with plans to increase annual deployment to between $25 billion and $35 billion next year.

The DFC has already committed over $14 billion across Africa, with more than $3 billion specifically linked to critical minerals and related infrastructure, as Washington seeks alternatives to Chinese-controlled supply chains.

DFC Head of Investments Conor Coleman stated the agency has completed $6 billion in transactions, approved another $13 billion, and has a global pipeline valued at approximately $100 billion.

Why it mattersMozambique's graphite is targeted for US investment as Washington seeks to challenge China's supply chain dominance.

Mozambique monitors anti-money laundering measures

Government reviews actions taken since removal from FATF grey list in October 2025.

Mozambique's Council of Ministers is monitoring anti-money laundering measures to maintain progress after its removal from the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) grey list in October 2025. Inocêncio Impissa, spokesperson for the Council of Ministers, stated that this monitoring ensures compliance with FATF recommendations.

The review assesses actions taken by the country, including indicators on cases submitted to the Office of the Attorney-General, those tried, and pending proceedings. Impissa announced that the report detailing these efforts will be published on the Ministry of Finance’s website for public access.

The report allows citizens to consult the results and the specific measures implemented by the government. This initiative aims to demonstrate continued commitment to financial transparency following the FATF's decision last year.

Why it mattersMozambique's government is monitoring anti-money laundering measures to maintain its financial standing.
