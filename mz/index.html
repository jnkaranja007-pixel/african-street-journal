Mondlane Trial Puts Government in Political Bind

A conviction could silence Mozambique's leading opposition figure, but the trial may also amplify his message and expose government actions.

Venâncio Mondlane's trial presents Mozambique's government with a difficult political choice. A conviction could sideline the country's most effective opposition politician, but the proceedings might also provide Mondlane with a national stage to criticize the disputed 2024 elections and government actions.

Both Mondlane and his legal team have requested the trial be broadcast live, viewing it as an opportunity to scrutinize the election process, police conduct, and the government's treatment of opponents. The charges, including "incitement to terrorism," carry potential sentences exceeding twenty years, raising questions about the necessity of Council of State authorization for proceedings.

The Supreme Court faces a test in impartially assessing this politically charged case, requiring the prosecution to prove each charge with evidence. The post-election unrest stemmed from widespread frustration over election results and the state's response, not solely from Mondlane's calls for demonstrations.

Why it mattersThe trial of Venâncio Mondlane affects the political landscape by potentially removing a key opposition figure or providing him a platform to challenge the government.

Malnutrition Kills Children in Nampula Province

Thirteen children died from acute malnutrition in the first six months of the year in Nampula.

Acute malnutrition continues to claim lives in Nampula province. In the first six months of the year, thirteen children died from the condition, out of more than sixteen thousand diagnosed. Poor nutrition is identified as the underlying cause of these deaths.

The pediatric ward at the Central Hospital of Nampula frequently experiences overcrowding during peak periods, indicating a significant burden of cases. The consistent rise in malnutrition diagnoses points to ongoing challenges in ensuring adequate nutrient intake for the province's youngest residents.

The situation highlights a persistent public health crisis in the region, with the number of affected children underscoring the scale of the problem. Efforts to address the root causes of poor nutrition are crucial to reducing child mortality in Nampula.

Why it mattersThirteen children died from acute malnutrition in Nampula province, affecting the health and survival of young lives.

President Revokes Central Bank Governor Appointment

Waldemar de Sousa's appointment as governor of the Bank of Mozambique was revoked less than 24 hours after being named, following public concerns.

President Daniel Chapo revoked the appointment of Waldemar de Sousa as governor of the Bank of Mozambique today, reversing a decision made less than a day prior. The Presidency cited "supervening issues" for the reversal, but did not confirm if it was related to a reported 2020 criminal case concerning administrative authorisations linked to hidden debts. Activist Adriano Nuvunga publicly raised the case shortly before the appointment was revoked, leading to public speculation.

The timing of the revocation, coupled with the public resurfacing of the 2020 case, invites the conclusion that the criminal investigation was the cause. However, this remains an inference as the Presidency has not officially confirmed it. The accusation was reportedly public knowledge six years prior, raising questions about the vetting process for high-level state appointments and the role of security services in reviewing potential appointees' records.

If the security services vetted Sousa, they should have identified the unresolved case and reported it, making the subsequent appointment questionable. Conversely, if they failed to discover information readily available in the public record, it indicates a serious lapse in their due diligence. This situation raises concerns about the integrity checks applied to presidential appointments, particularly for sensitive positions like the central bank governor.

Why it mattersInvestors may be concerned about the stability and integrity of leadership at the Bank of Mozambique following the rapid reversal of a key appointment.

Anamola Demands Fair Trial for Leader Venâncio Mondlane

The National Alliance for a Free and Autonomous Mozambique calls for an independent and transparent judicial process for its leader facing five criminal charges.

Anamola is demanding an independent, impartial, and transparent trial for its leader, former presidential candidate Venâncio Mondlane. The Supreme Court of Mozambique has summoned Mondlane to face five criminal charges stemming from post-election protests. The demonstrations, called by Mondlane to contest alleged fraud in the October 2024 general elections, began peacefully but escalated into riots. Mondlane is accused of public incitement to crime, collective disobedience, and terrorism-related offenses.

The Council of State, an advisory body to the Mozambican President, recently decided to lift Mondlane's parliamentary immunity to allow his trial. Mondlane holds a seat on the Council of State due to his position as the runner-up in the last presidential election. Anamola spokesperson Manuela Veloso stated that the trial must strictly adhere to the Constitution and national laws, emphasizing that respect for institutions does not equate to blind trust and defending peace should not mean accepting injustice.

Anamola rejects claims that Mondlane incited violence, attributing the unrest to police actions. According to the NGO "Decide" Platform, police fatally shot approximately 400 people and injured 619 during five months of disturbances starting October 21, 2024. While the Attorney General's Office initiated 31 legal cases against police officers, no convictions have been secured. Anamola also requested the trial be broadcast live on television and social media.

Why it mattersVenâncio Mondlane, a former presidential candidate and leader of Anamola, faces criminal charges, and his party is advocating for a fair trial, which could impact political stability.

EU Names New Ambassador to Mozambique

Francisco Fontán Pardo assumes the role with a focus on cooperation, peace, and stability.

The European Union has appointed Francisco Fontán Pardo as its new ambassador to Mozambique, succeeding Antonino Maggiore. Pardo officially takes up his post on Tuesday, with his mandate prioritizing the consolidation of cooperation and efforts to maintain peace in the country. The appointment was made by the EU's High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, Kaja Kallas.

In his new role, Pardo has pledged to make peace and the development of Mozambique key priorities. He stated his commitment to strengthening the relationship between the EU and Mozambique, advancing common goals for sustainable development, peace consolidation, stability, and shared prosperity. Pardo will present his credentials to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Cooperation in the coming days.

Pardo brings extensive diplomatic experience from the European External Action Service, having previously served as Deputy Head of the EU Delegation in Brazil and Chief of Staff for a former High Representative. He also served as the EU Ambassador to ASEAN. The EU has been a significant international partner for Mozambique, supporting its security, economic development, and institutional strengthening.

Why it mattersMozambique's peace and development are central to the new ambassador's mandate, affecting the nation's stability and economic progress.
