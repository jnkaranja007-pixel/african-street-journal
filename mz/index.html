Chapo meets Putin at Russia-Africa Summit

Mozambican President Daniel Chapo will attend the 3rd Russia-Africa Summit and meet with Vladimir Putin.

Mozambican President Daniel Chapo will travel to Russia from October 27 to 29 to attend the 3rd Russia-Africa Summit. He accepted an invitation from Russian President Vladimir Putin for the event, which aims to deepen cooperation between Russia and African nations. During the summit, leaders are expected to adopt declarations on the summit, a 2027–2029 action plan, and cooperation on peaceful artificial intelligence technologies.

On the sidelines of the summit, President Chapo is scheduled to meet with President Putin and engage with Mozambicans living in Russia and representatives of Russian companies. This visit follows Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov's July statement in Maputo, where he offered Russia's assistance to Mozambique in combating terrorism in Cabo Delgado. Lavrov stated Russia provides support including personnel training and weapons supply.

Lavrov previously met with Mozambique's Minister of Foreign Affairs and Cooperation, Maria dos Santos Lucas, in Maputo. He reaffirmed Russia's commitment to supporting Mozambique's fight against the insurgency in Cabo Delgado, which began in 2017 and has displaced over 1.1 million people. Lavrov noted that discussions focused on strengthening their strategic partnership, built on solidarity and mutual assistance during the anti-colonial struggle.

Why it mattersPresident Daniel Chapo will meet with President Vladimir Putin and engage with Mozambican communities and companies in Russia.

Mozambique boosts investment budget by 3.6 billion meticais

Government plans to restore flood-damaged infrastructure and public services using LNG revenues.

Mozambique's government will increase its public investment budget by 3.6 billion meticais, allocating priority to repairing infrastructure destroyed by recent floods. The funds will come from a carry-over balance of over 3.5 billion meticais from Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) revenues held in the Sovereign Wealth Fund's transitional account. This revision aims to strengthen the state's ability to meet urgent population needs and speed up economic and social recovery.

The State's internal resources will now rise to 446.5 billion meticais, with public expenditure increasing to 524.2 billion meticais. Public investment will grow from 100.8 billion to 104.4 billion meticais. Prime Minister Levi stated the additional resources will target roads, bridges, water systems, dikes, irrigation canals, dams, schools, and health facilities in southern provinces like Inhambane, Maputo, Gaza, and Sofala.

These interventions aim to restore public services, stimulate economic activity, particularly in agriculture, and improve road connectivity. Levi emphasized the government's commitment to ensuring natural resource revenues benefit the population directly. The plan seeks to accelerate recovery in affected areas and build national resilience for inclusive, sustainable economic growth.

Why it mattersThe Mozambican government is increasing its investment budget to repair infrastructure damaged by floods, which will affect public services and economic activity in affected provinces.

Mozambique reopens Palma fuel station by December

FUNAE expects to restore fuel supply capacity in Palma district by year-end.

Mozambique's National Fund for Energy (FUNAE) expects to reopen the Palma fuel station by December, following damage assessment and rehabilitation needs identified on Saturday, October 24.

FUNAE Chairman Mety Gondola visited Palma, Mecúfi, and Metuge districts to assess fuel station conditions, noting that Palma's station requires equipment replacement and tank restoration to improve fuel supply for vehicles and boats.

Restoring the Palma facility aims to meet the growing fuel demand from a gradual resumption of economic activity, easing pressure on the two existing stations that are struggling to meet current needs.

Why it mattersThe Palma fuel station reopening will improve fuel availability for vehicles and boats, supporting the resumption of economic activity.

Mozambique safari operator wins conservation award

Zambeze Delta Safaris received recognition at COP17 for combining trophy hunting with wildlife restoration and community development.

Zambeze Delta Safaris, a Mozambique-based company led by Mark Haldane, won a conservation award on October 22, 2026, at the U.N. Biodiversity Conference, COP17, in Yerevan, Armenia. The company integrates regulated trophy hunting with wildlife restoration, anti-poaching initiatives, and community development in a region of Mozambique previously devastated by civil war.

Haldane expressed that receiving the award was a humbling experience, stating he never imagined such recognition. The award coincides with COP17's review of global biodiversity targets, including Target 9 on sustainable wildlife management benefiting communities. Astrid Schomaker, executive secretary of the Convention on Biological Diversity, praised the win as something to celebrate.

Since Zambeze Delta Safaris began operations in Coutada 11 in 1994, African buffalo numbers have surged from approximately 1,100 to 35,000, and sable antelopes increased from 40 to 4,000. 24 lions reintroduced in 2018 have grown to about 120. A 2026 review of studies in sub-Saharan Africa found positive outcomes for wildlife or local people in nearly two-thirds of documented trophy-hunting cases.

Why it mattersMozambican wildlife populations have significantly increased due to the conservation efforts of Zambeze Delta Safaris.

STV celebrates 24 years of broadcasting in Mozambique

The television station marked its anniversary by reaffirming its dedication to providing accurate and quality information.

STV celebrated its 24th anniversary of broadcasting in Mozambique, marking over two decades of providing information, entertainment, and coverage of the nation's diverse realities. The anniversary event served as an occasion to reflect on the channel's journey and acknowledge the contributions of its staff.

During the celebration, STV reaffirmed its commitment to rigor, truth, and the quality of its content. The station highlighted its dedication to serving the Mozambican people through its programming.

The channel's 24-year presence signifies a long-standing commitment to informing the public and reflecting the Mozambican society.

Why it mattersSTV's 24th anniversary celebration reaffirms its commitment to providing information to Mozambicans.
