Gorongosa National Park cuts 212 jobs

The park restructured 10 departments after losing funding from the US Agency for International Development.

Gorongosa National Park has eliminated 212 jobs across 10 departments due to financial difficulties. The job cuts follow the loss of funding from the US Agency for International Development (USAID).

Sources familiar with the restructuring spoke to Zitamar News on condition of anonymity, citing fear of repercussions. The park has undergone a major restructuring impacting its operations.

The park's financial difficulties have led to these significant staff reductions. The loss of USAID funding directly contributed to the decision to cut jobs.

Why it matters212 employees of Gorongosa National Park lost their jobs.

Mozambique prison service on high alert for Mondlane trial

The state is preparing for unrest ahead of Venâncio Mondlane's trial, despite his claims he will not incite supporters.

Mozambique’s prison service, SERNAP, is escalating to its highest combat readiness level across all facilities starting November 16, the day before Venâncio Mondlane's trial begins. This heightened security measure is being implemented because the trial is treated as a potential national security crisis. SERNAP is redeploying guards from administrative roles, reinforcing external patrols, and coordinating with police and military forces, particularly in prisons that experienced incursions and escapes during the 2024 unrest.

Despite the state's preparations for potential public unrest, Mondlane stated in an October 18 interview that he would not call his supporters to the streets if convicted. The Council of State suspended Mondlane’s immunity on June 10 to allow the criminal case to proceed at the Supreme Court, a decision Mondlane contests as null. Amnesty International reported reckless force and mass arrests during the post-election crackdown in April 2025, with the civil society platform DECIDE counting around 315 deaths between October 2024 and January 2025.

The directive for increased prison security emphasizes proportionality and respect for human rights, though past actions by security forces have been criticized. Frelimo, the ruling party, stands to gain from the weakening of its prominent opponent, making the court's independence a significant factor in the proceedings.

Why it mattersVenâncio Mondlane faces a criminal case, and the state is preparing for public unrest around his trial.

Renamo expels former MP António Muchanga

The opposition party's National Council voted on Thursday to expel the vocal critic of leader Ossufo Momade.

Mozambique's main opposition party, Renamo, expelled former Member of Parliament António Muchanga on Thursday. The party's National Council made the decision in Maputo, citing serious disciplinary offences under article 15(f) of its statutes. Renamo spokesman Marcial Macome stated that a disciplinary process preceded the expulsion.

Muchanga, who has publicly called for leader Ossufo Momade's resignation over the past year, was a recognisable critic. His expulsion follows a pattern where Renamo chooses exclusion over accommodating competing views within its ranks. The party leadership presented the decision as protection for the institution.

This move may make meetings easier for Ossufo Momade but weakens Renamo's ability to unite its factions. The party lost voters in 2024 and needs to win them back. Muchanga's public profile gave a recognisable face to internal grievances, which remain unanswered by his removal.

Why it mattersRenamo, a major political party in Mozambique, expelled a former MP, affecting its internal unity and electoral prospects.

BNI sues former minister over business debt

Banco Nacional de Investimento seeks to recover funds lent to Topa Internacional, Lda., owned by ex-minister Ragendra Berta de Sousa.

The Banco Nacional de Investimento (BNI) is pursuing legal action against former Minister of Industry and Commerce, Ragendra Berta de Sousa, for an unpaid business loan. The debt was incurred by his company, Topa Internacional, Lda., based in Machava, Maputo province. A court notice from the Commercial Section of the Maputo City Judicial Court has been published, initiating a 20-day public announcement period for unknown creditors.

The court is calling on creditors of Topa Internacional, Lda., Ragendra Berta de Sousa, and Seouk-Kyu Chun to come forward within 10 days to claim the executive action case files. The BNI is the plaintiff in this case, seeking payment of a certain amount. The loan was specifically for financing the acquisition of movable goods, described as flexible packaging industry equipment.

Ragendra de Sousa led the sector between 2017 and 2020. During his tenure, he famously promised to resign if the Companhia de Búzi, located in Sofala province, was not rehabilitated, a commitment made in April 2018. The factory remains abandoned to date. The exact debt amount and the year of the loan are not disclosed in the public announcement.

Why it mattersFormer minister Ragendra Berta de Sousa faces legal action from BNI over a business debt owed by his company, Topa Internacional, Lda.
