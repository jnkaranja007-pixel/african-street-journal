Mozambique renews Kenmare titanium mine concession until 2044

The Council of Ministers approved the extension for the Moma mine, which supplies 6% of global titanium feedstocks.

Mozambique's Council of Ministers approved the extension of rights and concessions for Kenmare Resources' Moma heavy mineral sands mine in Nampula province until 2044. The approval, granted on October 13 and officially notified on October 29, includes changes to the tax, commercial, and investment regimes.

The Moma mine, operated by Kenmare since 2007, accounts for approximately 6% of the world's titanium feedstocks, supplying industries such as paint and plastics in over 15 countries. Managing director Tom Hickey stated the changes aim for a balance between Kenmare's interests and a greater contribution to Mozambique.

Royalties on gross revenues will increase progressively from 1% to 5% by 2044. Kenmare Resources had been negotiating the renewal since its initial 20-year authorization expired in December 2024, having previously threatened international arbitration over fiscal terms.

Why it mattersMozambican government approval of the Moma mine's concession renewal until 2044 affects the country's mining sector and its contribution to global titanium supply.

Companhia Industrial da Matola challenges import rules

The Cereals Institute seized 3,300 tonnes of wheat and blocked imports after the company filed a court challenge.

Companhia Industrial da Matola (CIM) is challenging import regulations set by the Cereals Institute (ICM) in court. On October 12, CIM served its challenge to the economy minister and ICM, leading ICM to announce the suspension of CIM’s imports and seize 3,300 tonnes of wheat at the Port of Matola the next day. ICM also instructed commercial banks, with a copy to the Bank of Mozambique, to reject CIM’s court certificate for future transactions.

Jurists consulted by Zitamar consider ICM’s actions unlawful, stating that the service of the challenge should have provisionally suspended further enforcement against CIM. ICM claims the Economy Ministry invoked an exception for serious urgency in the public interest, but Zitamar has not seen this declaration or any subsequent court ruling. The director-general of ICM, Luís José Jobe Fazenda, signed the directive to banks.

The import charge rose from $5 to $16 per tonne, potentially yielding substantial sums. Between May and mid-October, ICM recorded approximately 615,000 tonnes of rice and wheat imports. One importer reported paying $2m on its wheat imports in the previous month alone. Jurists are questioning the legality of the charge and the holding of its proceeds.

Why it mattersCompanhia Industrial da Matola faces blocked imports and seized goods due to a legal challenge against import regulations.

Mozambique and China advance cassava value chain

A meeting in Shenzhen mapped implementation of a memorandum of understanding signed in Maputo to boost cultivation, processing, and commercialization.

Mozambique and China have entered a new phase of cassava value chain development following a meeting in Shenzhen between the Mozambique Chamber of Commerce (CCMI) and Chinese partners. This follows a Memorandum of Understanding signed in Maputo on October 1.

The initiative, attended by CCMI Board Chairperson Thani Cabir and Executive Director Fileu Pave, will initially target the Gaza and Inhambane provinces. Local cooperatives, business associations, and the Longrun Group will engage in cassava cultivation, processing, and commercialization, with Longrun Group facilitating technology transfer and employment.

China annually imports four million tonnes of cassava chips, eight million tonnes of starch, 1.5 million tonnes of unprocessed cassava, and over 50,000 tonnes of flour, presenting a significant export opportunity for Mozambique. This sustained demand aims to diversify agricultural exports, boost domestic value addition, and uplift rural smallholders and agro-industrial SMEs.

Why it mattersMozambican farmers and small and medium-sized enterprises in Gaza and Inhambane provinces will benefit from increased opportunities in cassava cultivation, processing, and commercialization due to Chinese demand.

Mozambican Banks hold lending rate at 15.5%

The Association of Mozambican Banks kept its benchmark lending rate unchanged for the seventh month in a row.

The Association of Mozambican Banks (AMB) decided on October 30 to maintain its benchmark lending rate at 15.5%. This decision follows the central bank's move to keep its MIMO benchmark policy rate at 9.25% since January.

This 'prime rate' has decreased since January 2024, after being at a record high of 24.1% for six months. The AMB previously cut the rate by 10 basis points in January, February, March, and April. The rate has remained stable through October and November.

Bank of Mozambique Governor Felisberto Navalha stated on September 30 that maintaining prudence was necessary to avoid future irresponsibility. The Monetary Policy Committee's decision to hold the MIMO rate unchanged at 9.25% was influenced by economic risks and the conflict in the Middle East.

Why it mattersMozambican banks have held their benchmark lending rate steady, impacting the cost of borrowing for businesses and individuals.
