Nampula court suspends Praça do Metical construction

Civil society groups opposed the project's use of public parkland, prompting a legal review.

A court in Nampula has suspended construction work on Praça do Metical. The suspension came after civil society organisations opposed the project's location in the Jardim-Parque Popular. These groups argued that public spaces should remain accessible for residents' enjoyment.

The court's decision aims to halt work while it examines the project's compliance with urban space regulations. This review seeks to ensure the restoration of legality and the safeguarding of public interest. The construction had faced opposition shortly after the foundation stone was laid.

The legal examination will focus on the occupation and use of urban spaces. The court's ruling prevents further work until these issues are resolved.

Why it mattersThe construction of Praça do Metical in Nampula has been halted by a court order due to public opposition over the use of a park.

Mozambique forces face mobile insurgent tactics

Brigadier General Sérgio Estrela reported 154 attacks in five months across 16 districts, including Niassa.

Mozambican Defence Armed Forces (FADM) are confronting insurgents who have become more mobile and adaptable in their tactics, according to Brigadier General Sérgio Estrela, Mission Force Commander for EUMAM MOZ. An analysis presented highlighted the geographical spread of attacks, noting incursions into areas south and west of Cabo Delgado Province.

Data shared for the preceding five months recorded 154 attacks, primarily concentrated in Cabo Delgado but also extending into 15 districts there and one in Niassa Province, covering 120 localities. General Estrela also pointed to an increase in kidnappings for ransom as a tactic used by these groups.

The European Union Military Assistance Mission in Mozambique (EUMAM MOZ) is supporting the FADM's development of autonomy and operational capabilities. This partnership aims to bolster the forces' effectiveness in responding to national and regional security challenges and contributing to peace efforts.

Why it mattersThe FADM faces evolving security challenges due to mobile insurgent groups and their changing tactics.

Politics · 2026-10-30

EU-trained units struggle against Mozambique insurgents

Insurgents conducted 154 attacks in five months across 16 districts, while specialised forces are misused.

Mozambique has created 11 rapid-reaction units with European Union assistance to combat insurgents in Cabo Delgado and Niassa. The EU trained soldiers, supplied equipment, and is helping regenerate units between deployments. However, insurgents continue to operate across an expanding area, with specialised forces often used in ways that undermine their training.

Brigadier-General Sérgio Estrela, commander of the EU Military Assistance Mission, stated that the FADM are hindered by excessive command centralisation, intelligence weaknesses, and shortages of equipment and money. He noted that insurgents conducted approximately 154 attacks over the previous five months, spread across about 120 locations in 16 districts. Abductions, used for ransom and recruits, rose from 29% of attacks in the first half of the year to 56% in the most recent quarter.

Estrela explained that this type of enemy requires mobile units capable of receiving intelligence, deploying quickly, striking, and withdrawing. Instead, some rapid-reaction forces have reportedly remained in static positions for months, performing garrison duties. These issues are not resolvable by further training courses.

Why it mattersMozambican soldiers trained by the EU are not effectively countering insurgents due to systemic issues like command and equipment shortages.

Mozambique invites Russian firms to invest

President Daniel Chapo seeks Russian investment in agriculture, industry, and energy sectors for economic diversification.

Mozambican President Daniel Chapo has invited Russian companies and investors to explore opportunities in Mozambique, emphasizing mutual benefit. Speaking at the Russia-Africa Summit, Chapo stated the country aims to build a diversified economy that generates jobs, particularly for its young and female population.

Chapo highlighted mechanised agriculture, industry, infrastructure, transport, logistics, energy, tourism, digital transformation, and the blue economy as priority areas for Russian cooperation. He also advocated for stronger collaboration in education, technical training, health, and scientific research, aiming for investments that create lasting national capacity.

The Mozambican leader also called for enhanced international cooperation with Russia to combat terrorism, organised crime, money laundering, and cyber threats. He stressed that the summit's action plan should have clear priorities and effective monitoring to ensure consistent cooperation between Russia and African nations.

Why it mattersMozambican President Daniel Chapo is seeking foreign investment from Russian companies to develop key economic sectors and create jobs.

Mozambique economic growth fails to cut poverty, says central bank

The governor of the Bank of Mozambique warned that public debt and limited credit hinder improvements in living conditions.

Mozambique's economic growth remains insufficient to improve the population's living conditions, according to the Governor of the Bank of Mozambique, Felisberto Navalha. He spoke on Wednesday in Tete, highlighting the context of rising public debt, difficulties in accessing foreign currency, and a weak expansion of credit to the economy.

Navalha stated that the current economic trajectory does not adequately address poverty reduction. The central bank's assessment comes as the country faces challenges in its financial landscape. The 51st Council of the Bank of Mozambique was opened by Navalha in Tete.

The governor's remarks underscore ongoing concerns about the nation's economic performance and its impact on citizens. Further details on the council's discussions and potential policy responses were not immediately available in this report.

Why it mattersMozambicans are directly affected by insufficient economic growth which fails to improve their living conditions.
