Mozambique to Import 800 Bulls to Modernize Livestock Sector

The Minister of Agriculture, Environment and Fisheries announced the import plan at the FACIM trade fair to enhance national cattle breeds.

Mozambique's Minister of Agriculture, Environment and Fisheries announced plans to import 800 bulls at the 61st FACIM trade fair. The initiative aims to modernize the national livestock sector and improve cattle breeds. The announcement was made during the agricultural exhibition, a key highlight of the fair which concludes this Sunday.

The import of these bulls is intended to upgrade the genetic quality of the country's livestock. This move is part of a broader strategy to enhance agricultural productivity and the overall quality of animal husbandry within Mozambique. The specific breeds and origin of the bulls were not detailed in the announcement.

The FACIM fair, which stands for Feira Agro-Comercial e Industrial de Moçambique, serves as a major platform for showcasing agricultural and industrial advancements. The livestock exhibition, in particular, drew significant attention, with the minister's announcement being a key takeaway from this year's event.

Why it mattersFarmers will benefit from improved livestock genetics, potentially leading to higher quality meat and dairy products and increased herd productivity.

Insurgents Kill Two, Loot Mozambique Hunting Camp

Suspected Islamic State-linked militants looted and burned a camp owned by football manager Carlos Queiroz, killing two staff members.

An attack on the Mozambique Wildlife Adventures hunting camp near Marangira has resulted in the deaths of a South African conservation trainee and the camp's Mozambican head cook. Thirteen other employees remain unaccounted for following the incident. Suspected insurgents looted and burned the camp, located approximately 40km east of Marrupa and outside the Niassa Special Reserve, before fleeing with stolen goods in a vehicle. This mirrors a similar attack on the Chapungu-Kambako camp in April 2025.

The group's actions suggest a motive of acquiring supplies and transport, with indications they intended to advance further into Niassa province. This region is known for informal mining areas that could provide resources and income, and has also been a source of recruits for the insurgency. The choice of the hunting operation, owned by football manager Carlos Queiroz and hosting American clients, raises the possibility that the attackers sought international attention.

The killing of a South African national has caused alarm among safari operators, as South Africa is a key market providing clients, professional hunters, and expertise to Mozambique's hunting industry. While the American clients were safely evacuated by helicopter, the presence of foreigners at insurgent bases around Catupa suggests a potential for foreign involvement in directing attacks aimed at generating international notice.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the attack on a high-profile hunting camp with international clients and foreign staff could deter tourism and investment in Mozambique's safari industry.

Statistics Institute Admits Production Data Gaps

Mozambique's National Statistics Institute acknowledges inconsistencies and missing data in agricultural figures from 2021 to 2025.

Mozambique's National Statistics Institute (INE) has disclosed significant limitations in its agricultural production data for the period 2021-2025. The agency admits that figures for cultivated land and crop output contain gaps and inconsistencies, making year-on-year comparisons unreliable. For 2021 and 2022, estimates for cultivated land and crop production were provided by the agriculture ministry, while some 2024 figures were derived from administrative records, highlighting a lack of consistent, dedicated field surveys.

The INE deserves commendation for its transparency in revealing these statistical challenges. Such uncertainty is inherent in a country where a substantial portion of economic activity occurs in rural, informal settings that are difficult to monitor. However, this candor does not resolve the fundamental issue of policy evaluation. Without dependable baseline measurements, it becomes impossible to accurately assess the impact of agricultural policies or government targets, such as the aim to triple cotton production from 20,000 to 60,000 tonnes.

The lack of consistent measurement methods means that claims of increased production could stem from improved estimation techniques rather than actual growth in output. This ambiguity can obscure policy failures or allow success to be declared based on shifting data. Reliable statistics are crucial for effective governance and for understanding the true state of the nation's agricultural sector.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because unreliable production data hinders the ability to design, evaluate, and track the success of agricultural policies and targets.

Floods, poverty worsen child vulnerability in Mozambique

Sixteen thousand children in Gaza province face risks from floods, poverty, and cultural factors, prompting a government and partner investment.

At least sixteen thousand children in the districts of Mabalane, Chókwè, and Guijá, in Gaza province, are in vulnerable situations. Floods, poverty, and cultural practices are exacerbating these conditions, leading to early unions and school dropouts. The government, in partnership with other organizations, plans to allocate sixty-seven million meticais to implement protection measures in these three districts.

The investment aims to address the multifaceted challenges contributing to the vulnerability of these young individuals. Factors such as economic hardship and deeply rooted cultural norms are identified as significant contributors to the precarious state of child welfare in the region. The planned interventions are designed to offer a protective framework for the affected children.

This initiative seeks to mitigate the negative impacts of poverty and environmental challenges on children's lives. By focusing on protection actions, the program intends to create a safer and more supportive environment, aiming to reduce instances of premature unions and improve school attendance rates among the most at-risk youth in Mabalane, Chókwè, and Guijá.

Why it mattersSixteen thousand children in Mabalane, Chókwè, and Guijá are affected by floods, poverty, and cultural factors leading to premature unions and school abandonment.

Chapo Pledges Child Welfare, Cites Cabo Delgado Peace

Mozambique's President Daniel Chapo linked child well-being directly to ending terrorism and ensuring peace and security across the nation.

President Daniel Chapo reaffirmed the government's commitment to the welfare of Mozambican children, especially those in Cabo Delgado. He stated that ending terrorism and consolidating peace and security are essential conditions for protecting minors affected by the conflict. Chapo was speaking in Maputo during the VIII National Session of the Children's Parliament, where he addressed children's concerns about their rights and well-being.

The President identified the resolution of terrorism in Cabo Delgado as a priority to stop children's suffering and ensure their development. He noted that instability there prevents many children from having stable homes and attending school regularly. Chapo also addressed premature unions and early pregnancies, advocating for reinforced prevention efforts and societal involvement in combating these issues, stating, "We cannot normalize the abnormal."

When asked how he wished to be remembered, Chapo responded, "as a friend of the children," reiterating the government's dedication to protecting and promoting child rights. The Children's Parliament, under the theme "Child Welfare is Our Priority," also elected Neide Albertina Correia as its new president, who highlighted the importance of safe education, healthcare, adequate food, and protection from violence and exploitation for child welfare.

Why it mattersChildren in Mozambique, particularly those in Cabo Delgado, are directly affected by the ongoing conflict and societal issues like early marriage and pregnancy.
