Mozambique's interbank FX transactions surge 262.7% in Q2

The Bank of Mozambique reports a significant increase in foreign exchange activity, driven by extraordinary currency conversions and the restart of liquefied natural gas projects.

Interbank foreign exchange transaction volumes in Mozambique increased by approximately 262.74% in the second quarter of 2026 compared to the previous quarter. This surge reflects extraordinary foreign exchange conversions and the resumption of liquefied natural gas (LNG) projects, according to the Bank of Mozambique's Interbank Monetary and Foreign Exchange Markets Bulletin.

Spot foreign exchange transactions between commercial banks rose from US$4.46 million in the first quarter to US$16.16 million in the second quarter of 2026. This increase coincides with the renewed activity in the country's LNG sector, which is anticipated to be a primary driver of future foreign currency inflows into the national economy.

The resumption of the US$20 billion Mozambique LNG project, operated by TotalEnergies, in January after a four-year suspension, and the approval of a US$7.2 billion investment for the Coral Norte floating production platform, are key factors contributing to the heightened interbank activity. The Coral Sul project, operated by Eni, has been operational since 2022.

Why it mattersThe surge in interbank foreign exchange transactions indicates increased economic activity and foreign currency flows, which can affect the stability of the national currency and the cost of imported goods for all Mozambicans.

Business · 2026-08-27

EDM establishes rapid-response center for natural disasters

Electricidade de Moçambique (EDM) has created a new unit to improve its ability to respond to natural disasters, which caused €2 million in losses last rainy season.

Electricidade de Moçambique (EDM) has formed a rapid-response unit to address natural disasters, according to Joaquim Ou-Chim, speaking during the company's 49th-anniversary celebrations in Maputo. This initiative comes after the utility experienced €2 million in losses and required €8.1 million for infrastructure repairs during the last rainy season, from October to April.

The new unit prepares throughout the year to manage such situations, as climate change presents a significant challenge to Mozambique's goal of achieving universal electrification by 2030. EDM continues to work towards ensuring electricity supply to 58 administrative posts while also combating equipment vandalism and electricity theft.

EDM aims to make at least 420,000 new connections by the end of 2026, benefiting around two million people, contributing to the national electrification rate of 66.4%. In the first quarter of this year, the company connected over 75,000 new customers through the National Electricity Grid and autonomous solar and mini-hydro systems.

Why it mattersMozambican citizens will experience more reliable electricity access as the national utility improves its disaster response and expands its network.

Maputo Central Hospital records 3,014 traffic accident victims in first half

The country's largest health unit saw a slight reduction in cases and fatalities compared to the same period last year, but faces significant pressure on its services.

Maputo Central Hospital (HCM) registered 3,014 victims of traffic accidents between January and June 2026, a slight reduction from 3,124 cases in the same period last year. The hospital's spokesperson, Dino Lopes, stated that the monthly average of victims was 502 cases, down from 514 in 2025. Fatalities also decreased, with 10 deaths recorded compared to 13 in the first half of 2025.

Lopes explained that most victims who died arrived at the hospital in critical condition with multiple injuries, including long bone fractures, thoracic injuries, and traumatic brain injuries. Some patients arrived in hemorrhagic shock, losing their lives despite medical efforts. The high number of victims places significant strain on HCM's neurosurgery, orthopedics, and traumatology departments.

The hospital also noted a concern with the increase in accidents involving motorcycles, particularly among individuals aged 16 to 40. These accidents often result in fractures requiring specialized treatment and prolonged hospitalization. HCM receives patients transferred from other provinces, including Maputo and Gaza, for severe injuries, highlighting the broader impact on the healthcare system and the need for a public health approach.

Why it mattersThe high number of traffic accident victims, particularly those involving motorcycles, directly affects individuals aged 16 to 40 by causing severe injuries, prolonged hospital stays, and potential loss of income due to temporary or permanent disability.

Mozambique reviews labor law for digital platform worker protection

INTIC and the Ministry of Labour, Gender and Social Action met to update the Labour Law, focusing on safeguarding workers in app-based transport and delivery services.

Mozambique is reviewing its Labour Law to address challenges from digital technologies, particularly the growth of work through digital platforms. This initiative aims to harmonize legislation with national legal instruments and public policies, focusing on services mediated by technology platforms like app-based transport and goods delivery.

Lourino Chemane from INTIC emphasized the need to protect fundamental rights for digital platform workers and to develop mechanisms for regulating the digital environment safely and transparently. He also highlighted INTIC’s experience in electronic transactions legislation as a valuable contribution to the labor law revision.

Celina Mucambe, representing the Ministry of Labour, Gender and Social Action, stressed the importance of classifying employment relationships, identifying responsible entities for digital platforms, and creating dispute resolution mechanisms. She called for a technical and multidisciplinary approach to inspect and regulate these activities, ensuring transparency including algorithmic transparency.

Why it mattersDigital platform workers, including those in transport and delivery services, are directly affected as their fundamental rights and employment conditions are being considered for legal protection.

Culture · 2026-08-27

Timbila Festival opens in Quissico, showcasing Mozambican cultural heritage

The Timbila Festival in Quissico, Zavala, began with workshops on instrument construction, music, and dance, aiming to transmit traditional knowledge across generations.

The Timbila Festival commenced in Quissico, Zavala, featuring workshops led by local craftspeople on the construction and repair of timbilas. Participants engaged with traditional knowledge and techniques for making and maintaining this emblematic Mozambican instrument. Renowned timbila musicians and dancers also led sessions on music and dance techniques, covering playing methods, historical compositions, and body movement coordination.

These activities aim to create spaces for intergenerational knowledge transmission, bringing together masters, artists, young people, and communities interested in preserving this cultural expression. In the afternoon, from two o'clock to five thirty, training activities focused on sharing knowledge and experiences centered on the timbila.

The evening included a community dinner showcasing local cuisine, designed to highlight the community’s flavors and culinary practices while fostering informal socialization. The day concluded with a campfire discussion involving community leaders, academics, artists, and timbila knowledge holders, addressing the challenges of preserving, transmitting, and promoting this heritage among younger generations.

Why it mattersThe festival directly affects local craftspeople and musicians by providing a platform to share their skills and knowledge, ensuring the continuation of their cultural practices.
