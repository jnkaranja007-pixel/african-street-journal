DP World plans to raise Mozambique cargo capacity to 650 mil TEU

The company told President Daniel Chapo it wants to lift handling capacity from 225 mil to approximately 650 mil TEU by the second quarter of 2027.

DP World plans to increase its cargo-handling capacity in Mozambique from 225 mil to approximately 650 mil TEU by the second quarter of 2027. The company presented the plan to President Daniel Chapo at a meeting in Maputo on Tuesday.

The proposed expansion would raise capacity by more than doubling it, according to the figures presented to Chapo. The report identifies the meeting’s location and participants but gives no further details about the investment or how the additional capacity would be delivered.

The meeting also addressed another subject, but no details were provided. The capacity target is scheduled for the second quarter of 2027.

Mozambique judge halts Mondlane trial over late 223-page defence filing

The Supreme Court hearing was paused about an hour early after the defence asked to distribute copies of its written response to other parties.

A judge halted the first session of politician Venâncio Mondlane’s trial at Mozambique’s Supreme Court on Tuesday, about an hour before its scheduled end, after his defence presented a 223-page written challenge. The lawyers asked to give copies to the other parties the following day.

Judge Luís Mondlane questioned why a document of that length had not been provided earlier, noting that the prosecution’s case had been filed months ago. The defence said it did not have copies available at the hearing. The judge said lawyers had a responsibility to submit such a filing in advance, then set the hearing to resume Wednesday at 10:00.

Before the pause, prosecutors read 107 points across 44 pages of charges. Mondlane faces five alleged crimes, including incitement to terrorism; prosecutors link his public calls for strikes and protests to violence and damage during the post-election period. The defence disputes the timing of a separate claim by road concessionaire Revimo, which alleges losses from damaged toll booths and unpaid road use.

Why it mattersMondlane’s defence is due to present its 223-page challenge at the resumed hearing, while prosecutors have charged him with five crimes.

Mozambique police urge calm as Mondlane’s Supreme Court trial opens

The former presidential candidate faces five charges linked to protests after the 2024 election, while his ANAMOLA party demands a fair and independent process.

Mozambique’s Supreme Court began the trial of Venâncio António Bila Mondlane, a former presidential candidate and leader of the ANAMOLA party, at 10 a.m. on Tuesday. He faces five charges following protests that began after the October 2024 election and continued until January 2025.

The Republic of Mozambique Police (PRM) called on citizens to remain calm and avoid actions that threaten public order or people and property, saying it would act preventively and proportionately. Police and armoured vehicles were deployed across Maputo, with water-cannon vehicles stationed at city access points and some peripheral neighbourhoods.

ANAMOLA said the trial must respect the presumption of innocence and Mondlane’s right to a defence, and be free of political pressure. The protests killed more than 350 people, mostly at the hands of police, according to the report; no police officer has been tried over those deaths.

Why it mattersMondlane faces five charges, while ANAMOLA is pressing for his right to a defence and a trial free of political pressure.

Prosecution bases Mondlane case on Facebook livestreams

The Supreme Court trial centres on broadcasts he made during Mozambique’s 2024 post-election demonstrations.

Mozambique’s Public Prosecution has based its case against Venâncio Mondlane on Facebook livestreams he made during the 2024 post-election demonstrations. The case is being heard in a trial under way at the Supreme Court.

The prosecution attributes direct responsibility to Mondlane for mobilising the protests and for the violence, deaths and destruction. The livestreams are the basis cited for its case against him.

The trial is under way at the Supreme Court. The available report gives no ruling or further date for proceedings.

Why it mattersMondlane faces the Supreme Court over allegations linking his livestreams to protest mobilisation, deaths and destruction.

Guro opens 25 de Junho primary school and a supermarket

Mayor Latifo Vinho opened both facilities, with the school costing 5.8 million meticais to put up.

Mayor Latifo Vinho has inaugurated a new primary school and a supermarket in the municipality of Guro, Mozambique, adding both to its local infrastructure. The moves are being presented as the delivery of campaign pledges the mayor made just over two years ago.

The 25 de Junho Primary School was raised from the ground up for 5.8 million meticais, according to Club of Mozambique. The report names the supermarket as the second new facility but gives no cost for it.

The report does not give the school's student capacity, an opening date or any further details about the supermarket.
