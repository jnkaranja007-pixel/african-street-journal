Hollard Moçambique unifies insurance operations

The company now operates as a single entity, aiming to expand financial protection to more Mozambicans.

Hollard Moçambique has completed its merger of Hollard Seguros, Hollard Vida, and Global Alliance, consolidating operations under one brand. This unification allows the insurer to offer all non-life and life insurance branches, alongside pension fund management services, under a single identity.

The company's CEO, Henri Mittermayer, stated the ambition is to strengthen its presence and extend insurance protection to more Mozambicans. Hollard Moçambique plans to develop inclusive solutions, including agricultural insurance, education plans, and digital motor liability insurance, to reach individuals and small businesses with limited financial protection.

Hollard Moçambique will combine local market knowledge with an international network, supported by Tokio Marine. The strategy includes enhancing customer experience and investing in digital innovation to improve efficiency and claims processes, aiming to increase insurance sector participation in Mozambique.

Why it mattersMozambicans will have broader access to insurance products and financial services following the merger.

Mozambique bridge collapses again after repairs

A temporary Revuè River bridge in Manica province failed six days after reopening, disrupting traffic.

President Daniel Chapo announced plans for over $7.5bn in investments for Mozambique's Maputo, Beira, and Nacala transport corridors on Wednesday. The same day, a temporary metal bridge over the Revuè River in Manica province collapsed again. The bridge, on the road between Chimoio and Sussundenga, failed after a truck carrying sand or stones crossed it, disrupting traffic for the second time in a week.

The bridge had reopened only six days prior to the collapse. It is unclear if the truck exceeded the bridge's 30-tonne weight limit. This incident highlights ongoing issues with Mozambique's infrastructure management, where temporary emergency bridges are often used long past their intended lifespan.

The N1, the country's main north-south highway, also suffers from poor conditions on many sections. A $400m World Bank rehabilitation program for the N1 has been discussed for years, but significant improvements are still needed.

Why it mattersThe collapse of a temporary bridge in Manica province disrupts traffic and highlights infrastructure management issues in Mozambique.

Evergreen Marine launches China-Maputo container service

New AEF5 route offers Gauteng importers an alternative to Durban, with a shorter rail link from Maputo.

Evergreen Marine will start a new container service connecting China to Mozambique's port of Maputo on November 25, 2026. The AEF5 service's first vessel departs Shanghai, with stops in Ningbo, Nansha, Port Klang, Dar es Salaam, and Maputo before returning via Singapore. This route provides Gauteng importers with a new option for shipments from China, aiming to bypass potential border delays.

The port of Maputo offers a rail distance of 584 kilometers to Johannesburg, shorter than Durban's 720 kilometers, according to World Bank data. However, the actual delivery time and cost depend on shipping schedules, inland transport charges, and customs clearance at the Ressano Garcia and Komatipoort border posts. Maputo Port Development Company aims for a terminal capacity of 530,000 TEUs.

The commercial benefit for Gauteng importers hinges on whether the total door-to-door transit time and cost from Maputo can compete with the Durban route. Evergreen has not yet announced the sailing frequency or available capacity for the AEF5 service. This new route complements Evergreen's existing SAF service, which already serves Durban and Cape Town.

Why it mattersMozambican port of Maputo gains a new container service from China, potentially impacting trade routes and logistics for importers in Gauteng.

Mozambique reports 154 terror attacks in five months

Insurgents targeted 16 districts in Cabo Delgado and Niassa provinces, affecting 120 communities.

More than 150 terrorist attacks occurred in northern Mozambique between June and October, with most incidents concentrated in Cabo Delgado province. Brigadier-General Sérgio Estrela, Commander of the EU Military Assistance Mission in Mozambique (EUMAM MOZ), presented these findings in Maputo, noting the geographic spread and mobility of insurgent groups. The attacks affected 16 districts, including 15 in Cabo Delgado and one in Niassa, impacting 120 localities.

Estrela highlighted that insurgent tactics now include kidnappings and ransom demands, making monitoring important for effective responses. Local communities report limited presence of Mozambican Defence Armed Forces (FADM) in areas of Muidumbe and Mueda, allowing insurgents greater freedom of movement. The Islamic State of Mozambique claimed responsibility on November 1 for killing 14 people in Muidumbe and Mueda districts the previous week.

An investigator from the Institute of Security Studies noted that despite nine years of external training and equipment efforts for the Mozambican armed forces, concrete results on the ground remain insufficient. Communities continue to suffer from inadequate security and human rights violations.

Why it mattersOver 150 terrorist attacks in five months have affected communities in northern Mozambique, impacting security and human rights.

Mozambique plans Matola LNG terminal by 2027

A new liquefied natural gas terminal in Matola aims to secure energy supply as Pande and Temane gas fields decline.

Mozambique is preparing an alternative to the Pande and Temane gas production, which is expected to end in 2030. The country is advancing with a liquefied natural gas (GNL) terminal project in Matola, aiming for financial close by the end of 2027. The Beluluane Gas Company (BGC) announced the appointment of the VGI/PRDW consortium as Owner's Engineer for the terminal.

This appointment will help consolidate engineering projects and support the hiring of the contractor for the infrastructure's construction. The BGC stated this step is crucial for ensuring the supply of power to gas-fired plants in Chókwè, Ressano Garcia, and Maputo, which provide around 450 megawatts to Eletricidade de Moçambique (EDM). It will also supply the industrial sector in the Matola and Maputo regions.

The Pande and Temane fields, operated by Sasol, have produced 3,306.41 petajoules of gas up to October 2025. Sasol anticipates extraction will cease in 2030, though investments could extend operations to 2031. Commercial negotiations for GNL supply and financing for the Matola terminal are in their final stages, with further announcements expected soon.

Why it mattersMozambique's energy security and industrial activity are at risk due to the declining production of the Pande and Temane gas fields.
