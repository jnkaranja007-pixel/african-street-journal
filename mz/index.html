Mozambique prison service on high alert for Mondlane trial

The state is preparing for unrest on November 16, the day before Venâncio Mondlane's trial begins.

Mozambique's prison service, SERNAP, has ordered maximum combat readiness across all facilities from November 16, the day before Venâncio Mondlane's trial starts. This directive specifically addresses prisons affected by incursions and escapes during the 2024 unrest, with guards redeployed from administrative duties and external patrols strengthened. SERNAP will maintain coordination with police and military authorities to protect prisoners, staff, and neighbouring communities, while also requiring proportionality and respect for human rights.

Venâncio Mondlane stated in an interview on October 18 that he would not call his supporters to the streets if convicted. The Council of State suspended Mondlane’s immunity on June 10 to allow the criminal case to proceed at the Supreme Court. Mondlane argues this decision is null, as the Council can suspend a member for criminal proceedings but cannot lift immunity.

Amnesty International's April 2025 report, Protest Under Attack, documented reckless and unlawful force, mass arbitrary arrests, and deliberate restrictions on information during the post-election crackdown. The civil society platform DECIDE counted around 315 deaths between October 2024 and January 2025.

Why it mattersThe state is preparing for potential unrest and taking security measures around Venâncio Mondlane's trial.

Mozambique plans 415MW floating gas power plant

A new gas-fired powership will operate for three years to cover delays in building the Temane power plant.

Electricidade de Moçambique (EDM) and Karpowership are proposing a 415MW floating power plant that will run on natural gas in Maputo Bay. This project replaces a heavy-fuel-oil scheme that was blocked in 2023 due to environmental concerns. The vessel is expected to operate for an initial three years, bridging a gap while the Temane power plant is being built.

The proposed plant would be located in the Espírito Santo estuary at the Port of Maputo, in Matola, Maputo province. Environmental campaigners, including Justiça Ambiental, had opposed the earlier heavy fuel oil plan, citing Mozambique’s commitment to reduce carbon emissions. EDM expects to receive an environmental licence in the first half of 2027.

Karpowership will fund the $20m investment, aiming to recover costs through export revenues within 12 months. Construction and mobilisation are estimated to take six to eight months, but a generation start date has not yet been confirmed. The gas pipeline and grid connections could potentially serve future powerships.

Why it mattersMozambique's energy supply will be supplemented by a new floating power plant while a larger project is completed.

South Africa faces inflation from high oil prices

Brent crude reached $112.55 per barrel, increasing pressure on the South African economy.

South Africa's economy is under pressure as international oil prices rise, with the government seeking ways to lower fuel costs and protect purchasing power. The rand weakened against the dollar on Thursday amid Middle East tensions and a review of fiscal measures.

The price of Brent crude oil reached $112.55 per barrel, contributing to the economic strain. This situation is forcing the government to consider interventions to mitigate the impact on consumers.

The government is evaluating fiscal measures to address the rising cost of fuel and its effect on inflation. The weakening of the rand further complicates efforts to stabilize the economy.

Why it mattersSouth Africa's economy faces increased inflation due to rising international oil prices.

Mozambique discusses legal cannabis production

Maputo conference considers commercial and medicinal uses, while small growers' status remains unclear.

Mozambique's Attorney General’s Office hosted a national drugs conference in Maputo last week to discuss the commercial possibilities of cannabis. The meeting considered legalising controlled production, sale, and consumption, acknowledging the plant's medicinal and scientific potential. Commercial applications for pharmaceutical products and hemp fibre were noted, suggesting relevance beyond drug policy.

The conference's closing declaration discussed legalisation but did not propose a licensing system or clarify the fate of farmers growing a few plants for personal use. The document refers to recommendations in a separate final document, meaning no change in government policy is yet announced. This leaves a choice between creating a legal business for investors and leaving small growers subject to criminal law.

Across the border, South Africa’s Constitutional Court decriminalised private adult use, possession, and cultivation for personal consumption in 2018, while not authorising unrestricted commercial sales. Nova Democracia previously proposed public consultation on decriminalising consumption and legalising cultivation in Mozambique.

Why it mattersMozambique is considering legalising cannabis production, which could create new business opportunities for investors but leave small farmers facing prosecution.

Beira council evicts 200 families from land

Municipal authorities demolished homes in Muave, Sofala province, stating the land was illegally occupied and part of a protected airport zone.

At least 200 families in the Muave residential area of Beira, Sofala province, are homeless after municipal authorities demolished their homes on Wednesday. The families had allegedly built on land belonging to the Beira International Airport, a protected area.

Residents condemned the Beira Municipal Council's decision, citing a lack of prior warning and no alternative housing. Authorities stated the demolitions were necessary to enforce legislation, protect airport land, and ensure air navigation safety. The council also noted that the illegal occupation obstructed the city's land use plan.

The operation, involving municipal inspection services, the Municipal Police, and other state entities, aimed to restore legality. However, the demolition of the final construction was reportedly halted by the commander of the 13th PRM Station, impeding further work.

Why it matters200 families in Beira, Mozambique, have lost their homes due to municipal land evictions.
