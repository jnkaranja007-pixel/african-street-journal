Mozambique police urge calm as ex-presidential candidate Mondlane goes on trial

Venâncio Mondlane, who leads the ANAMOLA party, faces five charges linked to the post-election protests of late 2024 and early 2025.

Mozambique's Police Command-General has asked citizens to follow the trial of Venâncio António Bila Mondlane with "serenity", a first for the force in 51 years of the republic. The Supreme Court's criminal section was due to open the trial of the former presidential candidate at 10:00 on Tuesday, on five charges arising from protests that began in October 2024 and ran until January 2025.

In a statement issued on Monday, the police urged people to avoid any act that threatens public order or the safety of people and property, and said they would act in a "preventive and proportional" way. The capital is under a heavy security presence, with armed officers, tear gas, dogs, armoured vehicles and water tanks stationed at access points and in outlying neighbourhoods.

ANAMOLA, the party Mondlane leads, said on Sunday that the trial must follow the law, respect the presumption of innocence and be free of political interference. The protests left more than 350 people dead, most of them killed by police, according to the report, and no police officer has yet been tried for those deaths.

Why it mattersMondlane's supporters and Maputo residents face a heavy police presence during the trial, and the police say they will act against anyone who disrupts order.

Mozambique police urge calm before Mondlane trial

The trial of Venâncio Mondlane, a political figure, is due to open on Tuesday at the Supreme Court in Maputo.

Mozambican police on Monday asked the public to follow the trial of Venâncio Mondlane and to remain calm. The General Command of the Police issued a statement urging respect for public order and the legal limits on the right to protest.

The trial was due to open on Tuesday at the Supreme Court in Maputo, the country's highest court. The police statement set out the legal limits on protest and asked citizens to avoid anything that could disrupt the proceedings.

The statement did not say what measures the police would take if protests occurred during the trial.

Why it mattersProtesters and spectators planning to attend or demonstrate outside the court now face police warnings about the legal limits on protest.

Mozambique governor urges northern provinces to unite

Nampula governor Eduardo Mariamo Abdula called for the three northern provinces to develop as a single economic region.

At the first Northern Regional Forum, Nampula governor Eduardo Mariamo Abdula argued that Niassa, Cabo Delgado and Nampula should be developed together as one economic region, with the Northern Integrated Development Agency (ADIN) at its core. He pointed to Niassa's farmland, Cabo Delgado's gas and Nampula's trading networks, the port of Nacala and a railway corridor linking the region to wider markets.

Poverty is widespread across all three provinces: 65% of Nampula's population was poor in 2015, against 67% in Niassa and 53% in Cabo Delgado. The gas economy has changed little for them, since the onshore LNG project, suspended in 2021, is only now being revived, and Coral South works offshore with few ties to local businesses. Whether investment in one province can bring jobs to its neighbours is still unanswered.

Abdula complained that cotton is shipped out of Nampula unprocessed. Mozambique gins its cotton but sends the lint abroad instead of building a domestic textile sector. Nampula once led the country's cashew processing, but much of that capacity has gone, and a large share of the crop now leaves through Nacala unprocessed. A study by Eduardo Mondlane University and IIED tied that decline to the removal of export surcharges on raw nuts, a decision taken in Maputo.

Why it mattersFarmers in Nampula and Niassa face crops leaving unprocessed, so any regional plan that adds processing capacity would have to reverse a loss of local factories that began with a Maputo decision on export surcharges.
