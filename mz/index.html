Ka-Tembe seeks autonomous status amid Mocuba parliamentary move

A proposal for Ka-Tembe to become an independent municipality was presented to a national assembly deputy, seeking development parity with Mocuba.

A proposal to elevate Ka-Tembe from a Municipal District to an autonomous Municipality was put forward to National Assembly Deputy José Manteigas. The suggestion arose during a lunch meeting where discussions focused on national development and geopolitical shifts, including the planned relocation of the Assembly to Mocuba. The advocate for Ka-Tembe argued that if the area is no longer designated as the future seat of parliamentary power, it requires comparable investment to ensure local stability and economic progress.

The current administrative status leaves Ka-Tembe facing financial constraints and isolation, as it feels neglected by the Maputo Municipal Council. Becoming an independent municipality would grant Ka-Tembe the authority to retain local revenues and the political autonomy to attract, negotiate, and license direct investments in sectors such as tourism, agriculture, and fisheries. This shift aims to redirect resources towards essential local infrastructure and public services.

Deputy Manteigas, who represents the Zambezia province and hails from Mocuba, expressed enthusiasm for the potential transfer of the National Assembly to his home region, viewing it as a catalyst for development that could surpass the provincial capital, Quelimane. He reportedly listened attentively to the proposal for Ka-Tembe and agreed with the perspective presented, acknowledging the need for compensatory development measures.

Why it mattersKa-Tembe residents are affected by a potential loss of development opportunities if Mocuba becomes the new center of power, necessitating administrative changes for local economic growth.

Frelimo Congress Set for Nampula

The ruling party will hold its 13th congress in Nampula from July 20-25 next year, marking the first under Daniel Chapo's leadership.

Frelimo has scheduled its 13th congress in Nampula for July 20-25 next year, the first to occur under the leadership of Daniel Chapo. While the Central Committee meeting in Chimoio appeared routine, it signals the start of a year-long contest for party control. The congress is significant as delegates will elect a new Central Committee, which subsequently selects the Political Commission, Frelimo's primary executive body whose decisions often shape government actions.

President Chapo inherits party structures established before his tenure and lacks the established network previously commanded by figures like Armando Guebuza and Filipe Nyusi. The upcoming congress presents an opportunity for him to either consolidate power by installing loyalists in key positions or to govern with diverse, potentially competing, factions within the party. This choice will impact his administration's direction and his public image regarding openness and self-criticism.

The congress is expected to see a shift in power dynamics, with many anticipating the replacement of allies of former president Filipe Nyusi and other factions with those loyal to Chapo. This move, while altering the internal balance, is not anticipated to change the fundamental system, with expectations of maintaining existing privileges and patronage networks despite rhetoric about combating corruption.

Why it mattersThe upcoming Frelimo congress will determine the internal power structure of the ruling party, potentially influencing government decisions and the balance of political influence for the next term.

Renamo Schedules National Council Meeting for October

The party will convene in Maputo on October 21 and 22 to discuss its internal situation and the country's.

Renamo has scheduled its National Council meeting for October 21 and 22 in Maputo, following pressure from some members and former combatants. The party's spokesperson, Marcial Macome, confirmed the dates for the gathering.

The agenda for the internal meeting will focus on the current state of the party and broader national issues. This marks a significant internal political event for Renamo, bringing together key figures to deliberate on its future direction.

The decision to hold the council meeting indicates a move to address internal party dynamics and potentially reassess its role within the country's political landscape. The outcomes could influence future political discourse and party strategies.

Why it mattersRenamo members and former combatants are affected by the party's internal discussions on its situation and the country's.
