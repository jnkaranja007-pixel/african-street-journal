Maputo hospital fire destroys oncology service

Staff evacuated 22 patients safely from Maputo Central Hospital's oncology unit after a fire broke out Sunday morning.

A fire severely damaged Maputo Central Hospital’s oncology service on Sunday morning, forcing the evacuation of 22 patients. Hospital staff successfully moved all patients to safety despite smoke, flames, and reported explosions within the building. The fire destroyed much of the recently renovated facility, which provides scarce medical services.

The immediate cause of the fire remains unknown, and investigators are looking into whether automatic detectors and alarms were operational. They will also examine the electrical system's inspection status, the effectiveness of fire doors, and the availability of functioning response equipment for staff. A nurse first reported smelling smoke around 07:10.

The incident raises questions about the systems in place to detect and contain fires at the hospital. The investigation aims to establish how the fire started and assess the preventative measures that were in place.

Maputo hosts population and development conference

The Ministry of Planning and Development and UNFPA are holding a high-level meeting on November 4-5.

Mozambique's Ministry of Planning and Development (MPD), in partnership with the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA), will host a Conference on Population and Development in Mozambique on November 4 and 5. The event will take place at the Mozambique-China Cultural Centre in Maputo.

This high-level meeting aims to foster dialogue and define strategies concerning population and development within the country. Officials from the MPD and UNFPA are organizing the conference.

The conference is scheduled to run for two days, bringing together stakeholders to discuss critical issues related to population dynamics and their impact on national development.

Why it mattersThe conference in Maputo will address population and development strategies for Mozambique.

Mozambique road diversion authorised for gold mining

The National Road Administration granted permission for a diversion near Mavonde to HS Mining.

The National Road Administration (ANE) has authorised a road diversion in Manica for HS Mining to extract gold. The authorisation came from ANE’s Road Safety Department, which is part of its Central Road Maintenance and Safety Services.

The department requires the mining company to construct an alternative road of equal or better quality, ensure its maintenance, and install temporary signs. This authorisation was communicated to ANE’s Manica provincial delegation and the provincial directorates of Environment and Mineral Resources and Energy.

HS Mining is one of approximately 30 companies operating in Manica district, authorised to resume gold mining after a September 30 suspension. Similar authorisations have been granted to other mining firms in the district.

Why it mattersThe diversion of the Manica–Mavonde road will allow HS Mining to resume gold extraction, impacting local business operations.

Mozambican engineers to drill Coral North wells

Empresa Nacional de Hidrocarbonetos will use its joint venture to gain technical experience in a 10-month deep-water campaign.

Empresa Nacional de Hidrocarbonetos (ENH) will participate in drilling and completing six wells for Coral North. Through ENH Serviços and its joint venture with Saipem, Mozambican engineers and specialists will undertake this 10-month campaign. This marks a shift for ENH from its traditional role as a statutory shareholder in Mozambique’s gas projects.

ENH has stated ambitions to move beyond being a passive partner, aiming to undertake technical work and compete for contracts. The Coral North project offers an opportunity to demonstrate this change. Mozambican personnel will gain knowledge through this technically demanding deep-water drilling operation.

This experience is intended to provide ENH personnel with practical knowledge beyond what can be gained from reports or training. The company has spent decades holding state interests in petroleum projects operated by international firms.

Why it mattersMozambican engineers and specialists will gain technical capacity through direct involvement in a deep-water drilling campaign.

Health · 2026-11-04

Maputo Central Hospital resumes oncology services after fire

95 percent of patient records were saved from the blaze, allowing treatment to restart.

The Hospital Central de Maputo (HCM), Mozambique's largest health facility, has resumed oncology patient care in temporary facilities after a fire on Sunday. Clinical activity has moved to the Dermatology Service building. Hospital officials reported that 95% of oncology patient records were preserved because they were stored in the building's basement, away from the fire and water used to extinguish it.

Clinical Director Dércio Fernandes stated that all admitted patients were safely transferred without injury. 10 patients were moved to the Dermatology ward, while 12 were discharged. On Monday, the service saw 60 patients for consultations, 12 for emergencies, and 20 for outpatient chemotherapy. However, these chemotherapy patients had to wait for new records to be reconstituted as their daily treatment files were destroyed.

The hospital is working to reconstitute affected patient files to resume outpatient chemotherapy administration as soon as possible. Treatment chairs have been moved to the Dermatology Service building to continue daily care for 30 to 40 patients. Fernandes confirmed that nursing, psychology, and medical teams are mobilized to ensure patient follow-up despite the difficulties of service relocation and reorganization.

Why it mattersOncology patients in Maputo can continue their chemotherapy treatments and receive consultations and psychological support after a fire disrupted services.
