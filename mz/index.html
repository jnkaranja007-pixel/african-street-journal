Mozambique Investors Demand Reliable Infrastructure for Local Processing

Mining companies at a Nacala conference told the government that local processing of resources depends on dependable electricity and usable roads.

Mozambique's government is pushing mining and energy investors to process more resources locally and increase purchases from Mozambican businesses. However, at the CAVAME-d conference in Nacala, investors highlighted that reliable electricity and usable roads are prerequisites for such ambitions. Syrah Resources, operator of the Balama graphite mine, cited deteriorating road connections to Pemba and Nacala and continued reliance on diesel generators as significant constraints.

The company representative explained that further graphite processing would require dependable electricity, questioning the economics of adding production stages under current conditions. Nampula governor Eduardo Abdula acknowledged roads and reliable energy as priorities for industrial development, reinforcing the need for public authorities to invest, plan, and coordinate these essential services. The government's push for local content requires a reciprocal commitment to providing necessary infrastructure.

While retaining more value from Mozambique's resources through domestic processing can create jobs and expand the tax base, these benefits are contingent on competitive operations. The costs associated with unreliable power and difficult transport can undermine the economic viability of domestic processing, potentially leading to minimal investment despite contractual obligations.

Why it mattersInvestors face increased operational costs and uncertainty regarding the economic feasibility of local resource processing due to unreliable infrastructure.

Mozambican Farmer Achieves 30 Tons Per Hectare With New Techniques

Arlindo Elias Massingue adopted climate-smart agriculture practices, boosting yields and creating local jobs.

Arlindo Elias Massingue, a 47-year-old farmer in Boane, Maputo Province, has transformed his potato production to 30 tons per hectare through participation in the PRASL initiative. Previously limited by poor access to quality inputs and weak technical support, Massingue's yields were consistently low, impacting his family's food security and ability to pay school fees.

The PRASL initiative, a partnership including Solidaridad (Southern Africa) and the HEINEKEN Africa Foundation, provided Massingue with training in climate-smart agriculture, soil management, and crop protection. Access to certified seeds, fertilizers, and pesticides, along with support from service providers like Bayer, enabled him to implement modern farming techniques. He also invested in an irrigation pump and diversified his crops.

Massingue's success has created 25 seasonal jobs for community members, primarily women and youth, strengthening the local rural economy. He is now considered a model farmer, sharing his knowledge with others in his cooperative. "My life has changed: I now have a stable income, purchased my own irrigation pump, and assist other cooperative members," Massingue stated.

Why it mattersFarmers can increase yields and income by adopting new agricultural techniques and accessing better inputs.

Mozambique Justice Ministry Acknowledges Urgent Need to Protect Displaced

The government stated protecting citizens in Cabo Delgado is urgent, following a review of human rights recommendations.

Mozambique's justice ministry acknowledged the urgent need to protect citizens in Cabo Delgado, with permanent secretary Tuarique Abdala highlighting the suffering of displaced families and children on October 16. This statement came in response to questions about human rights priorities after Mozambique accepted 284 of 292 recommendations from its fourth Universal Periodic Review. While Abdala recognized the ongoing hardship, he did not outline specific actions or mechanisms to address the crisis.

The conflict in Cabo Delgado, ongoing since 2017, has resulted in over 6,600 deaths and displaced more than 1.2 million people. Between September and October alone, the International Organization for Migration recorded at least 4,445 newly displaced individuals. The government faces challenges in overcoming the insurgent threat, which it did not initiate, while also grappling with allegations of misconduct by state forces.

A key test for accountability involves the alleged killing of fishermen on March 15, where at least 13 people were reportedly killed by naval forces off the coast of Mocímboa da Praia. Human Rights Watch has gathered accounts implicating naval personnel, though the armed forces have denied responsibility, attributing the deaths to insurgents disguised in military uniforms.

Why it mattersDisplaced families and children in Cabo Delgado are suffering the consequences of the insurgency.

Albinism patients receive free dermatology care in six provinces

A specialized medical campaign offers free dermatological services to people with albinism across Mozambique.

People with albinism are receiving specialized and free medical attention as part of the VII Dermatological Expedition. This campaign, running from the 5th to the 28th of this month, is taking place in the provincial hospitals of Tete, Inhambane, and Xai-Xai, as well as the Beira General Hospital and the central hospitals of Nampula and Maputo.

The initiative is promoted by the International Laboratory of Photoprotection and Dermatology. The expedition aims to provide essential dermatological care to individuals with albinism, addressing specific health needs related to their condition. This outreach is crucial for early detection and treatment of skin conditions prevalent among this population.

This event highlights a significant effort to extend healthcare services to vulnerable communities. By bringing specialized care directly to provincial and central hospitals, the campaign ensures greater accessibility for those who might otherwise face barriers to receiving necessary medical treatment.

Why it mattersPeople with albinism are directly benefiting from free specialized dermatological care, addressing their specific health needs.

Migrants Return From South Africa Via Mozambique

At least 15,394 migrants returned from South Africa between April and July 2026 due to violence and anti-immigration protests.

At least 15,394 migrants returned from South Africa, passing through Mozambique, between April and July 2026. The International Organization for Migration (IOM), cited by Lusa, reported that violence, protests, and fears associated with anti-immigration demonstrations in South Africa prompted these returns. The data comes from the Migration Flows Monitoring Survey.

The survey indicates a significant movement of people seeking to leave South Africa during this period. The reasons cited by the IOM point to a deteriorating security and social climate within South Africa that is directly impacting migrant populations. Mozambique served as a transit country for many of these individuals.

This influx of returning migrants presents challenges and opportunities for Mozambique. The scale of the returnees suggests a need for humanitarian assistance and integration support. The situation highlights the volatile conditions faced by migrants in neighboring countries and the resulting cross-border movements.

Why it mattersMigrants are directly affected by violence and anti-immigration sentiment in South Africa, forcing their return through Mozambique.
