Roadworks disrupt N7 traffic in Moatize

Temporary restrictions will be in place from 7:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. daily.

Roadworks on the N7 in Moatize, Mozambique, will cause temporary traffic disruptions. The National Roads Administration (ANE) is undertaking these works as part of the Water Services and Institutional Support Project II (WASIS II).

Traffic will be managed with a STOP AND GO system between 7:30 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. daily because diversions are not possible. ANE will use warning signs, barriers, and marshals with flags to guide vehicles through the construction zones.

ANE has asked road users for understanding and cooperation, advising them to reduce speed, follow signs and marshals, maintain distance, avoid overtaking, and expect delays. These works aim to rehabilitate and construct water supply networks in Tete and Moatize.

Why it mattersRoad users in Moatize will experience traffic delays and need to follow new traffic management systems.

Mozambique government keeps public transport fares unchanged

The government and FEMATRO agreed on a temporary compensation mechanism for transport operators.

Public transport fares in Greater Maputo will remain unchanged after the government and FEMATRO reached an agreement. National Director of Transport and Safety Alberto Matusse gave the assurance that passengers can continue using services at current rates.

To maintain fares, the government will implement an extraordinary compensation mechanism for transport operators. Payments will range from 25,000 to 90,000 meticais, varying by vehicle type and operational needs, Matusse explained.

This measure is temporary, lasting for three months, during which the government and operators will monitor its implementation. They will assess results and analyze conditions for the sustainability of public transport services in Maputo City, Matola, Boane, and Marracuene.

Why it mattersResidents of Greater Maputo will not face increased public transport costs.

Mozambique farmers lose produce to spoilage

Transport shortages and high freight costs prevent perishable goods from reaching markets in Quelimane.

In Quelimane, Mozambique, tomatoes are rotting in fields and markets because farmers and traders cannot find buyers before the produce spoils. This widespread issue affects perishable agricultural products across the country, leading to significant waste at a time when many families struggle with food affordability.

Producers and traders in Quelimane cite transport shortages, high freight costs, and inadequate storage as key problems. One trader reported paying MZN700 for tomatoes in Gorongosa and MZN250 for transport to Quelimane, only to find the produce spoiled after three days without sales, making it difficult to recover the initial investment.

This situation highlights broader weaknesses in Mozambique's agricultural supply chains, including poor roads and expensive transport. Traders' reluctance to accept prices below their purchase cost, even when facing total loss, further exacerbates the problem of perishable goods deteriorating before they can be sold.

Why it mattersMozambican farmers and traders are losing their perishable produce and potential income due to an inability to get goods to market and sell them before they spoil.
