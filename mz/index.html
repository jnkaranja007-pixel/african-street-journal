Mozambique discusses cannabis legalisation

A national drugs conference in Maputo considered the commercial possibilities of cannabis, acknowledging its medicinal and economic potential.

Mozambique is exploring the commercial potential of cannabis, a plant currently treated as a police matter. A national drugs conference organised by the Attorney General’s Office in Maputo last week debated the possible legalisation of controlled cannabis production, sale, and consumption. The conference acknowledged the plant’s medicinal and scientific value, as well as its role in other countries' formal economies, citing pharmaceutical products and hemp fibre for textiles as commercial applications.

While the discussion opens the door for potential legal businesses and investment, it leaves the status of small-scale farmers growing cannabis unresolved. The conference declaration noted discussions on legalisation but did not outline a licensing system or address personal consumption. This leaves Mozambique facing a choice between creating a legal business framework for investors and potentially continuing to prosecute individual farmers for growing the plant.

This approach mirrors a familiar pattern of opportunity distribution, where companies with capital and legal resources could secure cultivation permits while small farmers might still face prosecution. Although commercial production requires controls not necessary for personal cultivation, this distinction does not inherently justify treating individual farmers as criminals. Neighbouring South Africa decriminalised private adult use, possession, and cultivation for personal consumption in 2018, while not authorising unrestricted commercial sales.

Why it mattersMozambican citizens who grow cannabis for personal use could continue to face prosecution even if commercial production is legalised.

Mozambique Beer Expo returns with new experiences

Maputo will host the annual beer festival on October 24, featuring gastronomy, music, and industry insights.

The Mozambique Beer Expo is returning to Maputo on October 24 at Repinga, starting at 12:00 PM. The event promises new experiences, surprises, and a celebration of beer culture, blending entertainment, knowledge, gastronomy, and music. This year's expo aligns with the global trend of daytime events, offering a relaxed outdoor atmosphere for attendees to enjoy lunch and an afternoon of discovery.

The festival begins on October 23 with an Industry Day at the JFS Corporate Tower in downtown Maputo, part of the Festival Maputo Fast Forward. This session will feature a panel discussion with industry representatives addressing challenges, innovation, and growth opportunities in Mozambique's brewing sector. The main expo on October 24 will include special beer tastings, interactive experiences, lounge areas, and diverse food options, with music provided by DJs and a samba group.

Organized jointly by the Maputo Municipal Council, ANDITUR, Revista Xonguila, Malta do Vinho, and Connoisseur Prestige Club, with production by Arena Eventos, the expo aims to promote tourism and boost the local economy. The brewing industry is significant for Mozambique, with two major production units in the Maputo region creating jobs and contributing to state revenue.

Why it mattersThe beer expo affects local businesses by promoting tourism and the brewing industry, creating jobs and revenue.

Mozambique investors demand reliable infrastructure for local processing

Mining and energy firms want government to improve roads and electricity before committing to more domestic resource processing.

Mozambique's government is pushing mining and energy investors to process more resources locally and increase purchases from Mozambican businesses. However, at the CAVAME-d conference in Nacala, investors highlighted the need for reliable electricity and usable roads as prerequisites for such ambitions. Syrah Resources, operator of the Balama graphite mine, cited deteriorating road connections to Pemba and Nacala and continued reliance on diesel generators as significant constraints. The company argued that further graphite processing would require dependable electricity, questioning the economics of adding production stages under current conditions.

Nampula governor Eduardo Abdula acknowledged that roads and reliable energy are priorities for industrial development, bringing the discussion back to the conditions investors require. The government has become more explicit about expecting jobs, domestic purchases, and greater local processing from investors. It must, however, be equally clear about the services and infrastructure its own institutions will provide, backed by budgets, deadlines, and responsible delivery institutions.

There is a sound economic argument for retaining more value from Mozambique's resources through domestic processing, which can create employment, expand the tax base, and generate business for local suppliers. But these benefits depend on operations that can compete and find buyers. Requiring domestic processing without addressing the costs of unreliable power or difficult transport may result in little actual investment.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the government's push for local processing because they require reliable infrastructure like roads and electricity to make new investments economically viable.

Mozambican farmer achieves 30 tons per hectare yield

Arlindo Elias Massingue adopted climate-smart techniques and improved inputs to boost potato production and create local jobs.

Arlindo Elias Massingue, a 47-year-old farmer in Boane, Maputo Province, has transformed his potato yields to 30 tons per hectare through the Promoting Regenerative Agriculture for Sustainable Livelihoods (PRASL) initiative. Previously, low yields due to poor access to certified seeds, fertilizers, and limited technical knowledge made farming a source of stress, impacting his ability to provide for his seven children.

Massingue's involvement with PRASL, a partnership including Solidaridad (Southern Africa) and the HEINEKEN Africa Foundation, provided him with training in climate-smart agriculture and modern potato cultivation. He gained access to quality inputs and learned techniques such as soil management and integrated pest control, significantly improving his farming practices and confidence.

The increased yield has brought stability to Massingue's household, enabling him to consistently meet family needs and pay school fees. Beyond his farm, he employed 25 seasonal workers, primarily women and youth, and invested in an irrigation pump, diversifying his crops and strengthening the local rural economy.

Why it mattersArlindo Elias Massingue and 25 community members are directly affected by the increased agricultural yield and job creation.

Mozambique faces questions over millions of children out of school

Millions of children are not in school in Mozambique, raising concerns about the quality of education and future competencies.

An estimated five million children are currently out of Mozambique's education system, according to reports from the Movement for Education For All (MEPT). This figure highlights a significant gap between students entering primary school and those who progress to higher levels, indicating a substantial loss within the educational journey.

The reasons cited for this widespread absence from school include poverty, early marriages, teenage pregnancies, artisanal mining, and other economic activities that lead to student dropout. These factors prevent children from not only attending school but also from acquiring meaningful skills and knowledge for life.

The situation prompts a critical examination of the education system, questioning whether enrollment and classroom attendance translate into actual learning and the development of critical thinking and problem-solving abilities. The focus is shifting from merely counting students to assessing their capacity to apply what they learn in a rapidly changing world.

Why it mattersMillions of Mozambican children are affected by being out of school, hindering their ability to learn and develop life skills.
