Mozambique joins Southern Africa power delegation to modernize energy grids

A delegation of approximately 15 government and private sector representatives from Mozambique, Angola, Botswana, and Zambia will visit the U.S. to explore advanced energy.

The U.S. Trade and Development Agency (USTDA) is hosting a delegation from Mozambique and three other Southern African nations to foster partnerships for modernizing the region's energy transmission and distribution systems. The group, comprising government and private sector decision-makers, will travel to Denver, Colorado, and Austin, Texas, from August 29 to September 5.

During their visit, the delegates will engage with U.S. companies and observe demonstrations of advanced technologies, including AI-enabled grid control systems, designed to enhance power networks. They are also scheduled to meet with publicly and privately owned utilities and regulatory bodies, such as the Colorado Public Utilities Commission and the Electric Reliability Council of Texas.

This initiative aims to address the growing demand for secure and modern power infrastructure in Southern Africa, driven by rapid population growth, urbanization, and increased industrial output, including critical minerals mining. The USTDA will also host a public business briefing in Denver on September 1, allowing U.S. companies to discuss commercial opportunities directly with the delegation.

Why it mattersMozambique's participation in this delegation could lead to improved energy infrastructure, potentially stabilizing power supply for its citizens and industries.

BCI donates 40 computers to Maputo institutions

BCI, a Mozambican bank, has provided over 40 desktop computers to various institutions in Maputo City and Province, aiming to boost social and digital inclusion.

The BCI bank recently donated more than 40 desktop computers to educational, health, cultural, and child protection organizations in Maputo. This initiative, part of the bank's 30th-anniversary celebrations, seeks to improve working conditions, learning opportunities, and access to information for communities.

Among the beneficiaries are Nhongonhana Secondary School and MAKOBO Platform, which received computer equipment to support their activities. Other recipients include the District Directorate of Education and Culture of KaMpfumo, the National Song and Dance Company, and the Machava General Hospital.

The bank stated that promoting inclusion means equipping schools, social organizations, health units, and cultural institutions with tools to better perform their missions. This effort is part of BCI's social responsibility policy, which prioritizes education, health, well-being, and food and nutritional security.

Why it mattersThis initiative directly affects students, patients, and vulnerable children in Maputo by providing them with improved access to technology and resources for education, health, and social support.

Maputo Central Hospital treats 3,014 road accident victims in six months

The country's largest hospital recorded a new phenomenon of accidents involving motorbikes, particularly affecting young people aged 16 to 40 with serious injuries.

Maputo Central Hospital (HCM) treated 3,014 road accident victims from January to June 2026, a slight decrease from the 3,124 victims in the same period of 2025. Ten deaths were recorded in 2026, down from thirteen in 2025, according to HCM spokesperson Dino Lopes.

The hospital is concerned about the rise in motorbike accidents, especially among individuals aged sixteen to forty. These patients often arrive with severe fractures to their upper and lower limbs, requiring extended hospital stays and increasing treatment costs, Lopes stated.

Many patients are admitted in critical condition with multiple traumas, including head injuries, chest injuries, and significant blood loss. This situation places considerable pressure on the hospital's neurosurgery and orthopaedics and traumatology services, Lopes added, as some patients require surgery while others need prolonged conservative treatment for complications like haematomas or cerebral oedema.

Why it mattersMozambican citizens, particularly young people aged 16 to 40, are directly affected by the increasing number of severe road accident injuries, leading to prolonged hospital stays and increased healthcare burdens.

Demolitions displace 100 families in Chibuto, destroying homes and belongings

Around 100 families in the Nhamuza area of Chibuto district, Gaza province, are now homeless after their houses were demolished a week ago, leaving them without shelter or.

About 100 families in Mozambique's Nhamuza area, Chibuto district, Gaza province, lost their homes and belongings to demolitions a week ago. Residents like Carlota, 56, reported losing three houses she built over years, along with food, clothes, and school materials for her six minor children. The demolitions have left families exposed to the cold and uncertain of where they will sleep.

The destruction has scattered corrugated iron sheets, broken blocks, reeds, clothes, and other personal items across the ground. Families are challenging the process, stating they do not understand how they lost homes they had built over many years, which they valued at between 200,000 and 300,000 meticais.

Carlota stated that her children are no longer attending school due to the loss of their school materials and the family's lack of food. She lamented, "We have no food and are starving. We are sad about all this and demand action against what we consider an injustice."

Why it mattersAbout 100 families in the Nhamuza area are directly affected by losing their homes, food, clothes, and children's school materials, leading to displacement, hunger, and interrupted education.

Hospital technician detained for blood sale in Nampula

A technician from the Nampula Hospital Blood Bank was arrested on charges of demanding three thousand meticais for blood for a patient.

A technician at the Nampula Hospital Blood Bank has been detained following accusations of charging for blood. The individual is alleged to have demanded three thousand meticais to provide blood for a patient admitted to the hospital.

This arrest comes amid recurring rumors of illicit charges for blood transfusions at the Nampula Central Hospital. This incident marks a concrete action taken against such alleged practices.

The detention highlights ongoing concerns regarding access to essential medical supplies and potential corruption within healthcare facilities in the region, affecting patients requiring urgent transfusions.

Why it mattersPatients in Nampula requiring blood transfusions are directly affected by alleged illicit charges, potentially delaying critical medical care.
