Chapo launches 2026-27 agricultural campaign in Niassa

The initiative aims to boost production, food security, and agribusiness transformation.

Mozambican President Daniel Chapo launched the 2026/2027 Agricultural Campaign on Monday in Sanga district, Niassa province. The ceremony marked the start of efforts to increase agricultural output and strengthen national food security.

According to a statement from the Presidency of the Republic, the campaign is designed to drive the transformation of Mozambique's agricultural sector. It aims to promote more productive, resilient, and market-oriented farming, transitioning from subsistence to commercial agriculture.

A key focus of the campaign is mitigating the impacts of the El Niño phenomenon. This includes implementing measures to address anticipated irregular rainfall and prolonged drought periods, particularly affecting the central and southern regions of the country.

Why it mattersMozambican farmers will implement measures to combat drought and irregular rainfall during the new agricultural campaign.

Three alleged robbers killed in Maputo shootout

Police say they encountered four individuals preparing to rob a microcredit business.

Three alleged robbers died in a shootout with police in Maputo's Infulene neighbourhood early Monday morning. The group was reportedly preparing to rob a residence that houses a microcredit operation. A fourth member of the group escaped and is currently at large.

Police spokesperson Cláudio Ngulele stated that officers on routine patrol encountered four individuals acting suspiciously near the residence. The individuals allegedly opened fire on the police, who returned fire, resulting in the deaths of three suspects. No police officers were injured in the exchange.

Ngulele indicated that preliminary information suggests the robbers targeted the residence because it belonged to an economic agent believed to be Chinese, and they intended to steal money. He urged economic agents to enhance security measures and the public to report suspicious activities.

Why it mattersThree alleged robbers died during a police operation in Maputo, with one suspect still at large.

Mozambique proposes $250 million LNG solution for South Africa

Mozambique's state energy company seeks investment for floating units to supply South Africa's gas needs.

Empresa Nacional de Hidrocarbonetos (ENH), Mozambique's state energy firm, is proposing a $250 million project to supply gas to South Africa. The plan involves two floating storage and regasification units (FSRUs), one near Beira and another at Inhassoro, to process LNG from the Rovuma Basin. This initiative aims to stabilize gas deliveries to South Africa's Secunda industrial centre as its domestic Pande and Temane gas fields near depletion.

ENH CEO and Chairman Rudêncio Morais pitched the project, seeking investment from chemicals giant Sasol and pipeline operator ROMPCO. The FSRUs would connect to a new 140-km coastal pipeline to feed regasified LNG into the ROMPCO network. This infrastructure is envisioned to be in place by 2030 or 2031, aligning with major LNG projects in the Rovuma Basin.

The project aims to address South Africa's growing demand and prevent supply shortages. Depleting gas supplies from the Pande and Temane fields have raised concerns for third-party users, particularly as Sasol prioritizes feedstock for its chemical facilities. New rules requiring Mozambique's LNG industry to supply 25% of production domestically could also support these deliveries.

Why it mattersSouth Africa's Secunda industrial centre faces potential gas shortages as its domestic fields deplete, impacting its chemical facilities.

Boy dies after stepping on landmine in Macomia

The 12-year-old was collecting cashew nuts when he triggered the explosive device near Nanjaba village.

A 12-year-old boy died on Thursday in Cabo Delgado after stepping on a landmine in Nanjaba, Macomia district. The boy lost his life while en route to Pemba Provincial Hospital after being transferred from Macomia Health Centre. He had been collecting cashew nuts with friends in a cashew grove near Nanjaba village when the incident occurred.

Sources confirmed the boy was immediately assisted but succumbed to his injuries due to the severity of the situation. Macomia residents believe terrorists, active in the region for nine years, placed the mine. Insurgents had exchanged gunfire with Defence and Security Forces days earlier after extorting fish from motorcyclists on the Mucojo-Macomia-sede route.

Macomia sources suggest the landmines may be part of a terrorist strategy to target Rwandan Defence Forces, who recently established a new base near Nanjaba. This base is situated in an area terrorists frequently use for North-South movements through Quissanga district.

Mozambique uses AI to boost child vaccinations

A new artificial intelligence platform helps identify at-risk areas to improve vaccine coverage for children.

Mozambique is deploying an artificial intelligence platform to identify areas with low vaccination rates, aiming to increase coverage for children. The Advanced Data Analytics Platform (PAAD), developed by Zenysis Technologies with the Ministry of Health and Gavi support, now covers over 80 districts across six provinces. This tool integrates data from various health systems, providing a unified view of vaccination status, vaccine availability, and service performance.

This initiative addresses the fact that 24% of Mozambican children are "zero-dose," meaning they have never received routine vaccines, according to 2022-2023 data. Gabriel Risk Martins of Zenysis stated that PAAD offers clearer insights into vaccination activities, helping health teams pinpoint communities with gaps and reducing data analysis time. In Zambézia province, immunization officer Alberto Jacenau found PAAD significantly simplified data analysis compared to using Excel.

Technological advancements coincide with improved vaccination indicators, with DTP3 vaccine coverage rising from 55% in 2022 to 70% in 2023, according to Unicef and WHO data. The PAAD technology was first used in 2019 during the response to Cyclone Idai, where it helped reduce cholera cases in Sofala province from around 400 daily to zero in under a month. Gavi's Bertrand Pedersen highlighted the potential of AI and data analysis in public health.

Why it mattersChildren in Mozambique who have not received routine vaccines will benefit from improved identification of areas needing vaccination services.
