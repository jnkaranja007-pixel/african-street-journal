Mozambique adds nearly 400,000 electricity connections

National Energy Director Marcelina Mataveia announced that electricity access reached 70.3 percent in the first half of 2026.

Mozambique has connected nearly 400,000 new users to electricity in the first half of 2026. National Energy Director Marcelina Mataveia announced the figures at the Energy Sector Working Group meeting in Maputo.

Mataveia stated that electricity access now stands at 70.3 percent, achieved through expanding the national grid and using off-grid solutions like mini-grids and solar home systems. The government is also investing in the north-south transmission line and major generation projects, including the Mphanda Nkuwa Hydroelectric Project.

These efforts are expected to boost economic development, improve public services in education and healthcare, and create better conditions for business, particularly in rural areas. Future projects include the Mozambique-Malawi interconnection and the Namaacha Wind Power Project.

Why it mattersNearly 400,000 new electricity connections will support economic development and business activity in Mozambique.

Mozambique bridge collapses again after repairs

A temporary Revuè River bridge in Manica province failed six days after reopening, disrupting traffic.

President Daniel Chapo announced plans for over $7.5bn in investments for Mozambique's Maputo, Beira, and Nacala transport corridors on Wednesday. The same day, a temporary metal bridge over the Revuè River in Manica province collapsed again. The bridge, on the road between Chimoio and Sussundenga, failed after a truck carrying sand or stones crossed it, disrupting traffic for the second time in a week.

The bridge had reopened only six days prior to the collapse. It is unclear if the truck exceeded the bridge's 30-tonne weight limit. This incident highlights ongoing issues with Mozambique's infrastructure management, where temporary emergency bridges are often used long past their intended lifespan.

The N1, the country's main north-south highway, also suffers from poor conditions on many sections. A $400m World Bank rehabilitation program for the N1 has been discussed for years, but significant improvements are still needed.

Why it mattersThe collapse of a temporary bridge in Manica province disrupts traffic and highlights infrastructure management issues in Mozambique.

Maputo water supply to be restricted this weekend

Maintenance work at SAAS-Maputo facilities will affect several neighbourhoods in Maputo City and Matola-Rio.

Water supply will be restricted in several neighbourhoods across Maputo City and the Matola-Rio area this weekend. Maputo Water and Sanitation (SAAS-Maputo) announced the disruptions are due to ongoing maintenance work.

The maintenance is taking place in the Matola-Rio Operational Area and at the Chamanculo Distribution Centre in Maputo City. SAAS-Maputo stated that services are expected to return to normal on Monday.

Residents in the affected areas are advised to prepare for the temporary interruption in water services. The exact duration of the disruption beyond Monday has not been specified in the announcement.

Terrorists launch 154 attacks in northern Mozambique

Insurgents targeted 16 districts between June and October, with most violence concentrated in Cabo Delgado province.

More than 150 terrorist incursions were recorded in northern Mozambique over five months, from June to October. The majority of these 154 attacks occurred in Cabo Delgado province, according to a presentation at a Centre for Strategic Analysis of the Community of Portuguese Language Countries (CAE/CPLP) seminar in Maputo.

Brigadier-General Sérgio Estrela, Commander of the EU Military Assistance Mission in Mozambique (EUMAM MOZ), told attendees that insurgent groups are mobile and adapt their tactics, including kidnappings for ransom. These attacks spread across 16 districts, 15 in Cabo Delgado and one in Niassa, affecting 120 localities and challenging Mozambique's Defence and Security Forces (FADM).

The Islamic State of Mozambique claimed responsibility on November 1 for killing 14 people in Muidumbe and Mueda districts the previous week. Local communities report limited FADM presence in these areas, allowing insurgents freedom of movement. An investigator noted that despite external training and equipment efforts, security and human rights violations persist.

Why it mattersOver 150 terrorist attacks in five months have disrupted security and led to killings in northern Mozambique, affecting local communities.
