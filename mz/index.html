Mozambique government backs Temane power plant with World Bank funds

Cabinet approves a support package including US$65 million from the World Bank to finish the gas-fired plant.

Mozambique's government has approved a support package for the Temane Thermal Power Plant (CTT) project, which is considered of national economic and strategic interest. The Council of Ministers approved the measure on November 4, 2026, to address significant delays caused by Cyclone Freddy, Tropical Storm Filipo, and the contractor abandoning the works. The project aims to generate electricity from natural gas in Inhambane province.

To ensure the plant's completion, the government will mobilise financing from the World Bank to cover Electricidade de Moçambique's (EDM) capital contribution of up to US$65 million. The concession period for the plant will be extended by an additional six months, amending the contract from 28 to 30 years. Financial benefits include reduced concession fees, falling from 3% to 2% during debt repayment and from 8% to 5% thereafter.

The support package also includes a temporary waiver of dividends for EDM, up to US$39 million, after which payments will resume. Additionally, the government will waive its right to claim compensation for delays, up to approximately US$10.8 million. The resolution authorises these financial adjustments and the extension of the concession contract.

Why it mattersThe government's support package and World Bank financing aim to complete the Temane power plant, which is vital for Mozambique's energy infrastructure and economic interests.

Insurgents kill 22 in Cabo Delgado farmland

Islamic State claimed 14 of the deaths, describing its victims as Christians, in Muidumbe and Mueda districts over two weeks.

At least 22 people have been killed in the past fortnight in the low-lying farmland of Cabo Delgado's Muidumbe and Mueda districts. Islamic State has claimed 14 of those deaths and described its victims as Christians. The killings follow the battle for the insurgents' Catupa base in Macomia district, which began in June, after which the defence forces have looked increasingly passive.

Groups of fighters broke away south and west. One spent around two months in Montepuez district, where troops clashed with it at least three times and tried to close the R698 road between Montepuez and Mueda. It then slipped into Niassa province, attacked a hunting camp at Marangira in September and returned east. Last month insurgents struck around Chai and Litamanda in Macomia and blocked the N380 between the two villages, a few kilometres from Rwandan and Mozambican positions, with no visible response.

The Local Force militia, brought into the armed forces by a 2023 decree that obliged the government to supply it with weapons, ammunition and allowances, has borne the burden in Muidumbe and Mueda. Its fighters have repeatedly run short of ammunition, and their allowances arrive months late. Four women released by the insurgents near Muatide told security forces their captors used binoculars and a drone to steer around Local Force patrols.

Why it mattersResidents of Muidumbe and Mueda districts face the killings, while Local Force militia fighters there are short of ammunition and paid late.

US seeks to boost military ties with Mozambique

Washington aims to intensify joint exercises and training to combat terrorism in Cabo Delgado.

The United States wants to strengthen its military and security cooperation with Mozambique, focusing on the northern province of Cabo Delgado. This intention includes increasing joint exercises, providing training for Mozambique's Defence and Security Forces, and improving intelligence sharing.

The goal of this enhanced cooperation is to combat terrorism and safeguard investments in the natural gas sector. The US aims to bolster Mozambique's capacity to address security challenges in the region.

This initiative signals a commitment to supporting Mozambique's efforts in maintaining stability and security in Cabo Delgado, a region that has faced significant security threats.

Why it mattersThe United States is seeking to bolster military cooperation with Mozambique to combat terrorism and protect gas investments.

Maputo mural honours musician Xidimingwana's 90th birthday

Artist Sebastião Coana's work on Salazar Building in Malhangalene celebrates the marrabenta legend.

A mural celebrating the 90th birthday of marrabenta musician Xidimingwana now adorns Maputo's landscape. Artist Sebastião Coana created the artwork on the Salazar Building in Malhangalene, with support from Millennium bim. The inauguration ceremony on November 10 was led by Maputo Municipal Council President Rasaque Manhique.

The mural depicts the life and career of Domingos Matias Honwana, known as Xidimingwana, who was born in Bilene-Macia in 1936. Rui Pedro, President of Millennium bim's Executive Commission, stated the tribute recognizes Xidimingwana's cultural importance, noting his songs accompanied generations and helped tell Mozambique's stories. The artwork aims to keep his journey visible and accessible in the city.

This project, a challenge from the Maputo Municipal Council to Coana, bridges artistic generations by interpreting Xidimingwana's marrabenta legacy through a contemporary lens. The mural transforms the building's facade into a public space where music, painting, and urban life converge, ensuring the tribute continues beyond its unveiling.

Why it mattersThe mural honours Xidimingwana, a significant figure in Mozambican music, on his 90th birthday.

BCI hosts Christmas games at children's centre

The bank's Solidarity Christmas initiative included a draughts championship and gift distribution.

The Arco-Íris Reception Centre in Mozambique hosted a draughts championship and games as part of BCI's Solidarity Christmas initiative on November 11, 2026. Bank employees, including Board Member George Mandawa, participated in activities with the children, distributing gifts and fostering social interaction.

The championship aimed to develop logical thinking, concentration, and decision-making skills among the young participants. George Mandawa stated that BCI is committed to initiatives promoting well-being and social inclusion, emphasizing the importance of strengthening children's self-esteem and abilities.

This nationwide programme, marking BCI's 30th anniversary, uses draughts as a central activity to promote learning and personal development. The initiative reinforces the bank's vision of social responsibility beyond financial activities, valuing community ties and investment in people.

Why it mattersChildren at the Arco-Íris Reception Centre received gifts and participated in games designed to boost their self-esteem and abilities.
