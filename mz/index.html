Mozambique mining titles yield no operations

Hundreds of mining concessions and certificates have not begun operations, with many inactive for over a decade.

Mozambique holds 169 mining concessions and 371 small-scale mining certificates where operations have not commenced, according to the Administrative Court's 2025 State General Account.

Of these inactive titles, 67 concessions and 72 certificates have remained dormant for more than 10 years, with the court finding no evidence of government-approved extensions for these delays.

The court recommended stricter oversight of licence holders' financial capacity and development progress, following the introduction of a new Mining Law in 2026 designed to enforce development timelines.

Why it mattersThe Administrative Court recommended tighter supervision of mining licence holders' financial capacity and progress towards development.

Sovereign fund watchdog says it was shut out of key policy work

Mozambique's Supervisory Committee of the Sovereign Fund says the Finance Ministry was slow to share information and left it out of drafting the Investment Policy.

The Supervisory Committee of Mozambique's Sovereign Fund, an independent body that monitors revenues from the Rovuma natural gas project, says it was excluded from drafting the fund's management instruments. It also says it has not received the fund's annual financial report.

The complaints appear in the committee's first-quarter 2026 report, sent to Parliament, and were cited by the Plan and Budget Commission while reviewing the fund's 2025 accounts. The report also points to the absence of independent validation of projections, external audit and an independent expert, and to the unpublished management agreement with the Bank of Mozambique, signed on 19 November 2025.

The committee described revenue management as reasonable and said there was no breach of the law or regulations, despite organisational problems. It has recommended revising the law creating the fund and its regulations, to extend its oversight to projects financed from gas revenues.

Mozambique Supreme Court trial uses new audio-visual recording

The Supreme Court in Maputo will begin the trial of Venâncio Mondlane on Tuesday, November 17, using new technology to record hearings.

The Supreme Court in Maputo will begin the trial of Venâncio Mondlane on Tuesday, November 17, using new audio and video recording technology for hearings. This system, which transcribes recordings into written documents, aims to significantly reduce trial length, according to Supreme Court judge-counsellor Pedro Nhatitima. The trial will take place in the criminal section of the Supreme Court, with daily sessions scheduled from 10:00 to 15:30 local time.

The case involves charges brought by the Public Prosecutor’s Office against Mondlane, a former presidential candidate who has not recognized the election results. The proceedings relate to post-election demonstrations between October 2024 and March 2025, during which over 400 people died in clashes with police and hundreds of businesses were vandalized. Mondlane faces charges including public glorification of crime and incitement to terrorism. The trial will hear from 12 prosecution witnesses, 20 defence witnesses, and two experts from mobile operators Vodacom and Tmcel.

The courtroom has a maximum capacity of 45 people and will include five Supreme Court judge-counsellors, two deputy attorneys-general, and legal representatives. Mondlane is standing trial before the Supreme Court because he is a member of the Council of State, which lifted his immunity.

Why it mattersVenâncio Mondlane faces charges of incitement to terrorism and public glorification of crime in a trial starting November 17.

Mozambican boy, 15, found brutally murdered

His 12-year-old friend remains missing after they disappeared from a Sant'Egidio Community program.

A 15-year-old boy, Juvêncio, was found dead with extreme signs of violence in the Matola River on Monday, November 9. He and his 12-year-old friend, Ilton, had disappeared on Saturday, November 7, after attending a Sant’Egidio Community program in Matola. Fishermen discovered Juvêncio’s body, which showed clear evidence of brutality.

Father Rolando Curzi, a priest with the Sant’Egidio Community, stated that the nature of the mutilations suggests the crime may be linked to magical-superstitious practices. Ilton is still missing, and the search continues in Matola. The Sant’Egidio Community is urging authorities to investigate the swift murder of the young peace advocate.

This is the first such case involving children attending the Community’s centre, though similar incidents have occurred involving minors, particularly boys with albinism. The Sant’Egidio Community runs a BRAVO! program for the civil registration of children to provide protection. Their Matola Nutrition Centre feeds about 500 disadvantaged minors weekly and offers educational support to over 100 on Saturdays.

Why it mattersThe Sant'Egidio Community is calling for swift investigation into the brutal murder of a 15-year-old boy and the ongoing search for his 12-year-old missing friend.

EU military mission joins Mozambique's first defence cooperation day

The EU's military assistance mission in Mozambique took part in the first Defence Cooperation Day, held at the Military Club in Maputo on 13 November 2026.

Brigadier-General Sérgio Estrela, commander of the force of the European Union Military Assistance Mission in Mozambique (EUMAM MOZ), attended the first Defence Cooperation Day in Maputo on 13 November 2026, accompanied by other mission military personnel.

The meeting, held at the Military Club under the theme "Cooperative Security: The Citizen at the Centre", brought the mission's officers together with Mozambican defence participants to discuss how security work can serve ordinary people.

The report does not give further details of the programme or outcomes, so the event is reported only as the first gathering of its kind.

Why it mattersOfficers from the EU mission and Mozambican defence bodies now have a shared forum for discussing citizen-focused security work.
