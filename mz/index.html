President Chapo Directs Bank of Mozambique Governor to Boost Foreign Currency and Credit

New governor Felisberto Navalha tasked with addressing foreign currency shortages, expanding credit access, and promoting innovation.

President Daniel Chapo has instructed Felisberto Navalha, the newly sworn-in governor of the Bank of Mozambique, to tackle critical economic challenges including foreign currency shortages and limited access to credit. Navalha is also expected to foster technological innovation and extend financial services to rural areas, all while maintaining low inflation and currency stability. These directives aim to support businesses needing foreign currency for imports and enable farmers and small companies to secure financing for expansion.

The president acknowledged that monetary policy alone cannot resolve these issues, noting that Mozambique's foreign currency deficit stems from weak and undiversified exports. He emphasized that high interest rates cannot compensate for fiscal indiscipline, poor infrastructure, or an unfavorable business environment. Chapo stressed the need for coordination between the Ministry of Finance and the central bank, particularly concerning state domestic borrowing which can absorb liquidity needed by companies.

The president's call for coordination is politically sensitive, as many in the ruling Frelimo party, government, and business circles opposed the previous governor's strict focus on inflation and tight credit. While the former governor's resistance to pressure was seen as a defense against politically motivated monetary policy, some of his actions were perceived as arbitrary. The new directives signal a shift towards a broader economic stability approach.

Why it mattersBusinesses and farmers are directly affected by the central bank's mandate to address foreign currency shortages and widen access to credit for expansion and imports.

Mozambique Economy Grows 1.7% in Second Quarter 2026

The National Statistics Institute reported that the tertiary sector, led by hotels and restaurants, drove economic activity.

Mozambique's economy expanded by 1.7% in the second quarter of 2026 compared to the same period last year, according to the National Statistics Institute (INE). The tertiary sector was the primary contributor, showing a 2.5% increase, with hotels and restaurants experiencing a significant 14.5% rise. Financial services also saw growth at 4.2%.

The primary sector contributed positively with an 1.8% increase, mainly due to agriculture, livestock, hunting, forestry, and related activities which grew by 2.0%. Mining extraction also saw a 1.7% increase, though fishing experienced a 1.1% decline. In contrast, the secondary sector contracted by 6.0%, largely driven by a 11.2% decrease in manufacturing.

Agriculture, livestock, hunting, forestry, and related activities represented the largest share of the economy at 31.6%, followed by mining extraction at 15.0%. Transport, storage, and information and communication services held an 8.0% share, with trade and repair services at 7.3%. Other sectors like public administration and manufacturing also contributed to the overall economic performance.

Why it mattersThe growth in the tertiary and primary sectors, alongside specific industry performance, affects businesses and employment across Mozambique.

Government and Partner Release Rural Funding

Seven hundred twenty-six million meticais are now available over five years for rural development projects, co-financed by IFAD and managed by Moza Banco.

A new financing line, the Linha de Financiamento a Empreendimentos Rurais, has been launched, making seven hundred twenty-six million meticais available over the next five years. This fund is specifically targeted at rural development initiatives.

The initiative is a joint effort, co-financed by the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD). Moza Banco will be responsible for managing these funds, ensuring their allocation to eligible rural enterprises.

This additional financing aims to support economic activities in rural areas, with the province of Nampula mentioned as a region that will benefit. The program seeks to boost development and investment in the countryside.

Why it mattersRural enterprises and development projects in Mozambique will receive increased financial support over the next five years.

Natural gas production benefits industries in Mozambique

The Empresa Nacional de Hidrocarbonetos (ENH) representative highlighted opportunities in the gas value chain at the MozIndus Forum.

Several industries across Mozambique are experiencing benefits from the country's natural gas production, according to Titos Nhabomba, a representative of the Empresa Nacional de Hidrocarbonetos (ENH). Speaking at the MozIndus Forum, held as part of FACIM 2026, Nhabomba detailed the opportunities present within Mozambique's gas value chain.

Nhabomba's presentation, titled “Local Processing of Minerals and Hydrocarbons – Opportunities in the Gas Chain in Mozambique,” specifically pointed to the positive impact on the food industry, among others. The forum aimed to explore the local processing of minerals and hydrocarbons, underscoring the potential for growth and development driven by the nation's natural resources.

The ENH representative's remarks emphasized the broader economic advantages derived from natural gas, suggesting a ripple effect across various sectors. This discussion at FACIM 2026 served to highlight the strategic importance of hydrocarbon resources in Mozambique's industrial development and economic diversification efforts.

Why it mattersIndustries in Mozambique, including the food sector, are benefiting from the availability of natural gas, which supports their operations and development.

El Niño Threatens Mozambique with Severe Drought

Meteorological forecasts predict the El Niño phenomenon will persist for four years, potentially causing the most severe drought since the 1950s.

Southern and central Mozambique face the prospect of one of the most severe droughts in the country's history, possibly exceeding the intensity of the early 1950s. Weather predictions indicate the El Niño phenomenon will continue for the next four years, increasing the risk of rainfall deficits and water scarcity across several regions. A significant precipitation deficit is anticipated between October 2026 and May 2027, with potentially severe impacts in numerous districts.

At least 30 districts in the southern and central regions are projected to be affected by severe drought. Specific areas identified include Tete province's Changara, Marara, Mágoè, Mutarara, and Chiúta districts; Sofala province's Machanga and Beira city; Manica province's Guro, Machaze, and Macossa districts; Inhambane province's Govuro and Panda; and Gaza province's Chókwè. Moderate impacts are also foreseen in Moamba (Maputo), Manjacaze and Chibuto (Gaza), Chiúre (Cabo Delgado), and Memba (Nampula).

Despite the grim outlook, the National Directorate of Water Resources believes Mozambique currently possesses some response capacity due to existing reserves in major reservoirs and soil moisture levels in the south. Recent rains have helped maintain damp soils and satisfactory water storage. Authorities are not currently planning severe water restrictions, prioritizing efficient resource management and distribution, though this could change from 2027 if drought conditions worsen, potentially leading to gradual restrictions impacting agriculture and industry first.

Why it mattersFarmers and investors in Mozambique's southern and central regions face potential crop failure and operational disruptions due to the predicted prolonged drought.
