Mozambique renews Kenmare titanium mine concession until 2044

The Council of Ministers approved the extension for the Moma mine, which supplies 6% of global titanium feedstocks.

Mozambique's Council of Ministers approved the extension of rights and concessions for Kenmare Resources' Moma heavy mineral sands mine in Nampula province until 2044. The approval, granted on October 13 and officially notified on October 29, includes changes to the tax, commercial, and investment regimes.

The Moma mine, operated by Kenmare since 2007, accounts for approximately 6% of the world's titanium feedstocks, supplying industries such as paint and plastics in over 15 countries. Managing director Tom Hickey stated the changes aim for a balance between Kenmare's interests and a greater contribution to Mozambique.

Royalties on gross revenues will increase progressively from 1% to 5% by 2044. Kenmare Resources had been negotiating the renewal since its initial 20-year authorization expired in December 2024, having previously threatened international arbitration over fiscal terms.

Why it mattersMozambican government approval of the Moma mine's concession renewal until 2044 affects the country's mining sector and its contribution to global titanium supply.

Mozambique plans private rail operators

State-owned CFM will tender sections of the national rail network to attract more freight traffic.

Ports and Railways of Mozambique (CFM) is preparing tenders to allow private companies to operate parts of the national rail network. The state-owned company aims to attract more freight traffic and boost Mozambique's role as a transit hub for neighboring countries. CFM will remain the infrastructure manager while private firms operate services under concession.

Mozambique's freight network uses three corridors linked to the ports of Maputo, Beira, and Nacala. In the first quarter of 2026, railways carried 3.6 million metric tons of goods, a 14.9% increase year-on-year. The government announced a 14-billion-metical investment program for 2025–2030 to upgrade infrastructure, including new locomotives and freight cars.

The Confederation of Economic Associations of Mozambique called for more reliable rail operations and reviewed freight rates in June 2026. Exporters sometimes favor road transport due to unpredictable delivery times and railcar shortages. The success of the concessions will depend on efficient coordination with ports, customs, and road transport.

Why it mattersBusinesses and exporters in Mozambique will have more options for moving goods through the national rail network.

COTUR and Solenta Aviation launch Maputo-Vilankulo flights

Three weekly flights will connect Mozambique's capital to the tourist town starting November 5.

COTUR has partnered with Solenta Aviation to launch three weekly flights between Maputo and Vilankulo. This initiative aims to strengthen domestic air links and boost tourism. The flights will operate in both directions, with services beginning on November 5.

The new air service is intended to enhance travel options for tourists visiting Mozambique. Solenta Aviation will operate the flights, connecting the capital city with the popular coastal destination.

The launch date of November 5 coincides with another event, though details were not provided in the report. The partnership between COTUR and Solenta Aviation is focused on improving national air connectivity.

Why it mattersMozambicans travelling between Maputo and Vilankulo will have a new direct flight option.

Mozambique and China advance cassava value chain

A meeting in Shenzhen mapped implementation of a memorandum of understanding signed in Maputo to boost cultivation, processing, and commercialization.

Mozambique and China have entered a new phase of cassava value chain development following a meeting in Shenzhen between the Mozambique Chamber of Commerce (CCMI) and Chinese partners. This follows a Memorandum of Understanding signed in Maputo on October 1.

The initiative, attended by CCMI Board Chairperson Thani Cabir and Executive Director Fileu Pave, will initially target the Gaza and Inhambane provinces. Local cooperatives, business associations, and the Longrun Group will engage in cassava cultivation, processing, and commercialization, with Longrun Group facilitating technology transfer and employment.

China annually imports four million tonnes of cassava chips, eight million tonnes of starch, 1.5 million tonnes of unprocessed cassava, and over 50,000 tonnes of flour, presenting a significant export opportunity for Mozambique. This sustained demand aims to diversify agricultural exports, boost domestic value addition, and uplift rural smallholders and agro-industrial SMEs.

Why it mattersMozambican farmers and small and medium-sized enterprises in Gaza and Inhambane provinces will benefit from increased opportunities in cassava cultivation, processing, and commercialization due to Chinese demand.

Revuè River bridge reopens with 30-tonne limit

Mozambique's ANE partially completed repairs, restricting vehicles to 30 tonnes.

The Revuè River bridge in Mozambique has reopened following partial repair work by ANE, but with a strict 30-tonne gross vehicle weight limit. Vehicles exceeding this limit must use alternative routes to avoid penalties, including fines and cargo transfer.

ANE stated that further maintenance, such as tightening bolts and reinstating the pedestrian walkway, is ongoing. These activities may lead to traffic restrictions or temporary interruptions for safety reasons.

The agency did not provide a date for the full completion of the bridge repairs or when the weight limit might be lifted.

Why it mattersHeavy vehicles exceeding 30 tonnes must use alternative routes, impacting transport and logistics.
