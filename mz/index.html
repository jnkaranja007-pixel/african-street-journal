Insurgents kill two, loot Mozambique hunting camp

A South African trainee and a Mozambican cook died in an attack on a camp owned by football manager Carlos Queiroz, with 13 staff missing.

Suspected Islamic State-linked insurgents attacked the Mozambique Wildlife Adventures hunting camp near Marangira, killing two employees and leaving 13 unaccounted for. The attackers looted the camp, burned one vehicle, and stole another laden with supplies before departing. This raid mirrors a previous attack on a camp within the Niassa Special Reserve in April 2025, suggesting a pattern of targeting operations for resources and transport.

The camp, located about 40km east of Marrupa, is outside the Niassa Special Reserve. The Mozambique Wildlife Adventures operation is owned by Carlos Queiroz, the football manager. Three American clients, who were hunting in a different part of the concession, escaped with staff into the bush and were later evacuated by helicopter. The killing of a South African citizen has raised concerns among safari operators in the region.

The attackers may have intended to move deeper into Niassa province, which is known for informal mining areas that could provide income and supplies, and has been a source of recruits for the insurgency. The choice of an internationally connected hunting operation, which hosted American clients and is owned by a globally recognized figure, raises the possibility that the insurgents are seeking targets that will generate international attention.

Why it mattersThe attack on the hunting camp, which resulted in the death of a South African and the disappearance of Mozambican staff, directly impacts the safety and operations of businesses involved in Mozambique's safari and hunting industry.

Statistics Institute Admits Production Data Gaps

Mozambique's National Statistics Institute acknowledges inconsistencies in agricultural figures from 2021 to 2025, hindering policy evaluation.

Mozambique's National Statistics Institute (INE) has revealed significant gaps and inconsistencies in its agricultural production data for the years 2021 through 2025. The institute admits that these figures cannot always be reliably compared across different years, impacting the ability to track progress accurately. In many instances, data for cultivated land and crop output in 2021 and 2022 were estimations provided by the agriculture ministry, while some 2024 figures were derived from administrative records rather than dedicated field surveys.

While the INE deserves commendation for its transparency in disclosing these limitations, the underlying issue remains. Statistical uncertainty is an inherent challenge in a country where a substantial portion of economic activity occurs in rural, informal sectors that are difficult to monitor. Concealing this uncertainty would be detrimental, but candor alone does not resolve the problem of establishing a dependable baseline for policy design and evaluation.

Without consistent and accurate measurements, it becomes impossible for policymakers to discern whether reported increases in production are due to actual growth or simply changes in estimation methods. This lack of reliable data also allows for the potential declaration of success or the concealment of failure by manipulating the figures used for comparison, undermining the effectiveness of government targets, such as the aim to triple cotton production.

Why it mattersPolicymakers and investors cannot reliably assess agricultural growth or the success of government targets due to inconsistent and estimated production data.

Insurgents Burn Tourist Camp, Steal Vehicles in Niassa

Armed attackers destroyed Mozambique Wild Adventures facilities and took 15 vehicles from the Marangira hunting concession.

Insurgents attacked and burned the Mozambique Wild Adventures (MWA) camps in the Niassa Special Reserve buffer zone on April third. The group also stole fifteen vehicles from the Marangira hunting concession. Approximately fifteen employees fled the complex, but no fatalities have been reported. The attackers reportedly destroyed mobile phone antennas before proceeding with the assault.

The destroyed MWA facility, a safari camp, was part of a three million euro investment supporting local villages within the reserve. Carlos Queiroz, a football coach and co-owner of MWA, is from Nampula. Mozambican Defense and Security Forces are currently pursuing the insurgents in the area, which is north of Marrupa district and borders the Niassa Special Reserve.

This incident follows a similar attack last year on the Kambako hunting area within the Niassa Reserve, where an unspecified number of people were taken hostage. The hunting industry, which generates over ten million dollars annually, provides necessary tax revenue and private security for the reserve, deterring illegal hunters.

Why it mattersThe attack on the tourist complex and theft of vehicles directly impacts the tourism business and its employees in the Niassa region.

Chapo Pledges Child Welfare Amidst Cabo Delgado Conflict

Mozambican President Daniel Chapo linked ending terrorism and ensuring peace to the well-being of children, especially those in Cabo Delgado.

President Daniel Chapo reiterated the government's commitment to the welfare of Mozambican children, particularly those in Cabo Delgado, during the VIII National Session of the Children's Parliament. He emphasized that ending terrorism and consolidating peace and security are essential conditions for protecting minors affected by the conflict. Chapo stated that instability in Cabo Delgado prevents many children from having stable homes and attending school regularly, making the resolution of terrorism a priority for their development.

Speaking in Maputo, Chapo also addressed issues of premature unions and early pregnancies, advocating for strengthened prevention efforts and societal involvement. He expressed a desire to be remembered as a "friend of the children," reaffirming the government's dedication to protecting and promoting child rights. The newly elected president of the Children's Parliament, Neide Albertina Correia, highlighted that the debates allowed children to present their challenges and propose solutions to various institutions.

Correia outlined that child welfare encompasses access to safe education, healthcare, adequate nutrition, and protection from violence, abuse, child labor, premature unions, trafficking, and exploitation. The VIII National Session, held under the theme "Child Welfare is Our Priority," also saw the election of Correia as the new president of the Children's Parliament, succeeding Elijas de Frank Caetano.

Why it mattersChildren in Cabo Delgado are directly affected by the ongoing conflict, which disrupts their education and overall well-being, according to President Chapo.

Mozambique Urges CPLP to Boost Business Ties

Minister Basílio Muhate called for converting cultural proximity into concrete economic opportunities for entrepreneurs and youth.

Mozambique is challenging member states of the Community of Portuguese Language Countries (CPLP) to transform their shared cultural heritage into tangible business, trade, investment, and employment opportunities, particularly for young people and small entrepreneurs. Minister of Economy Basílio Muhate stated in Maputo that the CPLP should aim to become a "community of opportunities" in the coming decades, using the Portuguese language as an economic bridge connecting companies, knowledge, capital, and markets across continents.

Muhate emphasized that a truly economic union would allow good ideas to cross borders freely, enabling small entrepreneurs in Maputo to find opportunities in cities like Luanda, Praia, São Tomé, Bissau, Dili, Rio de Janeiro, or Lisbon. This initiative complements Mozambique's regional integration efforts, with the CPLP serving as an additional platform for global engagement rather than a replacement for bodies like the Southern African Development Community (SADC).

The minister highlighted micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) as the backbone of CPLP economies, stressing their significant impact on job creation, income generation, innovation, and economic growth. He advocated for increased support for these businesses, including strengthening legal frameworks, simplifying state-private sector interactions through digitalization, and ensuring access to financing, technology, social protection, and markets, with a focus on climate resilience and digital transformation.

Why it mattersSmall entrepreneurs and young people in Mozambique are affected by the call to leverage CPLP connections for business growth and employment.
