Mozambique central bank boosts production lending

New rules allow banks to deduct 110% of investment loan principal from reserve requirements.

Mozambique's central bank introduced a special lending regime on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, to encourage financing for national production. The measure, effective for 18 months, allows credit institutions to reduce their reserve requirements by 110% for investment loans and 100% for working capital loans to eligible companies and cooperatives.

This initiative aims to stimulate the national economy by incentivizing banks to fund productive activities, including the acquisition of machinery and raw materials for sectors like goods production, processing, and tourism. Beneficiaries must meet tax and accounting obligations, demonstrate financial capacity, and present projects focused on import substitution or export diversification.

Banks must issue at least 50 new loans every six months, totaling a minimum of 6% of their average reserve requirements. Projects are encouraged to use domestic raw materials, with imports permitted only when local supply is insufficient or unsuitable.

Why it mattersMozambican businesses seeking investment or working capital for production can access new financing options.

Japan grants $100,449 for Matola school classrooms

The funding will build seven new classrooms, two toilet blocks, and upgrade existing facilities at the primary school.

Japan has signed a grant agreement worth US$100,449 to build new classrooms and facilities at Matola primary school in Mozambique. The project, implemented by the Community Association for Human Development (ASSCODECHA), will construct two blocks containing seven classrooms, each designed to accommodate 50 pupils.

The new facilities will include desks for students and access ramps for people with disabilities, aiming to improve learning conditions and create a more inclusive school environment. The project also provides for the expansion and rehabilitation of existing classrooms and the construction of two toilet blocks, with four toilets for boys and two for girls.

Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of Japan to Mozambique, HAMADA Keiji, attended the signing ceremony. The interventions are expected to expand access to education and support the continuity of children’s education in the region.

Why it mattersChildren in Matola will have improved learning conditions and expanded access to education.

Politics · 2026-10-29

EU-trained units struggle against Mozambique insurgents

Insurgents conducted 154 attacks in five months across 16 districts, while specialised forces are misused.

Mozambique has created 11 rapid-reaction units with European Union assistance to combat insurgents in Cabo Delgado and Niassa. The EU trained soldiers, supplied equipment, and is helping regenerate units between deployments. However, insurgents continue to operate across an expanding area, with specialised forces often used in ways that undermine their training.

Brigadier-General Sérgio Estrela, commander of the EU Military Assistance Mission, stated that the FADM are hindered by excessive command centralisation, intelligence weaknesses, and shortages of equipment and money. He noted that insurgents conducted approximately 154 attacks over the previous five months, spread across about 120 locations in 16 districts. Abductions, used for ransom and recruits, rose from 29% of attacks in the first half of the year to 56% in the most recent quarter.

Estrela explained that this type of enemy requires mobile units capable of receiving intelligence, deploying quickly, striking, and withdrawing. Instead, some rapid-reaction forces have reportedly remained in static positions for months, performing garrison duties. These issues are not resolvable by further training courses.

Why it mattersMozambican soldiers trained by the EU are not effectively countering insurgents due to systemic issues like command and equipment shortages.

Mozambique trains public servants in AI with Google

The U.S. and Mozambican governments partnered with Google for a one-day training on Super Intelligence and cloud computing.

Mozambican public servants received training in Super Intelligence (SI) and cloud computing in Maputo on October 29, 2026. The U.S. and Mozambican governments collaborated with Google for the one-day program, which aimed to enhance digital skills within the public sector.

Chargé d’Affaires Abigail L. Dressel and Minister Américo Muchanga attended the opening of the training, emphasizing U.S.-Mozambique cooperation. Joylynn Kirui, UpSkill Universe’s lead trainer and Super Intelligence specialist, led sessions covering SI opportunities in Africa, cloud infrastructure, data policies, and public administration applications.

The training included case studies, discussions, and hands-on exercises to explore SI applications, alongside addressing data protection, privacy, and necessary safeguards for responsible technology use.

Why it mattersMozambican public servants are being trained to use new technologies like Super Intelligence and cloud computing in their work.

Agriculture · 2026-10-29

IFC to boost Mozambique farm finance

The International Finance Corporation wants to increase funding for smallholder farmers and SMEs.

The International Finance Corporation (IFC), part of the World Bank Group, wants to increase cooperation with Mozambique. The IFC aims to mobilise finance and investment for agriculture, energy, logistics, tourism, digital transformation, and business development.

The IFC will pay special attention to smallholder farmers and micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs). This focus aims to improve access to funding and investment for these sectors of the Mozambican economy.

This renewed cooperation follows discussions between the IFC and Mozambique. The goal is to strengthen private sector development across several key economic areas.

Why it mattersMozambican smallholder farmers and small businesses will receive increased attention for financing and investment.
