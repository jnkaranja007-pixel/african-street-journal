Rwandan forces capture six suspected insurgents in Pangane village

The suspects were detained while buying food for insurgents in a village that has served as a supply point for the group.

Six individuals suspected of terrorism were captured by Rwandan forces last Friday night in Pangane village, in Mozambique's Macomia district. The suspects had reportedly infiltrated the local population and were discovered and reported to a Rwandan military position in the village while buying food for insurgents.

According to sources cited by the independent newspaper Carta de Moçambique, Pangane has been a key food supply point for the insurgents. In some cases, the group has destroyed businesses and beaten people selling alcohol and cigarettes, which it considers prohibited under its reading of Islamic law.

The arrests come months after the group stopped maintaining a constant presence in the region, following the deployment of an additional Rwandan military unit. Attacks in Cabo Delgado, including at Pangane, prompted more than 700 people to flee in August and September, according to the International Organization for Migration (IOM).

Why it mattersVillagers in Pangane who sell food, alcohol or cigarettes face attacks and extortion from the group, and more than 700 people have already fled attacks in Cabo Delgado this year.

Business · 2026-11-13

Mozambique mining titles yield no operations

Hundreds of mining licences and small-scale certificates have not led to any mining activity, according to a court assessment.

Mozambique holds 169 mining concessions and 371 small-scale mining certificates where no operations have begun, the Administrative Court reported. Of these, 67 concession holders and 72 certificate holders have been inactive for over a decade, the court found in its assessment of the 2025 State General Account.

The court found no evidence that the government had granted extensions for these delays, recommending tighter supervision of licence holders' financial capacity and progress. This assessment comes five months after Mozambique introduced a new Mining Law, Law 7/2026, which requires applicants to demonstrate technical experience and financial resources.

Under the new law, concession holders must begin mine development within 24 months and production within 48 months. However, the law protects rights acquired under existing concessions, meaning many titles identified by the court may remain under the previous legal framework for years, suggesting enforcement of existing rules was the core issue.

Why it mattersHundreds of mining licence holders have not begun operations, with 67 concession holders inactive for over a decade, prompting recommendations for stricter government oversight.

Politics · 2026-11-13

Sovereign fund watchdog says it was shut out of key policy work

Mozambique's Supervisory Committee of the Sovereign Fund says the Finance Ministry was slow to share information and left it out of drafting the Investment Policy.

The Supervisory Committee of Mozambique's Sovereign Fund, an independent body that monitors revenues from the Rovuma natural gas project, says it was excluded from drafting the fund's management instruments. It also says it has not received the fund's annual financial report.

The complaints appear in the committee's first-quarter 2026 report, sent to Parliament, and were cited by the Plan and Budget Commission while reviewing the fund's 2025 accounts. The report also points to the absence of independent validation of projections, external audit and an independent expert, and to the unpublished management agreement with the Bank of Mozambique, signed on 19 November 2025.

The committee described revenue management as reasonable and said there was no breach of the law or regulations, despite organisational problems. It has recommended revising the law creating the fund and its regulations, to extend its oversight to projects financed from gas revenues.

Business · 2026-11-13

Chapo courts Turkish investors for Mozambique gas, power and corridor projects

The president told business leaders in Antalya that the country has recently carried out public sector reforms that make it a favourable place to raise capital.

President Daniel Chapo invited Turkish businesspeople to invest in infrastructure, energy, transport, logistics, agriculture, tourism and industry in Mozambique. Speaking in Antalya, Turkey, he said the country is going through a favourable moment for raising capital after reforms in the public sector, and he acknowledged that Mozambique has projects and a vision that need financing.

He listed gas projects in the Rovuma Basin linked to ENI, TotalEnergies and ExxonMobil, and said Mozambique LNG has resumed activity in Cabo Delgado. He added that ExxonMobil's final investment decision is expected before the end of 2026. He also named the Maputo, Beira and Nacala corridors, the Mphanda Nkuwa project, a second phase of the Cahora Bassa hydroelectric dam, and a gas power plant of about 450 megawatts in Inhambane.

Chapo said Mozambican law provides for public-private partnerships, build-operate-transfer (BOT) arrangements and concessions. The official note did not say whether the Turkish businesspeople made proposals or took on any commitments.

Why it mattersTurkish companies weighing Mozambique's gas, power and corridor projects now have a named set of partnership and concession routes to pursue.

Health · 2026-11-13

Jordan's Princess Sarah Zeid visits Mozambique's Cabo Delgado

UN official assesses health and well-being of mothers and children affected by crisis in northern province.

Princess Sarah Zeid of Jordan, a Special Adviser to the UN Emergency Relief Coordinator’s Special Initiative on Maternal and Newborn Health in Crisis, visited Mozambique's Cabo Delgado province. She travelled to Palma, Mocímboa da Praia and Pemba to assess the situation of local communities, focusing on the health and well-being of mothers and children affected by the ongoing humanitarian crisis.

Princess Sarah Zeid met with Parliament Speaker Margarida Adamugi Talapa in Maputo on Thursday to discuss the United Nations' commitment to strengthening support for Mozambique. She highlighted the need for better coordination of humanitarian aid to reach vulnerable populations, particularly women, girls, mothers, and children, amidst financial and climate challenges.

The UN intends to increase its support for the health and well-being of mothers, children, and girls in Cabo Delgado. Princess Sarah Zeid expressed appreciation for the Mozambican Government's integrated approach to health and the empowerment of young women.

Why it mattersPrincess Sarah Zeid of Jordan met with Parliament Speaker Margarida Adamugi Talapa to discuss UN support for mothers and children in Cabo Delgado.
