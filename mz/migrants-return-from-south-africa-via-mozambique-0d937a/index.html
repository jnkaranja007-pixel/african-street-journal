Migrants Return From South Africa Via Mozambique

At least 15,394 migrants returned from South Africa between April and July 2026 due to violence and anti-immigration protests.

At least 15,394 migrants returned from South Africa, passing through Mozambique, between April and July 2026. The International Organization for Migration (IOM), cited by Lusa, reported that violence, protests, and fears associated with anti-immigration demonstrations in South Africa prompted these returns. The data comes from the Migration Flows Monitoring Survey.

The survey indicates a significant movement of people seeking to leave South Africa during this period. The reasons cited by the IOM point to a deteriorating security and social climate within South Africa that is directly impacting migrant populations. Mozambique served as a transit country for many of these individuals.

This influx of returning migrants presents challenges and opportunities for Mozambique. The scale of the returnees suggests a need for humanitarian assistance and integration support. The situation highlights the volatile conditions faced by migrants in neighboring countries and the resulting cross-border movements.

Why it mattersMigrants are directly affected by violence and anti-immigration sentiment in South Africa, forcing their return through Mozambique.
