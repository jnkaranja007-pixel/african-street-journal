Mozambique plans private rail operators

State-owned CFM seeks private firms to run sections of national rail network to boost freight.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-02 · 1 min read

Ports and Railways of Mozambique (CFM) is preparing tenders to allow private companies to operate parts of the national rail network. This move aims to attract more freight traffic and strengthen Mozambique's role as a transit hub for neighbouring countries.

CFM, which currently holds a monopoly, will remain the infrastructure manager while private firms operate services under concession. Mozambique's railways carried 3.6 million metric tons of goods in the first quarter of 2026, an increase of 14.9% from the previous year.

The plan follows a 14-billion-metical investment programme announced in April 2025 to upgrade infrastructure, including new locomotives and freight cars. In July 2025, Mozambique secured €144 million to improve the railway linking Maputo to the South African border, aiming to increase freight capacity to 19 million metric tons annually.

Why it mattersImporters and exporters will have more options for moving goods through Mozambique's rail network.
