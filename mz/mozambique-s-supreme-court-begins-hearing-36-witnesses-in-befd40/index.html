Mozambique's Supreme Court begins hearing 36 witnesses in Mondlane trial

The former presidential candidate faces five criminal charges, including incitement to terrorism, in a case heard at the court in Maputo.

Sources: Carta de Mocambique and O Pais · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Mozambique's Supreme Court began hearing witnesses on Wednesday in the trial of Venâncio Mondlane, a former presidential candidate charged with five crimes, including incitement to terrorism. The case is being heard in Maputo, and 36 witnesses are listed: 12 for the public prosecutor, four for the Mozambican road network company REVIMO, which joined the case as an assisting party, and 20 for Mondlane's defence.

The court is also due to hear three experts, including two from the mobile operators Tmcel and Vodacom. A technical expert from the Criminal Investigation Service (SERNIC), requested by prosecutors, is to assess the authenticity of an audio recording said to feature Mondlane and Albino Forquilha, leader of the opposition party PODEMOS (the Mozambican Democratic Party), discussing a plan to subvert the rule of law. Two declarants requested by the defence, activist Graça Machel and Brazilian businesswoman Samanta Lemos, are also listed; Lemos is to testify virtually.

Prosecutors' 12 witnesses were expected to testify over Wednesday and Thursday, followed by REVIMO's witnesses on Friday. Defence witnesses could begin on Monday, according to the report.

Why it mattersThe prosecution, REVIMO and Mondlane's defence must each put forward their witnesses, with the defence's 20 expected to begin on Monday.
