Mozambique's Supreme Court questions Mondlane over 2024 unrest

The former presidential candidate said his broadcasts sought support for electoral truth and justice, not destructive acts attributed to them.

Source: Carta de Mocambique · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Mozambique’s Supreme Court questioned former presidential candidate Venâncio António Bila Mondlane on Thursday about whether he understood that his live broadcasts could have consequences for street events in October, November and December 2024. The hearing in Process No. 52/2025P entered its third day, with five criminal-section judges asking about damage to public and private property, fear, constitutional subversion, police deaths and economic harm.

Mondlane said he had never intended such actions, describing parts of his speeches as metaphorical and intended to mobilise supporters for what he called electoral truth and justice for the murder of his lawyer, Elvino Dias. He argued that the subversion allegation stemmed from pre-existing ideas and said the same theory was raised during the investigation.

Mondlane said the case was opened on 02 October 2024, during the election campaign. He said the national criminal investigation service sent it to prosecutors that day, and that a prosecutor named Paulo Armando Sitoe made him a defendant and sought telephone interception; the city court authorised it with reservations.

Why it mattersMondlane must answer the judges’ questions about whether his broadcasts foreseeably led to the acts prosecutors cite, including damage to property and economic harm.
