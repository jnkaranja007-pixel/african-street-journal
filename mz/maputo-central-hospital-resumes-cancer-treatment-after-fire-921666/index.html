Maputo Central Hospital resumes cancer treatment after fire

95 percent of patient records were saved from the blaze that damaged the oncology unit.

Source: Carta de Mocambique · 2026-11-03 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The Central Hospital of Maputo, Mozambique's largest health facility, has resumed outpatient oncology services in the Dermatology Department's building after a fire on Sunday. The hospital's Clinical Director, Dércio Fernandes, stated that 95% of patient records were preserved in the building's basement, unaffected by the flames or firefighting water. Teams are now working to reconstruct damaged records to restart chemotherapy administration as soon as possible.

Fernandes reported that the 22 patients admitted to the oncology ward before the fire were safely transferred, with 10 moved to the Dermatology ward. On Monday, the service saw 60 patients for consultations, 12 for emergencies, and 20 for outpatient chemotherapy. However, these chemotherapy patients had to wait for new records to be created as their scheduled documents were destroyed.

The hospital aims to quickly restore daily treatments for the 30 to 40 patients who previously received day-hospital care. Specialized chemotherapy chairs have been moved to the Dermatology building to facilitate this. Despite challenges from the relocation and service reorganization, nursing, psychology, and medical teams are mobilized to ensure patient care continues.

Why it mattersCancer patients in Mozambique can now access chemotherapy and other oncology services at the Central Hospital of Maputo following a temporary relocation of services.
