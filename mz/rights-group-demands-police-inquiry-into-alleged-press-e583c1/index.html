Rights group demands police inquiry into alleged press attacks at Mondlane trial

The group says a police officer took a journalist’s microphone and assaulted a camera operator near Mozambique’s Supreme Court in Maputo.

Source: Noticias Lusofonas · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Mozambique’s Human Rights Defenders Network (RMDDH) has demanded a police investigation after journalists were allegedly attacked near the Supreme Court in Maputo, where politician Venâncio Mondlane is on trial. The group said a police officer forcibly took a microphone from a journalist and assaulted a camera operator.

Citing footage shared on social media, RMDDH called on the national police command to conduct a swift, independent and impartial inquiry, publicly explain what happened and identify and hold the officer responsible. The trial, which began on Tuesday, concerns five alleged crimes linked to post-election protests, including incitement to terrorism.

Prosecutors cite videos, phone recordings, bank data and documents about Mondlane’s movements in support of the charges. Mondlane has never accepted the October 2024 general election results, alleging fraud; prosecutors accuse him of contributing to the instability and violence that followed.

Why it mattersJournalists covering the trial face alleged interference, and the national police command is being pressed to identify the officer and explain the incident publicly.
