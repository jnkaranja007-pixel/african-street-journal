Somalia President Rejects Opposition Election Talks

President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud dismissed calls for consensus on upcoming elections, proceeding with government plans despite opposition warnings.

Source: Somali Guardian · 2026-10-21 · 1 min read

President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud has rejected calls for negotiations with opposition leaders regarding Somalia’s upcoming elections, stating his government’s plans will proceed as scheduled. The president dismissed demands for a consensus-based electoral process, asserting his government’s control over the arrangements. This stance signals a refusal to engage with opposition figures and regional states who advocate for an inclusive approach to ensure free and fair elections.

The government-appointed electoral commission announced that the presidential election is set for May, with parliamentary elections planned for April of the following year. President Mohamud affirmed that all electoral activities will follow his administration's timeline, and any opposition to the proposed process will be unsuccessful. This unilateral decision has been condemned by opposition leaders and the regional administrations of Puntland and Jubaland, who argue that a consensus-driven process is essential for legitimate elections.

Opposition leaders and the Puntland and Jubaland administrations have threatened to establish a parallel government if President Mohamud proceeds with a unilateral electoral process that excludes opponents and remains under his control. This political standoff risks exacerbating divisions between the federal government, opposition factions, and regional administrations, potentially leading to a broader political confrontation amid ongoing state-building efforts.
