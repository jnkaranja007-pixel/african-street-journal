Six Somali judges take presidential term dispute to East African court

The judges challenged the federal government after President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud rejected a ruling that his mandate expired on 15 May.

Source: Somali Guardian · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Six Somali Supreme Court judges, including ousted Chief Justice Baashe Yusuf Ahmed, filed a case against the federal government at the East African Court of Justice in Arusha on 18 November. They challenged President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud’s rejection of a 5 November ruling that his four-year mandate expired on 15 May 2026.

The court gave Somalia’s government 45 days to respond. The judges filed after security forces deployed around the Supreme Court, their personal guards were withdrawn and they were barred from travelling. They later took refuge at the Mogadishu residence of former President Sharif Sheikh Ahmed.

The Supreme Court had also ruled that parliamentary mandates ended on 14 April 2026 and ordered political leaders to agree on an electoral framework within 30 days, with elections within 90 days. The presidency says the constitutional amendments extending terms to five years support a move to direct elections.

Why it mattersThe federal government must file its response to the judges’ case within 45 days.
