Garissa Activists Launch Campaign Against FGM and School Dropouts

Human rights groups, government officials, and community leaders are partnering to combat female genital mutilation and address rising school absenteeism.

Human rights activists in Garissa have joined forces with the national government and local leaders to initiate a campaign targeting female genital mutilation (FGM) and the increasing number of school-age children not attending classes. The initiative, organized by the non-governmental organization Girlkind, involves police, administrators, religious figures, elders, women, youth, and boda boda riders. This collaborative effort aims to directly reach residents with its message.

The campaign represents a departure from traditional awareness methods, as organizers are proactively engaging communities in their neighborhoods rather than relying on public meetings. This direct approach was highlighted during a recent meeting in Iftin town, which served as a focal point for the initiative. Girlkind programme officer Hamas Abdi stated the organization's commitment to meeting people where they live.

The partnership seeks to raise awareness and implement strategies to reduce the prevalence of FGM and encourage school attendance. By involving a wide array of community stakeholders, the campaign aims to foster a collective responsibility towards protecting children's rights and ensuring access to education within Garissa County.

Why it mattersThe campaign affects children and families in Garissa County by addressing harmful traditional practices and promoting education.
