Somalia supreme court invalidates constitution changes

The court ordered elections within 90 days after striking down mandate extensions for the president and parliament.

Somalia’s Supreme Court on Thursday invalidated amendments to the 2012 Provisional Constitution, nullifying the extended mandates of the presidency and parliament. The court ordered that elections be held within 90 days.

This ruling followed President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud’s dismissal of Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf and his appointment of Ibrahim Eidle Suleiman, the president’s former legal adviser, as replacement. Five Supreme Court judges delivered the verdict, stating parliament ratified the amendments after its mandate expired, lacking legal authority for such changes.

Security forces stormed the Supreme Court premises shortly after the verdict and forced the chief justice out. The opposition welcomed the judgment and condemned the government’s actions as unlawful.

Why it mattersThe Supreme Court's ruling invalidates constitutional amendments and orders new elections, directly affecting the political leadership and electoral process in Somalia.

Turkish airstrike kills Somali woman in Jameeco

Hawo Madow was preparing tea when the strike hit her village, 160 kilometres northwest of Mogadishu.

An elderly Somali woman, Hawo Madow, died on October 27 when a Turkish airstrike hit the village of Jameeco. The strike occurred approximately 160 kilometres northwest of Mogadishu. Five people were affected by the attack, with another young man in critical condition.

Suley Ibrahim Dhere, a village resident, stated that Turkish aircraft carried out the bombardment. He said the village had done nothing to provoke them. Hawo Madow’s husband, Nasir, confirmed his wife was killed and two others wounded, stating no Al-Shabaab fighters were present.

Hawo Madow, who left behind five children, was preparing tea when the aircraft struck. Her niece, Mumino Osman Bashow, said the villagers were struggling with drought and dismissed claims that militants were being targeted. The attack followed an ambush on a Somali National Army convoy near Jameeco.

Why it mattersHawo Madow and other civilians in Jameeco were killed or injured by a Turkish airstrike while going about their daily lives.

Somalia rejects Israeli ambassador, planned embassy

Mogadishu calls moves in Hargeisa a violation of sovereignty, demanding reversal.

Somalia's Foreign Ministry rejected Israel’s appointment of an ambassador to Somaliland and plans for an embassy in Hargeisa on Tuesday. The ministry stated these actions were taken without the Federal Government's consent and are therefore "null and void and without legal effect."

Michael Lotem, Israel’s first ambassador to Somaliland, presented his credentials to Somaliland President Abdirahman Mohamed Abdullahi Irro in Hargeisa. A ceremony was also held to lay the cornerstone for a planned Israeli embassy. Somalia maintains Somaliland is part of its territory and lacks authority for independent diplomatic relations.

Israel formally recognised Somaliland on December 26, 2025, becoming the first UN member state to do so since the region declared independence in 1991. Somalia urged the international community not to recognise steps undermining its territorial integrity and warned of heightened tensions in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden region.

Why it mattersSomalia's territorial integrity and its standing in the region are directly affected by Israel's recognition of Somaliland and establishment of diplomatic ties.
