US forces pull out of Baledogle airfield over runway safety

Resurfacing the deteriorating runway would have cost tens of millions of dollars, US Africa Command said.

The United States has confirmed that its troops have left Baledogle Military Airfield in southern Somalia. Army Lieutenant General John Brennan, deputy commander of US Africa Command (AFRICOM), called the move a purely economic decision after the runway surface declined to the point where landing aircraft there had become dangerous.

Brennan said most personnel previously based at the site have been moved to Mogadishu and that the departure does not reduce American support for Somali forces fighting Al-Shabaab. US airstrikes against the group have continued in recent weeks, including in Lower Shabelle. Baledogle, about 90 to 100 kilometres northwest of Mogadishu, had served as a major hub for US counter-terrorism operations and training of Somalia's Danab special forces since around 2012.

AFRICOM has said a return remains possible if conditions change, though no firm plans have been announced. Ugandan and Somali forces are reported to remain at the site.

Why it mattersPersonnel have been moved to Mogadishu, and US strikes against Al-Shabaab continue elsewhere, so Somali forces keep American air support.

Security · 2026-11-13

Turkey denies plans to send 15,000 troops to Somalia

Ankara says its existing forces will keep to training and advisory work, after Uganda began pulling its own troops out of Somalia.

Turkey has rejected reports that it plans to send 15,000 troops to Somalia, saying the claims do not reflect the truth. The Ministry of National Defense said on Thursday that its forces already in the country would continue their training and advisory mission. Ministry spokesperson Rear Admiral Zeki Aktürk made the denial at the weekly press briefing in Istanbul.

Aktürk said the Somalia-Turkish Task Force and the Air Element Command already deployed there continue to help build Somalia's counter-terrorism capacity through military assistance, training and advisory activities. The 15,000 figure first appeared in early November in Somali media, which cited unnamed security sources and said Turkey had agreed to deploy soldiers before January 2027. Neither government had confirmed the numbers before the denial.

The reports followed Uganda's decision to begin withdrawing its forces after nearly 20 years, with all Ugandan personnel due to leave by the end of 2027. Turkey's mandate in Somalia has been extended through July 2028, and its forces are centred on Camp TURKSOM in Mogadishu.

Why it mattersSomali officials fear a security gap as Uganda withdraws, and the denial leaves Turkey's existing training mission at Camp TURKSOM as the only confirmed foreign military support.

Politics · 2026-11-13

Cruz ties Somalia envoy confirmation to Somaliland recognition

Senator Ted Cruz says his support for the US ambassador nominee to Somalia depends on Washington formally recognising Somaliland.

Senator Ted Cruz, a Texas Republican, said on Tuesday he will condition his support for confirming Devin Kennington as US ambassador to Somalia on the State Department formally recognising Somaliland. At a Senate Foreign Relations Committee hearing, Cruz pressed Kennington on President Donald Trump's description of Somaliland as a totally different country from Somalia.

Kennington said conditions on the ground in Somaliland were clearly different and that the administration wanted deeper engagement with authorities in Hargeisa on security, commercial and regional matters. He did not endorse formal recognition and restated that US policy recognises Somalia's sovereignty and territorial integrity, including over Somaliland. Cruz told him that confirmation would depend on the department following the president's guidance.

No date has been set for a committee vote on Kennington's nomination. Somaliland declared independence from Somalia in 1991 and is not widely recognised internationally, and Somalia's federal government continues to assert sovereignty over the territory.

Why it mattersKennington's nomination now hinges on a recognition question that the State Department has not moved on, leaving the ambassador post to Somalia without a confirmation timetable.
