Somali army suffers heavy losses in Al-Shabaab ambush

Hundreds of soldiers feared dead or wounded after militants attacked advancing forces near Burhakabo.

Somali army forces suffered significant casualties on Monday during an Al-Shabaab assault in the country's southwest. An estimated 2,000 troops advancing on Burhakabo were attacked in Jame’o village by militants using suicide car bombs. Al-Shabaab claimed to have killed 413 soldiers and wounded over 400, though these figures could not be independently verified.

The military's worst reverse in years saw troops fleeing within hours, with some remaining stranded in rural areas without a rescue operation, according to journalist Abdirizak Atosh. Military court chief Hassan Ali Nur Shute and ground forces commander Sahal Abdullahi Omar have opened an inquiry at Baledogle air base into the debacle. Commanders who abandoned the battlefield or military vehicles will face disciplinary action.

This rout is considered the army’s most severe defeat by insurgents since the 2023 Oswayne attack. Al-Shabaab also claimed that a Turkish air strike killed 15 captured special forces troops receiving medical treatment. Turkey reportedly destroyed some of its donated Kirpi armoured vehicles to prevent their propaganda use by militants, who claimed to have captured numerous army vehicles.

Why it mattersHundreds of Somali soldiers are dead or wounded following a major Al-Shabaab attack, impacting the nation's security forces.

Fugitive Duqow saves 40 Somali soldiers

Duqow, a man sentenced to death, rescued soldiers lost after fighting Al-Shabaab in Lower Shabelle.

A fugitive named Duqow rescued over 40 Somali Federal Government soldiers who were stranded for two nights in the Jameeco area of the Lower Shabelle region. The troops became lost after a battle with Al-Shabaab militants. Duqow and Macawiisley fighters found the soldiers, gave them food, and guided them to safety in Yaaqbiriweyne district.

Duqow, who has been on the run for nearly a year, was sentenced to death by military court judge Judge Shuute. He denied disarming the soldiers, stating he provided refreshments and contacted their commander. Duqow said he harbored no malice, declaring, “The federal government is my government and I have no intention of seeking revenge.”

The Somali Federal Government has not yet officially commented on the incident. Duqow’s current legal standing and the military’s official response remain unclear following the rescue.

Why it mattersDuqow, a fugitive facing execution, saved government soldiers who had battled Al-Shabaab.

AU troops to leave four bases in Somalia

Peacekeepers are pulling out of Lower Shabelle region due to financial strain, raising security concerns.

African Union peacekeepers will leave four bases in Somalia’s Lower Shabelle region next week due to severe financial strain. The withdrawal follows Washington's agreement to extend support for the mission until December 2027, after it had threatened to cut logistical assistance.

The mission has not specified which bases will close, but analysts warn that Somali forces may not be able to secure the sites quickly enough to prevent Al-Shabaab militants from taking them. Lower Shabelle is a strategic area southwest of the capital, Mogadishu, and AU deployments there have helped shield the city.

Somalia’s President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud urged Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni to keep Ugandan troops in Somalia during a visit this week. This comes days after Al-Shabaab fighters ambushed Somali government forces near Jameeco, killing hundreds of soldiers.

Why it mattersSomali forces may struggle to secure bases vacated by AU troops, potentially allowing Al-Shabaab to gain ground.
