Al-Shabaab seizes Coomaad village near Mataban in central Somalia

Militants took the settlement after army troops pulled back from brief clashes, extending a run of gains in the region.

Al-Shabaab fighters seized the village of Coomaad, roughly 30km from the strategic town of Mataban in Somalia's Hiiraan region, local media reported on Sunday. Army soldiers pulled out after short fighting, leaving the militants in control of a settlement on a key road that links central Somali towns.

The group has steadily regained rural areas and army posts between Mahas and Mataban in recent months, and Coomaad is its latest success. Towns in Hiiraan and neighbouring Galgadud have seen few major militant attacks for four years, and the advance has raised fears for them.

Al-Shabaab recently took Warxoolo near Guriel as well. The Hawadle clan, whose Mawisley militias have fought the group for years, is at odds with the federal government over the leadership of Hirshabelle state.

Why it mattersTraders and travellers on the road linking central Somali towns now face a militant presence at Coomaad, and residents of Hiiraan and Galgadud face renewed attacks after years of calm.

UN experts condemn president's removal of chief justice

UN experts call Somalia president's dismissal of chief justice a blatant interference with judicial independence.

United Nations independent experts have condemned President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud’s dismissal of Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed as a “blatant interference with the independence of the judiciary.” The experts stated on November 12 that the presidential decree of November 4 was a deliberate circumvention of constitutional safeguards, as no proposal was sought from the Judicial Services Commission nor parliamentary approval obtained.

The experts, including Special Rapporteur Margaret Satterthwaite, noted that a replacement chief justice was sworn in three days later with the President present, further eroding the rule of law. On November 5, five Supreme Court judges had ruled the term extensions unlawful and declared the mandates of the President and Parliament expired, ordering elections within 90 days. The same bench found the removal of Chief Justice Ahmed unconstitutional and calculated to obstruct their examination of the term-extension measures.

The government refused to comply with the court's rulings, deploying security personnel to the Supreme Court and withdrawing protection from seven judges who were then barred from travel. The experts condemned these actions as attempted intimidation and serious threats to the judges' security, calling for the immediate restoration of protection and withdrawal of security forces. They reminded Somalia that judicial independence is an essential guarantee of the rule of law and democracy.

Why it mattersUN experts condemned the dismissal of Somalia's Chief Justice and the deployment of security forces to the Supreme Court, citing threats to judicial independence and democracy.

Turkey denies plans to send 15,000 troops to Somalia

The Turkish Ministry of National Defense said its existing forces in Somalia will continue their training and advisory mission.

Turkey has denied reports that it plans to send 15,000 troops to Somalia, saying the claims do not reflect the truth. Ministry of National Defense spokesperson Rear Admiral Zeki Aktürk said on Thursday that Turkish forces already in the country will keep up their training and advisory work. He made the statement at a weekly press briefing aboard the TCG Savarona in Istanbul.

The 15,000 figure first appeared in Somali media in early November. Gaylan Media, citing unnamed security sources, reported that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud had asked allies for 20,000 foreign troops and that Turkey had agreed to deploy 15,000 soldiers to Barawe, Baidoa and Mogadishu before January 2027. Neither government had confirmed the numbers before the denial.

The reports came after Uganda began withdrawing its forces from Somalia after nearly 20 years, with all Ugandan personnel due to leave by the end of 2027. Turkey's main base is Camp TURKSOM in Mogadishu, where its trainers have helped build Somali National Army units, under a 10-year defence cooperation agreement signed in February 2024.

Why it mattersTurkish-trained Somali National Army units and the Camp TURKSOM base in Mogadishu stay in place, with Turkey's parliament having extended the mandate for its forces through July 2028.

Somalia's president asks to visit Uganda again within two weeks

Hassan Sheikh Mohamud wants a one-day meeting with Yoweri Museveni, his second trip to Uganda in a fortnight.

Mogadishu has asked for a one-day visit by Somalia's President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud to Kampala, where he would meet President Yoweri Museveni, The Somali Stream reported on Sunday. The trip would be his second to Uganda in a fortnight.

On his late October visit, Mohamud asked Museveni to hold off pulling out Ugandan troops that are holding key strategic towns in the Lower Shabelle region under a bilateral agreement. Museveni turned the request down, and the drawdown has already begun. Uganda has been one of the largest troop contributors to African Union missions in Somalia since 2007, and it keeps a separate bilateral presence in sensitive areas.

It is not clear whether Mohamud would repeat the troop request if the visit is approved. The outreach comes as his government faces a Supreme Court ruling that the extension of his term was unlawful, and Mohamud has sent two special advisers to several countries to seek financial support from key African Union and European Union partners.

Why it mattersUgandan troops leaving the Lower Shabelle region will change which forces hold key towns there, and the outcome of the visit will decide whether that drawdown is reversed.
