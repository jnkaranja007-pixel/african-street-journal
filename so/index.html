Somaliland President Pitches Region's Value to US Amid Rival Somali Delegation

President Irro travels to the United States to highlight Somaliland's strategic importance, while Somalia's federal government sends its own delegation.

Somaliland President Abdirahman Mohamed Abdullahi Irro departed Hargeisa on Thursday for the United States, aiming to promote his self-declared republic's geoeconomic and geosecurity significance, particularly its proximity to the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint. Officials stated Irro will stop in the United Arab Emirates before reaching the US. The president plans to emphasize Somaliland's rare earth mineral deposits and its strategic location near Yemen, which could support a US military presence and counter Houthi threats.

In parallel, Somalia's federal government has dispatched a delegation led by Deputy Prime Minister Salah Jama to Washington. This counter-diplomacy effort seeks to undermine Irro's mission. The competing diplomatic efforts highlight familial connections between the two delegations, with analyst Rashid Abdi noting that Jama is from Somaliland and related to Irro.

The Somaliland leader's visit focuses on presenting the territory as an emerging power to Washington and other Western capitals. The dual approach by Somaliland and the federal government underscores ongoing diplomatic maneuvering over regional influence and strategic partnerships.

Why it mattersSomaliland's leadership is seeking to establish strategic partnerships with the US, highlighting its resources and location.

Somali government shuts down 82,000 extremist social media pages

Mogadishu is intensifying its ideological campaign against armed groups by closing online platforms used for propaganda.

Somalia's federal government has closed 82,000 social media pages in 2026 that were allegedly used by terrorist groups to spread extremist propaganda. Abdullahi Hayir Duale, director general of the Ministry of Information, stated that the government is intensifying its ideological and public awareness campaign against armed groups. He described the fight against extremist groups as having three main fronts: military, economic, and ideological, emphasizing that ideological warfare is crucial for influencing large populations and is a key part of the government’s counterterrorism strategy.

Duale noted that the Ministry of Information has closed thousands of pages in recent years across platforms including Facebook, TikTok, and Telegram, and that the number of pages shut down this year continues to rise as 2026 is not yet over. He also acknowledged that extremist groups often re-emerge on social media using new names and pages after their accounts are removed, indicating an ongoing challenge.

To counter extremist ideologies, the Ministry of Information is utilizing national media, artists, television programs, and Somali religious scholars to educate the public. Duale highlighted that national media platforms reach millions of people both inside and outside Somalia, which aids the government in combating the spread of extremist narratives. This multi-pronged approach aims to address the ideological front of the conflict.

Why it mattersThe government's closure of social media pages affects the public by attempting to limit exposure to extremist propaganda and counter terrorism narratives.

Somalia Holds First Regional Anti-FGM Hearing

Mogadishu hosted a two-day public consultation on a proposed East African Community bill to combat female genital mutilation.

Somali officials, lawmakers, religious leaders, and civil society representatives met in Mogadishu to discuss a proposed East African Community legislation aimed at creating a regional framework to combat female genital mutilation (FGM). This two-day public hearing marked Somalia’s first in-country consultation with members of the East African Legislative Assembly (EALA) since joining the regional bloc.

The consultation is particularly significant as an estimated 99% of Somali women and girls have undergone FGM, according to a 2020 survey, and the country currently lacks a specific national law prohibiting the practice. "FGM being the subject of the EAC Elimination of FGM Bill, 2025, it was important for EALA to get the opinions and recommendations of the Somali government and citizens on the provisions of the law," stated Caroline Lagat, legal adviser for End Harmful Practices at Equality Now, which organized the event.

Participants reviewed the bill clause by clause, focusing on strengthening protection mechanisms, cross-border cooperation, and coordinated regional action against FGM. EALA member Falhada Dekow Iman, a sponsor of the bill, emphasized that the legislation should not be perceived as an attack on communities or their traditions. The hybrid hearing brought together representatives from federal government institutions, Somali and EALA lawmakers, and various civil society groups.

Why it mattersSomali women and girls are affected by the proposed regional legislation to combat female genital mutilation, a practice with 99% prevalence in the country.

Somalia and Qatar University Sign Deal for Technology University

A cooperation agreement between Somalia's Ministry of Education and the University of Doha for Science and Technology will establish a new university focused on technical.

Somalia's Ministry of Education, Culture and Higher Education has signed a cooperation agreement with the University of Doha for Science and Technology (UDST) to establish Somalia’s Technology University (TUS University). The pact, signed in Doha, outlines a framework for developing high-quality technical and vocational education, with UDST providing support for curriculum development, teacher training, quality assurance, and certification.

The initial phase of the project will concentrate on critical sectors such as technology, marine sciences, aviation, petroleum, engineering, energy, and health management. This initiative is part of Somalia’s National Transformation Plan 2025–2029, aiming to enhance educational quality and equip Somali youth with skills relevant to the labor market. A significant focus will be placed on training Somali teachers to staff the new institution.

The agreement was signed by Mohamed Abdullahi Mahmoud, director general of higher education for Somalia, and Prof. Rashid Benlamri, UDST’s vice president for education. The ceremony was attended by Somalia’s minister of education, culture and higher education, the Somali ambassador to Qatar, and senior UDST officials. The ministry expressed gratitude for Qatar's ongoing support.

Why it mattersSomali youth will gain specialized technical and vocational skills aligned with labor market needs through the establishment of a new technology university.

Egypt Pursues Release of Sailors Held Off Somalia

Cairo's Foreign Ministry is engaged in intensive diplomatic efforts and contacts with Somali and Yemeni authorities to secure the swift release of detained Egyptian sailors.

Egypt's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, International Cooperation, and Egyptian Expatriates confirmed on Thursday that it is undertaking intensive efforts and contacts to achieve the swift release of Egyptian sailors held aboard a vessel off the Somali coast. Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty is personally monitoring the situation and has directed continuous efforts on all fronts to ensure the sailors' safety and prompt release.

The ministry stated that ongoing intensive contacts are being made with relevant Somali and Yemeni authorities to follow developments and push for the sailors’ release. These efforts also aim to ensure their well-being, meet their basic needs, and provide adequate living conditions until the crisis is resolved and they can return home safely. The ministry is also in contact with the vessel's owning company to ensure their release and compliance with international law.

In late August, the Egyptian Foreign Minister confirmed Cairo's ongoing intensive efforts to secure the sailors' release. He stated that Somali authorities are in daily contact with the captors and are utilizing their strong tribal ties in the area where the vessel is held to facilitate the sailors' freedom. The ministry is also maintaining regular communication with the sailors' families to update them on the progress of these efforts.

Why it mattersEgyptian sailors are directly affected by being detained aboard a vessel off the Somali coast, facing uncertainty about their safety and well-being.
