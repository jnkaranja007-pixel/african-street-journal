Somali intelligence commander defects to Al-Shabaab

A senior NISA commander reportedly took sensitive intelligence files and equipment when he joined the militant group.

A senior commander of Somalia’s National Intelligence and Security Agency (NISA) paramilitary Duufaan unit has defected to Al-Shabaab. The commander, identified as Luqman, led the Turkish-trained unit and is believed to have taken laptops, communication gadgets, and files with him to Al-Shabab-controlled territory in southern Somalia.

Analyst Rashid Abdi stated that NISA is now severely compromised, with Al-Shabaab potentially gaining access to critical intelligence data, including names and addresses of informants in sensitive areas. Abdi noted that NISA is attempting to manage the crisis, but it may be too late.

The defection escalates an intelligence war within Somalia. The Duufaan unit was trained by Turkey and operated under Somalia’s spy agency.

Why it mattersSomalia's national security is threatened by a potential intelligence breach following the defection of a senior NISA commander.

Somalia court ruling declares president’s term extension illegal

The Supreme Court chairman and judges are being pursued after ruling that the president and parliament mandates have lapsed.

Somalia’s opposition Future Council condemned an alleged takeover of the Supreme Court by the president, accusing his administration of seizing judicial headquarters and hunting down court leadership. Security forces are reportedly pursuing Supreme Court chairman Bashe Yusuf Ahmed and judges after they declared the president’s term extension illegal. The opposition coalition supported the court’s decision that the mandates for both the president and parliament have lapsed, calling for consensus-based elections within 90 days.

The Future Council characterized the ruling as a crucial opportunity to defuse political conflict and resolve the nation’s standoff. The council warned citizens of serious dangers to Somali statehood, governance, and national security, holding the term-expired president accountable for any fallout. The council also noted that parliament’s mandate expired April 14, 2026, and the presidential term ended May 15, 2026.

The opposition coalition declared all contracts, policy decisions, and legislative agreements enacted after May 15, 2026, null and void. They pledged a full review once legal supremacy is re-established. The council appealed to the public and international partners regarding the court’s timeline.

Why it mattersThe country faces potential instability and a review of all government actions taken since May 15, 2026, due to the Supreme Court ruling.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Hawadle clan chief calls for mobilization against federal offensive

Clan leader Ugas Yusuf Ugas Hassan urged Hawadle members to arm themselves amid reports of a federal troop buildup.

Hawadle clan chief Ugas Yusuf Ugas Hassan on Friday called for clan members and other Hiran region residents to mobilize against a perceived federal offensive. He stated that rival clans were exploiting the federal government's name to advance their interests. The appeal came as reports indicated the federal government was preparing 1,500 troops for an offensive to retake Baladweyne, which Hawadle militias seized weeks ago.

The Hawadle militias were mobilized by former National Intelligence and Security Agency chief Abdullahi Mohamed Ali Sanbalolshe and other Hawadle politicians opposing the federal government and Hirshabelle administration. This political rift follows President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's endorsement of Ali Gudlawe for a second term as Hirshabelle state president, a decision Hawadle politicians see as a broken promise. Tensions escalated after Djiboutian forces reportedly facilitated the entry of Hirshabelle Vice-President Abditah Hussein Afrah into Baladweyne.

On Saturday morning, some federal security agency members defected and seized control of Baladweyne airport, vowing to block any federal or Hirshabelle officials. Ugas Hassan also urged Somali National Army members of his clan to abandon federal allegiance. Sanbalolshe arrived in Baladweyne under security protection from Ethiopian forces, who were also sighted along the road connecting Ferfer to Baladweyne.

Why it mattersClan members and residents of the Hiran region are being called to arms by their chief, potentially leading to conflict.
