World Bank weighs suspending Somalia budget support after court ruling

The funding review follows a Supreme Court decision that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's term has expired.

Following a Supreme Court ruling on November 5, the World Bank is considering a temporary halt to budget support for Somalia. The court found that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's four-year term ended on May 15, 2026, and that term-extension amendments approved by parliament in March could not be applied backwards. Former President Sharif Sheikh Ahmed had brought the petition.

Political leaders have been ordered to agree an electoral framework within 30 days and hold elections within 90 days, with the government limited to routine caretaker duties. The court also voided Mohamud's dismissal of Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed. Opposition parties and the Puntland and Jubaland administrations reject the extension, and the dispute has already sparked armed clashes in Mogadishu.

Somalia's 2026 budget framework projects revenue and grants of about $1.3 billion, including roughly $470 million from domestic sources. Budget support has run at $150 million or more in recent years, with the World Bank as a leading provider. A suspension would hit the federal government's ability to pay recurrent costs, including salaries and transfers to federal member states.

Why it mattersThe federal government faces a funding pause that directly threatens its capacity to cover recurrent costs, including staff salaries and regional transfers.

Al-Shabaab seizes Coomaad village near Mataban in central Somalia

Militants took the settlement after army troops pulled back from brief clashes, extending a run of gains in the region.

Al-Shabaab fighters seized the village of Coomaad, roughly 30km from the strategic town of Mataban in Somalia's Hiiraan region, local media reported on Sunday. Army soldiers pulled out after short fighting, leaving the militants in control of a settlement on a key road that links central Somali towns.

The group has steadily regained rural areas and army posts between Mahas and Mataban in recent months, and Coomaad is its latest success. Towns in Hiiraan and neighbouring Galgadud have seen few major militant attacks for four years, and the advance has raised fears for them.

Al-Shabaab recently took Warxoolo near Guriel as well. The Hawadle clan, whose Mawisley militias have fought the group for years, is at odds with the federal government over the leadership of Hirshabelle state.

Why it mattersTraders and travellers on the road linking central Somali towns now face a militant presence at Coomaad, and residents of Hiiraan and Galgadud face renewed attacks after years of calm.

Security · 2026-11-17

Somaliland detains five Americans over security threat

The five US nationals left the territory on an Ethiopian Airlines flight after being monitored by the intelligence agency.

The national intelligence agency in the self-declared Republic of Somaliland briefly detained five US nationals over national security threats, according to a post on X late on Sunday. The National Intelligence Agency of Somaliland stated that it monitored the group since their arrival and conducted a lengthy investigation into their activities.

The five individuals work for Parallel Black, a company registered in the US state of Delaware less than two months ago, a person familiar with the matter stated. They entered the territory following an invitation by Somaliland Foreign Affairs Minister Abdirahman Dahir Adam via a November 4 letter to conduct feasibility studies for potential investments.

The five Americans departed on an Ethiopian Airlines flight on Monday afternoon. Representatives for Parallel Black, the National Intelligence Agency Director-General Jama Mohamoud Egal, the Somaliland Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and the US State Department did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Why it mattersFive US nationals working for Parallel Black were detained and then left Somaliland on a Monday afternoon flight following an intelligence investigation.
