Somali official survives alleged Turkish drone strike

Hassan Abdulkadir, a key strategist for Laftagaren's forces, escaped unharmed after his motorcycle was hit.

Hassan Abdulkadir, former internal security minister of Southwest State and a key figure in Laftagaren’s forces, survived an alleged Turkish drone strike on Wednesday night. The strike hit a motorcycle carrying two of Laftagaren’s fighters, killing them. Abdulkadir and another military figure, Mustafa Qoomaal, were unharmed in the attack in the Bay region.

It is unclear if the strike targeted Abdulkadir, who is considered the main strategist for Laftagaren’s guerrilla campaign. His forces recently recaptured towns including Qansah-dhere, Burhakabo, and Dinsor. This is the second air raid on a Somali opposition politician; General Mahad Abdirahman survived an F-16 strike on September 3.

Abdulkadir has been seen in towns recently retaken by Laftagaren’s forces. The federal government had previously launched an offensive in March that ousted the former regional president. Laftagaren’s forces continue to consolidate their control over the Bay region.

Why it mattersHassan Abdulkadir, a key strategist for Laftagaren's forces, and Mustafa Qoomaal were directly affected by an alleged drone strike.

Somali opposition challenges new constitution in court

Sharif Sheikh Ahmed seeks to annul the controversial charter, with the Supreme Court awaiting the government's defense.

Somalia's Supreme Court has set Thursday as the deadline for the federal government to respond to a legal challenge against the nation's new constitution. Opposition leader Sharif Sheikh Ahmed filed the case seeking to annul the charter, which regional states and opposition figures deem unilateral. They argue it was drafted without broad consensus and approved in March without opposition members of parliament present.

President Hassan Sheikh has praised the new constitution as a new chapter for Somalia, but Puntland, Jubaland, and the opposition continue to recognize the 2012 draft constitution. This earlier text was developed through a broad-based agreement among all political stakeholders. The current legal battle is seen by many as a test of the Supreme Court's independence, a quality long questioned due to alleged executive influence on judicial decisions.

The federal government aims to win the case or at least delay the court proceedings. The opposition's legal action highlights a deep political division over the nation's foundational law, with differing interpretations of legitimacy and national recognition.

Why it mattersThe new constitution's legality is being challenged in court, affecting the governance structure of Somalia.

Somalia police enter Hormuud headquarters

Officers entered the telecom company's offices in Mogadishu under a court warrant amid regional tensions.

Somalia's police entered two Hormuud company premises in Mogadishu on Sunday, including its headquarters in Bakara Market and its main management headquarters on the road leading to the airport. Investigations are underway under a court warrant.

Initial reports suggest the investigation is linked to recent developments in Southwest state, where forces loyal to former regional president Abdiasis Laftagaren have retaken major towns. The federal government had previously imposed economic sanctions on Mr Laftagaren’s administration and anyone who co-operates with it.

The federal government's March offensive had ousted Mr Laftagaren and his administration, reversing gains made by federal forces.

Why it mattersThe police raid on Hormuud headquarters is linked to political tensions and sanctions in Southwest state.
