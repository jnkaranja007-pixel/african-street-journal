Somalia seeks 20,000 foreign troops after Uganda withdrawal

Turkey pledges 15,000 soldiers to bolster defences and prevent militant advances.

Somalia's President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud has asked allies for 20,000 troops to replace departing African Union forces, following Uganda's abrupt withdrawal. The president fears militant groups will seize territory vacated by the troops. Turkey has agreed to send 15,000 soldiers to strategic locations including Mogadishu, Barawe, and Baidoa before January 2027.

The deployment is part of a defence agreement between Somalia and Turkey, which aims to convert airstrips in Barawe and Baidoa into military bases. This move comes amid government concerns about defending Mogadishu after the African Union forces depart next year. The departure of Ugandan troops from key defensive positions near the capital has increased fears of a security vacuum.

Somalia's government is reportedly considering severing ties with some influential countries to rely more on Turkey's military support. Relations with neighbours Ethiopia and Kenya are already strained. Kampala refused President Mohamud's request to delay its troop withdrawal, heightening anxiety in Mogadishu.

Why it mattersSomali citizens face increased insecurity and potential militant advances due to the withdrawal of African Union forces and the government's reliance on foreign military aid.

Hawadle clan chief calls for mobilization against federal offensive

Clan leader Ugas Yusuf Ugas Hassan urged Hawadle members and others in Hiran to arm themselves against a federal troop movement.

Hawadle clan chief Ugas Yusuf Ugas Hassan on Friday called for his clan members and other residents of the Hiran region to mobilize against a perceived federal offensive. He urged able-bodied men to gather at his home and prepare to defend their territory. This appeal follows reports that the federal government is preparing 1,500 troops from the Hananbure military base in Galgadud region to retake Baladweyne from Hawadle clan militias.

The Hawadle militias seized control of Baladweyne weeks ago, mobilized by former NISA chief Abdullahi Mohamed Ali Sanbalolshe and other politicians opposing the federal government and Hirshabelle administration. Tensions escalated after Djiboutian forces reportedly helped Hirshabelle Vice-President Abditah Hussein Afrah enter Baladweyne airport, leading to gunfire. On Saturday morning, federal security agents from the Hawadle clan defected and seized Baladweyne airport, vowing to block officials.

Ugas Hassan specifically called on Somali National Army members of the Hawadle clan to abandon federal allegiance and defend their clan and region. This call came as former NISA chief Sanbalolshe arrived in Baladweyne under security from Ethiopian forces, who were also seen securing the route from Ferfer. Sanbalolshe had previously warned that Hawadle soldiers would refuse to fight their kin.

Why it mattersThe Hawadle clan chief's call to arms and the subsequent defection of federal security agents directly affect Hawadle clan members and the Hiran region.

Somalia chief justice flees Mogadishu citing presidential threats

Bashe Yusuf Ahmed alleges President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud threatened his security after a ruling on the president's term.

Somalia's Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed stated on Saturday that he no longer feels safe in Mogadishu, claiming President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud has threatened his personal security. His remarks followed his dismissal by the president on Wednesday, an action later deemed unlawful by five Supreme Court judges. Troops have since occupied the Supreme Court building.

The chief justice and the five judges have sought refuge at the residence of former President Sharif Sheikh Ahmed after their security details were withdrawn. Bashe questioned how ordinary citizens could feel secure if senior officials faced threats in the capital, noting that only those with weapons felt safe.

Bashe expressed hope for a future where justice prevails and all citizens stand equal before the law. The ruling by the five judges declared the president's term expired and his constitutional amendments unlawful.

Why it mattersThe chief justice and senior judges are seeking refuge and feel unsafe due to alleged threats from the president following a court ruling.
