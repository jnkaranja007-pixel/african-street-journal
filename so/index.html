UN ends 13 years of support for Somalia's justice institutions

The United Nations Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) concluded its direct support program for justice institutions and prison services on August 25, 2026.

The United Nations has ended its 13-year direct support program for Somalia’s justice institutions and prison services, with UNTMIS officially concluding its operations on August 25, 2026. This marks a significant shift as the Federal Government of Somalia aims to take full ownership of its justice system development.

Justice and Constitutional Affairs Minister Hassan Moalim Mohamud thanked the UN and international partners for their support, which included training and capacity-building. He stated that the expertise gained would form a foundation for future development of Somalia’s justice system and prison services.

The transition coincides with a reduction in the overall UN presence in Somalia, including staff downsizing and asset sales by the UNSOS support mission, partly due to funding shortages. The UNTMIS mission will gradually transfer responsibilities to Somali authorities and the UN country team by October 2026.

Why it mattersSomali citizens will experience changes in how justice and correctional services are administered as the federal government assumes greater responsibility.

Somali forces kill five, wound ten in Mogadishu slum demolition

Federal security forces used heavy machine guns and F-16 jets to clear the Tookiyo settlement, leaving residents displaced and sparking condemnation from opposition leaders.

Somalia's federal security forces killed at least five people and wounded more than ten others in Mogadishu on Sunday during an operation to forcibly clear the Tookiyo settlement in the Yaqshid district. The action, which began shortly after midnight on Saturday, is part of a wider campaign of slum demolitions in the capital, according to local media reports.

Witnesses described security forces opening fire on the densely packed neighborhood after residents met advancing troops with stones. Disturbing video footage reportedly showed government 'technical' pickup trucks mounted with DShK heavy machine guns firing into the residential area. Journalists and opposition leaders also reported low-altitude flights by Turkish Air Force F-16 fighter jets over the neighborhood, which they characterized as a show of force.

Opposition leader and former President Sharif Sheikh Ahmed condemned the security units, accusing them of brutality against unarmed civilians. Residents and journalists allege that the land being cleared in Tookiyo has been sold to wealthy, politically connected buyers, including a relative of the president, a claim previously made regarding a similar demolition at Mogadishu’s Siinaay market.

Why it mattersResidents of the Tookiyo settlement lost their homes and faced violence, while others in similar informal settlements across Mogadishu now fear displacement.

Turkish language expands in Somalia, challenging Arabic's dominance

An estimated 100,000 Somalis now speak Turkish, a number growing rapidly due to expanding Turkish-run schools and increasing business ties with Turkish companies.

The Turkish language is gaining traction in Somalia, with approximately 100,000 Somalis now speaking it, according to language educators in Mogadishu. This growth is fueled by the expansion of Turkish-run schools across the capital and increasing business connections with Turkish firms.

Turkish-run Maarif schools in Mogadishu and Hargeisa teach Turkish alongside English and Arabic to over 1,000 students. Many graduates from these schools are becoming fluent in three languages, rather than the traditional two, reflecting a broader shift in Turkish-Somali relations over the past decade.

The demand for Turkish is also driven by employment opportunities, as many young Somalis learn the language to work with Turkish companies or to qualify for university admission in Turkey. While Arabic remains Somalia’s official second language, some educators believe Turkish could eventually rival its status.

Why it mattersSomalis are directly affected by the growing presence of Turkish language education and its potential impact on employment and higher education opportunities.

Somalia forms committee to boost skills and employment

The Federal Government of Somalia established the National Committee for Skills Development and Employment to coordinate efforts in equipping workers with market-relevant.

Mogadishu – Somalia's Federal Government has accelerated its national strategy for skills development and job creation, with the new National Committee for Skills Development and Employment holding its first meeting today. Representatives from 13 federal ministries convened to discuss a coordinated approach to address chronic unemployment, particularly among the estimated 70 percent of Somalis under the age of 30.

Prime Minister Hamza Abdi Barre appointed the committee on July 18, 2026, tasking it with coordinating federal efforts across sectors like Education, Labor, Agriculture, Commerce, and Telecommunications. The committee, chaired by the Prime Minister’s Office, aims to bridge the gap between education, training, and labor market needs by aligning programs with market demands and technological changes.

Officials outlined a comprehensive strategy focusing on digital technology, ICT, manufacturing, agriculture, livestock, and business innovation. This initiative prioritizes youth and women to foster entrepreneurship and technical skills, aiming to expand the tax base, attract investment, and create a more resilient economy, according to a senior official from the Prime Minister’s Office.

Why it mattersSomali youth and women are directly affected as the government aims to provide them with market-relevant skills and formal employment opportunities.

Politics · 08/26/2026

Somalia, Pakistan sign defense pact, deepening military ties

Somalia and Pakistan have established their most extensive defense agreement in over three decades, focusing on military training and expertise exchange without deploying.

Somalia and Pakistan have signed their broadest defense agreement in over three decades, re-establishing a military relationship last seen before the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu. The agreement, signed on August four by Somalia’s Defence Minister, Ahmed Moallim Fiqi, and his Pakistani counterpart, Khawaja Muhammad Asif, covers counterterrorism cooperation, military training, and the exchange of military expertise.

Following the signing, Minister Fiqi led a delegation, including the commander of the Somali Navy and Coast Guard, to Pakistan’s General Headquarters in Rawalpindi. There, they met with Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir, Pakistan’s Chief of Army Staff, to discuss regional security and enhance bilateral defense cooperation. The delegation also met with Pakistan’s Chief of Naval Staff and Air Chief Marshal to discuss maritime security and support for the Somali Air Force.

Despite speculation, officials from both nations have clarified that the pact does not involve sending Pakistani combat troops to Somalia. The agreement focuses on institutional support, with the Somali delegation touring Pakistan’s National Defence University and Global Industrial & Defence Solutions, a state-owned military equipment company. This marks a significant step in rebuilding Somalia’s defense capacity through training and expertise rather than direct troop deployment.

Why it mattersThis defense pact directly affects Somali citizens by enhancing the capabilities of their armed forces, potentially improving national security and stability.
