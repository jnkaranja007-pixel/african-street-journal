Al-Shabaab attack kills eight near Somalia border

Suspected militants ambushed a vehicle carrying construction workers and police reservists in Kenya's Mandera County.

Eight people died in northeast Kenya on Tuesday when suspected Al-Shabaab militants attacked a vehicle near the Somali border. The victims included two National Police Reservists, five construction workers, and a driver. The attack occurred around 7 a.m. in Hagarsu, along the Mandera-Arabia-Fino-Lafey-Elwak road.

Mandera County Police Commander Robinson Ndiwa stated the vehicle was sprayed with bullets as the occupants headed to work. Heavily armed assailants reportedly blocked the road before opening fire. Ndiwa confirmed that investigations have begun and security agencies are pursuing the attackers, who retreated into nearby thickets.

This region has a history of attacks, including a 2014 incident where 36 quarry miners were killed. In December 2019, Al-Shabaab militants used improvised explosive devices to destroy road construction machinery valued at Sh39.8 million in the same area.

Why it mattersEight people were killed in a militant attack near the Kenya-Somalia border, impacting local security and livelihoods.

Somali soldiers desert frontlines after clan leader's call

Dozens of soldiers from the Hawadle clan have returned to Beledweyne following a directive from their traditional leader.

Somalia's army is experiencing significant desertions as soldiers from the Hawadle clan abandon frontlines. This follows a call from their clan leader, Ugas Yusuf Ugas Hassan, for servicemen to withdraw and assemble at his residence in Beledweyne. Footage shows numerous Somali National Army (SNA) soldiers arriving at the clan leader's home over the past two days.

The departures pose a challenge to the federal government's efforts to maintain a unified national force amid clan tensions. This situation arises as African Union and Ugandan forces withdraw, increasing pressure on Somalia's military. The reliability of frontline deployments and the army's capacity to fight Al-Shabaab are now questioned.

Ugas Hassan has reportedly told the returned soldiers they will launch an offensive once enough men have gathered, though no target or plan was specified. This follows an earlier appeal for Hawadle clansmen to arm themselves against a perceived government-backed offensive by rival clans. Soldiers have reportedly withdrawn from key positions in Galgaduud, Lower Shabelle, and Middle Shabelle regions.

Why it mattersThe desertions weaken Somalia's military as it prepares to take over national security responsibilities from international forces.

US troops leave Baledogle airfield over runway costs

US Africa Command cited tens of millions of dollars for repairs as the reason for the withdrawal.

United States troops have withdrawn from Baledogle Military Airfield in southern Somalia due to the deteriorating condition of its runway. Army Lieutenant General John Brennan, deputy commander of US Africa Command (AFRICOM), stated the move was an economic decision because the runway had become unsafe for aircraft. Most personnel from the airfield, located about 90-100 kilometres northwest of Mogadishu, have been relocated to Mogadishu.

General Brennan explained that resurfacing the runway would have cost tens of millions of dollars, making the decision purely economic. He emphasized that this departure does not signify a reduction in American support for Somali security forces fighting Al-Shabaab. US airstrikes against the militant group have continued, including operations in the Lower Shabelle region where Baledogle is situated.

The Baledogle airfield has served as a major hub for US counter-terrorism operations and training of Somalia’s Danab special forces since around 2012. Reports of the US departure emerged in late October, with AFRICOM initially citing operational security. Officials have indicated a potential return to the airfield if conditions change, though no firm plans exist.

Why it mattersThe withdrawal of US troops from a key military airfield affects Somalia's counter-terrorism efforts and its relationship with its international security partners.

Cruz links US envoy to Somalia to Somaliland recognition

Senator Ted Cruz stated he will block the US ambassador nominee unless Somaliland is formally recognised.

Senator Ted Cruz said on Tuesday he would block the confirmation of the US ambassador nominee to Somalia unless the State Department formally recognises Somaliland. At a Senate Foreign Relations Committee hearing, the Texas Republican questioned Devin Kennington, President Trump’s nominee, about the administration’s policy towards Somaliland.

Cruz highlighted Somaliland’s self-governance since 1991, its democratic practices, and its strategic location, noting that Israel recognised Somaliland in December 2025. Kennington acknowledged the different conditions on the ground in Somaliland compared to the rest of Somalia but stated current US policy recognises Somalia’s sovereignty. Cruz insisted that President Trump was correct in calling Somaliland "a totally different country" and that recognition should follow.

Somaliland declared independence from Somalia in 1991 and has maintained its own government, though it is not widely recognised internationally. The United States currently treats it as part of Somalia. No date has been set for a committee vote on Kennington’s nomination.

Why it mattersSomalia's territorial integrity and its relationship with the United States are directly affected by this political condition.
