Somalia seeks 20,000 foreign troops after Uganda withdrawal

Turkey pledges 15,000 soldiers to bolster defences and prevent militant advances.

Somalia's President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud has asked allies for 20,000 troops to replace departing African Union forces, following Uganda's abrupt withdrawal. The president fears militant groups will seize territory vacated by the troops. Turkey has agreed to send 15,000 soldiers to strategic locations including Mogadishu, Barawe, and Baidoa before January 2027.

The deployment is part of a defence agreement between Somalia and Turkey, which aims to convert airstrips in Barawe and Baidoa into military bases. This move comes amid government concerns about defending Mogadishu after the African Union forces depart next year. The departure of Ugandan troops from key defensive positions near the capital has increased fears of a security vacuum.

Somalia's government is reportedly considering severing ties with some influential countries to rely more on Turkey's military support. Relations with neighbours Ethiopia and Kenya are already strained. Kampala refused President Mohamud's request to delay its troop withdrawal, heightening anxiety in Mogadishu.

Why it mattersSomali citizens face increased insecurity and potential militant advances due to the withdrawal of African Union forces and the government's reliance on foreign military aid.

Somalia supreme court voids constitutional changes, orders elections

The court declared amendments extending presidential and parliamentary terms null and void, demanding elections within 90 days.

Somalia’s Supreme Court on Thursday declared null and void amendments to the 2012 Provisional Constitution, invalidating the consequential extension of the mandates of the presidency and parliament. The court ordered that elections be held within 90 days.

The ruling came after President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud dismissed Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf and appointed Ibrahim Eidle Suleiman, the president’s former legal adviser, as his replacement. Five Supreme Court judges, excluding Yusuf, handed down the verdict, stating that parliament ratified the amendments after its mandate expired, rendering it without legal authority for such changes.

The court directed Somalia’s political stakeholders to negotiate and reach a consensus to resolve the protracted electoral impasse within 30 days. Shortly after the verdict, Somali security forces deployed more than 30 military pickup vehicles and stormed the Supreme Court’s premises, forcing the chief justice out.

Why it mattersThe Supreme Court's ruling directly impacts the legitimacy of President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's administration and the timeline for future leadership.

Fugitive Duqow saves 40 Somali soldiers

Duqow, a fugitive sentenced to death, rescued 40 government soldiers lost after an Al-Shabaab battle.

A fugitive named Duqow rescued over 40 Somali Federal Government soldiers stranded for two nights in the Jameeco area of Lower Shabelle. The troops became disoriented after a battle with Al-Shabaab militants. Duqow and Macawiisley fighters provided food and guided the soldiers to safety in Yaaqbiriweyne district.

Duqow, who has been on the run for nearly a year and was sentenced to death by military court judge Judge Shuute, denied disarming the soldiers. He stated he hosted them, gave them refreshments, and contacted their commander. Duqow expressed no malice towards the government, saying, “The federal government is my government and I have no intention of seeking revenge.”

The Somali Federal Government has not yet officially commented on the incident. Duqow’s legal status and the military’s response remain uncertain following the rescue operation. Duqow stated he lives in the isolated area and loves his government.

Why it mattersDuqow, a fugitive facing execution, provided aid and safe passage to government soldiers, demonstrating an unexpected interaction with state forces.

Ugandan troops cede Barawe and Shalanbood to Somali army

Somali National Army assumes control of key southern towns after over a decade of Ugandan military presence.

Ugandan troops have transferred security control of the towns of Barawe and Shalanbood to the Somali National Army (SNA). This handover concludes more than 10 years of Ugandan military operations in these strategic southern Somalia locations.

The transfer encompasses military bases in Barawe, a coastal town with a port, and Shalanbood, a vital settlement on a transport corridor in the Lower Shabelle region. Ugandan military spokesman Colonel Chris Magezi stated on Monday that troops deployed under bilateral agreements would begin their withdrawal immediately.

This departure of Ugandan forces, part of a drawdown scheduled for completion by the end of 2027, intensifies scrutiny on the SNA's capacity to secure territory. Past transfers have resulted in Somali forces losing control of locations such as Biyo Cadde and Mahas to Al-Shabaab.

Why it mattersSomali citizens in Barawe and Shalanbood face uncertainty regarding security and potential Al-Shabaab activity following the withdrawal of foreign troops.

Somalia rejects Israeli ambassador and embassy plans

Mogadishu declares Israel's moves in Somaliland a violation of sovereignty, calling them null and void.

Somalia's Foreign Ministry on Tuesday rejected Israel's appointment of an ambassador to Somaliland and plans for an embassy in Hargeisa. The ministry stated these actions were taken without the Federal Government's consent and are therefore "null and void and without legal effect." Somalia maintains that Somaliland is part of its territory and lacks the authority to establish independent diplomatic ties.

Michael Lotem, Israel's first ambassador to Somaliland, presented his credentials to Somaliland President Abdirahman Mohamed Abdullahi Irro in Hargeisa. A ceremony was also held to lay the cornerstone for a planned Israeli embassy. Israel formally recognised Somaliland on December 26, 2025, becoming the first UN member state to do so since the region declared independence in 1991.

Somalia has urged Israel to reverse these decisions and called on the international community not to recognise steps that could undermine its territorial integrity. The government warned that the developments could heighten tensions in the strategically important Red Sea and Gulf of Aden region.

Why it mattersSomalia's sovereignty and territorial integrity are directly challenged by Israel's diplomatic moves in Somaliland.
