UN mission in Somalia closes after 30 years

The United Nations Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) ceased operations on Saturday, ending a long UN presence.

The United Nations Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) officially closed on Saturday, concluding over three decades of UN operations in the nation at the Somali government's request. This closure finalizes a two-year transition period mandated by Security Council resolutions 2753 (2024) and 2809 (2025). UNTMIS's functions have now been transferred to Somali institutions and various UN agencies, funds, and programmes.

UN Secretary-General spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric stated that the UN commends the Somali people's determination and acknowledges remaining political, security, humanitarian, and development challenges. The UN will continue its work in Somalia through resident agencies, funds, and programmes, collaborating with Somali authorities, the African Union, and international partners. Raisedon Zenenga, the mission’s officer-in-charge, described the closure as the start of a new partnership chapter.

A farewell ceremony took place in Mogadishu on October 26, attended by Zenenga and Deputy Prime Minister Salah Ahmed Jama, who called the closure a "day of victory." UN missions in Somalia date back to 1992, with UNTMIS succeeding UNSOM, which began in June 2013. The mission's closure occurs amidst a sharp decline in funding for Somalia.

Why it mattersSomali institutions are taking over functions previously handled by the UN mission, marking a shift in governance and international support.

Uganda begins Somalia troop pullout after 20 years

The first contingent of 109 soldiers departed Mogadishu airport on Friday, marking the start of a withdrawal.

Uganda has started withdrawing its troops from Somalia after two decades of fighting Al-Shabaab. A Kenya-registered aircraft chartered by Uganda departed Mogadishu airport on Friday carrying 109 soldiers, the first group to leave. Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni informed Somali President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud of his decision to withdraw forces before November 15, rejecting Mohamud’s request to delay the pullout.

Ugandan forces, stationed in the Lower Shabelle region, are set to hand over forward operating bases to the Somali army by November 4. Uganda first deployed troops in 2007, helping to drive Al-Shabaab from Mogadishu and other key towns. Their departure from river-crossing towns vital to Mogadishu’s security raises questions about the capital’s future safety.

Uganda People's Defence Force chief General Muhoozi Kainerugaba announced the withdrawal on Thursday, stating, “After 20 years of a lot of blood and sacrifice we are handing over security to our Somali brothers.” The withdrawal follows President Museveni's rejection of President Mohamud's plea for a delay during a visit to Uganda on Monday, October 26.

Why it mattersUganda's withdrawal of troops from Somalia after 20 years of fighting Al-Shabaab directly impacts the security of the capital, Mogadishu, and the Lower Shabelle region.

Fugitive rescues 40 Somali soldiers

Duqow, sentenced to death, provided food and guided stranded troops to safety in Lower Shabelle.

A fugitive named Duqow rescued over 40 Somali Federal Government soldiers who were stranded for two nights in the Jameeco area of the Lower Shabelle region. The soldiers had become lost following a battle with Al-Shabaab militants. Duqow and local Macawiisley fighters found the disoriented troops, gave them food, and led them to the safety of Yaaqbiriweyne district.

Duqow, who has been on the run for nearly a year and was sentenced to death by military court judge Judge Shuute, denied disarming the soldiers. He stated he hosted them, provided refreshments, and contacted their commander to report their situation. Duqow expressed love for his government, saying, "The federal government is my government and I have no intention of seeking revenge."

The Somali Federal Government has not yet officially commented on the incident, leaving Duqow's legal standing and the military's response uncertain. Duqow has been actively sought by state security forces for execution of his sentence.

Why it mattersA fugitive on death row saved government soldiers, highlighting a complex relationship between the state and individuals it pursues.
