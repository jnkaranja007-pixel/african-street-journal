Laftagaren forces attack Baidoa, seize army bases

Fierce gun battles erupted in Baidoa after forces loyal to ousted leader Abdiasis Laftagaren attacked from multiple directions, seizing two federal army bases.

Heavy fighting broke out in Baidoa on Thursday when forces loyal to former Southwest State President Abdiasis Laftagaren attacked the city from several directions. The attackers seized two federal army bases on the city's outskirts before entering Baidoa and engaging government troops in hours of intense gun battles. A spokesman for Laftagaren's Darwish forces stated they would continue fighting until the last federal soldier departed the region and control was fully restored to the people. The forces later withdrew from the city, ending the confrontation.

Southwest State Information Minister Nur Sidow claimed in Baidoa that Al-Shabaab was responsible for the attack, initiating it with suicide car bombs. The Somali federal government made a similar assertion last month following a prior attack by Laftagaren's forces on Baidoa. However, regional authorities and the federal government have not yet presented any concrete evidence to support the claim that Al-Shabaab was involved.

The recent clashes resulted in multiple fatalities and other casualties, though official figures remain unavailable. Among those killed was the commander of the personal guards for the current Southwest leader, Adan Madobe. Isaq Abdulle, commander of the Somali army’s 8th Brigade of the 60th Division, suffered serious injuries. Analysts note that Baidoa, once peaceful under Laftagaren, has become a battleground since his removal, with recurrent fighting challenging federal control.

Why it mattersThe fighting in Baidoa affects residents and military personnel through casualties and the disruption of daily life, while also highlighting ongoing security challenges.

Somalia Holds First Regional Anti-FGM Hearing

East African Legislative Assembly members met with Somali officials and civil society in Mogadishu to discuss a bill aimed at combating female genital mutilation.

Mogadishu hosted Somalia's inaugural public consultation with the East African Legislative Assembly (EALA) concerning a proposed regional framework to combat female genital mutilation (FGM). Somali government officials, regional lawmakers, religious leaders, and civil society representatives participated in the two-day hearing on the Elimination of Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) Bill, 2025. This event marks Somalia's first in-country engagement with EALA since its integration into the eight-member regional bloc at the end of 2025.

The consultation holds particular significance given Somalia's FGM prevalence, estimated at 99% of women and girls according to the 2020 Somali Demographic and Health Survey. The country currently lacks a specific national law prohibiting the practice. Equality Now, which organized the hybrid hearing, emphasized the importance of gathering opinions and recommendations from Somali stakeholders to foster acceptance of the proposed legislation.

Participants reviewed the bill clause by clause, focusing on strengthening protection mechanisms, enhancing cross-border cooperation, and coordinating regional actions against FGM. EALA member Falhada Dekow Iman, a sponsor of the bill, stated that the legislation should not be perceived as an attack on communities or their traditions. The hearing brought together representatives from federal government institutions, Somali and EALA lawmakers, and various civil society groups.

Why it mattersSomali women and girls are directly affected by the proposed regional legislation aimed at combating female genital mutilation, a practice with 99% prevalence in the country.

Somalia, Qatar University Sign Technology Education Pact

A new university focused on high-quality technical and vocational training will be established with support from the University of Doha for Science and Technology.

Somalia's Ministry of Education, Culture and Higher Education has signed a cooperation agreement with the University of Doha for Science and Technology (UDST) to establish the Technology University (TUS University). The pact, signed in Doha, outlines a framework for developing a university specializing in technical and vocational education.

Under the partnership, UDST will provide support for curriculum development, teacher training, quality assurance, and certification. The initial phase will concentrate on critical fields such as technology, marine sciences, aviation, petroleum, engineering, energy, and health management, aiming to enhance the skills of Somali youth.

This initiative aligns with Somalia’s National Transformation Plan 2025–2029, seeking to improve educational quality and develop labor market-aligned skills. A key focus will be training Somali teachers to staff the new university's administration and academic departments, with appreciation extended to Qatar for its ongoing support.

Why it mattersSomali youth will gain enhanced professional skills and knowledge through the establishment of a new technology university.

Militias Seize Somali Election Centers Amid Opposition

Armed groups occupied voter registration sites in Beledweyne, protesting planned elections and demanding fairness in candidate selection.

Armed militias occupied voter registration centers in Beledweyne on Tuesday, escalating opposition to the September 30 Hirshabelle municipal and parliamentary elections. The militias stated their objection to elections managed by a government-appointed commission, citing a process that allegedly bans all but one favored candidate. The Somali federal government has not yet responded to the militia actions.

Hawadle clan politicians also voiced strong opposition to the planned Hirshabelle election, deeming the process unfair and demanding their turn at the regional administration's presidency. These competing objections have created significant political tension in Beledweyne, directly impacting the designated electoral locations and disrupting the planned voting process.

The dispute centers on claims of a rigged electoral process favoring a single candidate while excluding others, alongside the Hawadle clan's push for leadership. With the electoral commission's date set, the conflict over the fairness and inclusivity of the Hirshabelle elections remains unresolved, and the federal government's stance is pending.

Why it mattersThe seizure of election centers and protests over candidate fairness directly affect the political process and stability in Beledweyne.

Somalia Concludes Security Governance Conference

A two-day meeting in Mogadishu focused on strengthening oversight, coordination, and reform across the nation's internal security sector.

Somalia's Ministry of Internal Security has concluded a national conference aimed at enhancing governance and coordination within the country's internal security sector. The two-day event, held September 1-2 in Mogadishu, brought together delegates to discuss strengthening oversight, deepening inter-agency collaboration, and building a financially sustainable security apparatus.

The conference addressed key areas including the Ministry's oversight role over the Somali Police Force, the National Intelligence and Security Agency, and the Immigration and Citizenship Agency. Discussions also covered reporting lines, accountability, performance monitoring, and the balance between operational autonomy and ministerial oversight, alongside federal and member state coordination.

Participants engaged in technical sessions focused on improving coordination gaps between the ministry, its agencies, and the broader security architecture. The agenda also included mechanisms for cooperation, deconfliction of mandates, and information sharing to foster a more cohesive and effective internal security framework.

Why it mattersThe conference affects security sector governance and coordination, which could impact the stability and operational effectiveness of internal security agencies.
