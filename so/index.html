Somalia ex-spy chief hints at Al-Shabaab alliance

Former intelligence chief Abdullahi Mohamed Ali Sanbalolshe did not rule out cooperation with Al-Shabaab to destabilize the government.

Former Somali intelligence chief Abdullahi Mohamed Ali Sanbalolshe stated his Hawadle clan intends to form a new regional administration in Hiran. This move follows the clan's failure to secure the presidency of Hirshabelle state under President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud. Sanbalolshe suggested this initiative could escalate into armed resistance against the federal government in Mogadishu.

Sanbalolshe indicated his movement would seek assistance from any party to remove President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud’s government. When asked about cooperating with Al-Shabaab, the militant group fighting the government, Sanbalolshe did not rule out the possibility. He also mentioned meeting leaders in the United Arab Emirates, including presidents of Somaliland, Puntland, and Jubaland, urging them to support a rebellion in Hiran.

Counterterrorism official Abdirahman Turyare recently warned of plans for armed rebellions in Hiran and Middle Shabelle regions. Sanbalolshe compared his movement's potential actions to those of Southwest State leader Abdiaziz Hassan Mohamed Laftagareen, who has faced accusations of coordinating with Al-Shabaab.

Why it mattersSomalia faces renewed fears of political instability and potential armed conflict as a former intelligence chief suggests forming a new regional administration and does not rule out an alliance with Al-Shabaab.

Somalia sends over 200 vehicles toward Burhakabo

Federal government forces are moving to retake towns seized by former Southwest president Abdiasis Laftagaren.

Somalia’s federal government dispatched more than 200 military vehicles towards Burhakabo on Saturday. The convoy, including armored personnel carriers and troop trucks, departed from Wanlawayn district. This move follows the seizure of Burhakabo by forces loyal to former Southwest president Abdiasis Laftagaren last week.

Residents along the Mogadishu-Baidoa road reported seeing a long column of military vehicles for hours early Saturday. Officials have stated their intention to retake towns recently lost to Laftagaren’s forces. Al-Shabaab-affiliated media reported one soldier killed and a vehicle damaged in an ambush along the Mogadishu-Afgoye corridor on Saturday.

The operation occurs amid tensions between Ethiopia and the federal government. Ethiopia stated its forces manning towns recaptured by Laftagaren's forces refused to engage in the conflict. Addis Ababa reportedly warned the UN Security Council of its right to self-defense if fired upon during a federal offensive.

Why it mattersSomalia's federal government is moving military vehicles to retake towns seized by former Southwest president Abdiasis Laftagaren.

Somalia police enter Hormuud headquarters

Police entered two Hormuud company premises in Mogadishu on Sunday under a court warrant.

Somalia’s police entered two Hormuud company premises in Mogadishu on Sunday, including its headquarters in Bakara Market and its main management headquarters on the road leading to the airport. Investigations are underway under a court warrant, Somali Stream reported.

Initial reports suggest the investigation is linked to recent developments in Southwest state, where forces loyal to former regional president Abdiasis Laftagaren have retaken major towns in Bay region. The federal government previously imposed economic sanctions on Mr. Laftagaren’s administration and anyone who co-operates with it.

These advances reverse gains made by the federal government during a March offensive that ousted Mr. Laftagaren and his administration. The federal government has not announced further steps regarding the investigation or the situation in Southwest state.

Why it mattersSomalia's police entered Hormuud company premises, affecting business operations.

Somalia opposition challenges new constitution in court

Sharif Sheikh Ahmed filed a case seeking to annul the charter approved in March without opposition MPs.

Somalia’s Supreme Court has given the federal government until Thursday to respond to a legal challenge filed by opposition leader Sharif Sheikh Ahmed. Ahmed seeks to annul a new constitution that was approved in March.

Regional states and the opposition consider the charter unilateral, claiming it was drafted by the current leadership without opposition members of parliament. President Hassan Sheikh hailed the document as a new chapter for Somalia, but Puntland, Jubaland, and the opposition do not recognize it, continuing to uphold the 2012 draft constitution.

The opposition's case is seen by many as a test of the court’s independence, as allegations of executive influence over the judiciary have persisted for years. The federal government is reportedly seeking ways to win the case or halt the proceedings.

Why it mattersThe opposition and regional states are challenging the legitimacy of the new constitution, which affects the country's governance structure.
