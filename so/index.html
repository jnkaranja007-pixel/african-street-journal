Somali official survives alleged Turkish drone strike

Hassan Abdulkadir, a key strategist for Laftagaren's forces, escaped unharmed after his motorcycle was hit.

Hassan Abdulkadir, former internal security minister of Southwest State and a key figure in Laftagaren’s forces, survived an alleged Turkish drone strike on Wednesday night. The strike hit a motorcycle carrying two of Laftagaren’s fighters, killing them. Abdulkadir and another military figure, Mustafa Qoomaal, were unharmed in the attack in the Bay region.

It is unclear if the strike targeted Abdulkadir, who is considered the main strategist for Laftagaren’s guerrilla campaign. His forces recently recaptured towns including Qansah-dhere, Burhakabo, and Dinsor. This is the second air raid on a Somali opposition politician; General Mahad Abdirahman survived an F-16 strike on September 3.

Abdulkadir has been seen in towns recently retaken by Laftagaren’s forces. The federal government had previously launched an offensive in March that ousted the former regional president. Laftagaren’s forces continue to consolidate their control over the Bay region.

Why it mattersHassan Abdulkadir, a key strategist for Laftagaren's forces, and Mustafa Qoomaal were directly affected by an alleged drone strike.

Somalia targets Iraq for livestock exports

Foreign ministers from Somalia and Iraq met in Baghdad to revive a joint committee and discuss agricultural trade.

Somalia is pursuing opportunities to export livestock to Iraq, aiming to diversify its market beyond current destinations. Somali Foreign Minister Abdisalam Abdi Ali and his Iraqi counterpart, Fuad Hussein, discussed this initiative in Baghdad on October 17-18.

The ministers spoke about reactivating a dormant Somalia-Iraq joint committee to strengthen cooperation in agriculture and trade. Somalia expressed interest in starting live-animal exports, a key industry supporting pastoral communities and related businesses.

Both sides agreed to accelerate the joint committee's work, though no specific date for livestock shipments was set. Expanding market access to Iraq could encourage investment in veterinary services and infrastructure for Somalia's livestock sector.

Why it mattersSomali livestock producers gain a potential new market for sheep, goats, cattle, and camels in Iraq.

Somalia opposition challenges new constitution in court

Former president Sharif Sheikh Ahmed seeks to annul the charter approved in March without opposition lawmakers.

Somalia's Supreme Court has given the federal government until Thursday to respond to a legal challenge against the country's new constitution. Opposition leader Sharif Sheikh Ahmed filed the case seeking to annul the charter, which was approved in March. Regional states and opposition groups argue the constitution was drafted unilaterally by the current leadership.

Sharif Sheikh Ahmed and opposing regional states like Puntland and Jubaland do not recognize the new constitution, continuing to adhere to the 2012 draft. They claim the 2012 text was developed through broad agreement among all political stakeholders, making it nationally recognized. The federal government, led by President Hassan Sheikh, hailed the new document as a new chapter for Somalia.

The opposition's court case is seen by many as a test of the judiciary's independence, which has historically been alleged to be influenced by executive decisions. The federal government aims to win the case or at least delay the court's proceedings.

Why it mattersSomali citizens are affected by the legal challenge to the new constitution, which could determine the country's governance framework.
