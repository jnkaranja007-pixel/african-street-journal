Mogadishu police clash with residents over forced evictions

Three protesters killed before armed residents ambushed police in Daynile district, forcing officers to abandon wounded.

Somali police clashed with armed residents in Mogadishu’s Daynile district on Sunday during forced evictions and demolitions. The operation, which began with the demolition of dozens of homes in the Warlaliska area, resulted in three protesters being killed by police earlier that morning. Later, residents ambushed police, leading to an exchange of gunfire and the abandonment of wounded officers and a police pickup truck.

Videos showed injured police officers being carried by residents, who had taken some wounded officers into their custody. Former President Sheikh Ahmed urged residents not to hand over the officers to the government, stating Somalia lacked a legitimate government and suggesting they be given to their families instead. Former Prime Minister Hassan Ali Khaire accused Mogadishu Police Commander Moalim Mahdi and Yasin Ganey, commander of the western police force, of orchestrating the Daynile evictions and demolitions.

The confrontation continued into Monday with both sides holding their positions, though no further clashes were reported. The police operation aimed to evict residents and demolish homes in the Warlaliska area of Daynile district.

Why it mattersResidents of Mogadishu's Daynile district are directly affected by forced evictions and demolitions of their homes, leading to deadly clashes with police.

Senior NISA commander defects to Al-Shabaab

A Turkish-trained commander reportedly took laptops, communication gadgets, and files.

A senior commander of Somalia’s National Intelligence and Security Agency (NISA) Duufaan unit has defected to Al-Shabaab. The commander, identified as Luqman, led the Turkish-trained paramilitary unit and reportedly took assets including laptops, communication gadgets, and files when he defected. Luqman is believed to be in Al-Shabab-controlled territory in southern Somalia.

Analyst Rashid Abdi stated that the Somali intelligence agency, NISA, is now severely compromised. Al-Shabab may have access to crucial intelligence data, such as names and addresses of informants in sensitive areas. NISA is reportedly scrambling to contain the crisis.

Luqman led the Duufaan unit, a paramilitary force trained by Turkey under Somalia’s spy agency. Abdi, an analyst with Nairobi-based Sahan Research, made his comments on Sunday.

Somalia opposition backs court ruling on president's term

Future Council declares contracts signed after May 15, 2026, null and void.

Somalia’s opposition Future Council on Sunday condemned the president’s alleged takeover of the Supreme Court, accusing his administration of hunting down judicial leadership. Security forces are reportedly pursuing the Supreme Court chairman Bashe Yusuf Ahmed and other judges after they ruled the president’s term extension illegal.

The opposition coalition supported the court’s decision that the mandates for both the president and parliament have lapsed, calling for consensus-based elections within 90 days. The council warned citizens of grave threats to Somali statehood and national security, holding the “term-expired” president accountable for any fallout.

The council declared all contracts, policy decisions, and legislative agreements enacted after April 14, 2026, for parliament and May 15, 2026, for the president, null and void. They pledged a full review once legal supremacy is re-established.
