Opposition files court challenge to Somalia's new constitution

Sharif Sheikh Ahmed is seeking to annul the charter, which regional states and opposition leaders call unilateral and unrepresentative.

Somalia’s Supreme Court has given the federal government until Thursday to respond to a legal challenge filed by opposition leader Sharif Sheikh Ahmed. Ahmed is seeking to annul a new constitution that was approved in March. Regional states, including Puntland and Jubaland, along with opposition figures, have declared the charter unilateral, arguing it was drafted without broad consensus.

The opposition claims they were blocked from the parliamentary session where the constitution was approved. They maintain that the 2012 draft constitution, which was developed through a wide-ranging agreement among all political stakeholders, remains the recognized national charter. The federal government, however, is reportedly seeking ways to win the case or delay proceedings.

The case is being viewed as a significant test of the Supreme Court's independence, as many have long alleged that the judiciary is influenced by the executive branch. The current leadership, led by President Hassan Sheikh, has hailed the new document as a pivotal step for the nation.

Why it mattersRegional states and opposition leaders are affected because they do not recognize the new constitution and continue to adhere to the 2012 draft.

Somalia speaker accuses Kenya of harbouring terrorists

Parliamentary speaker Abdulkadir Mohamed Nur demands Mogadishu question Nairobi over alleged support for ousted Southwest leader.

Somalia's parliament speaker, Abdulkadir Mohamed Nur, on Sunday accused Kenya of harbouring terrorists, demanding that the federal government question Nairobi's continued allowance of individuals conducting violence in Somalia. Nur specifically referenced Abdiasis Laftagaren, the ousted Southwest state leader, and his associates, whose forces recently recaptured several towns in the Bay region. The speaker urged swift diplomatic action against Kenya, particularly concerning alleged safe havens in the capital, Nairobi.

The accusations follow a swift counter-offensive by forces loyal to Laftagaren, who was removed from power in March with Turkish backing. His fighters retook the towns of Dinsor, Burhakabo, Awdinle, Berdale, and Qansah Dhere last week, while the federal government has struggled to mobilize forces to reclaim them. Efforts to involve Ethiopian peacekeepers on Mogadishu's side failed, as Addis Ababa maintained neutrality, deeming the conflict an internal political dispute.

Nur also called for accountability from Ethiopia, whose troops did not defend the towns recaptured by Laftagaren's loyalists. The speaker's remarks highlight growing tensions between Mogadishu and its neighbours amid internal political instability and the federal government's difficulty in asserting control over regional states.

Why it mattersSomali citizens are affected by the political instability and accusations between their government and neighbouring countries, which could impact regional security and stability.

Somalia police enter Hormuud headquarters

Authorities executed a court warrant at the telecom company's offices in Mogadishu, with investigations reportedly tied to Southwest state political tensions.

Somali police entered two Hormuud company premises in Mogadishu on Sunday, including its headquarters in Bakara Market and its main management office on the road to the airport. The actions were carried out under a court warrant, with investigations currently underway.

Initial reports suggest the investigation is linked to recent developments in the Southwest state. Forces loyal to former regional president Abdiasis Laftagaren have recently retaken major towns in the Bay region, reversing gains made by the federal government in an earlier offensive.

The federal government had previously imposed economic sanctions on Laftagaren’s administration and any entities cooperating with it. The precise nature of Hormuud's alleged involvement in the Southwest state's political situation remains unclear.

Why it mattersThe federal government's actions against Hormuud headquarters may disrupt telecommunications services for Somalis.
