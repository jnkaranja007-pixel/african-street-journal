Somalia launches fourth cybersecurity forum

Mogadishu University and the National Communications Authority host a two-day event to boost digital security.

Somalia's 4th National Cyber Security Forum began today in Mogadishu, organized by the National Communications Authority (NCA) and Mogadishu University. The two-day event brings together government, telecom companies, ISPs, banks, universities, and civil society to strengthen cybersecurity capabilities under the theme “Building Trust and Cyber Resilience Through Security by Design.”

Mogadishu University President Dr. Ibrahim Mohamed Mursal highlighted the university's new Cybersecurity and Artificial Intelligence department, stating universities are key to developing needed skills. NCA Director General Mustafa Yasin Sheikh noted progress including the Somali Computer Incident Response Team (SOMCIRT) launch and capacity-building programs, calling the forum a chance to share knowledge and develop solutions.

Minister of Communications and Technology H.E. MP Mohamed Adan Mo’allin officially opened the forum, commending the organizers and mentioning the planned National Cybersecurity Academy. He urged participants to develop practical recommendations to enhance national cybersecurity and public awareness, hoping for outcomes that benefit the Somali people.

Why it mattersThe forum aims to strengthen cybersecurity capabilities and promote secure digital systems for the Somali people.

Somalia president cancels Russia trip amid security fears

President Mohamud also faces pressure from Uganda to hold opposition talks or risk troop withdrawal.

Somalia's President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud canceled a planned trip to Russia this week due to rising security and political pressures. The decision followed an ambush by Al Shabaab in the Lower Shabelle region on Monday that killed dozens of Somali soldiers.

Uganda, a major contributor to the African Union force, urged Mohamud to quickly begin talks with the opposition, warning of troop withdrawal if he did not. Mohamud met Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni and requested Ugandan forces remain, but Museveni cited economic constraints and reiterated the demand for dialogue.

Mohamud called for dialogue with the opposition on Sunday night, dropping his earlier refusal to compromise on contentious issues, in an apparent effort to appease Museveni. Since returning to Somalia, Mohamud has been working to ease political tensions and resolve disputes through political means.

Why it mattersPresident Mohamud canceled a trip to Russia due to domestic security and political pressures, including an Al Shabaab ambush and demands from Uganda for opposition talks.

Somalia hosts fourth national cybersecurity forum

The National Communications Agency and Mogadishu University organized the two-day event focused on building cyber trust and resilience.

Mogadishu, Somalia hosted the fourth National Cybersecurity Forum on October 27, 2026, organized jointly by the National Communications Agency (HIQ) and Mogadishu University. The two-day event brought together government officials, technology and internet service providers, banks, universities, civil society groups, researchers, and cybersecurity experts. The forum's theme, "Building Cyber Trust and Resilience Based on Security-by-Design," aimed to strengthen cybersecurity capabilities and secure digital systems.

Dr. Ibraahim Maxamed Mursal, Rector of Mogadishu University, highlighted the institution's role in producing cybersecurity professionals, noting the launch of a new Cybersecurity and Artificial Intelligence specialization. He stated that cybersecurity is a shared responsibility requiring collaboration between government, universities, private companies, and the public. Mustafa Yaasiin Sheekh, Director General of HIQ, presented the agency's cybersecurity achievements, including the launch of the National Computer Emergency Response Team (SOMCIRT) and public awareness campaigns.

Minister of Posts, Telecommunications and Technology, Xildhibaan Maxamed Aadan Macallin, officially opened the forum, commending HIQ and Mogadishu University for organizing it for the fourth consecutive year. He mentioned the March 2026 launch of SOMCIRT and plans for a National Cybersecurity Academy by year-end. The Minister encouraged participants to exchange ideas and develop practical recommendations to enhance national cybersecurity and public awareness.

Why it mattersThe forum addresses cybersecurity challenges affecting government, universities, companies, and the public in Somalia.
