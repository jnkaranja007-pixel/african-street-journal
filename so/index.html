Somalia Parliament Speaker Accuses Kenya of Harboring Terrorists

Parliament speaker Abdulkadir Mohamed Nur demanded an explanation from Nairobi regarding alleged terrorist harboring following recent rebel advances.

Somalia's parliament speaker, Abdulkadir Mohamed Nur, on Sunday accused Kenya of harboring terrorists, stating the federal government should question Nairobi's continued allowance of individuals carrying out violence in Somalia. This accusation follows recent rebel advances in the Southwest state, where forces loyal to the ousted leader Abdiasis Laftagaren retook several towns. Nur specifically mentioned Nairobi as a location where these individuals are allegedly harbored.

Nur's remarks come as the Somali federal government has struggled to mobilize forces to retake the lost towns, including Dinsor, Burhakabo, and Awdinle. Efforts to involve Ethiopian forces on Mogadishu's side also failed, as Addis Ababa maintained neutrality, deeming the dispute a political power struggle beyond its peacekeepers' mandate. Laftagaren was ousted in March with Turkish airpower support.

The parliament speaker urged swift action through diplomatic channels against Nairobi and also called for Ethiopia to be held accountable for its troops' failure to defend the recaptured towns. Nur, described as a key link between the Turkish and Somali governments, emphasized the need for a response to the ongoing violence impacting the nation.

Why it mattersParliament speaker Abdulkadir Mohamed Nur is affected by the political instability and territorial losses in the Southwest state, prompting his accusations against Kenya and Ethiopia.

Somalia Police Raid Telecom Firm Headquarters

Authorities entered Hormuud company offices in Mogadishu under a court warrant, investigating potential links to regional political tensions.

Somalia's police forces conducted raids on two Hormuud company locations in Mogadishu on Sunday, including its main headquarters in Bakara Market and another office near the airport. The actions were carried out under a court warrant, with investigations reportedly underway.

Initial reports suggest the police action is connected to recent political developments in the Southwest state. Forces aligned with former regional president Abdiasis Laftagaren have reportedly regained control of several towns in the Bay region, reversing gains made by the federal government.

The federal government had previously imposed economic sanctions on Laftagaren's administration and any entities cooperating with it. The investigation into Hormuud's premises appears to be examining potential links to these sanctions and the ongoing regional instability.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because police raids on a major telecommunications company headquarters can signal regulatory uncertainty and potential disruption to business operations.

Somalia agrees to buy Pakistani fighter jets

A Turkish defense outlet reported Somalia's agreement to purchase JF-17 Thunder jets from Pakistan, with no official confirmation.

Somalia has reportedly agreed to acquire JF-17 Thunder fighter jets from Pakistan, according to a Turkish defense outlet. Neither the Somali nor Pakistani governments have confirmed the existence of a contract, and details regarding the price, number of aircraft, or delivery timeline remain undisclosed. This claim follows months of unconfirmed reports, including a February assertion that Somalia was negotiating for up to 24 Block III variants in a deal valued at approximately $900 million, figures also unconfirmed by officials.

The potential acquisition comes after the U.N. Security Council lifted its arms embargo on Somalia's federal government in December 2023, allowing for the purchase of military equipment. However, the resolution urged Mogadishu to improve weapons management and prevent unauthorized access to imported arms. Analysts suggest that acquiring advanced fighter jets would necessitate significant investment in new airbase infrastructure, air traffic control systems, maintenance facilities, and pilot training programs.

Diplomatic engagements have included talks between Somalia's Defence Minister Ahmed Moallim Fiqi and Pakistani naval and air force chiefs in August, where Pakistan expressed readiness to support Somali Air Force modernization. Subsequently, Pakistan's air defence commander visited Mogadishu in September, meeting with President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud and military commanders. During the meeting, a model of the JF-17 jet was presented, though no purchase announcement was made.

Why it mattersSomalia's potential acquisition of advanced fighter jets could impact its ability to secure its airspace and project power.

Somalia President Rejects Opposition Election Talks

President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud dismissed calls for consensus on upcoming elections, proceeding with government plans despite opposition warnings.

President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud has rejected calls for negotiations with opposition leaders regarding Somalia’s upcoming elections, stating his government’s plans will proceed as scheduled. The president dismissed demands for a consensus-based electoral process, asserting his government’s control over the arrangements. This stance signals a refusal to engage with opposition figures and regional states who advocate for an inclusive approach to ensure free and fair elections.

The government-appointed electoral commission announced that the presidential election is set for May, with parliamentary elections planned for April of the following year. President Mohamud affirmed that all electoral activities will follow his administration's timeline, and any opposition to the proposed process will be unsuccessful. This unilateral decision has been condemned by opposition leaders and the regional administrations of Puntland and Jubaland, who argue that a consensus-driven process is essential for legitimate elections.

Opposition leaders and the Puntland and Jubaland administrations have threatened to establish a parallel government if President Mohamud proceeds with a unilateral electoral process that excludes opponents and remains under his control. This political standoff risks exacerbating divisions between the federal government, opposition factions, and regional administrations, potentially leading to a broader political confrontation amid ongoing state-building efforts.

Why it mattersThe president's rejection of consensus on election timing and process affects all political stakeholders by potentially excluding them from fair competition.
