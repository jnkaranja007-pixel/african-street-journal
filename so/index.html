Türkiye Pledges Expanded Economic Cooperation for Somalia

Ankara will continue security support while increasing collaboration in energy, fisheries, and infrastructure projects.

Türkiye has committed to ongoing security assistance for Somalia's forces and pledged to broaden cooperation in key economic sectors. Turkish Ambassador Alper Aktaş stated that Ankara will maintain its support for Somali security institutions and counterterrorism efforts, while also expanding partnerships in energy, fisheries, and other strategic areas. Aktaş noted Somalia's visible security progress, acknowledging that significant challenges persist as the nation strives for greater self-sufficiency.

The ambassador highlighted Türkiye's role in training over 1,000 personnel for the Gorgor special forces and Haramcad units within the Somali Police Force, who are actively responding to security threats. He also mentioned increased air support for ground operations, though specific details on aircraft and impact were not provided. Aktaş emphasized that security gains are intrinsically linked to economic development, citing ongoing projects like the Çağrı Bey deep-sea drilling vessel and planned fisheries initiatives.

Türkiye's engagement extends to a proposed spaceport project and follows a March 2024 intergovernmental energy agreement for oil and gas exploration. While exploratory drilling is underway, the discovery of commercially viable resources is not guaranteed. The ambassador's remarks were made at Somalia’s National Internal Security Conference in Mogadishu, where he contrasted the city's past instability with its current signs of development and hope.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by Türkiye's commitment to expanding cooperation in energy, fisheries, and infrastructure, alongside continued security support which underpins economic development.

Somalia Schedules Galmudug, Hirshabelle Elections for September 30

The Independent National Electoral and Boundaries Commission announced new dates following a Supreme Court ruling that annulled Galmudug's prior results.

Somalia's electoral commission has set September 30, 2026, as the polling day for local council and parliamentary elections in the federal member states of Galmudug and Hirshabelle. This decision follows a Supreme Court ruling on August 23 that annulled Galmudug's previous election results due to irregularities, necessitating fresh polls. Hirshabelle will proceed with its originally planned elections.

Galmudug's electoral process will commence on September 10 with the designation of polling centers, followed by candidate submissions by September 20 and accreditation of party agents on September 26. Campaigning will end on September 28. Hirshabelle's process includes preliminary voter register publication on September 2, with candidate announcements on September 22 and campaigning concluding on September 28.

A senior electoral official stated that these elections are a crucial opportunity for citizens to exercise their democratic rights and affirmed the commission's commitment to transparency and fairness. The outcomes are significant for Somalia's political stability, impacting the establishment of legitimate regional administrations and progress toward national elections scheduled for 2027.

Why it mattersVoters in Galmudug and Hirshabelle will be able to participate in local council and parliamentary elections, exercising their democratic rights.

Japanese NGO director runs Somalia's sole deradicalisation program

Yosuke Nagai leads Accept International, the only foreign agency still working on reintegrating former Islamist fighters amid reduced donor aid.

Accept International, led by Yosuke Nagai, continues to operate as Somalia's only foreign-funded reintegration program for Islamist fighters. Nagai, who started his work in Somalia at 19, established a program for students in 2011 to address the insurgency's root causes, later expanding to gang members and then former Al-Shabaab fighters in collaboration with the government.

The NGO has worked with 1,700 imprisoned Al-Shabaab fighters and 250 defectors, also expanding its operations to Yemen, the Palestinian territories, and Indonesia. Nagai recalled his teachers warning him that his work was "freaking super dangerous," but he questioned what they were doing to help.

With Washington and other donors significantly reducing aid to Somalia, Accept International has become the sole remaining foreign agency focused on deradicalisation efforts in the country. The organization's work aims to provide a pathway for former fighters to reintegrate into society.

Why it mattersFormer Islamist fighters are affected by the program, which offers them a path to reintegration into society.

Somali Refugee Leads Labour Race for Starmer's Seat

Sagal Abdi-Wali, current leader of Camden council, is the favored Labour candidate to contest the Holborn and St Pancras by-election.

Sagal Abdi-Wali, a former child refugee from Somalia, is the front-runner for the Labour nomination in the Holborn and St Pancras constituency. The seat became vacant after Sir Keir Starmer announced his intention to stand down. Abdi-Wali, who currently leads Camden council and previously chaired Starmer's Constituency Labour Party, is reportedly considering a parliamentary bid.

Abdi-Wali arrived in the UK in the early 1990s with her family, fleeing civil war and famine in Somalia. She has been a Labour supporter for as long as she can recall and was first elected as a Labour councillor in 2022. Locally, she is regarded as an ally of Sir Keir and was appointed leader of Camden council following the May 7 local elections, where she pledged to champion social justice and deliver change.

The potential candidate has spoken about her family's move to London during the early 1990s. Her leadership of Camden council began after the local elections, with a commitment to social justice. She is now positioned to potentially replace Starmer as the Member of Parliament for Holborn and St Pancras, facing Zack Polanski in the upcoming by-election.

Why it mattersSagal Abdi-Wali, a former child refugee from Somalia, is positioned to potentially become a Member of Parliament in the UK, representing a significant political shift.

Parents Urged to Guide Daughters Amidst Modern Pressures

Community leaders call for increased parental involvement to protect young girls from social media and unhealthy influences.

Community leaders are urging parents to reflect on their roles in guiding daughters amidst evolving societal pressures. Concerns are being raised that the rapid pace of change, driven by social media and peer influence, is outpacing families' ability to provide adequate direction. While acknowledging the many young Somali girls who are respectful and ambitious, the focus is on addressing the growing challenges that can lead to exploitation and poor choices.

The call to action emphasizes that protection extends beyond basic needs like food and clothing to safeguarding faith, character, dignity, and emotional well-being. Drawing on religious teachings, leaders remind fathers of their responsibility as shepherds, responsible for the care of those under them. This role, they stress, involves not only financial provision but also crucial elements of time, attention, guidance, and presence.

This reflection is framed not as criticism but as an honest assessment of the community's direction. The emphasis is on collective responsibility, with fathers, mothers, families, and the wider community all playing a part in nurturing the next generation. The aim is to foster an environment where young women can thrive, protected from negative influences and empowered to reach their full potential.

Why it mattersParents and guardians are affected by the call to increase their guidance and protection of daughters facing modern societal pressures.
