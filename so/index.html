Security Forces Kill Five, Wound Ten in Mogadishu Slum Demolition

Somalia's federal security forces killed at least five people and wounded more than ten others during a violent operation to clear the Tookiyo settlement in Mogadishu.

Somalia's federal security forces killed at least five people and wounded more than ten others in Mogadishu on Sunday, local media reported. The operation forcibly cleared the Tookiyo settlement in the capital's Yaqshid district, part of a wider campaign of slum demolitions that has left residents without homes. The action began shortly after midnight on Saturday and continued into Sunday.

Residents met advancing troops with stones, prompting security forces to open fire on the densely packed neighborhood. Witnesses described the response as disproportionate. Video footage circulating on social media appeared to show government pickup trucks mounted with DShK heavy machine guns firing into the residential area, a tactic residents said was intended to terrorize. Journalists and opposition leaders also reported low-altitude flights by Turkish Air Force F-16 fighter jets over the neighborhood during the operation, which they characterized as a coordinated show of force.

Beyond opening fire, security forces are accused of storming homes and beating occupants, including children, the elderly, and women. Opposition leader Former President Sharif Sheikh Ahmed condemned the security units, accusing them of cowardice against Al-Shabaab militants and brutality against unarmed civilians. Residents and journalists claim the cleared land in Tookiyo has been sold to wealthy, politically connected buyers, including a relative of the president.

Why it mattersAt least five people were killed and more than ten wounded, directly affecting the residents of the Tookiyo settlement who lost their homes and faced violence.

Somalia forms committee to boost skills and employment for youth

The Federal Government of Somalia has accelerated its national strategy for skills development and job creation by establishing a new committee to coordinate efforts across 13.

Mogadishu – Somalia's Federal Government has launched a National Committee for Skills Development and Employment, holding its first meeting today. This committee, comprising representatives from 13 federal ministries, will coordinate efforts to equip Somali workers with market-relevant skills and address high unemployment, particularly among the 70 percent of Somalis under 30.

Prime Minister Hamza Abdi Barre appointed the committee on July 18, 2026, to bridge the gap between education, training, and labor market needs. The committee, chaired by the Prime Minister’s Office, includes ministries such as Education, Labor, Agriculture, Commerce, and Telecommunications, aiming for a unified policy framework.

The comprehensive strategy focuses on digital technology, ICT, manufacturing, agriculture, livestock, and business innovation. Officials stated that aligning training programs with market demands and technological changes will reduce reliance on imported labor and services, fostering economic self-sufficiency and sustainable development.

Why it mattersThe initiative directly affects young Somalis and women by creating targeted training and employment opportunities in key sectors, aiming to reduce their high barriers to employment.

Somalia Supreme Court annuls Galmudug election results, orders new vote

The Federal Supreme Court ruled on Monday that the July 30 Galmudug elections violated the constitution and electoral law, making the results void.

Somalia’s Federal Supreme Court has annulled the results of the Galmudug state elections held on July 30, citing constitutional and electoral law violations. Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed announced the decision in Mogadishu, stating that the errors in the vote administration were too severe to be fixed and damaged the integrity of the results.

The court has ordered the National Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (NIEBC) to conduct a new election promptly, adhering strictly to the constitution, electoral law, and related regulations. This ruling follows months of tension between federal officials and Galmudug’s government regarding the election process, and questions raised about the independence of the election body.

Until new, lawfully elected officials are in place, current Galmudug officials will remain in their posts to prevent a gap in local governance. This decision raises new doubts about Somalia’s shift towards one-person, one-vote elections and adds strain to the relationship between Mogadishu and Galmudug’s leaders, especially as Somalia prepares for national elections.

Why it mattersThe annulment of the Galmudug election results directly affects citizens in Galmudug who voted, as their votes are now void, and it impacts the officials whose positions are now temporary.

Somalia, Pakistan sign defense pact, excluding combat troop deployment

Somalia and Pakistan have established their broadest defense agreement in over three decades, focusing on military training and expertise exchange without deploying Pakistani.

Somalia and Pakistan have re-established a military relationship, signing a defense memorandum of understanding on August four. The pact, signed by Somalia’s Defence Minister Ahmed Moallim Fiqi and his Pakistani counterpart Khawaja Muhammad Asif, covers counterterrorism cooperation, military training programs, and the exchange of military expertise. This framework aims to build defense capacity for both armed forces.

Following the signing, Minister Fiqi led a delegation, including Somalia's Navy and Coast Guard Commander Rear Admiral Abdiwahab Abdullahi Omar, to Pakistan's General Headquarters in Rawalpindi. There, they met Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir, Pakistan’s Chief of Army Staff, to discuss regional security and enhanced bilateral defense cooperation. The delegation also met Pakistan’s Chief of Naval Staff, Admiral Naveed Ashraf, to discuss Indian Ocean maritime security and rebuilding Somalia’s navy.

Air Chief Marshal Zaheer Ahmed Baber Sidhu offered training and institutional support to the Somali Air Force. The Somali delegation also toured Pakistan’s National Defence University and Global Industrial & Defence Solutions, a state-owned military equipment company. Officials from both countries have clarified that the agreement does not involve the deployment of Pakistani combat troops to Somalia, focusing instead on an institutional relationship.

Why it mattersThis agreement directly impacts Somali citizens by shaping the future capabilities of their national defense forces and influencing regional security dynamics.

Turkish language expands in Somalia, driven by schools and business

An estimated 100,000 Somalis now speak Turkish, a number that is growing due to expanding Turkish-run schools and increasing business ties with Turkish companies.

The Turkish language is rapidly gaining traction in Somalia, with approximately 100,000 Somalis now speaking it, according to language educators in Mogadishu. This growth is fueled by the expansion of Turkish-run schools across the capital and increasing business connections with Turkish firms.

Turkish-run Maarif schools in Mogadishu and Hargeisa teach Turkish alongside English and Arabic, with over 1,000 students enrolled. Many graduates from these institutions become fluent in three languages, enhancing their employment prospects in a competitive market.

The demand for Turkish is also driven by employment opportunities, as many young Somalis learn the language to work with Turkish companies expanding in Mogadishu. Additionally, some students study Turkish specifically to qualify for university admission in Turkey, further solidifying its presence in the country's linguistic landscape.

Why it mattersSomalis are affected by the growing prevalence of Turkish, as it offers new educational and employment opportunities but could also shift the country's linguistic identity.
