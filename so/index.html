Somali army suffers heavy losses in Al-Shabaab ambush

Hundreds of soldiers feared dead or wounded after militants attacked advancing forces near Burhakabo.

Somali army forces suffered significant casualties on Monday during an Al-Shabaab assault in the country's southwest. An estimated 2,000 troops advancing on Burhakabo were attacked in Jame’o village by militants using suicide car bombs. Al-Shabaab claimed to have killed 413 soldiers and wounded over 400, though these figures could not be independently verified.

The military's worst reverse in years saw troops fleeing within hours, with some remaining stranded in rural areas without a rescue operation, according to journalist Abdirizak Atosh. Military court chief Hassan Ali Nur Shute and ground forces commander Sahal Abdullahi Omar have opened an inquiry at Baledogle air base into the debacle. Commanders who abandoned the battlefield or military vehicles will face disciplinary action.

This rout is considered the army’s most severe defeat by insurgents since the 2023 Oswayne attack. Al-Shabaab also claimed that a Turkish air strike killed 15 captured special forces troops receiving medical treatment. Turkey reportedly destroyed some of its donated Kirpi armoured vehicles to prevent their propaganda use by militants, who claimed to have captured numerous army vehicles.

Why it mattersHundreds of Somali soldiers are dead or wounded following a major Al-Shabaab attack, impacting the nation's security forces.

AU troops to leave four bases in Somalia

Peacekeepers will withdraw next week from Lower Shabelle region due to financial strain, impacting security near Mogadishu.

African Union peacekeepers will withdraw from four forward operating bases in Somalia’s Lower Shabelle region next week. The mission is scaling back under severe financial strain, according to local media reports on Thursday.

The pullback follows Washington's agreement to extend support for the mission until December 2027, after it threatened in July to cut logistical assistance. Analysts warn that Somali forces lack the capacity to occupy the sites quickly enough to prevent them from falling to Al-Shabaab militants.

Lower Shabelle is a strategic belt southwest of the capital, and AU deployments have helped shield Mogadishu. Somalia’s president Hassan Sheikh Mohamud travelled to Uganda this week urging President Yoweri Museveni to keep Ugandan troops in Somalia.

Why it mattersSomalia's security near the capital, Mogadishu, is threatened by the withdrawal of AU troops from key bases in the Lower Shabelle region.

Somalia president cancels Russia trip

Uganda threatens troop withdrawal if President Mohamud does not engage opposition

Somalia’s President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud canceled a planned trip to Russia this week due to security and political pressures at home. The decision followed an ambush by Al Shabaab in the Lower Shabelle region on Monday that killed dozens of Somali soldiers.

Uganda, a major contributor to the African Union force, pressed Mohamud to quickly open talks with the opposition, threatening to withdraw its troops. President Yoweri Museveni declined Mohamud's request to keep Ugandan forces in Somalia, citing economic constraints and warning of an Al-Shabaab takeover if advice was unheeded.

On Sunday, Mohamud called for dialogue with the opposition, dropping his earlier refusal to compromise on contentious issues, a shift aimed at placating Museveni. Since returning from Uganda, Mohamud has sought to ease political tensions through dialogue.

Why it mattersPresident Mohamud's security and political pressures could lead to the withdrawal of Ugandan troops, risking an Al-Shabaab takeover.

African journalists adopt AI and decent work action plan

Leaders met in Addis Ababa to address artificial intelligence risks and improve journalist working conditions.

Journalist leaders from across Africa have agreed on an action plan to promote responsible artificial intelligence (AI), better working conditions, and quality journalism. The plan was finalised at a two-day meeting in Addis Ababa on 24-25 October 2026, organised by the Federation of African Journalists (FAJ). The dialogue focused on how AI impacts journalism, including its potential for research and verification, alongside risks like fabricated content and disinformation.

The meeting also addressed the poor working conditions affecting journalists, such as low pay, insecure employment, and lack of contracts. Participants discussed how AI automation might reduce newsroom staff and explored labour rights and collective bargaining to empower journalists. Safety and protection from violence and legal harassment were also key concerns, with specific attention to online harassment targeting women journalists.

The action plan is informed by FAJ's recent continental survey of 1,586 journalists across 45 countries, which examined working conditions, safety, labour rights, and media freedom. This survey provides evidence for advocacy with media organisations and governments to improve the sector.

Why it mattersJournalists in Africa are developing strategies to address risks from artificial intelligence and improve their precarious working conditions.
