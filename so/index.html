Mawisley militias quit front lines in Hiran region

Clan fighters seize regional capital and government offices as leaders push for independent administration.

Hawadle clan militias, known as Macawisley, have abandoned front-line positions in Somalia’s central Hiran region, including in the Mataban and Buulo Barde districts. The fighters seized control of the regional capital, Beledweyne, over the weekend, taking over the provincial headquarters and other government offices. Approximately 2,000 heavily armed clan fighters are now in Beledweyne.

Clan leaders are pushing to form an administration independent of the federal government in Mogadishu and the Hirshabelle state. There are concerns that Hawadle soldiers serving in the federal army in Hiran could also defect, potentially leaving remaining government-held areas exposed to Al Shabaab. The Macawisley were formed in 2022 to fight Al Shabaab with support from the federal government, Ethiopia, and the United States.

Hawadle politicians accuse the federal government of reneging on a promise to give their clan the Hirshabelle presidency and of backing incumbent regional president Ali Gudlawe in a Mogadishu-organised re-election. A former head of the National Intelligence and Security Agency stated recently that they planned to set up an administration to confront the government in Hiran and would ally with anyone, including Al Shabaab, to remove President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud.

Why it mattersHawadle clan militias have abandoned front-line positions and seized the regional capital, Beledweyne, as clan leaders seek to establish an administration separate from the federal government.

UN mission in Somalia closes after 30 years

The United Nations Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) ceased operations on Saturday, ending a 30-year UN presence.

The United Nations Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) officially closed on Saturday, concluding over three decades of UN operations in the country at the Somali government's request. This closure finalizes a two-year transition period mandated by Security Council resolutions 2753 (2024) and 2809 (2025), with UNTMIS's functions now transferred to Somali institutions and UN agencies. The UN stated that it will continue to support Somalia through its resident agencies, funds, and programmes, working with Somali authorities, the African Union, and other international partners.

A farewell ceremony was held in Mogadishu on October 26, attended by mission officer-in-charge Raisedon Zenenga and Deputy Prime Minister Salah Ahmed Jama. Jama described the closure as a "day of victory." The UN spokesperson, Stéphane Dujarric, acknowledged ongoing political, security, humanitarian, and development challenges and urged continued international support for dialogue, reconciliation, and institution-building, as well as for addressing the Al Shabaab threat.

UN missions in Somalia began in 1992 with UNOSOM operations, followed by the UN Political Office for Somalia (UNPOS) from 1995, and UN mission UNISOM. The closure occurs amidst a noted decline in funding for Somalia. The separate African Union Support and Stabilisation Mission in Somalia (AUSSOM) mandate is not affected and expires on December 31 unless extended.

Why it mattersThe closure of UNTMIS marks the end of a long-standing UN presence and the transfer of responsibilities to Somali institutions.

Somali journalists train in digital safety and ethics

Two workshops in Mogadishu and Galkayo trained 69 journalists on ethical reporting, gender equality, and digital safety.

The National Union of Somali Journalists (NUSOJ) concluded two workshops in Mogadishu and Galkayo aimed at enhancing journalists' skills in ethical reporting, gender equality, and digital safety. These sessions, held between October 25 and 29, were part of national efforts to improve women journalists' leadership, safety, and participation in Somalia's media sector. A total of 69 journalists, including 35 women and 33 men, participated, exceeding the target of 60.

The workshops, supported by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) through the Bilan Project and funded by the European Union (EU), provided a platform for journalists to discuss professional standards and conditions for safer, fairer, and more gender-inclusive newsrooms. Nima Hassan Abdi, NUSOJ organising secretary, stated that these activities sharpened practical skills and offered a safe space to discuss discrimination, digital threats, and barriers facing women in media.

Discussions covered ethical and gender-sensitive reporting, verification, balanced reporting, and avoiding inflammatory language. Participants also addressed workplace conditions including harassment, discrimination, equal development opportunities, and the importance of contracts, timely salaries, and maternity leave. Digital safety sessions focused on online harassment, cyberbullying, and the misuse of digital content, including AI-generated images.

Why it mattersSomali journalists, particularly women, will benefit from improved safety, ethical standards, and equal opportunities in their newsrooms.

Uganda begins Somalia troop pullout after 20 years

The first contingent of 109 soldiers departed Mogadishu airport on Friday, ending two decades of presence.

Uganda has started withdrawing its troops from Somalia after 20 years of fighting Al-Shabaab. A chartered aircraft carried 109 Ugandan soldiers from Mogadishu airport on Friday, marking the first departure since army chief General Muhoozi Kainerugaba announced the pullout. Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni rejected Somali President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud’s request to delay the drawdown, which began after Museveni informed Mohamud of his plans on Thursday.

Ugandan forces, stationed in the Lower Shabelle region, are handing over forward operating bases to the Somali army until November 4. Uganda first deployed troops in 2007, helping to drive Al-Shabaab from major towns. Their departure from key river-crossing towns vital to Mogadishu's security raises questions about the capital's safety, despite recent claims of improvement.

General Kainerugaba announced on Thursday that Uganda had begun withdrawing its forces after two decades of sacrifice. He stated on X that security was being handed over to Somali brothers. The withdrawal follows President Museveni's rejection of President Mohamud's plea to delay the troop movement, made during a visit to Uganda on Monday, October 26.

Why it mattersUgandan troops are withdrawing from Somalia after 20 years, impacting the security of the Lower Shabelle region and Mogadishu.

Somali party attends Arab democracy forum in Jordan

The Somali Social Solidarity Party sent its chairman and international relations representative to the October 29-30 meeting in Amman.

The Somali Social Solidarity Party (SSUP) participated in the Arab World's Community Democracy Alliance forum in Amman, Jordan, from October 29 to 30, 2026. The meeting focused on democracy, social justice, and political cooperation in the region. SSUP's chairman, Yaasiin Maaxi Macalin, represented the party, accompanied by Xaliima Rashiid, the party's international relations representative.

Jordan's Community Democratic Party hosted the event, which included delegates from various political parties and movements, as well as members of the Progressive Alliance. Discussions centered on strengthening political dialogue, inter-party cooperation, and advancing community democracy. The SSUP stated its participation demonstrates its commitment to democracy, national unity, equal citizenship, and inclusive political engagement.

The forum concluded in Amman on October 30. Participants also discussed enhancing ties and cooperation among democratic parties in the region.

Why it mattersThe Somali Social Solidarity Party engaged in regional political discussions on democracy and cooperation.
