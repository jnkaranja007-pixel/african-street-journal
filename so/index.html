Somalia targets fisheries investment to boost production

The nation produces 30,000 tonnes of fish annually, only 5% of its potential capacity.

Somalia is seeking investment to develop its fishing industry, which currently produces 30,000 tonnes of fish annually. This output represents just 5% of the nation's estimated marine fisheries production capacity of 600,000 tonnes. The gap between current and potential production is approximately 570,000 tonnes each year.

Abdi Dirshe, Director General of the Ministry of Fisheries and Blue Economy, stated that developing the sector could create over 210,000 jobs and generate between US$1.8 billion and US$3 billion in annual revenue. However, challenges like limited vessels, inadequate equipment, and security issues constrain the industry despite Somalia's 3,333-kilometre coastline.

The government's National Transformation Plan 2025–2029 prioritises private sector involvement and public-private partnerships to grow fisheries. Export markets in the United States, China, the Middle East, and East Africa are targeted, but require improvements in product quality and safety standards.

Why it mattersSomalia's underdeveloped fishing industry has the potential to create over 210,000 jobs and generate billions in revenue.

Somalia president dismisses chief justice

President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud replaced Bashe Yusuf with Ibrahim Eidle Suleiman, a ruling party member.

Somalia’s President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud dismissed Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf on Wednesday in Mogadishu. The president appointed Ibrahim Eidle Suleiman, a member of the ruling party, as the new president of the Supreme Court.

Yusuf was reportedly preparing to rule on an opposition petition challenging the government’s new constitution. The opposition and several regional states have called the constitution’s ratification process unilateral and unconstitutional.

Analysts suggest Mohamud acted to prevent a ruling against the government. Weeks earlier, Bashe Yusuf had ordered a rerun of the Galmudug regional elections.

Why it mattersPresident Mohamud dismissed Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf, who was set to rule on a constitutional challenge, and appointed a ruling party associate to the Supreme Court.

Politics · 2026-11-05

Somalia rejects Israeli ambassador, embassy plan

Mogadishu declares Israel's moves in Somaliland a violation of sovereignty, null and void.

Somalia's Foreign Ministry on Tuesday rejected Israel's appointment of an ambassador to Somaliland and plans for an embassy in Hargeisa. The ministry stated these actions violate Somalia's sovereignty and territorial integrity, asserting they were taken without the Federal Government's consent and are legally void.

Israel's first ambassador to Somaliland, Michael Lotem, presented his credentials to Somaliland President Abdirahman Mohamed Abdullahi Irro last week. A ceremony was also held to lay the cornerstone for a planned Israeli embassy in Hargeisa. Somalia maintains Somaliland is part of its territory and cannot establish independent diplomatic relations.

Israel formally recognised Somaliland on December 26, 2025, becoming the first UN member state to do so since the region declared independence in 1991. Somalia urged the international community not to recognise these steps and warned that developments could heighten tensions in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden region.

Why it mattersSomalia's territorial integrity and its claims over Somaliland are directly challenged by Israel's diplomatic recognition and embassy plans.

Turkish airstrike kills Somali woman in Jameeco

Ankara's air campaign supporting President Mohamud's government hits civilians near an SNA ambush site.

An elderly Somali woman, Hawo Madow, died on October 27 when a Turkish airstrike hit the village of Jameeco, 160 kilometres northwest of Mogadishu. The strike killed five people, including Hawo, and critically injured another man, according to village resident Suley Ibrahim Dhere. This attack followed an ambush on a Somali National Army convoy near the same highway by Al-Shabaab militants.

Suley stated that Turkish aircraft carried out the bombardment, questioning the action as the village had done nothing to provoke an attack. Hawo Madow was preparing tea when the strike occurred. Her husband, Nasir, denied any Al-Shabaab presence in the village, stating, "Not at all! We did not even see a single Al-Shabaab fighter here."

Hawo Madow is survived by three sons and two daughters. Her niece, Mumino Osman Bashow, described the airstrike shattering the lives of villagers struggling with drought. Mumino dismissed the idea that the aircraft targeted militants, stating, "We are not Al-Shabaab, and there are no Al-Shabaab fighters here."

Why it mattersCivilians like Hawo Madow are killed and injured by airstrikes conducted by Turkey in support of the Somali president.

Politics · 2026-11-05

Ugandan troops hand over Barawe and Shalanbood to Somali army

The transfer of strategic towns in Lower Shabelle tests Somalia's security capacity without foreign support.

Ugandan troops have handed over security responsibility for the towns of Barawe and Shalanbood in southern Somalia to the Somali National Army (SNA). This handover marks the end of over a decade of Ugandan military presence in these key locations, including a strategic coastal town and a settlement on a major transport route.

Ugandan military spokesman Col. Chris Magezi announced on Monday that the withdrawal of troops deployed under bilateral arrangements is effective immediately. The phased drawdown will eventually include all Ugandan forces in Somalia, with a full withdrawal expected by the end of 2027.

The departure places scrutiny on the SNA's readiness to hold territory, following previous instances where Somali forces lost control of settlements after international troops withdrew, such as Biyo Cadde and Mahas. The transition occurs amid political divisions that risk diverting resources from national security efforts.

Why it mattersSomali citizens in Barawe and Shalanbood face uncertainty about continued security following the withdrawal of Ugandan troops.
