Al-Shabaab attack kills eight near Somalia border

Suspected militants ambushed a vehicle carrying construction workers and police reservists in northeast Kenya.

Eight people died in an attack by suspected Al-Shabaab militants in northeast Kenya on Tuesday. The victims included two police reservists, five construction workers, and a driver. The attack occurred around 7 a.m. in Hagarsu, along a road connecting Mandera to other towns near the Somali border.

Mandera County Police Commander Robinson Ndiwa stated that the vehicle was sprayed with bullets as the group headed to work. Heavily armed assailants reportedly blocked the road before opening fire. Ndiwa said investigations are underway and security agencies are pursuing the attackers, who retreated into nearby thickets.

This region has a history of terror attacks, including the December 2, 2014 incident where 36 quarry miners were killed. In 2019, Al-Shabaab militants used improvised explosive devices to destroy road construction machinery valued at Sh39.8 million in the same area.

Why it mattersThe attack directly impacts construction workers and police reservists in a border region prone to violence.

Somali soldiers desert frontlines after clan leader's call

Dozens of soldiers have returned to Beledweyne, raising concerns about military cohesion amid the security transition.

Dozens of Somali National Army soldiers have left their frontline positions and gone back to Beledweyne, the administrative capital of the Hiiraan region. They acted on instructions from their traditional leader, Ugas Yusuf Ugas Hassan, who asked Hawadle clan members in the military to pull back and report to his residence. Footage shows successive groups arriving over the past two days, turning the residence into a gathering point.

The mobilisation is a serious test for the federal government, which is trying to keep the army united while African Union forces draw down and Ugandan troops also begin to withdraw. The reported departures raise doubts about frontline deployments, especially in Galgaduud, Lower Shabelle and Middle Shabelle, where government forces are fighting Al-Shabaab.

Ugas Hassan reportedly told the returned soldiers they would launch an offensive once enough of them had assembled, without naming a target or setting out a plan. He had earlier urged Hawadle clansmen to take up arms and defend Beledweyne against an offensive he said rival clans, allegedly backed by government forces, were preparing. Those allegations have not been independently established.

Why it mattersThe departures weaken Somalia's army at a time when it must take over national defence and keep fighting Al-Shabaab, and the Hawadle leader's threat of an offensive raises the risk of armed clashes in Beledweyne.

Somali police and armed residents clash over forced evictions in Mogadishu

Three protesters died and dozens of homes were demolished in Mogadishu's Daynile district, where the standoff continued into Monday.

Somali police engaged in a firefight with armed residents in Mogadishu's Daynile district on Sunday, during the forced eviction and demolition of a neighbourhood in the Warlaliska area. The clashes began after police killed three protesters that morning, and the operation destroyed dozens of homes.

Later that afternoon, armed residents ambushed the police, forcing officers to abandon several wounded colleagues and a pickup truck mounted with an anti-aircraft gun. Videos online showed residents carrying injured officers. Former President Sheikh Ahmed urged residents not to hand the officers to the government and to give them to their families instead. Former Prime Minister Hassan Ali Khaire published photographs of Mogadishu Police Commander Moalim Mahdi and Yasin Ganey, commander of police in the western section of the capital, accusing them of driving the massacres, evictions and demolitions.

Both sides held opposing positions into Monday, when no further clashes were reported.
