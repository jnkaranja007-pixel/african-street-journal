Electoral Commission Accepts Galmudug Election Rerun After Court Ruling

Somalia's electoral commission will announce a new timetable for Galmudug elections following a Supreme Court decision that nullified the July 30 vote due to uncorrectable.

Somalia’s electoral commission has accepted a Supreme Court order to rerun the Galmudug municipal and regional parliamentary elections. The Supreme Court found irregularities in the July 30 vote that could not be corrected, leading to the nullification of the results. The commission stated its compliance with the ruling, citing the law and the supremacy of legal processes in resolving electoral disputes. A new timetable for the elections will be announced at a later date.

The Supreme Court Chief Justice, Bashe Yusuf, announced this week that the Galmudug elections must be held again after receiving complaints of fraud and vote-rigging from participating parties. The court's review confirmed irregularities that it deemed uncorrectable, prompting the decision to nullify the outcome. This ruling has generated divided reactions within Somali political circles, with some viewing it as a move to demonstrate the transparency of government-administered elections to the international community.

Others have praised the decision as a positive development for strengthening the rule of law in Somalia. The electoral commission has not yet set a date for the new vote, indicating that the timetable will be released subsequently. The rerun will proceed based on the Supreme Court’s findings regarding the uncorrectable irregularities from the previous election, placing renewed scrutiny on the conduct and administration of future Galmudug votes.

Why it mattersThe rerun of Galmudug elections affects political actors and institutions involved in the electoral process and governance.

Somali Entrepreneurs Build Businesses Without Banks

Despite a history of state collapse and insecurity, Somali business owners have shown ingenuity in creating successful enterprises and fostering economic recovery.

Somalia's private sector is demonstrating significant innovation, particularly in telecommunications, where mobile money has transformed commerce and connected millions. Entrepreneurs are developing successful enterprises even without established banking systems, showcasing resilience and adaptability in challenging circumstances.

This entrepreneurial spirit is a key component of the nation's progress, contributing to economic recovery alongside debt relief agreements and diplomatic partnerships with countries like Türkiye, Qatar, and the United States. These efforts bolster the capacity to strengthen institutions and security forces.

However, this economic growth is threatened by Al-Shabaab, which exploits unemployment and lack of opportunity for recruitment. Regional partners emphasize that military action alone is insufficient, advocating for socio-economic interventions like poverty reduction and employment to address the drivers of extremism and empower youth.

Why it mattersSomali entrepreneurs are building businesses and revolutionizing commerce through mobile money, demonstrating ingenuity despite difficult circumstances and contributing to economic recovery.

Somali Journalists Trained to Combat Terrorist Propaganda

Forty media professionals and influencers in Mogadishu completed a four-day course on identifying and reporting extremist disinformation online.

Forty Somali journalists, media professionals, and social media influencers have finished a four-day training program in Mogadishu focused on countering terrorist propaganda and online disinformation. Participants from public and private media, digital platforms, and federal member states learned to identify false information, analyze media, detect fake accounts, and report extremist claims responsibly.

The training addresses how groups like Al-Shabaab and ISIS-Somalia use the information environment as part of their operations, spreading falsehoods through messaging groups and recycled footage. These tactics can quickly frighten communities and damage public confidence before verified information becomes available, creating pressure on newsrooms and institutions that struggle to correct initial false claims.

Both Al-Shabaab and ISIS-Somalia, despite differences in size and structure, rely on propaganda to distort religion, exploit grievances, and exaggerate their strength for recruitment, intimidation, and funding. Their communications are designed for international audiences, with material produced in Somalia being translated and amplified across borders.

Why it mattersJournalists and influencers are trained to identify and report extremist claims, which could affect how information about security incidents and the groups' activities is disseminated to the public.

Gode Infrastructure Lags Behind Major Industrial Investment

The city faces significant pressure from planned fertilizer and oil refinery projects unless infrastructure and public services are urgently upgraded.

Gode City is poised for substantial industrial growth with the development of a large-scale fertilizer complex and an oil refinery, projects expected to produce three million metric tons of urea and process three point five million metric tons of crude oil annually, respectively. These investments have the potential to transform the city into an industrial hub, creating employment and stimulating supporting businesses. However, the scale of these projects will place immense pressure on Gode's existing infrastructure and public services, requiring significant upgrades to accommodate the influx of workers and service providers.

The central challenge for Gode is not the completion of these industrial projects, but its preparedness to manage the ensuing economic and population growth. A comprehensive corridor-development program is recommended to connect the city center, airport, industrial sites, markets, and transport routes. This program should include durable roads, effective drainage, street lighting, pedestrian walkways, organized parking, public transportation, and coordinated utility infrastructure.

The Somali Regional Government must prioritize Gode for immediate infrastructure development to avoid difficulties in managing urban pressures once industrial operations commence. Proactive planning and investment in public services and infrastructure are crucial to ensure that Gode can successfully transition into a major industrial center without being overwhelmed by its own growth.

Why it mattersFarmers may face challenges with market access and input availability if infrastructure development does not keep pace with industrial expansion.

UN Ends 13 Years of Somalia Justice and Prison Support

The United Nations Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia officially concluded its direct support program for the nation's justice institutions and prison services on August.

The United Nations Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) officially ended its 13-year direct support program for the country's justice institutions and prison services on August 25, 2026. A ceremony in Mogadishu marked the conclusion of UN rule-of-law assistance, with Justice and Constitutional Affairs Minister Hassan Moalim Mohamud thanking the UN and international partners for their contributions.

Minister Hassan stated that the end of UNTMIS activities represents a significant step towards the Federal Government and national institutions assuming full ownership of the justice system's development. He expressed confidence that the knowledge and capacity built during the UNTMIS support period will form a strong foundation for future advancements in Somalia’s justice and prison services.

The federal government affirmed its commitment to continuing the development of justice and correctional institutions, emphasizing the goal of national bodies fully taking responsibility for these services. Officials also highlighted the importance of ongoing cooperation and coordination between the Somali government and international partners to support this continued development.

Why it mattersThe conclusion of UN support affects national institutions by requiring them to take full ownership of justice and prison services, potentially impacting their capacity and development.
