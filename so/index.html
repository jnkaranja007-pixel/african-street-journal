Al-Shabaab ambush kills eight near Kenya-Somalia border

Attackers blocked a road in Mandera County and fired on a vehicle carrying police reservists and construction workers to work.

Eight people, including two police reservists, five construction workers and a driver, were killed on Tuesday when suspected Al-Shabaab militants ambushed a vehicle in northeast Kenya. Mandera County Police Commander Robinson Ndiwa said the attack happened at about 7 a.m. in Hagarsu, on the Mandera-Arabia-Fino-Lafey-Elwak road near the Somali border.

Residents saw heavily armed assailants block the road before opening fire on the vehicle, Ndiwa said. He said the motive had not yet been established, and that combined security agencies were pursuing the attackers, who retreated into nearby thickets.

The area recorded its worst terror attack on December 2, 2014, when 36 quarry miners were killed in their tents. In December 2019, militants used improvised explosive devices to destroy road construction machinery at Hagarsu Bida, valued at Sh39.8 million.

Why it mattersRoad crews and police reservists travelling this stretch of the Mandera border road now face the same ambush risk that has already killed workers here before.

UN experts call Somalia's chief justice dismissal a blatant interference

United Nations experts say the dismissal of Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed breaches the independence of Somalia's judiciary and back the Supreme Court's ruling against it.

United Nations independent experts have condemned President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's abrupt dismissal of Somalia's Chief Justice, Bashe Yusuf Ahmed, as a "blatant interference with the independence of the judiciary." In a statement issued on 12 November, the rapporteurs on judicial independence and on human rights and counter-terrorism said the presidential decree of 4 November was a deliberate circumvention of constitutional safeguards, issued the night before the Supreme Court was to rule on extending presidential and parliamentary terms from four to five years.

No proposal was sought from the Judicial Services Commission and parliamentary approval was not obtained. Three days later a replacement chief justice was sworn in at a ceremony attended by the President, an act the experts said further eroded the rule of law. The experts also called for the immediate restoration of protection for seven judges whose personal security was withdrawn despite acknowledged threats, and for security forces to be pulled back from the Supreme Court.

On 5 November, five Supreme Court judges ruled the term extensions unlawful, declared the mandates of the President and Parliament expired and ordered elections within 90 days. The same bench found the removal of Chief Justice Ahmed unconstitutional. The government refused to comply, and the experts warned that irregular reform and a weak judiciary risk undermining Somalia's fight against Al-Shabaab.

Why it mattersSeven judges who ruled against the government have lost their personal protection and are barred from travel, leaving the court's rulings on the mandates in doubt.

US troops leave Somalia's Baledogle airfield

Runway repair costs of tens of millions of dollars prompted the US Africa Command to withdraw personnel.

United States troops have withdrawn from Baledogle Military Airfield in southern Somalia due to the deteriorating condition of the runway, which had become unsafe for aircraft. Army Lieutenant General John Brennan, deputy commander of US Africa Command (AFRICOM), stated that the decision was purely economic, with most personnel relocated to Mogadishu. Resurfacing the runway would have cost tens of millions of dollars, Brennan told reporters.

Baledogle, located about 90-100 kilometres northwest of Mogadishu in the Lower Shabelle region, has served as a major hub for US counter-terrorism operations and training of Somalia’s Danab special forces since around 2012. The base experienced a large Al-Shabaab assault in September 2019. Despite risks, the US Navy awarded a contract of over $12 million for emergency runway repairs in 2018, and a new medical facility was announced in May 2025.

AFRICOM stated that the departure does not signify a reduction in American support for Somali security forces fighting Al-Shabaab, with US airstrikes continuing. Officials indicated a possible return to the airfield if conditions change, though no firm plans exist. Ugandan and Somali forces reportedly remain at the site. The US previously ordered nearly 700 personnel out in late 2020, with hundreds returning in 2022.

Why it mattersUS troops have withdrawn from Baledogle Military Airfield, a strategic hub for counter-terrorism operations, due to runway repair costs exceeding tens of millions of dollars.

Turkey denies plans to send 15,000 troops to Somalia

Ankara says its existing forces will keep to training and advisory work after Uganda began pulling its own troops out.

Turkey has denied reports that it plans to send 15,000 troops to Somalia, saying the claims do not reflect the truth. Ministry of National Defense spokesperson Rear Admiral Zeki Aktürk said on Thursday that Turkish forces already in the country would continue their training and advisory mission.

Aktürk said the Somalia-Turkish Task Force and the Air Element Command were helping to build Somalia's counter-terrorism capacity through military assistance, training and advisory activities. The figure first appeared in early November in Somali media, which cited unnamed security sources and claimed Turkey had agreed to deploy soldiers to Barawe, Baidoa and Mogadishu before January 2027.

Uganda has begun withdrawing its forces from Somalia after nearly 20 years, with all Ugandan personnel due to leave by the end of 2027. Turkey's main base is Camp TURKSOM in Mogadishu, opened in 2017, and its mandate in the country has been extended through July 2028.

Why it mattersUganda's withdrawal leaves Mogadishu and key routes in Lower Shabelle with fewer protecting forces, and Turkey's mandate now runs through July 2028.
