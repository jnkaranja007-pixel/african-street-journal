Ugandan troops hand over Barawe and Shalanbood to Somali army

Somali National Army takes control of two strategic towns in Lower Shabelle region from Ugandan forces.

Ugandan troops have handed over security responsibility for the towns of Barawe and Shalanbood in southern Somalia to the Somali National Army (SNA). This transfer ends over a decade of Ugandan military presence in the Lower Shabelle region.

The handover includes military bases in Barawe, a coastal town and port, and Shalanbood, a settlement on a key transport route. Ugandan military spokesman Col. Chris Magezi announced on Monday that the withdrawal of troops deployed under bilateral arrangements is immediate.

The departure places scrutiny on the SNA's readiness to hold territory, especially in a region where Al-Shabaab maintains a significant presence. Previous transfers have seen Somali forces lose control of settlements like Biyo Cadde and El Baraf.

Why it mattersSomali security forces must now secure territory previously held by foreign troops, testing their ability to counter Al-Shabaab and maintain government control.

One in 10 Somali women journalists feel safe

A new report by the National Union of Somali Journalists reveals widespread harassment and pay issues.

A new report by the National Union of Somali Journalists (NUSOJ) found that only one in 10 women journalists in Somalia feel safe at work. The report, "Unsafe, Underpaid and Unheard: The Workplace Crisis Facing Women Journalists in Somalia," surveyed women in Mogadishu and six federal member states.

More than half of the surveyed journalists reported feeling unsafe or very unsafe, with many experiencing harassment in newsrooms, in the field, and online, including AI-generated deepfakes. NUSOJ Secretary-General Omar Faruk Osman stated that women journalists face greater risks and lack protection, demanding written contracts, timely wages, and consequences for perpetrators.

The report also highlights that 46.5% of women journalists have been denied promotions due to their gender, and 63.8% received no workplace training. These findings were released on the International Day to End Impunity for Crimes against Journalists, with support from the UNDP and funding from the European Union.

Why it mattersWomen journalists in Somalia face significant safety, pay, and career advancement challenges, impacting their ability to work and report freely.

Somalia opposition agrees to talks with president

The National Salvation Forum will send a three-member delegation to meet a government committee on March 14.

Somalia’s opposition National Salvation Forum has agreed to give President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud a final chance to resolve political disputes through dialogue. The decision follows meetings in Mogadishu where opposition figures clashed with Mohamud over constitutional amendments and electoral plans.

Previous negotiation attempts failed, with the opposition accusing the president of unilateral reforms that could entrench his power. The dispute centres on constitutional changes and a proposed electoral system that leaders fear could allow Mohamud a third term. The opposition wants an agreement reached through direct, Somali-led negotiations.

As a first step, a three-member opposition delegation will meet a government technical committee on March 14 to agree on an agenda for future talks. The delegation includes former Galmudug state president Abdikarim Hussein Guled and lawmakers Abdirahman Abdishakur and Abdullahi Arab.

Why it mattersSomali citizens face uncertainty over constitutional changes and the framework for future elections.

Uganda begins Somalia troop withdrawal

Uganda's military started pulling out troops deployed under bilateral agreements, with all forces to leave by end of 2027.

Uganda has started withdrawing its troops from Somalia under bilateral agreements with the federal government, UPDF spokesman Chris Magezi announced Monday.

The first group of Ugandan soldiers has already returned home, and the complete withdrawal of forces deployed bilaterally will occur within days, Magezi stated.

All Ugandan forces, including those with the African Union mission, will be out of Somalia by the close of 2027, allowing Somali forces to take full security control.

Why it mattersUganda's military withdrawal will transfer full security responsibility to Somali Armed Forces.
