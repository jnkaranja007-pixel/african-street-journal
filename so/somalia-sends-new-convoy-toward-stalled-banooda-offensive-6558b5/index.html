Somalia sends new convoy toward stalled Banooda offensive

Residents reported dozens of vehicles heading toward Yaqbariwayne after Somali forces retreated following the 26 October attack.

Source: Somali Guardian · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Somalia’s federal government has sent a military convoy from Mogadishu toward Yaqbariwayne village, renewing the stalled Operation Banooda in Bay region. Residents near Bellidogle airbase reported dozens of vehicles, with some estimates above 100, including fuel and supply trucks, armored personnel carriers and gun-mounted vehicles.

The deployment follows the 26 October Jameeco-Leego attack, after which Somali forces retreated. The earlier offensive had suffered heavy losses, with hundreds of soldiers killed, and the government ordered the arrest of commanders who led it. More than 1,000 soldiers from a 3,000-strong contingent trained with Saudi funding were recently moved from Hanan Bure, but their share of the new force is unclear.

Banooda was launched to capture Burhakabo and other towns held by forces loyal to former Southwest president Abdiasis Laftagaren. Al-Shabaab attacks on the main roads had forced the earlier retreat, and the convoy’s ability to advance remains uncertain.

Why it mattersTroops and commanders must contend with Al-Shabaab attacks on the main roads as the government renews its push toward towns in Bay region.
