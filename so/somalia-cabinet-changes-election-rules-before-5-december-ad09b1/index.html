Somalia cabinet changes election rules before 5 December court deadline

The amendments introduce closed party lists and a 10% national-vote threshold for recognition as a national party.

Source: Somali Guardian · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Somalia’s cabinet approved amendments to 23 articles of the National Elections Law and eight articles of the law governing political organisations and parties at a meeting on Tuesday. Under the changes, parliamentary candidates will run on closed party lists, and any organisation winning at least 10% of the national vote becomes a recognised national party.

The cabinet acted 11 days before the Supreme Court’s 5 December deadline for the government and opposition to agree an electoral framework. The court had earlier ruled that the four-year mandate of President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud, which expired in May, gives the government no automatic right to extend it; the government rejects the ruling’s force, while the opposition regards it as the basis for a legitimate process.

Local councils, federal member state assemblies and both houses of parliament would be elected directly, with the president and other senior officials then chosen by those bodies. The government says the changes align older laws with the revised constitution.

Why it mattersThe government and opposition face the Supreme Court’s 5 December deadline to agree an electoral framework as the cabinet has approved changes to the rules.
