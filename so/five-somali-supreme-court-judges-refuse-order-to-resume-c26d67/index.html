Five Somali Supreme Court judges refuse order to resume duties

They say armed forces still control the court and that their personal protection has not been restored since the Nov. 5 rulings.

Source: Somali Guardian · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Five Somali Supreme Court judges have refused an order to resume work, telling Chief Justice Ibrahim Idle Suleiman that a Nov. 25 deadline had no legal basis. They say security forces still occupy the court building in Mogadishu and their personal protection has been withdrawn.

In their letter, the judges said they had not abandoned their posts and could not work independently amid threats, surveillance and restrictions on movement. The Nov. 5 rulings found President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud’s term extension unlawful and the dismissal of Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed invalid. The judges demanded the forces leave, their protection be restored and the decisions be enforced.

The presidency rejected the rulings, and the state attorney general said they had no legal effect. On Nov. 18, six judges, including the five who signed the term-extension ruling, challenged the government’s actions at the East African Court of Justice in Arusha.

Why it mattersThe five judges’ return to work depends on the withdrawal of security forces, restoration of their protection and enforcement of the Nov. 5 decisions.
