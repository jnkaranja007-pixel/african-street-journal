Morocco tightens African tomato exports as prices fall in Europe

At Rungis, Moroccan tomatoes now fetch €1.10 to €1.30 per kilogram, down from €1.80 to €2 as shipments rise and sales slow.

Source: Hespress English · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Moroccan tomato prices at France’s Rungis wholesale market have fallen to €1.10 to €1.30 per kilogram from €1.80 to €2, while authorities have tightened exports to African countries to protect domestic supply. A Moroccan industry source at Rungis attributed the European price drop to increased shipments and slower sales.

The source said traders were cutting prices to avoid losses on perishable tomatoes. Agriculture Minister Khalid Hatimi told Parliament that Morocco must establish domestic needs for essential farm products before allocating supplies abroad. He said European exports use established contracts, unlike shipments to African markets, where restrictions have tightened.

The Rungis source said African-bound shipments often relied on purchases from Inezgane’s wholesale market, reducing local availability and contributing to higher domestic prices. More tomatoes are expected from new Souss-Massa crops over the next two weeks, adding to European supplies.

Why it mattersAfrican-market suppliers buying through Inezgane face tighter export restrictions as Morocco prioritises domestic tomato availability.
