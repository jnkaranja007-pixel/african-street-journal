Morocco allocates MAD 400 million in transport subsidies

Government aims to shield consumers from rising fuel costs with increased direct payments to transport operators.

Morocco will provide MAD 400 million in direct subsidies to road transport professionals, supporting approximately 200,000 vehicles. Transport Minister Abdessamad Kayouh announced on Thursday that this new allocation is a 60% increase over the initial subsidy tranche. Payments will be disbursed every 15 days, starting November 10, to offset rising fuel prices and their impact on transport fares and consumer goods.

The subsidy is intended to absorb energy cost increases affecting passenger transport and the movement of essential goods, according to Minister Kayouh. He urged transport operators not to raise official fares, stating the financial support aims to protect end consumers and maintain logistics sector stability. The support applies to vehicles registered with the Transport Ministry and NARSA that meet eligibility criteria.

Government spokesperson Samir Belfkih stated that protecting household purchasing power is a strategic priority, with measures to increase incomes and curb inflation. The government is also preparing legislation to create six regional companies next year to organize markets and regulate distribution, addressing intermediaries who account for nearly 70% of some product prices. The government has ruled out capping fuel company profits or reducing fuel taxes.

Why it mattersMoroccan transport operators and consumers will benefit from direct subsidies and measures to control price inflation.

Morocco cabinet approves senior government appointments

Fatima Zahra Chaaba named director at the Ministry of Justice, among other key roles filled.

Morocco’s government approved appointments to senior positions across several ministries and public institutions on Thursday. Fatima Zahra Chaaba was appointed director of the National Institute for Court Clerks and Legal and Judicial Professions at the Ministry of Justice. Taoufik Mediani was appointed director of Legislation on Governance, Rights and Freedoms at the General Secretariat of the Government.

In economic sectors, Yassine Ahmimi was appointed director of Statistics, Studies, Monitoring and Evaluation at the Ministry of Industry, Trade, Investment and Economic Integration. Chafik Ssalouh was named director of Customs Control and Investigations at the Customs and Indirect Tax Administration. Safaa Reddaf was appointed director of Civil Aviation at the Ministry of Transport and Logistics.

Hafida Khouyi was appointed director of the National Higher Institute of Music and Choreographic Arts at the Ministry of Youth, Culture and Sports. El Houcine Haida was appointed director of the Skhirat-Temara Urban Agency, and Hicham El Khersani was named director of the Rabat-Salé Urban Agency. Said Kobra’s appointment as director of the Errachidia-Midelt Urban Agency was renewed.

Why it mattersThese appointments fill senior roles within Moroccan government ministries and public institutions.

Morocco secures US$112 million loan for port strategy

The French Development Agency loan will fund infrastructure, renewable energy, and fishing port modernization until 2030.

Morocco's National Ports Agency has secured US$112 million in financing from the French Development Agency (AFD) for its 2023-2030 investment programme. The 13-year loan, signed in Rabat on November 3, 2026, will support upgrades to port infrastructure, renewable energy and desalination facilities, fishing ports, and port quay electrification.

The financing specifically targets ports in southern Morocco, including Tan-Tan, Tarfaya, Laâyoune, Boujdour, Dakhla, and Lamhiriz, aiming to boost regional economic development and strengthen the country's Atlantic coast position. This loan is structured as a non-sovereign loan, placing repayment risk on the agency rather than the Moroccan government.

Moroccan ports processed 262.6 million tonnes of cargo in 2025, an increase of 8.9% from 241.2 million tonnes in 2024, with Tanger Med handling 10.3 million TEUs. The development of southern ports like Dakhla and Laâyoune is intended to capture transit trade with Sahel economies, a market currently served by West African ports.

Why it mattersMoroccan businesses and the national economy will benefit from improved port infrastructure and expanded trade routes.

Morocco prison population exceeds 102,000

Overcrowding reaches 158% as prison chief urges judges to reduce pretrial detentions.

Morocco's prisons held 102,941 inmates by the end of October, a rise from 98,692 a year prior, according to prison chief Mohamed Saleh Tamek. The national occupancy rate now stands at 158%, with some facilities exceeding 300% capacity. This strain impacts basic services, security, and daily operations.

Tamek informed lawmakers on Wednesday that the increasing inmate numbers are a primary obstacle to improving prison conditions. The growing population also burdens infrastructure, equipment, and staff, while escalating operating costs. He urged judicial authorities to decrease reliance on pretrial detention and utilize alternative sentences more effectively.

Despite legal reforms and the application of alternative sentences to 3,408 individuals between January and September, the prison population continues to grow. Automatic sentence reductions have benefited 152,487 cases, leading to 12,547 releases, but these measures are insufficient to curb the trend. Tamek stated that addressing the factors driving the growing number of people entering the prison system is necessary, not just expanding capacity or releasing inmates.

Why it mattersMorocco's prison overcrowding strains resources and affects the daily lives and conditions of over 102,000 inmates.

Three men attack Marrakesh hotel

Morocco faced Islamist terrorism for the first time in 1994 when three attackers targeted tourists at the Atlas Asni hotel.

In 1994, three men opened fire on tourists at the Atlas Asni hotel in Marrakesh. This event marked Morocco's first experience with Islamist terrorism.

The attack occurred during a period when Algeria was experiencing its own civil war, known as the Black Decade, between 1991 and 2002. This conflict pitted the Algerian government and army against Islamist groups.

Morocco had been spared the violence of Islamist terrorism prior to this 1994 incident, contrasting with the ongoing conflict in neighboring Algeria.

Why it mattersMoroccan tourists and residents were directly affected by the terrorist attack on the Atlas Asni hotel in Marrakesh.
