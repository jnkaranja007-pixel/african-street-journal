Health · 2026-10-28

Third child dies of diphtheria in Morocco

A 12-year-old boy in Ksar El Kebir died, prompting calls for better school health measures.

A 12-year-old boy from Ksar El Kebir has died from diphtheria in northern Morocco, marking the third child death from the disease. The boy died early Tuesday at Lalla Meryem Provincial Hospital in Larache after being unvaccinated and receiving intensive care. Health facilities in the Tangier-Tetouan-Al Hoceima region have reported four cases, with three patients recovering.

Parents' associations are demanding urgent vaccination and health monitoring in schools following the deaths. Said Kachani, president of the National Confederation of Parents’ Associations, noted progress in vaccination campaigns but acknowledged delays in preventive actions and coordination. Noureddine Akkouri, president of the National Federation of Parents’ Associations, urged families to check vaccination records and called for closer cooperation between health and education authorities.

Akkouri also demanded stronger health monitoring in schools and regular official updates for families, stating schools must remain safe places for learning. Diphtheria is a bacterial infection primarily affecting the respiratory system, with vaccination being the main preventive measure. Two other children recently died from the disease in Salé’s Sidi Abdellah neighbourhood, where health authorities initiated contact tracing and screening.

Why it mattersThree children have died of diphtheria in Morocco, leading to demands for improved school health measures.

Agriculture · 2026-10-28

Moroccan macaques raid orchards for easier meals

Endangered Barbary macaques in the Middle Atlas forests are turning to farms for food, researchers found.

Morocco's endangered Barbary macaques are increasingly raiding fruit orchards for food, a new study reveals. Researchers observed that monkeys living near farms spent less time foraging and travelled shorter distances compared to those in natural forest habitats. This shift is driven by the accessible and concentrated food supply orchards offer, especially as forest vegetation declines due to drought.

A study published in Global Ecology and Conservation between 2015 and 2023 found that the recruitment of immature macaques in forest populations fell by approximately half. While access to agricultural food was associated with better recruitment in the orchard population, researchers could not establish that eating crops alone caused the difference. These monkeys also displayed greater avoidance of humans in agricultural areas, recognizing the risks.

The macaques' reliance on farmland poses a challenge for farmers who may suffer crop losses and view the animals as pests. Protecting the endangered Barbary macaques, a key species in Morocco's Middle Atlas forests, requires ensuring their food security. However, this new feeding behaviour risks intensifying human-wildlife conflict.

Why it mattersMoroccan farmers face crop losses as endangered Barbary macaques raid their orchards for food.

Business · 2026-10-28

Ryanair adds 17 new Morocco routes

The airline will offer 5.3 million seats across 156 routes in its largest winter program for Morocco.

Ryanair is launching 17 new routes in Morocco for its winter 2026 program, its largest ever in the country, offering 5.3 million seats on 156 routes. This expansion adds 600,000 seats, a 12% capacity increase, connecting Morocco to 14 countries via 13 airports.

CEO Eddie Wilson announced the new routes from Germany, the UK, and Ireland, coinciding with the opening of Ryanair's fifth Moroccan base in Rabat with two aircraft. This new base brings the total number of Ryanair aircraft in Morocco to 16, with plans to station more of the 300 aircraft on order over the next seven years.

The airline's expansion represents a $1.6 billion investment and supports over 8,500 local jobs, according to the Moroccan National Tourism Office (ONMT). Wilson stated that international tourists typically spend between €700 and €1,000 per stay, benefiting local businesses and boosting destinations beyond major tourist hubs.

Why it mattersRyanair's expansion in Morocco represents a cumulative investment of $1.6 billion and supports more than 8,500 local jobs.

News · 2026-10-28

Morocco road accidents kill 23 in one week

Nearly 3,000 people were injured in 2,241 urban traffic incidents between October 19 and 25.

23 people died and 2,995 were injured in 2,241 road accidents across Morocco's urban areas from October 19 to 25. The General Directorate of National Security (DGSN) reported 132 of the injured sustained serious harm.

Driver inattention was the primary cause of these accidents, according to the DGSN report. Other significant factors included failure to yield, loss of vehicle control, pedestrian inattention, and speeding. Police also recorded 52,459 traffic violations during the same week.

Authorities issued 8,190 reports to prosecutors and collected MAD 9.52 million in fixed fines. Police impounded 5,606 vehicles and seized 8,190 documents as part of traffic enforcement operations.

Why it matters23 people died and nearly 3,000 were injured in road accidents in Morocco.

Politics · 2026-10-28

Moroccan parliament elects two commission presidents

Moulay Abderrahmane Blila of the RNI party was elected president of the Justice, Legislation and Human Rights Commission.

Morocco's upper house of parliament, the Chambre des conseillers, elected new presidents for two permanent commissions on Tuesday. The votes took place during a plenary session in Rabat.

Moulay Abderrahmane Blila, representing the Rassemblement national des indépendants (RNI) group, secured the presidency of the Commission of Justice, Legislation and Human Rights. Another president was elected for the Commission of the Interior, Local Authorities and Infrastructure.

The elections are part of the ongoing work of the parliamentary body. No further details on the elected officials or the commissions' future agendas were provided in the report.

Why it mattersThe Moroccan parliament elected new leaders for two permanent commissions, affecting the country's legislative process.
