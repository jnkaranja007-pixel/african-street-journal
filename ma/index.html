Morocco plans no-extra-cost roaming across more mobile networks

The government wants operators that are alone in an area to open their networks to other subscribers, with phones switching automatically.

Morocco plans to expand national roaming so mobile subscribers can use another operator’s network at no change in tariff, with phones switching automatically. Digital Transition Minister Ahmed Rahhou announced the plan on Monday in the House of Representatives, answering a question about extending telecom services nationwide.

The service already covers more than 7,000 zones. The ministry plans to require an operator that is the only one covering an area to admit subscribers of rival networks, because some zones cannot economically support all three operators.

The ministry will study satellite coverage for remote “white zones” with telecom regulator ANRT and operators, subject to safety and security requirements. A preliminary survey checked nearly 50,000 kilometres of roads; about 15,700 kilometres of provincial roads remain to be checked. Those checks will continue in 2027, and the ministry wants operators to cover the roads within a year or two at most. A rural internet plan is also expected to reach 2,000 weakly covered or uncovered zones this year.

Why it mattersSubscribers in areas served by only one operator would gain access to other networks without a tariff change; the ministry intends to extend road coverage within a year or two at most.

Morocco and Mauritania move to update 1986 trade pact

The two governments also plan a framework for agricultural and food shipments and an investment forum in Mauritania in early 2027.

Morocco and Mauritania have agreed to work toward updating their trade agreement, signed in August 1986, and to develop joint projects in agriculture, food processing, fisheries and energy. The plans were announced in a joint statement after trade ministers Omar Hejira of Morocco and Zeinebou Mint Ahmednah of Mauritania met in Nouakchott on Tuesday.

The countries will form a working group of trade ministries and relevant sectors to set rules for moving agricultural and food products between them before the end of January 2027. A second session of their Joint Trade Commission is scheduled for Morocco on January 27, 2027, while an investment forum is planned in Mauritania in early 2027.

The forum’s preparations will be led by Morocco’s Agency for the Development of Investments and Exports and Mauritania’s Investment Promotion Agency. The two sides also reached an initial agreement to establish a wholesale market in Nouakchott, with cold storage and slaughterhouses, to improve product marketing and regulate export channels.

Why it mattersTrade ministries and businesses handling agricultural and food shipments must develop a cross-border framework before the end of January 2027.

Morocco rises to 78th in 2025 climate risk ranking

Germanwatch’s index places the country 18 spots higher than in 2024, while its ranking for the 1996–2025 period fell to 99th.

Morocco ranked 78th among countries affected by extreme weather in 2025, up from 96th in 2024, according to the Climate Risk Index 2027, published by Germanwatch on Tuesday. The report was released during the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Türkiye.

The index measures recorded disaster impacts using six indicators, including deaths, people affected and economic losses. Germanwatch said the annual and 30-year rankings measure different periods: Morocco placed 99th for 1996–2025, compared with 83rd in the previous assessment covering 1995–2024.

The rankings do not measure future climate vulnerability, and shifts in position do not necessarily mean losses changed by the same amount. Germanwatch said damage is underreported in developing countries, particularly in Africa, and called for stronger early-warning systems, adaptation investment and international financing.

Why it mattersMorocco's recorded position differs across the annual and 30-year assessments, while Germanwatch called for early-warning systems, adaptation investment and international financing.

Waltz backs Morocco’s autonomy plan for Sahara

The U.S. ambassador to the United Nations restated Washington’s position during talks in Rabat with Foreign Minister Nasser Bourita.

Mike Waltz, the U.S. ambassador to the United Nations, met Morocco’s Foreign Minister Nasser Bourita in Rabat on Tuesday, 17 November, to discuss the Sahara dispute. Bourita reaffirmed Morocco’s commitment to a settlement granting autonomy under Moroccan sovereignty.

Waltz restated Washington’s recognition of Morocco’s sovereignty over the Sahara, according to Maroc Diplomatique. The discussions also covered meetings in Madrid and Washington and the withdrawal of the United Nations Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara (MINURSO) observers.

Maroc Diplomatique reported that the mission’s withdrawal had begun the day before. Waltz called on the separatist group to respect the ceasefire and rule out renewed hostilities; the reports gave no further details of the discussions.

Why it mattersMorocco’s foreign minister is pursuing an autonomy-based settlement, while Waltz urged the separatist group to maintain the ceasefire.

Sport · 2026-11-18

AS FAR places four players in CAF Champions League team

The Moroccan club won the 2026 women's title in Egypt and became the first side to retain the trophy, the Confederation of African Football said.

Morocco’s AS FAR placed four players in the Confederation of African Football (CAF) Women’s Champions League 2026 team of the tournament, announced Tuesday. The competition ran from 1 to 15 November in Egypt, where the club secured its third title.

The CAF said the 4-4-2 selection recognised players for their technical performances, consistency and decisive contributions during the tournament. AS FAR was the first club to retain the women’s Champions League title, according to the confederation.

AS FAR coach Hicham El Idrissi was named to lead the team of the tournament. The club’s title came after a 3-1 final win over South Africa’s Mamelodi Sundowns.

Why it mattersAS FAR’s four selected players and coach Hicham El Idrissi represent the Moroccan club in the CAF’s tournament team after its third continental title.
