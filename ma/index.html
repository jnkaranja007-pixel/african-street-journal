Casablanca power cuts hit several districts

The SRM Casablanca-Settat cited a national grid incident for the widespread power outage affecting the city.

Several Casablanca districts remained without electricity on Wednesday, November 11, by evening, despite power being restored in some areas. The Société régionale multiservices (SRM) Casablanca-Settat blamed an incident on the national grid of the ONEE for the disruption.

The power cuts began in the late afternoon, affecting multiple neighbourhoods across the metropolis. Power was notably restored to the city centre, Bourgone, and Sidi Maarouf districts by Wednesday evening.

No timeline for a full return to normal electricity supply has been announced by the SRM Casablanca-Settat. The outage lasted nearly four hours in some parts of the city.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in Casablanca experienced power outages on November 11, impacting daily life and operations.

Storm knocks out power across Casablanca and nearby areas

A fault in the national transmission network left several Casablanca districts and Nouaceur province without electricity on Wednesday afternoon.

Wednesday, November 11, brought thunderstorms and strong gusts that damaged Morocco's national high-voltage transmission network, cutting power across Casablanca and neighbouring areas. The Casablanca-Settat Regional Multiservice Company (SRM) said the outage began at 4:48 p.m. after an extra-high-voltage line operated by the National Office of Electricity and Drinking Water (ONEE) failed under the weather.

The fault cut supply to several SRM substations serving homes, shops and factories. Central Casablanca, Maârif, Al Fida, Aïn Chock, Ben M'Sik, Sbata, Sidi Othmane and Hay Hassani were hit, along with Bouskoura, Oulad Azzouz and Dar Bouazza in Nouaceur province. Traffic lights at major junctions went dark during the evening rush.

SRM said the failure started in ONEE's transmission grid, not its own distribution system, and that its teams were working with ONEE and switching supply routes to limit the impact. The company apologised to customers and described the fault as beyond its control, but did not say how many customers were affected or when full service would return.

Why it mattersHouseholds, shops and factories in eight Casablanca districts and three Nouaceur communities lost power in the late afternoon.

Morocco housing aid backlog hits 109,000

Over 109,000 eligible applicants have not received housing subsidies, while the country faces a 300,000-unit deficit.

Housing Minister Samir Belfkih told Parliament that more than 109,000 applicants who qualified for Morocco's direct housing assistance have yet to receive support. Of 237,456 eligible applications received by November 4, only 127,707 had been paid, leaving beneficiaries at 53.8% of those qualified. The country still faces a housing deficit estimated at 300,000 units.

Belfkih presented the figures while outlining the draft 2027 budget for his department. The scheme, which subsidises an average of 20% of the purchase price, has been allocated MAD 10.5 billion and has supported property purchases worth MAD 52.5 billion. It assisted 33,649 people in 2024, 48,671 in 2025 and 45,387 so far in 2026, and the minister said it coincided with more construction activity and housing finance.

Thousands of households also live in informal settlements or in buildings at risk of collapse. The ministry counts 121,940 households still in slums, of which 8,921 are covered by completed housing, 69,080 by projects under construction and 39,785 by projects still under study. Since 2004 the Cities Without Slums programme has improved conditions for 392,284 households, at a cost of MAD 76 billion.

Why it mattersMore than 109,000 qualified applicants are still waiting for the state subsidy that would help them buy a home.

Morocco launches business growth program in Casablanca

The PACTE TPME initiative aims to tailor support to very small, small, and medium-sized enterprises across all 12 regions.

Morocco PME organized a meeting in Casablanca on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, as part of its national tour for the PACTE TPME program, themed 'PACTE TPME: your growth accelerator'. This initiative, touring all 12 regions of the Kingdom, seeks to bring business support systems closer to companies and adapt them to local economic realities.

Launched on April 29, 2026, the program is part of the Acceleration and Transformation Plan for TPME, integrated into the ORBIT 2030 strategic development plan. Anouar Alaoui Ismaili, director general of Morocco PME, stated that PACTE TPME reflects a desire for more integrated and differentiated public support, considering each company's maturity and ambitions.

The PACTE TPME program focuses on four areas: fundamentals, resilience, competitiveness, and growth, with eight devices covering management, performance, quality, environmental transition, and development acceleration. The Casablanca meeting included presentations, discussions on development challenges, and individual consultations for entrepreneurs to present needs and learn about access to support.

Why it mattersMoroccan entrepreneurs will receive more tailored support to consolidate activities and achieve growth.

Moroccan football assembly set for December 12

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation's general assembly will elect new leadership on December 12.

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF) announced on Monday that its elective general assembly will take place on December 12. The meeting was chaired by FRMF president Fouzi Lekjaa.

During the meeting, Lekjaa emphasized the importance of discussions among the federation's officials. The FRMF's steering committee made the announcement regarding the assembly date.

The FRMF's general assembly will elect new leadership for the football body. The federation's rules of play are also expected to evolve.

Why it mattersMoroccan football stakeholders will participate in electing new leadership for the FRMF.
