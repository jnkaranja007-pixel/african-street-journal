Morocco's Hassan II Stadium to open by early 2028

Economy Minister Fouzi Lekjaa stated construction is progressing well for the stadium set to host major clubs and events.

Morocco's Hassan II Stadium in Benslimane is scheduled for completion by early 2028, according to Economy and Finance Minister Fouzi Lekjaa. Construction is progressing well on the facility, which is expected to significantly influence urban development in Greater Casablanca. The stadium aims to be ready for use by the national team and Casablanca clubs Raja and Wydad before the 2030 World Cup.

Minister Lekjaa, who also heads the Royal Moroccan Football Federation, indicated the stadium could host other events prior to the 2030 tournament. He noted that FIFA regulations require the World Cup final to be held at the largest capacity stadium, and Hassan II Stadium currently holds that distinction. Lekjaa emphasized the 2030 World Cup's vision extends beyond football to highlight shared Mediterranean history and promote intercultural dialogue.

The stadium's completion by early 2028 positions it to be a key venue for Morocco's preparations for the 2030 World Cup. The tournament, which marks 100 years since the first World Cup, is a joint bid involving Morocco, Portugal, and Spain. Minister Lekjaa highlighted the goal of creating opportunities for young people and promoting dialogue between civilizations.

Why it mattersMoroccan football clubs Raja and Wydad, along with the national team, will have a new venue for matches before the 2030 World Cup.

Moroccan lawmakers question 2027 budget's reach

MPs debate MAD 400 billion investment plan's impact on poorer regions and public services.

Moroccan lawmakers questioned Thursday whether the government’s MAD 400 billion public investment programme could reach regions left behind by economic growth. The House of Representatives’ Finance and Economic Development Committee debated the 2027 Finance Bill’s ability to deliver improvements in public services, employment, and household purchasing power.

Aziz Lebbar, a PAM lawmaker, estimated that approximately 70% of Morocco’s investment and economic activity was concentrated along the Tangier–Rabat–Casablanca corridor, while other regions shared the remaining 30%. He argued that regional investment centres should process applications within 15 to 21 days and that domestic manufacturers should receive stronger incentives to compete with imports.

Istiqlal MP Salma Al Alami questioned the government’s ability to recruit foreign doctors without addressing the conditions driving Moroccan medical graduates abroad. She noted that 580 foreign doctors were working in the country in June 2025, but recruiting more would not necessarily solve shortages in rural and underserved areas.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens in less developed regions and those relying on public services may not benefit from the proposed MAD 400 billion investment.

Morocco interior ministry sets November 15 deadline for election law changes

Political parties must propose amendments for the 2027 local, regional and professional elections by mid-November.

Morocco's Interior Ministry has set a November 15 deadline for political parties to submit proposals for changes to electoral laws ahead of the 2027 local, regional and professional elections. Preparations for these elections follow September's parliamentary contests.

The deadline was announced Thursday during a meeting between the interior minister and party leaders, part of consultations under royal instructions. Discussions focused on extending measures from the September 23, 2026, elections, which aimed to strengthen electoral integrity and prevent misconduct.

The proposed changes will enter the legislative process as the government aims to complete legal preparations for the 2027 electoral cycle, which includes communal, provincial, and regional councils, as well as professional chambers and the House of Councillors.

Why it mattersPolitical parties and the Interior Ministry are discussing changes to electoral laws that will affect the composition of local and regional institutions in 2027.

Casablanca hosts 32nd video art festival

The festival runs from November 10 to 14 under the theme 'Digital arts facing new memory configurations'.

Casablanca will host the 32nd edition of the International Video Art Festival (FIAV) from November 10 to 14. The Faculty of Arts and Human Sciences at Ben M’sik, part of Hassan II University of Casablanca, is organizing the event.

Under the theme « Les arts numériques face aux nouvelles configurations de la mémoire » (Digital arts facing new memory configurations), the festival will run for five days. FIAV brings together various forms of artistic expression.

The festival aims to explore the intersection of digital arts and evolving concepts of memory.

Why it mattersMoroccans interested in digital arts and memory will attend the festival in Casablanca.

Morocco forecasts 180,000 tonnes of dates

The agriculture minister announced the 2026-2027 harvest projection at an event in Erfoud.

Morocco's national date production is expected to reach approximately 180,000 tonnes for the 2026-2027 agricultural season. This forecast was announced by the Minister of Agriculture, Maritime Fisheries, and Food Sovereignty, Khalid Hatimy, on Wednesday in Erfoud, Errachidia province.

Hatimy shared the projection with reporters on the sidelines of the 15th edition of a local event. The minister's statement provides an official outlook for the upcoming date harvest.

The agricultural campaign figures were released during a period of focus on food production. The ministry's announcement offers a specific target for the country's date output.

Why it mattersMoroccan farmers are directly affected by the projected 180,000 tonnes of date production for the 2026-2027 season.
