Morocco's dams fill as heavy rains hit north

National reservoirs hold 11.21 billion cubic meters of water, reaching 65.82% capacity after storms.

Heavy rain brought up to 40 millimeters of rainfall to parts of northern Morocco this week, prompting authorities to prepare for possible flooding. The General Directorate of Meteorology issued an orange weather warning on Wednesday, expecting another 20 to 35 millimeters of rain in 16 provinces. Hail and strong winds could accompany the storms, which have already affected areas like Ifrane, which recorded 40 millimeters of rain over 24 hours.

Authorities in the Tangier-Tetouan-Al Hoceima region are reinforcing flood precautions, particularly near waterways. Concerns about reservoir operations have also surfaced, though sources dismissed reports of exceptional releases from Oued El Makhazine and Dar Khrofa dams. Oued El Makhazine's daily discharge of approximately 400,000 cubic meters is for electricity generation and irrigation, not an emergency response.

As of November 3, Oued El Makhazine was approximately 65% full and Dar Khrofa around 80.5%. This rainfall arrives as Morocco’s national reservoirs hold 11.21 billion cubic meters of water, reaching 65.82% of their capacity. This is more than double the 31.12% recorded on November 4, 2025, following a prolonged drought.

Why it mattersMoroccan farmers and citizens will benefit from replenished water reserves for agriculture and drinking water after a period of drought.

Morocco job shortfall affects 2.4 million people

Nearly half of young Moroccans aged 15 to 24 face unmet employment needs, according to the High Commission for Planning.

Morocco’s job shortfall impacted 2.4 million people in the third quarter of 2026, with nearly half of young people aged 15 to 24 facing unmet employment needs. The High Commission for Planning (HCP) reported that the national labor underutilization rate reached 19.6% between July and September. Young people faced a 44.5% rate, while women recorded 28.5%.

The 2.4 million affected include the strictly unemployed, those in the potential workforce not currently employed, and underemployed workers, with strict unemployment at 10.5%. The number of people actively seeking work and available rose to 1.22 million, an increase of 103,000 in three months. Paid employment declined by a net 173,000 to 10.47 million people.

Agriculture, forestry and fishing shed a net 145,000 jobs, a 5.6% decrease, while services lost 96,000 jobs. Industry added 69,000 jobs, the only sector to register an increase. Guelmim-Oued Noun region recorded the highest underutilization rate at 38.7%.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens seeking employment are facing difficulties finding or securing enough work, with 2.4 million people affected by the job shortfall.

Business · 2026-11-05

Morocco opens Nador West Med container terminal

The new East Terminal features 1,520 metres of quay and aims for 3.5 million TEUs annual capacity by 2030.

Morocco has started operations at the East Terminal of Nador West Med, a port and industrial complex that has drawn 54 billion dirhams (US$5.5 billion) in investment. The East Terminal alone required 5 billion dirhams (US$510 million) and includes 1,520 metres of quay with an 18-metre draft.

The terminal is expected to reach an annual capacity of 3.5 million TEUs by 2030, according to the Ministry of Equipment and Water. A consortium of Marsa Maroc and Terminal Investment Limited will operate the facility for 25 years.

Additional terminals for general cargo, bulk cargo, and roll-on/roll-off units are planned, alongside energy infrastructure including an LNG terminal. This development is part of Morocco’s national port strategy targeting 390 million tons total port capacity by 2030.

Why it mattersMorocco's new port facilities will support industrial activity and reduce reliance on other ports for fuel imports.

Business · 2026-11-05

Morocco launches development projects in Bir Gandouz and El Guergarat

New facilities include a solidarity market costing MAD 4.11 million and a sports ground for over MAD 821,000.

Morocco inaugurated and launched several projects in Bir Gandouz and El Guergarat on Tuesday to mark the first anniversary of Unity Day. These initiatives focus on strengthening commercial infrastructure, improving water supply, enhancing administrative services, and developing educational and sports facilities in southern Morocco.

In Bir Gandouz, a solidarity market opened, costing MAD 4.11 million and funded by local development committees and councils to support young entrepreneurs and boost the local economy. The Aousserd provincial directorate and two commercial agencies in Bir Gandouz and El Guergarat were also inaugurated with an investment of MAD 1.1 million to improve essential services. Work began on rehabilitating Bir Gandouz's drinking water network for MAD 5.16 million, aiming to reduce water losses and increase network efficiency.

A neighborhood sports ground was also opened in Bir Gandouz, costing over MAD 821,000 and funded by the Ministry of National Education, Preschool and Sports. The facility includes a mini-football pitch, fencing, and sports lighting, intended to encourage sports participation and social interaction.

Why it mattersResidents of Bir Gandouz and El Guergarat will benefit from improved commercial spaces, administrative services, water supply, and sports facilities.

Politics · 2026-11-05

Morocco cuts illegal cannabis cultivation by 88%

The Ministry of Interior reported a significant drop in cannabis farming areas from 134,000 hectares in 2003 to 15,496 hectares in 2026.

Morocco has drastically reduced illegal cannabis cultivation, cutting the area by 88.43% since 2003. The land used for this farming fell from approximately 134,000 hectares in 2003 to 15,496 hectares by 2026, according to the Ministry of Interior's performance report.

The ministry attributed this decline to field operations conducted by provincial committees tasked with combating illegal cannabis cultivation. Security operations through August 2026 also resulted in the seizure of over 274 tons of hash, 53 tons of cannabis plants, and significant amounts of other drugs, including cocaine and heroin.

The ministry is intensifying efforts against drug trafficking through enhanced security, training, and expertise exchange, with a focus on emerging drugs and psychotropic pills. Surveillance has increased along borders and coastlines, and awareness campaigns are underway to prevent drug use, especially among youth, with special attention to dealers near schools.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens are affected by increased security operations and awareness campaigns aimed at reducing drug use and trafficking.
