Morocco proposes tax amnesty for informal businesses

Traders and other informal operators can register for the 2027 Finance Bill without penalties for past undeclared activity.

Morocco's proposed 2027 Finance Bill aims to bring informal businesses into the formal sector by allowing them to register with tax authorities without penalties for previous undeclared years. This revived incentive targets street vendors, market traders, mechanics, carpenters, painters, and shopkeepers who have operated outside the tax system.

A representative from Casablanca’s Derb Omar traders’ association stated that informal activity includes businesses operating from permanent premises or temporary stalls, with previous estimates suggesting around 25,000 street vendors in key commercial areas. The proposed measure exempts retrospective tax treatment for those registering under the professional tax system, though other obligations like licensing and social security remain.

This initiative reflects Morocco's continued strategy of using voluntary registration incentives to expand the tax base, rather than relying solely on enforcement measures. Similar simplified mechanisms, such as the auto-entrepreneur framework established in 2014, have been introduced previously to encourage formalization.

Moroccan tomato prices hit 17 MAD per kilogram

Consumers in Tinghir face high costs for tomatoes, potatoes, and onions due to market issues and potential export impacts.

Tomato prices have reached 17 Moroccan Dirhams (MAD) per kilogram in some markets, significantly increasing the cost of groceries for Moroccan households. A visit to the Sunday market in Tinghir on November 8 found tomatoes selling for between 13 and 15 MAD, with prices reaching up to 17 MAD in other locations.

Potatoes and onions are also expensive, costing between 7 and 8 MAD per kilogram, which makes essential food items unaffordable for low-income families. Some consumers believe that exporting large quantities of produce to European and African markets is reducing local supply, leading to higher domestic prices.

A civil society activist in Tinghir, Hamid El Hadioui, has urged the government to temporarily halt exports of key agricultural products to meet domestic demand until prices decrease. However, market observers suggest that wholesale market inefficiencies, numerous intermediaries, and speculation also contribute to the inflated prices before produce reaches consumers.

Why it mattersMoroccan households are struggling to afford basic vegetables like tomatoes, potatoes, and onions due to rising prices.

Spain seeks Morocco intelligence on Ceuta migration

A Spanish judge ordered the government to release intelligence files shared with Morocco before the July Ceuta migration crisis.

Spain's National Court ordered the government on October 27 to declassify intelligence documents shared with Morocco before the July migration attempt towards Ceuta. Judge Maria Tardon gave the government 15 days to release files from the National Intelligence Center, including communications with Morocco’s General Directorate for Studies and Documentation and General Directorate for Territorial Surveillance. The judge wants to know what Spanish intelligence told Moroccan agencies and how Rabat responded to warnings about online groups organizing mass crossings.

Spanish intelligence alerted the Moroccan agencies on July 29 to online groups with around 180,000 members calling for mass crossings into Ceuta. Tardon is seeking the full text of intelligence reports sent to Morocco and any responses. The inquiry is occurring as migration is a central campaign issue for Spain's November 29 elections.

Social media has become a major tool for organizing irregular migration, with Moroccan authorities reporting a shift from traditional smuggling. Moroccan authorities estimated nearly 40,000 people were involved, while Spanish estimates reached 70,000. The National Human Rights Council documented 23 virtual groups with over 1 million members generating about 1,400 daily posts.

Why it mattersSpanish authorities are investigating intelligence shared with Morocco regarding a mass migration event that affected both countries.

Culture · 2026-11-09

Marrakech exhibition traces 60 years of Moroccan painting

The exhibition, titled “60 Years of Painting in Morocco,” examines the evolution of Moroccan visual arts.

Six decades of Moroccan painting are on display at an exhibition that opened Friday at the Daoudiate Cultural Center’s Art Gallery in Marrakech. The show, called “60 Years of Painting in Morocco,” explores how Moroccan visual arts have changed through various styles, methods, and generations.

The Moroccan Union of Professional Visual Artists (SMAPP) organized the event, which continues until November 30. It is part of celebrations for the Green March and Unity Day. Marrakech is the third city to host the exhibition, after Rabat and Casablanca.

A separate show at Khalid Fine Art Gallery honors painter Omar Bouragba. A symposium on Thursday brought together artists to discuss the future of Moroccan visual arts.

Why it mattersMoroccan artists and the public can view and discuss six decades of national painting.
