Morocco's Nador West Med port attracts $5.5 billion investment

The Nador West Med complex aims to become a key maritime, industrial, and energy hub connecting Europe and Africa.

Morocco's Nador West Med port complex has secured 54 billion dirhams ($5.5 billion) in public and private investment. Forbes reported this investment strengthens the country's goal to become a major maritime, industrial, and energy hub linking Europe and Africa. The first phase of its main container terminal is nearing operation, reflecting King Mohammed VI’s infrastructure strategy.

The port is designed to expand Morocco’s international trade role and benefit from global supply chain shifts. Its energy infrastructure includes a hydrocarbon terminal for 25 million tonnes annually and a planned liquefied natural gas terminal to process five billion cubic meters yearly. These facilities could bolster Morocco's energy security and industrial growth.

Forbes stated the port complex could reinforce Morocco's position as a link between European energy demand and African markets. The project is also expected to stimulate economic activity in Morocco’s eastern region through industrial investment and logistics services. Crown Prince Moulay El Hassan launched operations at the first container terminal on Saturday, October 31.

Why it mattersMorocco's Nador West Med port development aims to boost its role in international trade and energy markets, potentially benefiting regional economic activity.

Morocco demands UN account for 350 vehicles, assets after MINURSO exit

The UN mission's mandate expired October 31, leaving infrastructure and equipment in Morocco's southern provinces facing an uncertain handover.

Morocco's Royal Cabinet ordered MINURSO personnel, vehicles, and equipment to leave the southern provinces following the UN Security Council's failure to renew the mission's mandate.

The UN must now account for 350 vehicles, aircraft, and extensive communications infrastructure accumulated over 35 years of operations, according to UN records.

MINURSO has instructed personnel to remain at home until further notice as the mission's mandate expired on October 31, with no public announcement on asset inventory or withdrawal plans.

Why it mattersMorocco is demanding accountability for UN assets left behind after the mission's withdrawal from its southern provinces.

Morocco sees UN Sahara file closure after MINURSO mandate failure

Government spokesperson Samir Belfqih stated that Russia's veto of a UN resolution marks a new phase in the dispute.

Morocco's government declared Saturday that the UN's failure to renew MINURSO's mandate is a significant step toward closing the Sahara dispute at the United Nations. This development followed Russia's veto of a US-backed Security Council resolution.

Government spokesperson Samir Belfqih said the outcome reflects growing international support for Morocco’s sovereignty over its southern provinces and its autonomy initiative. He stated that the Royal Cabinet’s statement signals a new phase in the dispute, building on diplomatic momentum under King Mohammed VI.

Belfqih described MINURSO's 35-year presence as part of an outdated international framework, noting the diplomatic environment has changed since the mission's 1991 establishment. He reaffirmed Morocco's commitment to the ceasefire and continued development in the southern provinces.

Why it mattersThe Moroccan government views the UN's failure to renew MINURSO's mandate as a positive development for its position on the Sahara dispute.

Business · 2026-11-01

Canadian firm eyes Moroccan processing plant

Silver Hammer Mining is considering acquiring a 200-tonne daily capacity facility and indirect interests in local projects.

Canadian mining company Silver Hammer Mining is exploring the acquisition of a mineral processing plant in Morocco with a daily capacity of 200 tonnes.

Company executives visited the potential facility in September and are also seeking indirect stakes in Moroccan mining projects like the Akka polymetallic project through proposed mergers.

The company is seeking C$7 million to C$10 million in private financing, expected to close by November 30, to fund these ventures, with merger deadlines extended to January 31, 2027.

Why it mattersMoroccan mining sector operations and potential foreign investment are affected by Silver Hammer Mining's acquisition plans.

Morocco allocates 45.4 billion dirhams to health sector

The 2027 budget aims to improve access to care, from investments to medicines and staffing.

Morocco's government has allocated 45.4 billion dirhams to the health sector for 2027, confirming a significant budgetary effort. This funding is intended for various areas, including investments, medicines, staffing, and the autonomy of Groupements de Santé Territoriale (GST).

The budget's success will be measured by whether these allocated funds translate into accessible healthcare for citizens. The stated goal is to ensure that the financial resources effectively improve the quality and availability of medical services across the country.

The Ministry of Health has announced the budget as part of the 2027 Finance Bill (PLF 2027). Further details on the specific distribution of these funds and expected outcomes are anticipated.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens will be affected by the accessibility and quality of healthcare services funded by the 2027 health budget.
