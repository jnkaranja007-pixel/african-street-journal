Moroccan police seize 971 kilograms of cocaine

The drugs were found in a refrigerated container aboard a vessel originating from Brazil.

Moroccan police and customs officers seized 971 kilograms of cocaine at Tanger Med port on Tuesday, November 3. The shipment was intercepted inside a refrigerated shipping container aboard a vessel flying the flag of a Latin American country.

The General Directorate of National Security (DGSN) stated the drugs originated in Brazil and were bound for West Africa. Preliminary investigations confirmed the container had departed from a Brazilian port. The Tangier judicial police have opened an investigation into the circumstances and those involved.

Morocco’s Interpol National Central Bureau in Rabat will coordinate international investigations to trace the criminal network behind the trafficking operation.

Why it mattersMoroccan authorities intercepted a large cocaine shipment, preventing its entry into the country and potential distribution.

Morocco secures €100 million for port development

The financing agreement between the National Ports Agency and the French Development Agency targets southern provinces and green ports.

Morocco's National Ports Agency (ANP) and the French Development Agency (AFD) signed a €100 million financing agreement in Rabat on Tuesday. This funding is designated for the development of the Southern Provinces and green ports.

The agreement, signed by ANP Director General Mustapha Farès and AFD Director in Morocco Catherine Bonnaud, is the third package under their strategic partnership. Minister Nizar Baraka stated the funds will support the ANP’s 2023-2030 investment program, with a focus on port projects in Tan-Tan, Tarfaya, Laayoune, Boujdour, Dakhla, and Lamhiriz.

Baraka explained the projects aim to strengthen the integration of the Moroccan Sahara into the national economy and leverage its Atlantic position. French Ambassador Philippe Lalliot highlighted the agreement's strategic significance and Morocco's large-scale investments in port infrastructure, citing Tanger Med as an example.

Why it mattersMorocco's port sector and its southern regions will receive significant investment for development and economic integration.

Spain to dismantle Ceuta migrant shelters

About 13,000 people have returned to Morocco following a migration crisis.

Spain plans to dismantle temporary migrant shelters in Ceuta as approximately 13,000 people have returned to Morocco, according to Spanish Migration Minister Elma Saiz on Monday. The government aims to remove these facilities quickly, viewing it as a sign of normalcy returning to Ceuta after months of strain on its reception system. Spain had increased Ceuta's reception capacity from 500 to 7,500 places during the crisis.

Minister Saiz stated that the dismantling is proceeding because migrants have already gone back to Morocco, eliminating the need for further administrative processing. She defended the government's actions, asserting they followed both legal and humanitarian principles throughout the crisis. This move occurs amidst criticism regarding the treatment of migrant minors in Ceuta, with reports of children being left outdoors and allegations of pepper spray use.

The Spanish government has not provided a specific date for the complete dismantling of the remaining facilities. Saiz indicated the process will continue as the demand for temporary accommodation decreases. The emergency phase in Ceuta is considered to be ending with the scaling back of these accommodations.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens who were part of a large migration into Spain's Ceuta enclave are now returning home.

Morocco's lived poverty rate drops to 22%

Afrobarometer report shows 8 million Moroccans still face deprivation in basic necessities.

Morocco has seen its lived poverty rate fall by six percentage points over the last decade, reaching 22% of the population, according to a new Afrobarometer report. This means approximately 8 million people, out of Morocco's 37 million population, continue to experience moderate or high levels of deprivation. Morocco ranks among the top three African countries with the lowest lived poverty rates, following Seychelles at 7% and Mauritius at 12%.

The report measures lived poverty by how often households lack food, clean water, medical care, cooking fuel, and cash income. In Morocco, 12% of respondents reported frequent shortages of cash income, and 7% lacked medicine or medical care. Water shortages affected 3% of households, while 2% lacked cooking fuel and 1% insufficient food. These figures reflect those who went without these necessities many times or always in the past year.

Afrobarometer's findings indicate that material hardship is closely tied to employment, education, and public service access. Across surveyed African nations, 66% of unemployed individuals faced moderate to high lived poverty, compared to 48% of employed people. Rural residents reported higher rates at 68% versus 53% in urban areas. Difficulty obtaining medical care was reported by 63% of those in high lived poverty.

Why it mattersAbout 8 million Moroccans are still affected by moderate or high levels of deprivation in basic necessities like food, water, and medical care.

Spain dissolves parliament, delaying Sahrawi bill

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called for November 29 elections after his housing bill failed, impacting relations with Rabat.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez dissolved the Cortes and called for new elections on November 29. This decision followed the rejection of his housing bill and the withdrawal of support from the Junts party.

The snap election gamble puts sensitive issues with Morocco on hold and provides the Vox party with a campaign issue. The bill concerning Sahrawi citizenship has also been shelved.

Sánchez's move is a high-stakes gamble that could reshape Spain's political future and its diplomatic relations with Rabat.

Why it mattersMorocco's relationship with Spain and the status of Sahrawi citizenship are directly affected by Spain's political decisions.
