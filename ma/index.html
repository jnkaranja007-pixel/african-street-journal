Morocco raises defense procurement ceiling to MAD 190.5 billion

The 2027 Finance Bill proposes a 21.2% increase for future military equipment and defense industry commitments.

Morocco's draft 2027 Finance Bill proposes raising the ceiling for future military equipment and defense industry commitments by 21.2% to MAD 190.5 billion. This increase of MAD 33.33 billion compares to MAD 157.17 billion in 2026, seeking parliamentary authorization for spending commitments extending into the following financial year. The proposed ceiling has risen from MAD 124.77 billion in 2024 and MAD 133.45 billion in 2025, bringing the cumulative increase over three years to nearly 53%.

Under Article 36, the minister responsible for National Defense would be authorized to enter into commitments during 2027 against appropriations to be allocated in 2028. This authority is for the dedicated account for acquiring and repairing Royal Armed Forces equipment and supporting Morocco’s defense industry. The defense equipment account is separately allocated MAD 11.49 billion in expenditure for 2027.

The Integrated Territorial Development Fund would receive an advance commitment ceiling of MAD 15 billion, the second-largest amount among the provisions. Other authorizations include MAD 3 billion for the Special Roads Fund and MAD 2 billion for prison administration support. The draft also doubles annual expenditure through the Integrated Territorial Development Fund to MAD 10 billion, compared with MAD 5 billion in 2026.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens will be affected by increased government spending on defense procurement and territorial development.

Morocco's Bourita talks Sahara with Russia's Lavrov

The two foreign ministers discussed bilateral cooperation and UN negotiations on the Moroccan Sahara.

Moroccan Foreign Minister Nasser Bourita spoke by phone on Friday with Russian counterpart Sergei Lavrov about bilateral cooperation and coordination at the United Nations. The call, initiated by Morocco, also covered preparations for the upcoming Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow.

The discussions occurred as UN Security Council members review a US-drafted resolution concerning the Moroccan Sahara. This resolution proposes changes to MINURSO's mandate and centers negotiations on Morocco's autonomy proposal. Russia, a permanent Security Council member, will vote on the resolution before MINURSO's mandate expires on October 31.

The Russian Foreign Ministry stated that foreign policy coordination, including at the UN, was a topic of discussion. The specific inclusion of the Sahara draft resolution in their conversation was not detailed in the Russian statement.

Why it mattersMoroccan Foreign Minister Nasser Bourita and Russian counterpart Sergei Lavrov discussed UN negotiations over the Moroccan Sahara.

Morocco rights chief urges youth leadership investment

Amina Bouayach stated that investing in young people is essential for developing future decision-makers and advancing sustainable development.

Amina Bouayach, President of Morocco's National Human Rights Council, said on Friday in Rabat that sustained investment in youth leadership is essential to foster a new generation of committed decision-makers.

Bouayach stressed the need to provide young people with spaces for dialogue and expression, and policies that respond to their aspirations, noting that human rights form the foundation for participation and equality.

The seminar, organized by the ConnectingGroup International Foundation, also featured discussions on promoting youth participation in public life and fostering women leaders for conflict prevention and peacebuilding across Africa.

Why it mattersMoroccan youth are affected by the call to invest in their leadership for future decision-making and sustainable development.

Morocco court receives 71 election appeals

The Constitutional Court has received 71 appeals challenging election results, with more expected before the deadline.

Morocco's Constitutional Court received 71 election appeals by Tuesday afternoon, according to Hespress. The court expects more challenges to be filed by Friday as the legal deadline for contesting results approaches.

The appeals were submitted by the heads of candidate lists in several electoral districts. These challenges are part of the process following the recent legislative elections.

The Constitutional Court will now review these appeals. The final number of challenges is anticipated to rise before the official submission period closes.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens who voted in the recent legislative elections have had their results challenged before the Constitutional Court.

Morocco's health ministry changed names multiple times

Since independence, the department responsible for health has evolved its name and missions.

Morocco's ministry responsible for health has undergone several name changes since the country gained independence. These shifts reflect the evolving health needs of the population and the changing scope of the ministry's responsibilities. Najib Diouri, an international health economics expert, has examined this evolution.

Diouri's analysis notes that the ministry's leadership has also seen frequent changes, with ministers serving terms ranging from seven days to eight years. This frequent turnover in leadership has accompanied the department's name alterations over time.

The ministry's name changes and evolving missions have occurred as it has adapted to the country's sanitary requirements. The department has consistently worked to meet the health demands of the Moroccan people throughout its history.

Why it mattersThe frequent changes in the name and leadership of the health ministry affect the continuity of public health policy for Moroccans.
