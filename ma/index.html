Morocco offers game developers grants up to MAD 3 million for production

Moroccan video game companies can receive up to MAD 3 million for pilot production and MAD 1 million for prototypes under a new innovation program.

Morocco's Industry and Trade Ministry has opened applications for grants up to MAD 3 million for video game pilot production and MAD 1 million for prototypes. This initiative, part of the Innovation Support Fund's fourth call for projects, aims to develop a regional video game industry hub through 2028.

The program, which can support up to 20 projects annually with a MAD 50 million budget, covers up to 70% of eligible prototype costs and 30% for pilot industrialization. Applications for the 2026 round close on October 15, with eligible spending counted from January 1.

Micro, small, and medium-sized gaming companies with pre-tax revenue not exceeding MAD 200 million in the previous two years are eligible. High-growth startups with a strategic partnership with a large Moroccan company can also apply, following a November 2025 agreement that expanded the fund to include gaming companies.

Why it mattersMoroccan video game companies and high-growth startups can access significant funding to develop and produce their products, potentially boosting their growth and market reach.

IFC, EOS Group launch €60 million facility for Morocco's bad debt market

The International Finance Corporation and German debt specialist EOS Group are preparing a €60 million facility to acquire troubled loans in Morocco, marking the entry of.

Morocco's market for troubled loans is set to attract its first international investors, with the International Finance Corporation (IFC) and German debt specialist EOS Group preparing a €60 million facility. This initiative, reported by Asharq Bloomberg, will target nonperforming loans held by Moroccan banks, covering individuals and companies of all sizes, as well as real estate assets taken over during debt recovery.

The facility, known as DARP NPL EOS Morocco, will see the IFC, the World Bank’s private-sector investment arm, and EOS each contribute up to €30 million. This move comes as Morocco progresses towards establishing a regulated secondary market for nonperforming loans, a system supervised by Bank Al-Maghrib, which could allow banks to convert distressed loans into cash more efficiently.

Morocco's nonperforming loans reached MAD 104.7 billion by the end of June, an increase of about 1.9% from a year earlier, according to Bank Al-Maghrib. The biggest surge in bad debt, around 15%, occurred in 2020 due to the Covid-19 pandemic and economic slowdown. A functioning secondary market is expected to free up funds for new lending and reduce pressure on bank balance sheets.

Why it mattersMoroccan banks and borrowers are affected as this facility aims to clear troubled assets from bank balance sheets and provide new avenues for debt resolution.

TAQA Morocco doubles investment despite revenue drop

TAQA Morocco increased its capital spending to MAD 515 million in the first half of the year, more than double the previous year, even as revenue fell by 5.4%.

TAQA Morocco's capital spending more than doubled to MAD 515 million in the first half of the year, up from MAD 210 million a year earlier, according to its financial update. This increase occurred despite a 5.4% drop in first-half revenue to MAD 5.09 billion, attributed to scheduled maintenance and an unfavorable dollar-dirham exchange rate.

The company's second-quarter investment reached MAD 466 million, with funds directed towards low-carbon development, an overhaul of Unit 3, and operating and maintenance projects. Plant availability decreased to 86.6% from 93.4% due to a planned 25-day minor overhaul of Unit 3 and an inspection of Unit 2.

TAQA Morocco is expanding into desalination, renewable energy, natural gas, and low-carbon water and energy transport. A restructuring approved on July 17 will organize its activities into specialized subsidiaries as it transitions to a holding-company model, while net debt declined 10.1% to MAD 4.62 billion through cash generation and repayments.

Why it mattersMoroccan energy consumers may see changes in energy supply and pricing as TAQA Morocco invests in new areas like renewable energy and desalination.

Morocco reaffirms ASEAN-Africa connectivity role

Morocco's ambassador to ASEAN, Redouane Houssaini, highlighted the country's vision for comprehensive connectivity, encompassing digital networks, financial flows, energy.

Morocco reaffirmed its role as a bridge between the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) and Africa in connectivity, according to Ambassador Redouane Houssaini. Speaking at a meeting in Manila, Philippines, from August twenty-fourth to twenty-sixth, Houssaini emphasized that connectivity extends beyond physical infrastructure to include digital, financial, energy, and human links.

Houssaini cited projects like the Tanger Med port complex, which connects to over 180 ports in 70 countries, and the Royal Initiative for Sahel countries' access to the Atlantic Ocean, including Dakhla Atlantique, as examples of Morocco's efforts. He also mentioned the Nigeria-Morocco Africa-Atlantic Gas Pipeline, all contributing to strengthening economic and energy ties across the continent.

For human connectivity, the Moroccan Agency for International Cooperation (AMCI) facilitates South-South cooperation through training programs and scholarships. Morocco, granted ASEAN Sectoral Dialogue Partner status in 2023, aims to serve as a production and logistics hub near Europe and a gateway to African value chains, with concrete cooperation initiatives expected at the upcoming Morocco-ASEAN Sectoral Cooperation Committee meeting in Jakarta this fall.

Why it mattersThis initiative affects Moroccan and ASEAN businesses by creating new trade routes and investment opportunities through enhanced connectivity.

Morocco observes 96% partial lunar eclipse before dawn Friday

A deep partial lunar eclipse will be visible across Morocco before dawn on Friday, August 28, with Earth’s shadow covering about 96% of the moon’s surface at its peak.

Morocco will experience a significant partial lunar eclipse early Friday morning, with the moon’s visible surface approximately 96.3% obscured by Earth’s shadow, according to NASA. The clearly visible partial phase is expected to begin around 3:34 a.m. local time, as the moon enters Earth’s dark central shadow, known as the umbra.

The maximum eclipse will occur at about 5:12 a.m., with the partial phase concluding near 6:52 a.m. The final stages of visibility will depend on local moonset times, with the moon setting in Casablanca around 7:10 a.m. Observers are advised to find a location with an unobstructed western horizon for the best view.

During the event, the darkened lunar surface may appear copper, orange, or deep red due to Earth’s atmosphere scattering blue light and bending red and orange light towards the moon. Unlike a total lunar eclipse, a narrow section of the moon will remain directly illuminated, preventing complete darkness, though its appearance may resemble a “blood moon.” Morocco’s next visible partial lunar eclipse is anticipated on January 12, 2028.

Why it mattersResidents across Morocco will have the opportunity to observe a rare celestial event, a deep partial lunar eclipse, before dawn on Friday.
