Morocco Prosecutor Investigates Election Candidate's Drug Money Claims

The King’s Prosecutor General at Kenitra Court of Appeal has ordered an investigation into a candidate's statement alleging drug proceeds influenced the electoral process.

An investigation has been launched into a statement by an election candidate claiming that drug money was used to influence the upcoming legislative elections. The announcement came from the King’s Prosecutor General at the Kenitra Court of Appeal, following the spread of the statement on social media platforms. The candidate reportedly made the allegations during a party rally.

The Public Prosecutor’s Office initiated the probe to clarify the circumstances surrounding the candidate's statement and to ensure the integrity of the electoral process. This action is part of the office's mandate to oversee the proper conduct of elections and to apply the law accordingly. Legal measures will be taken based on the investigation's findings.

The investigation aims to address claims of illicit funds potentially impacting electoral operations. By examining the statement and its context, authorities seek to uphold legal standards and public trust in the election results. The outcome will determine any necessary legal actions against those involved in making or spreading such allegations.

Why it mattersThe investigation into a candidate's claims of drug money influencing elections directly affects the integrity of the political process and public trust in electoral outcomes.

Ounahi Signs Four-Year Deal with Panathinaïkos

Morocco international Azzedine Ounahi returns to Greek club Panathinaïkos on a permanent four-year contract after a loan spell.

Morocco midfielder Azzedine Ounahi has officially rejoined Panathinaïkos on a permanent four-year contract, the Greek club announced. The 26-year-old transfers from Spanish side Girona FC to Athens, where he previously played on loan during the 2024-2025 season. During that loan spell, Ounahi made 37 appearances, scoring five goals and providing seven assists.

Ounahi, a product of the Mohammed VI Football Academy, has also played for RC Strasbourg, Angers, and Olympique de Marseille. He is a key player for Morocco's national team, the Atlas Lions, with 55 caps and 11 goals. He was a standout in Morocco's historic 2022 World Cup semi-final run and scored two goals for his country in the 2026 World Cup round of 16 victory against Canada.

The midfielder's return to Panathinaïkos marks a long-term commitment to the Greek club. Ounahi aims to build on his recent career momentum, including his performances for the Atlas Lions in the 2026 World Cup, as he begins the next phase of his football career in Greece.

Why it mattersMoroccan footballer Azzedine Ounahi's permanent transfer to Panathinaïkos affects investors in the club and potentially the transfer market for players of his caliber.

Morocco Expands Mental Health Services by 2030

The national plan aims to significantly increase beds, psychiatric hospitals, and intermediate care facilities across all regions.

Morocco's Ministry of Health and Social Protection launched a 2030 Mental Health Plan in Rabat, establishing a national framework to enhance access to and quality of mental health care. The plan, announced Thursday, seeks to promote mental well-being, prevent disorders, and support rehabilitation and social reintegration.

By 2030, the initiative targets a substantial increase in mental health infrastructure, aiming to raise the number of beds from 2,466 to 4,728, psychiatric hospitals from 10 to 17, and intermediate structures from 4 to 24. Minister of Health and Social Protection Amine Tehraoui stated that investing in mental health is crucial for human capital, social cohesion, and individual dignity, emphasizing its importance for development projects.

The strategy incorporates early intervention, lifelong prevention, and support for vulnerable populations, including the elderly. It aims to integrate mental health into the core of the national health system, ensuring timely care accessible to all citizens. The plan, developed through broad consultation, will see structural reforms begin in 2026, with full implementation and evaluation by 2030.

Why it mattersThe plan affects all citizens by aiming to improve access to timely mental health care closer to their homes.

Morocco Adds 8,300 Millionaires, Ranks Third in Africa

The nation's millionaire population grew 55% over the past decade, outpacing continental growth.

Morocco is home to approximately eight thousand three hundred resident millionaires as of June 2026, placing it third in Africa behind South Africa and Egypt, according to the Africa Wealth Report 2026. A millionaire is defined as an individual with over one million US dollars in liquid investable wealth. This figure represents a significant increase from seven thousand five hundred millionaires in 2025 and six thousand eight hundred thirty-six in 2024.

South Africa leads the continent with forty-eight thousand two hundred millionaires, followed by Egypt with fifteen thousand one hundred. Morocco's count narrowly surpasses Nigeria's eight thousand one hundred, with Kenya ranking fifth with six thousand five hundred. These five nations collectively hold about sixty-eight percent of Africa's millionaire population. Casablanca remains the primary hub for private wealth, hosting an estimated two thousand eight hundred millionaires.

The report highlights Morocco's millionaire population growth of fifty-five percent over the last ten years, the highest among the top five African wealth markets and substantially exceeding the continental average of six percent. Marrakech saw an eighty-two percent increase in its millionaire population during the same period, with Tangier registering forty-two percent growth. Future projections indicate private wealth in Morocco could rise by over eighty-five percent in the coming decade.

Why it mattersMorocco's growing number of millionaires indicates an increase in liquid investable wealth within the country.

UN Report: World to Exceed 1.5°C Warming Limit

The United Nations Environment Programme's new report indicates global temperatures will likely surpass the 1.5°C threshold within years, necessitating immediate emission.

The world is now expected to exceed the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C warming limit, a risk boundary where every fraction of a degree increases disruptions like heat waves and droughts. The United Nations Environment Programme's report, Limiting Overshoot, states that even optimistic scenarios project warming to peak around 1.8°C, while current policies point towards at least 2.3°C.

The primary finding suggests the focus should shift from preventing the threshold crossing to managing how high temperatures rise and how quickly they can be reduced. This requires immediate and sustained cuts in carbon dioxide and methane emissions, alongside carbon removal strategies like reforestation, to achieve an "overshoot, peak and decline" pathway.

For water-stressed nations like Morocco, this means preparing for more severe heat, unpredictable rainfall, and increased pressure on agricultural systems and water reservoirs. The report warns that even if temperatures are pushed below 1.5°C, ice-sheet loss and sea-level rise will continue for decades, with prolonged periods above the threshold risking irreversible changes.

Why it mattersMorocco, as a water-stressed country, must prepare its water systems, farms, and infrastructure for more severe heat and unstable rainfall due to global warming.
