Morocco's IRESEN discusses smart grids at COP31

Morocco's Research Institute for Solar Energy and New Energies (IRESEN) highlighted challenges in adapting power grids for renewable energy at COP31 in Antalya.

Morocco's Research Institute for Solar Energy and New Energies (IRESEN) organized a side event at COP31 in Antalya on Tuesday to discuss adapting electrical networks for renewable energy integration. Speakers addressed challenges in transforming MENA region electricity systems due to decarbonization and the progress of solar and wind power, noting their fluctuating nature complicates grid stability. The event focused on the capacity of existing infrastructure to handle increasing renewable energy production while ensuring a continuous supply.

IRESEN Director General Samir Rachidi stated Morocco aims for 52% of its installed electrical capacity to be renewable by 2030, potentially reaching 60%. He emphasized the need for systems managing high solar production and low availability periods through storage solutions like batteries and pumped hydropower. Rachidi also highlighted the role of energy consumption digitalization in modulating usage and increasing demand flexibility.

The discussions underscored the shift from traditional electricity management to smarter, more flexible, and decentralized systems, particularly through Smart Grids development. This involves mobilizing digital technologies and automation to optimize electricity production, distribution, and consumption. IRESEN also aims to connect scientific research with economic and industrial actors for practical applications.

Why it mattersMorocco is working to increase its renewable energy capacity to 52% by 2030, requiring significant adaptation of its electrical grids.

Morocco faces European auto investment challenge

European countries aim to rebuild domestic car production, potentially diverting projects from Morocco.

Morocco faces increased competition for automotive investment as European nations work to rebuild their own car manufacturing industries, potentially redirecting projects previously planned for the Kingdom, according to Industry and Trade Minister Ryad Mezzour. He stated that Europe's industrial policy shift could encourage manufacturers to move production closer to home, weakening a key factor that made Morocco attractive: lower production costs.

Mezzour highlighted discussions around the European Parliament's proposed Industrial Acceleration Act, which targets having 80% of car production within Europe. This development, along with Europe's transition to electric vehicles, could affect Morocco's ability to attract industrial investment. He noted that discussions on these European policy changes began about 18 months ago, coinciding with the redirection of several expected investments away from Morocco.

The minister urged Morocco to prepare for these changes immediately by accelerating the adoption of robotics, industrial automation, and artificial intelligence. Strengthening innovation and expanding into more international markets, as well as supporting Moroccan brands, are also crucial steps. Mezzour described the shift as a major test for the national economy, requiring a coordinated effort from economic and political stakeholders.

Why it mattersMorocco's automotive sector, a significant source of investment and jobs, faces potential disruption as European countries prioritize domestic manufacturing.

Morocco faces connected medical device security risks

French alert highlights vulnerabilities in networked health equipment, raising concerns over patient data and care safety.

A French alert has identified a high risk of intrusion into vulnerable networked medical equipment in Morocco. The CERT Santé's warning means connected devices, such as infusion pumps and patient monitors, could be compromised.

This situation raises critical questions about the security of patient data and the safety of healthcare delivery within Morocco. The potential for breaches impacts the value of sensitive health information and forces difficult budget decisions regarding security upgrades.

The alert specifically targets networked medical devices, suggesting a widespread issue rather than isolated incidents. The implications for patient care and data privacy are significant, requiring immediate attention from health authorities and device manufacturers.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens' health data and the safety of their medical treatment are at risk due to potential cyber intrusions into connected medical devices.

Morocco braces for heavy rain and wind

The Directorate-General of Meteorology forecasts strong rainfall, hail, and dust storms from Wednesday to Thursday.

Heavy rains, locally strong showers, hail, and strong wind gusts with local dust are expected across several Moroccan provinces from Wednesday to Thursday. The Directorate-General of Meteorology (DGM) issued the forecast for the affected regions.

Rainfall amounts are predicted to range between 80 and 110 mm in the affected areas. The DGM's announcement details the expected weather conditions for the coming days.

The forecast covers multiple provinces within the Kingdom, indicating a widespread weather event. Residents are advised of the potential for severe weather conditions.

Why it mattersFarmers face potential crop damage and soil erosion due to the predicted heavy rainfall and strong winds.

Gulf states reaffirm support for Morocco's sovereignty over Sahara

The six GCC members backed Morocco's autonomy initiative as the basis for settling the regional dispute, according to a statement reported by Hespress English.

The six member states of the Gulf Cooperation Council have reiterated their support for Morocco's sovereignty over the Sahara and backed Rabat's autonomy initiative as a basis for resolving the regional dispute. Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain restated their positions during discussions at the United Nations General Assembly on the use of the Security Council veto.

The Gulf states reaffirmed Morocco's territorial integrity and said they backed efforts to reach a political settlement within the UN-led process, in line with relevant Security Council resolutions. They described the autonomy proposal, under Moroccan sovereignty and territorial unity, as a realistic and lasting political solution.

The GCC also praised Morocco's commitment to the ceasefire and its efforts to preserve security and stability, and commended Rabat's stated preference for de-escalation and a political approach to developments in the dispute.

Why it mattersMorocco's position on the Sahara has been restated by six Gulf governments, a backing that Rabat can cite in the UN-led talks on the dispute.
