Morocco education minister acknowledges learning crisis

Education Minister Mohamed Nabil Benabdellah told Parliament that 265,000 students drop out annually.

Morocco loses approximately 265,000 students each year due to a persistent learning crisis, Education Minister Mohamed Nabil Benabdellah stated in Parliament on Monday. The minister acknowledged significant weaknesses in learning outcomes despite efforts to expand educational access across the nation.

Speaking to the House of Representatives on November 16, Benabdellah reported that Morocco's education system currently serves 9.22 million students, with 49% of them being girls. He noted that preschool enrollment has reached 985,666 children across 12,661 institutions and 12,772 rural satellite units. However, he described student performance in reading, writing, comprehension, and mathematics as unsatisfactory, with some rankings being “shameful.”

The minister also pointed to overcrowded classrooms, teacher shortages, and disparities between public and private education as key challenges. Improving school infrastructure, reducing dropout rates, and increasing teacher involvement in reforms are now central to the government's education priorities.

Why it matters265,000 students are lost annually from the education system, impacting future workforce potential and individual opportunities.

MINURSO contingent begins withdrawal from Laâyoune

The first contingent of the UN mission has started leaving following the expiration of its mandate on October 31, 2026.

The first contingent of the United Nations Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara (MINURSO) began its withdrawal from Laâyoune on Monday, November 16, 2026. This marks the start of the first phase of the mission's withdrawal process after its mandate expired on October 31, 2026.

This operation follows a working session presided over by King Mohammed VI on November 4, 2026, which focused on arrangements for the mission's cessation of activities. The 'Coordination Office with MINURSO', under the Ministry of Interior, is tasked with managing the mission's liquidation and withdrawal in coordination with UN services.

This structure aims to ensure a progressive, fluid, and orderly departure, facilitating the evacuation of civilian and military personnel, as well as equipment. The process is designed to manage the necessary arrangements for the mission's staff and material assets.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Interior is coordinating the orderly departure of MINURSO personnel and equipment following the mission's mandate expiration.

IM Alliance to distribute SonoScape endoscopy systems in Morocco

The T2S Group subsidiary expands its healthcare offerings with a new agreement for advanced diagnostic and treatment devices.

IM Alliance, a subsidiary of T2S Group, has become an authorized distributor in Morocco for SonoScape Medical Corp.'s video-endoscopy systems. This partnership allows IM Alliance to offer an integrated range of endoscopy equipment, from columns and scopes to diagnostic and treatment devices, to healthcare facilities across the country. The agreement aims to strengthen IM Alliance's market position in digestive endoscopy.

The new distribution agreement covers SonoScape's full portfolio for digestive and pulmonary endoscopy, including gastroscopes, colonoscopes, duodenoscopes, bronchoscopes, and high-definition systems. Notably, the HD-650 4K system, featuring advanced LED lighting and virtual chromoendoscopy modes like SFI, VIST, and HCI, will be available to improve lesion detection and characterization.

These SonoScape devices complement IM Alliance's existing offerings of interventional endoscopy solutions from Boston Scientific, such as the SpyGlass™ DS cholangioscopy system and the Hot AXIOS™ stent. This consolidated approach provides a single point of contact for gastroenterology services, covering routine examinations to advanced interventional procedures.

Why it mattersMoroccan healthcare facilities can now access a wider range of advanced diagnostic and treatment endoscopy equipment through IM Alliance.

French justice minister to visit Morocco for talks

Gérald Darmanin will meet his Moroccan counterpart Abdellatif Ouahbi to discuss judicial cooperation and combatting crime.

French Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin is scheduled to visit Morocco on November 19 and 20 for official talks. He will meet with his Moroccan counterpart, Abdellatif Ouahbi, to discuss judicial cooperation between the two nations.

The visit comes amid growing momentum in Moroccan-French relations, with a particular focus on legal, judicial, and security cooperation. Discussions are expected to cover expanding coordination in combating organized crime and drug trafficking, including information exchange and cooperation on cross-border criminal activities.

The talks aim to advance bilateral judicial cooperation by building on existing agreements and joint work programs between the justice ministries of Morocco and France.

Business · 2026-11-17

Morocco's advertising agencies launch international tender for market study

The Union of Communication Consulting Agencies (UACC) has opened an international open tender for a strategic study of the Moroccan advertising market.

The Union of Communication Consulting Agencies (UACC), known as UACC, has announced an international open tender for a strategic study of Morocco's advertising market.

The initiative is carried out with the Communication Department of the Ministry of Youth, Culture and Sports, which is supporting it. The study is intended to structure the sector and measure the weight of the market.

The tender is the first formal step in commissioning the study, and the work will cover the advertising market as a whole.

Why it mattersFirms bidding for the contract will be the ones shaping how the advertising market is measured.
