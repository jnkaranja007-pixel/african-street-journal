Moroccan Graduates Await Diploma Equivalency Decisions

Parents of 73 architecture graduates urge Ukraine's ambassador to expedite stalled degree recognition applications submitted in September 2025.

Parents of Moroccan students who completed architecture degrees in Ukraine are petitioning Ukraine’s ambassador to Morocco to hasten the processing of their diploma equivalency applications. The students, part of the 2025 graduating class, continued their studies remotely from Morocco due to the ongoing conflict. Their applications, submitted to Morocco’s Ministry of Higher Education in September 2025, remain unprocessed because the Ministry of National Territory Planning, Urban Planning, Housing and City Policy has not yet approved them.

Sources indicate that the housing ministry has requested an assessment by a specialized sectoral committee in science and technology to evaluate the graduates' academic qualifications. However, no clear procedure or deadline has been established for this assessment, leaving the graduates in uncertainty. Parents are seeking intervention to ensure their applications are handled similarly to those of the 2024 graduating class, who also studied remotely under exceptional circumstances.

The National Association of Parents of Moroccan Students in Ukraine has joined the call for adherence to legal processing deadlines, referencing a March 2023 memorandum that permitted remote studies. The applications are currently marked as “under review” on the higher education ministry’s online platform. The sectoral committee responsible for the 2025 graduates' applications is expected to convene in September.

Why it mattersMoroccan architecture graduates are affected by delays in diploma equivalency, impacting their ability to practice their profession in Morocco.

Spain Rejects Morocco Talks on Ceuta, Melilla Status

Spanish Foreign Minister states that the occupied cities of Ceuta and Melilla are Spanish and EU territories, ruling out any negotiation with Morocco on their status.

Spain has definitively rejected any possibility of negotiating the status of Ceuta and Melilla with Morocco, according to Spanish Foreign Minister José Manuel Albares. Speaking to the Spanish Congress's Foreign Affairs Committee, Albares asserted that discussions on this matter with Morocco have never occurred and will not occur. He emphasized that both cities are Spanish territories and integral parts of the European Union, with their residents being Spanish and European by law and sentiment.

Albares's statements come in response to recent remarks by Moroccan Justice Minister Abdellatif Ouahbi, who indicated that Morocco's claims to Ceuta and Melilla remain on the agenda and are raised in meetings with Spanish officials. The Spanish minister stressed the importance of maintaining open diplomatic channels with Morocco, citing their shared border and the need for ongoing dialogue to manage current crises and prevent future ones.

The minister also highlighted successful cooperation between the two nations, noting that it facilitated the return of 90% of the approximately 80,000 migrants who entered Ceuta on July 30 and 31. Efforts are continuing to repatriate the remaining individuals. Albares reiterated that Spain views Ceuta and Melilla as unequivocally Spanish, responding diplomatically to Moroccan statements perceived as challenging Spain’s territorial integrity.

Why it mattersMoroccan claims to Spanish cities could affect bilateral relations and border management, impacting trade and travel for businesses and individuals.

Morocco Ministry Orders Schools to Start Classes on Schedule

New directives from the Education Ministry mandate that classes begin September 7 as planned, irrespective of student enrollment delays, while also ensuring no advance.

Morocco's Education Ministry has directed all schools to commence classes on September 7 as scheduled, regardless of late student enrollments. Secretary-General Hussein Qaddad issued a memo stating that students will not be required to pay registration fees before attending. The ministry also instructed schools to begin recording and reporting daily student absences to provincial education offices, which will compile this data for regional academies. This move aims to ensure consistent attendance tracking from the first day of the 2026-27 academic year.

Provincial authorities are tasked with forming teams to monitor textbook and school supply availability before the academic year begins. These teams, supervised by provincial education directors, will visit bookstores and sales points to identify any shortages or distribution delays. The ministry is coordinating with economic affairs departments and parent associations to ensure smooth distribution and availability of essential learning materials across all educational levels.

The ministry also emphasized the need for schools to be fully prepared for the academic year's start. This includes ensuring facilities are equipped, boarding services are operational, and canteen and transportation services are activated. The General Inspectorate and General Inspectorate for Educational Affairs will oversee these preparations and monitor student attendance and textbook availability, reporting data to the Directorate of Educational Work.

Why it mattersThis directive affects students by ensuring classes start on time and registration fees are not a barrier to entry, while also impacting school administrators and supply chain managers responsible for educational materials.

Earthquakes Cause Localized Damage to Alhambra Embrasures

Officials are increasing preventive inspections and securing areas after August seismic activity revealed "localized anomalies" in the historic monument.

Seismic activity in August has caused localized damage to specific elements of Granada's Alhambra, leading officials to enhance preventive inspections. The Alhambra Council reported "localized anomalies" in several embrasures, particularly near the Tower of Homage and the Quebrada Tower. These areas have been cordoned off and secured to prevent any falling debris, with stabilization and safety measures implemented.

Initial assessments by technical teams indicate that the affected embrasures, which were part of interventions from the early 20th century, do not exhibit significant structural damage. Data collection is underway for detailed surveys to better evaluate the condition of these structures. These actions are part of a broader inspection campaign initiated after a series of earthquakes in the Granada region.

The collected data will contribute to digital models analyzing building behavior during future seismic events as part of the "Alhambra Living Lab" project, which utilizes the monument's digital twin. The Alhambra Council stated that detailed inspections will continue alongside routine monitoring to ensure the monument's preservation and the safety of visitors and staff.

Why it mattersThis event affects cultural heritage preservation efforts and the safety protocols for visitors and staff at a major historical site.

US Tightens Student Work Rules, Threatens University Certification

New federal guidance scrutinizes international student work permits, potentially impacting universities' ability to enroll foreign students.

The U.S. government has intensified regulations for international students' work and practical training permits, signaling that universities failing to adhere to federal rules risk losing their foreign student enrollment certifications. A federal memorandum issued on August 24 identified an increase in curricular practical training cases that may not meet required standards. This training allows students to gain work experience integral to their academic programs.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement's Student and Exchange Visitor Program is enforcing the new guidance. Some universities, including UCLA and UC Berkeley, have already begun suspending or reviewing work authorization requests. This action is part of a broader administrative focus on universities, which has also included scrutiny of protests and other campus policies.

The updated memorandum places greater pressure on universities to ensure compliance with federal rules governing international student work programs. Civil rights advocates have voiced concerns regarding free speech and due process protections in relation to these actions and other recent visa revocations affecting students involved in protests.

Why it mattersInternational students seeking work experience in the U.S. face stricter regulations and potential delays in obtaining necessary permits.
