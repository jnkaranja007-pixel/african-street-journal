Morocco Traffic Accidents Kill 26 in One Week

The General Directorate of National Security reported 1,975 traffic accidents in urban areas, resulting in 2,803 injuries and 26 fatalities.

Morocco recorded 26 deaths and 2,803 injuries from 1,975 traffic accidents in urban areas between August 24 and August 30, according to the General Directorate of National Security. Among the injured, 125 individuals sustained serious conditions. Police identified driver inattention, speeding, and failure to yield as primary causes for these incidents.

Authorities also cited pedestrian inattention, insufficient following distances, loss of vehicle control, and unsafe maneuvers such as unauthorized turns and driving against traffic as contributing factors. During the same period, police enforced traffic laws by recording 52,052 violations, leading to 7,702 reports filed with prosecutors and MAD 9.69 million collected from 44,350 on-the-spot fines.

In addition to fines and reports, law enforcement impounded 5,434 vehicles, seized 7,702 documents, and removed 391 vehicles from circulation to address the widespread traffic violations. These measures were implemented to curb the high number of accidents and improve road safety across Moroccan cities.

Why it matters26 people were killed and 2,803 injured in urban traffic accidents, affecting public safety and emergency services.

Moroccan Police Chief Meets Japan Ambassador to Boost Security Ties

Morocco's top security official and Japan's ambassador met in Rabat to strengthen cooperation against terrorism and organized crime.

Morocco’s Director General of National Security and Territorial Surveillance, Abdellatif Hammouchi, met with Japan’s Ambassador to Morocco, Nakata Masahiro, in Rabat. The meeting, according to a DGSN statement, is part of both nations' commitment to enhancing joint security cooperation and diversifying their security partnership.

During their discussion, Hammouchi and Masahiro reviewed current international and regional security matters of mutual interest to both countries. They also expressed a shared desire to further develop and strengthen their collaboration across all security-related fields, aiming to align perspectives and coordinate efforts.

The meeting's objective is to reinforce security and police cooperation between Morocco's DGSN-DGST and its international partners. This initiative seeks to address security threats and challenges, including terrorism and cross-border organized crime, by coordinating joint efforts and aligning viewpoints.

Why it mattersThe meeting between Morocco's top police chief and Japan's ambassador is intended to bolster security cooperation, which could indirectly affect farmers through improved stability and reduced crime, and directly benefit investors through enhanced regional security and trade, while diaspora members might see improved travel safety.

Moroccan athlete wins 3,000m steeplechase gold at Mediterranean Games

Salaheddine Ben Yazid secured the title with a time of 8 minutes and 46.14 seconds, ahead of a compatriot.

Moroccan athlete Salaheddine Ben Yazid won the gold medal in the 3,000-meter steeplechase on Wednesday during the athletics competitions at the 2026 Mediterranean Games in Taranto.

Ben Yazid secured the title after finishing the race in 8 minutes and 46.14 seconds. He finished ahead of his compatriot Mohammed Tinddout, who clocked a time of 8 minutes and 46.58 seconds.

Algerian Omar Seraich took third place with a time of 8 minutes and 47.31 seconds. The event was part of the athletics competitions at the games.

Why it mattersMoroccan athletes are affected by this win as it brings a gold medal to their country in a major sporting event.

Nurses Union Protests University Fees, Demands Free Higher Education

An open letter to the minister of higher education rejects new fees and academic restrictions that hinder healthcare workers' pursuit of advanced degrees.

Morocco's Independent Union of Nurses and Health Technicians has formally protested the introduction of university fees and academic restrictions, arguing they impede healthcare professionals from pursuing higher education. In an open letter dated August 31, the union stated that Law No. 59.24 effectively ends free higher education by imposing costs and conditions that could limit nurses and health technicians from enrolling in master's and doctoral programs.

The union highlighted the already demanding nature of healthcare work, citing night shifts, continuous duties, and psychological pressure, alongside the challenge of balancing professional and family life. Ahmed Chenaoui, national secretary of the union, described the fees as a "serious government attack on the constitutional right to free education" and a commercialization of higher learning, urging for its swift and permanent reversal.

The union contends that the new law places significant financial burdens on healthcare workers instead of offering administrative support or incentives for academic leave. They warned that these costs could discourage further education, thereby restricting access to scientific research and professional development opportunities for nurses and health technicians, and called for the removal of all such restrictions.

Why it mattersHealthcare workers are affected by university fees that create financial burdens, potentially limiting their access to advanced academic degrees and professional development.

Thailand Cuts Visa-Free Stay for Moroccans

Moroccan passport holders will no longer qualify for a 30-day visa exemption starting September 15, ending a previous 60-day allowance.

Thailand is reducing its visa-free entry period for Moroccan citizens from 60 days to 30 days, effective September 15, 2026. This change is part of a broader revision of the country's visa policies, which will also introduce 15-day visa-free stays for Seychelles and Mauritius citizens and update visa-on-arrival schemes for others. The new regulations follow announcements published in the Royal Thai Government Gazette on August 31.

Previously, Moroccan travelers could enjoy up to 60 days visa-free in Thailand. Under the updated system, citizens from 60 countries, including several Middle Eastern and European nations, will be eligible for a 30-day visa exemption. However, Morocco is not on this new list, meaning its citizens will need to adhere to different entry requirements. The Tourism Authority of Thailand advised travelers to verify specific entry rules based on their nationality before departure.

The revised visa policy also includes specific stipulations for land border crossings, limiting 30-day visa-exempt entries to twice per calendar year for most nationalities. While bilateral agreements may still permit longer stays for some, the general trend indicates a tightening of short-term tourist access. The Tourism Authority had been considering these policy adjustments since May, with details finalized in July.

Why it mattersMoroccan travelers to Thailand will face shorter visa-free stays, impacting tourism and travel plans.
