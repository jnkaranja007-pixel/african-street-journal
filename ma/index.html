China opens direct ammonia shipping route to Morocco

A 30,000-tonne cargo ship has departed Nanjing for Morocco, establishing a new direct maritime link for a key fertilizer industry input.

A direct maritime route for liquid ammonia from China to Morocco has been established with the departure of the vessel Arlon, carrying 30,000 tonnes from Nanjing on October 13. This new route is significant for Morocco's fertilizer industry, particularly for producer OCP, which faces rising input costs and supply chain disruptions. The shipment's journey covers over 10,000 nautical miles around the Cape of Good Hope.

The shipment marks Jiangsu Andefu Energy Supply Chain’s first direct ammonia delivery to North Africa, though Morocco has previously imported substantial quantities from China. In May, Morocco received 51,050 tonnes of ammonia from China, making it the largest export destination that month. OCP has been redirecting ammonia purchases to markets east of the Suez Canal due to unfavorable prices and supplies elsewhere.

Geopolitical tensions and shipping disruptions have increased prices for ammonia and sulfur, impacting OCP's financial performance. The company's first-half revenue fell 7.3% to MAD 48.37 billion, with operating earnings declining. OCP is increasing production of triple superphosphate, which does not require ammonia, to mitigate nitrogen input costs, while also pursuing domestic green ammonia production by 2027.

Why it mattersMoroccan fertilizer producers are affected by the new direct shipping route for ammonia, a key industrial input, amidst rising costs and supply chain pressures.

Morocco Faces Calls to Abolish Death Penalty Before Global Congress

Human rights groups urge legislative reform and treaty ratification as the nation prepares to host an international anti-death penalty event.

Moroccan human rights organizations and activists on Tuesday renewed calls for the abolition of the death penalty, urging the government to reform national legislation and ratify an international treaty banning capital punishment. The calls come as the country prepares to host the 10th World Congress Against the Death Penalty. Participants at a conference stressed the need to accelerate abolition efforts and align the nation's criminal justice system with its international human rights commitments.

Abderrahim Jamai, coordinator of the Moroccan Coalition for the Abolition of the Death Penalty, stated that Morocco's upcoming hosting of the global congress carries significant political and human rights responsibilities. He called for the removal of capital punishment from all legislation and urged authorities to apply abolition retroactively to the 56 individuals, including one woman, currently on death row. Jamai also advocated for reviewing cases of prisoners whose sentences may have expired.

Abdellah Messad, secretary-general of the Moroccan Observatory of Prisons, noted that while international human rights have faced setbacks, global abolition efforts are supported by various country commitments, Morocco's hosting of the congress, and backing from 75 UN experts. He cited Morocco's 2011 Constitution, recommendations from the Equity and Reconciliation Commission, and the country's vote for a UN resolution calling for a moratorium on executions as national-level support for abolition.

Why it mattersHuman rights activists and organizations are affected by the potential abolition of the death penalty and the reform of national legislation.

Moroccan Government Narrows Job Creation Promises

The new government program pledges one million jobs but specifies net additions for only 250,000 industrial positions.

Morocco's new government has committed to creating approximately one million jobs by 2031, a pledge that was part of Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri's Authenticity and Modernity Party campaign. However, the government's program submitted to Parliament distinguishes between total job creation and net additions to employment. Only 250,000 industrial positions are explicitly promised as net additions, while the remaining 750,000 are distributed across outsourced services, sports, young farmers, and roles related to the 2030 World Cup.

The program details 270,000 jobs in high-value outsourced services, 40,000 in sports, 80,000 for young resident farmers, and 360,000 positions linked to organizing the 2030 World Cup. These World Cup-related roles constitute 36% of the total employment target, with activity expected in construction and services leading up to the event. Questions remain about the long-term survival of these positions and whether the farmer figures represent new employment or existing agricultural workers.

The government also aims to reduce unemployment to 7% by 2031 and increase women's economic participation to 21.2%. Mansouri's four-party coalition holds a majority in Parliament, with 207 out of 395 seats, ensuring passage of the program. The High Commission for Planning reported 193,000 net jobs created in 2025, though agriculture saw a loss of 41,000 positions during that period.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the government's specific commitment to net job creation, which is lower than the overall job creation target.

Morocco Women's Football Team Holds 64th FIFA Ranking

The Atlas Lionesses maintained their global position despite a slight decrease in points following recent international matches.

Morocco's women's national football team remains in 64th place in the latest FIFA Women's World Ranking. The Atlas Lionesses earned 1,384.71 points, a decrease from the 1,402.24 points recorded in June 2026. Their performance during the polling period included two wins, two losses, and one draw.

On the continental stage, Morocco ranks fourth in Africa. Nigeria leads the region at 43rd globally, followed by South Africa at 59th and Cameroon at 52nd. Cameroon, the recent African champion, climbed nine places in the global rankings. Ghana also fell five places, tying Morocco at 64th worldwide.

The recent Women's Africa Cup of Nations significantly influenced the rankings, with Malawi making a historic 49-place jump to 104th globally. This ranking period incorporated results from 183 international fixtures, including friendlies and qualifiers for the 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup.

Why it mattersThe women's national football team's ranking affects national pride and potentially future international competition opportunities.

Moroccan Education Minister Meets Preschool Teachers After Protests

Preschool teachers' unions will meet with education ministry officials on Wednesday to discuss outstanding professional and socioeconomic issues.

Morocco's education minister has invited representatives from preschool teachers' unions to a meeting at the ministry headquarters in Rabat on Wednesday. This invitation comes after a series of protests by preschool teachers, which included an overnight sit-in outside Parliament that was later dispersed. The meeting aims to address ongoing demands from educators.

The discussions are expected to involve Mohamed Nabil Benabdellah, the minister of national education, preschool education, vocational training and skills development, and Kamal Hachoumi, the delegate minister in charge of preschool education and combating school dropout. Union sources consider this meeting a significant development in their campaign for solutions to their professional and socioeconomic challenges.

The three-union coordination body has been advocating for several key changes, including the integration of preschool teachers into the public sector, an increase in wages, and the elimination of intermediary associations in their employment processes. These demands highlight the persistent issues faced by preschool educators in the country.

Why it mattersPreschool teachers are affected by the ongoing negotiations for better wages and public sector integration.
