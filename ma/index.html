Spain finds no evidence Morocco linked to Ceuta migrant influx

Spain's government stated there is no evidence connecting Morocco to the mass arrival of approximately 70,000 migrants in Ceuta on July 30 and 31.

Spain's Justice Minister Félix Bolaños confirmed that there is no evidence linking Morocco to the mass entry of migrants into Ceuta, rejecting opposition claims of Rabat's involvement. Bolaños told the Spanish parliament’s Joint National Security Committee that Spain had no prior or precise information regarding the scale of the influx, which saw about 70,000 people enter the Spanish enclave.

Bolaños defended Spain's relationship with Morocco, describing it as based on cooperation and good neighborliness, and accused some opposition politicians of spreading "lies" and "doubts" without evidence. He also noted that neither the Spanish nor Moroccan governments had raised the issue of sovereignty over Ceuta and Melilla during their bilateral contacts.

Regarding migrant minors, Bolaños stated that Morocco has expressed willingness to cooperate on returns and family reunification, though these procedures are complex and must comply with Spanish legal safeguards. He emphasized that Spain's migration policy, partly due to cooperation with countries like Morocco, is achieving better results in controlling irregular migration than other European countries.

Why it mattersSpanish and Moroccan governments are affected by the need to manage migration flows and maintain diplomatic relations, while opposition parties are affected by their claims being refuted.

Morocco showcases video game ecosystem at Gamescom 2026

Morocco is participating for the first time in Gamescom 2026, the world’s largest video game event, to promote its gaming sector internationally.

The Moroccan Agency for Investment and Export Development (AMDIE), in collaboration with the Ministry of Youth, Culture and Communication, is leading Morocco’s participation at Gamescom 2026 in Cologne, Germany. This marks the country's first official presence at the event, which runs from August 26 to 30, with a national pavilion featuring an institutional delegation and 11 Moroccan video game development studios.

AMDIE stated that this participation aims to promote Moroccan expertise in the gaming sector, offering studios a platform to connect with publishers, investors, and major international industry players. The goal is to open new business and partnership opportunities for Moroccan companies, support their internationalization, and integrate them into global value chains.

This initiative builds on momentum from events like the “Morocco Gaming Expo,” which in May 2026 attracted over 80,000 visitors, 100 Moroccan exhibitors, and 48 international investors from 24 countries. Gamescom 2026 serves as an international showcase for Moroccan gaming talent and companies to present creations and explore collaborations.

Why it mattersMoroccan video game development studios and companies are directly affected by gaining a platform to connect with international publishers, investors, and industry players, potentially leading to new business and partnership opportunities.

Moroccan security agencies deny cyberattack on databases

Morocco's General Directorate of National Security (DGSN) and the General Directorate for Territorial Surveillance (DGST) stated their information systems remain secure, refuting.

Moroccan security authorities on Thursday denied reports of a cyberattack on their information systems or security databases. The DGSN-DGST joint pole stated that recently circulated personal data did not originate from a breach of their systems, which are protected by the highest standards of cybersecurity.

The authorities clarified that the personal data published online consisted of old information obtained from databases operated by insurance companies and organizations providing health and social coverage. They also warned against the circulation of fabricated photographs falsely presented as images of security personnel and altered documents allegedly from Moroccan security services.

The DGSN-DGST pole noted that some fake material uses uniforms and military ranks not present in Morocco and contains incorrect visual identities and linguistic errors. Security investigations into the circulation of this data and fabricated material will continue under the supervision of competent authorities.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens are affected by the circulation of old personal data and fabricated security information, which could lead to confusion or misuse.

Farida Jaidi advocates for public sanitation standards in Morocco

Farida Jaidi, founder of the Environmental Women Association, launched an initiative to raise awareness about access to clean and secure public toilets.

Farida Jaidi, founder of the Environmental Women Association, has initiated a campaign called “100 days, 100 messages, one fight: dignity.” The initiative aims to raise public awareness regarding the need for clean and secure public sanitation facilities across Morocco.

The campaign highlights the importance of addressing this issue, which the former ambassador and diplomat states has major implications for development and public health. Jaidi's efforts seek to remove the stigma surrounding discussions about public hygiene.

The initiative focuses on advocating for improved standards in public restrooms, emphasizing their role in ensuring dignity and well-being for all citizens. This push for better sanitation infrastructure is presented as a critical step for the country's overall progress.

Why it mattersCitizens who use public spaces are affected by the lack of clean and secure sanitation facilities, impacting their health and dignity.

Morocco coach Ouahbi tours Europe to assess players for AFCON qualifiers

Coach Mohamed Ouahbi will visit several European countries in early September to evaluate Moroccan international players before finalizing his squad for the upcoming Africa Cup.

Morocco's national football team coach, Mohamed Ouahbi, will travel to Europe in the first week of September to meet with players ahead of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifying matches. The tour aims to assess the physical and technical condition of Moroccan internationals, particularly those who recently moved clubs, are returning from injuries, or have caught the technical staff's attention.

Ouahbi's itinerary includes meetings with established players like Nayef Aguerd and Abdessamad Ezzalzouli, whose fitness is crucial for the team. He will also check on young talents such as Abdullah Ouazane from Ajax and Soufiane Farsi from Schalke, as part of efforts to get an up-to-date assessment of European-based players before naming his final squad.

The Confederation of African Football has scheduled Morocco's initial two qualifying matches. The Atlas Lions will host Gabon on September 25 at Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat, with a 7 p.m. GMT kickoff. They will then travel to South Africa to play Lesotho on September 29, with the match starting at 1 p.m. GMT at Toyota Stadium in Bloemfontein.

Why it mattersMoroccan football fans and players are directly affected by the coach's decisions, as the tour will determine the national team's squad for crucial AFCON qualifiers.
