Morocco raises income tax exemption threshold

Starting January 1, 2027, 87% of employees will pay no income tax, with potential monthly gains of MAD 500.

Morocco's 2027 Finance Bill proposes increasing the annual income tax exemption threshold from MAD 40,000 to MAD 60,000. Economy and Finance Minister Fouzi Lekjaa stated last Friday that this change, effective January 1, 2027, will exempt 87% of employees from income tax. The reform is projected to cost the state MAD 5.5 billion, with most affected employees gaining around MAD 500 monthly.

The new tax rates will see income between MAD 60,001 and MAD 80,000 taxed at 10%, MAD 80,001 to MAD 100,000 at 20%, MAD 100,001 to MAD 180,000 at 34%, and income above MAD 180,000 at 37%. Professional expense deductions will rise to 45%. Researcher Oussama Boughda noted that while employees are the primary beneficiaries, the changes also apply to professional and real estate income.

The reform will be phased in until 2031, when top tax rates will be 30%. Boughda estimated monthly gains could range from less than MAD 100 to MAD 500, depending on individual circumstances and family status. For instance, an employee earning MAD 10,000 monthly could see their net salary increase from MAD 8,904 to MAD 9,346.

Why it mattersEmployees and individuals with professional or real estate income in Morocco will see changes in their tax liabilities and potential monthly earnings.

Moroccan Inspector General meets UN mission commander in Agadir

Lieutenant General Mohammed Berrid and Major General Jawwad Ahmed discussed mission activities and coordination.

Lieutenant General Mohammed Berrid, FAR Inspector General and Commander of the Southern Zone, received Major General Jawwad Ahmed, the new Force Commander of the United Nations Mission in the Southern Provinces, at the Southern Zone Headquarters in Agadir on Thursday.

The meeting focused on the UN mission's activities, with both officials expressing satisfaction with the high levels of coordination achieved in operational, security, demining, and logistical support fields, according to a statement from the General Staff of the Royal Armed Forces.

This meeting occurred on October 29, 2026, at the FAR Headquarters in Agadir.

Why it mattersMorocco's military leadership met with the new UN mission commander in its southern provinces to discuss operational coordination.

Royal Air Maroc adds fifth weekly flight to Brazil

The national carrier plans daily flights to Sao Paulo by 2028 and a new route to Rio de Janeiro by end of 2027.

Royal Air Maroc (RAM) launched its fifth weekly direct flight between São Paulo and Casablanca on Thursday, less than two years after re-entering the South American market.

The airline plans daily flights from São Paulo by 2028 and a new route to Rio de Janeiro by the end of 2027, aiming to boost its presence in Brazil and connect to over 100 destinations.

The route carried approximately 80,000 passengers in 2025 with an 84% load factor, and Morocco welcomed 60,000 Brazilian visitors that year, a 35% increase.

Why it mattersMorocco's national airline is expanding its South American routes, connecting more travelers to and from the country.

Moroccan MPs question 2027 finance bill details

Lawmakers raise concerns over social aid, student grants, and electricity bill exemptions.

Moroccan MPs debated the 2027 Finance Bill on Thursday, with specific concerns raised about social aid and student grants. The House of Representatives’ Finance and Economic Development Committee heard calls to review eligibility criteria for financial support and ensure fairer public investment distribution.

PAM MP Imane Lamaoui highlighted limited university options in Ouarzazate, forcing students to incur extra costs. She welcomed a plan to increase grant recipients to 430,000 but argued that current income-based criteria exclude many needy students. RNI’s Taoufik Kamil questioned the government’s economic policy, noting that food prices have risen 29% since 2017, and criticized electricity bill exemptions that may miss poorer households.

Kamil also pointed out that despite increased health budgets, the sector’s problems persist. MPs stressed that student grants are an investment in Morocco’s future, warning that dropouts mean the country loses potential skilled workers. The debate also touched on advanced regionalization and the need for clear targets in human capital investment.

Morocco parties get November 15 deadline for election law changes

Interior Ministry seeks proposals for 2027 local, regional, and professional elections following parliamentary vote.

Morocco’s Interior Ministry has set November 15 as the deadline for political parties to submit proposed changes to electoral laws for the 2027 local, regional, and professional elections. This move follows September's parliamentary elections and initiates preparations for the next electoral cycle under royal instructions.

The deadline was announced after consultations on Thursday between the interior minister and party leaders, including those outside parliamentary groups. Discussions focused on extending measures from the September 23, 2026, elections, aimed at strengthening electoral integrity, to the upcoming contests for communal, provincial, and regional councils, professional chambers, and the House of Councillors.

Party representatives shared initial observations, with the ministry requesting written proposals to shape draft amendments. The government aims to complete legal preparations for the 2027 elections promptly. Participating parties expressed willingness to contribute to the process, which follows a September parliamentary election that reshaped the political landscape.

Why it mattersPolitical parties in Morocco must propose changes to electoral laws by November 15 for the 2027 elections.
