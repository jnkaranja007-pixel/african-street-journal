Morocco cuts self-employed tax rate

The government proposes lowering the tax rate for self-employed workers from 30% to 20% while introducing measures to prevent employer abuse.

Morocco plans to reduce the tax rate for self-employed workers from 30% to 20% as part of its 2027 budget proposal. The House of Representatives Finance and Economic Development Committee approved the changes on Wednesday. The government aims to ease the tax burden on independent workers and prevent companies from misclassifying employees as self-employed to avoid higher employment taxes.

Economy and Finance Minister Fouzi Lekjaa stated that some employers hire workers as self-employed to bypass income tax rates that can reach 20% or 25%, paying only a 1% tax. The proposed amendments raise the annual turnover limit for services provided to the same client from MAD 80,000 to MAD 100,000. Earnings up to this new limit will be exempt from withholding tax at source. If turnover exceeds MAD 100,000, the withholding tax rate will drop to 20%.

These changes align the tax treatment of self-employed individuals with the broader income tax reform, which increased the annual income tax exemption threshold to MAD 60,000. Lekjaa emphasized the government's intention to preserve the benefits of self-employment while ensuring companies do not exploit the system to evade taxes. Morocco has approximately 430,000 businesses registered under the self-employed system.

Why it mattersSelf-employed workers earning over MAD 100,000 annually will see their withholding tax rate decrease to 20% under the proposed 2027 budget.

Moroccan MPs push marketing reform to bring down food prices

Lawmakers say some produce doubles or triples in price after leaving wholesale markets, with losses on tomatoes and potatoes reaching around 30%.

Moroccan lawmakers on Thursday pressed for changes to how farm produce is marketed and distributed, and for tighter oversight of middlemen, as food prices dominated debate on the Agriculture Ministry's draft 2027 budget. During a meeting of the House of Representatives' Productive Sectors Committee, parliamentary groups blamed weak links in the distribution chain for much of the gap between what farmers receive and what consumers pay.

Ahmed El Abbadi, head of the Progress and Socialism group, said cutting the number of intermediaries and curbing speculation were essential to lower prices. He cited a parliamentary fact-finding mission on agricultural marketing, which found that some products double or triple in price after leaving wholesale markets, while losses on tomatoes and potatoes reach around 30%. He called for investment in packaging, storage and refrigeration.

The Istiqlal group urged a production policy based on annual and medium-term supply and demand forecasts, and more cereals, legumes, seeds and fodder crops. It said agricultural exports should not undermine Morocco's food and water security.

Why it mattersConsumers face higher prices for produce, while farmers take losses from spoilage and weak storage, so changes to the distribution chain would affect both.

Rabat hospital performs first liver transplant from a living donor

The donor was the recipient's sister, and both women have since gone home from the Rabat hospital.

The University Hospital International Mohammed VI in Rabat carried out its first liver transplant from a living donor on October 15. The organ came from the recipient's sister, a young patient with severe acute hepatitis.

The hospital said the operation was its first of this kind. Both the donor and the recipient have since returned home.

The report does not give further details on the hospital's plans for future living-donor transplants.

Why it mattersPatients at the hospital now have a second option for a liver transplant, since a living relative can donate part of the liver.

French firm Colas Rail wins €29 million Moroccan rail contract

The railway operator ONCF has awarded Colas Rail work on track, catenary and civil engineering in the Casablanca area.

Morocco's National Railways Office (ONCF) has awarded French firm Colas Rail a contract worth 29 million euros, roughly 316 million dirhams, as part of the Kenitra–Marrakesh high-speed rail project. The work covers track, catenary and civil engineering in the Casablanca area, between Roches-Noires station and the Nouaceur Technicentre.

The deal includes railway rehabilitation, new rail tracks, catenary installation and related civil engineering. Hervé Le Joliff, Executive Vice President of Railways at Colas Rail, said Morocco is a strategic market and that the contract reaffirms the company's commitment to high-speed rail in the Kingdom. Colas Rail, which has operated in Morocco for more than 17 years, employs around 550 staff.

The company has carried out more than 40 projects across Morocco, including work on the high-speed line. It is already building the Kenitra–Marrakesh line under two contracts secured in January 2025, worth 250 million euros in total.

Why it mattersMorocco's rail network gains another 29 million euros of track and electrification work, with Colas Rail's crews adding to the 550 people it already employs in the country.
