Morocco Bars Convicted Candidates From 2026 Elections

New rules disqualify individuals with first-instance or final convictions on appeal from running, strengthening electoral integrity.

Morocco's upcoming 2026 parliamentary elections will enforce stricter candidate eligibility, barring those convicted of offenses that undermine electoral ethics. Candidacies can be rejected or annulled if violations are confirmed after submission. Individuals with first-instance convictions for serious charges, or final convictions on appeal leading to loss of eligibility, will also be ineligible to run.

These measures are part of a strengthened legal framework designed to ensure electoral integrity, fair competition, and transparency, aiming to restore public trust in elected bodies. The reforms also extend the ban for officials removed from office to two full electoral terms, preventing their swift return to public service after misconduct.

Penalties for electoral crimes are toughened, with increased prison terms and financial penalties, and some violations now classified as crimes. The legislation also addresses digital risks, criminalizing the use of social media, online platforms, and AI for electoral fraud, including spreading false information or manipulating content. Paid political advertisements on foreign platforms are banned to prevent external influence.

Why it mattersCandidates and officials convicted of electoral offenses or serious misconduct are directly affected by these new eligibility rules and extended bans.

King's Visit Threatens Morocco-Spain Dispute Over Ceuta, Melilla

A planned royal visit to Spanish enclaves on Morocco's coast could reignite a decades-old territorial dispute, according to a Moroccan official.

A planned visit by Spain’s King Felipe VI to the North African cities of Ceuta and Melilla could reignite tensions with Morocco, according to Yahya Yahya, head of a Moroccan committee campaigning for the territories’ return. Yahya stated the visit could trigger a stronger reaction than a similar trip in 2007 by Spain’s former King Juan Carlos I and Queen Sofia, which sparked widespread anger and condemnation. He warned that a new visit could provoke an even stronger response and said the Spanish government would bear responsibility for any consequences.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez confirmed King Felipe VI plans to visit both cities, citing “sufficient arguments and reasons” and appropriate conditions, though no date was announced. Yahya, who heads the Committee for the Liberation of Ceuta, Melilla and their affiliated islands, described the territories as an “existential” issue for Morocco, comparable to the Western Sahara issue. He accused Spanish leaders of reviving a sensitive dispute and vowed his committee would be present to reaffirm Morocco's claim.

Ceuta and Melilla are Spanish territories on Morocco’s northern coast, which Morocco considers part of its territory while Spain maintains sovereignty. The dispute arises as Spain and Morocco have sought to strengthen bilateral ties in recent years, particularly on migration and security cooperation. Sanchez described cooperation with Morocco on migration as “very positive.”

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens and officials are affected by the potential visit as it reignites a sensitive territorial dispute over enclaves they consider part of their nation.

Moroccan Jewish community urges election turnout, abstains from endorsements

Community representatives stated that voting decisions are personal and should reflect each citizen’s preferred political program, rejecting a separate electoral bloc.

Morocco’s Jewish community is encouraging its members to participate in the upcoming September 23 parliamentary election, but will not endorse any specific party or candidate. Community representatives emphasized that voting choices are personal and should align with individual political preferences, discouraging the idea of Jewish Moroccans acting as a distinct electoral bloc with unique demands.

Jacky Kadoch, president of the Jewish community in the Marrakech-Safi region, stated that political parties should serve Jewish citizens under the same policies and commitments applied to all Moroccans, without requiring special platform measures. He noted that matters concerning Jewish institutions have traditionally been managed through the Interior Ministry and praised the state's support for preserving Jewish heritage sites.

Kadoch estimated that fewer than 2,000 to 2,200 Jewish people currently reside in Morocco, with some areas having only 20 to 30 community members. Eligible voters living abroad can participate via the proxy voting system available to registered Moroccans overseas.

Why it mattersJewish citizens in Morocco are encouraged to vote, but their individual choices are paramount, with no party endorsement offered.

Amrabat Joins FC Utrecht on Free Transfer

Veteran forward Nordin Amrabat, 39, signed with FC Utrecht after departing Wydad AC, with his brother's influence playing a key role.

Dutch Eredivisie club FC Utrecht has officially signed 39-year-old forward Nordin Amrabat on a free transfer. The move takes immediate effect, making him eligible for upcoming matches after training with the squad. Amrabat joins the club following his departure from Moroccan side Wydad AC.

The transfer was influenced by his brother, former FC Utrecht midfielder Sofyan Amrabat, who contacted technical director Jordy Zuidam. FC Utrecht management expressed enthusiasm, highlighting Amrabat's career at PSV Eindhoven, Galatasaray, Málaga, and Watford. Amrabat shared his pride, calling the move the 'cherry on the cake' of his career.

Amrabat noted his family's connection to the club through his brother's time in the youth academy and their excitement to return to Stadion Galgenwaard. He will now aim to make an immediate impact for FC Utrecht, which is currently 17th in the Eredivisie standings.

Why it mattersMoroccan forward Nordin Amrabat's transfer to FC Utrecht affects diaspora football fans by bringing a veteran player with family ties to the club back to the Netherlands.

UN Rights Team Assesses Moroccan Ombudsman's Work

A United Nations human rights delegation visited Laayoune to review mediation and citizen rights protection mechanisms.

An international human rights delegation, led by Teresa McCordy of the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights, met with officials at the Moroccan Ombudsman Institution's regional office in Laayoune. The visit focused on understanding the office's role in mediation, redress, and safeguarding citizens' rights when dealing with public authorities. Cherif Laroussi, the regional delegate, provided an overview of the office's functions, including complaint processing and inter-governmental communication.

Discussions during the visit centered on enhancing the accessibility of mediation and redress systems for citizens and strengthening the protection of rights. The delegation and Moroccan officials also explored avenues for improving the quality of public services. Moulay Lahcen Naji, chairman of the Independent Commission for Human Rights in Africa, also attended the meeting, underscoring the collaborative nature of the exchange.

McCordy showed particular interest in the regional office's methods for handling complaints and its communication strategies with public institutions. This engagement is part of wider initiatives aimed at fostering cooperation and sharing best practices in human rights and mediation.

Why it mattersThe UN delegation's assessment of the Ombudsman's office affects citizens by potentially improving their access to rights protection and public services.
