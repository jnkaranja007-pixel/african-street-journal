Morocco's $11.3 Billion Bad-Debt Market Attracts First International Investors

The International Finance Corporation and German debt specialist EOS Group are preparing a €60 million facility to acquire troubled loans in Morocco.

Morocco's $11.3 billion market for troubled loans is set to attract its first international investors, as the International Finance Corporation (IFC) and German debt specialist EOS Group prepare a €60 million facility. This initiative, reported by Asharq Bloomberg, marks the IFC's first entry into Morocco’s distressed-debt market. The country is also moving towards establishing a regulated secondary market for nonperforming loans.

Under the planned DARP NPL EOS Morocco facility, both the IFC and EOS will each contribute up to €30 million. This facility will target troubled loans held by Moroccan banks, encompassing debts from individuals and companies of all sizes. It will also focus on real estate assets that financial institutions have acquired through debt recovery efforts, offering banks a new method to clear distressed assets from their balance sheets.

Morocco's nonperforming loans have increased by an average of 6% annually over the last decade, reaching MAD 104.7 billion by the end of June, a 1.9% rise from the previous year, according to Bank Al-Maghrib. A functioning secondary market could enable banks to convert these distressed loans into cash, potentially freeing up funds for new lending and alleviating pressure on bank balance sheets. A draft law for a regulated system to sell troubled loans is currently undergoing consultations.

Why it mattersMoroccan banks, individuals, and companies with troubled loans are directly affected as new international investment aims to clear distressed assets and potentially free up funds for new lending.

Dolly Parton dies at 80, family announces

The country music singer, songwriter, actress, and philanthropist, known for hits like “Jolene” and “9 to 5,” passed away at the age of 80.

Dolly Parton, the country music legend whose career spanned over six decades, has died at 80, her family announced on Tuesday. Her nephew disclosed her death in an Instagram video, according to People, though a cause of death was not immediately announced.

Born into a poor family in rural Tennessee in 1946, Parton moved to Nashville after high school and became one of country music’s most successful performers. She won 11 Grammy Awards and was inducted into both the Country Music Hall of Fame and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. Her husband of nearly 60 years, Carl Dean, died in 2025.

Beyond her musical career, Parton also starred in films such as "9 to 5" and "Steel Magnolias." Her philanthropic efforts included the Imagination Library, which distributed hundreds of millions of free books to children.

Why it mattersFans of country music and film worldwide are affected by the loss of a prominent cultural figure whose work spanned music, acting, and philanthropy.

European Commission reports extreme weather impacts summer crops

A European Commission report indicates that persistent heatwaves and low rainfall have affected summer crops and feed supplies across Western and Central Europe.

Extreme weather conditions, including sustained heat and drought over the past two months, have impacted summer crops and feed supplies in Western and Central Europe, according to a report released Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, 2026, by the European Commission. The report from the Joint Research Centre's agricultural resource monitoring system highlighted significant effects in France, southern Germany, northern and central Italy, Austria, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Hungary, and western Romania.

High temperatures combined with a lack of rain have reduced crop yields and feed availability, with irrigation water scarcity worsening water stress in several regions. Benelux countries, Slovenia, and Croatia have seen heat and drought lower summer crop yield forecasts, particularly for maize. The executive body of the European Union noted that feed supplies are a concern across much of Western and Central Europe due to reduced pasture areas.

The European Commission reiterated its commitment to supporting farmers to ensure continued provision of affordable, quality food, stating that the Common Agricultural Policy plays a key role in making agriculture more resilient to climate change. This policy supports investments in efficient irrigation, climate-resilient infrastructure, water-saving technologies, and the transition to more climate-resistant crops.

Why it mattersFarmers in affected European regions face increased pressure on their crops, feed, livestock, and incomes due to drought and extreme heat.

Morocco observes 96% partial lunar eclipse before dawn Friday

A deep partial lunar eclipse will be visible across Morocco early Friday, with Earth’s shadow covering about 96% of the moon’s surface at its peak.

Morocco will experience a partial lunar eclipse before dawn on Friday, August 28, with Earth's shadow obscuring approximately 96% of the moon's visible surface. The event's peak, when the moon will appear copper, orange, or deep red, is expected around 5:12 a.m. local time, according to NASA and local eclipse calculations.

The penumbral phase will begin at about 2:23 a.m., though initial shading may be difficult to discern. The clearly visible partial phase starts at approximately 3:34 a.m. and will conclude around 6:52 a.m. In Casablanca, the moon is expected to set at about 7:10 a.m., influencing the final visible stages for observers.

Unlike a total lunar eclipse, a narrow section of the moon will remain directly illuminated, preventing complete darkness. Observers are advised to find an area with an unobstructed western horizon for the best view. The next visible partial lunar eclipse in Morocco is anticipated on January 12, 2028.

Why it mattersResidents across Morocco will have the opportunity to observe a significant celestial event, with the moon appearing dramatically colored for several hours before sunrise.

Italian authorities investigate Moroccan man's death during arrest in Bologna

Italian authorities are continuing their investigation into the death of 42-year-old Moroccan Abdellah Fakir, who died on July 19 during an arrest by police officers in Bologna.

New forensic findings have emerged in the ongoing investigation into the death of Abdellah Fakir. Initial tests on handcuffs used during his arrest revealed no clear fingerprints, a detail Fabio Anselmo, the family's lawyer, described as "particularly strange" and potentially significant for the inquiry.

Further analysis detected traces of a substance believed to be blood, though its nature is not yet definitively determined and awaits additional testing. Other biological and hair traces were also found and are currently being analyzed to identify their nature and origin.

Italian media reported that the public prosecutor's office conducted these tests on seized equipment, including the handcuffs. Authorities are focusing on the details of the arrest to determine the circumstances surrounding Fakir's death, which occurred while officers were attempting to restrain him on Svivo Street in Bologna.

Why it mattersThe family of Abdellah Fakir is directly affected by the ongoing investigation into his death during an arrest, as new forensic findings are emerging.
