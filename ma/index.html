Morocco's Mansouri defends new government structure

The head of government addressed parliament, explaining decisions on ministerial appointments and budget deadlines.

Head of government Fatima Zahra Mansouri defended her new administration before the House of Councillors on October 21, addressing accusations of disorder and continuity with the previous government. She explained the dismissal of vocational training minister Madiha Khayr, stating that concerns about Khayr's past emerged only after her appointment. Mansouri requested Khayr's dismissal from King Mohammed VI, who approved it, citing difficulties in overseeing public funds.

Mansouri also defended the government's decision to approve and submit the draft 2027 Finance Bill before securing parliamentary confidence, emphasizing the need to meet the October 20 statutory deadline. She described this as an achievement that prevented financial year delays, though constitutional experts have raised disagreements about the legality of proceeding before investiture.

Furthermore, Mansouri rejected claims that her government was merely a continuation of the previous one led by Aziz Akhannouch. She dismissed the National Rally of Independents' accusation that her coalition reproduced past policies without a distinct agenda, stating that her government was formed around shared political choices. She also explained that rural development was moved from the Agriculture Ministry due to the failure of previous programs to yield expected improvements for rural residents.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens are affected by the government's decisions regarding ministerial appointments, budget processes, and rural development programs.

Morocco divorce cases rise 4.38% in 2025

Total divorce and judicial separation filings reached 154,377, reversing a slight decline from the previous year, according to judicial data.

Morocco recorded 154,377 divorce and judicial separation cases in 2025, a 4.38% increase from the prior year. This rise follows a small decrease in 2024, when filings dropped to 147,895 from 151,168 in 2023. The Supreme Council of the Judicial Power's annual report, published in the Official Gazette, indicates that consensual divorce and judicial separation due to marital discord remain the most common reasons for ending marriages.

Divorce cases saw a slight uptick in 2024 to 40,212 from 40,028, and further increased by 1,252 cases in 2025. Judicial separation filings, however, experienced a decline in 2024 to 107,681 from 111,140, before rising by 5,230 cases to 112,911 in 2025. Consensual divorce constituted nearly 97% of all divorce cases throughout the three-year period analyzed.

The prevalence of consensual divorce is attributed to procedural flexibility and mutual agreement between spouses, which can mitigate conflict and reduce negative consequences for families. Judicial separation based on marital discord, known as shiqaq, accounted for the majority of judicial separation cases. The report distinguishes divorce as a dissolution of the marital bond and judicial separation as a court-ordered dissolution based on specified grounds.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens undergoing marital dissolution are affected by the increasing number of divorce and judicial separation cases.

Africa Eco Race to cross Morocco's southern provinces in January

The 18th edition of the rally will start in Tangier and pass through Laayoune and Dakhla before entering Mauritania, following past disruptions.

Organizers of the Africa Eco Race have announced the route for the rally's 18th edition, scheduled to begin on January 24, 2027. Competitors will travel from Tangier through several Moroccan cities, including Meknes, Erfoud, M’Hamid, Assa, Laayoune, and Dakhla, before crossing into Mauritania at Guerguerat. The race, which combines off-road racing with long-distance navigation, will conclude on February 6 near Dakar, Senegal. Organizers are planning to enhance logistical and safety measures along the route.

The passage through Morocco's southern provinces has previously faced objections from the Polisario Front. In 2020, demonstrators briefly obstructed the rally at Guerguerat, though participants were allowed to continue. This incident occurred before a wider blockade of the road later that year. The movement had previously warned organizers about risks in the Sahara, but the 2022 rally proceeded without reported security issues.

The Africa Eco Race, created in 2009, revived the traditional African route for the rally after the Dakar Rally relocated. Morocco, Mauritania, and Senegal have been key locations for the competition. The 2021 edition was cancelled due to the COVID-19 pandemic, and severe weather conditions led to the cancellation of the opening stages in January 2026. Tangier will host the start for the second consecutive year in 2027.

Why it mattersCompetitors in the Africa Eco Race will navigate through Morocco's southern provinces, facing potential route disruptions and security considerations.

Morocco beat Angola 5-2 to win fourth Futsal AFCON title

The Atlas Lions secured their fourth consecutive continental championship by defeating Angola in the final on home soil.

Morocco clinched their fourth consecutive Futsal Africa Cup of Nations title on Wednesday, beating Angola 5-2 in the final at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Sports Hall in Rabat. Hicham Dguig’s team extended their continental dominance with a victory that saw them score three second-half goals.

Othmane El Idrissi opened the scoring for Morocco, but Joao Cambangula equalized for Angola. Youssef Jouad restored the hosts’ advantage before halftime. After the break, Idriss Raiss El Fenni, Ismail Amazal, and Bilal Bakkali added to the score, while Fortes Anderson netted Angola’s second goal.

This triumph marks Morocco's fourth consecutive African championship, following wins in 2016, 2020, and 2024. The team, coached by Dguig throughout this period, has established a commanding record in African futsal.

Why it mattersMoroccan futsal players and fans are affected by this victory as it secures their fourth consecutive continental championship title.

Boy, 11, found dead as Ziz river levels rise

Rescue teams recovered the body of an 11-year-old boy swept away by the swollen Ziz River in Erfoud, Morocco, following heavy rainfall.

Rescue teams recovered the body of an 11-year-old boy on Wednesday after he was swept away by the swollen Ziz River in Erfoud, southeastern Morocco. The child disappeared around 6 p.m. Tuesday, prompting a search involving local authorities and Civil Protection personnel. Specialist divers later joined the operation, with local outlets reporting his body was found Wednesday evening, roughly 24 hours after he went missing.

The incident occurs as rising water levels have prompted warnings across the Drâa-Tafilalet region, where the Guir-Ziz-Ghris Basin Agency activated flood-monitoring and early-warning stations on October 18. The agency urged residents to avoid riverbeds and refrain from crossing waterways as increased runoff moved through the region’s river network. Water levels have also risen sharply at Hassan Addakhil Dam near Errachidia, where the reservoir’s filling rate increased from 65.04% on October 19 to 71.99% the following day.

Authorities have continued urging caution around swollen rivers and low-lying crossings, where rapidly moving water can remain dangerous even after rainfall subsides. The latest incident follows several days of heightened flood monitoring in southeastern Morocco, with the condition of roads and communities along the Ziz River still requiring assessment.

Why it mattersAn 11-year-old boy is dead after being swept away by the Ziz River, and residents are warned to stay away from waterways due to rising levels.
