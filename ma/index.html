El Mansouri government wins parliamentary investiture

Morocco's House of Representatives approved the government program with 204 votes in favor.

Morocco’s Head of Government Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri secured parliamentary investiture on Thursday after her government’s program received 204 votes in favor and 127 against in the House of Representatives. A total of 331 lawmakers participated in the vote, which concluded a plenary session where El Mansouri addressed remarks on her government’s program for the 2027-2031 term.

During the session, El Mansouri defended the government’s decision to discuss the 2027 Finance Bill before investiture, stating it did not violate the Constitution. She also engaged in sharp exchanges with the opposition Justice and Development Party (PJD) regarding electoral integrity and women’s employment. El Mansouri, a lawyer, asserted her understanding of constitutional law in her defense of the government's actions.

El Mansouri accused the PJD of double standards on elections, questioning their claims of fairness when they won and allegations of manipulation when they did not. She highlighted the Interior Ministry's participatory approach to electoral legislation, which included all parties, even the PJD, in its development.

Why it mattersMoroccan citizens are affected by the new government's program and its approach to the Finance Bill and electoral integrity.

Morocco central bank detects 4,064 counterfeit banknotes in 2025

Cash in circulation in Morocco reached a record MAD 513 billion in 2025, a 15.5% increase year over year.

Morocco's central bank, Bank Al-Maghrib, detected 4,064 counterfeit banknotes in 2025, a 10% decrease from the previous year. These fake notes were valued at nearly MAD 574,000. The detection rate fell to 1.2 counterfeit notes per million in circulation, down from 1.5 in 2024.

Despite the drop in counterfeits, cash in circulation surged to a record MAD 513 billion in 2025, a 15.5% increase from the prior year. This highlights a continued reliance on physical money, with cash in circulation now representing 30% of Morocco's GDP. The central bank attributed the improved counterfeit detection to stronger security features on new banknotes and upgraded processing equipment.

MAD 200 banknotes were the most frequently detected counterfeit denomination, making up 56% of fakes found in 2025. Copying, imaging, and color-printing techniques accounted for 97% of identified counterfeiting methods. The central bank's 2023 banknote series now accounts for 27% of banknotes in circulation.

Why it mattersMorocco's economy relies heavily on cash, with circulation reaching MAD 513 billion in 2025, affecting businesses and individuals using physical money.

Morocco wins fourth African futsal title

The Atlas Lions defeated Angola 5-2 in the Futsal Africa Cup of Nations final in Rabat.

Morocco secured its fourth African futsal title by defeating Angola 5-2 in the Futsal Africa Cup of Nations final held in Rabat on Wednesday. The Confederation of African Football (CAF) praised the Atlas Lions' dominance after the victory.

Othmane Idrissi opened the scoring for Morocco in the 17th minute, with Angola equalizing a minute later. Youssef Jouad re-established the lead before halftime, and second-half goals from Idriss Raiss El Fenni, Ismail Amzal, and Bilal Bakkali sealed the win. Anderson Fortes scored Angola's second goal late in the match.

This win marks Morocco's third consecutive continental title and makes them the first nation to achieve four tournament victories. Angola finished as runner-up, while Egypt claimed third place by beating Algeria 8-1.

Why it mattersMoroccan futsal players and their supporters in Rabat celebrated a fourth continental title.

Climate · 2026-10-23

Morocco construction boom strains coastline

Sand extraction for building projects is damaging coastal areas, prompting calls for alternatives.

Morocco's construction boom is depleting sand resources, threatening coastal erosion in some areas. The intensive extraction of sand, a key material for building, has raised environmental concerns. This practice has become a significant threat to the nation's coastlines.

The United Nations issued a warning on May 12 regarding the planet's most extracted resource. The UN report highlights the unsustainable nature of current sand mining practices. Officials are exploring alternatives to lessen the construction sector's dependence on this finite resource.

The search for replaceable materials is underway as the construction sector faces pressure to adopt more sustainable practices. The long-term impact on coastal ecosystems and infrastructure remains a concern for environmentalists and policymakers.

Why it mattersMorocco's construction sector's reliance on sand extraction is causing coastal erosion.

Moroccan departures fuel Canary Islands migrant surge

Migrant arrivals at Spain’s Canary Islands jumped 159% in two months, with Morocco a key departure point.

Migrant arrivals in Spain’s Canary Islands surged 159% between August 16 and October 15, reaching 4,162 people. This represents a sharp increase from 1,608 arrivals during the same period in 2025, with daily averages rising from 21 to 68. Despite this surge, total arrivals since January remain 33.5% below last year’s levels.

Spanish Interior Ministry figures indicate Morocco is a major departure point for these journeys. Police sources estimate around 60% of boats reaching the islands depart from Morocco’s Atlantic coast. Between September 16 and October 15 alone, 3,378 migrants arrived, a significant acceleration from the previous month.

The Canary Islands’ regional government has requested additional police resources and the deployment of the EU border agency Frontex. Regional officials have warned of mounting pressure on reception facilities, particularly on El Hierro, where several recent arrivals have involved deaths at sea.

Why it mattersMoroccan departures are a main cause of the surge in migrant arrivals in the Canary Islands.
