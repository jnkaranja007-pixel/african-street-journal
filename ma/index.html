Morocco Central Bank Clarifies Bad Debt Sales Framework

A planned framework for selling nonperforming loans will not result in debt forgiveness for borrowers, according to Bank Al-Maghrib.

Morocco's central bank, Bank Al-Maghrib, has clarified that a new framework for selling nonperforming loans does not entail debt forgiveness. The initiative, involving the International Finance Corporation and German group EOS, aims to establish a private company to purchase portfolios of unpaid loans from banks. This company will then be responsible for recovering the outstanding amounts directly from borrowers.

A source familiar with the matter stressed that the purchase of these distressed debts will not lead to the cancellation of borrowers' financial obligations. The primary goal is to reorganize the relationship between banks and entities acquiring their nonperforming assets. Financial institutions and investment funds involved will communicate recovery procedures to affected borrowers, under rules designed to prevent exploitation.

This clarification accompanies draft legislation, Law No. 02.26, which is currently under public comment. The proposed law establishes a legal structure for credit institutions to transfer nonperforming loans under specific conditions. It defines nonperforming debt and regulates the transfer process, ensuring that the identity of the creditor changes but the underlying debt remains unless a separate agreement is made.

Why it mattersBorrowers are affected because they will remain liable for their outstanding debts even after the loans are sold to a new entity.

Moroccan boxers win six medals at Mediterranean Games

The national team secured two gold medals, one silver, and three bronze, marking a historic achievement after only two months of preparation.

Morocco's national boxing team achieved a historic success at the Mediterranean Games in Taranto, clinching six medals including two golds. National Technical Director Gabriele Martelli announced the medal haul upon the team's return, highlighting victories by Saïda Lahmidi in the -70 kg category and Soulaimane Samghouli in the -65 kg class. The team also secured a silver medal through Widad Bertal (-54 kg) and three bronze medals from Mounia Toutir (-65 kg), Yasmine Mouttaki (-51 kg), and Abdelhak Nadir (-70 kg).

These performances were achieved with just two months of focused preparation, integrating discipline, psychological training, nutrition, and physical conditioning. The Royal Moroccan Boxing Federation (FRMB) views these results as a significant step towards future international competitions, including the World Championship and Olympic qualifiers. Vice-president Nabil Hilmi noted that Morocco had not won two gold medals in a single edition of the Games since 1987, and Saïda Lahmidi's gold was a first for Moroccan women's boxing in the competition.

Gold medalist Saïda Lahmidi expressed happiness with the achievement, attributing it to team solidarity and effective training camps, while Soulaimane Samghouli highlighted the high level of competition. The Moroccan team finished third in the boxing medal table, behind Spain and Italy. The FRMB plans a national boxing development program to build on this momentum and continue making history.

Why it mattersMoroccan boxers achieved historic success, winning six medals at the Mediterranean Games, which serves as a positive indicator for future international competitions.

Morocco Minister Urges Prevention in African Peace Efforts

Nasser Bourita called for tackling conflict root causes and investing in sustainable peace during an African Union session in Luanda.

Moroccan Minister of Foreign Affairs, Nasser Bourita, stressed the importance of prioritizing conflict prevention in Africa's peace and security strategies. Speaking at the 26th Extraordinary Session of the African Union's Executive Council in Luanda, Bourita emphasized addressing the underlying causes of conflicts by integrating the peace-security-development nexus into the continent's preventive actions. He stated that Morocco fully supports the session's goals and the Luanda action plan, advocating for coherence between the African Union and the United Nations.

The session, focused on strengthening and operationalizing the African Peace and Security Architecture, aims to foster a peaceful and secure continent. This extraordinary meeting was convened following a decision from the 39th Ordinary Session of the Assembly of Heads of State and Government in February 2026. The summit's agenda included enhancing conflict prevention and resolution mechanisms and their financing, with Morocco reaffirming its commitment to the Luanda action plan's implementation.

The proceedings are expected to conclude with the adoption of a decision, the Luanda action plan, and its implementation matrix. Minister Bourita also highlighted that financing mechanisms must strictly align with the Union's budget procedures. The summit's orientation is dedicated to strengthening conflict prevention and resolution mechanisms across Africa, with a focus on investing in factors that contribute to lasting peace and security.

Why it mattersMoroccan Minister Bourita's call for prioritizing conflict prevention affects African nations by urging a focus on root causes and sustainable peace mechanisms.

Moroccan Navy Intercepts 201 Migrants Bound for Canary Islands

The Royal Navy intercepted a boat carrying 201 individuals, including women and minors, attempting to reach the Canary Islands.

Morocco's Royal Navy intercepted a pirogue carrying 201 would-be irregular migrants approximately 185 km southwest of Dakhla. The group was attempting to reach the Canary Islands when they were stopped on Saturday.

The rescued individuals, who included women and minors, were safely returned to the fishing village of Roc-Chico, located 190 km southwest of Dakhla. According to a statement from the General Staff of the Royal Armed Forces, they received necessary medical care on board before being handed over.

Following their return, the migrants were transferred to the Royal Gendarmerie to undergo standard administrative procedures. The operation highlights ongoing efforts to manage irregular migration routes from North Africa towards Europe.

Why it mattersThe interception affects potential migrants by preventing their journey and subjecting them to administrative procedures upon return.

Moroccan Gessime Scores Twice as Strasbourg Wins First Ligue 1 Match

Yassine Gessime scored two goals to secure Strasbourg's 2-1 comeback victory over Lens, marking their first win of the season.

Strasbourg secured their first Ligue 1 victory of the season with a 2-1 comeback win against Lens on Saturday. Moroccan international Yassine Gessime was the hero, scoring twice to overturn a first-half deficit. Lens had taken the lead in the 38th minute through a Florian Thauvin penalty.

Gessime equalized in the 48th minute and then netted the decisive goal in the 85th minute, completing the turnaround for Strasbourg. This victory is the first for the team under new coach Hugo Oliveira, following an opening day 4-0 defeat to Olympique de Marseille. The win places Strasbourg on three points after two matches.

The result leaves Lens also on three points, having started their season with a 5-2 victory over Auxerre. Strasbourg's next league match is an away fixture against Troyes on September 6, while Lens will host Lorient the following Saturday. Both teams have now played two games in the French league campaign.

Why it mattersMoroccan forward Yassine Gessime's performance directly impacts his team's standing and his personal profile in French football.
