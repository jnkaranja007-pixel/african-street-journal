Morocco budgets MAD 19.4 billion for 2027 subsidies

The government will continue to support essential goods like butane, sugar, and soft wheat flour.

Morocco plans to allocate MAD 19.4 billion for subsidies on butane gas, sugar, and soft wheat flour in 2027. This funding aims to maintain state support for essential household products amidst global market volatility and pressure on citizens' purchasing power. The subsidy report, part of the draft 2027 Finance Bill, details the government's intention to shield consumers from fluctuating international commodity prices.

The average subsidy for a 12-kilogram butane cylinder was MAD 61.79 between January and August 2026, with costs rising to approximately MAD 70 during Middle East conflict. Total butane subsidy expenditure reached an estimated MAD 9 billion by August 2026, a 4.95% increase from 2025. Sugar subsidies amounted to about MAD 2.7 billion for the first eight months of 2026, while support for soft wheat flour was nearly MAD 800 million.

Additional support was provided to road transport professionals, with MAD 2.488 billion mobilized between January and September 2026 to offset rising diesel prices. Subsidies for selected food products in Morocco’s southern provinces totaled MAD 64 million.

Why it mattersMoroccan households will continue to receive financial support for essential food and fuel products.

Morocco plans to double e-cigarette tax in 2027

New tax measures will also apply to refillable devices and components, expanding the definition of e-cigarettes.

Morocco plans to double the domestic consumption tax on electronic cigarettes from MAD 50 to MAD 100 per unit starting January 1, 2027. This 100% increase, part of the 2027 Finance Bill, will extend taxation to a broader range of vaping products, including refillable devices and their components. The legislation, Bill No. 60.26, redefines electronic cigarettes to cover rechargeable and refillable products, whether or not they contain nicotine, and includes devices and their separate components.

The proposed tax hike is expected to affect importers and distributors, who will need to adjust customs declarations and commercial practices. The extent of any retail price increase will depend on distributors’ profit margins and how much of the additional tax businesses pass on to consumers. This measure remains subject to parliamentary approval.

Conventional cigarette taxes will also rise gradually between 2027 and 2031. The specific tax per 1,000 cigarettes will increase from MAD 645 to MAD 1,053, while the proportional component rises from 57.5% to 61.5%. The government expects tobacco consumption tax revenue to reach MAD 19.32 billion in 2027, up from MAD 17.72 billion in 2026.

Why it mattersMoroccan importers, distributors, and consumers will face increased costs due to new taxes on e-cigarettes and conventional tobacco products.

Climate · 2026-10-26

Morocco targets 24 billion cubic meters dam storage by 2027

The country has completed eight major dams since 2020, adding 1.89 billion cubic meters of storage capacity.

Morocco aims to reach a total dam storage capacity of 24 billion cubic meters by the end of 2027, according to the draft 2027 Finance Bill. Since 2020, eight major dams have been finished, increasing storage by 1.89 billion cubic meters. Five more dams are already filling, adding 746 million cubic meters.

Construction is underway on 14 major dams and six medium-sized facilities, with plans for another major dam and an expansion in 2027. The 2022-2027 program for 155 small dams has completed 24 and has 32 under construction. Morocco also drilled 5,059 boreholes between 2020 and 2025 to assess groundwater.

Water transfer projects aim to secure supplies for Tangier and support Rabat and Casablanca. The government is also developing real-time water quality monitoring and reviewing dam water allocation regulations to enhance water security.

Why it mattersMorocco's water security plans will affect its citizens through improved drinking water supply and resource management.

Morocco U-17 women's team to play World Cup knockout match October 28 or 29

The Atlas Lionesses will compete in the round of 16 at the Mohammed VI Football Complex near Rabat.

Morocco's U-17 women's national team will play in the round of 16 of the 2026 FIFA U-17 Women’s World Cup on October 28 or 29. The matches will be held at the Mohammed VI Football Complex near Rabat, with games starting at 4 p.m. and 7 p.m. Moroccan time. The team's opponent, exact date, and kickoff time are still to be confirmed.

The Atlas Lionesses qualified for the knockout stage as one of the best third-placed teams, earning three points in Group A. They opened the tournament with a 4-0 win against New Zealand but lost their next two matches to Germany (4-2) and Argentina (5-1), conceding nine goals in those games. Fans can watch the matches for free on FIFA+ or on Arryadia via digital terrestrial television in Morocco.

The team must improve their defense after conceding nine goals in their final two group matches. The quarterfinals are scheduled for November 1, followed by the semifinals on November 4 and the final on November 7.

Why it mattersMorocco's U-17 women's national football team is competing in the knockout stages of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup.

Moroccan authorities search for five missing fishermen

Rescue operations continue off Safi coast for crew of Lalla Khadija, missing since Friday.

Moroccan authorities are searching for five fishermen whose boat, Lalla Khadija, went missing off the coast of Safi. The Ministry of Maritime Fisheries announced the ongoing search and rescue operations for the crew of the traditional fishing vessel, registered under number 7-7016.

The alert was raised on Friday, October 23, at 5 p.m. when the boat's owner reported its failure to return to Safi port. The Maritime Search and Rescue Coordination Center in Bouznika launched operations, involving two rescue boats from Safi and Agadir, fishing vessels, and merchant ships. Two helicopters from the Royal Gendarmerie and Royal Navy also joined the aerial search.

Difficult weather and sea conditions are hindering the search efforts. Civilian and military resources continue to operate under the coordination of the Bouznika rescue center to locate the missing vessel and its five crew members.

Why it mattersFive fishermen are missing at sea off Morocco's coast.
