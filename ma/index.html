Moroccan Municipalities Cut Spending to Fund Water Projects

Provincial authorities are directing municipalities to prioritize water supply and infrastructure in their 2027 budgets due to ongoing shortages.

Provincial authorities in four Moroccan regions are urging municipalities facing severe water shortages to cut nonessential spending and prioritize water supply projects as they prepare their 2027 budgets. Recommendations have been sent to municipal leaders in Casablanca-Settat, Rabat-Salé-Kénitra, Marrakech-Safi, and Beni Mellal-Khénifra. Municipalities are asked to assess projects against local water conditions and resident needs, directing available funding toward securing water supplies, maintaining distribution networks, and upgrading essential infrastructure.

This intervention comes as municipal committees review spending proposals ahead of October council sessions for the debate and approval of draft 2027 budgets. For municipalities with limited revenue, prioritizing water projects will require postponing other programs. Provincial authorities have recommended tighter scrutiny of spending on activities, equipment, and projects not directly connected to essential public services. Local councils are encouraged to tailor budgets to the severity of water stress, available resources, and existing infrastructure conditions.

Although Morocco’s reservoirs recovered significantly following improved rainfall in 2026, several areas continue to experience structural water pressure from years of drought, growing consumption, and uneven infrastructure. Higher national reservoir levels do not necessarily resolve local shortages or distribution problems. The recommendations could affect projects and recurring expenses previously included as routine, with deferrable programs potentially moved to later budget cycles to fund water-related repairs and interventions.

Why it mattersMunicipalities in four regions of Morocco are being directed to cut nonessential spending and reallocate funds to water supply and infrastructure projects due to ongoing water shortages.

Morocco extradites two Swedish drug suspects

Two Swedish men accused of leading large-scale drug trafficking operations have been extradited from Morocco following a new police cooperation agreement.

Morocco has extradited two Swedish nationals suspected of orchestrating major drug trafficking operations, marking the initial public outcome of a bilateral police accord. Swedish police stated Friday that the men are believed to have significantly impacted Sweden’s illicit drug market and will face judicial proceedings regarding detention requests.

One suspect, 28, allegedly managed a wholesale distribution network supplying drugs to prominent criminal organizations across Sweden and other Nordic nations. Swedish authorities connected this investigation to drug seizures in Uppsala, Stockholm, and Malmö during spring 2025, which included 94 kilograms of 3-CMC and 56 kilograms of amphetamine. The 28-year-old also faces accusations of attempted aggravated extortion.

The second extradited man, 42, reportedly held a senior role in a criminal network, facilitating the smuggling of substantial cannabis quantities into Sweden between 2020 and 2021. This extradition follows a police cooperation agreement signed in April by Sweden’s National Police Commissioner Petra Lundh and Morocco’s director general of national security, Abdellatif Hammouchi, which prioritizes action against suspects operating from Morocco.

Why it mattersTwo Swedish men accused of leading large-scale drug trafficking operations have been extradited from Morocco, impacting the flow of illegal drugs into Sweden.

Morocco Tourism Reaches 14.1 Million Arrivals, Sets August Record

The nation welcomed 14.1 million tourists by the end of August 2026, a 4.5% increase driven by a record-breaking August.

Morocco recorded 14.1 million tourist arrivals by the end of August 2026, an increase of 4.5% compared to the previous year, with 608,000 more visitors. This growth was significantly boosted by August, which saw over two million tourists for the first time, a 3% rise from the prior year. The Ministry of Tourism attributed this success to the summer season and sustained tourist activity.

Minister Fatim-Zahra Ammor stated that the 2023-2026 roadmap, which focused on targeted investments in key areas, has yielded positive results in both visitor numbers and socio-economic impact. She expressed optimism for the future, indicating that the next phase aims to further expand these gains and ensure broader benefit across more regions and tourism stakeholders.

The sustained level of tourist activity and the strong performance in August contributed to the overall increase in arrivals. The government's strategic roadmap appears to be driving tangible outcomes, with plans to extend these benefits to a wider geographical and economic base within the tourism sector.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the strong tourism growth, which indicates a successful strategy and potential for further economic expansion in the sector.

Morocco, Italy Sign Business Internationalization Pact

An agreement between the Moroccan Chamber of Trade in Italy and Iccrea Banca aims to boost trade and support businesses expanding between the two nations.

The Moroccan Chamber of Trade in Italy (CCMI) and Italian banking group Iccrea Banca have signed an agreement in Rome to strengthen bilateral trade and internationalize businesses. The pact, signed by CCMI President Marouan El Mansoub and Iccrea Banca's Massimo Meliconi, will support Italian companies entering the Moroccan market and facilitate commercial exchanges. It also aims to assist members of the Moroccan community in Italy with new entrepreneurial ventures between the two countries.

El Mansoub stated the agreement will significantly support Moroccans living in Italy, aiding new commercial projects and the development of existing activities in the Kingdom. He believes this initiative marks a new step in strengthening economic relations, fostering collaboration, investment, and development opportunities for businesses in both nations. Meliconi added that the agreement expands the tools available to Iccrea Banca's members for international business development.

The pact seeks to facilitate access to skills, networks, and business opportunities in Morocco, which is described as a dynamic and promising market for Italian businesses. The Kingdom offers significant potential in sectors like energy, infrastructure, manufacturing, agriculture, automotive, and tourism, owing to its economic stability, strategic location, and favorable investment environment.

Why it mattersMoroccan and Italian businesses will benefit from enhanced support for international expansion and commercial exchanges, fostering new investment and development opportunities.

Morocco's Envoy Attends Spain Protest Meeting After Ambassador Summoned

Spain's Foreign Ministry confirmed the chargé d'affaires attended a meeting on August 21 regarding remarks on Ceuta and Melilla.

Spain's Foreign Ministry confirmed that Morocco's chargé d'affaires attended a protest meeting on August 21, representing Ambassador Karima Benyaich. The ambassador had been summoned by Spain over remarks made by two Moroccan ministers concerning the Spanish cities of Ceuta and Melilla. The ministry stated that Benyaich was not in Spain at the time, leading the chargé d'affaires to attend in her stead. Spanish officials opted not to postpone the meeting due to the urgency of conveying Madrid's protest.

The diplomatic action followed statements by Moroccan Minister of Islamic Affairs Ahmed Toufiq and Justice Minister Abdellatif Ouahbi. Toufiq referenced historical scholars and a book during an event attended by King Mohammed VI, describing it as a reference for Moroccans liberating cities on the Atlantic coast. Ouahbi reiterated Morocco's claim of historical and geographical sovereignty over Ceuta and Melilla, a stance he has previously stated remains under discussion with Spanish officials.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez publicly rejected the positions expressed by the Moroccan ministers. Speaking before Spain's Congress, Sánchez announced that his government had summoned Morocco's ambassador on August 21 to communicate its disapproval, deeming the remarks about Ceuta and Melilla unacceptable to Spain. The ambassador was also informed of the complaint electronically.

Why it mattersMoroccan ministers' remarks on Ceuta and Melilla have led to diplomatic protests from Spain, affecting bilateral relations.
