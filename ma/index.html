Morocco Showcases Video Game Sector at Global Expo

Morocco is participating for the first time at Gamescom 2026 in Germany to promote its video game development studios and expertise on an international stage.

Morocco is officially showcasing its video game industry at Gamescom 2026 in Cologne, Germany, from August 26 to 30. The Moroccan Agency for Investment and Export Development (AMDIE), in partnership with the Ministry of Youth, Culture and Communication, is leading the nation's debut with a pavilion featuring 11 local video game development studios. This initiative aims to promote Moroccan know-how in the gaming sector and provide participating studios with a platform to connect with global industry players.

AMDIE stated that this participation represents a significant step in promoting Moroccan expertise internationally. The event offers a visibility platform within a major global market, facilitating connections for Moroccan studios with publishers, investors, and other key figures in the international video game industry. The agency seeks to foster new business and partnership opportunities, support the internationalization of Moroccan companies, and enhance their integration into global value chains.

Gamescom 2026 serves as an international showcase for Moroccan gaming talent and companies to present their creations and expertise. This presence aligns with Morocco's ongoing efforts to structure and develop its national video game ecosystem, building on events like the "Morocco Gaming Expo," which attracted over 80,000 visitors and 48 international investors in May. The expo highlighted Morocco's growing potential in the rapidly expanding gaming sector.

Why it mattersMoroccan video game studios are affected by gaining international visibility and potential partnerships at Gamescom 2026.

Moroccan Security Denies Cyberattack on Databases

Authorities state that recently published personal data originated from insurance and health coverage providers, not a security system breach.

Morocco's joint security directorates, the DGSN and DGST, on Thursday denied reports of a cyberattack targeting their information systems and security databases. The authorities stated that personal data recently circulated online was old information obtained from insurance companies and health coverage providers. They emphasized that their systems are protected by high cybersecurity standards and are not connected to the public internet.

The security bodies also warned against the circulation of fabricated images and altered documents falsely presented as belonging to Moroccan security services. They noted that some fake material displays non-existent uniforms and ranks, contains incorrect visual identities, and exhibits linguistic errors, indicating it did not originate from official sources.

Investigations are ongoing into the circulation of the data and fabricated material under the supervision of competent authorities. The DGSN-DGST pole reiterated that their security databases have not been compromised and that the claims of a breach are unfounded, attributing the leaked information to external, non-governmental sources.

Why it mattersInvestors may be concerned about the security of sensitive data circulating online, even if it does not originate from government systems.

Spain Finds No Evidence of Moroccan Role in Ceuta Migrant Surge

Justice Minister Félix Bolaños stated that Spain lacks evidence linking Morocco to the mass arrival of approximately 70,000 migrants in Ceuta on July 30-31, while affirming.

Spain's government announced Thursday that no evidence suggests Morocco orchestrated or encouraged the mass entry of migrants into Ceuta on July 30 and 31. Justice Minister Félix Bolaños told a parliamentary committee that Spain possessed no prior intelligence indicating the scale of the influx, which saw an estimated 70,000 people arrive. Bolaños rejected opposition claims of Moroccan involvement, attributing them to "lies" and urging respect for Rabat's cooperative relationship with Spain.

Bolaños emphasized that neither the Spanish nor Moroccan governments had discussed sovereignty over Ceuta and Melilla during recent bilateral meetings. He confirmed that Spain's national security reports had long identified potential large-scale migration movements toward Ceuta and Melilla, but stated that no previous event had reached the magnitude of the July incident. The minister defended the Spanish government's response, highlighting increased administrative presence and investments in the city.

Regarding migrant minors, Bolaños noted Morocco's willingness to cooperate on returns and family reunification, while stressing the need to adhere to Spanish legal safeguards. He criticized rhetoric linking the crisis to racism and xenophobia, describing Ceuta as a "laboratory" for hate speech. Spain's migration policy, he argued, achieves better results than other European nations due to cooperation with countries like Morocco, Mauritania, Senegal, Mali, and Algeria.

Why it mattersSpanish officials deny Moroccan involvement in a mass migrant influx, impacting bilateral relations and border management strategies.

Morocco braces for 46 C heat as Saharan thermal low persists

The General Directorate of Meteorology forecasts extreme heat in inland and southeastern regions, while coastal and mountain areas remain cooler.

Morocco is set to experience continued hot weather, with temperatures potentially reaching 46 degrees Celsius in certain areas, according to the General Directorate of Meteorology. Houssine Youabed, a communications official, stated that hot conditions will persist, especially in eastern, southeastern, and inland southern provinces. Maximum temperatures are predicted to range from 40 C to 46 C in these regions.

Temperatures will be significantly lower near the coast and in the Atlas Mountains, expected between 24 C and 28 C. Other areas, including the Saiss plain and Tadla plains, will see temperatures between 32 C and 38 C. Youabed noted that a Saharan thermal low is pushing hot air masses from desert areas, causing the temperature rise, particularly inland.

While these high temperatures are expected, Youabed clarified that it does not necessarily indicate a return of a nationwide heat wave but rather normal late-summer fluctuations. Localized thunderstorms are possible in mountainous and eastern regions due to cooler air at higher altitudes creating atmospheric instability.

Why it mattersFarmers and rural communities face direct impacts from extreme heat and potential water stress, while investors may see effects on energy demand and infrastructure.

Morocco Coach Travels to Europe for Player Assessments

Coach Mohamed Ouahbi will visit European clubs in early September to evaluate players ahead of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers.

Morocco's national football team coach, Mohamed Ouahbi, is planning a tour of several European countries starting the first week of September. This trip is part of his preparations for the upcoming 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifying matches against Gabon and Lesotho. Ouahbi aims to assess the physical and technical condition of European-based players, including those who recently changed clubs or are returning from injuries.

The coach intends to meet with key Moroccan internationals such as Nayef Aguerd and Abdessamad Ezzalzouli, whose fitness is crucial for the team. The tour will also focus on emerging talents like Ajax's Abdullah Ouazane and Schalke's Soufiane Farsi, who have impressed in the new season. These meetings are designed to provide Ouahbi with an up-to-date evaluation before he finalizes his squad for the qualifiers.

Morocco is scheduled to play Gabon on September 25 in Rabat and then travel to face Lesotho on September 29 in Bloemfontein, South Africa. These matches are the opening fixtures for the 2027 AFCON qualifiers. The Confederation of African Football has appointed referees for both games, with South African Abongile Tom officiating the match against Gabon and Cameroonian Abdou Abdel Mvouta overseeing the game against Lesotho.

Why it mattersMoroccan football players based in Europe, particularly those in new clubs or returning from injury, will be evaluated by their national coach for upcoming AFCON qualifiers.
