Morocco risks losing EU aid for electric cars made by Renault and Stellantis

Three European Parliament committees are due to vote on a text defining the rules on December 1.

Electric cars made in Morocco by Renault and Stellantis could lose European Union aid, according to TelQuel. The report says the car industry would bear the collateral damage of a European effort to protect its market from Chinese competition.

Three committees of the European Parliament are due to vote on a text that defines which vehicles qualify for the aid. The report says Morocco risks being left outside the European door even though the cars are built inside the kingdom.

The vote on the text is scheduled for December 1, the date the report gives for the committees' decision on the rules.

Wafa Assurance acquires La Marocaine Vie from Saham Group

The deal includes a long-term bancassurance partnership allowing Saham Bank to continue offering insurance products through its network.

Wafa Assurance has signed an agreement to acquire 99.98% of the capital of La Marocaine Vie from the Saham Group. This transaction aims to strengthen Wafa Assurance's position in life insurance, savings, and retirement services within the Moroccan market.

In 2025, Wafa Assurance reported 15.2 billion dirhams in gross consolidated premiums, while La Marocaine Vie recorded 2.5 billion dirhams. Saham Bank, which acquired its stake in La Marocaine Vie following the purchase of Société Générale Marocaine de Banques in December 2024, will now focus on its core banking operations.

The agreement includes a long-term commercial partnership between Wafa Assurance and Saham Bank. This arrangement ensures that Saham Bank clients can continue to access insurance solutions through their existing bank branches while the two companies develop new joint offerings.

Why it mattersThe acquisition and new bancassurance partnership will change how Saham Bank clients access insurance products and how the two insurers operate in the market.

Colas Rail wins €29 million Moroccan rail contract

The French firm will carry out track, catenary and civil engineering works in the Casablanca area for the Kenitra–Marrakesh high-speed line.

Morocco's National Railways Office (ONCF) has awarded French firm Colas Rail a 29 million euro contract for the Kenitra–Marrakesh high-speed rail project. The work covers track, catenary and civil engineering between Roches-Noires station and the Nouaceur Technicentre in the Casablanca area.

Hervé Le Joliff, Colas Rail's executive vice president for railways, said Morocco is a strategic market and that the contract reaffirms the company's commitment to high-speed rail in the Kingdom. Colas Rail has operated in Morocco for more than 17 years and employs around 550 staff.

The company is already working on the same line under two contracts secured in January 2025, worth 250 million euros in total. Its Moroccan teams have carried out more than 40 projects across the country, including several modernisation programmes on the rail network.

Why it mattersThe contract brings new rail construction work to Casablanca and adds to the 550 Colas Rail staff already employed in Morocco.

Casablanca police arrest 15-year-old over false school bomb threat

Police say the teenager made two anonymous calls on Friday falsely threatening a car bomb outside a Casablanca school.

Moroccan police arrested a 15-year-old student in Casablanca on Friday on suspicion of making a false report of a terrorist threat against schools in the city, the General Directorate of National Security (DGSN) said. The arrest was carried out by the Casablanca Judicial Police Brigade with the General Directorate for Territorial Surveillance (DGST) on November 13.

A school in Casablanca received two calls on Friday morning from an unidentified number, in which the caller threatened to detonate a car bomb in front of the building. Security services activated emergency safety procedures for serious threats. Investigators later established the threats were fabricated and that a minor was allegedly responsible.

The 15-year-old has been placed under judicial investigation under the supervision of the public prosecutor's office to establish the motives behind the incident. Police are still searching for a second minor suspected of involvement in the calls, which targeted several educational institutions in Casablanca.

Why it mattersSchools in Casablanca that received the threats must now deal with the aftermath of the emergency alerts, while the two suspects face a judicial inquiry.

Princess Lalla Oum Kalthoum oversees mental health agreement

A new partnership aims to boost mental health care in the Rabat-Salé-Kenitra region.

Princess Lalla Oumkeltoum visited the new 120-bed psychiatric hospital in Kenitra on Friday to enhance mental health services for the Rabat-Salé-Kenitra region. Her Royal Highness, president of the Lalla Oumkeltoum Foundation for Mental Health, toured various departments including the outpatient clinic, day hospital for the elderly, and addiction treatment unit. She learned about the operations and patient care at these facilities.

During the visit, Princess Lalla Oumkeltoum chaired a ceremony where a framework partnership agreement was signed between the Ministry of Health and Social Protection and the Lalla Oumkeltoum Foundation for Mental Health. This agreement supports the implementation of the national “Mental Health Plan 2030” and the Foundation’s own strategic action plan.

The Lalla Oumkeltoum Foundation is dedicated to promoting mental health for vulnerable individuals through awareness, prevention, and support initiatives. The partnership aims to assist people with mental disorders and their families, aligning with the foundation's statutes and the High Royal Care for mental well-being.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Health and Social Protection and the Lalla Oumkeltoum Foundation for Mental Health signed a partnership agreement to implement the "Mental Health Plan 2030".
