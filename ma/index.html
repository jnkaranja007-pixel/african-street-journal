Morocco raises transport fuel subsidies by 60%

Government allocates MAD 400 million more to prevent rising costs from affecting consumer prices.

Morocco has increased exceptional fuel subsidies for road transport operators by 60%, allocating an additional MAD 400 million. Transport Minister Abdessamad Kayouh announced on Monday that the government has distributed over MAD 11 billion in subsidies since March 2022. Freight operators received MAD 6.9 billion, or 62% of the total support, with taxis receiving 26%.

These new payments raise fortnightly subsidies for small taxis from MAD 800 to MAD 1,280 and for grand taxis from MAD 1,100 to MAD 1,760. Intercity bus operators will now receive MAD 5,600, up from MAD 3,500, while urban buses get MAD 4,960. Freight truck payments are now between MAD 2,080 and MAD 3,360 based on vehicle size.

The government approved the extra funding on November 3 to shield households from higher transport costs, particularly impacting fruit and vegetable prices. The ministry has also processed over 80% of approximately 5,500 complaints regarding subsidy applications and payments.

Morocco hosts Israeli defense delegation

Officials discuss defense cooperation, military equipment, and cybersecurity during a visit by Israeli Defense Ministry Director General Amir Baram.

Amir Baram, Director General of the Israeli Ministry of Defense, visited Morocco on Monday leading a large delegation for official talks. Abdeltif Loudyi, Morocco's Minister Delegate for National Defense Administration, met Baram at the administration's headquarters. Lieutenant General Mohammed Berrid, Inspector General of the FAR, also met Baram at the General Staff Headquarters of the Royal Armed Forces.

During the meetings, officials expressed satisfaction with current defense cooperation, which includes information protection, cybersecurity, and training. They highlighted progress in acquiring military equipment and technologies, and in the defense industry. Discussions also focused on strengthening the defense industrial base and developing cybersecurity capabilities.

Both sides reaffirmed their willingness to enhance bilateral cooperation through action plans from the Joint Military Commission. This commission serves as a mechanism for exchange, coordination, and exploring new cooperation avenues.

Moroccan parties reject Spanish World Cup final threats

Political figures in Morocco criticized Spanish opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo's demands regarding the 2030 World Cup final.

Moroccan political parties have condemned threats by Spanish opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo to withdraw Spain from co-hosting the 2030 FIFA World Cup if the final is not awarded to Spain. Several Moroccan figures called for the joint tournament, hosted by Morocco, Spain, and Portugal, to remain free from political disputes, especially with Spain's early elections scheduled for November 29.

Jamal El Asri of the Unified Socialist Party labeled Feijóo’s statements as electioneering, warning that such remarks could escalate tensions before FIFA's decision. He also criticized Moroccan Finance Minister Fouzi Lekjaa's premature declaration about the final being held in Benslimane. Ilham Belfhili of the Moroccan Union for Democracy stated that the final venue is a sporting matter for FIFA, not an electoral issue for Spanish politicians, asserting Morocco's readiness to host even if Spain and Portugal withdrew.

Mohamed Nabil Benabdallah of the Progress and Socialism Party also viewed the Spanish right’s comments as campaign tactics. The Popular Movement Party stated that all three hosts have the right to compete for the final, emphasizing Morocco's equal partnership and capability. Morocco, Spain, and Portugal are jointly bidding to host the 2030 World Cup.

Sánchez rules out Ceuta, Melilla talks with Morocco

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez reaffirmed support for Moroccan autonomy in the Sahara dispute.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez stated on Sunday, November 8, that sovereignty over Ceuta and Melilla would not be discussed with Morocco. Sánchez described territorial integrity as a red line for his government, emphasizing that the status of the two cities has never been a subject of debate in bilateral relations.

Despite ruling out sovereignty talks, Sánchez defended cooperation with Rabat, citing shared economic, cultural, and security interests, especially in controlling irregular migration. He acknowledged that managing the Ceuta border would be more difficult without Moroccan cooperation, even with increased Spanish investment in border security over the past eight years.

Sánchez also defended Spain's 2022 decision to support Morocco's autonomy initiative for the Sahara, stating it was a political conviction and not a bargain. He noted that five decades of deadlock had led other countries to reconsider their positions, making autonomy under Moroccan sovereignty a more viable settlement. Spain remains a major humanitarian donor to Sahrawi refugee camps.

Why it mattersMoroccans and Sahrawis are directly affected by Spain's stance on the Sahara's autonomy and the management of its borders with Ceuta.

Morocco wins Arab beach soccer cup for first time

The Moroccan national team defeated Mauritania 6-5 in a dramatic final match held in Port-Said, Egypt.

Morocco's national beach soccer team secured their first-ever Arab Cup title on Monday, defeating Mauritania 6-5 in the final. The match took place in Port-Saïd, Egypt.

Morocco trailed 4-2 during the game but staged a remarkable comeback to clinch the victory. This win marks a significant achievement for the team in the history of the competition.

The team's historic win brings the cup to Morocco for the first time. Further details on the tournament's reception in Morocco were not provided in the report.

Why it mattersMoroccan athletes have won a major regional sporting competition.
