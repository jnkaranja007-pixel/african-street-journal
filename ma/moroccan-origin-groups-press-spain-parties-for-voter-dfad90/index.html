Moroccan-origin groups press Spain parties for voter representation

The campaign comes as Vox calls for a review of naturalizations involving more than 300,000 Spanish citizens of Moroccan origin.

Source: Hespress English · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Moroccan-origin associations in Spain are urging parties to address their demands for political representation before the November 29 general election. On November 24, the Islamic Commission of Spain called on eligible Muslim voters to exercise their electoral rights and take part in decisions affecting Spanish society.

Commission president Aiman Adlbi said parliamentary decisions on government formation and public institutions affect Muslim citizens. Casa Marruecos held a gathering on November 15 in Quart de Poblet, near Valencia, to discuss racism, discrimination and political participation. Its vice-president, Boutaina El Hadri, said parties could no longer count on the community’s support without responding to its demands. She estimated that about 300,000 Spanish citizens of Moroccan origin were eligible to vote; the figure is not a confirmed electoral count.

The Socialist Party held an event for immigrant communities on November 24. El País reported that Vox wants to review naturalizations involving more than 300,000 Moroccan-origin citizens, but its report said the party’s programme does not establish wrongdoing in those cases.

Why it mattersEligible Moroccan-origin Spanish citizens are being urged to vote, while Vox’s proposed review concerns naturalizations involving more than 300,000 people.
