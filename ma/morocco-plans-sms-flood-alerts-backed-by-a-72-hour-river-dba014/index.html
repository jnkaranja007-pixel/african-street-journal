Morocco plans SMS flood alerts backed by a 72-hour river forecast

The early-warning system would use rainfall and river-level readings to identify threatened communities before water rises.

Source: Hespress English · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Morocco is preparing a national early-warning system that would send SMS alerts to residents in flood-prone areas, using forecasts intended to flag dangerous river surges up to 72 hours ahead. Equipment and Water Minister Nizar Baraka set out the measures before the House of Councillors on November 17, which was the date the ministry was scheduled to brief parliament.

The service, coordinated with the General Directorate of Meteorology, the Interior Ministry and disaster-risk authorities, would combine rainfall measurements with river-level monitoring. Local officials could then prepare evacuations, close vulnerable roads and warn residents. Baraka said the three-day horizon does not guarantee that every flash flood can be predicted.

Baraka said the government was updating its national flood-risk atlas and working to extend forecasting to 10 days. The plans follow February flooding that inundated more than 110,000 hectares and forced nearly 188,000 people from their homes across Larache, Kénitra, Sidi Kacem and Sidi Slimane.

Why it mattersLocal officials in Larache, Kénitra, Sidi Kacem and Sidi Slimane would gain warning time to move people and close roads before rivers rise.
