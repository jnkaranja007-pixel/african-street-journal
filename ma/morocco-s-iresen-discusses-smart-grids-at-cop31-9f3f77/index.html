Morocco's IRESEN discusses smart grids at COP31

Morocco's Research Institute for Solar Energy and New Energies (IRESEN) highlighted challenges in adapting power grids for renewable energy at COP31 in Antalya.

Source: Hespress English · 2026-11-11 · 1 min read

Morocco's Research Institute for Solar Energy and New Energies (IRESEN) organized a side event at COP31 in Antalya on Tuesday to discuss adapting electrical networks for renewable energy integration. Speakers addressed challenges in transforming MENA region electricity systems due to decarbonization and the progress of solar and wind power, noting their fluctuating nature complicates grid stability. The event focused on the capacity of existing infrastructure to handle increasing renewable energy production while ensuring a continuous supply.

IRESEN Director General Samir Rachidi stated Morocco aims for 52% of its installed electrical capacity to be renewable by 2030, potentially reaching 60%. He emphasized the need for systems managing high solar production and low availability periods through storage solutions like batteries and pumped hydropower. Rachidi also highlighted the role of energy consumption digitalization in modulating usage and increasing demand flexibility.

The discussions underscored the shift from traditional electricity management to smarter, more flexible, and decentralized systems, particularly through Smart Grids development. This involves mobilizing digital technologies and automation to optimize electricity production, distribution, and consumption. IRESEN also aims to connect scientific research with economic and industrial actors for practical applications.

Why it mattersMorocco is working to increase its renewable energy capacity to 52% by 2030, requiring significant adaptation of its electrical grids.
