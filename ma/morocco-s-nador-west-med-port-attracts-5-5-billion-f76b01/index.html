Morocco's Nador West Med port attracts $5.5 billion investment

The Nador West Med complex aims to become a key maritime, industrial, and energy hub connecting Europe and Africa.

Sources: Hespress English and TelQuel · 2026-11-01 · 1 min read

Morocco's Nador West Med port complex has secured 54 billion dirhams ($5.5 billion) in public and private investment. Forbes reported this investment strengthens the country's goal to become a major maritime, industrial, and energy hub linking Europe and Africa. The first phase of its main container terminal is nearing operation, reflecting King Mohammed VI’s infrastructure strategy.

The port is designed to expand Morocco’s international trade role and benefit from global supply chain shifts. Its energy infrastructure includes a hydrocarbon terminal for 25 million tonnes annually and a planned liquefied natural gas terminal to process five billion cubic meters yearly. These facilities could bolster Morocco's energy security and industrial growth.

Forbes stated the port complex could reinforce Morocco's position as a link between European energy demand and African markets. The project is also expected to stimulate economic activity in Morocco’s eastern region through industrial investment and logistics services. Crown Prince Moulay El Hassan launched operations at the first container terminal on Saturday, October 31.
