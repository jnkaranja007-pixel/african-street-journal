Namibia's mines chamber expects uranium levy deal early in 2027

Chief executive Fabian Shaanika said talks with the finance ministry on proposed sliding-scale export levies have been constructive.

Sources: The Windhoek Observer and Namibia Economist · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Namibia's Chamber of Mines expects to reach an agreement with the finance ministry early in 2027 on proposed sliding-scale export levies for uranium, chief executive Fabian Shaanika said. He said he remains optimistic about the industry's prospects next year despite the levy proposal and geopolitical uncertainties.

Shaanika said in a November update that engagements with the ministry had been constructive. The proposed levy revisions were among the policy and regulatory issues that shaped his first six months leading the chamber, which represents the mining industry.

Uranium output in Namibia rose 20.8% in 2025, according to the chamber, driven by strong global nuclear demand and higher production at the Husab, Rössing and Langer Heinrich mines in the Erongo region. The country already applies a 0.25% export levy on uranium oxide.
