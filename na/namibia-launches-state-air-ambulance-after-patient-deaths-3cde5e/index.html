Namibia launches state air ambulance after patient deaths

The new service uses a Learjet and helicopters to speed up emergency medical transfers.

Source: New Era Live · 2026-11-06 · 1 min read

Namibia launched its first state-owned air ambulance service in Windhoek on Wednesday, following the deaths of patients who could not reach specialized hospitals in time. Health minister Esperance Luvindao said the service aims to reduce travel time for critically ill and injured patients, especially in remote areas.

Luvindao recounted the loss of a 12-year-old boy from Katima Mulilo and other patients after an April 2026 road accident near Otjiwarongo as key motivators. Vice President Lucia Witbooi stated the service ensures survival is not determined by location, addressing challenges from Namibia's vast geography.

The service includes a Learjet equipped for critical care, with Government Air Transport Services providing aircraft and pilots. Helicopter evacuations will also be available through cooperation with the Namibian Police and Defence Forces, ensuring specialized medical personnel accompany patients to national referral hospitals.
