US Democrats' Congress takeover puts Namibia's minerals and AGOA in focus

Democratic control of both US chambers could reshape Washington's trade and investment priorities toward Africa, including Namibia's mineral wealth.

Source: The Windhoek Observer · 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The Democratic Party's takeover of both chambers of the United States Congress in the November 3 midterm elections could change Washington's trade, investment and development priorities toward Africa. The shift also limits President Donald Trump's ability to advance his legislative agenda during the remaining two years of his presidency. For Namibia, the changes could affect its critical minerals, its emerging energy industry and its access to the American market, including the African Growth and Opportunity Act (AGOA).

Opposition Member of Parliament Veraruhe Kandorozu said the Democratic resurgence could open room for renewed American engagement with Africa in trade, development and democratic governance. He said African economies have benefited from American engagement through AGOA, development assistance and trade, and that some of those gains faced setbacks under President Donald Trump and the Republican administration.

US Ambassador to Namibia John Giordano has been encouraging American investors to look at the country's mineral potential, especially uranium and rare earth elements. In April 2026 he told Bloomberg that uranium is the big prize for both countries, linking US interest to rising nuclear power demand, including from artificial intelligence data centres. At an August reception ahead of the Namibia Mining Expo, he said the US wants a full value-chain approach that extends investment beyond extraction into refining, processing, manufacturing and recycling.

Why it mattersNamibian mining and energy firms seeking American capital, and exporters relying on AGOA access, face possible changes in US policy under the new Congress.
