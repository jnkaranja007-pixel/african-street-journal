Bank of Namibia buys 425kg of gold

The central bank aims to acquire 600kg to bolster national financial resilience and diversify reserves.

Source: The Windhoek Observer · 2026-10-31 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The Bank of Namibia has purchased 425kg of gold from local mines as part of its gold accumulation programme, aiming for a total of 600kg. This initiative, started last year, strengthens the country's financial resilience and diversifies its international reserves. Deputy governor Nicholas Mukasa stated that these gold purchases have significantly contributed to the recent increase in international reserves.

Namibia's international reserves grew from N$58 billion to N$59.3 billion between the end of July and September. These reserves currently provide 3.6 months of import cover, which is adequate for currency peg support and international financial obligations. Gold offers an additional layer of protection against geopolitical tensions and volatile financial markets, acting as a store of value.

Gold complements the central bank's traditional reserve assets because its value does not necessarily move in the same direction as other holdings. This diversification strategy is also being pursued by countries like South Africa and Ghana, which use locally produced commodities to enhance national financial security. China and Poland have also expanded their gold holdings for protection against external shocks.

Why it mattersNamibia's financial reserves are being strengthened by acquiring gold, offering a buffer against global economic instability.
