Koryx values Namibia’s Haib copper project at US$2.5 billion

The pre-feasibility study projects 130 000 tonnes of payable copper a year in the mine’s first 10 years and targets early construction works in 2027.

Source: The Windhoek Observer · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Koryx Copper valued its proposed Haib copper project in southern Namibia at US$2.5 billion in after-tax net present value in a pre-feasibility study. The study projects average annual payable copper production of 130 000 tonnes in the first 10 years of operation, falling to 105 000 tonnes over the mine’s life.

The study estimates a 24-year mine life and upfront capital expenditure of US$2.06 billion. It puts the after-tax internal rate of return at 24.9%, based on a copper price assumption of US$5 per pound. Haib, in the //Karas region near the South African border, is also expected to produce gold and molybdenum.

Koryx is targeting completion of a definitive feasibility study in 2027, followed by early construction works towards the end of that year. The proposed operation would use open-pit mining and a processing plant designed to handle 48 million tonnes of ore annually.

Why it mattersKoryx must complete a definitive feasibility study in 2027 before it can proceed with planned early construction works towards the end of that year.
