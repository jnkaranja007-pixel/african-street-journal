Namibia president flags engineer shortage

President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah says Namibia needs more qualified technical professionals to build and run infrastructure.

Sources: The Brief and Namibia Economist · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Namibia's public service is losing engineers faster than it can keep them, President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah warned at the inaugural Namibia Engineering Excellence Awards in Windhoek. “The shortage and high turnover of engineering professionals in Government must concern us all,” she said.

Her concern is that building roads and buildings is not enough; the state also needs engineers to plan, regulate, maintain and manage national assets. She said engineering expertise would be critical to the Sixth National Development Plan (NDP6), especially in infrastructure, industrialisation, housing, agriculture and manufacturing. Priorities, she added, cannot be met by policy statements alone.

She urged government, universities, technical institutions, professional bodies and private firms to work together on engineering skills. Engineering students need more scholarships, bursaries, internships, apprenticeships and mentorship, she said, and mining, energy, construction and transport companies should invest more in engineering education. She also proposed exploring a National Engineering Education and Innovation Fund, backed by public and private contributions, to help students and give young engineers loan-based seed capital to commercialise local technologies.
