Meatco proposes three-month wage cuts as exports remain suspended

The state-owned processor says a 50% reduction in working hours from January would save about N$16.7 million, while December pay also faces cuts.

Source: The Windhoek Observer · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Namibia’s state-owned Meat Corporation of Namibia (Meatco) has proposed cutting ordinary working hours and basic pay by 50% for three months from 1 January, as suspended beef exports and weaker domestic demand squeeze revenue. The company announced the measures on Tuesday after consulting the Namibia Food and Allied Workers Union (NAFAU).

Employees without enough accrued annual leave would receive two-thirds of their usual December remuneration; those with sufficient leave would be paid in full using their leave. Meatco said November salaries would be paid in full. It estimates the proposed January-to-March cuts would save about N$16.7 million against a monthly wage bill above N$15.5 million.

Meatco plans to close its offices and production facilities from 4 December to 4 January. The company has also proposed deferring December’s 13th cheques until operations and cash flow normalise, while preserving employees’ entitlement subject to a formal union agreement. NAFAU secretary general Jacob Penda said the union would agree only to terms favourable to employees under their contracts.

Why it mattersMeatco employees face two-thirds December pay unless they have enough leave, while the union must negotiate any deferred 13th cheques and three-month cuts.
