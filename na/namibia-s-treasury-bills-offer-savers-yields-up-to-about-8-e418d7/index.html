Namibia’s treasury bills offer savers yields up to about 8.01%

The government securities mature in 91 to 365 days, and individuals pay no tax on their interest.

Sources: The Brief and Namibia Economist · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Namibian savers can lend to the government by buying Treasury bills, short-term securities sold at a discount and repaid at face value on a fixed date. Bills are offered for 91, 182, 273 or 365 days through regular auctions run by the Bank of Namibia.

At the central bank’s 5 November 2026 auction, reported average yields ranged from about 7.75% on 91-day bills to about 8.01% on the longest term. Interest on government bills is exempt from tax for individuals, according to Abraham Eita’s advisory article in The Brief.

Investors can sell bills before maturity on the secondary market, but a sale at market-related rates can bring a capital loss if interest rates have risen since purchase. Eita says the fixed repayment date can also help savers plan for costs such as school fees or emergencies.

Why it mattersIndividual savers receive tax-exempt interest and can choose a repayment term from 91 to 365 days.
