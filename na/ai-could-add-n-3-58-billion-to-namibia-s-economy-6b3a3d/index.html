AI could add N$3.58 billion to Namibia's economy

A new study ranks Namibia 36th out of 56 Commonwealth nations in AI readiness, citing infrastructure and youth unemployment.

Source: The Brief · 2026-11-06 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Artificial intelligence could boost Namibia's economy by N$3.58 billion over the next decade, according to a 2026 Commonwealth Youth AI Readiness Index study. The Ayre Think Tank report estimates potential GDP gains between N$1.73 billion and N$5.43 billion, with a moderate scenario projecting N$3.58 billion. This study was released on Thursday during the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda.

The report places Namibia 36th among 56 Commonwealth countries, scoring 42.7 out of 100, below the study's benchmark of 53. High youth unemployment, with 47% of young people in the labour force jobless, and limited electricity access reaching 59.3% of the population, are identified as major challenges. Inadequate digital infrastructure, including slow broadband and internet disruptions, further restricts the country's ability to benefit from AI.

Namibia has approximately 800,000 young people aged 15 to 29 who could benefit from AI-related opportunities. However, the index gave Namibia a low score of 3.1 out of 10 for decent work, enterprise, and transitions. Researchers noted gaps in digital education, with computing not recorded in the curriculum, and insufficient comparable evidence of digital and AI skills among young Namibians.

Why it mattersYoung Namibians face significant barriers to participating in an AI-driven economy due to high unemployment and inadequate digital and educational infrastructure.
