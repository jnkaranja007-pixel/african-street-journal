Namibia agriculture grows 17.8% as construction contracts

The farm sector added 0.8 percentage points to second-quarter growth, while construction fell 15.5%, according to the statistics agency.

Source: The Brief · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Namibia’s economy grew 4.8% in the second quarter of 2026, as agriculture expanded 17.8% and construction contracted 15.5%, according to the Namibia Statistics Agency. Agriculture’s growth followed good rainfall and contributed 0.8 percentage points to the national result.

Agriculture, forestry and fishing accounted for about 23% of employed people in the 2018 Labour Force Survey, while the farm sector averaged 7.9% of gross domestic product over the past decade, figures cited by the Namibia Investment Promotion and Development Board show. But agriculture had declined 2.7% in the same quarter of 2025, illustrating how output moves with rainfall.

The report argues that dams, irrigation schemes, storage facilities and feeder roads are among the infrastructure needed to make farming more productive and stable; construction supplies those projects.

Why it mattersFarm workers and rural households are exposed to swings in rainfall as agriculture’s output reversed from a 2.7% decline in the same quarter of 2025 to 17.8% growth.
