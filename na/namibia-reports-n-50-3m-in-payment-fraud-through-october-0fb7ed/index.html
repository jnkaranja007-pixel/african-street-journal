Namibia reports N$50.3m in payment fraud through October

The Bank of Namibia says card, electronic funds transfer and electronic-money channels recorded the losses, with EFT fraud driving a first-half increase.

Source: The Brief · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Namibia recorded N$50.3 million in reported fraud involving card, electronic funds transfer (EFT) and electronic-money payments from January to October 2026, Bank of Namibia Deputy Governor Leonie Dunn said at a Windhoek summit on Tuesday.

Financial institutions recovered about N$15.6 million in the first six months, equal to 48% of reported fraud value. EFT fraud rose 47.3% to N$21.9 million from N$14.8 million in the same period of 2025, while card fraud fell 10.8% to N$10.4 million and electronic-money fraud dropped 72.6% to about N$229,000. Dunn said victims often authorised EFT transfers after being manipulated, making recovery harder; no electronic-money fraud losses were recovered.

The central bank gazetted revised cybersecurity rules for banks and the national payment system in June and July. It is also working with the financial services industry to revise fraud rules, while a sector council is developing an incident response plan and information-sharing platform.

Why it mattersFinancial institutions recovered only 48% of reported fraud value in the first six months, leaving card, EFT and electronic-money users and businesses exposed to unrecovered losses.
