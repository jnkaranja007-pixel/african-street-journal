FNB Namibia urges stronger digital safeguards as 72% of transactions go online

The bank says more than 92% of its customers are digitally enabled, and an industry writer warns that fraudsters are exploiting that convenience.

Source: The Brief · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

FNB Namibia says more than 72% of its financial transactions now take place through digital channels, and more than 92% of its customers are digitally enabled. In an article for The Brief, the bank's Amoré Nicholson argues that security must be designed around how people now use digital services.

Nicholson says fraudsters phone customers posing as their bank and use urgent warnings about missing money to pressure them into hurried decisions. Artificial intelligence (AI), which can reproduce voices and write convincing messages, makes traditional checks such as spotting spelling mistakes less reliable.

The article cites INTERPOL, the international police organisation, whose African Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2026 says AI enables 55% of reported cybercrime across Africa. INTERPOL's 2026 Global Financial Fraud Threat Assessment estimates AI-assisted fraud is 4.5 times more profitable than conventional methods.

Why it mattersBanks must now design security around distracted customers, because fraudsters can use AI to target far more people at low cost.
