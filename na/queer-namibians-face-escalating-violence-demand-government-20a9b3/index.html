Queer Namibians Face Escalating Violence, Demand Government Protection

A former Mr. Gay Namibia contestant writes from abroad, detailing a wave of hate crimes and urging lawmakers to enact hate crime legislation.

Queer Namibians are facing escalating targeted hate and deadly violence, prompting calls for government protection and comprehensive hate crime legislation. Maria Del Rosario, formerly Wendelinus and the first Mr. Gay Namibia, wrote from abroad detailing the brutal attacks, including the rape and murder of Christof “Sexy” Frederik and the assault of Brian Araeb. At least six queer individuals have died in a recent wave of violence, which Rosario attributes to political and religious rhetoric.

Rosario, a transgender woman, implores all Namibians to stand up and demand that the government fulfill its constitutional duty to protect all citizens. She highlights Article 8 of the Namibian Constitution, which mandates safeguarding life and dignity. The plea is for the justice ministry and parliament to introduce legislation that imposes aggravated sentences for crimes motivated by prejudice based on sexual orientation and gender identity.

The letter serves as a stark warning to the queer community within Namibia, particularly young transgender girls and gay boys, to prioritize safety while navigating their identities. Rosario advises moving in groups, not trusting strangers blindly, and watching out for one another. She emphasizes that queer individuals should not have to live in fear or hide in their own country, demanding that Namibia become a safe place for all its children.

Why it mattersQueer Namibians are directly affected by escalating violence and a lack of legal protection, leading to fear for their safety and lives.
