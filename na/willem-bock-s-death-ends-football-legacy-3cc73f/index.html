Willem Bock's Death Ends Football Legacy

The former Orlando Pirates defender, known for his financial support and activism, died at 66 after a battle with diabetes.

Willem Bock, a former fullback for the Orlando Pirates and a staunch supporter of the club, has died at the age of 66. Bock, who was born in 1960 at Otjonjaukari farm in the Omaheke region, passed away after a prolonged illness with diabetes. He was one of 15 siblings and his family later relocated to Windhoek. Bock was remembered as a kind-hearted and generous individual who often provided financial assistance to the cash-strapped club.

Bock's footballing talent was evident from a young age, with his nephews also becoming notable players. He was part of the initial group of young Namibians selected to represent the country internationally at a youth level following Namibia's independence in 1990. His early education began at Augeikhas Primary School in Windhoek, and he attended several other schools before being expelled from Augustineum for his anti-apartheid activism.

Messages of condolence have been pouring in for the departed defender, affectionately known as 'Boeta Wokes'. He is survived by his parents, Piet and Frieda Bock, and his siblings. Bock's legacy extends beyond the football pitch, as he was also recognized for his socialite nature and willingness to engage with those around him, including strangers. His passing marks the end of a significant chapter for the Orlando Pirates community.
