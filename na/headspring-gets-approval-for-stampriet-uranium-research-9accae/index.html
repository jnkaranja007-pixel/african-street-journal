Headspring gets approval for Stampriet uranium research boreholes

The company says it will use no chemical reagents in the research phase and plans to submit a revised pilot-testing application.

Source: The Namibian · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Headspring Investments said on 3 November it had received authorisation to drill additional research boreholes for its proposed Wings Uranium Project in Namibia’s Stampriet Artesian Basin.

The company says the research phase will use no chemical reagents, but the authorisation does not permit chemical leaching or establish that the mining method is safe for groundwater. The Stampriet Aquifer Uranium Mining Association (Sauma), which is calling for clarity, wants details of what was approved, the conditions attached and the evidence supporting it.

Headspring says it will submit a revised application for pilot testing. Communities in south-eastern Namibia rely on the basin for drinking water, livestock and agriculture, with alternatives extremely limited; the aquifer system is shared with Botswana and South Africa.

Why it mattersSouth-eastern communities dependent on the basin for drinking water, livestock and agriculture need clarity on the borehole approval’s conditions and supporting evidence.
