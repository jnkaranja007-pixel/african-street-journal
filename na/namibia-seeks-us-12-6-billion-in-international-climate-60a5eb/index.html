Namibia seeks US$12.6 billion in international climate funding by 2035

National planning chief Kaire Mbuende says the country needs US$14 billion to meet its climate commitments and cut emissions.

Sources: The Brief and Namibia Economist · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Namibia needs US$14 billion by 2035 to meet its climate commitments, with about 90% expected from international support, National Planning Secretariat Director General Kaire Mbuende said at the United Nations Climate Change Conference in Antalya, Türkiye, on Wednesday.

The funding is part of Namibia’s Third Nationally Determined Contribution, which targets cuts of about 23 million tonnes of carbon dioxide-equivalent emissions and stronger resilience to climate change. Mbuende said US$12.6 billion would need to come from abroad, while Namibia expects to raise US$1.4 billion domestically.

He urged conference participants to provide adaptation finance mainly as grants and highly concessional funding, warning that loans could deepen debt burdens. Developing countries need US$455 billion to US$584 billion annually to deliver their climate commitments, according to estimates he cited.

Why it mattersNamibia’s climate plan depends on international funders providing about US$12.6 billion by 2035, with US$1.4 billion to be raised at home.
