Namibia starts second FMD vaccination round in //Kharas

Veterinary teams began the campaign on 16 November, as the outbreak remained at 131 confirmed cases across 18 farms.

Sources: New Era Live and The Brief · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Namibia’s veterinary service began a second compulsory round of foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) vaccinations in the Karasburg Veterinary District on 16 November. The campaign runs through 28 November and covers cattle immunisation, post-vaccination monitoring and disease surveillance in affected and high-risk buffer zones.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries, Water and Land Reform reported 131 confirmed cases across 18 farms, with the virus confined to the district. During the first round, veterinary teams vaccinated 8,267 cattle on 90 farms, according to the ministry. Officials have asked livestock owners to gather cloven-hoofed animals for vaccination and inspection.

The outbreak was first confirmed in the district on 23 September. Namibia’s wider response to contain the southern outbreak is estimated to require N$190.2 million; about N$16.2 million has been mobilised in donations and pledges.

Why it mattersKarasburg livestock owners must gather their cloven-hoofed animals for vaccination and inspection before the campaign ends on 28 November.
