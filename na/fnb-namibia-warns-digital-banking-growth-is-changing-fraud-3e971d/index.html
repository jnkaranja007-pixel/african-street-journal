FNB Namibia warns digital banking growth is changing fraud risks

Digital channels now carry most of the bank's transactions, and criminals are using artificial intelligence to make impersonation harder to spot.

Sources: The Brief and Namibia Economist · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Across FNB Namibia's customer base, the share of people using digital services has reached more than 92%, and digital channels now handle over 72% of its financial transactions. Amoré Nicholson, the bank's digital banking head, says that shift has made digital services part of everyday life in a geographically dispersed country.

Nicholson warns that fraudsters rely on urgent calls and messages that impersonate banks, pushing customers to approve transactions before they can think carefully. Artificial intelligence (AI) can reproduce voices and create convincing correspondence, making older advice such as checking for spelling errors less reliable.

The African Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2026 by INTERPOL (the International Criminal Police Organization) says AI enables 55% of reported cybercrime across Africa. Its global fraud assessment estimates that AI-assisted fraud is 4.5 times as profitable as conventional methods. Nicholson says banks must design security for customers who may be distracted or frightened.

Why it mattersFNB Namibia's customers increasingly manage money digitally, with over 72% of transactions taking place through those channels.
