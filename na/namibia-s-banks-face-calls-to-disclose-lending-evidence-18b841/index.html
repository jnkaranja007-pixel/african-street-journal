Namibia's banks face calls to disclose lending evidence

Black entrepreneurs say finance requirements favour businesses with wealth and established banking ties, while banks say loans are assessed by commercial risk.

Source: The Windhoek Observer · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Namibian black entrepreneurs say they face different rules when seeking bank finance, renewing debate over whether the country operates one integrated private sector or two economies divided by race and history. The Windhoek Observer feature says many emerging black-owned firms are smaller, undercapitalised and reliant on government contracts, while established businesses often have assets and banking relationships.

The entrepreneurs' complaint is not that banks refuse all lending, but that requirements, assessments and commercial relationships appear to favour applicants with property, wealth and a business track record. Banks say they assess repayment capacity, collateral, cash flow and commercial viability, and have a duty to protect depositors' money and maintain sound balance sheets.

The feature argues that banks should open their books so evidence can test the claims. It also says the broad contrast does not describe every company: some black-owned firms operate independently of government, while some white-owned firms rely on public contracts or face financial difficulty.
