FMD costs Namibia N$150 million weekly

Foot-and-mouth disease outbreak disrupts trade across SADC, threatening farmers' incomes and export markets.

Namibia's livestock industry is losing an estimated N$150 million weekly due to foot-and-mouth disease (FMD), with combined regional losses reaching N$30 billion. The outbreak has led to movement and marketing restrictions across the Southern African Development Community (SADC), impacting farmers' incomes, employment, and access to export markets.

Namibia's weekly losses include N$118 million from cattle, N$26 million from sheep, and N$6 million from goats, totaling approximately N$655 million monthly. Farmers continue to incur operational expenses like salaries and fodder, amounting to N$230 million weekly, while livestock marketing remains disrupted. The Namibian government has allocated N$180 million to combat FMD.

Approximately 265,000 jobs are at risk as restrictions affect livestock production and marketing. Namibia's beef exports, 94% of which go to markets like the European Union and Norway requiring FMD-free status without vaccination, are particularly vulnerable. The Ministry of Agriculture is in talks with new markets including Tunisia, Morocco, and Nigeria.

Why it mattersNamibian farmers and agricultural workers face significant financial losses and job insecurity due to FMD-related trade restrictions.

Namibia private sector credit grows N$5.84 billion

Businesses and households borrowed more in nine months, but inflation erodes real gains.

Namibia's private sector credit increased by N$5.84 billion in the first nine months of 2026, a 39% rise from the same period last year. This growth brought the total credit book to N$128.2 billion by September, with N$1.06 billion added in that month alone.

Businesses borrowed N$656.8 million, 62% of September's increase, while households added N$403.5 million. Despite this nominal growth, inflation at 5.4% meant real private sector credit growth was only 0.2% in September, down from 0.3% in August.

Almandro Jansen, an economist at Simonis Storm, noted that much of the borrowing increase reflects higher prices rather than real expansion. Corporate credit growth accelerated to 6.7% in September, but Jansen cautioned that sustained growth is needed to confirm a business investment recovery, especially with drought and fuel costs impacting agriculture.

Why it mattersBusinesses and households in Namibia are borrowing more money, but inflation means they are not actually getting more purchasing power.

Namibian man claims murder charges are false

Teodor Shipanga, 49, told the Windhoek High Court he has no knowledge of killing a colleague and stealing N$2.5 million.

Teodor Shipanga, 49, told the Windhoek High Court yesterday that charges of murder and robbery against him are false. Shipanga claims he has no knowledge of the alleged crimes, stating that no police docket was opened and that a judge had cancelled his charges. He is accused of murdering security colleague Shain van Wyk, 22, on November 11, 2011, and stealing N$2.5 million and a cash-in-transit vehicle.

The state alleges Van Wyk was shot in the head while transporting cash from northern Namibia to Windhoek with Shipanga. Van Wyk's body was found in a culvert near Okahandja, and the abandoned, partly burnt vehicle was found in Windhoek. A former vault manager, Mina van Wyk, testified that she had set the route for the two men and that Shipanga was issued a shotgun before their trip. She also stated that Shipanga's security uniform and ID were found near the burnt vehicle.

Shipanga pleaded not guilty to all charges in June, protesting the trial's constitutionality. He specifically asked the prosecutor, Anna Amukugo, to be removed from the case. The trial continues.

Politics · 2026-11-03

Namibia minister announces fuel price hike

Petrol prices will increase by N$2 per litre and diesel by N$3 per litre starting in November.

Namibian Minister of Mines and Energy Modestus Amutse announced a fuel price increase for November due to international market pressures. Petrol 95 will rise by N$2 per litre, while diesel 50ppm and Diesel 10ppm will increase by N$3 per litre.

The price hikes will affect basic living costs for Namibians, with stagnant salaries unlikely to offset the rise. Minister Amutse confirmed the increases in a statement.

Separately, Minister Amutse has referred allegations of corruption and bribery involving the Namibia Diamond Trading Company (NDTC) CEO and other officials to the Anti-Corruption Commission (ACC) for investigation.

Why it mattersNamibians will face higher costs for transportation and goods due to increased fuel prices.

Health · 2026-11-03

Namibia hosts world autism congress

Windhoek will gather international experts and advocates from November 3-5 to discuss autism support.

Namibia will host the sixth World Autism Organisation Congress in Windhoek from November 3 to 5, 2026. The event will bring together international speakers, researchers, and autism advocates to discuss better support for people with autism. Organisers state the congress occurs as Namibia's autistic community remains underserved.

Autism Association of Namibia founder Petra Dillmann stated the congress offers a platform for the autistic community to advocate for themselves. She explained that the event will allow participants to learn directly from autistic individuals about their needs and experiences. The congress aims to unite parents, educators, health professionals, academics, employers, government representatives, and people with autism.

Discussions will cover daily life support, early intervention, education, employment, rights, advocacy, ageing, and sensory processing. Professional development opportunities will be available for psychologists, therapists, medical practitioners, teachers, and researchers. Registration for in-person and virtual attendance is open via the congress website.

Why it mattersThe congress in Windhoek aims to improve support for Namibia's underserved autistic community by bringing together various stakeholders and international expertise.
