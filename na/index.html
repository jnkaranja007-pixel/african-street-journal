Bank of Namibia grants farmers loan holidays

Commercial banks must offer up to 24-month repayment breaks to farmers and businesses hit by disease and drought.

The Bank of Namibia directed commercial banks on Friday to provide loan repayment moratoriums of up to 24 months for farmers and businesses affected by Foot-and-Mouth Disease and anticipated El Niño conditions. Banking institutions must assess borrowers' financial circumstances and income losses before granting relief, which can range from one to 24 months. Banks may also restructure loans to make repayments more manageable, though interest will continue to accrue unless otherwise agreed in writing.

This intervention follows concerns raised by the agricultural and banking sectors about financially distressed farmers and businesses. Eligibility extends to companies within the agricultural value chain whose income depends on farming. Borrowers must demonstrate that their income has been adversely affected by the disease outbreak or climatic conditions. The central bank also introduced protections for credit bureau reporting, requiring banks to report clients receiving relief as performing.

The Bank of Namibia stated the measure aims to support the agricultural sector's viability, protect livelihoods, and limit financial system risks. Agriculture is a significant contributor to employment, food security, and economic growth. The FMD outbreak and adverse climatic conditions pose risks across the agricultural value chain. Banks must provide written reasons for declining applications, and borrowers can lodge complaints through internal processes.

Namibians question prison comfort versus street struggle

Life outside correctional facilities offers less security and fewer basic services than life inside, prompting serious discussion.

Namibians are debating whether life inside prison has become more comfortable than life on the streets, with citizens facing daily struggles for food, shelter, and employment. Street vendors have their goods confiscated, while inmates receive accommodation, meals, water, electricity, and healthcare.

The discussion highlights a disparity in the provision of basic needs and opportunities between those incarcerated and those living freely but in poverty. While prisoners are human beings deserving of dignity and rehabilitation, questions arise about the commitment to improving the lives of law-abiding citizens struggling to survive.

The core issue is creating a society outside prison that offers more meaningful opportunities and dignity than the security some inmates may find within. The argument is not for harsher prison conditions but for increased investment in crime prevention, job creation, and support for vulnerable communities to make life outside prison more viable.

Why it mattersNamibians struggling with poverty, unemployment, and lack of basic services are questioning the government's focus when compared to the provisions offered to prisoners.

Woman found dead in Otjiwarongo flat

Police investigate multiple stab wounds after ex-boyfriend dies in road incident.

Kamilla Rickerts, 34, was found dead with multiple stab wounds in her ex-boyfriend’s flat in Otjiwarongo on Sunday. Police confirmed that Jovin Diergaardt, her ex-boyfriend, allegedly picked her up on Saturday and took her to his flat. Diergaardt later died in a road incident near Swakopmund on Sunday morning.

Otjozondjupa police spokesperson senior inspector Maureen Mbeha stated that preliminary investigations suggest Kamilla was last seen with Diergaardt around midday on Saturday. Diergaardt reportedly travelled to Swakopmund and died about 15km from the town towards Arandis, with police indicating he may have jumped in front of a moving truck. Kamilla had recently moved to Otjiwarongo and had been there for a week before her death.

Heinrich Rickerts, Kamilla’s brother, described her as his best friend and confidante, noting she helped raise their niece and younger sister. Kamilla, who would have turned 35 on November 14, will be buried near Rehoboth next Saturday. Police investigations into the incident are ongoing, and they have cautioned against spreading unverified information.

Why it mattersKamilla Rickerts' family and friends are affected by her death and the circumstances surrounding it.

Bank of Namibia buys 425kg of gold

The central bank's gold accumulation programme aims to boost financial resilience and diversify reserves.

The Bank of Namibia has purchased 425kg of gold from local mines as part of its gold accumulation programme, aiming for a total of 600kg. This initiative, started last year, seeks to strengthen the country's financial resilience and diversify its international reserves. Deputy governor Nicholas Mukasa stated that gold purchases have significantly contributed to the recent increase in international reserves.

Namibia's international reserves grew from N$58 billion to N$59.3 billion between the end of July and September. These reserves currently provide 3.6 months of import cover, which is adequate for currency peg support and international obligations. Gold offers an additional layer of protection against geopolitical tensions and volatile financial markets, unlike foreign currency assets.

Gold complements traditional reserve assets because its value does not always move with other holdings, promoting prudent reserve management. South Africa and Ghana also hold gold as a reserve asset, with Ghana pursuing a domestic gold purchasing strategy. China and Poland have also expanded their gold holdings for protection against external shocks.

Why it mattersNamibia's financial reserves are being strengthened by acquiring gold, offering a buffer against global economic instability.

Namibia loses N$67.8bn to illicit financial flows

The finance minister expressed deep concern over suspected money laundering and tax evasion impacting public services.

Namibia potentially lost N$67.8 billion to illicit financial flows over three years, averaging N$22.6 billion annually, according to the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC). Finance Minister Ericah Shafudah stated these unlawful activities, including tax evasion and money laundering, undermine domestic revenue and economic development.

Shafudah highlighted that such diversions deprive the country of resources needed for public services and infrastructure. She warned that professional confidentiality should not shield financial crimes, urging accountants and auditors to report suspicious transactions and cooperate with authorities.

Legislative reforms are underway to improve public finance management, including the proposed Public Finance Management Bill. The government also aims to strengthen the Auditor-General's office and modernise the regulation of the accounting profession.

Why it mattersNamibians are directly affected as lost revenue could fund essential services like education and healthcare, and create jobs.
