Agriculture · 2026-10-26

foot-and-mouth disease threatens N$2.4 billion livestock value

Namibia Agricultural Union estimates daily losses of N$13 million due to ongoing marketing disruptions.

Namibia's livestock industry faces potential losses of N$2.4 billion over six months due to foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) disrupting marketing, according to the Namibia Agricultural Union (NAU). The projected losses include N$1.382 billion in primary production expenses and debt repayments, N$378 million in abattoir fixed costs, and N$331 million from reduced export slaughtering.

Leigh-Ann Nehoya, an Agricultural Economist with the NAU, stated that the disruption could lead to daily losses of N$13 million across the livestock value chain. This situation highlights the economic significance of livestock farming, which supports farmers, workers, suppliers, transport operators, auctioneers, and abattoirs, putting pressure on businesses and household incomes.

The NAU estimates that livestock producers south of the Veterinary Cordon Fence require approximately N$230 million monthly for operating expenses and debt repayments. These ongoing financial obligations continue despite the inability to market animals, with projected six-month losses including N$81 million in foregone auction commission revenue and N$114 million in lost transport revenue.

Health · 2026-10-26

Pohamba helped Geingob reveal cancer diagnosis

Former first lady Monica Geingos said Penehupifo Pohamba created a safe space for public health disclosures.

Former first lady Monica Geingos stated that Penehupifo Pohamba created a safe environment for former president Hage Geingob to announce his prostate cancer diagnosis. Geingos spoke at Pohamba’s memorial service in Windhoek on Saturday, explaining that Geingob revealed his successful battle with cancer at a 2013 conference hosted by Pohamba. Geingob disclosed his illness that day because he trusted Pohamba and her work, Geingos said.

Geingos added that Geingob’s public announcement encouraged men across Namibia to seek testing for prostate cancer. She described Pohamba as a maternal figure who inspired trust, leading people to feel safe discussing sensitive health issues. Pohamba, who died on October 17 at age 78, served as Namibia’s first lady from 2005 to 2015.

Geingos also recalled Pohamba’s generosity, noting that she left money in a bank account for her when she took over as first lady. Pohamba continued to show concern for others even in her final hospital days. Pohamba was known for supporting vulnerable communities and promoting health awareness.

Business · 2026-10-26

FNB Namibia bondholders face November deadline

Physical bond certificates must be converted to electronic records by November 9 to remain tradable.

First National Bank of Namibia bondholders must convert their physical bond certificates for code FNBJ28S into electronic records by November 9, 2026. After this date, these bonds will no longer be tradable unless dematerialised and transferred into the Namibia Central Securities Depository (CSD) system. The dematerialisation process replaces physical certificates with electronic ownership records in the CSD without affecting investor ownership.

The bank announced that the register for FNBJ28S will be closed from November 6 to November 9, 2026, to facilitate this conversion. During this closure, only dematerialisation-related transactions will be permitted, halting transfers and trading. FNB Namibia stated that holding bonds electronically will enable more efficient trading on the Namibia Securities Exchange (NSX), faster settlement, and direct interest payments.

Electronic records will also remove the risks of loss, damage, theft, or forgery associated with physical certificates. Bondholders must appoint a Central Securities Depository Participant (CSDP) to manage the dematerialisation process, which involves opening an account with the CSDP and submitting original certificates. Investors should confirm fees directly with their chosen CSDP before proceeding.
