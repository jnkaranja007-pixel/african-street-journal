Namibian credit grows by N$5.84 billion in nine months

Businesses and households borrowed more as rising prices increased the nominal value of loans, but real growth remained flat.

Namibia's private sector credit increased by N$5.84 billion over the first nine months of 2026, a 39% rise from the same period last year. This growth brought the total credit book to N$128.2 billion by September, with N$1.06 billion added in that month alone. Businesses borrowed N$656.8 million, or 62% of the September increase, while households added N$403.5 million.

Almandro Jansen, an economist at Simonis Storm, noted that while nominal credit growth reached 5.6% annually in September, inflation at 5.4% meant real growth was only 0.2%. This suggests that much of the borrowing increase reflects higher prices rather than a significant expansion of real economic activity. Corporate credit growth accelerated to 6.7% in September, driven by demand for other loans and advances and commercial mortgage lending.

Household credit growth remained at 4.9% in September, but after adjusting for inflation, it contracted by approximately 0.5%. Jansen cautioned that sustained growth in business lending over several more months would be needed to confirm a recovery in business investment, especially with drought risk and fuel costs impacting agriculture and transport sectors.

Why it mattersBusinesses and households are borrowing more money to cover the same volumes of goods and services due to rising prices.

FMD costs Namibia N$150 million weekly

Namibia's livestock industry faces severe disruption as foot-and-mouth disease halts trade and threatens jobs.

Namibia's livestock industry is losing an estimated N$150 million weekly due to foot-and-mouth disease (FMD), according to Namibia Agricultural Union (NAU) President Thinus Pretorius. These losses stem from disrupted trade and marketing restrictions across the Southern African Development Community (SADC). The weekly figure includes N$118 million from cattle, N$26 million from sheep, and N$6 million from goats.

The outbreak is disrupting farmers' incomes and employment, with industry operations requiring N$230 million weekly for salaries, fodder, and fuel despite marketing halts. Approximately 265,000 jobs are at risk as production, processing, and marketing activities are affected. The government has allocated N$180 million to combat FMD, an amount Pretorius contrasted with the industry's estimated monthly losses of N$655 million.

Restoring export market access, particularly to the European Union and Norway which require FMD-free status without vaccination, could impact economic growth by 5% to 6%. These markets previously received about 10,000 tonnes of Namibian beef annually. The Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries, Water and Land Reform is in talks with new markets including Tunisia, Morocco, and Nigeria.

Why it mattersNamibian farmers and agricultural workers face significant income loss and job insecurity due to FMD-related trade restrictions.

Namibian man claims murder charges are false

Teodor Shipanga, 49, told the Windhoek High Court he has no knowledge of killing a colleague and stealing N$2.5 million.

Teodor Shipanga, 49, told the Windhoek High Court yesterday that charges of murder and robbery against him are false. Shipanga claims he has no knowledge of the alleged crimes, stating that no police docket was opened and that a judge had cancelled his charges. He is accused of murdering security colleague Shain van Wyk, 22, on November 11, 2011, and stealing N$2.5 million and a cash-in-transit vehicle.

The state alleges Van Wyk was shot in the head while transporting cash from northern Namibia to Windhoek with Shipanga. Van Wyk's body was found in a culvert near Okahandja, and the abandoned, partly burnt vehicle was found in Windhoek. A former vault manager, Mina van Wyk, testified that she had set the route for the two men and that Shipanga was issued a shotgun before their trip. She also stated that Shipanga's security uniform and ID were found near the burnt vehicle.

Shipanga pleaded not guilty to all charges in June, protesting the trial's constitutionality. He specifically asked the prosecutor, Anna Amukugo, to be removed from the case. The trial continues.

Windhoek to host world autism congress

The sixth World Autism Organisation Congress will gather international experts and advocates in Namibia's capital from November 3-5.

Namibia will host the sixth World Autism Organisation Congress in Windhoek from November 3 to 5, 2026. The event will bring together international speakers, researchers, and autism advocates to discuss support for people with autism. Organisers state the congress comes at a time when Namibia’s autistic community remains underserved.

Autism Association of Namibia founder Petra Dillmann said the congress offers a platform for the autistic community to advocate for themselves and share their needs directly. The gathering aims to unite parents, educators, health professionals, academics, employers, government representatives, and people with autism to improve support services.

Discussions at the congress will cover daily life support, early intervention, education, employment, rights, advocacy, ageing, and sensory processing. Professional development opportunities will also be available for psychologists, therapists, medical practitioners, teachers, and researchers to engage with international research and practices.

Why it mattersThe congress aims to improve support for Namibia's underserved autistic community by bringing together professionals and advocates to discuss needs and practices.

Andrada Mining's Namibia contribution rises to N$775m

The company's economic impact grew 6% in the 2026 financial year, driven by wages, royalties, and local procurement.

Andrada Mining's direct economic contribution to Namibia increased by 6% to N$775.2 million in the 2026 financial year. This growth was fueled by wages, royalties, and local procurement as the company expanded its operations at Uis Mine. The company's total economic impact exceeded £33.17 million, up from £31.39 million in the previous financial year.

Remuneration paid locally at Uis reached approximately N$103.3 million, an increase from N$100.38 million in FY2025. Andrada recruited 126 employees and appointed 11 local graduates to a 12-month internship program, with two interns later employed permanently. The company worked with 241 active suppliers, of which 78% were Namibian businesses.

Andrada estimated its activities generated an additional N$330.2 million in indirect economic benefits across Namibia, using the World Bank's mining-sector economic multiplier. Chief executive Anthony Viljoen stated that the Uis expansion creates greater opportunities for employment, local procurement, and skills development. The company is also exploring desalination for long-term water supply solutions.

Why it mattersNamibian businesses and workers benefit from increased wages, local procurement, and job opportunities created by Andrada Mining's expanded operations.
