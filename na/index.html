Swakopmund worker killed when concrete wall collapses during demolition

Stephanus Hamutenya, a casual worker employed by the appointed contractor, died after a section of wall fell on him at the Municipal Rest Camp.

Stephanus Hamutenya, a casual worker, was fatally injured when a section of an existing concrete wall collapsed on him during demolition work at the Municipal Rest Camp in Swakopmund on Wednesday, 11 November 2026. He was working with other employees on the demolition of existing bungalow structures at the Fish Bungalows when the wall gave way.

The Municipality of Swakopmund said in a media statement that fellow workers pulled Hamutenya from beneath the rubble and that paramedics treated him before taking him to hospital, where he died. The municipality named Frampton Investment CC as the appointed contractor that engaged him and said it is working with the contractor and relevant authorities to establish the full circumstances.

All construction and demolition activities at the site have been temporarily suspended while investigations and safety assessments are carried out. The municipality extended its condolences to Hamutenya's family, friends and colleagues.

Why it mattersAll construction and demolition work at the Fish Bungalows site is suspended while investigators and safety assessors examine what caused the wall to fall.

Namibia signs N$174m contract for Windhoek hospital site

Elderwood Trading CC and Shangsheng Construction will prepare the Havana site for the capital's first district hospital within 12 months.

The construction of Windhoek's first district hospital has moved forward with a N$174 million contract signed for site preparation. Elderwood Trading CC, in joint venture with Shangsheng Construction, will undertake bulk earthworks and install essential infrastructure services over 12 months at the Havana site. The contract covers access roads, stormwater drainage, water supply, sewerage networks, and electricity services.

Penda Ithindi, executive director for the ministry of health and social services, called the contract signing a historic milestone for Namibia's healthcare sector. He noted that Windhoek's population now exceeds half a million, making new healthcare infrastructure urgent. The new facility aims to ease pressure on Windhoek Central Hospital and Katutura Intermediate Hospital, allowing them to focus on specialised care.

Idi Itope, chief executive officer of the Central Procurement Board of Namibia (CPBN), stated that the N$174 million investment must translate into tangible development. He urged the contractor to adhere to the 12-month implementation period and maintain quality standards. The CPBN oversees the contract, which is the first phase of the hospital development, with hospital building construction to follow.

Why it mattersThe N$174 million infrastructure contract for Windhoek's first district hospital site moves the project forward, aiming to improve healthcare access for the capital's growing population.

Namibia pension fund holds N$5.1 billion for investment

The Government Institutions Pension Fund has billions available for viable projects but lacks bankable proposals.

Namibia's Government Institutions Pension Fund (GIPF) has N$5.1 billion available for investment in viable projects, despite the country facing unemployment and a housing crisis.

Penda Ithindi, GIPF board chairperson, stated at a recent symposium that N$23.1 billion of the fund's N$28.2 billion unlisted investment portfolio has been invested, with the remaining N$5.1 billion awaiting financially viable, well-governed, and secured opportunities.

The fund cannot lower its investment standards for pension contributions, but the situation highlights a need for a national project preparation facility to develop bankable proposals for housing, energy, water, and agriculture.

Namibia president flags engineer shortage

President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah says Namibia needs more qualified technical professionals to build and run infrastructure.

Namibia's public service is losing engineers faster than it can keep them, President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah warned at the inaugural Namibia Engineering Excellence Awards in Windhoek. “The shortage and high turnover of engineering professionals in Government must concern us all,” she said.

Her concern is that building roads and buildings is not enough; the state also needs engineers to plan, regulate, maintain and manage national assets. She said engineering expertise would be critical to the Sixth National Development Plan (NDP6), especially in infrastructure, industrialisation, housing, agriculture and manufacturing. Priorities, she added, cannot be met by policy statements alone.

She urged government, universities, technical institutions, professional bodies and private firms to work together on engineering skills. Engineering students need more scholarships, bursaries, internships, apprenticeships and mentorship, she said, and mining, energy, construction and transport companies should invest more in engineering education. She also proposed exploring a National Engineering Education and Innovation Fund, backed by public and private contributions, to help students and give young engineers loan-based seed capital to commercialise local technologies.

Namibian universities urged to add compulsory job-search module

A proposal would require every degree and diploma programme to teach CVs, cover letters and interview skills.

Namibia's universities and colleges should ask whether they are preparing students for jobs or only for graduation, The Namibian reports in an opinion piece. Students spend three or four years and their own money on diplomas and degrees, but a qualification alone does not show them how to present themselves to employers.

The writer proposes that all universities and colleges in Namibia introduce a compulsory module on job search and career development across every academic programme. The module would teach practical skills such as writing a professional CV, a convincing cover letter and answering questions confidently in a job interview.

Citing the Namibia Statistics Agency's 2023 Labour Force Survey, the piece says overall unemployment stood at 36.9% and youth unemployment at 44.4%. It adds that some graduates have the technical knowledge a job requires but struggle to communicate it to employers.

Why it mattersGraduates would have to complete a career development module in every academic programme if the proposal is adopted.
