MP asks for N$10m for kapana traders

Republican Party MP Mathias Mbundu proposed a relief fund for informal meat sellers impacted by foot-and-mouth disease restrictions.

Republican Party MP Mathias Mbundu has asked the government to allocate N$10 million to assist kapana vendors and informal meat traders affected by the foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) outbreak. Restrictions implemented to control the disease have disrupted meat supplies, impacting traders who rely on daily sales. Mbundu made this proposal during the 2026/27 mid-year budget review in the National Assembly.

Mbundu stated that the government must protect the livestock industry while also considering the financial impact on informal meat traders. He explained that many vendors depend on meat sales for essential needs like food, rent, and supporting their children. He requested that the Ministry of Finance, in collaboration with other ministries and local authorities, establish a once-off relief fund to provide temporary financial aid to eligible traders.

The proposed fund aims to help qualifying traders cover household expenses and maintain their businesses during the outbreak. Mbundu urged Finance Minister Ericah Shafudah to consider this N$10 million fund within the current budget adjustments or find alternative financing. Last week, Shafudah allocated an additional N$179 million to the Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries, Water and Land Reform to manage the FMD outbreak.

Why it mattersInformal meat traders in Namibia face financial hardship due to restrictions imposed to control a foot-and-mouth disease outbreak.

Standard Bank Namibia supports 68 entrepreneurs

68 Namibian entrepreneurs completed a business support programme, with 27 pitching for funding.

Standard Bank Namibia has concluded its 2026 Blue Growth Series, a programme aimed at supporting small and medium enterprises (SMEs). A total of 68 entrepreneurs completed the initiative, which partnered with the Namibia Investment and Finance Academy (NIFA). The programme seeks to help local businesses overcome challenges in accessing finance and developing their operations.

The Blue Growth Series prepared entrepreneurs to meet the requirements of financial institutions. According to Standard Bank Namibia, five entrepreneurs secured financing, 42 reported increased business income, and 16 opened new business accounts. Hellen Amupolo, Standard Bank Namibia Executive Officer for Business and Commercial Banking, stated that SMEs are the backbone of Namibia’s economy, requiring skills and market access alongside capital.

27 businesses deemed investment-ready participated in a Pitch Day, presenting to potential financiers and buyers. The programme also provided subsidised management accounts and advisory services. The number of participating enterprises grew from 40 in 2025 to almost 80 in 2026, with almost 120 businesses supported over two years.

Why it matters68 Namibian entrepreneurs completed a business support programme designed to improve their access to finance and business development.

Old Mutual raises SEED prize pool to N$650 000

15 entrepreneurs will now receive financial support across five categories in the 2026 programme.

The Old Mutual Foundation has increased the 2026 SEED: Seasons prize pool to N$650 000, recognizing more entrepreneurs across Namibia. Instead of three overall winners, the programme will now award first, second, and third place in each of its five categories, resulting in 15 entrepreneurs receiving financial support.

Mignon du Preez, Old Mutual Namibia Group Marketing, Public Affairs and Sustainability Executive, stated that the response inspired them to broaden the prize structure to support more entrepreneurs. The total prize allocation is N$650 000 across all five categories.

The five categories are SEED Level Up, SEED Green, SEED Play, SEED Made, and SEED Manufacturing, recognizing youth entrepreneurship, environmental sustainability, sport and fitness, creative industries, and manufacturing. Applications, which close tomorrow, are available at selected Old Mutual branches and via QR codes on social media.

Why it matters15 Namibian entrepreneurs will receive financial support for their businesses through the expanded Old Mutual SEED: Seasons programme.

Namibia secures N$1 billion for green hydrogen projects

The Green Climate Fund approved US$56 million for early-stage development and construction financing.

Namibia will receive N$1 billion (US$56 million) from the Green Climate Fund (GCF) to develop green hydrogen and industrial decarbonisation projects. The funding, approved at the GCF’s 46th Board meeting in South Korea, will be channelled through the SDG Namibia One Fund. Of Namibia’s allocation, US$15 million will finance early-stage project development, and US$41 million will provide equity for construction.

This financing is part of the Southern African Industrial Decarbonisation Facility, which aims to mobilise US$1.2 billion across Namibia and South Africa. Environmental Investment Fund of Namibia CEO Benedict Libanda stated the funding will strengthen Namibia’s green hydrogen industry and support job creation. The SDG Namibia One Fund has already signed development funding agreements with four major projects, including Hyphen green hydrogen and HyIron’s Oshivela green iron.

The GCF funding targets between 30 and 40 industrial decarbonisation projects across Namibia and South Africa, aiming to install 1.52 gigawatts of renewable energy capacity. This initiative is expected to avoid an estimated 24.2 million tonnes of emissions. Catherine Koffman, GCF Regional Director for Africa, noted the facility is designed to unlock large-scale investment in challenging industries.

Why it mattersNamibian green hydrogen and industrial projects will receive climate financing to support development and construction.

Namibian construction federation urges action on industry bill

The Construction Industries Federation of Namibia says a new bill could establish a construction council after more than a decade of delays.

The Construction Industries Federation of Namibia (CIF) is calling for concrete action on the long-awaited construction industries bill. Works minister Veikko Nekundi announced the legislation is nearing submission to the Cabinet’s legislation committee. CIF chief executive Bärbel Kirchner stated that this announcement has renewed hopes for establishing a construction council, over 10 years after the bill was first presented to parliament.

Kirchner noted that previous unfulfilled promises warrant caution, recalling that an earlier bill reached the National Assembly in 2010. She mentioned a government promise at the 2019 economic growth summit to establish a construction council by the end of the 2019/20 financial year. Further work on the legislation involved consultations facilitated by then deputy minister James Sankwasa, with a revised draft reaching the Cabinet in 2022.

The CIF hopes the renewed momentum will lead to legislation and implementation, suggesting measures like contractor registration and matching project sizes to local capacity. Kirchner added that an effective council could have supported these measures years ago, potentially widening participation and strengthening local businesses. The federation also highlighted skills shortages at technical, supervisory, and senior levels across the industry.

Why it mattersNamibian construction businesses and workers are affected by delays in establishing a construction council that could support local capacity and address skills shortages.
