Plastic Prices Drive Manufacturing Inflation Up 23.5%

Namibia's manufacturing producer prices saw a significant annual increase in the second quarter of 2026, primarily due to a 74.9% surge in rubber and plastic product costs.

Namibia's manufacturing sector experienced a 23.5% year-on-year rise in producer prices during the second quarter of 2026, largely propelled by a substantial 74.9% increase in the cost of rubber and plastic products. This surge made rubber and plastics the largest contributor to price hikes within the manufacturing industry. Other sectors also reported notable annual price increases, including diamond cutting and processing at 31.2%, meat processing at 22.5%, and non-metallic mineral products at 17.9%.

On a quarterly basis, manufacturing producer prices climbed by 1.1%, with the overall index reaching 129.1 points. This quarter-on-quarter rise was also significantly influenced by higher prices in rubber and plastic products, which rose by 12.4%, alongside increases in dairy products (4.9%), grain mill products (4.7%), and fish processing (3.8%). The Namibia Statistics Agency (NSA) reported these figures in its Producer Price Index (NPPI) Bulletin.

The broader economic landscape also saw considerable producer price inflation, with the overall Producer Price Index for Namibia increasing by 50.7% year-on-year. Mining and quarrying producer prices saw a dramatic 79.8% annual rise, while electricity producer prices declined by 19.9%. Water collection, treatment, and supply prices saw a marginal 0.5% increase quarter-on-quarter but a 0.9% decrease year-on-year.

Why it mattersManufacturers and businesses relying on rubber and plastic inputs face increased production costs, potentially impacting their profitability and pricing strategies.

Namibia Urges Investment in Drought Preparedness Over Response

Environment Minister Indileni Daniel stated that Namibia can no longer afford to react to drought only after disaster strikes, advocating for proactive measures.

Namibia's Minister of Environment, Forestry and Tourism, Indileni Daniel, has called for increased investment in drought preparedness, emphasizing that the nation must shift from reactive disaster response to proactive planning. Speaking at the UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD COP17) in Mongolia, Daniel stressed that drought is a persistent national reality, not a distant threat, exacerbated by rising temperatures and unpredictable rainfall. These conditions significantly impact agricultural production, water security, human health, rural livelihoods, and economic growth, potentially exposing over one million people to food insecurity during severe droughts.

Daniel highlighted that Namibia's experience has demonstrated the critical need to invest in preparedness before crises escalate. The country is currently enhancing its drought resilience through early-warning systems, contingency planning, sustainable land management, water security initiatives, and ecosystem restoration. These efforts align with Namibia's Third National Action Programme under the UNCCD, its National Drought Policy, and Land Degradation Neutrality targets, which provide frameworks for building resilience.

The minister also pointed to Namibia's history of advocating for international drought action, including hosting UNCCD COP11 and the first African Drought Conference. She noted that with approximately 85% of Namibia's land comprising rangelands, pastoral communities are particularly vulnerable to climate shocks. Daniel underscored the necessity of international cooperation, stating that coordinated action is essential for lasting solutions, as drought transcends institutional boundaries.

Why it mattersFarmers and pastoral communities are directly affected by the call for drought preparedness, as it impacts agricultural production and rural livelihoods.

Keetmanshoop truck incident sparks community anger over lack of arrest

A 10-year-old boy died after being run over by a municipal water truck, and residents are questioning the absence of any arrests.

Keetmanshoop authorities are investigating the death of 10-year-old Mohammed Austin, who was killed by a municipal water truck in the Paradise informal settlement. Mayor Emrico Blaauw expressed deep sadness and pledged full cooperation with law enforcement, stating the council is discussing support for the grieving family. The incident occurred on Sunday when the boy, a Grade 3 pupil, was reportedly playing with other children.

Preliminary findings suggest the children challenged each other to jump onto the moving truck, and Austin fell between the vehicle and its water tank. Paramedics pronounced him dead at the scene. The municipality is cooperating with police, but community members, including Julien Dreyer, have voiced outrage, demanding accountability for the driver and questioning why no arrest has been made.

Crime investigation coordinator Nicodemus Mbango confirmed no arrest has been made, citing preliminary findings that children were chasing and jumping onto the truck. He stated that holding the driver accountable would be unfair without evidence of negligence, and the case will be sent to the prosecutor general's office for a decision. The mayor urged caution from parents and drivers around heavy vehicles.

Why it mattersCommunity members are affected by the death of a child and the lack of accountability for the driver of the municipal truck.

Government Buys N$40m in Local Grain to Support Farmers

Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare announced a N$40 million government purchase of grain from local farmers to address market access issues and mitigate post-harvest losses.

The Namibian government has allocated N$40 million to purchase grain directly from local farmers through the Agricultural Marketing and Trading Agency (AMTA). Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare stated the funds will be channelled via the finance and agriculture ministries to support crop producers facing challenges accessing reliable markets. This intervention aims to mitigate post-harvest losses and ensure farmers benefit from their yields, addressing issues like weak domestic processing and insufficient storage.

Farmers have recently struggled to find buyers for their substantial harvests, with regions like Zambezi reporting large yields but only one commercial miller. This disconnect between production and market access, exacerbated by a lack of coordinated off-take, has led to fears of waste. The government's move is also part of a broader drought relief program in anticipation of the El Niño weather pattern, which threatens extreme agricultural drought and water scarcity.

The government assured farmers that systems are in place to procure all available grains. Prime Minister Ngurare confirmed that this support is designed to assist those with bumper harvests who are struggling to sell their produce. Additionally, the Ministry of Education, Innovation, Youth, Sport, Arts and Culture has been instructed to incorporate locally produced food into the national school feeding programme.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected as the government is intervening to purchase their grain, preventing post-harvest losses and providing financial returns.

Strong Mineral Prices Boost Namibia's Export Earnings

Despite lower production volumes in some key commodities and rising energy costs, favorable international prices for gold and uranium are bolstering Namibia's export revenue.

Namibia's export earnings are being significantly boosted by elevated international prices for gold, uranium, copper, and zinc. The Chamber of Mines of Namibia reported that while the diamond market remains weak and high oil prices increase operational costs, the strong performance of other key minerals is a positive factor. The Bank of Namibia has revised its 2026 economic growth forecast down to 2.1%, partly due to weaker activity in primary industries like mining.

Gold prices have shown substantial year-to-date gains of 34% and were 22% higher in July compared to the previous year, though they saw a 4% month-on-month decrease. Uranium prices also maintained a strong upward trend, with year-to-date increases of 18% and a 19% rise in July compared to 2025. These favorable prices are crucial for supporting revenue and investment for uranium producers and developing projects.

The mining sector is navigating a transitional year in 2026, characterized by lower production volumes but resilient value generation due to strong commodity prices. While gold output has declined due to operational changes and zinc production is subdued, the Chamber of Mines anticipates renewed production growth, investment, and job creation in the medium term through new projects and expansions. Maintaining a stable operating environment is key to realizing this potential.

Why it mattersInvestors are directly affected by the strong commodity prices for gold and uranium, which are boosting export earnings and producer profitability, despite rising operational costs.
