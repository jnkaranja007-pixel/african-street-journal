Namibia Rejects US Health Data Deal Over Privacy

Government officials cited national laws and privacy concerns, leading the US to withdraw specimen sharing from funding negotiations.

Namibia has rejected a United States proposal for health data and specimen sharing due to concerns over national sovereignty and privacy. Government officials stated that the proposed agreement, which would have provided over N$145 million annually for HIV and tuberculosis programs, violates Namibian laws. The draft agreement granted the US government extensive access to medical information, infringing on constitutional privacy rights and national control over biological resources.

The objections led the US government to confirm in November 2025 its withdrawal of the biological specimen sharing component from funding talks. However, the broader health data sharing aspect remains under negotiation, causing delays. Officials noted that the proposed terms contravened Namibia’s Access to Biological and Generic Resources Act and the Public and Environmental Health Act by bypassing requirements for export permits, prior informed consent, and benefit-sharing.

This dispute risks undermining Namibia's position in global health negotiations, including the WHO's Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing system. The government is exploring alternative funding sources, estimated at N$730 million (US$45 million) for 2027, to sustain its HIV-AIDS programs. Negotiations continue as the US budget appropriation deadline approaches, with talks involving the attorney general's office.

Why it mattersNamibian citizens are affected as their personal health data privacy and constitutional rights are at risk from proposed US access.

Skeleton Basin Investments Lists on NSX Development Capital Board

The capital pool company, incorporated in Namibia, aims to acquire an operating business or assets following its effective listing on August 28, 2026.

Skeleton Basin Investments Limited has been listed on the Namibia Securities Exchange (NSX) Development Capital Board (DevX) as a Capital Pool Company. The company, registered in Namibia, stated that it had met all requirements for the listing, which became effective on August 28, 2026. Skeleton Basin Investments will trade on the NSX under the share code RIG.

As a Capital Pool Company, this listing provides Skeleton Basin Investments with a platform to pursue investment opportunities and potentially acquire businesses or assets, subject to regulations. Cirrus Securities (Pty) Ltd served as the company’s sponsor for the listing, which was announced on August 28, 2026, after NSX approval. This addition contributes to Namibia's growing capital market.

The NSX reported growth in both equity and debt listings in 2024 and 2025, following years of minimal expansion. As of December 31, 2025, the equity market included 61 securities, and the debt market had 56 bonds issued. The NSX Strategic Plan 2025–2027 aims to increase market capitalization by 10% by December 2027 through a supportive market environment for listings and liquidity.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the listing provides a vehicle to identify and pursue future investment opportunities within Namibia's capital market.

Saka goal seals Arsenal win over troubled Aston Villa

Bukayo Saka scored the only goal as Arsenal secured a 1-0 victory against Aston Villa, who continue to sell players amid financial rule concerns.

Arsenal maintained their perfect start to the Premier League season with a 1-0 win over Aston Villa, thanks to a second-half strike from Bukayo Saka. The victory at Villa Park marked Mikel Arteta's 250th league game in charge and extended Arsenal's winning streak to two games without conceding a goal. The team fielded the same starting lineup that secured a comfortable 3-0 win against Coventry in their season opener.

Aston Villa's struggles intensified with their second consecutive defeat, following a 4-0 loss to Brighton. The club has been selling key players, including Ezri Konsa, who made his Arsenal debut against his former team after a £51 million transfer, as they aim to comply with Premier League financial regulations. Villa's squad has undergone significant changes, impacting their performance on the field.

The match saw debuts for Arsenal's Ezri Konsa and Aston Villa's Nicolas Jackson and goalkeeper Zion Suzuki. Villa's Emiliano Buendia hit the crossbar early on, but Arsenal eventually took control. Despite a penalty appeal and a free-kick near the box, Saka's decisive goal secured the points for Arsenal, though a late concern arose when Declan Rice limped off.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by Aston Villa's player sales, which are driven by financial rule compliance and impact the team's competitive standing.

Yamal, Raphinha score twice as Barcelona routs Rayo 5-2

Teenage star Lamine Yamal and Raphinha both scored two goals each to help Barcelona maintain their perfect start to the campaign.

Barcelona secured a 5-2 victory over Rayo Vallecano on Monday, with Lamine Yamal and Raphinha each scoring twice. The win marks Barcelona's third consecutive victory of the season. Yamal, aged 19, reached a personal milestone of 50 goals for the club with his first strike of the match, achieving this two years younger than Lionel Messi did.

Rayo Vallecano took an early lead through Sergio Camello, but Barcelona quickly responded with quickfire goals from Raphinha and Yamal. Raphinha's performance continued his strong goal-scoring form, and he is now La Liga's top scorer. The match also saw Florian Lejeune score an own goal, extending Barcelona's lead.

Despite Rayo Vallecano pulling a goal back through Camello's second of the game, Raphinha secured the win with his second goal. Yamal further extended Barcelona's advantage in stoppage time with a curling finish. Manager Hansi Flick noted Yamal's return to top form after a hamstring injury in April.

Why it mattersFootball fans and investors in sports teams are affected by Barcelona's continued success and the performances of key players like Yamal and Raphinha.

Farm Workers Secure Retirement Homes

A survey found that 95% of generational farm workers have homes away from farms for their retirement.

Most generational farm workers on Namibia’s commercial farms already possess homes away from their workplaces for retirement, according to a 2026 survey by the Agricultural Employers’ Association (AEA). The survey identified 62 generational farm workers, defined as those born, raised, and employed on a farm for at least 30 years, with 59 of them having pre-arranged retirement residences.

The AEA’s Farm Workers’ Project, initiated in 2019, aims to improve living conditions and retirement planning for these long-serving employees. The survey also revealed that 328 employees, representing 11% of those covered by responses, have a pension or savings plan. The AEA noted that farm workers would also gain from the upcoming National Pension Fund.

The survey also assessed employer satisfaction with services relevant to farm operations and employee welfare. The AEA/LWV office received the highest satisfaction rating at 67.42%. The Social Security Commission scored 61.56% satisfaction, while local ministry of labour offices, clinics, hospitals, schools, and hostels received lower ratings, with hospitals and schools showing significant dissatisfaction.

Why it mattersGenerational farm workers are directly affected by having secured housing for their retirement, ensuring a place to live after leaving their current employment.
