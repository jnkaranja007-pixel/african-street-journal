Namibia's NHE seeks N$572m from investors for 1,032 homes

The state housing agency says it needs institutional capital to build homes, as a backlog of more than 270,000 units persists.

Investor money of N$572.49 million is what the National Housing Enterprise (NHE) wants to build 1,032 homes across Windhoek, Ongwediva, Oshakati and Opuwo.

Speaking at the Government Institutions Pension Fund (GIPF) Pension and Investment Symposium, NHE Manager for Infrastructure Development Indileni Set-Sam Iipinge said the projects are investment-ready, with sites, budgets and buyers identified. The largest share, N$299.42 million, is for 477 opportunities in Windhoek's Otjomuise; Ongwediva needs N$120.88 million for 260, Oshakati N$125.42 million for 228, and Opuwo N$26.77 million for 67.

Iipinge blamed a shortage of suitably structured institutional finance, not demand or projects, for the slow pace. The agency estimates the capital could be recovered within 24 to 36 months through sales and property transfers, with construction taking 12 to 18 months, and the work is expected to support between 950 and 1,300 jobs.

Why it mattersInvestors who put capital into the scheme would recover it from home sales and land transfers within two to three years, according to the agency.

Truck crash in Windhoek kills passenger after brake failure

A truck crashed into the College of the Arts theatre yard after its brakes failed, and police have opened a culpable homicide case.

A 51-year-old Namibian man died on Monday morning after a truck crashed into the College of the Arts theatre yard in Windhoek.

Police spokesperson chief inspector Elifas Kuwinga said the incident occurred at around 10:37 between Robert Mugabe Avenue and Schwerinsburg Road. A 43-year-old Namibian man was driving the truck from east to west when the brakes failed, and the driver could not regain control before the vehicle hit the theatre yard. The passenger died at the scene.

Police have opened a culpable homicide case. The identity of the deceased is being withheld until his next of kin have been informed.

Why it mattersThe passenger's family must be told before his name can be made public, so they will hear of the death first.

Swakop Uranium invests $300 million in Husab Mine expansion

The company's Industrial Heap Leach Project will add a new processing method to recover value from lower-grade uranium resources.

Swakop Uranium is expanding its Husab Mine with an Industrial Heap Leach Project, a US$300 million investment to improve resource use and boost long-term production. This project introduces an additional processing method to recover value from lower-grade uranium deposits, strengthening Namibia's position in the global industry.

The project will create over 800 temporary jobs during construction and approximately 200 permanent positions once operational. Swakop Uranium Chief Executive Officer Luo Wei stated the initiative reflects the company's commitment to innovation, responsible resource management, and long-term investment in Namibia.

This heap leach project is the result of over a decade of research, with initial work beginning in 2013 and a semi-industrial trial successfully completed in August 2025. The project was presented at the 2025 Namibia Public-Private Forum, chaired by President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah, highlighting private sector investment's role in national development.

Why it mattersThe expansion will create over 800 temporary jobs and 200 permanent positions, contributing to Namibia's economic development.

Namibia trains 32 Hai||om youth in business farming

The Hai||om San Traditional Authority aims to shift subsistence farmers to profit-driven practices and curb rural migration.

The Hai||om San Traditional Authority has trained 32 young people in Outjo in modern, commercial farming skills through its Farmers Business School. Traditional Authority Secretary Pius Augomeb stated that the graduates are expected to apply their new knowledge to their land across the Kunene region. This intensive programme aims to move the community from subsistence farming to profit-driven agriculture.

Augomeb urged the graduates to view farming as a business enterprise, emphasizing its potential for household income, food security, and economic independence. He encouraged a new mindset focused on planning, productivity, financial discipline, livestock management, market orientation, and reinvestment. The initiative also seeks to reduce economic vulnerability and stem rural-to-urban migration.

Graduating student Christine Uwu-Khaes called on her peers to use their technical and financial skills to benefit their households and community. The ceremony coincided with the groundbreaking for an integrated small stock distribution initiative for communal areas, intended to foster long-term economic self-reliance among the Hai||om community.

Why it matters32 young farmers in the Kunene region have been trained in business farming techniques to improve household income and food security.

Namibia takes over RCMRD chairmanship from Zambia

Minister Inge Zaamwani says the chairmanship will concentrate on finances, staff skills and cooperation between member states.

Namibia has taken over the chairmanship of the Conference of Ministers of the Regional Centre for Mapping of Resources for Development (RCMRD) from Zambia, which held the post for two years. Agriculture minister Inge Zaamwani assumed the role at the 14th conference in Swakopmund, which runs from 16 to 17 November 2026.

Zaamwani told delegates that member states must pay their contributions in full and on time, and use more of the centre's services. She said geospatial information has become a strategic tool for development, not only a technical function, and that it is needed for decisions on farming, fisheries, water, land and infrastructure. Her remarks were delivered on behalf of President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah, who urged ministers to turn discussions into resolutions with deadlines.

The two-day meeting is also due to consider the centre's Strategic Plan for 2027 to 2030, which will guide its priorities. Zambia's minister of lands, Patrick Mucheleka, said the outgoing council had worked for two years to strengthen the institution, though challenges remain, and thanked Namibia for hosting at short notice after Malawi could not proceed.

Why it mattersMember states that fall behind on their RCMRD contributions will be asked to pay in full and on time, and the strategic plan for 2027 to 2030 will set the centre's direction.
