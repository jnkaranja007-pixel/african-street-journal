President intervenes to save N$41.1 billion Dangote investment

Namibian president personally persuaded industrialist Aliko Dangote to continue with the project after his team faced bureaucratic delays.

President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah revealed on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, that Namibia nearly lost a N$41.1 billion investment from Nigerian industrialist Aliko Dangote due to government bureaucratic delays. The Dangote Namibia Tank Farm and Regional Pipeline Project, valued at US$2.5 billion, was at risk of being abandoned. President Nandi-Ndaitwah personally intervened by contacting Dangote to plead for patience and ensure the project proceeded.

The President stated she placed the project directly under the Office of the President, assigning National Planning Commission Director General Ambassador Kaire Mbuende to oversee its implementation. This action followed past experiences where similar projects, including a proposed shipyard, were lost after investors became frustrated with administrative obstacles over several years. The shipyard investors withdrew after four years of trying to establish their project.

Nandi-Ndaitwah expressed determination to prevent another investment loss, citing the urgent need for job creation and poverty reduction. She instructed relevant ministries and the National Planning Commission to work closely with Dangote Industries, declaring the project a "presidential project" until completion. This intervention aims to create an environment where investors are supported by efficient processes, rather than requiring presidential intervention.

Why it mattersNamibian citizens are affected by the potential loss of major investments that could create jobs and reduce poverty due to bureaucratic inefficiencies.

Namibia launches state air ambulance after patient deaths

The new service uses a Learjet and helicopters to speed up emergency medical transfers.

Namibia launched its first state-owned air ambulance service in Windhoek on Wednesday, following the deaths of patients who could not reach specialized hospitals in time. Health minister Esperance Luvindao said the service aims to reduce travel time for critically ill and injured patients, especially in remote areas.

Luvindao recounted the loss of a 12-year-old boy from Katima Mulilo and other patients after an April 2026 road accident near Otjiwarongo as key motivators. Vice President Lucia Witbooi stated the service ensures survival is not determined by location, addressing challenges from Namibia's vast geography.

The service includes a Learjet equipped for critical care, with Government Air Transport Services providing aircraft and pilots. Helicopter evacuations will also be available through cooperation with the Namibian Police and Defence Forces, ensuring specialized medical personnel accompany patients to national referral hospitals.

Why it mattersNamibians in remote areas will have faster access to emergency medical care, potentially saving lives lost due to long road travel distances.

Namibia to send teachers to The Bahamas

The two nations will deepen educational cooperation, addressing a shortage of maths and science educators.

Namibia has signalled its willingness to assist The Bahamas with deploying qualified Namibian educators. The deployment will address The Bahamas’ deficit of teachers in mathematics, science, and technical vocational subjects. This initiative underscores deepening bilateral cooperation in education between the two countries.

Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare and his Bahamian counterpart Philip Davis discussed the teacher deployment on the sidelines of the 2026 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda. Ngurare assured that Namibia will evaluate the manpower request through local State institutions, balancing The Bahamas’ needs with Namibia’s staffing requirements and professional standards.

The leaders also discussed expanding trade and investment, with Namibia identifying beef, grapes, blueberries, and dates for export to the Caribbean. A Bahamian trade and investment mission, led by Prime Minister Davis, is scheduled for March 2027 to finalise commercial partnerships.

Why it mattersNamibian educators will gain overseas experience and The Bahamas will receive teaching staff for mathematics, science, and technical vocational subjects.

AI could add N$3.58 billion to Namibia's economy

A new study ranks Namibia 36th out of 56 Commonwealth nations in AI readiness, citing infrastructure and youth unemployment.

Artificial intelligence could boost Namibia's economy by N$3.58 billion over the next decade, according to a 2026 Commonwealth Youth AI Readiness Index study. The Ayre Think Tank report estimates potential GDP gains between N$1.73 billion and N$5.43 billion, with a moderate scenario projecting N$3.58 billion. This study was released on Thursday during the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda.

The report places Namibia 36th among 56 Commonwealth countries, scoring 42.7 out of 100, below the study's benchmark of 53. High youth unemployment, with 47% of young people in the labour force jobless, and limited electricity access reaching 59.3% of the population, are identified as major challenges. Inadequate digital infrastructure, including slow broadband and internet disruptions, further restricts the country's ability to benefit from AI.

Namibia has approximately 800,000 young people aged 15 to 29 who could benefit from AI-related opportunities. However, the index gave Namibia a low score of 3.1 out of 10 for decent work, enterprise, and transitions. Researchers noted gaps in digital education, with computing not recorded in the curriculum, and insufficient comparable evidence of digital and AI skills among young Namibians.

Why it mattersYoung Namibians face significant barriers to participating in an AI-driven economy due to high unemployment and inadequate digital and educational infrastructure.

Namibian woman accused of murder to remain in custody

Nambwa Matengu, 38, faces charges of murder, attempted murder, and arson after a fatal fire in Katima Mulilo.

Katima Mulilo resident Nambwa Matengu, 38, will spend Christmas in custody after appearing in court on Thursday, accused of setting her flatmate's room ablaze in August. Nsala Mwazi, 42, died in the fire while trying to push her two daughters to safety through burglar windows. The state alleges Matengu used petrol to start the fire at approximately 00:34 on August 24 in the Shackdweller location, blocking Mwazi's escape route from the outside.

Matengu faces charges of murder, two counts of attempted murder, and arson. She informed the court she is awaiting a state legal aid lawyer. State prosecutor Cedric Mundia opposed bail, citing ongoing investigations and public interest. Magistrate Davy Kambinda postponed the case to February 8 for the accused to receive legal representation and for investigations to proceed.

Family and friends of the deceased gathered at the court to protest any possibility of Matengu being released on bail. Matengu has been in custody since her arrest. The court heard that the police found a 5-liter container smelling of petrol near Mwazi's bedroom door.

Why it mattersNambwa Matengu faces murder and arson charges for allegedly killing her flatmate and endangering her two daughters in Katima Mulilo.
