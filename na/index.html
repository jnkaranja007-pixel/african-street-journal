Namibia traders excluded from FMD relief

Informal meat vendors in Windhoek say government aid for Foot-and-Mouth Disease bypasses them, leaving them without cashflow.

Informal meat traders and kapana vendors in Windhoek’s 7de Laan settlement are demanding answers from authorities regarding financial relief for the Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) outbreak. The Bank of Namibia’s BID-42 intervention offers loan repayment moratoriums to commercial and communal farmers, but grassroots traders say they have been excluded. This leaves them without support amid severe supply disruptions and soaring meat prices, impacting their ability to operate.

Klaas Losper, a 62-year-old meat distributor supplying informal markets, told NBC News that the outbreak cut off his income and drove wholesale meat costs up. "Only farm owners are receiving assistance from government. My question is when will small traders from the community and informal markets receive any assistance from government?" Losper asked, highlighting the vital role informal distributors play in providing affordable protein. Emgard Ndukireepo, a kapana and sausages seller, said supply bottlenecks and high costs have halted her daily trade, forcing her household of 11 to survive on a N$1 600 disability grant.

Ndukireepo appealed to the government for direct, targeted relief mechanisms for small-scale vendors in informal settlements. She warned that without urgent intervention, grassroots traders will remain the silent casualties of the FMD outbreak. The current relief measures are designed to free up cash flow for farmers to sustain operations during livestock movement restrictions.

Why it mattersInformal meat traders in Windhoek are struggling to source stock and afford resale prices due to FMD outbreak disruptions, impacting their ability to earn and support their households.

Namibia pension fund holds N$5.1bn for projects

The Government Institutions Pension Fund has committed capital awaiting investment in viable Namibian projects, particularly in infrastructure.

The Government Institutions Pension Fund (GIPF) has N$5.1 billion in committed capital that it is ready to invest in suitable Namibian projects. The fund's Board of Trustees Chairperson Penda Ithindi stated this at the GIPF 2026 Pension and Investment Symposium in Windhoek. Ithindi explained that the fund has a conservative investment philosophy and projects must meet specific criteria before capital is released.

Infrastructure, Ithindi said, is where committed money sits furthest from being spent, because projects need extensive preparation before they qualify for institutional financing. Proposals must show financial viability, adequate security and sound governance before the fund commits. Despite these hurdles, unlisted investment commitments rose by about 10% in the period under review, and the fund has received around N$7.2 billion in distributions from the portfolio to date.

GIPF's domestic investment portfolio has passed N$100 billion, or 48% of total assets, above the 45% regulatory minimum. Its energy and infrastructure investments total roughly N$7 billion, alongside N$2.7 billion in small and medium enterprise financing and N$207.4 million in information and communications technology fibre infrastructure. The fund's investments have added 545 megawatts of electricity generation capacity.

Why it mattersInfrastructure developers seeking GIPF money must first complete project preparation, guarantees and governance checks before any of the N$5.1 billion can flow to them.

Old Mutual commits N$300 000 to Kunene food security

The funding supports gardening infrastructure, equipment, and training through the Green Futures Garden Project.

The Old Mutual Foundation has spent N$300 000 on community agriculture in Namibia this year, bringing its total expenditure since 2024 to N$900 000.

This year's investment focused on the Kunene region, establishing three gardens at Outjo Secondary School, Bergsig School, and Jimmy’s Community Centre as part of the Green Futures Garden Project. These sites are expected to serve approximately 1 900 pupils, teachers, and residents.

Mignon du Preez, Old Mutual Namibia group marketing, public affairs and sustainability executive, stated the initiative aims to empower communities to grow their own food.

Why it mattersThe Old Mutual Foundation's N$300 000 investment will establish three gardens in the Kunene region, benefiting around 1 900 people.

Ignite Namibia unveils granite pencil and local bottled water

Both products were commissioned by Ignite Namibia and launched at a breakfast event in Windhoek on Wednesday.

Ignite Namibia launched two locally developed products on Wednesday: a pencil made from granite and wood sourced in Otjiwarongo, and locally purified bottled water. The launch took place at a breakfast event at the NUST Hotel School in Windhoek.

The pencil was developed by John Namupala, a creative from the College of the Arts. Deputy minister of information and communication technology Wenzel Kavaka, who gave the keynote address, welcomed the products and said they could create value chains, business opportunities and employment. Ignite Namibia co-directors Venessa Auchas and Armas Shivute Armas attended the launch.

With further investment in production equipment, finishing and packaging, the pencil could grow into a stationery and corporate merchandise business. The bottled water enters a market where established brands already have distribution networks in supermarkets and hotels.

Why it mattersNamibian suppliers and buyers of stationery and bottled water now face new local competitors, and the products must move from innovation to consistent commercial production to reach shelves.
