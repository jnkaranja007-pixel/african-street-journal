Namibia allocates N$179m for FMD fight

Lawmakers question if the funding is sufficient to support farmers facing disease and drought.

Members of parliament debated the government's N$179 million allocation to combat foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) during the 2026/27 mid-year budget review. Finance minister Ericah Shafudah announced the funds, raised by a 2% spending cut across government offices, ministries, and agencies. Lawmakers expressed concerns that this amount may not be enough to adequately support affected livestock farmers.

Affirmative Repositioning MP George Kambala questioned the sufficiency of the funds, calling for strengthened veterinary services, improved livestock identification, and a restocking program for communal farmers. MP Willem Amutenya urged the government to ensure the N$179 million yields results, emphasizing the need for adequate equipment for veterinary officials and better disease monitoring. Namibia Economic Freedom Fighters MP Kalimbo Iipumbu stressed investment in veterinary services, water infrastructure, and disease control.

The Bank of Namibia directed commercial banks to offer farmers and businesses up to 24-month loan repayment moratoriums due to FMD and anticipated El Niño conditions. This intervention, under Determination BID-2, requires banks to assess borrowers' financial situations before granting relief, which can include loan restructuring. Banks must report clients granted relief as performing, provided their credit quality has not materially deteriorated.

Why it mattersNamibian farmers and agricultural businesses face financial distress from foot-and-mouth disease and drought, prompting government funding and central bank relief measures.

Namibia remembers sanitation pioneer Libertina Amathila

Former deputy prime minister Amathila, Namibia's first woman doctor, drove to eliminate bucket toilets.

Former deputy prime minister Libertina Amathila, Namibia's first woman medical doctor, is remembered for her drive to eliminate bucket toilets across the country. Amathila, 85, who was part of the Tanganyika group, retired from politics 16 years ago. Former colleagues recall her energy and imagination, noting she often left her office to help marginalized communities.

Former prime minister Nahas Angula stated that Amathila used her position to support communities with limited basic services, often establishing clinics or houses. Nurse Erika Akweye, who worked with Amathila during the liberation struggle and after independence, noted her contributions extended beyond politics. Amathila, born in Fransfontein in 1940, pursued medicine after witnessing poor hospital conditions.

Amathila studied medicine in Poland, graduating in 1969 and becoming Namibia's first woman doctor. She later earned postgraduate diplomas in nutrition and public health from the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine. During the liberation struggle, she provided healthcare to Namibians in Swapo refugee camps and encouraged women's family planning and education.

Why it mattersLibertina Amathila's political career and efforts to improve basic services like sanitation and healthcare directly impacted Namibian citizens.

Bank of Namibia buys 425kg of gold

The central bank aims to acquire 600kg to bolster national financial resilience and diversify reserves.

The Bank of Namibia has purchased 425kg of gold from local mines as part of its gold accumulation programme, aiming for a total of 600kg. This initiative, started last year, strengthens the country's financial resilience and diversifies its international reserves. Deputy governor Nicholas Mukasa stated that these gold purchases have significantly contributed to the recent increase in international reserves.

Namibia's international reserves grew from N$58 billion to N$59.3 billion between the end of July and September. These reserves currently provide 3.6 months of import cover, which is adequate for currency peg support and international financial obligations. Gold offers an additional layer of protection against geopolitical tensions and volatile financial markets, acting as a store of value.

Gold complements the central bank's traditional reserve assets because its value does not necessarily move in the same direction as other holdings. This diversification strategy is also being pursued by countries like South Africa and Ghana, which use locally produced commodities to enhance national financial security. China and Poland have also expanded their gold holdings for protection against external shocks.

Why it mattersNamibia's financial reserves are being strengthened by acquiring gold, offering a buffer against global economic instability.

Namibia loses N$67.8bn to illicit financial flows

The finance minister warned that unlawful activities like tax evasion and money laundering deprive the nation of funds for public services.

Namibia potentially lost N$67.8 billion to illicit financial flows over three years, averaging N$22.6 billion annually, according to a Financial Intelligence Centre assessment. Minister of Finance Ericah Shafudah stated that these suspected flows raise serious concerns about unlawful financial activities, tax evasion, and money laundering. These practices undermine domestic revenue and economic development, Shafudah warned at the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Namibia National Accounting Summit.

Shafudah highlighted that the scale of these illicit financial activities could deprive Namibia of resources needed for essential public services and infrastructure. She noted that the potential diversion of financial resources is particularly concerning given the country’s substantial development needs. The minister urged accountants and auditors to strengthen their role in detecting suspicious transactions and reporting financial misconduct.

The government is committed to improving domestic revenue collection and strengthening financial oversight, Shafudah said. Legislative reforms are underway, including the proposed Public Finance Management Bill, to enhance fiscal discipline and transparency. The Accountants and Auditors Regulatory Authority Bill aims to modernise the regulation of the accounting and auditing profession.

Why it mattersNamibians are deprived of resources for education, healthcare, infrastructure, and employment creation due to illicit financial flows.

Windhoek warns of park safety risks

The city's chief executive officer stated the municipality cannot guarantee safety in Zoo Park, potentially exposing it to legal claims.

Windhoek's Zoo Park, once a central gathering place, is now a concern for public safety, according to the city's chief executive officer, Moses Matyayi.

Matyayi informed a parliamentary standing committee that the municipality cannot guarantee safety in certain areas of the park, warning that visitors could be injured and expose the city to legal action.

The City of Windhoek is advertising a six-month tender for landscaping and irrigation upgrades, while the park's amphitheatre has been unused for over 15 years and a 2023 agreement with Innovate Group for renovation has unclear implementation status.

Why it mattersResidents of Windhoek are at risk of injury in Zoo Park due to the municipality's inability to guarantee safety.
