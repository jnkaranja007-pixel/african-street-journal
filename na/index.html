Namibia's manufacturing producer prices rise 23.5% as plastic costs surge

Namibia's manufacturing sector saw producer prices jump by 23.5% year-on-year in Q2 2026, primarily driven by a 74.9% increase in rubber and plastic product prices.

Namibia's manufacturing producer prices increased by 23.5% in the second quarter of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025, according to the Namibia Statistics Agency (NSA). This rise was largely due to a 74.9% annual increase in the cost of rubber and plastic products, marking the largest surge within the manufacturing sector.

Other significant annual price increases included diamond cutting and processing at 31.2%, meat processing at 22.5%, non-metallic mineral products at 17.9%, and other food products at 16.8%. On a quarterly basis, manufacturing producer prices grew by 1.1%, with rubber and plastic products contributing a 12.4% increase.

The broader economy also experienced producer price pressures, with Namibia's overall Producer Price Index climbing by 50.7% year-on-year and 8.8% quarter-on-quarter. Mining and quarrying recorded the highest increase among major sectors, with producer prices rising by 79.8% annually and 20% quarterly.

Why it mattersBusinesses and consumers are affected by these rising producer prices, as increased costs for manufactured goods, especially plastics, can lead to higher retail prices for various products.

Namibia shifts drought strategy to proactive preparedness

Namibia's Minister of Environment, Forestry, and Tourism, Indileni Daniel, advocates for greater investment in drought preparedness, moving away from reactive emergency responses.

Namibia is strengthening its drought preparedness through early-warning systems, contingency planning, sustainable land management, water security measures, and ecosystem restoration. This shift was announced by Minister Indileni Daniel at the UNCCD COP17 in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, where she emphasized treating drought as a development, economic, and resilience priority.

Daniel stated that Namibia, one of the driest countries in sub-Saharan Africa, faces recurring droughts, rising temperatures, and variable rainfall. These conditions pressure agricultural production, water security, human health, rural livelihoods, and national economic growth, potentially exposing over one million people to food insecurity during severe drought periods.

The country's Third National Action Programme under the UNCCD, National Drought Policy, and Land Degradation Neutrality targets provide frameworks for resilience. Daniel also highlighted Namibia's long-standing efforts in international drought action, including hosting UNCCD COP11 in 2013 and the first African Drought Conference in 2016, which resulted in the Windhoek Declaration.

Why it mattersThis shift affects over one million people in Namibia who can be exposed to food insecurity during severe drought periods, by aiming to reduce the impact of recurring droughts on their livelihoods and health.

Keetmanshoop municipality investigates boy's death under water truck

A ten-year-old boy died after being run over by a municipal water truck in Keetmanshoop, prompting an investigation by local authorities and law enforcement.

Mohammed Austin, a Grade 3 pupil, died on Sunday in the Paradise informal settlement after allegedly falling between a municipal water truck and its water tank. Preliminary findings from acting regional commander deputy commissioner Getruida !Kharuxas indicate the boy was playing with other children who challenged each other to jump onto the moving truck.

Keetmanshoop mayor Emrico Blaauw stated the municipality is cooperating with law enforcement to investigate the incident and is discussing support for the family. Blaauw also urged parents to caution children against playing near heavy vehicles and called on drivers to exercise vigilance in residential areas.

Despite community outrage and questions from resident Julien Dreyer regarding accountability, no arrests have been made. ||Kharas crime investigation coordinator Nicodemus Mbango confirmed the driver has not been arrested, stating that the circumstances suggest children were chasing and jumping onto the truck, and no negligence by the driver has been found yet.

Why it mattersThe community of Keetmanshoop is directly affected by the death of a child and the ongoing investigation into the incident, raising concerns about safety and accountability.

Government allocates N$40 million to buy local grain, averting farmer losses

Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare announced a N$40 million government allocation to purchase grain directly from local millers through the Agricultural Marketing and Trading Agency.

The government has committed N$40 million to procure grain from local farmers, a move aimed at preventing post-harvest losses and supporting agricultural output. This financial intervention, announced by Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare, follows consultations with local millers and producers, addressing challenges faced by crop farmers.

The funds will be channeled through the ministries of finance and agriculture to ensure farmers benefit from their harvests. Ngurare stated that the government has put systems in place to buy all available grains from local farmers nationwide, optimizing the harvest season. This initiative is part of a broader drought relief program to prepare for the anticipated El Niño weather phenomenon.

In addition to direct grain purchases, the government is integrating locally produced food into national school feeding programs. The Ministry of Education, Innovation, Youth, Sport, Arts and Culture has been instructed to incorporate locally sourced food into its school feeding scheme requirements, further supporting local producers.

Why it mattersThis intervention directly affects grain farmers by providing a market for their produce and impacts the education sector through the integration of local food into school feeding programs.

Namibia's mining sector sees strong gold, uranium prices despite production dips

Elevated international prices for gold and uranium are boosting Namibia's export earnings and producer profitability, despite weaker production volumes and a struggling diamond.

Namibia's mining sector is experiencing a transitional year in 2026, with strong gold and uranium prices cushioning the impact of lower production across several key commodities. Gold prices increased by 34% year-to-date, with July's average price 18% higher than in 2025. Uranium prices also maintained a strong upward trend, rising 18% year-to-date and 19% compared to the average price in 2025, according to the Chamber of Mines of Namibia.

Despite these favorable prices, the Bank of Namibia revised the country's overall economic growth forecast for 2026 downward to 2.1%, partly due to weaker activity in primary industries including mining. Gold output has declined following a transition at Otjikoto, and zinc production remains subdued. The diamond market continues to be a significant drag, with the diamond price index falling from 91 points in September 2025 to approximately 82 points by August 2026.

The Chamber of Mines anticipates that strong commodity prices will provide an important buffer through export earnings and revenues, despite the immediate-term constraint of lower production. Looking beyond 2026, the medium-term outlook remains positive, with new projects and expansions expected to strengthen and diversify Namibia's mining industry, fostering renewed production growth, investment, employment, and export earnings.

Why it mattersNamibia's economy is directly affected by the performance of its mining sector, as strong mineral prices contribute to export earnings and fiscal contributions, while production declines impact overall economic growth.
