AI could boost Namibia's economy by N$3.58 billion

A new study ranks Namibia 36th out of 56 Commonwealth countries for AI readiness.

Artificial intelligence could increase Namibia’s economy by N$3.58 billion over the next decade, according to a 2026 Commonwealth Youth AI Readiness Index study. The Ayre Think Tank report estimates this gain under a moderate adoption scenario, with potential increases ranging from N$1.73 billion to N$5.43 billion.

The study, released on Thursday, ranks Namibia 36th out of 56 Commonwealth nations with a score of 42.7 out of 100, below the readiness benchmark of 53. High youth unemployment, with 47% of young people in the labour force jobless, and limited electricity access, reaching only 59.3% of the population, are identified as major barriers.

Researchers noted that inadequate digital infrastructure, slow broadband, and internet disruptions further hinder participation. Ayre Think Tank founder Calvin Ayre stated that governments must equip young people with skills for emerging technologies, as AI will transform work and wealth creation.

Why it mattersYoung Namibians face challenges in benefiting from AI-driven economic growth due to high unemployment and poor digital infrastructure.

Windhoek City Sporting Club becomes Pamwe

The club aims for professionalisation, youth development, and commercial sustainability with a new identity.

Pamwe Sporting Club has officially launched its new identity, formerly known as Windhoek City Sporting Club. The rebranding took place on Wednesday at the Pionier Boys School Sports Ground, with a vision for professionalisation, youth development, and long-term commercial sustainability.

Club president Ndafelenga Ndafyaalako stated the change represents a move towards a professionally managed and commercially independent institution. Key development pillars include women's senior football, youth football, and facilities management. The women's programme has already seen five national team call-ups and two scholarships.

The club is seeking corporate partnerships for player welfare, equipment, and infrastructure development, including a second football field. Senior women's team captain Wanda Garoes noted the name Pamwe, meaning 'together', reflects the club's solidarity, which contributed to their 2026 Namibia Football Association Cup semi-final appearance.

Why it mattersNamibian football clubs are undergoing transformations to achieve professional status and financial independence.

Shortcut Part 3 film premieres Saturday

Optimistic Media Group launches third instalment of Namibian film series at Maerua Mall.

Optimistic Media Group will premiere ‘Shortcut Part 3’, the third film in its Namibian series, at Maerua Mall’s Ster-Kinekor on Saturday at 18h00. Director Leonard Tshikesho and cast members will attend the event. The movie, filmed in Windhoek and Swakopmund, follows street criminals Spikiri and Buzzman as their rivalry escalates over N$250 000 in stolen cash.

The plot sees Buzzman hijack a vehicle to return to Windhoek after escaping correctional transport, while Spikiri seeks help from friends. State agent Linda Shikongo, played by Paulina Shivute, goes undercover to apprehend them. The film explores fractured loyalties and desperate choices as the pursuit for the money intensifies.

Premiere tickets cost N$100 and include popcorn and a drink. The film continues the story of the two former friends whose relationship has unravelled into bitter rivalry.

Why it mattersNamibians can attend the premiere of a new local film and watch it in cinemas.

Windhoek owes N$932.7 million in unpaid bills

The City of Windhoek is conducting mass disconnections to recover N$932.7 million in overdue municipal accounts.

The City of Windhoek is owed N$932.7 million in outstanding municipal bills and has begun a mass disconnection exercise for accounts in arrears for over 30 days. This action is part of the municipality’s credit control policy to recover revenue needed for operations and essential services.

City officials stated the disconnection exercise is a lawful credit control measure, not a punishment or governance failure, aimed at recovering the N$932.7 million debt. Revenue collected funds bulk water and electricity purchases, refuse removal, and infrastructure maintenance, with widespread non-payment risking delays in repairs and service reliability.

The City Council approved N$724 million in debt relief measures between 2023 and 2025, with N$383.9 million already written off, and residents can still apply for assistance. The municipality also rejected claims of a defective billing system, stating a 2024 anomaly affected few accounts and was corrected.

Why it mattersResidents of Windhoek face service disconnections and potential legal action if they do not settle their municipal bills, which total N$932.7 million.

Two new clubs join Namibia Premier Football League

Onambula United and KK United will compete against 14 established teams in the 16-club league.

The 2026/2027 Namport Namibia Premier Football League season kicks off with two new clubs, Onambula United and KK United, entering the 16-team competition. These newcomers face established football heavyweights, aiming to prove their place among Namibia's elite teams.

Onambula United's head coach Festus Paulus described their promotion as a significant milestone, crediting the players, technical team, management, and local community. He believes top-flight status will bring national exposure and potential sponsorship, while KK United will host Millennium Julinho in their opening match.

Coach Paulus's immediate priority for Onambula United is securing top-flight survival and avoiding relegation. The club is struggling with financial difficulties, raising questions about its ability to sustain participation at the highest level without adequate resources and administrative structures.

Why it mattersNamibian football fans will experience new teams and challenges in the country's top-flight league.
