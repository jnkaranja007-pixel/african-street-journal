MP questions air ambulance operation since 2003

A Learjet 31 air ambulance, equipped for N$500 000, may not have flown since 2003, says Nelson Kalangula.

Opposition Member of Parliament Nelson Kalangula questioned the operational status of Namibia's new state-owned air ambulance, alleging its Learjet 31 has been grounded since 2003. The Ministry of Health and Social Services spent approximately N$500 000 equipping the aircraft, which was an existing government asset. Kalangula raised concerns about maintenance, spare parts, and the infrastructure needed for remote operations.

Kalangula stated the Learjet 31 was kept in a hangar after being replaced by a newer aircraft, questioning the high operational costs for a health sector needing medicine and facilities. He doubted its suitability for remote areas, suggesting 4x4 vehicles or helicopters are more appropriate. The shadow minister also questioned the availability of pilots, medical teams, and night-time emergency response capabilities.

Executive director Penda Ithindi confirmed the aircraft was a government asset to bolster emergency medical services for state patients needing urgent evacuation without charge. Vice president Lucia Witbooi launched the service on Wednesday, acknowledging its success depends on sustained funding, maintenance, and training. The service will also access helicopter evacuations via police and defence force collaboration.

Namibian president demands infrastructure match housing growth

President Nandi-Ndaitwah warns that towns and cities risk public health crises if water and sewerage systems cannot cope with new homes.

President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah has instructed municipalities to expand water supply and sanitation infrastructure alongside new housing projects across Namibia. She stated on Friday in Swakopmund that existing bulk infrastructure in many towns is nearing its capacity due to growing urban populations.

Nandi-Ndaitwah warned that municipalities could create sanitation and public health problems if housing construction continues without sufficient upgrades to existing infrastructure. She stressed that increasing the number of houses without strengthening supporting infrastructure would place further pressure on municipal services already struggling to meet existing demand.

The president highlighted the importance of adequate sanitation for human dignity and improved living conditions. She identified desalination, atmospheric water extraction, and cooperation with Angola on water infrastructure as potential measures to expand Namibia’s water supply.

Why it mattersNamibian residents in towns and cities face potential sanitation and public health issues if housing development outpaces essential infrastructure upgrades.

Businessman denies receiving N$148 million loan

Benjamin Hauwanga claims he never accessed funds despite signing loan agreements for Bank Windhoek money.

Businessman Benjamin Hauwanga stated he never received or used N$148 million from a Bank Windhoek loan, despite signing agreements in June 2024. Hauwanga claims he does not know who operated the account where the money was allegedly transferred. He became aware of the account only after seeking a bank statement on October 30, 2026, which showed transactions from June 2024.

A court order requires Hauwanga, his wife Mirjam, and five businesses to repay over N$137 million. Bank Windhoek's head of legal collections, Athalia Wallace, stated the bank advanced N$148.9 million under loan agreements signed between May and June 2024. Wallace noted that Hauwanga and his businesses missed court deadlines and failed to appoint new lawyers.

Hauwanga reported the matter to the police and requested an investigation by the Bank of Namibia, citing damage to his reputation. He has asked the bank for documents explaining the account and its transactions. The loan agreement specified a five-year repayment period for the funds transferred on June 12, 2024.

Why it mattersBenjamin Hauwanga and his businesses are being sued for over N$137 million in loan repayments he claims he never received.

NUST wins two continental awards for research and innovation

The Namibia University of Science and Technology received honours at the 2026 SARIMA Excellence Awards and the 2026 Africa Startup Ecosystem Builders Awards.

The Namibia University of Science and Technology (NUST) has received dual continental recognition for its work in research management and entrepreneurial support. NUST was honoured at the 2026 Southern African Research and Innovation Management Association (SARIMA) Excellence Awards and the 2026 Africa Startup Ecosystem Builders (ASEB) Awards.

The university was celebrated for its leadership in advancing research management and for supporting entrepreneurial ecosystems. These awards highlight NUST's commitment to fostering innovation and development within the region.

NUST's achievements at these continental events underscore its growing influence in research and innovation management across Africa.

Why it mattersNamibian students and researchers at NUST will benefit from enhanced support for their innovative ideas and entrepreneurial ventures.
