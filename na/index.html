Namibian Athletes Depart for COSSASA Games in Zimbabwe

Approximately 65 young athletes will compete in athletics, football, and netball, aiming for podium finishes and international experience.

Team Namibia has arrived in Bulawayo, Zimbabwe, for the Confederation of School Sport Associations of Southern Africa (Cossasa) Games, running from August 31 to September 2, 2026. The delegation of about 65 athletes will represent the country in athletics, football, and netball. Preparations included a national training camp in Windhoek, which focused on both physical readiness and fostering unity among participants from various schools, clubs, and regions.

The selection process prioritized performance and merit, with specific criteria applied to each sport. Athletics selections considered results from the NSSU National Athletics Competition, while football and netball teams were chosen through trials assessing positions, combinations, and overall team balance. Elkana Ileka, who will also compete in the Youth Olympic Games, is among the selected athletes.

Namibia enters the games with clear objectives, targeting podium finishes and personal bests in athletics, and aiming for competitiveness and advancement in football and netball. The Cossasa Games serve as a crucial platform for young Namibians to gain international exposure, test their skills against regional peers, and develop qualities like discipline and teamwork, aligning with the NSSU's broader talent development goals.

Why it mattersYoung Namibian athletes are affected by gaining international competition experience and developing key life skills through participation in the Cossasa Games.

Liberation Icon Helao Shityuwete Dies at 92

Namibia mourns the passing of a veteran of the liberation struggle and former Robben Island prisoner who championed workers' rights.

Liberation struggle veteran Helao Joseph Shityuwete died on August 29, 2026, days after his 92nd birthday. Shityuwete, a former Robben Island prisoner and advocate for workers' rights, dedicated his life to fighting apartheid and preserving the memory of Namibia's liberation struggle. His death follows closely after the nation's commemoration of Heroes’ Day on August 26.

President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah described Shityuwete as a courageous patriot whose life exemplified sacrifice and resilience in the pursuit of Namibia's freedom. She noted his unwavering commitment to the liberation of Namibia and his lifelong advocacy for the dignity and rights of workers. Shityuwete's imprisonment on Robben Island was a significant chapter, yet his dedication to Namibia's freedom remained strong even after enduring hardship.

In the years following independence, Shityuwete continued to serve the country through his writings and advocacy. He played a crucial role in ensuring that the sacrifices of the liberation struggle were remembered. His legacy extends beyond his political activism to encompass the freedom he fought for, the workers whose rights he defended, and the history he helped preserve for future generations. The President extended condolences to his wife, Jane, and their family.

Why it mattersNamibians are affected by the loss of a key figure in their nation's liberation struggle and a champion for workers' rights.

Namibia Joins Global AI Governance Commission

The country gains a seat at the table to shape international artificial intelligence standards and policies.

Namibia has been appointed as a founding member of the new AI for Good Global Commission, positioning the nation to participate in international discussions on artificial intelligence governance. ICT Minister Emma Theofelus announced the development on Friday, stating that the country must actively engage in the AI revolution rather than remain on the sidelines. The commission, launched in July by the International Telecommunication Union, is co-chaired by Rwandan President Paul Kagame and Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff.

Minister Theofelus emphasized that Namibia's membership provides a platform to influence the development of AI norms and standards. She highlighted the potential for artificial intelligence to address domestic challenges, improve public services, enhance education and skills, boost business productivity, support sectors like agriculture, and aid government decision-making through data analysis. The minister stressed that AI is an evolving reality that Namibia must embrace.

The announcement coincides with Namibia's preparation to host the 9th National ICT Summit from October 12 to 16 in the Oshana region, marking the first time the event will be held outside the capital. The summit's theme, “Harnessing ICT for Inclusive Growth: Digital Pathways to Reduce Unemployment and Eradicate Poverty,” underscores the ministry's commitment to extending digital transformation across the country. Discussions will focus on ICT's role in job creation, youth empowerment, and poverty reduction.

Why it mattersNamibia's participation in the global AI commission allows it to influence how artificial intelligence is developed and deployed, potentially impacting its own technological advancement and digital economy.

The Namibian Marks 41 Years Amidst Financial Media Challenges

Namibia's independent newspaper celebrates four decades of reporting while facing declining advertising revenue and the rise of AI news aggregators.

The Namibian newspaper is celebrating its 41st anniversary, marking a significant milestone for an independent media outlet born during apartheid. Founded on August 30, 1985, by Gwen Lister, the publication has a history of overcoming adversity, including firebombings and intimidation, to champion media freedom and democracy in Namibia. Its commitment to 'Telling It Like It Is' has been a cornerstone of its operation.

The newspaper's anniversary arrives at a critical juncture for the global media industry, which is experiencing a sharp decline in advertising income. This revenue loss is attributed to shifts towards digital platforms like Facebook and Google, and more recently, artificial intelligence companies that provide information without directing traffic to news organizations. The Namibian emphasizes that continued public and advertiser support is essential for its survival and its ability to hold power accountable.

Despite Namibia's constitutional protections for freedom of expression and an open media environment, the 2026 African Media Barometer indicates incomplete reforms and underperforming institutions. The failure to implement the 2022 access to information law highlights a gap between legal protections and practical application, underscoring the need for greater political will to address structural challenges affecting journalism, including funding and newsroom independence.

Why it mattersThe Namibian's continued operation and its ability to report on political dynamics and misinformation are directly impacted by its financial struggles and the need for public support.

Namibian Farm Wages Climb 15.86% to N$5,087.51 Monthly

A biennial survey by the Namibia Agricultural Union shows a significant rise in total remuneration packages for farm employees.

The total remuneration package for permanent and temporary employees on Namibia’s commercial farms has risen to N$5,087.51 per month, a 15.86% increase from the previous survey. This figure, compiled by the Namibia Agricultural Union, reflects an upward trend from N$4,391.20 in 2024. The package includes weighted monetary remuneration, housing, employee livestock, and transport for children and sick employees.

Weighted monetary remuneration saw a substantial increase to N$3,599.69 from N$2,931.28, while the value of free housing slightly decreased to N$1,023.73. The value of employee livestock and free transport also increased. The survey, which excludes annual bonuses and other benefits like firewood and utilities, found that 90.95% of employers provided annual salary increases.

Access to electricity in employee households rose to 66.22%, with solar power access increasing to 36.35%. Firewood access remained universal. The survey also noted a slight decline in dependents per employee and a decrease in reported HIV/AIDS and tuberculosis cases among the workforce, with employers confirming treatment for affected individuals.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected by the increase in labor costs reflected in the higher remuneration packages.
