Amnesty International Criticized for Selective Outrage on Human Rights

The human rights organization faces scrutiny for its approach to Namibia while global crises unfold.

Amnesty International's engagement with Namibia on human rights issues has drawn criticism for what some perceive as selective outrage. The organization is accused of lecturing Namibia as if it were a moral delinquent, overlooking the country's complex challenges and its shared history with neighboring Angola.

While acknowledging Namibia's internal human rights issues, such as potential denial of healthcare and education to drought-displaced Angolans, the critique highlights a perceived imbalance. This is particularly noted in light of Amnesty's own documented reports on the devastating humanitarian catastrophe in Gaza and Israeli attacks in Lebanon, where significant civilian casualties, including women and children, have been reported.

The argument posits that Namibia, a small African nation dealing with migration challenges along a historically intertwined border, should not be equated with a colonial power or a state waging distant military campaigns. The shared cultural and familial ties across the Namibia-Angola border necessitate a nuanced application of international human rights advocacy, sensitive to African realities and history.

Why it mattersNamibian state actions regarding drought-displaced Angolans, including denial of healthcare, education, and humane treatment, are the subject of scrutiny.

President Nandi-Ndaitwah urges corporates to invest in lasting national development

Namibian businesses are called to shift corporate social investment beyond symbolic gestures to create tangible, long-term socio-economic benefits for communities.

President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah has challenged Namibian businesses to transform corporate social investment (CSI) from symbolic donations into impactful, long-term national socio-economic development. Speaking at the inaugural Namibia Corporate Social Investment Mukopano in Windhoek, she urged companies to focus on whether their investments create lasting benefits for communities, emphasizing that CSI should be a genuine investment in society, not a public relations exercise or compliance requirement.

The president, represented by National Planning Commission director general ambassador Kaire Mbuende, highlighted that the true measure of corporate investment lies in outcomes like improved education, healthcare, youth empowerment, and stronger household livelihoods, rather than the size of announcements or visibility of donations. MTC spokesperson Tim Ekandjo, speaking for the Mukopano partners, announced seven national CSI priority areas including youth employment, skills development, education, water, sanitation, health, food security, enterprise development, and infrastructure.

The initiative aims to develop a national CSI response action plan, identify priority projects, mobilize funding, and increase corporate participation. President Nandi-Ndaitwah also urged the private sector to expand internships and apprenticeships, provide start-up equipment to young graduates, and increase investment in rural communities, stressing that profitable businesses are indispensable for economic growth and national development.

Why it mattersCorporations are urged to invest in long-term socio-economic development, focusing on outcomes like youth empowerment and improved livelihoods, rather than just symbolic donations.

Joe's Fade Cuts Opens Walvis Bay Branch, Creates Seven Jobs

The Namibian grooming brand expands its services to the coastal town, aiming to meet growing demand and contribute to the local economy.

Joe’s Fade Cuts has launched a new branch in Walvis Bay, creating seven jobs as part of its national expansion strategy. Founder Joel Mwatilefu cited increasing demand for quality men's grooming and the potential of the new market as key drivers for the move. The company aims to contribute to the local economy through job creation and skills development for young Namibians.

The new outlet offers professional haircuts, fades, and full men's grooming services, including a VIP experience, in a modern and comfortable setting designed for the local market. Mwatilefu stated that the company maintains its high standards across all branches and plans to increase its workforce as customer demand grows. The business also intends to use the branch as a platform for barbering and grooming skills training.

Expanding into Walvis Bay presented challenges such as site selection, branch setup, team recruitment, and market introduction. Mwatilefu attributed the successful launch to careful planning, teamwork, and focused marketing efforts. Joe’s Fade Cuts is now looking to further expand its presence into other regions of Namibia.

Why it mattersYoung Namibians seeking employment and skills development in barbering and grooming are directly affected by the new branch's job creation and training initiatives.

Namibia Wins Medals Despite Cosassa Games Challenges

Namibian football and athletics teams achieved success at the Confederation of Southern African Schools Sports Association Games in Bulawayo, Zimbabwe.

Namibia's participation in the recent Confederation of Southern African Schools Sports Association (Cosassa) Games in Bulawayo, Zimbabwe, was deemed a success, with the country's football and athletics teams securing medals. Despite some administrative and technical issues, the teams displayed strong performances. General team manager Nomin Lucas noted that while the games were generally well-organized, there were areas for improvement in communication and coordination among officials.

The Namibian football team earned a bronze medal, while the athletics contingent collected multiple gold, silver, and bronze medals across various events. Athletes represented Namibia with pride and good sportsmanship. A notable incident involved young athlete Hafeni Fillemon, who was initially barred from a final due to a lost bib number. An appeal was successfully lodged, allowing him to compete and ultimately win gold in the boys U/14 100m final.

Lucas highlighted that such administrative and technical challenges, particularly affecting young athletes, require careful and fair handling. These incidents, while presenting learning opportunities, did not detract from Namibia's overall positive performance at the games. The event took place from August 31 to September 3.

Why it mattersNamibian student athletes are affected by administrative and technical challenges at international sporting events, impacting their ability to compete fairly.
