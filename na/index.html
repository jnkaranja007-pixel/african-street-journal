Meatco resumes limited slaughter after FMD outbreak

Namibia's beef producer Meatco will slaughter cattle for two days to generate revenue amid export market disruptions.

Meatco will temporarily resume slaughtering operations on October 27 and 28, 2026, processing cattle from its own feedlot. This limited operation follows a foot-and-mouth disease outbreak in the Kharas Region that disrupted access to export markets. The beef processed will be sold exclusively within Namibia.

Deputy board chairperson Stephanie de Klerk stated that this temporary reopening does not signal a return to normal operations or the resumption of buying cattle from farmers. While domestic sales will generate some revenue, Meatco warned that local market prices cannot replace the higher margins earned from international markets, leaving the company under continued financial pressure.

The disruption has affected Meatco's cash flow, prompting emergency cost-containment measures. The company is exploring alternative sales channels with domestic retailers and hospitality businesses. A broader resumption of slaughtering and livestock procurement depends on further directives from the Directorate of Veterinary Services, with no timeline provided for restoring normal operations.

Why it mattersNamibia's beef industry faces export disruptions and financial pressure due to a foot-and-mouth disease outbreak.

Namibia clears table grape exports to China

China's zero-tariff policy offers Namibian producers a pricing advantage for grapes and wild aquatic products.

Namibia has approved table grape exports to China, opening a new market under the countries' agricultural cooperation agreement. Chinese ambassador Zhao Weiping announced the approval, which also covers wild aquatic products, at a seminar in Windhoek. This move provides Namibian grape exporters with a pricing advantage due to China's zero-tariff policy for African countries.

Exporters must demonstrate consistent quality and reliable supply to scale up volumes in the Chinese market. The agreement diversifies Namibia's export base beyond single commodities, creating additional revenue streams. Minister Inge Zaamwani stated Namibia is shifting towards irrigation and climate-smart farming, with four areas identified for agricultural development cooperation with China.

This cooperation follows President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah’s July 2026 visit to China, which focused on food security and farming technology. The focus on value addition matters because fresh grape exports capture less value than processed products, and Namibia has limited agro-processing capacity. Key for investors will be the borehole project's water delivery and Namibian exporters' ability to meet Chinese phytosanitary requirements.

Why it mattersNamibian farmers and food producers gain direct access to the large Chinese market for their table grapes and wild aquatic products.

Namibian microlender skipped loan checks

The financial regulator found a microlender failed to verify borrower repayment ability before issuing loans.

Namibia’s financial regulator, Namfisa, discovered a microlender issued loans without the required documents to prove borrowers could afford repayments. The finding occurred during an on-site inspection in the second quarter of 2026. Namfisa did not disclose the number of loans affected or the specific lender.

The inspection revealed instances of non-compliance, including granting loans without mandatory supporting documentation for affordability assessments. The microlending industry’s total loan book grew by 27% quarter on quarter to N$9.3 billion by the end of June 2026. Namfisa stated further action could be considered after final findings are available.

Namibian microlenders dominate high-risk categories, with 43 of 52 non-bank financial institutions in Stage 5 (near insolvency) being microlenders. Another 37 of 38 institutions in Stage 4 (serious doubts about future) were also microlenders. An administrative penalty against one microlender is under consideration.

Why it mattersNamibian borrowers face potential excessive financial pressure because a microlender skipped loan affordability checks.

Namibia Securities Exchange sets listing rules

Companies must use approved sponsors and submit documentation for exchange approval before public announcements.

Firms aiming to list on the Namibia Securities Exchange (NSX) must first engage an approved Namibian sponsor. This sponsor will guide the company through the formal application process, including identifying and preparing the necessary documentation for submission. The NSX reviews these documents to ensure they are complete and satisfactory before an application can proceed.

Following the review, the application is presented to the listing committee for consideration. If the committee approves, the application moves to the NSX board for final approval for a primary listing. Companies are restricted from making public announcements about their listing intentions until final approval is granted by the executive committee.

A company can announce it has submitted an application once the NSX has received a complete submission, but this does not guarantee approval. The time required for a listing varies based on the application's complexity and the process of meeting all requirements and obtaining necessary approvals.

Why it mattersCompanies seeking to list on the Namibia Securities Exchange must follow a specific process involving sponsors and approvals.

Nanso Zambezi seeks government support for student welfare

The Namibia National Students Organisation regional chapter faces challenges implementing congress resolutions due to lack of government assistance.

The Namibia National Students Organisation (NANSO) in the Zambezi Region stated on Wednesday that a lack of government support hinders its work addressing student concerns. Regional representatives voiced these issues during a panel discussion hosted by the ministry of information and communication technology’s Zambezi Regional Office.

NANSO representatives cited difficulties in securing transport and logistical aid from government offices, emphasizing the need for stronger collaboration. The organisation had sought N$445 000 for its 19th National Students Congress in August, which was affected by government funding cuts. Resolutions from the congress called for improved student welfare and sustained government support for recognised student bodies.

Shazzy Liswaniso, overseeing gender affairs, said collaboration should extend beyond meetings to regular communication and follow-ups. Munisola Munisola, the regional finance officer, noted that fundraising plans for next year require government and stakeholder support, including logistical assistance for meetings.

Why it mattersStudents in the Zambezi Region face challenges with accommodation, registration, mental welfare, and learning resources due to a lack of support for NANSO.
