Namibia mobilises N$369 million for FMD outbreak

Finance Minister Ericah Shafudah announced the funding, reallocated from government expenditure ceilings, to contain the disease.

Namibia has mobilised N$369 million to combat a foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) outbreak, with N$179 million reallocated from government expenditure ceilings to the agriculture ministry. Finance Minister Ericah Shafudah announced the funding on Thursday during the 2026/27 Mid-Year Budget Review, as authorities intensify vaccination and surveillance in the //Kharas and Zambezi regions. This intervention comes after a N$1.5 billion downward revision in government revenue projections and a weaker economic outlook.

The FMD outbreak, first confirmed on September 23, 2026, has led to livestock movement restrictions, raising concerns for agricultural production and rural livelihoods. Veterinarians have vaccinated 42,286 cattle across 34 crush pens in affected areas. In the //Kharas Region, 131 FMD cases have been confirmed across 18 farms, with movement restrictions and vaccination measures remaining in force.

Economists warn that FMD, drought, and rising fuel prices pose significant risks, potentially increasing government borrowing and reducing public spending. Economist Almandro Jansen estimates government revenue could fall by N$2.1 billion, widening the budget deficit to 6.5% of GDP. Rising grain prices and the prospect of poor rainfall could also push up food inflation.

Why it mattersNamibian farmers face increased costs and restrictions due to the foot-and-mouth disease outbreak and potential drought conditions.

Angola invites Namibia to join oil refinery project

Namibian president Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah concluded a visit to Angola where the Lobito refinery was discussed.

Angolan president João Lourenço invited Namibia to take an equity stake in the Lobito oil refinery, currently under construction. Lourenço extended the invitation on Monday at the end of Namibian president Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah’s visit to Angola. The refinery is designed to process 200 000 barrels of crude oil daily.

Presidential spokesperson Jonas Mbambo stated that any discussion of investment arrangements is premature as the bi-national commission only concluded on Monday. He noted that similar invitations were extended to Botswana and Zambia, and that Namibia requires careful consideration of large investments to ensure they are in the country’s best interests.

Rodney Cloete, shadow minister for international relations and trade, suggested Namibia should prioritize fuel storage at Walvis Bay for greater control over fuel supply and price. Angola’s state-owned oil and gas company Sonangol leads the refinery construction, having committed US$1.6 billion to the building cost and seeking finance partners for the remaining US$4.8 billion.

Why it mattersNamibia has been invited to become a shareholder in Angola's Lobito oil refinery, a decision that requires careful consideration of its potential financial and strategic benefits.

Namibia gains zero-tariff access to China market

China's new policy offers Namibian businesses easier entry into one of the world's largest consumer markets for goods like beef and seafood.

Namibia's export opportunities are set to expand as China introduces a zero-tariff policy for eligible African goods. This trade adjustment aims to make African products more competitive and improve market access for Namibian businesses. The policy could reshape trade relationships and create significant new avenues for growth.

The zero-tariff framework allows Namibian businesses to gain easier access to China's vast consumer and industrial markets, which have a strong demand for agricultural products, seafood, minerals, and value-added goods. Namibia is known for premium beef, table grapes, high-quality seafood, and mineral resources, all of which stand to benefit from this policy.

This initiative signals China's commitment to deepening economic ties with Africa amid global trade uncertainties. For Namibia, it presents an opportunity to move beyond raw commodity exports, focus on value addition, and potentially stimulate investment, innovation, and job creation across various economic sectors.

Why it mattersNamibian businesses, particularly those in agriculture and mining, will benefit from reduced export costs to China.

Namibia electrified vehicle sales jump 127% in September

59 hybrid, plug-in hybrid, and battery-electric vehicles sold as fuel costs rise.

Sales of electrified vehicles in Namibia increased significantly in September, with 59 units sold across all vehicle segments. This surge represents a 127% rise in sales for hybrid, plug-in hybrid, and battery-electric models.

The increase in electrified vehicle sales is attributed to consumers seeking energy-efficient alternatives amidst rising fuel costs, according to a monthly vehicle sales report by IJG Research.

This trend indicates growing consumer interest in alternative energy vehicles within the Namibian market.

Why it mattersNamibian consumers are buying more electrified vehicles due to rising fuel costs.

man arrested for child sexual abuse

A 60-year-old man is accused of sexually abusing a 15-year-old girl and offering her N$50.

Police in Namibia's Oshikoto region arrested a 60-year-old man for allegedly sexually abusing a 15-year-old girl. The incident occurred at the man's home in Omuxulu village, near Muthiya, last month. The suspect reportedly offered the girl N$50 after the abuse.

Commissioner Ndafudha Iifo of the Oshikoto police confirmed the arrest and stated the man appeared in the Muthiya Magistrate's Court. His case was postponed to February 24, 2027, for further investigation into the sexual abuse charges. Police noted that such cases are often difficult to investigate.

Authorities urge the public to report sexual abuse cases promptly to aid police investigations. They also stressed the importance of immediate medical attention for victims of sexual assault. Parents are advised to remain vigilant and monitor their children, as they can become targets of abuse from relatives or acquaintances.
