Namibia reburies six liberation heroes, establishes two symbolic graves

A 12-year process culminated Monday with the reinterment of six liberation struggle heroes at Heroes’ Acre and the establishment of symbolic graves for two others whose remains.

President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah announced the completion of a process to bring liberation struggle heroes home, which began in 2014. The remains of Eliaser Inamutwika Tuhadeleni, Hafeni Immanuel Shifidi, Nduuvu Fillemon Nangolo, Immanuel Nathaniel Maxuilili, Simon Petrus Niilenge, and Festus Nehale were reinterred at Heroes’ Acre. Symbolic graves were established for Brendan Kangongolo Simbwaye and Tobias Hainyeko.

Nandi-Ndaitwah described the reinterment as an act of national remembrance and restorative justice, correcting historical wrongs against heroes buried far from home or in unmarked graves. The Nehale family, speaking through Armas Nandjigua, described the return of Festus Nehale, repatriated from South Africa, as the restoration of dignity and recognition of his contribution to the liberation struggle.

Families of those with symbolic graves, including the Hainyeko and Simbwaye families, used the ceremony to appeal for intensified efforts to locate the missing remains. South Africa’s Minister of Defence and Military Veterans, Angelina Motsekga, representing President Cyril Ramaphosa, pledged to communicate these concerns to the South African government, acknowledging the ongoing pain of missing liberation fighters.

Why it mattersThe reburial and symbolic graves provide closure and a place of remembrance for families of liberation heroes, addressing historical injustices.

Prime Minister accelerates Etaka Canal development with community partnerships

Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare proposed a community-led infrastructure strategy to expedite the concrete development of the 130-kilometer Etaka-Uuvudhiya Canal, aiming to make it.

Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare advocated for a strategic partnership between the government and traditional authorities to convert the Etaka-Uuvudhiya Canal into a reliable water source for local communities and agricultural development. Speaking at a high-level consultation with Omukwaniilwa Johannes Mupiya and senior headmen, Ngurare introduced a community-led infrastructure strategy to expedite the canal’s concrete development.

The Prime Minister suggested that each traditional authority along the canal identify and manage the development of at least five kilometers, with government backing through the Food-for-Work and Cash-for-Work programs. This initiative aims to enhance water flow and retention in the 130-kilometer canal, which was built around 1974 and connects the Olushandja Dam to natural pans in Uuvudhiya.

Ngurare urged the Ongandjera Traditional Authority to spearhead the initiative by designating its five-kilometer section and initiating work promptly. He also encouraged other traditional authorities, including Uukolonkadhi, Uukwaluudhi, and Uukwambi, to adopt similar measures, linking this effort to broader plans to utilize water from Calueque in Angola for agricultural development and national food security.

Why it mattersLocal communities and farmers in the Omusati, Oshana, and Oshikoto regions are directly affected by the proposed rehabilitation and management of the Etaka Canal, which provides essential drought relief and agricultural support.

Namibian Deputy Minister urges entrepreneurs to expand regionally using digital tools

Deputy Minister Dino Ballotti encouraged Namibian businesses to leverage digital technology for growth beyond the country's three million consumers, targeting markets like.

Deputy Minister of Education, Innovation, Youth, Sports, Arts and Culture Dino Ballotti stated that Namibia's small population should not limit business ambitions. Speaking at the Communications Regulatory Authority of Namibia’s (CRAN) Digital Transformation and Entrepreneurship Breakfast in Oshakati, Ballotti advised entrepreneurs to use digital platforms, electronic payments, cloud services, artificial intelligence, and social media to reach customers in neighboring countries.

Ballotti emphasized that digital technology redefines market boundaries, allowing businesses to serve clients in Lusaka or Johannesburg without needing a physical branch. However, he cautioned that regional expansion requires capital, market knowledge, reliable logistics, appropriate payment systems, intellectual property protection, regulatory compliance, and an understanding of diverse consumer cultures, including language differences like Portuguese in Angola.

The Deputy Minister highlighted the government's role in creating conditions for entrepreneurs to experiment and grow, rather than guaranteeing success. CRAN's proposed regulatory sandbox is seen as significant for allowing innovators to test products in a controlled environment, reducing risk and cost. CRAN chief executive Emilia Nghikembua also stressed that connectivity alone is insufficient; it must enable economic participation, such as international payments and secure online sales for small businesses.

Why it mattersNamibian entrepreneurs are directly affected by the call to expand regionally and the government's efforts to create a supportive regulatory environment for digital business growth.

Ministry defends fuel deal after jet fuel shortage

Namibia's Ministry of Mines and Energy stated that a recent jet A-1 fuel shortage is due to a commercial issue, not the government's supply agreement with Vitol.

A jet A-1 fuel shortage has affected Namibia since last week, causing Lufthansa Cargo to suspend flights and Discover Airlines to divert to Luanda for refueling. The Ministry of Mines and Energy clarified that the issue stems from a commercial arrangement between two oil marketing companies, Puma and Vivo, after a jet fuel consignment failed inspection at Walvis Bay.

Energy Minister Modestus Amutse stated on Monday that the government's three-month fuel supply deal with Vitol, Vivo’s parent company, is not responsible for the shortage. That agreement specifically covers petrol and diesel to ensure supply continuity and affordability, not jet A-1 fuel. The next jet fuel shipment is expected on Saturday.

Amutse is considering including jet A-1 fuel in the regulatory and supply security framework to prevent future disruptions. Nelson Kalangula, the Independent Patriots for Change’s shadow transport minister, criticized the incident for exposing weaknesses in the aviation fuel sector and damaging Namibia’s aviation reputation.

Why it mattersThe jet fuel shortage directly impacts airlines operating in Namibia, causing flight disruptions and potential economic consequences for the country's aviation sector.
