Eliaser Iyambo heads Namibia Gambling Board

Iyambo, with over 26 years in law enforcement, will lead the board from November 2026 to October 2029.

Eliaser Iyambo will chair the Gambling Board of Namibia for a three-year term from November 2026 to October 2029.

Iyambo, who has over 26 years of experience in law enforcement and criminal justice, will be supported by vice-chairperson Anastasia Siteketa and board members Tudiminapo Shindume, Rachel Mundilo, Dr Penehafo Shindume, and Dr Elsie Hindjou.

Minister of Environment, Forestry and Tourism Indileni Daniel urged the new board to uphold transparency and accountability, stating her door is open for support during the appointment ceremony on Friday.

Why it mattersEliaser Iyambo's appointment as chairperson of the Gambling Board affects national development and sustainable solutions.

Namibian banks face cash access challenges

Customers struggle with unavailable cash and non-functional ATMs during busy holiday shopping periods.

August and December are busy months in Namibia, especially in northern towns like Omafo, as people travel and shop, increasing demand for banking services. Customers face long queues and find ATMs without cash or out of order, despite cash remaining essential for daily transactions.

Hidipo Hamata, who has personally experienced ATM failures, stated that banks should use transaction data to anticipate demand and replenish machines before problems arise. He noted that unreliable banking affects taxi operators, informal traders, and small businesses that depend on customer payments.

Hamata emphasized that banks must ensure cash availability and protect customer privacy at ATMs, stating that trust in banking services comes with responsibility. He believes digital payments should not excuse poor cash availability.

Why it mattersNamibians face difficulties accessing their money during peak shopping seasons, impacting their ability to pay for essential goods and services.

Windhoek faces N$932.7 million in unpaid bills

The City of Windhoek is conducting mass disconnections for accounts in arrears for over 30 days.

The City of Windhoek is owed N$932.7 million in outstanding municipal bills and has begun a mass disconnection exercise. This action targets accounts that have been in arrears for at least 30 days, as part of the municipality’s approved credit control policy to recover revenue for operations and essential services.

City officials stated the disconnection exercise is a lawful credit control measure, not a punishment or a sign of governance failure, aimed at recovering N$932.7 million in debt. Revenue collected funds bulk water and electricity purchases, refuse removal, and infrastructure maintenance, with non-payment risking delays in repairs and service reliability.

Residents facing financial difficulties can negotiate payment arrangements or apply for debt relief, which has already seen N$383.9 million written off between 2023 and 2025. The municipality also rejected claims of a defective billing system, stating an anomaly in 2024 affected few accounts and was corrected.

Why it mattersResidents of Windhoek face service disconnections and potential legal action if they do not pay their municipal bills.

Windhoek City Sporting Club rebrands as Pamwe

The club aims for professionalisation and commercial sustainability with new pillars including women's football and facilities.

Pamwe Sporting Club has officially launched its new identity, changing its name from Windhoek City Sporting Club. The rebranding took place on Wednesday at the club’s home ground, the Pionier Boys School Sports Ground. Club president Ndafelenga Ndafyaalako announced an ambitious vision focused on professionalisation, youth development, and long-term commercial sustainability.

Ndafyaalako stated the rebranding signifies a move towards a professionally managed and commercially independent institution, with key development pillars being women’s senior football, youth football, and facilities management. The women’s programme has already seen players earn national team call-ups, secure scholarships, and win a Cosafa tournament medal. The club is seeking corporate partnerships for player welfare, equipment, and infrastructure upgrades.

The club's journey began as Ramblers, evolving into Windhoek City Sporting Club for the 2024/2025 season. The new name, Pamwe, meaning 'together', reflects the team's solidarity, which contributed to reaching the semi-finals of the 2026 Namibia Football Association Cup. Head coach Andrew Gabriel highlighted the opportunity to build on achievements and improve football structures, aiming to develop disciplined athletes and leaders.

Why it mattersNamibian football players and the club's future development are directly affected by this rebranding and strategic shift.

Politics · 2026-11-08

President Nandi-Ndaitwah meets Swakopmund residents

The Head of State engaged citizens on Friday about housing, jobs, and services.

President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah met with residents of Swakopmund and surrounding communities on Friday. The town hall meeting, facilitated by Erongo Governor Natalia /Goagoses, provided a platform for citizens to raise concerns directly with the Head of State. This engagement comes as Namibia navigates economic challenges, unemployment, and expectations surrounding its oil and gas industry.

The meeting addressed longstanding issues including housing, land delivery, municipal services, and economic opportunities, particularly for young people. Governor /Goagoses highlighted the importance of governors acting as bridges between national leadership and communities. The President's participation offers an opportunity to demonstrate government accessibility and understanding of citizens' realities.

Swakopmund and the Erongo region are vital to Namibia's mining, tourism, fishing, and energy sectors. The discussions are expected to cover the distribution of economic benefits from investments and the future of the fishing industry. The success of the meeting will be measured by whether the raised concerns lead to tangible improvements and decisions.

Why it mattersNamibians in Swakopmund and surrounding areas directly raised concerns about housing, jobs, and services with their President.
