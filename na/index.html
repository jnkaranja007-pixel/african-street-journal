Bank of Namibia raises repo rate by 25 basis points

Annual home loan repayments increase by N$4 000 as prime lending rate hits 10.50%

The Bank of Namibia increased its repo rate by 25 basis points to 7.00% on 28 October 2026, raising the prime lending rate to 10.50%. This move will increase annual repayments on a typical N$1 million home loan by approximately N$4 000 since the start of the current interest rate hiking cycle. The central bank’s decision aims to protect the Namibia dollar’s peg with the South African rand and manage inflation.

Governor Ebson Uanguta announced the decision following a monetary policy review, noting it restores the traditional 25-basis-point gap between Namibia’s repo rate and South Africa’s policy rate. This helps limit capital outflows and supports currency stability. Households are already facing higher fuel and electricity costs, with petrol prices increasing by N$1.50 per litre and NamPower tariffs by 3.7% in October.

The agricultural sector faces pressure from higher borrowing costs, input prices, and drought risks. The Bank of Namibia forecasts 2.1% economic growth for 2026, but rising costs may affect spending. Analysts expect rates to remain between 10.50% and 10.75% through 2027, with potential cuts later in the year.

Why it mattersNamibian households with home loans will see their annual repayments increase due to the central bank's repo rate hike.

Old Mutual raises SEED prize pool to N$650 000

15 entrepreneurs will now receive financial support across five categories in the 2026 programme.

The Old Mutual Foundation has increased the 2026 SEED: Seasons prize money to N$650 000, recognizing more entrepreneurs across Namibia. This expansion means 15 entrepreneurs will receive financial support, with first, second, and third place winners in each of the five categories receiving N$60 000, N$40 000, and N$30 000 respectively.

Mignon du Preez, Old Mutual Namibia group marketing, public affairs and sustainability executive, stated that the strong interest and quality of applications inspired the broadened prize structure. Applications for the programme, which supports youth entrepreneurship, environmental sustainability, sport, creative industries, and manufacturing, close on October 30, 2026.

The OM SEED initiative, standing for Old Mutual Sustainable Economic Empowerment Drive, aims to equip entrepreneurs with knowledge, skills, and resources. Application forms are available at Old Mutual branches and via QR codes on social media platforms.

Why it mattersEntrepreneurs across Namibia will benefit from increased financial support and recognition for their businesses.

Namibia construction bill faces calls for action

The Construction Industries Federation of Namibia urges concrete steps as the long-awaited bill nears Cabinet submission.

The Construction Industries Federation of Namibia (CIF) is calling for concrete action on the construction industries bill, which works minister Veikko Nekundi says is nearing submission to the Cabinet’s legislation committee. CIF chief executive Bärbel Kirchner stated that the announcement renews hopes for establishing a construction council, over a decade after the bill was first presented to parliament. Kirchner noted previous unfulfilled promises warrant caution, recalling an earlier bill that reached the National Assembly in 2010.

Kirchner mentioned that the government promised a construction council by the end of the 2019/20 financial year, a commitment also stated in parliament by former prime minister Saara Kuugongelwa-Amadhila. Further legislative work followed consultations facilitated by then works deputy minister James Sankwasa, with a revised draft reaching the Cabinet in 2022. The CIF hopes this renewed momentum will lead to legislation and implementation, enabling measures like contractor registration and categorisation to match project sizes with local capacity.

The CIF believes an effective council could have supported measures like dividing large contracts into manageable lots to widen participation and strengthen local businesses. The federation also welcomed the local contractors development programme for SMEs. Kirchner highlighted skills shortages at technical, supervisory, and senior levels, arguing that skills development must extend beyond small contractors to prepare the sector for new projects and retain experienced personnel.

Why it mattersNamibian construction businesses and their employees are affected by the potential establishment of a construction council and the implementation of measures to support local contractors and address skills shortages.

Namibia seeks stronger business ties with Russia

President Nandi-Ndaitwah called for joint ventures and technology transfer at the Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow.

Namibian President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah urged stronger business-to-business partnerships with Russia to boost investment and economic benefits. Speaking at the Third Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow on Thursday, she called for joint ventures, technology transfer, and skills development to move beyond political ties into tangible economic outcomes.

The President stated that Namibia seeks partnerships to expand local production, promote value addition, and strengthen regional value chains, particularly in agriculture, energy, industry, and technology. She emphasized that historical solidarity should form the basis for a more dynamic partnership focused on practical results like industrialisation and local value addition.

Namibia also seeks cooperation in modern farming technologies, improved market access, and climate-resilient agriculture, including livestock development and disease control. Enhanced veterinary cooperation and capacity-building are seen as essential, alongside efforts to close Africa’s water investment gap and implement the Namib Declaration.

Why it mattersNamibian businesses and the economy will benefit from new investment, technology transfer, and expanded production capacity through partnerships with Russia.

Namibia Breweries partners launch N$13 million water security project

The five-year Swakop Basin Collective Action Project aims to secure 386 million litres of water annually by 2030.

Namibia Breweries Limited (NBL), WWF Namibia, and the Global Water Challenge have signed a N$13 million, five-year agreement to improve water security in the Swakop River Basin. This initiative, also known as the NBL Water Balancing Project, aims to secure approximately 386 million litres of annual water benefits by 2030 through land restoration and improved management across 153 hectares.

The project brings together government, civil society, and the private sector to address water challenges and enhance climate resilience. Waldemar von Lieres, managing director of NBL, stated that the company has invested over N$35 million since 2015 in reducing its own water footprint. The Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries, Water and Land Reform remains committed to strengthening water security through sustainable management.

Interventions by 2030 will include managed aquifer recharge, removal of invasive alien plants, and improved water governance. This includes developing a Sub-Basin Water Allocation and Abstraction Plan. NBL is working towards reducing its water use to 2.4 litres per litre of product by 2030 under its Brew a Better Namibia strategy.

Why it mattersCommunities, farms, and industries across central Namibia will benefit from strengthened water security in the Swakop River Basin.
