Namibia's chief vet rallies //Kharas leaders on FMD

Dr Kennedy Shoombe toured the region to assess containment strategies and boost veterinary teams battling the outbreak.

Namibia’s Chief Veterinary Officer Dr Kennedy Shoombe visited the //Kharas region this past weekend to assess the Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) outbreak response. Shoombe, accompanied by officials from the Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries, Water and Land Reform, began his tour in Keetmanshoop on Friday. The mission aimed to audit containment strategies and address operational challenges faced by veterinary teams since the outbreak was confirmed on 23 September, affecting 18 farm units.

Since the outbreak, the Directorate of Veterinary Services has implemented strict measures including movement bans and vaccination campaigns. Shoombe met with //Kharas Regional Council Chairperson Gerrit Witbooi to discuss surveillance data and the role of regional governance in community mobilisation. He also met with //Kharas Regional Police Commander Deputy Commissioner Gertruida !Kharuxab to streamline joint operations between the police and veterinary officials at roadblocks.

Shoombe also met with personnel at the Keetmanshoop State Veterinary Office to hear about their logistical challenges during surveillance, blood sampling, and vaccination drives. The engagement aimed to strengthen coordination between the ministry and regional leadership to ensure public cooperation with quarantine protocols and prevent the virus from spreading northward, which could threaten Namibia’s livestock export industry.

Why it mattersThe Chief Veterinary Officer is assessing containment strategies and reinforcing collaboration between government agencies and regional leaders to control the Foot-and-Mouth Disease outbreak affecting 18 farm units.

DBN targets US$750m in climate finance by 2029

The Development Bank of Namibia aims to raise the money through partnerships with development lenders and private investors, against a national climate investment need.

The Development Bank of Namibia (DBN) plans to raise US$750 million in climate finance between 2027 and 2029. It will seek the money through partnerships with development finance institutions and private-sector investors. The bank set out the strategy at the 31st Conference of the Parties (COP31), positioning itself as a platform for mobilising capital for Namibia's climate needs.

According to DBN's COP31 investor briefing, Namibia faces climate investment requirements of US$14.07 billion, with 88% of the funding conditional on international support. The bank is seeking US$340 million in co-financing from development finance institutions and private investors. The proposed structure also includes US$250 million in concessional financing from the Green Climate Fund (GCF), US$150 million in direct DBN debt and US$10 million in project preparation grants.

The strategy will run through three investment platforms covering water, food and energy systems, the green energy transition, and climate-resilient infrastructure. The bank says they are aligned with Namibia's Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC 3.0) and the Sixth National Development Plan (NDP6). Since entering renewable energy in 2014, DBN has provided cumulative financing of US$1.58 billion, supporting combined generation capacity of 81.6 megawatts as of March 2026.

Why it mattersDevelopers and lenders can now apply to DBN for a share of the climate money, which the bank says will go to water, energy and infrastructure projects.

Namibia urges UN to back Western Sahara's self-determination

Ambassador Penda Naanda asked the Security Council to restart talks aimed at a peaceful settlement of the dispute.

In New York on Monday, Namibia's Permanent Representative to the United Nations, Ambassador Penda Naanda, asked the UN to uphold the right of Western Sahara's people to decide their own future. Namibia framed the appeal around its own liberation history, arguing that Africa's last remaining colony should be allowed to determine its status.

Naanda made the case during a General Assembly debate, where he urged the Security Council to resume efforts towards a peaceful resolution of the Western Sahara question. The call put Namibia's voice behind a demand for a political settlement rather than a continued stalemate.

The debate took place in New York on Monday, and the report gives no further outcome or timetable for the Security Council's response.

Why it mattersThe appeal puts Namibia on record in a General Assembly debate over whether the Security Council will restart talks on Western Sahara.

Mupetami wins Namibia sport journalist award for sixth time

Journalist Limba Mupetami of The New Era has been named sport journalist of the year at the Namibia Annual Sports Awards, her sixth win.

Limba Mupetami, a multi-award-winning journalist with The New Era, has been named sport journalist of the year at the Namibia Annual Sports Awards for the sixth time. She previously won the honour in 2018, 2019, 2021, 2024 and 2025.

The awards are organised by the Namibia Sports Commission to recognise excellence in Namibian sport, honouring athletes as well as individuals and organisations who help sport grow. Speaking to The Namibian on Sunday, Mupetami said she was humbled and honoured, adding that every story, challenge and late night had been part of her journey.

This month the Africa Sports Ventures Group also announced her nomination for its most influential African women in sports awards, which celebrate women transforming sport across Africa and the diaspora.

Why it mattersMupetami's recognition as a journalist covering Namibian sport adds to her standing for the continental women-in-sports award that she has been nominated for.
