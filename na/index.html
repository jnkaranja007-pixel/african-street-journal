Namibia launches N$41.6 billion Dangote fuel project

President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah commissioned the Walvis Bay tank farm and pipeline, marking a fast industrial breakthrough.

Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare hailed the N$41.6 billion Dangote Namibia Tank Farm and regional pipeline project, commissioned by President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah at Walvis Bay, as a monumental triumph. This fuel infrastructure project is one of the fastest and largest industrial breakthroughs in Namibia’s modern history, with its origins in August 2025 bilateral talks.

Ngurare stated on social media that the project demonstrates visionary leadership and political commitment, noting that the investment was secured and the project launched in just over a year. He emphasized that the partnership leverages internal African wealth to solve energy challenges, aligning with Namibia's diplomatic agenda at the 28th Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting 2026 in Antigua and Barbuda.

The Dangote project supports Namibia's Sixth National Development Plan (NDP6) by transforming the country into an industrial hub with robust infrastructure and energy self-sufficiency. The tank farm will serve as a strategic fuel reserve, insulating Namibia and neighbors from global supply chain shocks, while the project is set to create thousands of direct and indirect jobs.

Why it mattersThe N$41.6 billion Dangote fuel project at Walvis Bay will create thousands of jobs and provide energy security for Namibia and its neighbors.

Namibia cuts hake quota by 13%

The government reduced the Total Allowable Catch for the 2026/2027 season to 116 000 tonnes due to declining fish stocks.

Namibia's government has reduced the hake Total Allowable Catch for the 2026/2027 fishing season by 13%, cutting it by 17 000 tonnes to 116 000 tonnes. This decision follows reports of declining fish stocks and aims to ensure the long-term sustainability of marine resources.

The ministry of agriculture, fisheries, water and land reform stated the reduction is a precautionary measure for fisheries management. The Rock Lobster TAC remains unchanged at 180 tonnes. Fishing right holders whose rights expire during the season will receive prorated allocations based on remaining validity periods.

The ministry is consulting stakeholders on a long-term objective of achieving 100% wet fish allocation to boost onshore value addition and employment. The government also urged fishing companies to increase fish supplies to the domestic market due to potential meat shortages from the ongoing foot-and-mouth disease outbreak.

Why it mattersNamibian fishers and the fishing industry will face reduced catches and potential changes to fishing rights, impacting livelihoods and business operations.

Windhoek City disconnects water and electricity over N$932.7m debt

City CEO Moses Matyayi stated disconnections are a credit control measure, not punishment for residents.

The City of Windhoek is disconnecting water and electricity services to recover N$932.7 million in unpaid bills. City CEO Moses Matyayi stated the disconnections are a legal measure under the Credit Control Policy, not an act to punish residents.

Shaun Gariseb of the Khomas Residents and Ratepayers Association launched a social media campaign calling for a payment boycott, accusing the City of billing errors and escalating debts. Matyayi warned that a civic strike risks crippling infrastructure and municipal operations.

Matyayi insisted the disconnections are a vital credit control intervention to recover public funds, rejecting claims of punitive action or administrative failure. The City plans to intensify these disconnections in the coming weeks.

Why it mattersWindhoek residents face disconnection of essential services like water and electricity due to outstanding municipal debts.

Namibia cuts spending to curb debt growth

Government revises expenditure down by 0.9% to N$105 billion amid a N$1.5 billion revenue shortfall.

Namibia's government has reduced its total expenditure by 0.9% to N$105 billion for the 2026/27 financial year to control debt. Finance Minister Ericah Shafudah announced on Wednesday that instead of borrowing more to cover a N$1.5 billion revenue shortfall, funds were reallocated from underperforming projects. This move aims to contain debt growth and manage rising interest payments that limit development funding.

Shafudah stated in Parliament that N$685 million was redirected from non-performing projects and offices. The reallocated funds will support urgent priorities including N$179 million for foot-and-mouth disease containment, grain reserves, the public servants’ medical aid scheme, and the Contingency Fund. She emphasized that containing the disease outbreak is important for protecting beef export access to the European Union and the United Kingdom.

The mid-year budget review adjusts spending priorities due to changing economic conditions. Namibia, as a lower-middle-income economy, focuses on productivity, skills development, technology, and diversification for sustained growth. Bilateral trade negotiations are underway to explore resuming livestock and livestock product exports.

Why it mattersNamibian citizens will be affected by reduced government spending on projects and the reallocation of funds to address immediate concerns like disease outbreaks and essential services.
