Namibia secures N$940m for green hydrogen projects

The Green Climate Fund will channel US$56 million to support industrial decarbonisation and green ammonia production.

Namibia has secured N$940 million (US$56 million) in climate financing from the Green Climate Fund (GCF) to support green hydrogen and associated industrial projects. The funding, part of US$140 million approved for Climate Investor Three, will be channelled through the SDG Namibia One Fund. This financing aims to support early-stage project development and construction phases for green hydrogen production, green ammonia, and green methanol.

The financing will be managed by Namibia Energy Transition Fund Managers, a partnership including the Environmental Investment Fund of Namibia (EIF). EIF chief executive Benedict Libanda stated the financing addresses capital and technical needs for Namibia’s green hydrogen industry, aiming to increase local participation. The SDG Namibia One Fund has already secured development funding agreements for four projects, including the Hyphen green hydrogen project and HyIron’s green iron project.

The wider Southern African facility aims to mobilise US$1.2 billion across Namibia and South Africa, supporting 30 to 40 projects and installing approximately 1.52 gigawatts of renewable energy capacity. This blended finance model combines public and private capital to reduce investment risks and attract commercial investors. Climate Investor Three has a global fundraising target of US$1.2 billion.

Why it mattersNamibian businesses and industries will receive financial support to develop green hydrogen production and associated downstream industries, aiding decarbonisation efforts.

Bank of Namibia offers farmers 24-month loan relief

Agricultural borrowers affected by drought and disease can restructure debt and defer payments for up to two years.

The Bank of Namibia introduced a 24-month relief framework to aid farmers and agricultural businesses impacted by drought and disease. The measures aim to ease loan repayment pressures for those facing reduced income and cash flow issues. Commercial banks must offer eligible borrowers options for debt restructuring, repayment deferrals, and recovery financing.

Under Determination BID-42, banks can grant a repayment holiday of up to 24 months on the principal of performing loans for working capital, term loans, and equipment financing held as of September 22, 2026. Banks are prohibited from charging penalty interest or additional fees for these holidays. A burden-sharing principle requires banks to ensure borrowers are not financially worse off regarding interest rates or credit costs.

The framework also allows for distressed loan restructuring, including extending repayment periods or reducing interest rates for those already struggling. New Agricultural Recovery Financing facilities may be provided on preferential terms. Borrowers receiving relief will continue to be reported as performing to credit bureaus, protecting their credit records.

Why it mattersFarmers and agricultural businesses in Namibia can access up to 24 months of relief on loan principal repayments due to drought and disease.

Cran appoints Doreen Zaaruka as human capital executive

Zaaruka brings extensive experience in human resources and organisational development to the communications regulator.

The Communications Regulatory Authority of Namibia (Cran) appointed Doreen Zaaruka as its executive for human capital, effective 1 November 2026. Zaaruka previously served as strategic executive for human capital and corporate services at the Otjiwarongo municipality.

Zaaruka joins Cran with experience in human resources management, organisational development, strategic leadership, and performance management. Cran chief executive Emilia Nghikembua stated that Zaaruka’s appointment reflects the authority’s commitment to attracting high-calibre leadership. Nghikembua added that Zaaruka’s experience will be invaluable in building a high-performing organisation.

Zaaruka said she is honoured to join Cran and looks forward to strengthening employee capabilities and organisational effectiveness. In her new role, she will oversee Cran’s human capital function, including talent management and workforce planning. Zaaruka holds a Master of Leadership and Change Management and is a Certified Balanced Scorecard Professional.

Why it mattersNamibians working in the communications sector will be affected by Zaaruka's oversight of employee growth and organisational effectiveness at Cran.

Windhoek approves land for convention centre, hotel, stadium

The City Council also approved land for 104 residential plots in Cimbebasia Extension 10.

The City of Windhoek has approved land for a 5,000-seat international convention centre, a 510-room hotel, and a sports hub. The development, proposed by the Namibia Investment and Development Agency, will be located in Venus Township and include a shopping mall and residential properties. The City Council also approved land for a sports hub with a 4,000-seat stadium, an African Football Museum, and a football academy.

This mixed-use development aims to boost Windhoek's capacity for international conferences and sporting events, attracting investment and tourism. The municipality anticipates job creation during construction and operation, benefiting local contractors and suppliers. The City Council also approved Cimbebasia Extension 10, covering 63,175 square metres, to increase residential land availability.

Cimbebasia Extension 10 will comprise 107 erven, including 99 residential plots, with approximately 104 erven expected for public sale. The development is intended to expand housing opportunities as Windhoek faces growing demand amid population growth. The City has not disclosed the investment value or construction timelines for the convention centre and sports hub.

Why it mattersNamibians seeking housing will have more residential plots available in Windhoek.

Namibia drills 12 boreholes in Oshikoto region

Six boreholes are fully installed to combat water scarcity affecting agricultural productivity and food security.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries, Water and Land Reform drilled 12 boreholes in Namibia's Oshikoto region during the 2025/2026 Financial Year. Six of these boreholes are now fully installed, with a short pipeline also constructed, to address persistent water scarcity. Deputy Minister Ruthy Masake announced these developments on Friday at the 2026 World Food Day commemoration in Onyaanya.

Masake stated that these interventions, a collaboration with the Oshikoto Regional Council and development partners, aim to improve water access and reduce community travel distances. She highlighted that Oshikoto faces significant water scarcity, impacting agricultural productivity, food security, and economic development, and exacerbating the vulnerability of food-insecure communities, especially during droughts.

These boreholes are part of broader government efforts to build resilience in agrifood systems, including initiatives like the Strategy for the Transformation of the Agri-food Sector and the Green Scheme Programme. Masake acknowledged farmers' contributions to food security and stressed that innovation, including traditional knowledge, is important for nourishing tomorrow.

Why it mattersThe drilling of 12 boreholes in the Oshikoto region aims to improve water availability for agricultural productivity and food security, directly benefiting communal farmers and livestock management.
