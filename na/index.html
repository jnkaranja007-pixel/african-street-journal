Keetmanshoop Municipality Fails to Pay October Salaries

The minister confirmed the municipality's inability to pay staff, citing mismanagement and refusing a bailout.

Keetmanshoop municipality is unable to pay its employees for October, confirmed Urban and Rural Development Minister James Sankwasa. The municipality faces nearly N$100 million in debt to NamWater and has outstanding resident payments exceeding N$84 million. Sankwasa stated the ministry will not provide a bailout, attributing the crisis to the council's actions and questioning the recent travel of three councillors to Cape Town without approval.

The financial distress follows the municipality's use of Build Together Project funds and money owed by the Keetmanshoop Electricity Business Unit to cover September salaries for over 250 employees. A council resolution from September 21, 2026, revealed insufficient funds in the Main Cheque Account to meet the payroll deadline. This temporary measure has not resolved the ongoing financial crisis, leading to the current inability to pay October wages.

Sources suggest that campaign promises made by the Landless People’s Movement during the 2020 elections may have contributed to the debt, with allegations that some residents stopped paying utility bills after being promised free services. The municipality's debt to NamWater has significantly increased since before the Covid-19 pandemic, partly due to providing water as a public health measure to suspended accounts.

Why it mattersMunicipal employees are affected because they will not receive their October salaries.

FirstRand Namibia Shareholders Approve All AGM Resolutions

Shareholders overwhelmingly backed financial statements, dividends, and director appointments at the bank's annual general meeting.

FirstRand Namibia shareholders have overwhelmingly approved all ordinary resolutions presented at the bank's annual general meeting on October 16, 2026. The resolutions included the adoption of the annual financial statements for the year ending June 30, 2026, and the confirmation of dividends, both receiving support from 88.27% of the shares voted. No votes were cast against these key financial approvals.

The meeting also saw the re-election of non-executive director Samantha Balsdon and independent non-executive director Markus Johannes Lubbe, alongside the appointment of Conville Raymond Britz to fill a vacancy. While most director appointments and the reappointment of external auditors garnered strong support, the appointment of Audit and Risk Committee members showed some division, with Lubbe's committee appointment receiving 83.38% approval and 4.89% against.

Further resolutions passed included director remuneration for the 2026 and 2027 financial years, each receiving 88.18% support, and the approval of the Reward Philosophy and Principles Policy with 88.25% backing. The final resolution, authorizing the signing of documents, was also approved by 88.27% of the shares voted, indicating broad shareholder consensus on the bank's governance and operational matters.

Kavango East Youth Learn Climate-Smart Farming Skills

A new center in Ndiyona Constituency aims to equip young people with practical agricultural knowledge for sustainable production and entrepreneurship.

Young people in Namibia's Kavango East region will gain hands-on agricultural experience at the newly opened Linus Shashipapo Climate Change Action Centre. The facility, located in Ndiyona Constituency, is designed to bridge climate action with employment and entrepreneurship opportunities for youth. Education ministry officials stated the center could become a regional hub for practical learning, moving beyond traditional classroom instruction.

The center will expose participants to horticulture, sustainable production methods, and climate-smart technologies, enabling them to learn by doing. This initiative aims to help young individuals acquire skills for establishing their own productive ventures while learning to maintain food production amidst changing climate conditions. Officials emphasized that the center's success relies on continued cooperation among involved institutions and stakeholders.

The project is intended to demonstrate how sustainable agriculture can create community opportunities and address environmental challenges. The education ministry plans to document the center's outcomes to assess if the model can be replicated in other Namibian areas, particularly those with limited access to land, training, technology, and markets for young people.

Why it mattersYoung people in Kavango East are affected by gaining practical skills for climate-smart agriculture and potential entrepreneurship.

Kalangula Questions N$70 Million Pedestrian Bridge Costs

An opposition politician is demanding a detailed breakdown of the estimated N$70 million cost for two pedestrian bridges.

Nelson Kalangula, shadow minister for works and transport for the Independent Patriots for Change, has questioned the government's N$70 million estimate for two pedestrian bridges along Windhoek's Western Bypass. Kalangula is demanding a detailed expenditure breakdown from Works and Transport Minister Veikko Nekundi, citing N$35 million per bridge as an "unbelievable" cost amid other national development needs. Designs for the bridges have been approved, with construction slated to begin between November 19 and December 1, 2026.

Minister Nekundi has defended the estimate, stating the government is open to cheaper alternatives that meet infrastructure requirements. Kalangula seeks an itemized list of costs, including materials, labor, environmental assessments, and engineering fees. He also wants a comparison with similar past projects, such as the bridge near China Town, and details on safeguards against cost overruns and delays common in state infrastructure projects.

The proposed bridges follow safety concerns on the Western Bypass, where fatal crashes prompted the installation of speed humps as a temporary measure. Nekundi reported zero pedestrian crashes since the humps were installed, a significant reduction from previous monthly incidents. However, Kalangula described the speed humps as a costly, congestion-causing intervention that public perception suggests was a manufactured crisis to justify expensive tenders.
