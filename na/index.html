Speaker defers motion to remove her from National Assembly post

National Assembly Speaker Saara Kuugongelwa-Amadhila deferred a motion seeking her removal, citing procedural concerns about ruling on her own position.

National Assembly Speaker Saara Kuugongelwa-Amadhila has postponed a motion by Independent Patriots for Change (IPC) MP Michael Mulunga to remove her from her post. Kuugongelwa-Amadhila stated on Wednesday that she needed to consider if the motion could be tabled, as Mulunga was appealing to the very person he sought to remove. The motion, described as the first of its kind since independence, concerns the speaker's duty to act fairly and impartially in applying parliamentary rules.

Mulunga argued that the speaker had failed to ensure the participation of all members, citing instances where proceedings collapsed due to rulings and opposition parties withdrew over conduct during a vote. He contended that parliamentary rules require the speaker to apply them evenly. The motion invoked Article 51(2) of the Namibian Constitution, which allows for the speaker's removal by a National Assembly resolution.

The parliamentary session descended into chaos, leading Prime Minister Elijah Ngurare to move for adjournment. This followed a confrontation the previous day where Affirmative Repositioning (AR) leader Job Amupanda was escorted out and criticized the speaker's leadership. Amupanda accused the speaker of managing the legislature emotionally rather than procedurally and called for new elections, claiming his movement could significantly reduce the ruling party's seats.

Why it mattersParliamentary proceedings and the functioning of the National Assembly are directly affected by the speaker's ability to preside impartially and the procedural challenges raised.

Farmer and Businessman Dies in Aircraft Crash

Jan-Hendrik ‘Johnny’ Hanekom, a respected farmer and owner of Johnny’s Auto, died Sunday after his aircraft crashed on his farm.

The farming and business communities in the Hardap and ||Kharas regions are mourning the death of Jan-Hendrik ‘Johnny’ Hanekom, 69, who died this weekend. Hanekom, a farmer in the Maltahöhe district and owner of Johnny’s Auto at Keetmanshoop, died on Sunday morning after an aircraft he was piloting crashed on his farm, Sandrucken. Police have opened an inquest docket into the incident. Hanekom was reportedly flying to patrol farm camps when the crash occurred.

Witnesses reported seeing the aircraft’s flaps folding due to wind pressure before it descended rapidly toward the ground. They rushed to the scene and found Hanekom motionless. The Helmeringhausen Farmers’ Association described Hanekom as a man passionate about farming and livestock, whose death is a significant loss to the industry. The business community in Keetmanshoop remembered him as a respected businessman who was willing to share his knowledge and mentor others.

The Namibia farming community lauded Hanekom as an excellent and respected farmer whose hard work, knowledge, and dedication significantly contributed to the agricultural sector, leaving a great void. The Chamber of Business at Keetmanshoop stated he will be missed for his commitment and willingness to mentor aspiring business persons. Condolences have been extended to his family, relatives, friends, and associates.

Why it mattersThe death of Jan-Hendrik ‘Johnny’ Hanekom affects the farming and business communities through the loss of a respected figure and contributor to both sectors.

Africa Bitcoin Corporation Executives Placed on Leave

Namibian exchange-listed firm Africa Bitcoin Corporation (ABC) has put its CEO and CIO on precautionary leave following South African regulator debarment orders.

Africa Bitcoin Corporation Limited (ABC), listed on the Namibia Securities Exchange, has placed its chief executive officer Warren Wheatley and chief investment officer Akshay Karan on precautionary leave. This action follows debarment orders issued by South Africa's Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) against the two executives and the company’s head of media and investor relations, Tatum Wheatley. The FSCA orders restrict them from involvement in financial products or services.

The three individuals dispute the FSCA's findings and plan to seek reconsideration and suspension of the decisions. However, no suspension order has been granted, leaving the debarment orders effective. ABC stated that these orders do not apply to the company or any other group entity, with no findings or penalties issued against any group entity. As a precaution, ABC removed Wheatley and Karan from all executive and decision-making roles.

Stafford Masie, an existing executive director, has assumed the role of interim CEO, effective August 31, for an initial one-month period, subject to board review. Warren Wheatley has resigned as a director of ABC, and Wheatley, Karan, and Tatum Wheatley have also resigned as directors of wholly owned subsidiary Altvest Credit Opportunities Fund Limited (ACOF).

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as key executives of Africa Bitcoin Corporation are on precautionary leave due to regulatory debarment orders, impacting company leadership and operations.

Expired test kits hinder fatal crash probe

Expired blood alcohol testing kits and a broken breathalyser machine complicate the investigation into a fatal Ondangwa accident.

An Ondangwa fatal accident investigation is hampered by expired blood alcohol testing kits and a malfunctioning breathalyser machine, according to an Oshana police regional commander's report. The crash on Tuesday afternoon resulted in one death and six injuries when a minibus allegedly lost control after colliding with a hatchback, then hitting street vendors and pedestrians. Ananias Mvula, 44, died at the scene, while six women sustained injuries and were taken to hospital.

The minibus driver tested positive for alcohol with a reading of 0.69 mg/L, exceeding the legal limit, but investigators face evidentiary challenges. Police stations in the Oshana and neighbouring Oshikoto regions have expired blood alcohol testing kits, and the regional breathalyser machine is also out of order. The driver was detained overnight due to the incident's seriousness, with police expected to decide on Wednesday whether to continue holding him or release him on a warning.

Cases of culpable homicide and driving under the influence of alcohol have been opened. The injured women, including Cecilia Kandenge with serious head injuries, and Rosalia Simon, Elizabeth Namtanga, Linda Nteka, Johanna Mumbandje, and Hanna Amwele with moderate injuries, were transported by private ambulances. The police report highlights the evidentiary difficulties arising from the expired and malfunctioning equipment.

Why it mattersThe investigation into a fatal road accident is complicated by expired and malfunctioning police equipment, potentially affecting the prosecution of the driver.

Langer Heinrich Mine Secures 21Mlb Uranium Contracts Through 2030

Paladin Energy's operation has a substantial sales base as uranium market fundamentals continue to strengthen, with 44% of contracted volumes at fixed prices.

Paladin Energy's Langer Heinrich Mine has secured contracts for 21 million pounds (Mlb) of uranium through 2030, establishing a strong sales foundation amid strengthening market conditions. The mine sold 4.35Mlb of uranium oxide (U3O8) in the 2026 financial year at an average price of US$70 per pound. Of the contracted volumes for 2026–2030, 44% are subject to base-escalated and fixed pricing, while the remainder is linked to market-related pricing.

The company stated that 85% of Langer Heinrich’s 70Mlb ore reserve is either exposed to market-related prices or remains uncontracted, positioning the mine to benefit from potential price increases. On an ore-reserve basis, 15% of contracted uranium has base-escalated and fixed prices, with 56% subject to market-related prices. The mine, located 85 kilometres northeast of Walvis Bay, has produced over 51Mlb of U3O8 to date.

Namibia contributes approximately 13% to global uranium production, according to Paladin, which cites UxC’s second-quarter 2026 Uranium Market Outlook. Langer Heinrich employs 420 people, 99% of whom are Namibian nationals, and supports local initiatives in youth education, development, health, and community engagement. Uranium is transported by road to Walvis Bay for storage before shipment to global customers, including facilities in the United States, Canada, France, and China.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the mine's substantial contracted sales base and its positioning to benefit from potential increases in uranium prices.
