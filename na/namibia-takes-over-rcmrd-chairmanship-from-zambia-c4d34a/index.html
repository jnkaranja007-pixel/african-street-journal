Namibia takes over RCMRD chairmanship from Zambia

Minister Inge Zaamwani says the chairmanship will concentrate on finances, staff skills and cooperation between member states.

Sources: The Windhoek Observer and The Brief · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Namibia has taken over the chairmanship of the Conference of Ministers of the Regional Centre for Mapping of Resources for Development (RCMRD) from Zambia, which held the post for two years. Agriculture minister Inge Zaamwani assumed the role at the 14th conference in Swakopmund, which runs from 16 to 17 November 2026.

Zaamwani told delegates that member states must pay their contributions in full and on time, and use more of the centre's services. She said geospatial information has become a strategic tool for development, not only a technical function, and that it is needed for decisions on farming, fisheries, water, land and infrastructure. Her remarks were delivered on behalf of President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah, who urged ministers to turn discussions into resolutions with deadlines.

The two-day meeting is also due to consider the centre's Strategic Plan for 2027 to 2030, which will guide its priorities. Zambia's minister of lands, Patrick Mucheleka, said the outgoing council had worked for two years to strengthen the institution, though challenges remain, and thanked Namibia for hosting at short notice after Malawi could not proceed.

Why it mattersMember states that fall behind on their RCMRD contributions will be asked to pay in full and on time, and the strategic plan for 2027 to 2030 will set the centre's direction.
