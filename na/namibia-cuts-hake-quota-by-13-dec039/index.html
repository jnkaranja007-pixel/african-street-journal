Namibia cuts hake quota by 13%

The government reduced the Total Allowable Catch for the 2026/2027 season to 116 000 tonnes due to declining fish stocks.

Source: The Windhoek Observer · 2026-11-09 · 1 min read

Namibia's government has reduced the hake Total Allowable Catch for the 2026/2027 fishing season by 13%, cutting it by 17 000 tonnes to 116 000 tonnes. This decision follows reports of declining fish stocks and aims to ensure the long-term sustainability of marine resources.

The ministry of agriculture, fisheries, water and land reform stated the reduction is a precautionary measure for fisheries management. The Rock Lobster TAC remains unchanged at 180 tonnes. Fishing right holders whose rights expire during the season will receive prorated allocations based on remaining validity periods.

The ministry is consulting stakeholders on a long-term objective of achieving 100% wet fish allocation to boost onshore value addition and employment. The government also urged fishing companies to increase fish supplies to the domestic market due to potential meat shortages from the ongoing foot-and-mouth disease outbreak.

Why it mattersNamibian fishers and the fishing industry will face reduced catches and potential changes to fishing rights, impacting livelihoods and business operations.
