Chad weighs income alternatives for disabled workers displaced from Nguéli bridge

A three-day N’Djamena workshop proposed entrepreneurship, small trade, vocational training and access to finance after restructuring halted workers’ activities.

Sources: Alwihda Info and Tchadinfos · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Chad’s government and partners are considering income alternatives for people with disabilities who formerly worked at Nguéli bridge, after restructuring in the border area interrupted their activities. A three-day workshop on the options ended on Wednesday, 18 November, at the National Library in N’Djamena.

The proposals include entrepreneurship, small-scale trade, vocational training and access to financing. Sitina Abdelmadjid Hassan Haggar, deputy secretary-general of the ministry responsible for social action, represented the ministry at the closing ceremony. Organiser Hassan Manke said recommendations needed to be turned into concrete action.

Galmaï Moussa Abdraman, director-general of the national agency for the protection and promotion of the rights of people with disabilities, promised to follow up on the initiatives. The workshop focused on preserving participants’ financial independence and supporting social inclusion.

Why it mattersFormer workers with disabilities whose activities stopped after border-area restructuring need income alternatives, with follow-up on the workshop’s proposals promised by the national agency director-general.
