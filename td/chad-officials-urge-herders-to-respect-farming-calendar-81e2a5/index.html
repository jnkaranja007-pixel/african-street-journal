Chad officials urge herders to respect farming calendar

Loug-Chari authorities conduct field missions to prevent farmer-herder conflicts by promoting dialogue and awareness.

Source: Alwihda Info · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Authorities in Chad's Loug-Chari department are intensifying awareness campaigns to prevent conflicts between farmers and herders as the agricultural season ends. The departmental secretary-general, Sougouma Hassaballah Ahmat, led a field mission this November to engage nomadic herders and ensure respect for the official timeline, which prohibits early herd arrivals until December 15, 2026.

Accompanied by local officials and traditional leaders, Ahmat visited herder camps in Boudamassa and Moualé-Kousiwahit. The mission aimed to sensitize herders to administrative rules, explaining that a rainfall deficit delayed harvests, necessitating that livestock remain away from cultivated areas. This measure seeks to allow farmers to complete their harvests without damage and avoid tensions.

The delegation stressed three key points: strict adherence to the December 15, 2026, date for herd movement into grazing areas, prevention of damage to crops which often sparks disputes, and strengthening social cohesion between the complementary farming and herding communities.

Why it mattersHerders in Loug-Chari must wait until December 15, 2026, to move livestock into farming areas to prevent crop damage and community disputes.
