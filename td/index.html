Chad officials urge herders to respect transhumance dates

Authorities in Loug-Chari are meeting with nomadic herders to ensure they arrive after December 15.

Officials in Chad's Loug-Chari department are engaging nomadic herders to prevent conflicts with farmers. On November 2, 2026, the acting prefect, Sougouma Hassaballah Ahmat, led a mission to the N'Djamena-Bousso axis and Bousso interior. The goal was to inform herders about the official transhumance calendar, which sets December 15, 2026, as the arrival date for nomadic herders.

The mission included security forces, municipal authorities, and traditional leaders. Hassaballah Ahmat stressed the importance of respecting the December 15 arrival date to allow farmers to finish harvesting, especially after a dry agricultural season. Herders were also asked to avoid entering farmlands before crops are gathered and to maintain peaceful coexistence.

This initiative aligns with a national circular on managing the transhumance season. Herders encountered on the routes appeared receptive to the preventive measures. The authorities emphasized that both agriculture and herding are vital for the local economy and food security.

Why it mattersFarmers in Loug-Chari face potential crop damage and conflict if herders arrive before their harvest is complete.

Health · 2026-11-03

Chad health officials review maternity network

Health leaders in Moyen-Chari, Mandoul, and Logone Oriental are meeting in Sarh to improve emergency obstetric and neonatal care.

Health officials from Chad's Moyen-Chari, Mandoul, and Logone Oriental regions met in Sarh on November 2, 2026, to revise the National Network of Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (SONU) maternity services. The workshop aims to assess current health facility operations, identify shortcomings, and propose improvements for pregnant women, new mothers, and newborns in emergency situations.

Dr. Nehoulné Gaston, interim delegate for Public Health in Moyen-Chari, highlighted the need for a maternity network that meets population needs, citing shortages of qualified staff, insufficient medical equipment, and the distance to some health facilities. Dr. Tarda Olivier from UNFPA stressed that better care also requires regular medicine supplies and effective coordination between health centers for patient transfers. The Secretary-General of Barh-Koh, Allahognan Ngomsembaye Voltaire, noted the high rates of maternal and neonatal deaths in Chad and difficulties women face accessing care.

The workshop, organized by the Ministry of Public Health in partnership with UNICEF, WHO, UN Women, and UNFPA, will conclude on Thursday. Concerns raised include low attendance at maternity clinics and barriers to family planning services. The event seeks concrete solutions to these persistent challenges.

Why it mattersMothers and newborns in Chad face risks due to inadequate emergency obstetric and neonatal care services.

Health · 2026-11-03

N'Djamena ice factory shut for hygiene breaches

Municipal sanitary police closed an ice production unit in N'Djamena on November 2, 2026, after finding serious hygiene failures.

Municipal sanitary police closed an ice production unit in N'Djamena's 10th arrondissement on Monday, November 2, 2026. The closure followed the discovery of severe hygiene and sanitation failures at the facility, which produced ice for human consumption.

During a public health inspection, technical teams found irregularities in the manufacturing equipment and general production conditions. These deficiencies posed high health risks to consumers, leading to the immediate shutdown.

Culture · 2026-11-03

Hundreds gather for souls of departed

Abbé Felipe Rebollo led prayers for the dead at Toukra cemetery on November 2, 2026.

Hundreds of faithful gathered at Toukra cemetery in N’Djamena on Monday, November 2, 2026, for a solemn prayer service for the souls of the departed. Abbé Felipe Rebollo, parish priest of Saint Joseph de Ngonba, led the ceremony, joined by other priests from N’Djamena.

Standing among whitewashed tombs, Abbé Rebollo preached that death is a passage to eternal life, not an end. Prayers were offered for deceased parents, children, priests, sisters, flood victims, and soldiers who died for the nation. A procession followed, with general blessing of the graves using holy water.

One attendee from Ngonba, in tears, testified that the church's prayer assures her son is not alone. Abbé Rebollo and his fellow priests, accompanied by altar servers, walked through the cemetery, sprinkling graves and encouraging the faithful to do the same for their lost loved ones.

Why it mattersHundreds of faithful in N'Djamena gathered to pray for the souls of the departed, including flood victims and fallen soldiers.
