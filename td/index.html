Chad court keeps two men in custody over alleged Boko Haram links

The Tribunal de grande instance in N'Djamena ordered both suspects held while investigators pursue further inquiries into their alleged ties to the group.

Two men appeared before the Tribunal de grande instance in N'Djamena on Thursday, 12 November 2026, facing charges that include fraud and complicity with Boko Haram. Both denied the accusations.

The main suspect allegedly took money from a security chief in exchange for information on the group's movements. He told the court he got his intelligence through a former Boko Haram member who had been released after a social reintegration programme, but he could not provide that person's phone number or other contact details. The second man, a motorcycle taxi driver, is charged with complicity for transporting the main suspect.

The judge found the statements too uncertain to justify release and ordered both men held in provisional detention. The case was sent back for further investigation.

Why it mattersTraders and families in the area face the continued detention of two men as the case remains open with no trial date set.

Chad launches free cataract surgery drive for 700 people in Mongo

Patients in Guéra province will receive free operations, medicines and care under a national blindness programme.

A mission from Chad's Ministry of Public Health launched an ophthalmology campaign in Mongo, in Guéra province, on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. It will provide free treatment to 700 patients with cataracts, working with the NGO Direct Aid and its partner, Al Dossari hospital.

Dr Tomtinbeye Nandogngar, an eye specialist at the National Reference University Hospital in N'Djamena, said the aim is to help people with visual impairment regain their sight. Operations, medicines and all necessary care will be provided at no cost to patients.

The campaign is run under the national programme to fight blindness.

Security · 2026-11-13

Chad police inspect stations after child street operations

Police chief Tougoud Digo Maïdé visited three N'Djamena police stations on November 12, 2026, following recent operations to control and apprehend children on the streets.

The Director General of the National Police, Police Controller Tougoud Digo Maïdé, inspected three police stations in N'Djamena on November 12, 2026. The visit followed two days of operations to control and apprehend children found in the streets. He was accompanied by the delegate from the Ministry of Women and Early Childhood Protection.

The delegate, Fatimé Ali Outouma, stated that her ministry works closely with security forces to protect minors, as this is an essential mission. She noted that most of the children encountered originate from the provinces. Outouma emphasized the need for increased awareness among local communities so parents and leaders take responsibility before sending children to N'Djamena.

Outouma expressed gratitude to the National Police for including her department in the initiative. She highlighted that children in the capital are exposed to dangers on streets, roundabouts, and markets. Her ministry will take steps to sustain this joint strategy with law enforcement to combat the practice effectively.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Women and Early Childhood Protection will work with law enforcement to protect children exposed to dangers on N'Djamena's streets.

Climate · 2026-11-13

Chad to validate groundwater monitoring framework in N'Djaména

A two-day workshop in N'Djaména, running 12 to 13 November, is set to approve a strategic framework for monitoring underground water.

A workshop to validate Chad's strategic framework for groundwater monitoring is being held in N'Djaména from 12 to 13 November.

UNICEF's deputy representative, Dr Sylvain Nkwenkeu, said that waiting for boreholes to lose flow sharply or for water quality to deteriorate is not an option, citing climate variability, rising water needs and population displacement.

Nour Saleh Haggar, secretary general of the Ministry of Water and Energy, said water resources are a major issue for Chad's development, given droughts, floods and growing pressure on their availability and quality.

Why it mattersOfficials want a monitoring system in place before boreholes lose flow or water quality declines.

Chad and Morocco's Dakhla region discuss youth vocational training partnership

Morocco's Dakhla-Oued Eddahab region has offered technical support for Chadian trainers and short practical courses for young people.

Chad's Minister of Higher Education, Scientific Research and Vocational Training, Dr Sitack Yombatina, received Yanja El Kattat, president of the Moroccan region of Dakhla-Oued Eddahab, in audience on Thursday, 12 November 2026. The talks focused mainly on cooperation in technical and vocational training, and the Moroccan delegation included the president of the African Chamber of Commerce and Services of Dakhla.

The Moroccan side expressed interest in providing technical support to Chadian trainers, sharing experience, and running short practical courses for young people. The courses are meant to help them enter the job market by learning trades and building professional skills. In reply, Yombatina said the Chadian state places particular importance on youth vocational training and pointed to the ultramodern Sadjeri technical and vocational training centre in N'Djamena, and the Sarh centre, whose inauguration is announced soon.

The meeting opens prospects for collaboration in strengthening trainers' capacities, transferring skills and providing qualifying training for young people.
