President Tshisekedi Assesses Kasai-Oriental Projects, Inaugurates Pastoral Complex

President Félix-Antoine Tshisekedi arrived in Mbuji-Mayi to review infrastructure projects and inaugurate a religious and educational complex.

President Félix-Antoine Tshisekedi arrived in Mbuji-Mayi on Saturday, August 29, 2026, to assess key development projects in the Kasaï-Oriental region. His visit includes evaluating the modernization of Bipemba Airport, the rehabilitation of National Road No. 1, and the renovation of urban road networks. The president's agenda also features discussions with local authorities, led by Governor Jean-Paul Mbwebwa, regarding the province's economic priorities, particularly the revival of the MIBA mining company.

A significant event during this tour is the inauguration on Sunday, August 30, of the Notre-Dame de l'Espérance University Parish (PUNDE) and the Notre-Dame du Balai Sanctuary. This pastoral complex, initiated by Abbé Blaise Kanda, has a capacity of one thousand three hundred places and aims to stimulate religious and educational development within the region.

This marks President Tshisekedi's sixth visit to Mbuji-Mayi, underscoring the importance of the Kasaï-Oriental region in his administration's development plans. The focus on infrastructure and economic revival, including the MIBA, signals a commitment to improving the province's economic landscape and public services.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the president's assessment of infrastructure projects and discussions on reviving the MIBA mining company, which could impact economic activity.

Fire Guts N'Djamena Market, Causing Major Material Damage

An overnight blaze at the Diguel market in N'Djamena has been contained, but officials are assessing significant losses to vendors.

A fire broke out at the Diguel market in N'Djamena shortly after midnight on August 30, 2026. Municipal services and firefighters responded quickly to the scene, working for several hours to bring the flames under control. The fire was finally extinguished around 3 a.m. No human casualties were reported, but substantial material damage has occurred.

The Mayor of N'Djamena, Senoussi Hassana Abdoulaye, along with the mayor of the 8th arrondissement, observed the firefighting and security operations. Central municipal services, supported by firefighters from the 8th arrondissement, worked to contain the blaze and secure the area to prevent any resurgence of the fire.

The N'Djamena municipality has expressed solidarity with the affected traders. An assessment of the damage is underway to determine the full extent of the losses and to plan appropriate measures for those impacted by the incident.

Why it mattersTraders at the Diguel market are affected by significant material losses due to the fire, with an assessment underway to determine appropriate support measures.

Sahel States Alliance Thwarts Mutiny Attempt in Niamey

Loyalist forces successfully suppressed a mutiny targeting Niger's air base and sensitive sites in the early hours of August 29, 2026.

The Confederation of Sahel States (AES) announced that a destabilization attempt by mutinous soldiers in Niamey was foiled during the night of August 28 to 29, 2026. Attacks specifically targeted Air Base 101 and several other sensitive locations within the city before being contained by loyalist Republican forces.

A statement released from Ouagadougou indicated that the coordinated action of the loyalist forces was responsible for quelling the mutiny. The AES commended the professionalism, courage, and loyalty of Niger's Defense and Security Forces in response to the incident, reaffirming their full support for Niger's institutions.

The AES urged populations in Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger to remain vigilant against disinformation and divisive tactics that might accompany such events. This mutiny attempt occurs as the regional organization continues its joint efforts toward sovereignty and security.

Why it mattersThe AES, comprising Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger, is affected by this mutiny attempt as it disrupts their ongoing joint efforts for sovereignty and security.

UN Agency Urges End to Statelessness

The UNHCR calls for governments to increase actions to eradicate statelessness, which affects at least 4.5 million people.

The UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) is urging governments to intensify efforts to end statelessness, a persistent exclusion that deprives individuals of fundamental rights. At least 4.5 million people are stateless or have uncertain nationality, with millions more uncounted. Sixty-five years after a convention aimed at reducing statelessness, the problem continues, with legal non-recognition passed down through generations.

This lack of legal status prevents individuals from obtaining passports, civil registration, and exercising basic rights. In some instances, it impedes the ability to register a birth, marry, own property, open a bank account, or travel. Discriminatory laws, gaps in nationality laws, and arbitrary deprivation of nationality are persistent causes of this issue.

Women are disproportionately affected, as in 24 countries they cannot pass their nationality to their children under the same conditions as men. The absence of nationality can lead to profound social and economic marginalization, impacting access to essential services and opportunities.

Why it mattersStateless individuals are affected by being denied fundamental rights such as obtaining passports, civil registration, and the ability to travel.

Children Sell Water Sachets on N'djamena Streets

Minors between 8 and 15 years old are forced into street vending to survive amid widespread parental neglect and poverty.

In N'djamena, children as young as eight are selling sachets of water in markets and bus stops for 25 to 50 francs each to earn money for food. These minors, often without parental support or facing difficult family circumstances like divorce, unwanted pregnancies, or parental death, are compelled to find their own means of survival. This street trade exposes them to significant dangers as they navigate the city to find customers and then return to restock their goods.

Explanations for this phenomenon range from parental irresponsibility to societal issues such as divorce and forced marriages, leaving mothers unable to support their children. UNICEF highlights that these children are deprived of their rights to health, adequate food, and education, with nearly 3 million school-aged children in Chad not attending school. This lack of education and support pushes them towards delinquency and, for girls, increases their risk of sexual exploitation and prostitution.

The practice of child street vending in Chad reflects a broader societal failure, impacting the entire community. UNICEF emphasizes that children have a right to protection from violence, exploitation, and armed groups, and deserve a stable home environment. The current reality for many Chadian children, however, forces them into precarious situations, making them vulnerable to recruitment by armed groups and other forms of exploitation.

Why it mattersChildren aged 8 to 15 are forced into street vending to earn money for survival due to parental neglect and poverty.
