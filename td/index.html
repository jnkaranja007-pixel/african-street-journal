Education · 2026-11-04

UNET demands student bus and canteen services in Chad

The University of N'Djamena's student union has given authorities 72 hours to restore services.

The student union at the University of N'Djamena, UNET, has demanded the immediate restoration of university canteens and the effective deployment of student buses. UNET's executive secretary, Ortoroum Mathurin, stated on November 3 that students have been attending classes for a month without access to the canteens, despite prior commitments from the Minister of Higher Education.

Mathurin also highlighted that while sufficient buses exist, they are rarely used, causing significant disruption for students. The union expressed frustration over the lack of response from relevant services to a letter sent on September 25, 2026, which remains unanswered.

UNET has set a 72-hour deadline for the authorities to make the buses operational and reopen the university canteens.

Why it mattersUniversity of N'Djamena students are affected by the lack of essential services like transportation and food.

Chad's Déby receives Algerian foreign minister

Mahamat Idriss Déby Itno and Ahmed Attaf discussed bilateral cooperation and Sahel security in N'Djamena.

Chad's leader, Marshal Mahamat Idriss Déby Itno, met with Algeria's Foreign Affairs Minister Ahmed Attaf in N'Djamena on March 11, 2026. Attaf delivered a written message from Algerian President Abdelmadjid Tebboune to Déby Itno. The meeting took place at the Toumaï palace.

Chad mayor inspects Adré neighbourhood infrastructure

N'Djamena's 1st arrondissement mayor Djamal Yaya Moussa assessed public land encroachment and road issues.

N'Djamena's 1st arrondissement mayor, Djamal Yaya Moussa, visited the Adré neighbourhood on Monday, November 2, 2026, to assess local difficulties. He was accompanied by secretary-general Soumaïne Tom Brahim during the inspection.

The visit focused on issues faced by residents, including encroachments on public land designated for general interest constructions and problems with road infrastructure. Mayor Moussa reminded the community that state land reserves are exclusively for public benefit and urged the neighbourhood delegate to take full responsibility.

The mayor's inspection aimed to address the challenges impacting the Adré neighbourhood's development and public services.

Health · 2026-11-04

Chad prime minister announces dengue measures

Prime Minister Allah-Maye Halina ordered free healthcare and increased hospital capacity to combat a dengue outbreak.

Chad's Prime Minister Allah-Maye Halina announced five emergency measures on Monday, November 2, 2026, to address a severe dengue outbreak overwhelming public hospitals in the capital, N'Djamena. The government's response followed a period of perceived inaction amidst rising fatalities.

The Prime Minister's plan includes making all healthcare services free of charge and increasing the capacity of public health facilities to ease overcrowding. A national sanitation campaign has also been launched, involving public cleaning efforts and media coverage.

These measures aim to combat the spread of dengue, a disease transmitted by mosquitoes. The effectiveness of the plan hinges on logistical execution, particularly ensuring the availability of medicines in hospitals and the timely response of public administration to the epidemic's urgent pace.

Why it mattersThe dengue outbreak in Chad's capital is directly affecting the health of its citizens, leading to government intervention in public healthcare services.

Agriculture · 2026-11-04

Chad officials meet over early herder arrivals

Local authorities in Bébédjia are working to prevent conflicts between herders and farmers after an early influx.

Chad's Bébédjia rural sub-prefecture held a security meeting on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, to address early arrivals of transhumant herders. Secretary General Sougui Daoussa Dicko led the discussion with local administrators, conflict prevention committee members, herders, farmers, and civil society representatives. The meeting focused on preventing and managing conflicts arising from the premature presence of herders.

Authorities plan an urgent field visit to affected areas, emphasizing adherence to a September 28, 2026, circular. Key actions include securing herder transit corridors and reception zones, and organizing monthly meetings with sedentary herders and ferrick chiefs. Sougui Daoussa Dicko stressed proactive awareness campaigns and legal compliance to protect the agricultural season, already impacted by poor rainfall.

Participants highlighted communication gaps and the need for early departure corridors for the transhumants. A consensus was reached to deploy teams to the field and increase surveillance to guide herders to designated grazing areas before January 1, 2027. The goal is to resolve crop damage issues in the Nya region.

Why it mattersFarmers in Chad's Nya region face potential crop damage and conflict due to the early arrival of herders, impacting the agricultural season.
