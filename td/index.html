Coris Bank launches Islamic finance window in N'Djamena

The new Coris Bank International Baraka Tchad agency offers deposit, financing, and leasing services based on Islamic principles.

Coris Bank International officially launched its Baraka Tchad agency in N'Djamena on Thursday, October 29, 2026. The new branch, located in the Ridina neighborhood of the 5th arrondissement, is dedicated to Islamic finance services.

The launch ceremony was led by Saleh Bourma Ali, Chad's Minister Delegate for Finance, and attended by Coris Bank International Tchad General Director Fidèle Toko and CNAIT President Cheikh Abdadayim Abdoulaye Ousmane. The agency aims to provide secure deposit accounts, purchase-resale financing with deferred payment (mourabaha), and professional equipment leasing (ijara).

Coris Bank International Tchad plans to introduce further Islamic finance products like Moudaraba, Wakala, and Istisna'a. General Director Fidèle Toko stated that each client's request will be assessed individually according to bank procedures and eligibility criteria.

Why it mattersChadian residents and businesses can now access new financial products adhering to Islamic principles, including deposit accounts and financing options.

N'Djaména mayors meet on local taxes

A new framework aims to improve coordination and resource sharing among the capital's 10 districts.

Mayors from N'Djaména's 10 districts met on October 30 to implement a new framework for local governance. Central Mayor Senoussi Hassana Abdoulaye led the discussion on practical steps for the newly signed Charter for Local Communal Dialogue and Consultation.

The meeting focused on operationalizing a joint order from September 30, 2026, which establishes a mixed commission for collecting property and housing taxes across the capital. This commission will oversee tax collection and taxpayer awareness efforts.

The new framework is designed to foster institutional dialogue, share experiences, and harmonize public policies between the city and its districts. The goal is to improve coordination, avoid overlapping actions, and find joint solutions for common challenges affecting the capital's residents.

Why it mattersResidents of N'Djaména will be affected by new tax collection methods and coordinated public services.

Chad ministry bans justice movement march

The Ministry of Public Security prohibited the Mouvement national pour la justice march scheduled for October 31, 2026.

Chad's Ministry of Public Security formally banned a peaceful march planned by the Mouvement national pour la justice (MNJ) for October 31, 2026, citing risks of unrest. The order, signed by Minister General Ali Ahmat Aghabache on October 30, 2026, applies nationwide. The ministry warned that organizers face prosecution for offenses like vandalism or breaking and entering if the ban is ignored.

The implementation of this ban is assigned to the Director General of National Police, the Director General of National Gendarmerie, the Commander of the National and Nomadic Guard, and the Delegate General of the Government for the commune of N'Djamena. The MNJ had planned the march across the entire national territory.

The government's decision was made due to the MNJ's alleged failure to respect legal provisions for organizing public gatherings. The ministry's arrêté specifically mentioned potential infractions such as property damage and unauthorized entry.

Chad faces rising deaths amid health crisis

Hospitals overflow and funerals increase as authorities report only 14 deaths.

Chad is experiencing an alarming health situation with multiplying deaths and overflowing hospitals in N’Djamena and the provinces. The Ministry of Health reported 14 deaths as of October 26, a figure that contrasts sharply with daily observations of widespread illness and funerals.

The rainy season has brought malaria, compounded this year by dengue fever, leading to overwhelmed health centers and pharmacies. In some facilities, patients lie on the floor due to lack of space. Daily burials at major cemeteries are estimated between 50 and 80, according to cemetery agents.

On Friday, October 30, 26 bodies were presented for funeral prayers at the Markaz mosque in N’Djamena alone. This situation highlights a significant discrepancy between official figures and the reality on the ground, with many deaths likely occurring undiagnosed at home.

Why it mattersChadian citizens are facing increased mortality and overwhelmed healthcare facilities due to malaria and dengue fever.
