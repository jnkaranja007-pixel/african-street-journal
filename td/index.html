Chadian court clerks issue strike notice over pay and procedure disputes

The National Union of Court Clerks has announced a two-week strike after failing to secure government action on pay raises and alleged procedural abuses.

Court clerks in Chad have announced a two-week strike, set to begin on August 31 and conclude on September 15, 2026. The National Union of Court Clerks (SYNAPGREF) issued the notice following an extraordinary general assembly on August 28 in N'Djamena. The union cited a lack of progress on decrees to increase allowances and annual performance bonuses for clerks, as well as an unanswered request for a meeting with the Minister of Justice on June 9.

SYNAPGREF also raised concerns about circulars from the president of the N'Djamena High Court, which they claim impose unauthorized duties on clerks and confuse procedural documentation. The union views these directives as an abuse of authority and a misunderstanding of clerical procedures. Furthermore, the assembly highlighted the presence of non-clerk trainees performing judicial functions, which they assert violates the March 15, 2022 law on the status of court personnel. They demand the removal of these trainees to ensure legal integrity.

A follow-up general assembly is scheduled for September 16 to assess the situation and determine the future course of action. The union attributes the current impasse to a 'lack of political will' from the Ministry of Justice. The strike action directly impacts the functioning of the justice system, potentially delaying court proceedings and administrative tasks.

Why it mattersCourt clerks are affected by a lack of progress on pay increases and alleged procedural violations within the justice system.

Chadian Official Addresses Security and Farmer-Herder Conflicts

The new government delegate for Salamat province met with local administrators and traditional leaders to discuss public order and development priorities.

Ahmat Choua Rozi, the new government delegate for Salamat province, presided over a meeting in Amtiman with prefects, sub-prefects, and traditional chiefs on August 31, 2026. The gathering was a standard introductory session to discuss administrative protocols and provincial issues.

Key topics included ensuring the security of people and their property, fostering development, and addressing the persistent conflicts between farmers and herders. Rozi emphasized the importance of respecting hierarchy, maintaining public order, and enhancing collaboration between administrative bodies and traditional leadership for effective provincial management.

The delegate conveyed directives from higher authorities, aligning with the government's program and the Head of State's vision. He urged officials to perform their duties with rigor and integrity, focusing on the benefit of the population and calling for unified efforts to accelerate development in sectors like security, education, and social cohesion.

Chad Minister Outlines Bilingual Education Goals for 2026-2027 School Year

Minister of National Education Dr. Mahamat-Ahmad Alhabo emphasized strengthening teacher training and developing adapted pedagogical materials.

Chad's administrative school year begins September 1, 2026, with Minister of State for National Education, Bilingualism, and Civic Promotion, Dr. Mahamat-Ahmad Alhabo, setting priorities. The upcoming year, themed 'A Bilingual School for a Nation,' aims to accelerate educational system reform. The minister highlighted the importance of French-Arabic bilingualism as a tool for national cohesion and global engagement, aligning with presidential vision.

Key initiatives include enhancing initial and ongoing teacher training, creating and distributing suitable teaching resources, and improving human resource management and supervision. The minister also stressed the need for rigorous monitoring of bilingualism implementation and fostering a culture of responsibility and excellence. He called for improved school environments, urging parents, communities, and financial partners to collaborate.

School leaders are tasked with prioritizing good governance, discipline, and student safety. The minister exhorted all education sector personnel to return to their posts for optimal preparation of the academic year. This effort aims to equip Chad's youth with the tools for a prosperous future, building on resilience shown despite past social unrest and logistical challenges.

Why it mattersThe education system's focus on bilingualism and teacher training directly impacts students' learning and future opportunities.

Lionel Messi Retires from International Football

After twenty years with the Argentine national team, the 39-year-old captain announced his departure citing a lack of energy and a desire to make way for younger players.

Lionel Messi announced on Monday, August 31, 2026, his decision to end his international football career with Argentina. The 39-year-old captain stated that after two decades with the Albiceleste, he has given all he can and feels he has nothing left to offer. He expressed that the moment has come to step aside for the next generation of players.

In a message shared on Instagram, Messi described his decision as painful but necessary, emphasizing that he has always given his utmost for the national team. He conveyed his gratitude to fans, teammates, coaches, and family for their unwavering support throughout his extensive career, which saw him win major international trophies.

Messi assured supporters that he will continue to back the team from the sidelines, remaining a fan in good times and bad. He reflected on the unforgettable memories and the pride of achieving dream triumphs for Argentina alongside his teammates, concluding with a fervent declaration of love for his country.

Why it mattersFootball fans worldwide, particularly those who follow the Argentine national team, are affected by the retirement of their long-time captain and star player.

Chad, Gates, Dangote Foundations Deepen Health Partnership

Chad's Finance Minister met with representatives from the Gates and Dangote Foundations to discuss funding for vaccination programs and polio eradication efforts.

Chad's Minister of Finance, Tahir Hamid Nguilin, met on August 31, 2026, with a delegation from the Gates and Aliko Dangote Foundations. The meeting focused on securing sustainable financing for vaccination programs and mobilizing resources to intensify the fight against polio.

The delegation, led by Dr. Chris Elias and Ms. Zouera Youssoufou, aimed to strengthen the strategic partnership between the foundations and the Chadian government. Discussions also covered the overall financing of vaccination initiatives within the country.

Following the meeting, Ms. Youssoufou commended Chad for its performance in implementing health programs, noting the significant progress the nation has made. This collaboration underscores a commitment to improving public health outcomes through strategic financial support.

Why it mattersThe Gates and Dangote Foundations are strengthening their partnership with Chad to fund vaccination programs and combat polio, directly impacting public health initiatives.
