New Governor Installed in Chad's Salamat Province

Ahmat Choua Rozi officially assumed leadership of the Salamat province on Friday, August 28, 2026, following a government decree.

Ahmat Choua Rozi was officially installed as the new Governor of Salamat province on Friday, August 28, 2026, in Am-Timan. The ceremony was presided over by Limane Mahamat, the Vice-Prime Minister for Territorial Administration and Decentralization. The installation follows decree n° 2122 issued on August 3, 2026. The outgoing governor, General Ismat Issakha Acheikh, expressed pride in his tenure, stating that his achievements in the province speak for themselves and were made possible through the collaboration of all provincial stakeholders.

Vice-Prime Minister Limane Mahamat reminded the new Governor of his responsibilities, emphasizing his role as the dignified face of the state, a guarantor of civil peace, social cohesion, and public tranquility. He stressed the shared responsibility to consolidate security in Salamat and ensure its progress benefits all its inhabitants. The Minister instructed Rozi to proactively address and neutralize potential sources of tension before they escalate.

The newly installed Governor, Ahmat Choua Rozi, conveyed his gratitude to President Mahamat Idriss Déby Itno for the trust placed in him. Rozi pledged to carry out his duties with impartiality, rigor, humility, and a constant spirit of dialogue, in accordance with the Republic's laws. The ceremony in Am-Timan was attended by a large, engaged crowd.

Why it mattersThe installation of a new governor in Salamat province affects the local population by establishing new leadership responsible for peace, cohesion, and public tranquility.

Syria Reconnects to Global Card Payments After 15 Years

Mastercard and QNB Group processed the first international card transaction in Syria, marking a significant step in modernizing the country's payment systems.

Mastercard and QNB Group have successfully completed the first international card transaction in Syria in over 15 years. This event signifies a major advancement in the nation's payment infrastructure, reconnecting its system to the global Mastercard network. QNB Syria processed the transaction using an internationally issued Mastercard on a local point-of-sale terminal with an eligible merchant.

This milestone transaction lays the groundwork for accepting international payments within Syria, aiming to integrate consumers, businesses, and financial institutions into the global digital economy. The Governor of the Central Bank of Syria, S.E. Mohammed Safwat Raslan, stated that this achievement demonstrates the value of public-private collaboration in enabling secure international payment acceptance and fostering sustainable economic development.

Yousef Mahmoud Al-Neama of QNB Group highlighted that providing merchants with international payment capabilities offers customers more choices and convenience, supporting a transition to a more modern and secure digital payment ecosystem. Adam Jones of Mastercard added that this is a crucial step towards broader and more secure digital payment acceptance across the market, fostering a more connected and inclusive payment environment.

Why it mattersSyrian businesses and consumers can now engage in international digital transactions, facilitating trade and economic integration.

ONASER Educates Road Users on Traffic Law Compliance

The National Road Safety Office conducted a campaign across N'Djamena intersections to remind drivers and pedestrians of traffic regulations.

The National Road Safety Office (ONASER) deployed teams to several intersections in N'Djamena's Project for the Improvement of the Douala-N'Djamena Road Corridor (PCDN) area on Friday. The campaign aimed to inform road users about the importance of strictly adhering to traffic laws, including obeying road signs, speed limits, and instructions related to ongoing construction work.

ONASER urged all users to exercise caution, vigilance, and civic responsibility, especially near construction zones. The goal is to reduce accident risks and ensure the safety of workers, residents, and all road users. Drivers of 'clandos,' motorcyclists, motorists, and pedestrians were targeted in the awareness drive.

Those who participated in the sensitization expressed satisfaction with the initiative and pledged to adopt responsible driving habits. ONASER reiterated that everyone shares responsibility on the road and that respecting rules is crucial for saving lives. The campaign focused on promoting safer road practices within the city.

Why it mattersRoad users in N'Djamena are affected by the campaign as it aims to reduce accident risks and promote safer driving practices.

Armed men intrude on journalist's home claiming security search

Four masked men, identifying as national security agents, entered a journalist's home without a warrant, later admitting they had the wrong address.

Journalist Hynda Ahmet Chérif reported an armed intrusion at her home on Friday, August 28, 2026. Four masked men, carrying pistols and knives, entered her residence during the day while several occupants were at the mosque for prayer. The men claimed to be from the National Security Agency and stated they were conducting a search based on a tip.

When Chérif repeatedly asked for a warrant, the armed men refused, asserting they needed no official documentation. The situation shifted when a household employee questioned if they were at the correct home. After confirming Chérif's identity, one man made a phone call and the group departed on motorcycles, with one apologizing for the mistake and claiming they had the wrong house.

This incident has raised significant concerns about citizen safety and the sanctity of private residences. The journalist is questioning the true identity of the intruders, uncertain if they were genuine state agents or criminals impersonating officials. The event highlights anxieties regarding state authority and potential misuse of power.

Why it mattersJournalists and citizens are affected by the potential for arbitrary state intrusion and the fear of impersonation by criminals, impacting personal safety and trust in authorities.

Chadian and CAR Officials Discuss Border Conflicts

Cross-border prefects from Chad and the Central African Republic met to address community disputes and explore strategic infrastructure corridors.

Officials from Chad's Logone Oriental region and the Central African Republic's Yadé province convened in Mbaïbokoum, southern Chad, for a two-day conference. Supported by Concordis International, the meeting aimed to enhance participants' skills in managing recurring border conflicts and to analyze the potential for creating corridors with strategic infrastructure.

The conference, themed 'Prevention and Management of Community Conflicts Related to Transhumance at the CAR-Chad Borders,' served as a platform for dialogue and information sharing. It focused on community disputes largely stemming from the transhumance of herders from West and Central Africa and Cameroon seeking pastureland, with the goal of establishing leadership and strategies to address these issues.

Prefect Idriss Souleymane Bouyemé of the Monts de Lam department highlighted that border areas face numerous security challenges, including agro-pastoral conflicts, land disputes, cross-border crime, and uncontrolled movements of people and livestock, all of which can undermine social cohesion.

Why it mattersCross-border prefects from Chad and the Central African Republic are meeting to address community conflicts and explore infrastructure development.
