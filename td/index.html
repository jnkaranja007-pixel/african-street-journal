Chad health ministry reports 14 dengue deaths amid rising fatalities

Official figures from October 26 state 14 dengue deaths, while one mosque held 26 funerals on October 30.

Chad's Ministry of Public Health reported 14 deaths from dengue fever as of October 26, a figure sharply contrasted by local observations. Cemeteries in N'Djamena are reportedly full, and on October 30, the Markaz mosque alone conducted funeral prayers for 26 deceased individuals.

Hospitals, including the National Reference Hospital and private clinics, are overwhelmed, with patients reportedly being asked to lie on the floor. Families are seeking treatment while the government remains silent on the escalating crisis, offering no public response or appeal for international aid.

While provinces face intercommunal conflicts over resources, the health crisis is seen by some as a way for these issues to resolve themselves. Meanwhile, government officials met at the Radisson Blu hotel to discuss their actions, seemingly detached from the reality on the ground.

Why it mattersThe official reported death toll of 14 from dengue fever is starkly different from the 26 funerals held at a single mosque in N'Djamena on October 30, indicating a significant undercount of fatalities.

Chad land dispute halts N'Djamena health center construction

An association reports construction continues on land designated for a public health center in N'Djamena's Walia neighborhood.

The Association Action for the Well-being and Community Development in Chad (ABEDEC-TCHAD) has raised concerns about a land dispute in N'Djamena's Walia neighborhood that has lasted over eight years. The group denounces irregular land allocations and ongoing construction on a site reserved for a public health center. ABEDEC-TCHAD highlighted the situation on October 31, 2026, stating that a commission's 2018 report was irregularly established.

ABEDEC-TCHAD accuses Gaukam Claude, head of restructuring, and Hassana Hamit Mahamat, former deputy mayor, of irregularities in land distribution. The association claims a sharing of spaces on February 27, 2017, violated a 2008 decree on land allocation commissions. They also question how some beneficiaries obtained land documents and note that legal appeals by affected owners have been unsuccessful.

The dispute centers on land provided by a private individual for the Ngonba public health center, established administratively on September 20, 2016. The center currently operates in rented premises due to lack of site access. ABEDEC-TCHAD alleges Bolguel Lame Emmanuel is proceeding with construction despite a suspension order from June 3, 2025, confirmed on October 22, 2025. Work resumed on October 27, 2026, on the health reserve land.

Why it mattersResidents of N'Djamena's Walia neighborhood are affected by a land dispute that is preventing the construction of a public health center and has led to continued work on the designated site.

Education · 2026-11-01

Charity gives school kits to N'Djamena pupils

The Association de bienfaisance pour l’harmonie et le développement social donated supplies to La Sagesse school on October 31, 2026.

The Association de bienfaisance pour l’harmonie et le développement social (ABHDS) provided school kits to students at La Sagesse school in N'Djamena on Saturday, October 31, 2026.

Hayat Saad Mahadi, ABHDS secretary, stated the donation reflects the association's commitment to education and student support, echoing a call from President Mahamat Idriss Déby Itno. School director Yahya Hissène noted the gifts boost student morale and thanked ABHDS president Zakaria Abdoulaye, who lives abroad.

The ABHDS, led by founder Zakaria Abdoulaye, focuses on solidarity and community development. The school is located on rue des 40 mètres.

Why it mattersStudents at La Sagesse school in N'Djamena received school supplies from a charitable organization.

Chad schedules Assoungha by-election for December 13

The National Election Management Agency announced the vote following the death of a member of the National Assembly.

Chad's National Election Management Agency (ANGE) will hold a by-election in the Assoungha department of Ouaddaï province on Sunday, December 13, 2026. The vote is necessary because the previous representative, Hassan Moussa Yakhoub of the RNDT-Le Réveil party, has died. ANGE president Ahmed Bartchiret announced the election calendar on October 31 in N'Djamena.

Ahmed Bartchiret stated that candidate applications will be accepted from October 1 to October 5, 2026. The election campaign will begin 21 days before the vote and end 24 hours before polls open, as per the electoral code. Bartchiret urged political actors to act responsibly and maintain a peaceful political climate during the process.

The Assoungha seat has become vacant for the second time in the current legislative term. The ANGE has taken all necessary steps to ensure a transparent, credible, and fair election. Bartchiret also called on Assoungha voters to participate and for media professionals to provide balanced coverage.

Why it mattersAssoungha voters will elect a new representative to the National Assembly after their previous deputy passed away.

Health · 2026-11-01

Chad morgue registers over 300 deaths in October

The Markaz mosque morgue in N'Djamena processed more than 300 bodies, mostly from natural causes.

The Markaz mosque morgue in N’Djamena registered over 300 bodies in October 2026. The majority of these deaths were attributed to natural causes, with fewer than 20 cases linked to accidents during the month.

According to an official at the facility, many of the deceased suffered from rapidly progressing illnesses that lasted only three to 10 days. The morgue prepares at least seven bodies daily for funeral prayers during the three daily prayer times.

The facility is experiencing a daily influx of deceased individuals. The packet does not provide further details on the implications of this situation.

Why it mattersOver 300 people in N'Djamena died in October, with most deaths attributed to natural causes and rapid illnesses.
