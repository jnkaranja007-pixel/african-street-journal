Chadian medical order warns against informal circumcisions after three deaths

The Moyen-Chari provincial council of the National Order of Physicians in Chad issued a warning today after three children died following a circumcision in Sarh's Kissimi.

Dr. Kambe Helngda Christophe, president of the provincial council, stated on August 27, 2026, from his office at Sarh's Modern Hospital, that the deaths occurred within twenty-four hours of the procedure. He emphasized that medical acts performed outside appropriate health settings carry risks and urged families to use only qualified professionals.

The Order stressed that circumcision is not merely a traditional act but requires strict hygiene, proper equipment, and infection prevention measures, including tetanus. Dr. Christophe warned that neglecting these precautions can lead to severe complications for children and advised parents against entrusting health procedures to unqualified individuals.

To prevent future tragedies, the Order recommends that parents seek services at Sarh's health facilities, such as the provincial hospital, district hospital, or health centers, where a surgeon is available for such interventions under proper medical conditions. The Order also called on untrained practitioners to cease their activities and requested administrative and judicial authorities to increase controls.

Why it mattersFamilies in Sarh are directly affected by the health risks associated with informal medical procedures, which can lead to severe complications or death for their children.

Chad launches workshop on pastoral contingency plans for 13 provinces

The Ouaddaï province's Secretary General, Djingar Gaspard, opened a three-day workshop in Abéché to develop pastoral contingency plans for 13 Chadian provinces.

The workshop, which began on Thursday, aims to enhance the ability to anticipate, prevent, and manage crises affecting pastoral communities and livestock systems. Around thirty participants from the concerned provinces are analyzing risks and vulnerabilities to create localized plans.

Mht Adoum Seid, representing the provincial delegate for Livestock and Animal Production, and Chérif Abakar Doutoum, head of mission for the supervising ministry, emphasized the importance of anticipation and active involvement from stakeholders in preventing pastoral crises.

Djingar Gaspard highlighted the strategic scope of this contingency plan, describing it as an essential tool to protect livestock and herders from future shocks. This initiative by PRAPS 2 seeks to strengthen the resilience of pastoral systems and populations in its intervention zones.

Why it mattersPastoral communities and livestock systems in 13 Chadian provinces are directly affected by the development of plans to manage future crises.

Adventist Health Department offers free medical campaign in Djarao

The Seventh-day Adventist Churches in Chad's Health Department is providing free screenings, consultations, and health awareness in Djarao, Mayo-Kebbi Est province.

The Health Department of the Seventh-day Adventist Churches in Chad is conducting a free health campaign in Djarao, Mayo-Kebbi Est province, from August twenty-seventh to twenty-ninth, two thousand twenty-six. Coordinated by Dr. Mbadjivia Kabdana, the initiative offers medical consultations, screenings, and health education.

Medical teams, supported by staff from the Béré Adventist Hospital, are providing free medical consultations and screenings for blood sugar and high blood pressure. These services are accompanied by various health education activities for the community.

The campaign also includes awareness sessions on cholera prevention, offering practical advice on hygiene, drinking water, and sanitation. Conferences are being held to discuss harmful traditional practices affecting newborns, as well as high blood pressure and diabetes.

Why it mattersResidents of Djarao are directly affected by gaining access to free medical screenings, consultations, and health education that address prevalent health concerns like cholera, diabetes, and hypertension.

Health · 2026-08-27

Moundou health district reviews first half 2026 activities

Health officials in Moundou are evaluating the implementation of their operational action plan for the first six months of 2026, focusing on vaccination and data reporting.

The Moundou Centre health district is holding a steering committee meeting from August twenty-sixth to twenty-seventh, 2026, to assess its health activities for the first semester of the year. The Prefect of Lac-Wey department, Ahmed Oumarou Djibrilla, presided over the opening ceremony, attended by health officials and stakeholders.

The meeting's theme addresses challenges in routine vaccination, epidemiological surveillance, and the quality of health data reporting. According to Dr. Djimadoum Dionadji, the chief medical officer for the district, these sessions provide a forum for discussion among those involved in implementing and monitoring health activities.

The chosen theme highlights the need to strengthen three key health system pillars: routine vaccination, epidemiological surveillance, and data reporting quality. Routine vaccination is a primary intervention to prevent avoidable diseases and reduce illness and death, especially among children under five years old.

Why it mattersResidents of Moundou, particularly parents of children under five, are affected as the meeting aims to improve health services directly impacting disease prevention and mortality rates in the region.

Chadian Ministry Seeks Firms for Soil Mapping Project

Chad's Ministry of Agricultural Production and Industrialization is inviting specialized firms to conduct rapid soil characterization and create decision-making maps.

The Ministry of Agricultural Production and Industrialization, through the RENFORT project funded by IFAD, has issued a call for expressions of interest to select a specialized firm. This firm will be responsible for rapid soil characterization and developing decision-making maps within the intervention zones of the ILSA project.

The mission, estimated to last 60 days, aims to generate operational information to guide agroecological interventions, demonstration activities, and sustainable soil fertility management strategies. Interested firms must demonstrate expertise in cartography, soil analysis, geographic information systems (GIS), remote sensing, and/or digital agriculture.

Candidate pre-selection will be based on a 100-point grid, with 10 points for activity nature, 30 for general experience, and 60 for specific experience. Firms with at least three similar missions, particularly in pedological mapping or soil fertility assessment for recognized funders, will receive particular attention. Expressions of interest are due by September 18, 2026, at 12 hours, at the IFAD projects office in N'Djamena.

Why it mattersChadian firms with expertise in soil analysis and mapping stand to gain contracts from this government initiative, impacting their business operations and revenue.
