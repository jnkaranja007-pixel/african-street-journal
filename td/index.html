Chad Security Ministry Bans Police Intervention in Community Disputes

A directive from the Ministry of Public Security prohibits all internal security forces from engaging in community conflicts, threatening disciplinary action.

Chad's Ministry of Public Security has issued a formal order forbidding members of the Internal Security Forces (FSI) from intervening in community disputes. Minister Ali Ahmat Akhabach signed the directive on September 1, 2026, in N'Djamena, emphasizing that any violation will result in severe disciplinary sanctions.

The ban applies to all FSI personnel, including generals, senior and junior officers, non-commissioned officers, peacekeepers, and soldiers. The ministry has tasked the directors general of the National Police, National Gendarmerie, Intelligence and Investigations, and the commander of the National and Nomad Guard with ensuring strict adherence to this new policy.

This measure aims to prevent security forces from becoming involved in local disagreements. The directive serves as a clear warning against any form of interference by law enforcement in the resolution of community-based conflicts across the country.

Why it mattersThe directive affects community members by potentially altering how local disputes are addressed without security force involvement.

Chad administrators begin 2026-2027 school year preparations

The administrative back-to-school period has officially started across Chad, focusing on preparing educational institutions for the upcoming academic year.

The 2026-2027 administrative school year commenced on September 1, 2026, in Chad's educational facilities. Dr. Mahamat Ahmat Alhabo, the Minister of State for National Education, Bilingualism, and Civic Promotion, launched the period under the theme, "A bilingual school at the service of a nation."

This administrative phase is designed to initiate preparations for the new school year, ensuring that schools are organized before classes officially resume. The process is underway in various institutions, including Lycée and Collège La Concorde, Lycée Félix Éboué, and the Lycée d’Enseignement Technique Commercial.

The administrative start allows educational staff to finalize logistical and organizational tasks. This ensures that when students return, the learning environment is ready, facilitating a smooth transition into the academic curriculum and the promotion of bilingual education.

Why it mattersStudents and educators are affected by the administrative start which prepares schools for the upcoming academic year and the implementation of a bilingual education system.

Prosecutor seeks one year jail for fraud defendant

A N'Djamena court heard a fraud case where the prosecutor requested a one-year prison sentence and a fine.

The N'Djamena High Court on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, heard a case involving a defendant at the Klessoum detention center. The prosecutor dropped charges of forgery and uttering a forged document, proceeding only with fraud. He requested a one-year prison sentence and a fine of one hundred thousand FCFA for the public treasury.

The dispute originated from a partnership between the defendant and a local association involved in fundraising. The plaintiff claims the defendant defrauded them of over three hundred million FCFA. However, the defense lawyer disputes this amount, calling it imaginary and stating the actual transaction volume was seventy million FCFA.

The defense argued that the plaintiff filed multiple complaints and that their client is a victim of a scam by an "untouchable" third party whose testimony the court refused to hear. The defense claims the plaintiff and this third party are artificially inflating the debt to hold the defendant solely responsible. The court has reserved its decision for a future hearing.

Why it mattersThe defendant faces a potential one-year prison sentence and a fine of one hundred thousand FCFA for alleged fraud.

Defense Seeks Acquittal for Abdoulaye Miskine

The second day of Abdoulaye Miskine's trial heard testimony from co-accused as civil parties demanded reparations for alleged war crimes.

The second day of Abdoulaye Miskine's trial on Tuesday, September 1, focused on the testimony of co-accused individuals. The Coordination of Civil Society Associations and Human Rights Defense (Cascidho), acting as a civil party, reiterated accusations of torture, rape, and forced disappearance, seeking reparations for victims. Miskine's defense team characterized the proceedings as fitting and outlined a strategy for acquittal, centering on an objection to the court's jurisdiction raised by the main defendant.

Mahamat Digadimbaye, coordinator for Cascidho, stated that his organization filed the complaint against Abdoulaye Miskine and others, citing crimes against humanity including torture, rape, and forced disappearance. He emphasized that the Chadian people expect justice and that Miskine operated as a warlord at the border, causing atrocities that left hundreds of victims unable to attend the trial. Cascidho, which initially filed a complaint in 2020, is seeking damages for the harm suffered by victims due to the actions of Miskine's elements.

The trial is proceeding with contradictory exchanges between parties as interrogations continue. Cascidho's involvement as a civil party since 2020 highlights allegations of kidnappings, unlawful detentions, cattle theft, rape, and assassinations committed within Chadian territory. The defense is preparing its case for acquittal, focusing on procedural arguments regarding the court's authority to hear the case.

Why it mattersThe trial of Abdoulaye Miskine affects victims of alleged war crimes by potentially providing reparations and justice for atrocities. Investors may be impacted by the legal proceedings and their implications for stability and governance.

Xi Jinping Calls for Enhanced Development and Security at SCO Summit

Chinese President Xi Jinping outlined a vision for the Shanghai Cooperation Organization focused on shared prosperity, regional security, and equitable global governance.

At the 26th meeting of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) Council of Heads of State in Bishkek, Chinese President Xi Jinping emphasized the organization's quarter-century of existence. He addressed fellow leaders, including Kyrgyzstan's Sadyr Japarov and Uzbekistan's Chavkat Mirziyoïev, recalling the foundational principles of mutual trust, shared benefit, and non-confrontation that define the Shanghai Spirit.

Amidst contemporary geopolitical challenges and international tensions, President Xi articulated a four-point plan. This vision prioritizes development and common prosperity, strengthens regional security, centers on peoples to consolidate cultural ties, and promotes more equitable global governance. China pledges to make the SCO a priority region for its Belt and Road Initiative, proposing an international AI cooperation center and increased renewable energy capacity.

The summit also marked the transition of the SCO presidency to Pakistan, with Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif accepting the role. China committed to implementing numerous technological and educational projects within the SCO framework over the next three years, aiming to foster deeper cooperation and development among member states.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as China plans to make the SCO a priority region for its Belt and Road Initiative, including the creation of an AI cooperation center and increased renewable energy capacity.
