DRC Government Protects Academic Paths Amidst Conflict

The government has implemented temporary measures for eleven public higher education institutions in Goma and Bukavu to safeguard student progress and diploma validity.

The Democratic Republic of Congo government announced it is taking targeted, temporary measures affecting eleven public higher education institutions in Goma and Bukavu. These actions aim to protect students' academic progress and the value of their degrees amidst interference from the AFC/M23 group. The measures, implemented during the academic break, are designed to ensure student orientation and the continuation of studies in legally compliant institutions before the 2026-2027 academic year begins.

These steps are not a general closure of higher education in North and South Kivu, nor an abandonment of students or residents in occupied areas. The government stated that all regularly obtained academic achievements remain protected, and no student will need to restart their studies. The measures are founded on articles 42, 43, and 45 of the Constitution and national education law, and will be continuously monitored and reassessed based on the security situation and the restoration of institutional legality.

The government's decision comes in response to the AFC/M23 group's alleged interference, including the removal of established management committees and the irregular installation of its members in academic roles. These conservatory measures are intended to preserve the integrity of the academic system and ensure that educational pathways remain secure and valid for students affected by the conflict.

Why it mattersStudents in Goma and Bukavu are directly affected by these measures, which aim to protect their academic progression and diploma validity despite the conflict.

Adventist Churches Launch Free Health Campaign in Mayo-Kebbi Est

A three-day health initiative in Djarao offers free medical consultations, disease screenings, and health education, including cholera prevention and advice on traditional.

The Department of Health of the Adventist Churches Mission in Chad is organizing a free health campaign from August 27 to 29 in Djarao, Mayo-Kebbi Est province. Led by Dr. Mbadjivia Kabdana, the initiative aims to provide free medical consultations, screenings for blood sugar and high blood pressure, and health education sessions.

The medical team, supported by staff from the Adventist Hospital of Béré, will also conduct awareness activities on cholera prevention, offering practical advice on hygiene, safe water, and sanitation. Conference sessions will address harmful traditional practices concerning newborns, as well as hypertension and diabetes.

This campaign seeks to improve community health through accessible medical services and targeted education. It provides an opportunity for residents to receive health checks and learn about managing common health issues and preventing infectious diseases.

Why it mattersResidents of Djarao are directly affected by the free medical screenings and health education provided by the Adventist Churches Mission.

Business · 08/27/2026

Neptune Group Opens Modern Mall in Obala

The Maithé Mall Mboua, a multi-billion CFA franc commercial center, aims to boost local production and create jobs, with government officials in attendance.

Obala, Cameroon – The Neptune Group inaugurated its first Maithé Mall Mboua on August 26, 2026, marking a significant economic development for the Lekié region. The modern supermarket, located about thirty kilometers north of Yaoundé, is described as one of the most advanced and comprehensive retail spaces in the Centre region. The opening ceremony was attended by the Minister of Commerce, Luc Magloire Mbarga Atangana, and the Vice-President of the CEMAC Commission, Charles Assamba Ongodo, among other dignitaries.

Dr. Antoine Ndzengue, CEO of Neptune Group, stated that the mall's primary objective is job creation and supporting the government's import-substitution policy by promoting local products. The Minister of Commerce hailed the facility as a "new generation" commercial center, symbolizing the dynamism of Cameroon's national economy and the retail sector. The mall is expected to stimulate regional agro-pastoral and forestry production through local supply contracts, aligning with President Paul Biya's call for increased domestic production and consumption.

The Maithé Mall Mboua also aims to provide access to skilled trades like baking and butchery, while bringing high-quality goods closer to local populations and facilitating access to everyday consumer items. Neptune Group, already a major player in Cameroon's petroleum industry with numerous service stations and sub-regional trading activities, is diversifying into retail with this substantial investment, which also spans finance, tire industry, and maritime logistics.

Why it mattersThe Maithé Mall Mboua's focus on local sourcing and job creation directly impacts farmers and investors in the retail and agricultural sectors.

News · 08/27/2026

Attack kills 12 near Chad-Sudan border

A security source reported an airstrike targeted a market used by Sudanese paramilitary forces for fuel procurement.

Twelve people died Thursday in Chad following an airstrike in a region close to the Sudanese border. A Chadian security source indicated that N'Djamena and Washington attribute the attacks on Chadian territory to the Sudanese army.

The strike, according to the anonymous security source, hit a market in the Adikong area. This location is reportedly where the Rapid Support Forces, a Sudanese paramilitary group engaged in conflict with the Sudanese army, obtain fuel.

The incident occurred in a border zone where tensions are high due to the ongoing conflict in neighboring Sudan. The specific target was identified as a fuel supply point for the Rapid Support Forces.

Why it mattersTwelve people were killed in an attack near the Sudanese border, affecting livelihoods and safety in the region.

N'Djamena Children Roam Streets Lacking Education

Youth in Chad's capital spend time collecting recyclables or playing football instead of attending school, contributing to societal disorder.

Children in N'Djamena are observed congregating in groups, spending their days outside the home engaged in street football or collecting discarded bottles and metal from trash bins. This lack of structured activity and education leaves them vulnerable to harmful behaviors such as early smoking and begging. The adult literacy rate in Chad is approximately twenty-seven percent, with men at thirty-five percent and women at eighteen percent, indicating a broader educational deficit.

The city faces rising insecurity, and experts suggest that a lack of educational and social guidance for youth contributes to juvenile delinquency. Parents are urged to take responsibility for their children's upbringing, and training centers need strengthening to provide early vocational skills. Without proper direction, unemployed youth can become a danger to society, exacerbated by poverty and limited job prospects.

While authorities consider development strategies to reduce unemployment and boost school enrollment, parents must actively guide their children away from street life. The potential for modernizing agriculture and livestock sectors offers opportunities for youth employment, but these require focused development initiatives to absorb the growing number of young people.

Why it mattersYoung people in N'Djamena are affected by a lack of educational and social structures, leading to delinquency and societal problems.
