Chad students end university strike

The National Union of Chadian Students (UNET) lifted a three-day strike at the University of N'Djamena on November 13, 2026, but maintained its demands.

The National Union of Chadian Students (UNET) at the University of N'Djamena announced on November 13, 2026, that it had ended a strike that began three days earlier. The student organization cited actions taken by the National Centre for University Services (CNOU) as the reason for lifting the strike, while affirming that their demands remain unresolved.

After three days of protests, UNET has called for students to resume their university activities. In a statement, the organization thanked the striking students for their mobilization and sense of responsibility. It urged everyone to return to classes calmly and with respect.

UNET also issued a call for unity and responsibility, encouraging students to continue working towards improving their living and study conditions. This objective remains a priority for the organization despite the lifting of the strike.

Why it mattersStudents at the University of N'Djamena are returning to classes after a three-day strike, with their demands for improved living and study conditions still pending.

Sarh students gain seven buses for campus travel

The government supplied three new and four used buses to the university's student services centre, and students report mixed views on their condition.

Seven buses, three new and four used, have been in service for a month for students at the University of Sarh in southern Chad, moving them between their homes and the university sites. The vehicles were supplied by the government to the Centre secondaire des œuvres universitaires (CSOU), the body that runs student services at the university.

Many students say the buses bring relief from transport problems they faced regularly, though some have doubts about the state and comfort of the used vehicles. Tambarina Mitterand, secretary general of the Union nationale des étudiants tchadiens (UNET) in the Moyen-Chari region, said the buses answer a problem that weighed heavily on student travel, and he urged students to look after them.

Nanlangar Kodalbaye, director of the CSOU in Sarh, thanked the government and said steps will be taken to manage the buses better and end overcrowding. He also said measures are being considered to improve university meals, including hygiene, meal quality and portion sizes.

Why it mattersStudents who live far from the campus can now ride to lectures on government-supplied buses, though overcrowding is still a problem the centre has promised to fix.

Health · 2026-11-14

N'Djamena street food risks health

Uncovered food sold in Chad's capital is exposed to dust, flies, and exhaust fumes, raising health concerns.

In N'Djamena, Chad, food items like potatoes, locusts, grilled sweet potatoes, and watermelon slices are sold openly in markets and streets, often near busy roads. These foods are exposed to dust, flies, and exhaust fumes, creating significant health worries for consumers despite enabling small traders to earn a living.

Residents express alarm over the unsanitary conditions, with one N'Djamena inhabitant noting that customers continue to buy food with flies landing directly on it. Vendors cite difficulties such as a lack of appropriate selling spaces and the challenge of completely avoiding contamination from flies and dust.

Health experts warn that unprotected food can carry pathogens that cause foodborne illnesses like acute diarrhea or poisoning. The visible sale of exposed food items across the city's streets and sidewalks highlights a critical public health issue.

Why it mattersResidents in N'Djamena are exposed to potential foodborne illnesses from uncovered street food sold near roads.

Chad hosts Africa Executive Meeting to boost trade

N'Djamena meeting focuses on transport corridors, youth employment, and investment to foster African economic integration.

The Africa Executive Meeting 2026 convened in N'Djamena on Friday, November 13, focusing on transport corridors, inter-African trade, youth employment, and investment. The gathering aimed to convert cooperation possibilities into projects that create value and jobs.

Institutional representatives and economic actors discussed themes including "corridors, decongestion, and inter-African trade," "human capital, youth employment, and entrepreneurship," and "industrial transformation and investment." Abderrahmane Gademi, interim administrator of Chad's Chamber of Commerce, Industry, Agriculture, Mines, and Crafts, expressed a desire to support and encourage participation in the event.

Discussions highlighted the need for economic actors' participation, financing mobilization, and infrastructure for better goods circulation. The meeting was intended as a platform for exchanges and for forging partnerships to develop new market prospects.

Why it mattersEconomic actors and institutional representatives met to foster cooperation and create jobs through investment and improved trade routes.

Business · 2026-11-14

Dakhla seeks stronger Chad economic ties

Morocco's Dakhla-Oued Eddahab region wants to boost trade with Chad in farming, training, tourism, and renewables.

El Khattat Yanja, president of the Dakhla-Oued Eddahab Regional Council, wants to increase economic exchanges with Chad. He expressed this desire during a visit to N'Djamena for the Africa Executive Meetings on Friday, November 13. Yanja highlighted his region's development strategy, focusing on fishing, agriculture, tourism, renewable energy, and port infrastructure.

The Dakhla-Oued Eddahab region, in southern Morocco, is being positioned as a key economic hub for sub-Saharan Africa. Yanja presented the development levers of this region, emphasizing investments made since 2015 under a new development model for southern regions. Efforts to improve air connectivity and attract investors were also noted.

The Moroccan official aims to foster cooperation in agriculture, training, tourism, and renewable energies. These sectors form the core of the region's strategy to become a logistical pole.

Why it mattersMoroccan officials are seeking to strengthen economic ties with Chad in key sectors like agriculture and renewable energy.
