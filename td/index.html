Cameroon launches 90-day Ebola preparedness plan

The plan aims to prevent virus importation and ensure rapid response to any alert.

Cameroon activated a 90-day emergency plan on October 28, 2026, to guard against Ebola importation. The strategy, running from November 2, 2026, to January 31, 2027, was presented by the health minister. This roadmap aims to consolidate national capacities for prevention, detection, and response.

The plan was unveiled by the Director of Disease and Epidemic Control, involving partners and sectors. It mobilizes all regions and 13 focal points from various administrations. The goal is to prevent virus importation and ensure a swift, effective response to any alert.

Key priorities include coordination, epidemiological surveillance, investigation, rapid response teams, laboratory diagnosis, case management, infection control, and risk communication. The plan focuses on detecting, isolating, confirming, and managing imported cases, especially at land borders with Nigeria and international airports.

Why it mattersCameroon's 90-day Ebola preparedness plan affects its citizens by aiming to prevent the virus from entering the country and ensuring a quick response if an alert occurs.

Chad prefect urges peace between farmers and herders

Aboudeïa prefect Mahamat Ismaïl Hassan Kari also called for increased school enrollment and an end to violence against women.

Aboudeïa prefect Mahamat Ismaïl Hassan Kari visited Am-Habilé on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, urging residents to foster peaceful coexistence between herders and farmers. The meeting, held 80 km east of Aboudeïa in the Salamat province, was attended by administrative officials, security forces, and community leaders. The prefect also stressed the importance of sending children to school and preventing violence against women.

The call for peace comes as fields and pastures in the region often compete for the same land. Noh Idriss Hamid, the sub-prefect of Am-Habilé, welcomed Kari and his delegation, which included administrative and security officials. The gathering addressed multiple key issues for the assembled population.

The prefect's message directly addresses the daily realities faced by both agricultural and pastoral communities in the area.

Why it mattersFarmers and herders in Chad's Salamat province face competition for land, and the prefect's message aims to prevent conflict.

N'Djamena begins road repairs in 1st arrondissement

Roads degraded by the rainy season in the 1st arrondissement of N'Djamena are being resurfaced starting October 29, 2026.

Work to resurface roads damaged during the rainy season began in N'Djamena's 1st arrondissement on October 29, 2026. The road repair project is managed by the 1st arrondissement's development service.

The deputy mayor, Mahamat Hassan Mahamat, supervised the start of the work, accompanied by the session secretary, Hissein Mody, and a work controller. The team began repairs on the main Djougoulier road to improve traffic flow on this busy route.

The repairs aim to restore accessibility to the affected areas.

Why it mattersResidents of N'Djamena's 1st arrondissement will experience improved road conditions and easier travel.

Chad court sentences FDPC leader to 15 years

The Front démocratique du peuple centrafricain (FDPC) disputes the September 9, 2026 conviction of General Abdoulaye Miskine.

Chad's justice system sentenced General Abdoulaye Miskine, leader of the Front démocratique du peuple centrafricain (FDPC), to 15 years in prison on September 9, 2026. The FDPC announced its strong concern over the ruling on October 29, 2026, from Gamboula, calling the decision heavy with human, political, and judicial consequences.

The movement questions the conditions under which the judgment was made and insists on an irreproachable procedure that respects defense rights and fair trial principles. The FDPC requested that Chadian judicial authorities guarantee General Abdoulaye Miskine access to all legal appeals, as well as his safety, physical and moral integrity, and medical care.

The FDPC also invited international organizations and human rights institutions to monitor the case while respecting the independence of Chadian justice. The movement reaffirmed its commitment to peace, dialogue, and justice, intending to contest the sentence through legal, diplomatic, and peaceful means.

Why it mattersThe conviction of FDPC leader General Abdoulaye Miskine to 15 years in prison affects the political stability and human rights of individuals involved in the conflict.

Politics · 2026-10-29

Chad launches national forum on traditional authority

Traditional leaders from 23 provinces gathered in Mongo to address conflict causes and promote peace.

Chad's national forum for traditional authorities opened on October 29 in Mongo, Guera province. Vice-Premier Minister Limane Mahamat, representing the head of state, launched the three-day event. The forum aims to identify conflict causes and propose solutions for crisis management and peaceful coexistence.

Traditional leaders from all 23 provinces are discussing the roots of clashes, including farmer-herder, intercommunal, and land disputes. These conflicts have caused loss of life, division, and social fragmentation. The leaders will formulate recommendations to curb ongoing violence.

The president of the High Council of Traditional Chiefdoms, Tamitah Djidingar, emphasized the role of traditional authority in uniting communities. He stated that chiefs must protect all under their jurisdiction without discrimination and act to de-escalate tensions and preserve human life.

Why it mattersTraditional leaders are meeting to address conflicts that affect communities across Chad.
