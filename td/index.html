Chad president sacks officials over pasture dispute

Marshal Mahamat Idriss Déby Itno ordered civilians disarmed after a crisis meeting in Ennedi Ouest province.

Chad's President Mahamat Idriss Déby Itno imposed immediate sanctions on military and administrative officials in Torboul, Ennedi Ouest province, on Saturday, November 7, 2026. The sanctions followed a crisis meeting convened shortly after the president's arrival to address tensions over pasture and water access that threatened to escalate into open conflict.

General Hamoda Mahamat Béchir, commander of defense zone n°8, was demoted and suspended for failing to enforce disarmament orders and for perceived inaction. The prefects of Torboul and Mourtcha, along with the sub-prefect of Am-Djirémé, were also dismissed. The president instructed the head of state security services to disarm civilians in the area, stating that state service requires obedience to hierarchy, not community.

The president began a security tour of several provinces, with Ennedi Ouest being the first stop. The delegate general for Ennedi Ouest, Goukouni Allatchi Tchoubougou, presented the ongoing tensions. General Hamoda Mahamat Béchir's deputy will assume his interim duties.

Why it mattersPresident Déby Itno took disciplinary action against military and administrative leaders and ordered civilian disarmament due to a conflict over resources.

Chad cadastre regularises land titles

The process involves traditional leaders and multiple government departments to confirm land origins and boundaries.

Chad's land registry is undertaking a lengthy process to regularise land titles, involving multiple government departments and traditional authorities. Director of Cadastre Ahmat Mahamat Makaïla stated that this procedure is frequent and often concerns plots acquired from customary owners, known as 'boulama', or village chiefs. The goal is to establish land origin, precisely identify parcels, and advance files towards official land titles.

The regularisation process begins with a user's application and its registration, followed by a physical identification of the plot by a technician. Confirmation from traditional authorities is a required step before the file is sent to relevant services for study, including verification in the GIRAF database. Makaïla noted that this procedure requires numerous checks and mobilises several services.

The Cadastre department, under the Ministry of Land Management, Urban Planning and Housing, aims to prevent conflicts by ensuring clear land ownership. The detailed steps are designed to confirm the legitimacy of land acquisition and the precise boundaries of each parcel before a title is issued.

Why it mattersLand owners in Chad face a complex and lengthy process to secure official titles for their properties, which can involve traditional leaders and multiple government checks.

Chad constitutional council adopts AI tools

Agents trained on artificial intelligence applications for administrative and legal work.

The Constitutional Council of Chad has adopted artificial intelligence tools following a five-day training session held from November 3 to November 7, 2026. Agents from various departments participated in the training at the Kempinski Hotel, learning about AI's potential, practical uses, and necessary precautions for implementation in a legal and institutional setting.

Organized with support from the European Union's PAPPE project, the training aimed to enhance staff skills and promote responsible technology use, respecting law, confidentiality, and data protection. Council President Jean-Bernard Padaré described the initiative as a first for Chad's institutions, emphasizing AI's ability to improve efficiency and speed up administrative and legal tasks.

Padaré stressed that while AI can assist with tasks like drafting, research, and translation, it must not replace human judgment or responsibility. The council prioritizes security, confidentiality, and legal compliance in adopting these new technologies to support its constitutional and electoral duties.

Club RFI Mongo educates Chad students on digital tools

Students in Mongo's Guéra province attended a workshop on using digital resources for learning and success.

The Club RFI Mongo, with the Taalim Tchad platform, held a digital literacy workshop on Saturday, November 7, 2026, for students at lycées A and B in Mongo, Guéra province. The event, part of the Digital November celebrations, aimed to show students how to use digital tools to improve their studies and achieve academic success.

Idriss Mamadou Brahim, president of Club RFI Mongo, spoke at the session, emphasizing the role of digital resources in shaping young people and broadening their global perspectives. The workshop focused on the theme "Youth and education in the digital age: inform, learn, and succeed through digital tools."

The initiative encourages students to leverage digital opportunities for enhanced learning. This event highlights the growing importance of digital skills for educational advancement in Chad.

Why it mattersStudents in Chad's Guéra province learned how to use digital tools to improve their education and academic success.

Road accident kills three in Chad's Mandoul

A tyre burst caused a vehicle to overturn on the Nderguigui axis, resulting in multiple injuries.

Three people died and several others were injured in a road accident on November 7, 2026, in Chad's Mandoul province. The crash occurred on the Nderguigui axis.

Mandoul central prefect Oumar Issaka Fouzari stated that a tyre burst caused the vehicle to lose control and overturn. The victims, mostly traders heading to the Bedjondo market, received initial treatment at the Péni health centre.

The bodies of the deceased were taken to the Koumra morgue. This accident has reignited discussions about road safety and the need for drivers to be more cautious.

Why it mattersThe accident directly affected traders and their families in Chad.
