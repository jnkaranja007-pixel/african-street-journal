Chad Province Lifts Emergency Measures, Maintains Night Patrols

The governor of Lac Province officially ended the state of emergency and curfew, which had been in place since May, but security forces will continue night patrols.

The state of emergency and curfew in Chad's Lac Province have been officially lifted, according to a decree dated August 27, 2026. General Saleh Haggar Tidjani, the government delegate for the province, announced the end of these measures, which had been in effect since May. The curfew, which previously restricted movement between eight p.m. and six a.m., is now abrogated, allowing residents to resume normal activities.

The provincial authority's decision marks the end of an emergency period that began on May 6, 2026, and was extended by ninety days on May 26. Security services have been instructed to facilitate the population's return to daily routines. Despite the lifting of restrictions, authorities are maintaining continuous night patrols to ensure the area's security and apprehend individuals posing a threat to public tranquility.

Residents have been urged to remain vigilant at all levels to prevent any potential incidents. The move aims to restore normalcy while ensuring continued security in the region. The delegate general emphasized the need for ongoing security operations to maintain peace and safety for the inhabitants of Lac Province.

Why it mattersResidents of Lac Province can now move freely at night, but must remain vigilant due to ongoing security patrols.

General Hissein Brahim Mahamat Itno Dies

The former head of state security's general directorate and a figure in Chad's security forces passed away on August 27, 2026.

General Hissein Brahim Mahamat Itno died during the night of August 27, 2026. He was a former chief of staff for the general directorate of state institutions security services (DGSSIE) and a significant figure within Chad's security apparatus.

Mahamat Itno had a career primarily within the state security services. He previously served as an aide-de-camp to Marshal Idriss Déby Itno. He was recognized for his professional rigor and held several notable command positions throughout his service.

His past roles included leading the DGSSIE and later directing the National Military Equipment Manufacturing Company (MANEM). His death marks the passing of a long-serving official within the country's security and defense sectors.

Why it mattersThe death of a former high-ranking security official and director of a military manufacturing company may affect state security and defense sector leadership.

Algeria Offers 450 Scholarships to Chadian Students

The Algerian government has opened applications for 450 university scholarships for Chadian high school graduates who meet specific academic and age requirements.

The National Center for University Works (CNOU) announced that applications are now open for 450 scholarships offered by Algeria to Chadian high school graduates from scientific streams C, D, and E, who completed the June 2026 exams. This initiative provides a new opportunity for young Chadian graduates seeking higher education in Algerian public universities for the 2026-2027 academic year.

Eligibility for these scholarships requires candidates to have achieved a general baccalaureate average of 12 out of 20 or higher. Additionally, applicants must demonstrate an average of at least 10 out of 20 in core scientific subjects, including mathematics, physics-chemistry, and biology. Candidates must also be between 18 and 23 years old.

Interested candidates who meet these criteria are instructed to prepare their application files and submit them to the CNOU, located on Sahoulba Gontchomé Avenue in Klémat. The deadline for submitting applications is Friday, September 4, 2026, at noon.

Why it mattersChadian students who have recently graduated from high school are affected as they can apply for scholarships to pursue higher education in Algeria.

Syria processes first international card payment in 15 years

Mastercard and QNB Group facilitated the transaction, reconnecting Syria's payment system to the global network after a long period of isolation.

Syria has reconnected to international card payments for the first time in over fifteen years, following a successful transaction processed by Mastercard and QNB Group. This event marks a significant step in modernizing the country's payment infrastructure. QNB Syria processed the initial transaction on a point-of-sale terminal with an eligible local merchant using an internationally issued Mastercard.

The successful transaction establishes the foundation for accepting international payments within Syria, aiming to integrate consumers, businesses, and financial institutions into the global digital economy. This development is expected to support the country's long-term economic growth. The Governor of the Central Bank of Syria, S.E. Mohammed Safwat Raslan, stated that this advancement demonstrates the value of public-private collaboration in enabling secure international payment acceptance and facilitating trade.

Yousef Mahmoud Al-Neama, Group Chief Business Officer of QNB Group, highlighted that providing merchants with access to international payment capabilities allows businesses to offer more choices and convenience to customers. Adam Jones, President of Mastercard's West Arabia division, described the transaction as a crucial step towards a more connected, secure, and inclusive payment environment in Syria, paving the way for broader acceptance of international cards.

Why it mattersSyrian businesses and consumers can now engage more directly with the global digital economy through international card transactions.

Mali Government Expresses Solidarity with Algeria Over Deadly Fires

Bamako sent condolences and affirmed support to Algeria following devastating wildfires that caused significant damage and loss of life.

The Malian government officially expressed its deep compassion and sincere condolences to the Algerian authorities and people on Friday, August 28, 2026. This gesture follows the recent deadly wildfires that have struck Algeria, causing widespread destruction.

In a formal statement, Bamako acknowledged the victims of these devastating incidents, which also resulted in numerous injuries and substantial material damage. The government of Mali reaffirmed its full solidarity during this difficult time for the Algerian nation.

Mali also offered prayers for the eternal rest of the deceased and wished for a swift recovery for those injured in the tragedy. The official communication highlighted the shared sense of loss and support between the two nations.

Why it mattersThe Malian government's expression of solidarity with Algeria over deadly fires affects diplomatic relations and international cooperation between the two countries.
