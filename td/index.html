Health · 2026-11-02

Chad health minister inspects dengue response in N'Djamena

Dr. Abdelmadjid Abderahim visited hospitals to check diagnostic kits and patient care amid a dengue outbreak.

Chad's Minister of Public Health and Prevention, Dr. Abdelmadjid Abderahim, visited health facilities in N'Djamena on Sunday, November 1, 2026, to assess the response to a dengue epidemic. He toured the Union of Chagoua, Notre-Dame des Apôtres, and Sultan Kasser hospitals, inspecting emergency services, pharmacies, and laboratories.

During his visit, the minister examined suspect case investigation forms and confirmed the availability of rapid diagnostic test kits. He praised the healthcare workers' efforts and stressed the importance of prevention, warning the public against self-medication for dengue symptoms.

Dr. Abderahim urged health officials to increase public awareness campaigns about the disease. The ministry announced the visit on its Facebook page, detailing the minister's focus on ensuring the capacity of health structures to detect and treat patients effectively.

Why it mattersThe health minister's visit directly addresses the public health crisis of a dengue epidemic in N'Djamena, affecting the population's well-being.

Chad arrests 39 after deadly intercommunal clashes

Government ministers visited Ngoura after 25 people died and 22 were injured in violence between two communities.

Government ministers visited the Ngoura department in Hadjer-Lamis on Saturday, October 31, 2026, following deadly violence between two communities. 39 people were transferred to N'Djamena to face judicial authorities after the clashes. The violence, which occurred between the Hadjer-Lamis and Bahr El-Ghazal provinces, resulted in 25 deaths and 22 injuries.

The delegation, led by the Minister of Public Security and Immigration, General Ali Ahmat Aghabach, and the Minister Delegate for Decentralization, Dr. Ahmat Oumar Ahmat, arrived the day after the confrontations. Authorities stated their commitment to fully investigate the events, identify responsibilities, and ensure justice is served. Security measures are being implemented to de-escalate tensions and protect residents.

The government aims to foster dialogue between the communities to achieve lasting peace. The intervention follows intercommunal clashes that have caused significant loss of life and injury.

Why it mattersIntercommunal violence in Chad has led to deaths, injuries, and arrests, with government intervention to restore peace.

Education · 2026-11-02

Chad teachers demand better pay and conditions

Teachers cite overcrowded classrooms, poor facilities, and unpaid bonuses as reasons for their dissatisfaction.

Teachers across Chad, including in N'Djamena and other provinces, are increasingly dissatisfied due to worsening working conditions. Many face financial, professional, and material difficulties, with a lack of textbooks forcing them to rely on note-taking. Classes are often held in vulnerable makeshift shelters or dilapidated buildings.

Abdoulaye Mahamat Saleh, an Arabic teacher, stated that public sector teachers will only be satisfied with substantial improvements, including the repayment of withheld salary portions and bonuses for housing and supplies. English teacher Assan Hadji Kalssou highlighted the challenge of managing classes of 70 to 100 students, making discipline and individual follow-up impossible.

Abdallah Djibrine Adam urged the government to implement existing decrees regarding career advancements and salary payments. Teacher unions like the Syndicat des Enseignants du Tchad (SET) are demanding renewed social dialogue and the full application of the decree on teacher status, alongside regular payments for community teachers and the lifting of the study leave moratorium.

Why it mattersTeachers and students in Chad are directly affected by the poor state of educational facilities and resources, impacting the quality of instruction.

Culture · 2026-11-02

Chad mourns writer Isaac Tédambé

The 72-year-old author of "République à vendre" was laid to rest in his home village.

The body of Chadian writer Isaac Tédambé, who died in N’Djaména on October 28, 2026, at age 72, left the capital on Saturday, October 31, for Ganli, his native village in Mayo Kebbi Ouest. A tribute ceremony was held at his home in the Amtoukouin neighborhood before his final journey.

Close relatives, neighbors, friends, and members of the literary community gathered at Tédambé’s residence. Speakers remembered him as a committed man, a novelist attentive to social and political realities, and as a father, friend, and colleague. Literary figures highlighted his unique place in national literature.

Isaac Tédambé, born March 3, 1954, was an economist by training, a novelist, and a painter. He became known for his 2003 novel "République à vendre," which became one of his most recognized works.

Why it mattersChadian readers and the literary community are mourning the loss of Isaac Tédambé, a prominent writer and painter.

Agriculture · 2026-11-02

Chad officials set strict rules for herders

Loug-Chari department secretary-general bans nomadic herders before December 15 to prevent farmer-herder clashes.

Sougouma Hassaballah Ahmat, secretary-general of Chad's Loug-Chari department, has issued strict measures to manage pastoralist mobility and prevent conflicts with farmers during the 2026 agricultural campaign. A directive signed on October 26, 2026, prohibits nomadic herders from entering the Loug-Chari zone before December 15, 2026.

Local authorities and security forces are tasked with enforcing these rules, which follow a September 28, 2026, circular from the Vice-Prime Minister. The measures encourage dialogue between communities to define herding corridors and establish crop release calendars to minimize crop destruction and potential clashes. Any incidents must be officially documented within 24 hours.

A follow-up system requires situation reports every 12 days to ensure compliance. These actions aim to protect agricultural production and organize livestock movement through coordinated planning, fostering peaceful coexistence between farmers and herders.

Why it mattersFarmers in the Loug-Chari department face potential crop destruction and conflict with herders.
