Chad president reshapes judiciary with new appointments

A presidential decree names new judges and prosecutors across courts and tribunals nationwide.

President Idriss Déby Itno has issued a decree appointing new magistrates to various judicial positions across Chad. The appointments, made after consultation with the Superior Council of the Magistracy, affect courts of appeal, and tribunals of major instance, commerce, labour, and peace. These changes redraw the composition of judicial seats and public prosecutor's offices throughout the country.

In N'Djamena, new councillors were named to the Court of Appeal, including Baizebo Tao and Mme Damnoji Mbaidouboutar. The General Prosecutor's office at the Court of Appeal will see Adoum Sarre Daye serve as 1st Substitute General. At the Tribunal of Major Instance, judges like Abdelkerim Bouri Azakir and Mme Hanan Saleh Mahmout were appointed, alongside new judges for instruction cabinets.

Judicial roles were also filled in other regions, with Mahamat Alchazali Saleh becoming Vice-President of the Bongor Tribunal of Major Instance and Atteib Mahamat Abakar appointed President of the Bokoro Tribunal of Major Instance. The decree, N° 2935 /PR/CSM/2026, also saw appointments in Guelengdeng, Moundou, Abéché, Iriba, Faya, Guéréda, and Tine.

Why it mattersJudicial appointments by the president affect the administration of justice for all citizens appearing before Chadian courts.

Father sues ex-wife over daughter's circumcision

Police detained Mariam Youssouf Seid after Zenab Mahamat Tahir, aged three, was allegedly circumcised without consent.

A father in Chad has filed a complaint against his ex-wife for allegedly circumcising their three-year-old daughter, Zenab Mahamat Tahir, without his consent. The accused, Mariam Youssouf Seid, was taken into preventive detention on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, following the complaint filed on Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

The alleged genital mutilation occurred last year when the child was three years old. Police sources confirmed the information, which was also verified by the plaintiff, Mahamat Tahir Moustapha. The detention is intended to allow for a medical examination before the case proceeds to the justice system.

Mahamat Tahir Moustapha stated that the procedure was performed while he was on a mission outside Chad, and he believes it will have long-term negative health consequences for his daughter. He also noted that female genital mutilation is prohibited by Chadian law.

Why it mattersA father is suing his ex-wife after their three-year-old daughter underwent female genital mutilation without his consent, an act prohibited by Chadian law.

Chad officials meet to manage herder arrivals

Loug-Chari department officials are planning a mission to inform nomadic herders about arrival dates.

Officials in Chad's Loug-Chari department met on Friday, November 6, 2026, to plan how to inform nomadic herders about arrival restrictions. The meeting, chaired by Sougouma Hassaballah Ahmat, president of the Departmental Peace Committee (CODEPAR), aimed to ensure herders do not arrive before December 15, 2026.

The meeting focused on strategies to widely communicate the official message to herders. This initiative seeks to prevent potential conflicts between pastoral and farming communities and maintain peace. CODEPAR aims to facilitate peaceful coexistence between farmers and herders in the region.

The decision prohibits herder arrivals in the Loug-Chari department before December 15, 2026. The meeting was held at the ANADER offices in Bousso.

Why it mattersNomadic herders in Chad's Loug-Chari department are directly affected by new arrival date restrictions aimed at preventing conflict.
