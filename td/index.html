Chad bans shops and stalls in and around schools

Prime Minister Allah-Maye Halina’s circular also bars telecoms pylons, advertising signs and non-school events at public and private schools.

Chad’s Prime Minister, Amb. Allah-Maye Halina, signed a circular on 16 November 2026 banning commercial and non-school activities inside and around public and private schools. It prohibits street stalls, kiosks, shops and mobile vendors, as well as bars and drinking venues.

The circular also bars telecommunications pylons and advertising signs, gambling and card-playing spaces, and livestock raising unless it is part of an approved educational project. Halina said unauthorised installations threaten pupils’ physical safety and disrupt lessons.

Administrative, municipal and education authorities are to survey irregular installations, carry out necessary clearances and keep school grounds free of them. The circular also calls for regular monitoring, citing a 4 November 2024 law on the division of powers.

Why it mattersAdministrative, municipal and education authorities must identify and clear irregular installations, then monitor school grounds regularly.

Health · 2026-11-18

Chad's BASE trains Mandoul health workers on family planning and post-abortion care

The session, funded by the French Development Agency under the PASFASS2 project, is meant to sharpen providers' practical skills in sexual and reproductive health.

The Bureau d'Appui Santé et Environnement (BASE) is running a training session in Mandoul province for health providers on family planning and post-abortion care, under the PASFASS2 project.

The French Development Agency (AFD) is financing the session, which BASE says is meant to consolidate providers' technical and practical skills. Participants are being asked to refine their practice so that care is appropriate, effective and respectful of patients' rights.

The training also stresses continuity of services, compliance with national standards and a setting that protects patients' dignity. BASE says the project aims to widen access to essential services for the population.

Why it mattersHealth workers in Mandoul are being trained to deliver family planning and post-abortion care that meets national standards.

Chad trains girls in digital tools in Moyen-Chari

The 17 November session in Sarh covered phone basics, online sales and ways to protect personal data, organisers said.

Chad’s digital transformation support project, with consultancy Ebenyx, held a digital-skills session for girls and women on 17 November at the Ngarta Tombalbaye Palace of Arts and Culture in Sarh, the capital of Moyen-Chari province.

Participants learned basic mobile-phone functions, internet communication, selling products through social media and how to protect personal data. Layibé Patalet, an Ebenyx cybersecurity engineer who led the training, said some women own phones without knowing their full range of functions.

The organisers said the session was intended to improve participants’ use of digital tools in daily life and economic activities, including presenting goods online, finding customers and receiving orders. Participants also heard about scams, fake profiles, fraudulent messages and account theft.

Why it mattersParticipants learned to present products, find customers and receive orders online without opening a shop.

Education · 2026-11-18

African Union education centre reviews girls’ schooling in N’Djamena

The delegation examined school re-entry measures, programmes for girls out of school and the results of the #AfricaEducatesHer campaign during a 11-13 November mission.

A delegation from the African Union International Centre for the Education of Girls and Women in Africa completed an evaluation mission in N’Djamena, Chad, from 11 to 13 November 2026. It reviewed the situation of girls’ education and the implementation of the continental #AfricaEducatesHer campaign.

Led by programme officer Boubakar Yougbare, the team met Chad’s education officials and visited schools, a girls’ vocational training centre and a centre for survivors of gender-based violence. The delegation also examined school re-entry arrangements and programmes for girls who are not enrolled, while authorities outlined ongoing initiatives and progress.

The mission included discussions with international organisations and civil-society groups. The centre said the visit was supported by the World Bank through the East Africa Girls’ Empowerment and Resilience project; the delegation also identified mothers’ education associations, hygiene kits and listening centres among practices in use.

Why it mattersChadian education officials and schools were reviewed on measures to return girls to school and serve those who are not enrolled during the 11-13 November mission.

Education · 2026-11-18

Chadian professor elected secretary-general of francophone medical deans’ conference

Foumsou Lhagadang won the post in Bangui after an election process involving 19 countries, giving Chad a representative in the conference leadership.

Chadian professor Foumsou Lhagadang was elected secretary-general of the Conference of Deans of French-Speaking African Medical Faculties in Bangui, Central African Republic. The election process involved 19 countries.

Lhagadang is dean of the Faculty of Health Sciences at the University of N’Djamena and heads obstetrics at the Mother and Child University Hospital. The conference brings together deans of French-speaking medical faculties, with cooperation focused in part on training and medical education.

He has trained generations of doctors and helped develop obstetrics and gynaecology in Chad, according to Tchadinfos. His new role is at a continental body whose work includes strengthening medical education.

Why it mattersDeans from medical faculties across 19 countries took part in the election, and Lhagadang's new role links Chad to their cooperation on medical training.
