Ethiopia Finance Minister, IFC Director Boost Private Investment

Ethiopia's Finance Minister and the International Finance Corporation's director met to increase private sector investment in the country.

Ethiopian Finance Minister Ahmed Shide met with Makhtar Diop, the managing director of the International Finance Corporation (IFC), a World Bank subsidiary, on September 2, 2026. The meeting aimed to strengthen bilateral cooperation and boost private sector investment in Ethiopia. Minister Shide presented the progress of the government's macroeconomic reforms, highlighting their positive impact on the business climate and private opportunities.

Diop acknowledged the government's reform efforts and reiterated the IFC's commitment to supporting Ethiopia's development priorities. The IFC's current portfolio in Ethiopia stands at $500 million, spread across sectors like agribusiness, manufacturing, financial services, health, tourism, and telecommunications. Both officials discussed priority areas for enhanced partnership, including public-private partnerships and investments in transport, energy, and housing infrastructure.

The IFC also confirmed its intention to support significant projects, such as the future Bishoftu International Airport, the development of independent power producers, and the mobilization of large-scale private capital. This collaboration signals a concerted effort to attract and facilitate private investment across key sectors of the Ethiopian economy.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the confirmed commitment of the IFC to support infrastructure projects and mobilize private capital, potentially increasing investment opportunities.

Chad Sub-prefect Assesses Health Facilities, Cites Informal Care

A mission is evaluating seven health centers in Lac-Iro sub-prefecture to address issues like low official facility use and informal medical practices.

Chad's Lac-Iro sub-prefecture is undergoing a health facility assessment, with a mission launched on September 1, 2026, to evaluate services and identify challenges. The tour, scheduled until September 19, will visit seven health centers to review patient care, agent difficulties, and potential improvements. The initial stop at the Bohobé health center revealed that many residents seek care from unlicensed vendors, unauthorized practitioners, or traditional healers, posing significant health risks.

Health officials also noted insufficient prenatal consultations among pregnant women, crucial for preventing complications. Structural issues, including a lack of human and financial resources, difficulties supporting auxiliary staff, and the proliferation of informal clinics in villages, further complicate access to safe healthcare. Sub-prefect Doumhassem Gedéon emphasized the need to regulate these informal practices and ensure care adheres to standards.

Gedéon urged traditional and community leaders to raise awareness among residents, encouraging them to utilize official health structures. He reiterated a circular note prohibiting the unregulated opening of health facilities, warning that non-compliance would result in legal sanctions. The mission will continue its tour, with the next visit planned for September 3 at the Boli health center.

Why it mattersResidents of Lac-Iro sub-prefecture are affected by the availability and safety of healthcare services, with a focus on improving official facility access and regulating informal medical practices.

Minister Launches Road Safety Brigade to Protect Travelers

Chad's Minister of Transport, Aviation, and Meteorology has initiated a new road safety unit aimed at increasing traffic control and prevention efforts.

Fatima Goukouni Weddeye, Chad's Minister of Transport, Aviation, and Meteorology, officially launched the active road safety brigade of the National Road Safety Office (ONASER) on September 2. This new unit comprises agents from the National Police, the National Gendarmerie, and ONASER personnel.

The brigade's primary mission is to intensify prevention, control, and traffic surveillance actions to better protect road users. Dr. Zam-Zam Ahmat Karambal, Director General of ONASER, emphasized that achieving this goal requires collective effort and synergy among all stakeholders.

Karambal stated that road safety is inherently a collective endeavor, not the sole responsibility of one institution. She called for constant coordination and mobilization from all parties involved to work together in raising awareness and preventing accidents on the roads.

Why it mattersRoad users in Chad are affected by the new brigade's focus on increased traffic control and prevention efforts to enhance safety.

Niamey Governor Bans Military Uniforms for Civilians

Governor Assoumane Abdou Harouna issued a decree prohibiting civilians from wearing military attire, citing security concerns and imposing severe penalties for violations.

The governorate of Niamey has formally forbidden civilians from wearing military uniforms, including insignia, across the region. This directive, issued under communiqué N°2026-001/GRN by Governor General Assoumane Abdou Harouna, aims to address security challenges. The ban comes after observations of young civilians wearing complete military outfits during various ceremonies, a practice deemed incompatible with the current security situation.

The measure strictly prohibits the wearing of military uniforms throughout Niamey region, with exceptions only for government members possessing special authorization. The Niamey City Police Directorate has been tasked with ensuring strict enforcement of these new regulations. Authorities warn that any breach of this prohibition will be met with the full force of the law.

This decision reflects a heightened awareness of security risks and aims to prevent potential misuse or misrepresentation associated with military attire. The governorate emphasizes that adherence to this ban is crucial for maintaining order and security within the region.

Why it mattersCivilians in Niamey are affected by this ban as they can no longer wear military uniforms, facing legal repercussions if they do.

Association Launches Music Tech Hub in Moundou

ARC EN ZIK aims to provide digital music production training to young people outside the capital city.

The cultural association ARC EN ZIK launched the first cohort of Music Tech Hub in Moundou on Tuesday, September 1. This initiative is dedicated to training young people in digital music production professions. The project addresses the observation that while musical talent exists across Chad, access to tools, technical skills, and training opportunities is concentrated in N’Djamena.

Djerambeté Medehodjingar, project coordinator for the association, explained that a young person from Moundou, Sarh, Abéché, or any other city should not have to travel to N’Djamena to begin acquiring skills in digital music production. Music Tech Hub establishes a space in Moundou for learning, practicing, and sharing knowledge related to digital music.

Participants in the Music Tech Hub program have access to necessary equipment for music creation, including computers, MIDI keyboards, and headphones. This program aims to decentralize access to music production education and resources within Chad.

Why it mattersYoung Chadians in Moundou and other cities are affected by gaining access to digital music production training and equipment locally.
