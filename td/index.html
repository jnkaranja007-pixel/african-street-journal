Agriculture · 2026-10-22

Chad launches project to boost legume and fodder crop value chains

The TARSPro II project aims to empower women economically and improve food security in Mayo-Kebbi Ouest province.

The TARSPro II project, lot 2, was officially launched on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, in Pala, Mayo-Kebbi Ouest province. The initiative, presided over by the provincial government delegate Abdelmanane Kathab, focuses on intensifying legume and fodder crop value chains. Its objectives include the economic empowerment of women, enhancing agricultural production, and strengthening nutritional security within the province.

Neyom Toukoua Djaba, the consortium coordinator and president of the Association of Women for Self-Promotion (AFAP), stated that the launch represents a significant step for the province's agricultural transformation. TARSPro II, an acronym for 'Agricultural Technologies and Innovations for Increased Resilience of Production Systems and Family Farms,' is funded by the Swiss Directorate for Development and Cooperation (DDC).

The project is being implemented by the West and Central African Council for Agricultural Research and Development (CORAF) across five countries: Chad, Niger, Benin, Burkina Faso, and Mali. This initiative seeks to build resilience in agricultural systems and support family farms through technological advancements and innovative practices.

Why it mattersFarmers in Mayo-Kebbi Ouest province will benefit from improved agricultural production and economic empowerment through this project.

South Sudan condemns deadly market bombing in Abyei

Juba demands an investigation and emergency humanitarian access after an October 19 airstrike on Amiet market caused civilian casualties and damage.

The South Sudanese government has strongly condemned an airstrike on Amiet market in the Abyei region on October 19, 2026. The attack resulted in significant loss of life, numerous injuries, and substantial material damage. The Office of the President issued a statement on October 21, expressing condolences to the bereaved families and solidarity with the people of Abyei, emphasizing that markets, vital for community commerce and sustenance, should not be sites of conflict.

In its official communication, Juba called for an immediate cessation of violence that endangers civilians. The government is demanding a thorough investigation to bring those responsible to justice. Furthermore, it is requesting secure access for medical personnel and humanitarian aid to reach the affected area. The authorities also urged the public to remain calm and reject retaliation.

South Sudan affirmed its commitment to protecting its citizens and announced coordinated investigations into the incident. The government aims to prevent future attacks and ensure the safety of its people. The condemnation highlights the ongoing security concerns in the Abyei region and the government's stance against such violent acts impacting civilian populations.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese civilians in Abyei are directly affected by the bombing of a market, leading to casualties and damage.

African Union Commission president reaffirms human rights commitment

Mahmoud Ali Youssouf pledged continued dedication to dignity, justice, and equality on the 40th anniversary of the African Charter.

Mahmoud Ali Youssouf, president of the African Union Commission, reaffirmed the institution's commitment to human dignity, justice, and equality. This statement was made on the occasion of Africa Human Rights Day, which this year commemorates the 40th anniversary of the entry into force of the African Charter.

Youssouf emphasized the commission's dedication to upholding the principles of human rights and fundamental freedoms, as outlined in the Union's instruments. He highlighted the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights as a cornerstone of the African human rights framework.

The day's observance underscores the ongoing importance of these rights and freedoms across the continent. The 40th anniversary of the charter's implementation serves as a significant milestone in the pursuit of these values.

Why it mattersChadian citizens are affected by the reaffirmation of human rights commitments, which impacts their dignity, justice, and equality.

Education · 2026-10-22

Chad youth face bleak future due to poor health and education

Young Chadians are seen as a burden, with public services in health and education increasingly outsourced to the private sector.

Chad's youth, comprising 60% of the active population, are largely overlooked in public policy, despite being lauded for their resilience. Instead of being viewed as a potential asset, this demographic is often treated as a social burden, with their future jeopardized by a lack of adequate health and education. Many young people are falling prey to drug abuse, recruitment by terrorist groups and traffickers, or dying in perilous journeys across deserts and seas.

The health of young Chadians is a significant concern, with widespread issues including infections like STIs and tuberculosis, malnutrition, and psychosomatic disorders, diabetes, and hypertension linked to stress. Education is also suffering, as the state, overwhelmed, has indirectly outsourced these critical services to the private sector. Without effective oversight, private institutions are providing substandard services, often of lower quality than what public facilities would offer.

This shift towards private provision means that schools, clinics, and hospitals are operating as commercial entities, substituting for the state's role. This trend has extended into security and even justice, indicating a broader withdrawal of public services. The consequence is a generation facing diminished opportunities due to compromised health and education, impacting their ability to thrive in a knowledge-based global landscape.

Why it mattersYoung Chadians are affected by the poor quality of health and education services, limiting their future prospects.
