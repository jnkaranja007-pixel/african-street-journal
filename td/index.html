Chad launches land dispute chambers

New judicial bodies aim to secure property rights and reduce conflicts over land.

Chad officially launched its land chambers on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, to secure property and improve dispute resolution. The Ministry of Justice, in partnership with the Ministry of Urban Planning, established these chambers within judicial jurisdictions across the country.

Justice Minister Ndolenodji Alixe Naïmbaye stated that the chambers will facilitate real-time information exchange between courts and land services. She emphasized that while technical expertise will inform judges, the courts will retain independent decision-making power, urging citizens to use legal channels instead of private justice or violence.

The Minister of Urban Planning, Mahamat Assileck Halata, described the reform as a response to the President's concerns about recurring land conflicts. He expressed hope for the future creation of a dedicated land tribunal, noting the commitment of the land registry and magistrates involved.

Why it mattersThe new land chambers will directly affect Chadian citizens by providing a legal framework for resolving property disputes and preventing violence.

N'Djamena university students announce four-day strike over buses and restaurants

The National Union of Chadian Students says the strike runs from November 10 to 13, 2026, over unmet demands at the University of N'Djamena.

The National Union of Chadian Students (UNET), the union's section at the University of N'Djamena, has called a strike from November 10 to November 13, 2026. The union said in a statement issued on November 10 that its main demands have not been met.

Those demands include the effective running of buses on university routes and the reopening of university restaurants. UNET says the strike is meant to press the National Center for University Services (CNOU) to take responsibility for the student community.

The packet does not give further details on talks, responses from the CNOU or the number of students taking part.

Why it mattersStudents at the University of N'Djamena face four days without the campus bus and restaurant services they have been demanding.

ARED brings local authorities and civil society together to prevent conflict in Mandoul

The meeting in Koumra on November 10, 2026, gathered administrative and military officials with civil society groups to discuss how to keep community tensions from turning.

The Association for the Promotion of Education and Development (ARED) held a meeting on November 10, 2026, at the Champagnat cultural centre in Koumra, in Chad's Mandoul province. The gathering brought together administrative and military authorities and civil society organisations to discuss the conflicts affecting the province.

Madjiyera Ngar Alkoa, coordinator of ARED, which is implementing the project, said the meeting offered a rare chance to bring the actors closer together. He told participants that people must speak frankly and honestly so that field actions can be pooled.

The meeting was organised under the LAPIA project, which supports social cohesion and peaceful coexistence in southern Chad and has been running for two years. Three themes were debated: justice and social cohesion, the role of civil society in strengthening cohesion, and conflict prevention through stronger coordination between actors.

Why it mattersResidents of Mandoul, where community tensions are the focus, are the ones whose local mechanisms for preventing conflict the meeting is meant to strengthen.

Sport · 2026-11-11

Chad football shows signs of revival

Journalist Mbaigolmem Aubain says a true renaissance requires unity among football stakeholders.

Chad's national football league is restarting after years of difficulty, with the national team, the Sao, entering competitions. The new federal team has also stated ambitions, creating hope for the sport.

However, sports journalist Mbaigolmem Aubain stated on Tchadinfos that these steps are more of a "surge of pride" than a full revival. He noted positive signals but stressed that a true renaissance depends on all football actors speaking with one voice.

Aubain identified a lack of cohesion and conflicting interests among football stakeholders as the main barrier to rebuilding the sport. He described the current environment as "clan-like" and divisive, hindering development efforts.

Why it mattersChad's football stakeholders must unite for the sport to truly recover.

Fire destroys dozens of homes and part of a market in Chad village

Witnesses say strong winds carried flames from one compound to the next in Koubo Azarack, Salamat province, on Tuesday, November 10, 2026.

A violent fire destroyed part of Koubo Azarack village, about 40 kilometres southeast of Am-Timan in Chad's Salamat province, on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. According to witnesses, several dozen homes and part of the village market were destroyed. The fire broke out around 9 a.m. and, pushed by strong wind, spread quickly from one compound to the next until it reached the market.

On site, blackened mud-brick walls, collapsed roofs, and burned jars and granaries lie among ash-covered ground. Families are sifting through the debris to recover a few belongings. The material toll has yet to be determined, and no loss of life has been reported so far.

Residents told the outlet the fire appears to have started from a hearth at the centre of the village. The exact cause has not been established at this stage.

Why it mattersFamilies who lost homes and market goods in Koubo Azarack must now rebuild with whatever they can salvage from the ash.
