Health · 2026-11-17

Chad launches free diabetes and blood pressure screening in N'Djamena

The five-day drive, which also introduces biometric patient enrolment, runs across several sites in the capital.

Chad's National Programme for the Fight against Diabetes and Non-Communicable Diseases launched a free screening campaign for diabetes and high blood pressure on Monday, 16 November 2026, at the Mother and Child Hospital in Toukra, N'Djamena. The five-day operation, run with the Chadian Society of Endocrinology, Metabolic Diseases and Nutrition, aims to catch cases early and bring health services closer to residents.

The national programme's coordinator, Dr Habiba Abdoulaye Affadine, cited a diabetes prevalence of 7% in 2024, based on figures from the International Diabetes Federation. She said late diagnosis, a shortage of specialised health workers and difficulty reaching quality care are among the main problems, and that complications from diabetes are a major cause of illness and death.

The campaign is also the first edition of the "3.0 Santé Tchad" caravan, which includes biometric enrolment of patients. Activities are planned in the 1st, 6th, 7th and 9th districts of the capital. The Sanofi GHU by SE project is supporting the effort by training health professionals and digitising care services.

Why it mattersPatients in the capital's 1st, 6th, 7th and 9th districts can get tested for free over five days, and be enrolled biometrically in the caravan.

Chad's Gondja festival spotlights second-hand clothing as a path to women's autonomy

The second edition of the Gondja festival runs from November 24 to 26 at the Talino Manu space in N'Djamena, with exhibitors presenting their goods to the public.

The organising committee of the Gondja festival announced at a press conference on Monday, November 16, 2026, that the second edition will take place from November 24 to 26 at the Talino Manu space. The event is built around the theme "Gondja au féminin: quand la friperie crée l'autonomie", which roughly translates as "Gondja for women: when second-hand clothing creates autonomy".

The artistic director, Nodjiwameem Doumdanem Nady, said that every second-hand garment and market stall reflects an economic reality often overlooked: women and young people who start businesses and see second-hand clothing as a route to independence. Over the three days, exhibitors will show a wide range of products, letting visitors meet people working in the sector.

The festival also includes a donation drive for orphanages, which Nady said shows that economic activity and community engagement can go together.

Why it mattersTraders and shoppers in the second-hand clothing sector can meet at Talino Manu from November 24 to 26, and donations collected will go to orphanages.

Education · 2026-11-17

AFD evaluates ALAPAJ program progress in Mandoul

A mission led by Sylvain Clément assessed infrastructure and training activities for local communities.

A mission from the Agence française de développement (AFD) visited Chad's Mandoul province to evaluate the progress of the ALAPAJ program. The mission, led by AFD official Sylvain Clément, assessed infrastructure and activities at non-formal basic training centers.

The AFD team met with local partners, including the NGO Enfants du Monde and operational teams from SODER-TCHAD and PADIESE, to discuss the program's impact on local communities. They reviewed results, construction progress, and any challenges encountered in project implementation.

This field visit aimed to ensure the effectiveness of the actions and evaluate improvements in training conditions and job prospects for participants, reaffirming the commitment of the AFD and the European Union to the ALAPAJ program's sustainability.

Why it mattersThe Agence française de développement and the European Union are assessing the impact of the ALAPAJ program on training and job prospects for Chadian communities.
