Chad launches housing plan as N'Djamena adds 20 000 households a year

The government's new programme targets homes and serviced plots, while the ministry says it will stop allocating undeveloped land.

Source: Alwihda Info · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Chad launched a national programme for resilient housing and planned urban development on Friday, 20 November 2026, at a housing meeting in N'Djamena. Housing Minister Mahamat Assilek Halata said the capital receives 20 000 new households each year.

The state, through the Sahel Property Fund (SOPROFIM), has produced 62 homes a year since 2015 for that volume of families, while preparing about 5 000 plots, Halata said. He put a new home at 20 to CFA26 million and said most civil servants earn between 100 000 and 200 000 francs a month; access to credit is nearly impossible for people in informal work.

The ministry said it would no longer grant undeveloped land because land and building materials are expensive. It has commissioned two studies to guide expansion toward Djarmaye and Koundoul.

Why it mattersFamilies arriving in N'Djamena face a housing supply of 62 state-built homes a year against 20 000 new households annually, and the ministry is shifting from undeveloped land grants.
