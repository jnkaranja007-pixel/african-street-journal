Lake Chad experts urge customs reforms for cross-border trade

The proposed changes include one-stop border posts and rules tailored to small businesses as insecurity and informal trade constrain a market of about 30 million people.

Source: RFI Afrique · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Experts at a recent regional forum in Yaoundé called for urgent reforms to make trade across the Lake Chad basin simpler and safer. The basin brings together Nigeria, Chad, Niger, the Central African Republic and Cameroon, and has a market of about 30 million people.

Sakalassou Loumouda, who runs a women’s cooperative in Yagoua, Cameroon, said customs officials on both sides of the Chad border make traders clear food products before they can sell them. The Lake Chad Basin Commission says women account for between 60 and 70% of cross-border traders. In Nigeria’s Borno state, they conduct 74% of the activity, despite threats from Boko Haram and other armed groups, the state’s planning minister said.

The UN Economic Commission for Africa recommends one-stop border posts, rules of origin suited to small businesses and a regional system to track non-tariff barriers, especially with the African Continental Free Trade Area in force.

Why it mattersWomen traders carrying food between Cameroon and Chad face customs procedures on both sides, while regional experts are pressing for one-stop border posts.
