18 African countries meet in Conakry on extending social protection

The four-day seminar, organised by the Conférence interafricaine de la prévoyance sociale (CIPRES), brings delegations from 18 African countries together.

Source: Guinee Matin · 2026-11-17 · 2 min read

A seminar on extending social protection to people who are not yet covered opened in Conakry on Monday, 16 November 2026. The Conférence interafricaine de la prévoyance sociale (CIPRES), a regional body for social security, is hosting delegations from 18 African countries, who will exchange experiences over four days on how to widen protection to populations that are insufficiently covered.

Bakary Sylla, director general of Guinea's Caisse nationale de sécurité sociale (CNSS), the national social security fund, said the seminar would help improve operational plans already under way and that other countries' successes and difficulties would inspire Guinea. He said the system was originally designed for salaried workers, while many citizens work in the informal sector or in rural areas, and that craftspeople, farmers, traders and self-employed professionals should be able to rely on national solidarity against sickness, old age and accidents.

Sylla said the CNSS launched an actuarial study with the International Labour Organization in 2023, which identified the risks to cover as sickness, old age and occupational hazards. Guinea's labour and social protection minister, Ramatoulaye Camara, said coverage across the CIPRES area is about 10 percent of the population, and she described reforms under way, starting with the legal and institutional framework.

Why it mattersInformal workers such as craftspeople, farmers and traders, who are not covered by the salaried-worker system, would gain protection against sickness, old age and accidents under the reforms discussed.
