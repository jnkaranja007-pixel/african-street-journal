Guinean court jails artist Jack Woumpak for ethnic insult online

A court in Coyah sentenced him to 24 months, with 18 suspended, after prosecutors had sought three years in prison without suspension.

Sources: Guinee Matin, Africaguinee and Mosaiqueguinee · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

A court in Coyah, in Guinea, sentenced the artist Jack Woumpak, whose real name is Jacques Gbignamou Haba, to 24 months in prison on Friday, 20 November, with 18 months suspended. He must serve six months.

The court convicted him of insulting people on racial or ethnic grounds and spreading unsubstantiated news through a computer system. Prosecutors had asked for three years in prison without suspension. He was arrested after a Facebook video in which he attacked, among others, the Malinke community, to which President Mamadi Doumbouya belongs.

This is not his first conviction of this kind. In June 2023 the same court gave him 12 months, suspended, and a fine of 10 million Guinean francs over remarks against the Peul community, and ordered the video removed and an apology published.

Why it mattersEthnic insult online now carries a jail term in Guinea, and Woumpak faces six months behind bars.
