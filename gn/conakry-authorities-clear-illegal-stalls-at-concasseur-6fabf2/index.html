Conakry authorities clear illegal stalls at Concasseur market

Officials in Gbessia commune cleared the Jakarta area of the market, where residents say drug use and prostitution had taken hold, to make room for a modern butcher shop.

Sources: Africaguinee and Guinee Matin · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Authorities launched a clearance operation on Saturday, November 14, 2026, at the Concasseur market in the Gbessia commune of Conakry. Local officials, representatives of the Directorate of Land Planning and Urban Development (DATU) and security forces were present to oversee the removal of structures illegally occupied inside and around the market.

Mamadou Aliou Barry, president of the Hamdallaye 1 neighbourhood council, said the area, known locally as "Jakarta", had become a place of prostitution and drug use, and that several young people who took the drug kush ended up in hospital and later died. The site is to host a modern butcher shop. Some occupants said they were given only 72 hours to leave, but Barry said the commune first notified the neighbourhood on October 9 and that residents were warned on site on October 10 and again a week later.

The operation is part of a wider public sanitation campaign to free public spaces, according to local officials. Barry said occupants had a month to prepare, a timeline the complaining occupants dispute.

Why it mattersTraders and occupants who built on the cleared site have lost their stalls, and the area is being reserved for a modern butcher shop.
