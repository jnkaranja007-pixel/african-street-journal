Truck insurance validity questioned in fatal accident trial

A court in Dubréka is examining the insurance policy of a truck involved in a July 30 accident that killed three people.

The trial of Alhassane Sow, a truck driver accused of involuntary manslaughter, continued on September 4 at the Dubréka Court of First Instance. The proceedings focused on civil liability and the validity of the truck's insurance. The plaintiff's legal team disputes the insurance policy presented, suggesting Vista Gui, identified as the vehicle's owner, should be held civilly responsible for the crash's consequences.

The civil party's lawyer argued that a truck insured on the same day it caused an accident was likely not insured at all. However, the defense maintains the policy covers the date and location of the incident. To resolve this dispute, the court has requested the insurance contract for review, aiming to verify coverage conditions and duration. The prosecutor suggested the coincidence of dates does not automatically invalidate the coverage.

The July 30 accident resulted in three fatalities, including two Chinese nationals and their Guinean driver. The civil party highlighted the impact on the victims' families and intends to seek damages. The court has adjourned the case until September 11, requiring both parties to submit additional documentation regarding the insurance policy and potential civil liabilities.

Why it mattersThe trial's outcome regarding insurance validity and civil liability could impact businesses operating commercial vehicles and their insurers.
