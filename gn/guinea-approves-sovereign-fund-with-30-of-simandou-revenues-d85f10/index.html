Guinea approves sovereign fund with 30% of Simandou revenues

The law directs 30% of the project’s tax and non-tax receipts to the fund and sets out contributions totalling 900 million dollars over five years.

Sources: Africaguinee, Mosaiqueguinee and Guinee Matin · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Guinea’s parliament approved a law on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, creating a sovereign fund that will receive 30% of tax and non-tax revenues from the Simandou mining project. The fund will also receive 5% of equivalent revenues from other extractive projects that begin production after the law takes effect.

The bill passed with 121 votes in favour, none against and two abstentions, according to the report presented to lawmakers. The state is to contribute 150 million dollars in each of the first two years and 200 million dollars in each of the next three, guaranteeing 900 million dollars over five years. The remaining 100 million dollars requires specified funding sources.

The fund is also intended to finance viable national and local projects, buffer economic shocks and preserve mining income for future generations.

Why it mattersThe state must specify funding sources for the remaining 100 million dollars of the fund’s five-year plan.
