Guinean journalist challenges Burkina Faso broadcaster's satire of Doumbouya over mining

Zakaria Camara says the editorial by Burkina Faso's public broadcaster (RTB) offered caricature and claims about justice without evidence or responses from those involved.

Source: Mosaiqueguinee · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Guinean journalist Zakaria Camara has criticised an editorial by Burkina Faso's public broadcaster, RTB, which satirised Guinea's President Mamadi Doumbouya and questioned justice in the country's gold sector. Camara wrote that the editorial's title promised a look at gold but instead prompted controversy over justice and a presidential caricature.

Camara said the editorial did not identify the court cases, decisions or established facts behind its claim of "two-speed justice". He also questioned its comparison of clandestine operators with licensed industrial investors, arguing that their legal status, duties and responsibilities differ. He said satire cannot replace investigation or evidence, and asked whether mining authorities, operators, investors or mining-law experts had been heard.

He said RTB had the right to criticise Guinea's public policy and use satire, but argued that its status as a public broadcaster called for discernment, rigour and responsibility. Camara is a journalist with CAVI MEDIA, a Guinean media outlet.

Why it mattersCamara is challenging the editorial's treatment of Guinea's mining policy and urging RTB to support its claims with evidence and responses from those involved.
