Mauritania intercepts 94 young Guineans bound for the Canary Islands

Mauritanian coast guards stopped the boat off Nouadhibou on 19 November, and the group is now being housed in Rosso while it waits for travel documents.

Source: Africaguinee · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Mauritanian coast guards intercepted 94 young Guineans off Nouadhibou on 19 November, aboard a boat heading for the Canary Islands. The group had left the artisanal port of Boffa, in Lower Guinea, on 13 November.

Mauritanian authorities said the boat was in distress when they intervened. The migrants were taken to Rosso, on the Mauritania-Senegal border, where several are reported to have serious health problems and to describe precarious living conditions.

Mohamed Keita, the local representative of the Council of Guineans in Mauritania, is hosting and caring for them while they wait for travel permits. The group is asking for help to return to Guinea. No deaths have been reported among those counted, though many cases of illness have been noted.

Why it mattersThe 94 young men and boys in Rosso cannot go home until travel permits are issued, and several are reported to be ill.
