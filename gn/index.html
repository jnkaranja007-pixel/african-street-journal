Guinean Government announces emergency measures after Dar-es-Salam landslide

Following a deadly landslide at the Dar-es-Salam landfill, the Guinean government has implemented emergency measures to secure the site, assist victims, and rehouse affected.

The Guinean government announced a series of emergency measures this Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, 2026, to address the aftermath of a landslide at the Dar-es-Salam landfill. These actions include securing the site, providing care for victims, and organizing the rehousing of populations at risk, as stated by the Minister of Territorial Administration and Decentralization, Madame Camara Djénabou Touré.

The measures involve deploying mixed teams to identify and survey at-risk homes near the landfill and immediately securing the perimeter to prevent further incidents. The Ministry of Health has set up medical provisions and is distributing mosquito nets to those affected. Additionally, a collective burial for the victims of the tragedy will be organized.

Currently, 542 displaced individuals are housed at Dixinn Centre 1 primary school, and approximately 1,500 people requiring rehousing have been identified. The government plans to demolish the Dar-es-Salam landfill completely in the coming days and identify new relocation sites across Conakry for the affected residents.

Why it mattersResidents of Dar-es-Salam are directly affected by displacement, loss of homes, and the need for new housing and medical support following the landslide.

Six bound bodies found in Forécariah mass grave

Six unidentified men were discovered blindfolded and with their hands tied in a mass grave in a forest area of Sabakhouré, Allassoyah sub-prefecture, on Monday, August.

The discovery of six bound and blindfolded male bodies in a mass grave in Sabakhouré has prompted an investigation by local authorities. The bodies, found in a forest area, were transported to the Forécariah prefectural hospital for examination to determine their identities and the circumstances of their deaths.

Ibrahima Soriba Touré, the local mayor, confirmed the discovery after being alerted by a sector chief about unusual signs at a work site. The sub-prefect, police commissioner, and prosecutor were informed and visited the scene, where they found the six unidentified individuals.

The incident has caused shock and concern among residents in the Forécariah prefecture. An inquiry is expected to shed light on the exact circumstances surrounding the deaths, as the identities of the victims and the cause of death remain unknown.

Why it mattersThe discovery of six unidentified bodies raises concerns about safety and security for residents in the Forécariah prefecture.

President Doumbouya decorates former CNT members for national service

President Mamadi Doumbouya awarded honorary distinctions to former members of the National Council of the Transition (CNT) for their service from January 22, 2022, to July 17.

Conakry, Guinea – President Mamadi Doumbouya has honored former members of the National Council of the Transition (CNT) with medals, according to decrees read on state media this Tuesday, August twenty-fifth. The decorations recognize “eminent services rendered to the Nation” by the recipients during their tenure.

The awards elevate several key figures from the transitional parliamentary institution to various national orders. Dr. Dansa Kourouma, former President of the CNT, was named Grand Officer of the National Order of the Kolatier. Professor Elhadj Hassan Bah, president of the Health, Education, Social Affairs, and Cultural Commission, received the rank of Commander of the National Order of the Kolatier.

Additionally, Madame Hadja Maimouna Yombouno, the first Vice-President of the CNT, was decorated as a Commander of the National Order of Merit. Other former cabinet members, bureau members, and advisors of the CNT were also elevated to the rank of Commander of the National Order of Merit for their contributions to the nation.

Why it mattersThis event affects former members of the National Council of the Transition by formally recognizing their service with national honors and distinctions.

Guinea appoints Kouramoudou Keïta as Deputy Director General of Budget

Kouramoudou Keïta's official appointment aims to strengthen the leadership team and accelerate case processing within the strategic budget directorate.

Guinea's budget directorate saw a leadership change with the official appointment of Kouramoudou Keïta as Deputy Director General of the Budget. The announcement was made through a decree read on Tuesday evening, August twenty-fifth, two thousand twenty-six, on the national broadcaster, Radiodiffusion Télévision Guinéenne (RTG).

This new appointment is intended to reinforce the existing leadership team within the strategic directorate. The government's stated goal is to accelerate the processing of various files and administrative tasks handled by the budget office.

The move signals an effort to enhance efficiency and operational capacity in a key financial institution. The budget directorate plays a central role in managing the nation's financial resources and allocations.

Why it mattersThis appointment affects Guinean citizens by potentially speeding up government financial processes and resource allocation, which can impact public services and economic stability.

Guinea demolishes prison, plans new housing and administrative buildings

The Maison Centrale de Conakry, built in 1933, has been demolished to make way for new residential and public service infrastructure in the capital.

The Maison Centrale de Conakry, originally designed for three hundred people but housing nearly two thousand detainees at its evacuation, has been completely demolished. The site, located in the heart of Kaloum, is now cleared for new development, though the full scope of the project remains undisclosed by presidential officials.

Mohamed Lamine Sy Savané, Minister of Urbanism, Housing, and Land Management, confirmed that the liberated land will host new housing units and administrative buildings. This initiative aims to address the ministry's program for constructing housing and facilities for public services, according to the minister.

The authorities' stated ambition is to modernize Conakry and other major cities by developing infrastructure that supports economic and tourism activities. Amara Camara, Minister Secretary General to the Presidency, indicated that the president's approach prioritizes action over words, promising that the relevance of this transformation will soon become clear.

Why it mattersResidents of Conakry will see new housing and administrative buildings constructed on the site of the former Maison Centrale prison, potentially improving living conditions and public services.
