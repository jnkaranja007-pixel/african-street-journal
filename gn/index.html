Guinea hosts Simandou Mining Summit for investors

Conakry will connect global investors with Guinean businesses from November 11 to 14, 2026.

Guinea will host the first Simandou Mining Summit in Conakry from November 11 to 14, 2026, aiming to attract global investors and highlight national businesses. The summit intends to position Guinea as a major investment destination, focusing on the Simandou mining project and fostering economic partnerships. Ibrahima Dieng, founder of Easycom, stated that the event's success will be measured by its ability to make national entrepreneurship a key player in the country's economic transformation.

The summit will bring together mining operators, investors, financial institutions, and economic partners in Conakry. This event follows the recent operation of the Simandou project, including the first passenger train circulation on October 12. Guinea aims to convert international attention into structured investments, contracts, jobs, and opportunities for national companies, as part of its Simandou 2040 program for infrastructure modernization and economic diversification.

Conakry will serve as a platform for negotiations, potentially accelerating partnerships that would normally take months to explore. Guinean businesses in sectors like industrial maintenance, logistics, public works, security, and technical supplies can find new opportunities. Dieng emphasized that the summit should showcase Guinea's local skills and entrepreneurial fabric as capable of supporting major development projects, ensuring national companies benefit from Simandou's generated investments.

Why it mattersThe Simandou Mining Summit in Conakry aims to create investment and job opportunities for Guinean businesses and citizens.

FAPGAZ-SA receives 800 tonnes of butane gas

The arrival of the gas at Conakry Port aims to meet rising domestic demand and ensure regular supply.

FAPGAZ-SA announced the arrival of a ship carrying 800 tonnes of butane gas at the Autonomous Port of Conakry on Saturday, November 7, 2026. This delivery is part of the company's strategy to ensure a steady supply of domestic gas to the national market.

The significant cargo is intended to address a growing demand for domestic gas. The company stated this shipment will help guarantee a regular supply for consumers.

The arrival of the butane gas is a direct response to increasing consumer needs in Guinea.

Why it mattersGuinean households will benefit from a more regular supply of domestic gas.

16 Burkinabè nationals intercepted at Guinea-Ivory Coast border

Authorities returned the group, including minors, to Guinea after they were intercepted near the Ivory Coast frontier.

16 nationals from Burkina Faso, including several minors and one woman, were intercepted on Friday, November 6, 2026, at the border between Guinea and Ivory Coast. The group was attempting to return to their home country after reportedly engaging in clandestine artisanal gold mining in Boola, located in Guinea's Beyla prefecture.

Ivorian authorities denied passage to several members of the group, particularly minors and those without travel documents, and sent them back to the Guinean side. The 16 individuals were then placed under the care of the regional police in Nzérékoré, with Guinean authorities arranging their return to Burkina Faso.

Sawadogo Prospère, a Burkinabè national accompanying the group, stated they left Boola following a formal ban on illegal gold mining in Guinea. He guided them because none of the younger individuals could speak French, and he did not want to abandon them. The group included several individuals aged 15 to 19, and another who joined from Siguiri.

Guinean opposition leader Aliou Bah freed after 22 months

Mamadou Aliou Bah, leader of the Liberal Democratic Movement, completed a civic re-integration program after his release.

Mamadou Aliou Bah, president of the Liberal Democratic Movement (MoDeL), regained his freedom on Friday, November 6, 2026, after nearly two years in detention. The announcement came from his political party, which stated he had finished a 45-day civic training and re-integration preparation course. He had been pardoned by President Mamadi Doumbouya on October 2, 2026.

Bah was arrested on December 26, 2024, at the Pamelap border post while traveling to Sierra Leone. He was sentenced on January 7, 2025, to two years in prison for offending and defaming the head of state via a computer system. The Kaloum Court of First Instance handed down the sentence, which was upheld on appeal on May 28, 2025.

The MoDeL expressed gratitude to supporters and reaffirmed its commitment to democracy and the rule of law. The party's statement followed Bah's completion of his civic re-integration period.

Why it mattersOpposition leader Mamadou Aliou Bah has been released from prison after serving a sentence for defamation.
