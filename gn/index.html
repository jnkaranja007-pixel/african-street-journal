EDG Union Clashes With Ratoma Commune Over Debt

The electricity union accuses local officials of violence and unpaid bills totaling hundreds of millions of Guinean francs.

Tensions have escalated between the Électricité de Guinée (EDG) union and the Ratoma commune authorities over a disputed debt of hundreds of millions of Guinean francs, the deployment of prepaid meters, and the forceful closure of a local EDG agency. The EDG union claims Ratoma municipality engaged in "intolerable" acts of violence and accumulated "dizzying" payment arrears.

According to Titi Sékou Camara, the union's secretary general, communal agents accompanied by law enforcement allegedly stormed the Taouyah agency last Friday, closing premises, assaulting workers, and leading to hospitalizations. Camara stated the union will file a complaint and suspend electricity supply until payments are made, emphasizing the commune's obligation to install prepaid meters and settle its debt, which he put at over 600 million GNF.

The Ratoma commune, however, disputes the claimed debt amount, calling it "untruths" and asserting that the overall debt attributed to the municipality includes various state sector services. Boubacar Korboyah Baldé, the commune's director of micro-projects, offered a different perspective on the financial obligations.

Why it mattersThe EDG union and Ratoma commune are in conflict over unpaid electricity bills and the installation of prepaid meters, affecting the provision of electricity services.

Guinea Fishery Resumes After Two-Month Ban

Minister Fatima Camara urges sustainable practices as industrial and semi-industrial fishing activities restart within 60 nautical miles.

Industrial and semi-industrial fishing activities officially resumed in Guinea on September 1, 2026, following a two-month biological rest period. The closure, observed from July 1 to August 31, aimed to allow marine species to reproduce and preserve the marine ecosystem. Minister of Fisheries and Maritime Economy, Fatima Camara, called on all sector stakeholders to adhere strictly to sustainable resource management rules upon restarting operations.

The resumption of fishing activities is permitted in the maritime zone extending up to the 60 nautical miles limit, as per the 2026 Maritime Fisheries Management and Planning Plan. During the closure, the government maintained an enhanced surveillance system with collaboration from various maritime services. To mitigate potential shortages and household impacts, authorities had previously authorized duty and tax exemptions for the import of fifteen thousand tons of fishery products.

Minister Camara emphasized that the end of the biological rest does not signify an end to conservation efforts, stressing shared responsibility for preserving and valuing the nation's marine wealth. Scientific assessments of marine stocks are planned to evaluate the impact of the closure and inform future resource management decisions. All actors, including fishmongers, fishermen, shipowners, and economic operators, are urged to exercise rigor and responsibility.

Why it mattersThe resumption of fishing activities directly impacts the availability and price of seafood for Guinean consumers and the livelihoods of those in the fishing industry.

Central Bank Suspends Union Delegation

Guinea's central bank governor has ordered the provisional suspension of the bank's union delegation following a labor court ruling.

The union delegation at the Central Bank of the Republic of Guinea (BCRG) has been provisionally suspended by order of Governor Dr. Karamo Kaba. The decision, communicated via a circular on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, follows a labor court ruling that mandated the suspension of the union's activities.

The governor's directive explicitly prohibits any union activities within the BCRG premises, with Dr. Kaba warning that any violations will be met with strict legal consequences. A member of the suspended union delegation confirmed receipt of the notification but declined to comment further on the matter.

This union representative stated that the situation would be managed responsibly and in accordance with the court's decision. The BCRG has informed its staff about the prohibition of union activities and the potential repercussions for non-compliance with the governor's order.

Why it mattersEmployees of the Central Bank of the Republic of Guinea are directly affected by the prohibition of union activities and the potential legal repercussions.

UBA Guinée Offers School Loans Up to 30 Million GNF

The bank's new 'Prêt Scolaire' aims to help families finance educational expenses with repayment terms up to ten months.

UBA Guinée has launched a new 'Prêt Scolaire' (School Loan) product to assist families with the costs associated with the upcoming school year. The financing solution allows eligible clients to access up to 30 million Guinean Francs (GNF) to cover educational expenses.

The initiative is designed to help parents better prepare for and manage the financial demands of back-to-school season. UBA Guinée stated that the loan offers adapted repayment options, extending up to ten months, and is available in all UBA Guinée branches from September 1 to December 31, 2026.

This program reflects the bank's commitment to providing practical financial solutions for its customers' daily needs and supporting families during significant periods. Interested clients are encouraged to visit UBA Guinée branches to inquire about eligibility criteria and application procedures.

Why it mattersFamilies in Guinea are affected by the new school loan, which can help them finance educational expenses for their children.

Orange Guinée Awards Millions in Quiz Prizes

The telecommunications company held its third prize ceremony for the Quiz MégaWin 3, awarding significant cash prizes to lucky customers.

Orange Guinée has once again rewarded its customers through the Quiz MégaWin 3 competition, with the third prize distribution event taking place at the company's premises. A new group of winners received their rewards, with prizes reaching up to 8 million Guinean Francs (GNF). This event underscores the quiz's promise to allow customers to test their knowledge, accumulate points, and win substantial prizes.

Among the recipients was Mohamed Camara, who won 5 million GNF. Camara shared his experience, stating he discovered the quiz on RTG and decided to participate despite initial doubts. He continued to play, accumulating points, and was eventually contacted by Orange with the news of his winnings. He expressed his satisfaction and recommended others to participate, emphasizing that trying one's luck can lead to pleasant surprises.

The ceremony, filled with joy and emotion, highlighted the success of the Quiz MégaWin in engaging customers and providing tangible rewards. Camara's story serves as an example of how perseverance in playing the quiz can result in significant financial gains, reinforcing the competition's appeal to a broad customer base.
