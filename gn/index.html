Central Bank Union Leaders Acquitted of Charges

The Kaloum Court of First Instance dismissed all charges against three union officials of the Central Bank of the Republic of Guinea, ending a high-profile legal dispute.

Three union leaders from the Central Bank of the Republic of Guinea (BCRG) were acquitted of all charges by the Kaloum Court of First Instance on Friday. Momo Camara, Aboubacar Sidiki Camara, and Aboubacar Yansané were accused of "outrage to an official, violence, and assault and battery" following a complaint filed by Warrant Officer Daouda Sankhon.

The court found insufficient evidence to establish guilt and dismissed the civil party's and public prosecutor's requests. This verdict brings a definitive end to a judicial confrontation that had created tensions within the country's primary financial institution.

The acquittal of the BCRG union officials resolves a significant internal dispute. The case had been closely watched, highlighting ongoing labor relations within Guinea's central bank.

Why it mattersInvestors may see this as a sign of improved stability and reduced legal risk within Guinea's financial sector, potentially encouraging further investment.

Guinea Regulator Targets Unlicensed Media Outlets

The High Authority of Communication (HAC) will now focus on bringing media companies into compliance after previously addressing journalist conduct, according to its president.

Boubacar Yacine Diallo, president of Guinea's High Authority of Communication (HAC), announced on August 28, 2026, that the organization will now focus on regulating media outlets themselves. Diallo stated that the ranks of journalists have been "cleaned up" and the next step is to address the conformity of media companies. He noted that new media outlets are frequently established under conditions that violate current regulations.

Diallo acknowledged that many young people are entering media entrepreneurship due to a desire to work in journalism and a general entrepreneurial spirit of the times. He suggested that some of these individuals may be acting out of ignorance of the existing rules rather than a deliberate intent to break them. The HAC president called on media professionals to support the institution's efforts to "clean up" the sector.

The HAC president praised Guinean journalists for their role in maintaining peace during recent electoral periods, describing the press as a potential factor for peace and stability when it chooses to be. He contrasted this with the potential for media to become a destabilizing force if it does not act responsibly. The announcement followed a press conference at the HAC headquarters in Kaloum.

Why it mattersNew media businesses may face regulatory scrutiny and potential closure if they do not comply with established rules, affecting media entrepreneurs.

Guinean Census Agents Arrested Over Unpaid Wages and Service Motorcycles

National Civil Registry program supervisors in multiple Guinean localities have been detained by police for refusing to return official motorcycles until their salary arrears are.

Tensions are escalating between communal supervisors of Guinea's National Civil Registry Program (PN-RAVEC) and their superiors, leading to arrests in several areas. Agents demanding payment of overdue salaries have been detained by law enforcement for refusing to return service motorcycles. The detentions occurred in locations including Gaoual, Beyla, and Guéckédou, as agents insisted that returning the vehicles was conditional on receiving their full salary arrears.

In Guéckédou on August 27, seven census agents were arrested and forced to surrender their motorcycles. A communal technical supervisor confirmed the incident, stating that local authorities were acting on orders from higher up. One agent reported being summoned to the prefecture, where, despite initial discussions, gendarmes arrived and compelled them to hand over the vehicles, vowing to continue their protest without the equipment.

This conflict arises as the contracts for these agents are set to terminate on August 31. A circular dated August 13 from the national program coordinator, Intendant General (2s) Aboubacar Biro Condé, had previously called for the return of program equipment. Communal supervisors also report facing daily intimidation amidst the ongoing dispute over unpaid wages, which some claim amount to 16 months of arrears.

Why it mattersInvestors face potential disruptions in administrative data collection and census operations due to the unresolved labor dispute and arrests.

Former Minister Defends $250 Million Stadium Spending

Bogola Keamou Haba questions the debate over stadium renovation costs, arguing the focus should be on what the investment finances, amid public skepticism.

Former Youth and Sports Minister Bogola Keamou Haba is defending the expenditure of $250 million on rehabilitating Conakry's 28-Septembre and Nongo stadiums. He argues that the public debate should center on the financing of these projects rather than the total amount spent. While the provisional approval of the 28-Septembre stadium offers relief after years of national teams playing abroad, Haba acknowledges that factors like the Ebola pandemic, COVID-19, and state failures have also contributed to the prolonged lack of modern facilities.

Haba, who served as minister from March 13, 2024, to January 22, 2026, faces scrutiny for his role, alongside predecessors, in the country's ongoing sports infrastructure deficit. Critics suggest he should have begun his defense with an apology for the state's failure to provide adequate facilities. Instead, his extensive efforts to justify the spending have led some to perceive him as detached from the public's disappointment and the consequences of a lack of long-term sports policy.

Despite criticisms of his narrative's accuracy and his perceived defensiveness, Haba raises a pertinent question: what exactly has been financed with the $250 million? The source suggests that only an independent audit can definitively answer this persistent question, implying a lack of transparency surrounding the project's financial details and outcomes.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the lack of transparency and the need for an independent audit to clarify how $250 million was spent on stadium rehabilitation.

Mamadou Barry Appointed Director General of Guinea's New National School of Administration

President Mamadi Doumbouya names Mamadou Barry to lead the newly established ENA, a transformation of the former INFP, aimed at modernizing public service training.

Mamadou Barry has been appointed as the new Director General of Guinea's National School of Administration (ENA), a significant development in the nation's public service reform. The ENA is a strategic evolution of the National Institute for Training and Professional Development (INFP) and will operate under the Ministry of Modernization of Administration and Public Service. Its primary mission is to provide initial and ongoing training for state officials, aiming to strengthen their capacities.

Barry, who holds a Public Law degree from Gamal Abdel Nasser University and further qualifications in public management and international law from Quebec and UQAM in Canada, brings over two decades of experience in governance. He previously served as Deputy Director General of the INFP and has been a key figure in international cooperation, acting as a focal point for ENAP Quebec in Guinea and a strategic contact for CAFRAD in Morocco.

His extensive background includes roles as an electoral expert and political analyst at the UNDP Governance Unit and as a Senior Program Officer at the National Democratic Institute (NDI). Barry's appointment is seen as a move to leverage his expertise in public administration and international relations to guide the new institution.

Why it mattersGovernment officials and public servants will receive new training opportunities at the ENA, potentially impacting their career development and administrative capabilities.
