Aliou Bah released after 23 months in detention

Guinea's opposition leader Mamadou Aliou Bah completed a civic reinsertion program and regained freedom on November 6, 2026.

Mamadou Aliou Bah, president of the Liberal Democratic Movement (MoDeL), was freed on Friday, November 6, 2026, after nearly two years of detention. He had been granted a pardon on October 2, 2026, by President Mamadi Doumbouya, and subsequently completed a 45-day civic reinsertion and reintegration preparation course. Bah was arrested on December 26, 2024, at the Pamelap border post while traveling to Sierra Leone.

Bah was initially sentenced to two years in prison on January 7, 2025, by the Kaloum Court of First Instance for "offense and defamation against the head of state via a computer system." This conviction was upheld on appeal on May 28, 2025. The MoDeL party announced his release in a statement, expressing gratitude for the solidarity shown to their president and reaffirming their commitment to democracy and the rule of law.

The MoDeL party stated that Bah could return to his home on the evening of his release. His detention period lasted nearly 23 months. The party's national directorate officially confirmed his liberation on November 6, 2026.

Why it mattersOpposition leader Aliou Bah has been released from prison after serving a sentence for offenses against the head of state.

Guinea striker Serhou Guirassy out with muscle strain

The forward will miss the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers against Eritrea due to a thigh injury.

Guinea's star striker Serhou Guirassy will miss the upcoming 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers against Eritrea after a medical examination confirmed a thigh muscle strain. He sustained the injury on Wednesday during a Champions League match for Borussia Dortmund, where he was substituted before halftime in his team's 2-1 victory over Real Betis.

Guirassy's absence deprives coach Paulo Duarte of a decisive player, who scored Guinea's winning goal against Kenya in the previous international window. Dortmund coach Niko Kovac stated that Guirassy would remain in Germany for treatment, and that the club and the Guinean Football Federation are coordinating his management. The forward is also out for Dortmund's Saturday league match.

The Guinean squad also faces the absence of defender Saïdou Sow, who has a knee injury and has been replaced by FK Karviná forward Ousmane Condé. Soumaïla Sylla is also out and will be replaced by Sékou Salifou Sylla.

Why it mattersGuinea's national football team will play two matches for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations without their leading goal scorer, Serhou Guirassy, and defender Saïdou Sow.

Guinea minister attacks MP over electricity exports

Foreign Minister Morissanda Kouyaté called critics of Guinea's electricity export policy 'illiterates with the skin of intellectuals'.

Member of Parliament Dr. Ousmane Kaba questioned Guinea's policy of exporting electricity to Gambia, Senegal, and Guinea-Bissau while the capital, Conakry, struggles with power access. Kaba described the situation as a "paradox" during a finance law debate on Saturday.

Foreign Minister Dr. Morissanda Kouyaté responded forcefully on Thursday, stating that Guinea had no electricity when President Doumbouya took power. He criticized those who claim Guinea exports power while lacking it, calling them "illiterates with the skin of intellectuals."

Minister of Economy Mariam Ciré Sylla offered a more technical explanation regarding the profitability of these electricity sales. The debate highlights ongoing political tensions surrounding the nation's energy management and infrastructure.

Why it mattersGuinean citizens are affected by political debate over electricity access and export policies.

Guinea academic seeks historian study grants

Dr Safiatou Diallo wants scholarships for Guinean historians to support the Simandou project.

Dr Safiatou Diallo, a Guinean academic, called for scholarships for historians on Friday, November 6, 2026, at the National Library's open days closing. She argued that knowledge of history and cultural identity must not be sidelined by the industrial and technological ambitions of large development projects like Simandou. Diallo, president of the Centre for Innovation and Research for Development (CIRD) and the Association of Historians of Guinea, spoke in Conakry.

Diallo highlighted a critical shortage of local experts in heritage management and conservation, stating Guinea often needs external human resources for courses. She advocated for specialized study grants for young Guinean historians and researchers to ensure the country develops its own specialists. This call came during a conference on the cultural heritage's place in the Simandou mega-project.

The academic stressed the importance of understanding the National Library's resources and creating exchange spaces for cultural stakeholders. She believes combining economic development with socio-cultural development requires more Guinean historians to guide the nation's future.

Why it mattersGuinean students and researchers in history and cultural heritage lack opportunities for specialized training due to a shortage of local experts.

Guinea hosts West African energy meeting

Conakry welcomes delegates for the 21st General Assembly of WAPP to discuss regional electricity integration.

Guinea hosted the 21st General Assembly of the West African Power Pool (WAPP) in Conakry from Friday, November 6, to Saturday, November 7, 2026. Prime Minister Amadou Oury Bah opened the meeting, which focused on strengthening the regional electricity market.

The two-day assembly's theme was "Consolidating the regional electricity market: a lever for resilience for West Africa." Discussions included electrical interconnections, state cooperation, and the establishment of a regional power market. Prime Minister Bah emphasized energy's role in economic development, industrialization, and improving living conditions.

Prime Minister Bah called for increased energy solidarity among West African nations, advocating for a unified West African energy community with common standards. He also highlighted Guinea's need for drastic reforms in its national energy system to support the Simandou 2040 economic program and ensure diversified, equitable economic presence across the country.

Why it mattersPrime Minister Amadou Oury Bah opened a meeting of West African energy officials to discuss regional electricity integration and national energy reforms needed for Guinea's Simandou 2040 program.
