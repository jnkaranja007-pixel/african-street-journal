Central Bank Union Leaders Acquitted of Charges

The Kaloum Court of First Instance acquitted three union leaders from the Central Bank of the Republic of Guinea on charges of contempt and violence.

The Kaloum Court of First Instance on Friday, August 28, 2026, acquitted Momo Camara, Aboubacar Sidiki Camara, and Aboubacar Yansané, all members of the union delegation at the Central Bank of the Republic of Guinea (BCRG). They had been accused of contempt of an official, violence, and assault, following a complaint filed by Warrant Officer Daouda Sankhon.

The court dismissed all claims from the civil party and the public prosecutor, citing insufficient evidence to establish guilt. This verdict brings a definitive end to a highly publicized legal dispute that had created tensions within the nation's primary financial institution.

The three union officials were facing charges stemming from an incident involving an agent of the institution. Their acquittal signifies a resolution to the judicial proceedings that had been ongoing and closely watched within the financial sector.

Why it mattersUnion leaders at the Central Bank of the Republic of Guinea were acquitted, resolving a legal dispute that had caused internal tensions.

Guinea Regulator Targets Illicit Media Outlets

The High Authority of Communication will now focus on media compliance after cleaning up journalist ranks, according to its president.

Boubacar Yacine Diallo, president of Guinea's High Authority of Communication (HAC), announced on August 28, 2026, that the organization will now address the conformity of media outlets themselves. This follows an earlier effort to regulate journalists. Diallo stated that new media are frequently created in conditions that violate current regulations, though he acknowledged that many young people enter the field due to a lack of awareness of these rules.

Diallo praised Guinean journalists for their role in maintaining peace during recent elections, calling the press a potential factor for stability when it chooses to be. He urged media professionals to support the HAC's initiative to clean up the sector. The HAC president noted that while the ranks of journalists have been purified, the focus must now shift to the media organizations themselves.

The HAC is preparing to host the 12th General Assembly of the regulatory institutions of the UEMOA space next week in Guinea. Diallo expressed understanding for young entrepreneurs seeking to enter media, attributing their actions often to a desire to practice journalism and innovate rather than a deliberate intent to break laws. The HAC aims to bring these media into compliance with established rules.

Why it mattersMedia entrepreneurs and existing outlets face new regulatory scrutiny and potential sanctions for non-compliance with established rules.

Tokounou Prefecture Lacks Basic Infrastructure After Promotion

Local authorities and residents express satisfaction with the new administrative status but urgently need water, electricity, and health facilities.

Tokounou's recent elevation to prefecture status has brought hope to local officials and residents, though significant needs remain. The community anticipates development and essential infrastructure, including water, electricity, and healthcare services, to match its new administrative standing. Mayor Adama Kourouma stated that the promotion must translate into tangible progress, urging local leaders and diaspora members to collaborate for the area's growth and ensure it receives the necessary support for a healthy administration.

Health sector needs are particularly acute, with the local health chief, Sékou Sylla, highlighting a lack of equipment and facilities for adequate patient care. He specifically requested hospital beds, solar kits for electricity, and housing for staff. Women's representative Fanta Diawara echoed these sentiments, expressing satisfaction with the prefecture status but detailing daily struggles due to the absence of basic amenities like water, electricity, and proper infrastructure for youth, women, and medical personnel.

Beyond the administrative upgrade, the people of Tokounou are looking for concrete improvements. Access to clean water, reliable electricity, and functional health facilities are now top priorities for local authorities and inhabitants. The new prefecture status is seen as an opportunity to address these long-standing deficiencies and foster development within the community.

Why it mattersThe residents of Tokounou are affected by the lack of basic infrastructure such as water, electricity, and health facilities, despite the town's recent promotion to prefecture status.

Former Minister Defends $250 Million Stadium Spending

Bogola Keamou Haba, former Minister of Youth and Sports, is defending the allocation of $250 million for stadium rehabilitation.

Former Minister of Youth and Sports, Bogola Keamou Haba, is seeking to justify the expenditure of $250 million on the rehabilitation of two capital city stadiums. While the provisional approval of the 28-Septembre stadium offers some relief after years of national team and club matches being played abroad, the author of the piece suggests this temporary approval for only one of two stadiums is not cause for celebration.

Haba, who served from March 13, 2024, to January 22, 2026, is associated with the current state of sports under-equipment, alongside his predecessors. The author suggests that instead of defending his actions, Haba should have expressed regret and sought forgiveness from the public for the state's failure in sports infrastructure development, attributing past issues to Ebola, COVID-19, and state failures.

The article questions whether Haba's defense is an attempt to deflect scrutiny amidst a government crackdown on corrupt officials. It posits that Haba's extensive efforts to justify himself, rather than addressing the public's suffering due to a lack of vision and ambition in sports policy, leave an impression of insensitivity. The core question remains: what exactly was financed with the $250 million, a question only an independent audit can answer.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by questions surrounding the rational use of $250 million allocated to stadium rehabilitation, necessitating an audit to clarify expenditures.

Guinean General Warns Soldiers Against Social Media Misuse

General Abdoulaye Keïta stressed confidentiality and discipline for military personnel posting online, warning of consequences for breaches.

The Guinean military is proactively addressing potential misuse of social media by its personnel. General Abdoulaye Keïta, Chief of Staff of the Army, addressed soldiers at the Special Battalion of Conakry, emphasizing the importance of confidentiality and reserve. He stated that every soldier represents their unit and the army, and must refrain from publishing unauthorized information, images, or videos related to military activities.

General Keïta warned that disseminating confidential information online could lead to severe consequences, urging soldiers to maintain behavior consistent with army values and to consult their superiors. To enforce these regulations, an inter-army commission has been established at the general staff headquarters. This body is tasked with identifying military personnel active on social media and gathering intelligence on their identities and units.

This initiative aims to prevent security risks and protect strategic information within the armed forces. The establishment of the commission signifies a strong stance by the military hierarchy to maintain discipline and control over information shared by soldiers online. The focus is on ensuring that all personnel understand and adhere to the rules of confidentiality and operational secrecy.

Why it mattersMilitary personnel in Guinea are directly affected by these new regulations, which impose restrictions on their online activities and warn of repercussions for violating confidentiality rules.
