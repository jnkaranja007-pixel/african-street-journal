China and Guinea launch Simandou 2040 project

The integrated iron ore project involves a mine, railway, and port, with total infrastructure investments exceeding $20 billion.

Chinese and Guinean companies are jointly developing the Simandou iron ore project in Guinea's mountains. The project includes a mine, a trans-Guinean railway, and the Morébaya port, with total infrastructure investments expected to surpass $20 billion.

Once fully operational, the Simandou project aims for an annual capacity of 120 million tonnes. Baowu Resources, a subsidiary of China Baowu, is integrating environmental, social, and governance standards into the development and operation of the northern Simandou mining chain.

On November 6, Baowu Resources released "The Sustainability of the Simandou Project" in Shanghai, the first public ESG report focused on Simandou and the first thematic ESG report on an overseas integrated iron ore and infrastructure project involving a Chinese company.

Why it mattersThe Simandou 2040 program aims to boost Guinea's economic transformation through mining, with total infrastructure investments exceeding $20 billion.

Guinea seeks $693 million for Conakry urban rail link

The transport ministry presented the heavy rail plan, with an estimated investment cost of 693 million dollars, to investors at the Simandou Mining Summit.

Guinea's authorities are looking for 693 million dollars to build an urban rail line linking Kaloum and Kagbelen in the Greater Conakry area. The project, called Heavy Rail Transit, was presented to investors on the second day of the Simandou Mining Summit.

The Ministry of Transport is leading the scheme, and the cost is estimated as capital expenditure, known as CAPEX. The stated aim is to ease movement across Greater Conakry, where traffic congestion slows residents' journeys.

The presentation did not give a construction timeline or the exact route of the line.

Why it mattersInvestors are being asked to finance a 693 million dollar railway that would carry commuters between Kaloum and Kagbelen.

Business · 2026-11-16

Guinea invites investors into a diversified economy at Simandou summit

The Simandou Mining Summit opened on Kassa Island on 11 November 2026 with a call for investors to help turn mineral wealth into growth in several sectors.

Guinea opened the Simandou Mining Summit on Kassa Island on 11 November 2026, inviting investors to help the country use its mineral resources as the engine of a more diversified economy. The summit's stated aim is to bring foreign capital into sectors beyond mining.

The invitation names industry, energy, agriculture, infrastructure and services as the main areas of that ambition. Organisers presented the summit as a clear signal that the government wants outside partners to take part in building those sectors.

The source reports no figures, deals or follow-up dates from the event. For investors, the immediate change is an open invitation to take part in projects across those five sectors.

Why it mattersInvestors can now look to industry, energy, agriculture, infrastructure and services as the sectors Guinea is inviting outside capital to develop alongside mining.
