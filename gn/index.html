Doumbouya Updates Maritime Fishing Rules

President Mamadi Doumbouya has enacted new regulations for Guinea's maritime fishing sector, replacing 1997 rules.

President Mamadi Doumbouya established new general measures for implementing Guinea's maritime fishing code on August 31, 2026. The new law, L/2025/019/CNT, replaces regulations from 1997 to align the sector's oversight with current realities. These measures apply to all individuals and entities involved in fishing activities within Guinean territorial waters, including fishing gear, vessels, and equipment.

The decree also covers establishments involved in packaging, storage, processing, conservation, marketing, transformation, or distribution of fish and related species. It extends to the transport vehicles for these products and any person or entity conducting these activities. The new framework aims to modernize and adapt the management of the maritime fishing industry to contemporary conditions.

This regulatory update is designed to provide a more relevant and effective framework for Guinea's maritime fishing sector. By superseding the outdated 1997 legislation, the government seeks to ensure better management and development of this vital industry, reflecting the current economic and environmental landscape.

Why it mattersThis new regulatory framework affects businesses and individuals engaged in any aspect of Guinea's maritime fishing industry, from catching to distribution.

Guinea Hosts Audiovisual Regulators Amidst Disinformation Fight

The 12th General Assembly of audiovisual regulators from UEMOA and Guinea convenes in Conakry to address social media, cybersecurity, and social cohesion.

Conakry is hosting the 12th General Assembly of the Platform of Audiovisual Regulators of UEMOA member countries and Guinea (PRA-UEMOA-Guinée). The meeting brings together representatives from eight UEMOA nations and Guinea to discuss challenges in regulating social media, cybersecurity, and maintaining social cohesion. The assembly aims to harmonize legal and technical tools for digital spaces and strengthen the legitimacy of regulatory bodies.

The gathering occurs as digital platforms expand, fake news proliferates, and new forms of information manipulation, including AI and deepfakes, emerge. Discussions will focus on harmonizing frameworks to better manage the digital environment and enhance the control of digital platforms. Media professionals will also receive training to adapt to new information challenges, fostering an exchange of experiences among the nine participating countries.

During the assembly, Guinea assumed the presidency of the Platform, with Boubacar Yacine Diallo, president of the High Authority of Communication, taking leadership. The event's theme highlights the critical need to protect information flow without stifling expression and to regulate without censoring, acknowledging the dual nature of social media in expanding public debate while also serving as vectors for disinformation and hate speech.

Why it mattersThe meeting of audiovisual regulators affects Guinea and eight other West African nations by seeking to harmonize digital space regulations and combat disinformation, which can impact public trust and social stability.

Lionel Messi Retires from International Football

After two decades with the national team, the seven-time Ballon d'Or winner cites exhaustion and the emergence of new talent as reasons for his departure.

Lionel Messi has announced his retirement from international football after more than twenty years representing Argentina. The decision, described as deeply painful, comes after careful consideration and a feeling that he has given all he can to the national team.

Messi stated that he had written his message on July 21, two days after the final, and that recent events concerning his father had solidified his resolve. He emphasized his lifelong commitment to the Albiceleste, fighting to bring joy and pride to Argentinians through football.

The captain expressed gratitude to fans for their unwavering support over two decades, promising to remain a supporter from the outside. He also thanked his family, coaches, teammates, and management, leaving with cherished memories and pride in the moments shared with the team and nation.

Why it mattersArgentinian football fans are affected by the retirement of their long-serving captain and star player, Lionel Messi.

Syndicate Cuts Power to Ratoma Mayor's Office Over Unpaid Bills

The Electricity of Guinea union disconnected the mayor's office from the power grid and filed a lawsuit after its agents were assaulted.

The union representing Electricity of Guinea (EDG) has cut power to the Ratoma mayor's office and initiated legal action following an alleged physical assault on its agents by gendarmes acting on the mayor's orders. The union stated that the mayor's office owes over 600 million Guinean francs in unpaid electricity bills. EDG is demanding full payment and the installation of a prepaid meter before power is restored.

According to the union's secretary general, agents from the Taouyah agency were attacked, with some requiring hospitalization. He visited the injured agents and confirmed the extent of the damage. The union plans to work with the General Directorate on further actions. The disconnection and legal complaint are the first measures taken in response to the incident and the outstanding debt.

The union leader encouraged its agents to continue their work, emphasizing their national service and legal protections. Attempts to get a statement from Mayor Kabinè Diawara were unsuccessful, as he was absent from his office. A vice-mayor directed inquiries to the chief of staff, who was given contact details.

Why it mattersThe Ratoma mayor's office is affected by a power cut and legal action due to unpaid bills and alleged assault on utility workers.

Compagnie des Bauxites de Guinée advances refinery project with shareholders

High-level meetings in Paris consolidate alignment on next steps and adopt an action plan for the refinery's construction.

The Compagnie des Bauxites de Guinée (CBG) held two days of high-level work sessions in Paris on August 20 and 21, 2026, to discuss its refinery project's progress. Participants included CBG executives, Guinean state representatives, and HALCO shareholders, all under the guidance of the Ministry of Mines and Geology and CBG's leadership.

These discussions focused on reviewing the project's current status, solidifying agreement on upcoming phases, and establishing an action plan for close monitoring of the roadmap. The shared objective is to advance the necessary steps to commence initial construction work by the end of 2026.

The strong shareholder engagement during these meetings highlights the project's momentum and the collective commitment to its realization. This refinery initiative is part of CBG's broader strategy for local resource transformation, aiming to enhance value creation and foster a more integrated mining industry in Guinea.

Why it mattersShareholder meetings and action plans are advancing the construction timeline for a new refinery, directly impacting the mining sector's local transformation efforts.
