Guinea officials examine 27 civil status draft texts

A two-day workshop in Conakry reviewed legal drafts to improve civil registration and identification.

Guinea's Ministry of Territorial Administration and Decentralization, through the National Program for Administrative Registration for Civil Status (PN-RAVEC) and the National Office of Civil Status and Identification (ONECI), concluded a technical workshop in Conakry on October 22, 2026. Participants examined 27 draft texts related to civil status and the identification of individuals. The workshop, held on October 21 and 22, aimed to strengthen the legal framework for civil registration and identification in Guinea.

The World Bank financed the workshop, with UNICEF providing support as a partner. The laws concerning civil status and the identification of individuals were adopted on October 25, 2023, and the reviewed application texts will facilitate their operational implementation.

The PN-RAVEC and ONECI, under the MATD, reaffirmed their commitment to a reliable and secure civil status system. This event is an important step in modernizing Guinea's national civil status and identification system.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Territorial Administration and Decentralization is working to improve the civil status and identification system for Guinean citizens.

Guinea parliament approves $336M loan for highway

The loan will finance 160 kilometers of roadworks to open an agricultural corridor in the north-central region.

Guinea’s Parliament has approved a €300 million (US$336.45 million) loan for the construction and paving of the Labé-Tougué national road. The project covers over 160 kilometers of roadworks, including a 149-kilometer section of the main road, 11 kilometers of the Banti-Koubia access road, and a 200-metre bridge over the Bafing River. Construction is expected to take 36 months.

This investment targets a region with significant agricultural potential, including corn, fonio, peanuts, potatoes, and livestock. Poor road conditions have hindered the commercialization of these resources, with produce currently reaching Conakry, 400 kilometers away, via roads that become impassable during the rainy season from May to October. Paving the corridor aims to enable year-round movement and reduce transport costs.

The project is part of Guinea’s Simandou 2040 strategic development programme. Benefits depend on complementary investments in agricultural marketing and distribution networks. Logistics operators will assess the corridor’s value based on its connection to the broader Trans-Guinean network serving Conakry’s port and Simandou mining developments.

Why it mattersFarmers in the Fouta Djallon highlands will benefit from improved road access for their produce and livestock.

Sport · 2026-10-23

Mamady Doumbouya's RF224 hosts afterwork in Conakry

The Renouveau Football 224 list presented its four-year plan for Guinean football development at the event.

The campaign for the presidency of the Guinean Football Federation (FGF) intensified on Thursday, October 22, 2026, with Mamady Doumbouya's Renouveau Football 224 (RF224) list hosting an afterwork event in Conakry. The gathering served to introduce the RF224 team and discuss their project for developing Guinean football, aiming to break from past practices.

Sega Diallo, a member of the RF224 list, explained that the group's pragmatic four-year program intends to restore hope for Guinean youth, revive football, and address the sector's difficulties. He noted the strong presence of business leaders and statutory members at the event, who expressed confidence in the need for change and new dynamics to help young Guineans reach the national team.

Kamil Zayatt, former captain of the Syli national team, voiced his support for RF224, believing the team's new vision will improve Guinean football, which has faced challenges for over 20 years. Ousmane Boly Traoré, CEO of Orange Guinea and an FGF partner, also attended, affirming his support for the list and its project.

Why it mattersThe RF224 list's project aims to develop Guinean football, affecting players, coaches, and administrators.

Politics · 2026-10-23

Mohamed Bank’s Bangoura takes charge at OGP

New director general Mohamed Bank’s Bangoura aims to restore stability and improve performance at the Guinean Office of Advertising.

Mohamed Bank’s Bangoura officially took over as the new Director General of the Guinean Office of Advertising (OGP) on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, in Conakry. His deputy, Alya Camara, also assumed his new role. The new leadership faces challenges including restoring stability, strengthening governance, and improving the institution's performance.

The handover ceremony was attended by interim Minister of Communication, Digital Economy and Innovation Souleymane Bah, and Presidential Advisor and OGP Board Chairman Thierno Mamadou Bah. Alya Camara, who served as interim director for three months, pledged his support to the new team, emphasizing collaboration and continuity of public action.

Bangoura stated his mandate will focus on listening, responsibility, and performance. He aims to understand the institution's realities, build on existing achievements, and make necessary decisions for its development. The OGP is tasked with serving the nation through modernization and enhanced performance.

Why it mattersMohamed Bank’s Bangoura and Alya Camara have taken new leadership roles at the Guinean Office of Advertising, impacting the institution's governance and performance.
