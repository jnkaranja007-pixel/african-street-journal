Civil Society Evaluates Landslide Response

The CNOSCG assessed government aid for victims of the August 22-23 Dar-Es-Salam landslide and offered recommendations.

The Conseil National des Organisations de la Société Civile Guinéenne (CNOSCG) visited Dar-Es-Salam on Saturday, August 29, to evaluate the government's response to the landslide that occurred on the night of August 22-23. The organization acknowledged the government's swift action in assisting those affected by the disaster.

During their field mission, the CNOSCG focused on the conditions of the victims, the injured, and those displaced by the collapse. The group's assessment aimed to understand the immediate support provided and to identify areas for improvement in future disaster management efforts.

The CNOSCG's evaluation follows the tragic event that struck the Dar-Es-Salam area. The organization's engagement underscores the importance of civil society oversight in ensuring effective and compassionate aid delivery during crises.

Why it mattersThe CNOSCG's assessment of the government's response to the Dar-Es-Salam landslide affects victims and the injured by evaluating their care.

EDG Teams Restore Power After Kipé Substation Failure

Électricité de Guinée dispatched crews to fix a fault at the Kipé substation, aiming to restore service to three affected communes.

Électricité de Guinée (EDG) has deployed technical teams to address a power outage caused by equipment failure at the Kipé substation. The company stated its priority is to progressively restore service and return to normal supply in the impacted areas.

The outage affects several neighborhoods within the communes of Ratoma, Lambagny, and Gbessia. EDG assured that its technical staff are mobilized to re-establish the electricity supply under the best possible conditions while the situation is being resolved.

While repairs are underway, EDG is asking users for patience and has reminded them that its call center, accessible at 626 11 11 11, remains open for inquiries. The company aims for a swift return to normal operations.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in Ratoma, Lambagny, and Gbessia are experiencing power outages due to equipment failure at the Kipé substation.

Guinea Basketball Team Defeats Cameroon 90-67

The Syli Basket secured a decisive victory in their final World Cup qualifier, finishing the window with two wins.

Guinea's national basketball team, Syli Basket, concluded the fourth window of the 2027 World Cup qualifiers with a dominant 90-67 win over Cameroon on Sunday, August 30, 2026. Coach Corey Alan Frazier's squad delivered a strong performance against a well-ranked Cameroonian side in their third and final match of this qualifying period.

This victory followed an 81-61 win against Cape Verde on August 28, though Guinea had previously lost to South Sudan 71-81. With two wins and one loss in this window, Syli Basket ended on a positive note, improving their standing in Group E.

The team's overall performance in this window has seen them climb to second place in Group E with 15 points. Cameroon leads the group with 16 points, highlighting the competitive nature of the qualification standings.

Guinean Group Demands Landfill Collapse Investigation

The Forum of Social Forces of Guinea calls for transparency after a deadly August 22-23 landfill collapse killed 34 people.

The Forum of Social Forces of Guinea (FFSG) expressed deep dismay over the deadly collapse at the Dar-es-Salam landfill in Gbessia commune, which occurred on the night of August 22-23, 2026. The incident resulted in 34 fatalities, approximately thirty injuries, and left families without homes. The FFSG extended condolences to the bereaved and solidarity to the injured and displaced.

According to the FFSG, the tragedy was not merely a meteorological accident but indicated collective and institutional responsibility. Residents had long warned of the site's dangers without decisive action. The FFSG noted the suspicious timing of the landfill's collapse on the same day it was officially announced to be closed, raising serious questions.

The FFSG demanded truth, transparency, and justice for the victims, along with strong, lasting protective measures for the population. The group stated that the incident reveals systemic failures in managing hazardous sites and responding to community concerns, necessitating a thorough and independent inquiry.

Why it mattersThe FFSG is affected by the landfill collapse because it resulted in fatalities, injuries, and displacement, prompting their call for an investigation and protective measures.

Twenty African NGOs Demand Truth on Six Bodies Found in Guinea

Human rights organizations are calling for an independent investigation into the discovery of six blindfolded and bound bodies in Forécariah.

Twenty African human rights organizations are demanding that Guinean authorities fully investigate the discovery of six bodies in a mass grave in Sabakhouré, Allassoyah sub-prefecture, Forécariah prefecture. The victims, found with their eyes blindfolded and hands tied, were discovered on August 24, 2026, in the Kindia administrative region. The organizations expressed concern that these findings could indicate extrajudicial, summary, or arbitrary executions.

While urging caution and a thorough judicial inquiry with forensic expertise, the groups are requesting autopsies and DNA tests to formally identify the victims and provide answers to their families. They also called for the protection of witnesses, families, and human rights defenders documenting the case. The NGOs plan to approach ECOWAS and the African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights for an urgent fact-finding mission.

On the International Day of the Victims of Enforced Disappearances, the signatories emphasized the right to truth and justice for families, stating the discovery must not go unaddressed. Their collective demand is for an end to mass graves, nameless disappearances, and impunity. The organizations seek to ensure that all hypotheses are examined within a credible judicial framework.

Why it mattersTwenty African human rights organizations are affected by the discovery of six bodies, demanding an investigation into potential extrajudicial executions and the right to truth and justice for the victims' families.
