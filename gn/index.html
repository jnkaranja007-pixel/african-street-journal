Guinea launches public finance reform plan

The government has launched a strategic plan to reform public finances from 2026 to 2030, aiming for systemic transformation.

The Guinean government, through the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Budget, officially launched a two-day workshop in Conakry to validate the Strategic Plan for Public Finance Reform (PREFIP) for 2026-2030. The event brought together ministerial officials, public institution executives, and technical partners to discuss a new financial direction for the state. The stated objective is to fundamentally restructure public financial management to ensure that every franc spent has a concrete impact on citizens' daily lives.

Dr. Mamadou Barry, Executive Secretary of the Technical Unit for Monitoring Public Finance Reform Programs and Reforms, highlighted the significance of this new roadmap. He stated that the PREFIP 2026-2030 is not merely a continuation of past efforts but represents a change in scale, moving from a logic of gradual improvement to a dynamic of systemic transformation. This plan is based on the diagnosis from the PEFA++ 2024 evaluation and is structured around five axes, 17 programs, and over a hundred structuring actions.

Key international standards are integrated into the PREFIP 2026-2030, including commitment-based budgeting and the transition to program-based budgeting and accrual accounting. This initiative aims to overhaul the public financial system, moving beyond incremental changes to achieve a comprehensive, systemic overhaul that is expected to yield tangible benefits for the population.

Why it mattersCitizens are affected by the plan to ensure every franc spent has a concrete impact on their daily lives through public financial reforms.

Boké officials injured in mining road crash

The governorate's cabinet director and regional mines inspector were seriously hurt when their vehicle collided with a mining truck.

A serious traffic accident occurred on the mining road between Boké and Gaoual on Wednesday, October 21, 2026. The official vehicle carrying Dr. Hadja Gnalen Condé, director of cabinet for the Boké governorate, collided with a mining truck belonging to Alliance Mining Commodities Guinée (AMC-G SA). Dr. Condé, along with the regional inspector of Mines and Geology for Boké, Ismaël Ouattara, were among those severely injured.

The incident happened approximately 40 kilometers from Boké, between the Boké prefecture and the Wendou M’Bour sub-prefecture. Also injured were the director's assistant, Mariama Touré, and the gendarme driving their vehicle. The exact circumstances of the collision are still under investigation. The group was returning from Wendou M’Bour after attending a ceremony where AMC-G SA presented checks for its surface tax payment for 2025.

This mining royalty, totaling 961.64 million Guinean francs, is designated for the rural communities of Koumbia and Wendou M’Bour in the Gaoual prefecture. The health status of the victims, who are receiving care at the Boké regional hospital's emergency unit, has not yet been updated. The accident occurred on a route frequently used by mining operations.

Why it mattersThe director of cabinet and regional mines inspector were injured in a road accident.

Kankan court convicts five for shisha, 28 face cannabis charges

Judicial proceedings in Kankan have resulted in prison sentences and fines for shisha use, with a separate group awaiting trial for cannabis offenses.

Five individuals in Kankan have been sentenced to prison and fines for consuming shisha, while three others received suspended sentences for possessing and selling shisha accessories. The sentences were handed down on October 21, 2026, following legal actions initiated by the public prosecutor's office to combat drug use. These convictions were made under Guinea's 2012 law prohibiting tobacco, specifically article 12, which addresses shisha use.

In a separate case, 28 people arrested near the Milo River for alleged involvement with Indian hemp, commonly known as cannabis, appeared before the Kankan Court of First Instance. Their trial has been postponed to October 28, 2026, for the opening of debates. These arrests were part of broader operations targeting cannabis consumption and shisha use, as stated by the public prosecutor, Ibrahima 1 Camara.

The 28 individuals facing cannabis charges were apprehended following requisitions to the police and gendarmerie. After an investigation, 39 people were initially detained, leading to the 28 who appeared in court for possession, consumption, and sale of cannabis. The prosecutor confirmed these actions are in line with efforts to enforce existing laws against illicit substances and prohibited smoking devices.

Why it mattersResidents of Kankan are directly affected by court rulings and ongoing legal processes related to drug use and the sale of prohibited items.

Football federation election focuses on resource mobilization

Candidates for the Guinean Football Federation election are being judged on their ability to secure funding, not just promises.

With the Guinean Football Federation (Féguifoot) election approaching on October 27, the focus for statutory members is on who can best secure the necessary resources for the sport's advancement. Guinean football possesses talent and passion but critically lacks funding, a common challenge across African football federations. This financial deficit impacts local competitions, training centers, infrastructure, and team preparations, often leading to over-reliance on state subsidies.

Relying solely on government grants is seen as a path to precariousness, as state budgets have competing priorities and finite resources. For Guinean football to achieve consistent performance, it must adopt a business-like approach, developing revenue streams, attracting partners, managing budgets rigorously, and ensuring accountability. This requires leaders capable of securing private sector investment, sponsorships, and potentially diaspora contributions, while also valuing competitions and broadcasting rights.

The candidacy of Mamadi Doumbouya, currently Director General of ARPT and a former executive committee member of Féguifoot, is highlighted for his administrative experience and his team of entrepreneurs, including Bakary Diaby, Abdoul Sonoco, and Abass Camara. This group is noted for its recognized success in resource mobilization, suggesting a professional approach to fundraising. Transparency in fund utilization will be crucial for maintaining partner confidence and ensuring sustainable progress for Guinean football.

Why it mattersFootball clubs and administrators in Guinea are affected by the need to find new funding sources beyond state subsidies to improve the sport.

Police inspector thanks public for support after mother's death

General Mamadouba Paye Camara expressed gratitude for widespread sharing of his mother's passing and funeral notices.

Inspector General Mamadouba Camara of the Police and Civil Protection has issued a message of thanks following the death of his mother, Hadja Iya Yansané. He conveyed his and his family's sincere gratitude for the public's swift response and kindness in relaying the sad news and death notices.

Camara stated that the widespread sharing of the information allowed relatives, friends, and acquaintances to share in their grief, even across distances. He highlighted the professionalism, respect for their mourning, and elevated sense of humanity shown by those who assisted, which deeply touched his family.

The Inspector General also thanked individuals for their constant availability and unwavering support, which provided immense comfort during the difficult period. He prayed for divine rewards for their generosity and for his late mother to receive God's mercy and eternal paradise.

Why it mattersGuinean citizens are affected by the public expression of gratitude from a high-ranking official following a personal loss.
