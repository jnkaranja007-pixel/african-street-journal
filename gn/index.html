Guinea minister details electricity export revenue use

Mariama Ciré Sylla explained revenue from Kaléta and Souapiti dams goes to maintenance and regional energy sharing.

Guinea's Minister of Economy, Finance and Budget, Mariama Ciré Sylla, addressed questions on revenue from electricity sold to neighbouring countries. The sales are part of regional energy interconnection, with funds primarily used for maintaining the EDG power plants. She noted that while countries pay, some have arrears, and Guinea also faces payment delays.

Ibrahima Abé Sylla, a Guinean MP and former Energy Minister, discussed the financing of the Kaléta and Souapiti dams. He stated the Guinean state funded 40% of the Kaléta infrastructure, with the remaining one billion financed by a Chinese credit. For Souapiti, he explained Kaléta was used as collateral to fund its development.

The MP highlighted that Souapiti has a capacity of approximately 350 megawatts. He indicated that using Kaléta as collateral for Souapiti is a financial arrangement that continues to affect Guinea.

Why it mattersGuinean citizens are affected by how revenue from electricity exports is used for national infrastructure maintenance and regional energy partnerships.

Guinea army celebrates 68th anniversary

The military reaffirmed its loyalty to President Mamadi Doumbouya during the event in Koubia.

Guinea's army marked its 68th anniversary on Sunday, November 1, 2026, with celebrations in Koubia. For the first time, the ceremony took place at the newly constructed military camp on the outskirts of the city. Citizens gathered at the infantry camp to join the festivities, which began with the raising of the national flag.

Civilian and military leaders addressed the attendees. The mayor of Koubia, Mamadou Malifa Diallo, welcomed participants, while the camp commander, Colonel Florent Olémou, recounted the army's history and highlighted reforms by President Mamadi Doumbouya. The secretary general of the prefecture, Sékou Keïta, conveyed the prefect's congratulations to the soldiers.

The celebration included prayers for peace and national unity by elder Elhadj Mamadou Saliou Diallo. A shared meal followed, and a gala match was planned between the defense forces and a team from the education and health sectors. The event aimed to foster closer ties between the army and the population.

Why it mattersThe army reaffirmed its loyalty to President Mamadi Doumbouya, showing continued support for the head of state.

Business · 2026-11-02

Guinea minister seeks balanced trade with Turkey

Minister Aboubacar Kourouma urged Turkish firms to invest in Guinea's production sector.

Guinea's Minister of Industry and Commerce, Aboubacar Kourouma, met with his Turkish counterpart, Ömer BOLAT, in Istanbul on November 1, 2026. The ministers discussed bilateral trade, which reached nearly $300 million in 2025. Kourouma called for more balanced exchanges and encouraged Turkish companies to invest in Guinea's industrial base, including construction materials, agri-food, and textiles.

Kourouma also requested Turkish support for Guinean SMEs through technology transfer and training. Minister BOLAT raised concerns about administrative hurdles for Turkish investors. In response, Kourouma appointed a dedicated advisor to streamline these processes. BOLAT invited Kourouma to a halal products fair, and Kourouma invited BOLAT to lead a business mission to Guinea.

Both nations agreed to appoint focal points to monitor their partnership and strengthen cooperation in standardization. They aim to increase trade volume to $500 million, a goal first set in 2016. Efforts will also focus on facilitating economic operators' mobility and protecting investments. Kourouma is scheduled to attend the COMCEC opening ceremony on November 2, 2026.

Why it mattersGuinean businesses and the national economy are affected by the call for increased Turkish investment and balanced trade.

FRATEL meeting sets 2027 digital infrastructure priorities in Conakry

The 24th annual meeting of the French-speaking telecommunications regulators network concluded with plans to accelerate territorial coverage.

The 24th annual meeting of the French-speaking telecommunications regulators network (FRATEL) concluded in Conakry on Friday, October 30, 2026. The international gathering focused on measures to support digital infrastructure deployment and defined strategic priorities for 2027.

Adama Condé, Deputy Director General of Guinea's Posts and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority (ARPT), highlighted President Mamadi Doumbouya's commitment to digital transformation. Condé stated that infrastructure and technologies are the third pillar of his Simandou 2040 program. Patricia Amand, the newly elected President of FRATEL for 2027 from Côte d'Ivoire's ARTCI, presented the outcomes, emphasizing the use of global economic modeling tools and the need to reduce the digital divide.

FRATEL's 2027 priorities include accelerating territorial coverage to reduce the digital divide by strengthening fixed networks and mobile coverage across all regions. The network also stressed the importance of using data and global tools like those from the ITU to support effective deployment.

Why it mattersGuinean authorities hosted an international meeting to define priorities for digital infrastructure development and deployment by 2027.

Guinea U-20 team returns home after winning UFOA-A tournament

The Syli U-20 squad arrives at Conakry airport at 19:45 on Monday, November 2, 2026.

Guinea's under-20 football team, the Syli U-20, is expected back in Conakry on Monday, November 2, 2026. The team won the UFOA-A tournament, securing qualification for the next Africa U-20 Cup of Nations. Their flight is scheduled to land at 19:45.

The Guinean Football Federation (FGF) has invited the public to give the young champions a warm welcome. This victory returns Guinea to the U-20 Africa Cup of Nations after several years.

The FGF stated that the Syli U-20 players have written a new chapter in the history of Guinean football. Their success means they have surpassed the achievements of previous generations who could not qualify for the tournament in recent years.

Why it mattersThe Syli U-20 team's victory qualifies them for the Africa U-20 Cup of Nations, bringing national pride and recognition to young athletes.
