Guinea's gold mining ban drives thousands from Siguiri mining sites

Miners and their families are leaving Kintinian and nearby gold areas after the government banned unregulated artisanal gold digging, a measure announced on 2 November.

Thousands of artisanal gold miners and their families are leaving the mining areas of Siguiri, in northern Guinea, after the government banned unregulated artisanal gold mining in the region. Departures are multiplying in the new prefecture of Kintinian, including Bouré Nafadji, as workers lose their main source of income.

Mohamed Camara, originally from Kissidougou, said he is heading home because he depends entirely on gold digging and has no other trade. Nafina Traoré, a native of Faranah, said her family has no money left and her children sometimes go a full day without eating, and she has appealed to President Mamadi Doumbouya to help women.

Abou Touré is leaving Kintinian before his savings run out rather than wait for the support measures the authorities have announced. The government says the ban is meant to stop soil degradation, the destruction of farmland and the pollution of waterways caused by the activity.

Why it mattersFamilies who relied on gold digging for food and rent now have no income in Siguiri and must return to their home villages.

Doumbouya signs Guinea's 2026 revised budget law

President Mamadi Doumbouya has enacted the revised finance law and allocated funds to ministries.

President Mamadi Doumbouya of Guinea has officially enacted the Revised Finance Law (LFR) for 2026, following its approval by the Parliament. The decree, announced on Friday, November 13, 2026, formalizes the ordinary law L/2026/005/AN, dated November 7, 2026, which pertains to the current fiscal year's finances.

In a subsequent official act on the same day, President Doumbouya issued a decree to distribute the payment credits from the state budget among various ministerial departments and republican institutions for 2026. These decrees become effective immediately upon their signing and will be officially registered and published in the Republic's Official Journal.

The revised budget law and the decree on credit allocation are now in effect, guiding the financial operations of Guinea's government ministries and institutions for the remainder of 2026.

Guinea's election chief warned of pressure

Minister Djenabou Touré urged Mohamed Aly Condé to prioritize Guinea's interests over political or personal demands.

Djenabou Touré, Guinea's Minister of Territorial Administration and Decentralization, officially installed Mohamed Aly Condé as the new Director General of Elections on Friday, November 13, 2026. Condé faces the significant task of organizing upcoming senatorial and regional elections by the end of the year, as well as supplementary elections in 2027.

Minister Touré cautioned Condé about the intense daily pressures he will encounter from candidates, authorities, and the public, often fueled by misinformation. She stressed the importance of adhering strictly to the law and regulations, advising him to listen to all advice but ultimately do what is right and good for Guinea, not for individual candidates or herself.

Touré also emphasized the need for close collaboration with the Deputy Director General and other stakeholders, including the press and civil society, to ensure professional and impartial electoral operations. Condé, formerly a legal advisor to the institution, pledged to uphold impartiality and continue the work to consolidate Guinea's institutional framework.

Why it mattersThe new Director General of Elections must navigate internal and external pressures to ensure fair elections for Guinea.

Brooklyn building collapse kills two Guineans, injures pregnant survivor

Search operations continue at the Flatbush site, where eight people died when a three-storey building fell on Wednesday, 11 November.

A three-storey building collapsed in Brooklyn's Flatbush neighbourhood, New York, on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, killing eight people, including two Guinean nationals. About 10 others were injured, among them a Guinean woman, Fatoumata Binta Bah, who was pulled from the rubble with her baby.

Her husband, Mamadou Mouctar Baldé, a Guinean who shared the apartment with the victims, said he had left the building only five minutes before it fell. The two men who died, Mamadou Bhoye Barry and Mamadou Bhoye Ndiaye, were both from Guinea-Conakry. Baldé said Ndiaye had been lying down until a courier arrived with an online order for him.

The Guinean Community of America (GUICA NY), which represents the diaspora in New York, said condolences will be received on 15 November from 18:30 at the Utica Mosque, 65 Utica Avenue, Brooklyn. Rescue operations were still under way at the site, and the group said it would share reliable updates as they become available.

Why it mattersFamilies of the two dead men and the injured survivor now depend on the GUICA NY's updates and the 15 November gathering for news.

Business · 2026-11-14

BCA SARL attends Simandou Mining Summit

The Guinean industrial supplier showcased its global network of 250 suppliers at the Conakry event.

BCA SARL, a Guinean industrial supply company, participated in the Simandou Mining Summit 2026 in Conakry from November 11 to 14. The company, represented by its General Manager M. Éloi César TOLNO, highlighted its role as a structured and reliable partner for Guinea's private sector. BCA SARL has over five years of experience and a global network of 250 professional suppliers.

The summit, themed 'Mining industry, engine of economic diversification in the era of Simandou 2040,' aimed to foster business between international investors, mining operators, financial institutions, and Guinean private sector actors. BCA SARL positioned itself as a qualified player in the industrial value chain, capable of meeting the new supply demands generated by the Simandou 2040 national industrialization roadmap, which represents over 200 billion USD in investments over 15 years.

BCA SARL specializes in supplying technical supplies, construction equipment, specialized consumables, and professional tools to sectors including mining, oil and gas, construction, energy, and public administration. The company's strength lies in its rigorously qualified global supplier network, ensuring clients find necessary products at the best prices, delivery times, and compliance standards without leaving Guinea.
