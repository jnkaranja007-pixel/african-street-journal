Court hears arguments in case against Central Bank union leaders

A court in Kaloum heard final arguments in the case against three union officials from the Central Bank of the Republic of Guinea, with a decision now pending.

The trial of Momo Camara, Aboubacar Sidiki Camara, and Aboubacar Yansané, leaders of the Central Bank of the Republic of Guinea (BCRG) union delegation, concluded its arguments on Thursday, August 27, 2026, at the Kaloum Court of First Instance. The three are accused of "contempt of agent, violence, and assault" following a complaint filed by Warrant Officer Daouda Sankhon.

During the hearing, the civil party's lawyer requested the court to find the defendants guilty and order them to pay 100 million Guinean francs in damages to his client. The public prosecutor also sought a guilty verdict for contempt, violence, and assault, recommending a fine of one million Guinean francs for each of the three defendants.

Defense lawyers argued that the accusations were not sufficiently established and asked the court to reject the public prosecutor's demands. The case has been adjourned for deliberation, and the court's decision is now awaited.

Why it mattersThis legal case directly impacts the employment and financial standing of three union leaders at the Central Bank, and could set a precedent for labor relations in the country.

Makanéra Kaké supports anti-corruption efforts, urges due process

Former deputy Makanéra Kaké backs the government's anti-corruption campaign but stresses the importance of respecting legal procedures and the presumption of innocence.

Makanéra Kaké, a former deputy, voiced support for the government's anti-corruption drive on Thursday, August 27, 2026, during an interview with Télé 24. He emphasized the need for firmness against those who damage the economy through corruption, stating that corruption leads to deaths due to lack of resources.

However, Kaké cautioned against premature accusations, particularly regarding the recent dismissals of ministers Mory Condé and Mourana Soumah. He highlighted the principle of presumed innocence, arguing that it is better to free a thousand guilty individuals than to condemn one innocent person.

Kaké suggested that rigorous application of the organic law on finance laws could significantly strengthen prevention and control mechanisms against corruption. He believes that if this law is respected, it would become very difficult to embezzle funds, even if complete eradication of corruption is not possible.

Why it mattersThis story affects citizens by highlighting the ongoing debate about due process and fairness in high-profile corruption cases, which can impact public trust in government institutions and the justice system.

Demoudoula bridge access road shows degradation signs

Four months after inauguration, the access road to the Demoudoula bridge connecting Ratoma and Lambanyi communes in Conakry is already showing signs of degradation.

The access road to the Demoudoula bridge, part of five metallic bridges inaugurated on April twenty-ninth, is experiencing early degradation. Torrential rains in the Guinean capital have caused the asphalt to crack near the railway sector, according to an on-site observation on Thursday, August twenty-seventh, 2026. Motorbike taxi driver Mamadou Aliou Sow expressed surprise at the rapid deterioration, stating the road significantly eases daily travel.

Local residents attribute the issues to both environmental factors and human activity. Halimatou Thiam, a resident, noted design flaws where poorly oriented gutters cause water to pool on the roadway during heavy downpours. Additionally, the management of runoff water is complicated by makeshift constructions near and within drainage channels, blocking the sanitation network and accelerating asphalt deterioration.

Further damage includes a height limit gantry, set at three point five meters, which was recently struck by an oversized heavy vehicle. A resident identified as DK emphasized the importance of civic responsibility, highlighting that accumulated waste and blockages in drainage channels contribute to the road's destruction. The Ministry of Public Works is urged to intervene to prevent further damage.

Why it mattersCommuters like Mamadou Aliou Sow who use the Demoudoula bridge access road daily are affected by its rapid degradation, which hinders their travel and increases maintenance costs.

Opposition leader demands inquiry into Forécariah mass grave

Six unidentified bodies, found tied and blindfolded in a mass grave in Allasoyah, Forécariah, have prompted calls for an independent investigation from a Guinean opposition leader.

The discovery of a mass grave in Allasoyah, Forécariah, containing six unidentified bodies, has led to calls for an inquiry from opposition parliamentary group president Abdoulaye Kourouma. The bodies were found on August twenty-fourth, two thousand twenty-six, in Sabakhouré, tied and blindfolded, according to local sources present at their exhumation.

Kourouma stated on August twenty-seventh, two thousand twenty-six, that his group plans to question the Minister of Justice and the Attorney General to initiate investigations into the incident. He also emphasized the need for the Ministry of Security to play its role fully, according to an interview with Africaguinee.com.

However, Kourouma noted that immediate fieldwork by parliament is limited due to the current parliamentary calendar, with only the Assembly's bureau functioning. Another opposition deputy, Dr. Faya Milimono, also demanded an independent and credible investigation into the circumstances of the deaths, as reported by Mosaiqueguinee.

Why it mattersThe discovery of six bodies in a mass grave and the subsequent political demands for an investigation directly affect the safety and security of residents in Forécariah and raise concerns about justice and accountability in Guinea.

Guinea's Syli national basketball team loses opening World Cup qualifier

The Guinean national basketball team was defeated by South Sudan with a score of 81 to 71 in their first match of the FIBA Qatar 2027 World Cup qualifiers.

Guinea's Syli national basketball team started the fourth window of the FIBA Qatar 2027 World Cup qualifiers with a loss on Thursday. South Sudan beat the Guineans by ten points, with a final score of 81 to 71, according to Mosaiqueguinee.com.

Despite the determination of the Guinean players during an intense match, the Syli could not avoid defeat. This marks a poor start for Guinea in this crucial qualifying window for the World Cup.

The team's next opponent will be Cape Verde, a match that Mosaiqueguinee.com describes as decisive for Guinea. The Syli must win to remain in contention for one of the top three spots, or they will need to secure a qualifying place for the playoffs.

Why it mattersBasketball fans in Guinea are affected as their national team faces an uphill battle to qualify for the FIBA Qatar 2027 World Cup after an opening loss.
