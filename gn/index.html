Guinean Government announces measures for Dar-es-Salam landslide victims

Following a recent tragedy at the Dar-es-Salam landfill, the Guinean government has unveiled emergency measures to secure the site, care for victims, and relocate at-risk.

The Guinean government announced a series of emergency measures this Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, 2026, to address the aftermath of the Dar-es-Salam landfill disaster. These actions aim to secure the site, provide care for those affected, and organize the relocation of populations exposed to risks, according to the Minister of Territorial Administration and Decentralization, Madame Camara Djénabou Touré.

The measures include deploying mixed teams to identify and inventory at-risk homes around the landfill and immediately securing the perimeter to prevent further incidents. Health support involves a medical system from the Ministry of Health and the distribution of mosquito nets to victims. Additionally, 542 victims are currently housed at Dixinn Centre 1 primary school, and about one thousand five hundred people needing relocation have been identified.

Structural measures include the complete demolition of the Dar-es-Salam landfill in the coming days and identifying new sites for relocating affected populations across Conakry. Social provisions involve organizing a collective burial for victims and mobilizing state services for a coordinated response. The minister urged household heads to cooperate with authorities when retrieving personal belongings before final evacuation.

Why it mattersThe government's actions directly affect the 542 victims housed in Dixinn Centre 1 primary school and the approximately one thousand five hundred people who need relocation, providing them with immediate support and future housing solutions.

Authorities discover six bound bodies in Forécariah mass grave

Six unidentified male bodies, found blindfolded and with hands tied, were discovered in a mass grave in a forest area of Sabakhouré, Allassoyah sub-prefecture.

On Monday, August twenty-fourth, two thousand twenty-six, six unidentified male bodies were found in a mass grave in a forest area within the Sabakhouré sector of the Allassoyah sub-prefecture. The victims were discovered blindfolded and with their hands tied, raising questions about the circumstances of their deaths, according to Guinéenews.

Ibrahima Soriba Touré, the mayor of the locality, confirmed the discovery on Tuesday morning to Guinéenews. He reported that citizens noticed unusual signs at their workplace, leading to the notification of the sub-prefect, who then alerted the police and prosecutor. Authorities subsequently visited the site.

Following the initial observations, the six bodies were transported to the Forécariah prefectural hospital. Examinations will be conducted there to establish their identities and gather information regarding the circumstances of their deaths. The identities of the victims and the exact causes of death remain unknown as an investigation is underway.

Why it mattersThis event affects the local community of Sabakhouré and Allassoyah by causing distress and concern due to the discovery of six unidentified bodies, impacting their sense of safety and well-being.

Guinea demolishes prison for new housing and administrative buildings

The former Maison Centrale in Kaloum, originally built for three hundred people, will be redeveloped into new housing and public service facilities.

The Maison Centrale prison in Conakry, built in 1933, has been demolished, clearing a strategic plot of land in Kaloum. The facility, which housed nearly two thousand detainees at its evacuation, will be transformed into new housing and administrative buildings, according to Mohamed Lamine Sy Savané, Minister of Urbanism, Housing, and Land Management.

Minister Sy Savané stated that the freed-up space will allow his ministry to advance its program for constructing housing and facilities for public services. This initiative is part of a broader government ambition to modernize the capital and other major cities, providing new infrastructure to foster economic and tourism activities.

Amara Camara, Minister Secretary General to the Presidency, indicated that the full scope of the project would be revealed in the near future. He emphasized President Mamadi Doumbouya's approach of prioritizing action over words, suggesting the transformation's relevance will soon become clear to the public.

Why it mattersThis development directly affects residents of Conakry by creating new housing and public service infrastructure in a central location.

Guinean hospitals digitize services, improve patient care and payment transparency

The implementation of the Visa Medical system in health facilities across Guinea, including Macenta and Beyla, is transforming patient journeys and hospital management.

Digitalization of hospital services in Guinea is significantly reducing consultation times and improving patient care, according to Dr. Milimono Sah, chief of general surgery at Macenta Prefectural Hospital. He states that the new system allows for at least ten minutes of consultation per patient, a considerable improvement from the previous system which struggled with high patient volumes.

The new system also enhances transparency in payments. An inquiry conducted from July twenty-second to twenty-fifth found that eighty-four out of ninety patients reported paying exclusively at the official counter. This indicates a reduction in informal payment practices and an improvement in financial control within the hospital.

Patients at Macenta Hospital report a positive change from the previous paper-based system, which involved multiple service visits, long queues, and frequent re-purchases of medical booklets. The new digital card is seen as a renewal, streamlining processes and limiting informal practices, according to the Secretary General of the Committee for Relations with Users.

Why it mattersPatients in Guinea are directly affected by reduced waiting times, improved care, and increased transparency in hospital payments, making healthcare more accessible and trustworthy.

Guinea establishes sovereign fund, boosts mining revenue and foreign investment

Guinea has created a one billion USD sovereign fund to manage natural resource revenues and saw foreign direct investment increase by 457% in 2025, reaching 7 billion USD.

Guinea has established a sovereign fund with an initial one billion USD to invest 70% of natural resource revenues into strategic social projects for vulnerable populations. This initiative aims to prevent the "Dutch disease" often associated with resource-rich nations, according to Abdoulaye Diallo writing in Mosaiqueguinee. The fund is a first for Guinea and is part of broader efforts to manage the country's increasing mining wealth.

The nation's financial landscape is changing, with projections for mining revenue growth of 500 million USD one year after the first iron ore exports. This growth is largely attributed to the operationalization of the Simandou iron ore mines, which began operations four years ago. The mines produce high-grade hematite, approximately 65% content, sought by global steelmakers for its lower carbon footprint.

Foreign direct investment (FDI) in Guinea reached 7 billion USD in 2025, placing it second in Africa after Egypt and first in West Africa, according to a UNCTAD report. This represents a 457% increase from 1.4 MUSD in 2024. The country also experienced approximately 6% economic growth in 2025, with projections for annual growth to reach 10% from 2026, driven by increased mining revenues and local processing of resources, including a new gold refinery.

Why it mattersThe establishment of a sovereign fund and increased mining revenues directly impact the Guinean economy and its citizens through social investments and job creation.
