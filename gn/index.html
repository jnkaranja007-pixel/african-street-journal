Guinea president lists 92 occupational diseases

President Mamadi Doumbouya established a decree on November 5, 2026, detailing compensable professional illnesses.

President Mamadi Doumbouya has established a framework for compensating occupational diseases in Guinea. A decree released on Thursday, November 5, 2026, lists 92 professional illnesses eligible for compensation. This regulation defines the legal and technical basis for recognizing these conditions and creates a specialized body to monitor and update the list.

The decree categorizes these illnesses into four main groups: those caused by chemical agents, physical agents, biological agents, and those affecting specific organs or functions. Each table within the decree's annex is structured with three columns to guide doctors, employers, and social security organizations in determining compensation rights.

This measure aims to strengthen social protection for Guinean workers. The decree also establishes a mechanism for the ongoing monitoring and updating of the list of compensable professional diseases.

Why it mattersGuinean workers affected by 92 specific occupational illnesses are now covered by a new compensation framework.

Guinean man freed after six months in jail for theft

Amadou Diallo was acquitted by the Dixinn court on November 5, 2026, for a crime he denied committing.

Amadou Diallo walked free on Thursday, November 5, 2026, after spending over six months in prison. He was detained on April 24, 2026, accused of theft by his neighbor, Mamadou Bobo Diallo. The Dixinn Court of First Instance found Amadou Diallo not guilty of the charges, stating the offense was not constituted.

Amadou Diallo told the court he had only climbed a wall to enter his own home when he was arrested. The public prosecutor, Mamadou Yaya Fatoumata Boiro, had requested a one-year sentence, with six months suspended, believing Diallo was guilty because he was apprehended in the complainant's yard at night. The court, however, did not follow the prosecution's recommendation.

Amadou Diallo explained that an altercation occurred after he climbed the fence and was questioned by his neighbor. The neighborhood chief intervened, and the dispute was brought before the court. The verdict means Amadou Diallo is released from detention.

Why it mattersAmadou Diallo was wrongly imprisoned for over six months and has now been released.

Guinean school director says paid tutoring is indispensable

Mohamed Mara states that extra lessons help students grasp difficult concepts and improve grades.

Mohamed Mara, director of the Excellence Moussa Traoré school in N’Zérékoré, stated that paid tutoring is indispensable for students' academic success. He explained that while school provides the foundation, parents must arrange for private tutors to help students excel. Mara noted that different students learn at different paces, and some require repeated explanations to understand lessons taught in class.

Mara acknowledged that some parents hesitate due to the costs associated with tutoring, but he believes the benefits outweigh the expenses. He mentioned that some teachers offer affordable rates, though not all parents recognize the value of these supplementary lessons. The school director also highlighted that his institution offers free tutoring, but attendance is a challenge, with some students citing household chores as a reason for absence.

The director emphasized that student success relies on a collective effort from teachers, parents, and school administrators. He pointed out that even with free support sessions at his school, a lack of consistent student participation hinders their effectiveness. Some students' parents are called in, but the same excuses for absence are often repeated.

Why it mattersParents in Guinea are paying for extra lessons to help their children succeed in school.

Politics · 2026-11-06

FIM board reviews budget execution

Guinea's Mining Investment Fund faces delays in public procurement processes, impacting project timelines.

The Mining Investment Fund (FIM) held its 10th ordinary board meeting in Conakry on Thursday, November 5, 2026. Participants reviewed the activity report up to August 31, 2026, and discussed a budget revision following the adoption of the amended Finance Law.

Aboubacar Kagbè Touré, the board chairman, noted progress in resource mobilization for 2025 and 2026 and overall spending control. However, he highlighted a low budget execution rate, attributing the delays to slow public procurement procedures.

Touré urged the general directorate to work with technical services to speed up the finalization of pending dossiers. He stressed that a budget's performance is measured by its absorption capacity, while also defending the need for legal compliance in disbursements.

Why it mattersGuinea's citizens are affected by the slow execution of budgeted projects due to procurement delays.
