Parliamentary Union Suspends Staff Union Activities

The General Secretary of the National Assembly cited an expired mandate and political interference as reasons for the suspension.

Activities of the parliamentary administration's union bureau in Guinea have been suspended by Secretary General Aboubacar Camara. The decision, announced via a communiqué on August 31, 2026, stems from the union bureau's mandate expiring in 2024 without regular renewal. Camara stated that a bureau with an expired mandate cannot legally exercise its representative functions.

Camara also pointed to "contextual elements likely to create confusion between union representation and political engagement." He noted the political involvement of some union leaders and their concurrent holding of political responsibilities. The secretary general believes union activities should not be used for purposes outside their scope, such as political positioning, institutional pressure, or personal disputes, which he argued violates principles of independence and neutrality.

The suspension comes as the parliamentary administration prepares for the new legislative session, reorganizes structures, and gears up for the parliamentary recess. Camara emphasized that this period requires stability, administrative discipline, and clear responsibilities. The suspension is effective immediately and remains in place until further notice, due to the expired mandate and lack of renewal.

Why it mattersParliamentary staff and union representatives are affected by the suspension of union activities, impacting their ability to represent members' interests.

Health Ministry Incinerates Over 20 Containers of Dangerous Products

Health Minister Khaïté Sall oversaw the destruction of confiscated medical supplies deemed unfit for consumption at Kagbelen Dry Port.

Guinea's health sector underwent a decisive operation as over twenty containers filled with expired and dangerous health products were incinerated at the Kagbelen Dry Port. Minister of Health and Public Hygiene, Khaïté Sall, supervised the immediate destruction of these items, which were deemed unfit for consumption and posed a direct threat to public health.

The significant seizure resulted from the vigilance of the National Technical Committee for the Fight Against Illegal Medical and Paramédical Practices, supported by Customs and the National Gendarmerie. Minister Sall highlighted that the intercepted products were dangerous and would have directly harmed the population if they had reached the market. She commended the involved services and reaffirmed the government's commitment to combating illicit trafficking.

Minister Sall ordered the immediate incineration of the seized cargo to preserve national health security and urged the public to remain vigilant and purchase supplies only from authorized pharmacies. She emphasized that the fight against fraudulent products is a collective responsibility, aiming to protect citizens from harmful substances.

Why it mattersThe incineration of dangerous health products directly affects citizens by preventing the circulation of potentially harmful substances in the market.

Truck Hits and Kills Motorbike Taxi Driver in Lambanyi

A young motorbike taxi driver died after being struck by a truck carrying coal sacks on Saturday afternoon.

A fatal traffic accident occurred around four in the afternoon on Saturday, September 5, 2026, near the railway crossing on the road connecting Cité Enco 5 to Lambanyi. Mamadou Aguibou Sow, a motorbike taxi driver, was fatally struck by a truck. The incident happened near a signaled crossing.

Witnesses reported that Sow, who worked at Carrefour Canadien, was riding behind a truck that had just unloaded coal sacks. As the truck approached the signaled crossing, the motorbike taxi driver reportedly fell. The truck driver then attempted to reverse, leading to the fatal collision.

The truck driver fled the scene, leaving the vehicle behind. The truck's apprentices stated they were looking for their employer. The victim sustained fatal injuries and did not survive the accident.

Why it mattersThe death of a motorbike taxi driver impacts individuals and families who rely on this transport service for their livelihoods.

FDSI and SONOCO WAQF Sign Five-Year Social Protection Pact

The two institutions will collaborate on health and education programs to aid vulnerable populations in Guinea.

The General Directorate of the Social Development and Indigence Fund (FDSI) and SONOCO WAQF have formalized a five-year partnership agreement in Conakry to bolster social protection efforts across Guinea. The agreement, signed by FDSI Director General Lansana Diawara and the Executive Director of SONOCO WAQF, establishes a framework for joint initiatives aimed at improving the lives of those facing socio-economic challenges.

This collaboration prioritizes health and education sectors, focusing on the empowerment of vulnerable individuals. The implementation will leverage Guinea's Unified Social Register (RSU) to ensure precise targeting of beneficiaries and enhance the coordination of services. This initiative aligns with the government's broader agenda to modernize the nation's social protection system.

The five-year convention underscores a commitment to sustained cooperation and structured action in key social areas. FDSI and SONOCO WAQF aim for this partnership to serve as a catalyst for human development, social inclusion, and stability within Guinea.

Why it mattersThe FDSI and SONOCO WAQF partnership will focus on health and education programs, directly impacting vulnerable populations by improving access to essential services.

Business · 2026-09-06

Truck insurance validity questioned in fatal accident trial

A court in Dubréka is examining the insurance policy of a truck involved in a July 30 accident that killed three people.

The trial of Alhassane Sow, a truck driver accused of involuntary manslaughter, continued on September 4 at the Dubréka Court of First Instance. The proceedings focused on civil liability and the validity of the truck's insurance. The plaintiff's legal team disputes the insurance policy presented, suggesting Vista Gui, identified as the vehicle's owner, should be held civilly responsible for the crash's consequences.

The civil party's lawyer argued that a truck insured on the same day it caused an accident was likely not insured at all. However, the defense maintains the policy covers the date and location of the incident. To resolve this dispute, the court has requested the insurance contract for review, aiming to verify coverage conditions and duration. The prosecutor suggested the coincidence of dates does not automatically invalidate the coverage.

The July 30 accident resulted in three fatalities, including two Chinese nationals and their Guinean driver. The civil party highlighted the impact on the victims' families and intends to seek damages. The court has adjourned the case until September 11, requiring both parties to submit additional documentation regarding the insurance policy and potential civil liabilities.

Why it mattersThe trial's outcome regarding insurance validity and civil liability could impact businesses operating commercial vehicles and their insurers.
