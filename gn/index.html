60 Conakry vigilance members trained on disaster risk

Guinea's Environment Ministry trained 60 vigilance committee members in Conakry on environmental disaster prevention.

60 members of vigilance committees in Conakry received training on environmental disaster prevention and management on October 29, 2026. The Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development, through the National Center for Catastrophes and Environmental Emergencies, organized the event to mark the International Day for Disaster Risk Reduction. The training aimed to enhance community actors' skills in risk identification, early warning, and disaster prevention.

Dr. Karim Samoura, Secretary General of the Ministry of Environment, presided over the ceremony and emphasized the critical role of local populations in disaster prevention. He highlighted vigilance committee members as the first alert relays within communities, stressing that national policies require local anchoring to succeed. The initiative seeks to build trust in civic engagement and equip participants with knowledge for environmental safety.

The training session urged participants to become ambassadors for environmental safety and prevention in their neighborhoods. They were encouraged to promote good sanitation practices, respect public utility easements, and protect drainage systems and green spaces. The government reaffirmed its trust in civic engagement through this training.

Why it matters60 community members in Conakry were trained to identify risks and provide early warnings for environmental disasters.

Guinea hosts francophone telecom regulators

Conakry meeting addresses digital infrastructure deployment and connectivity challenges.

Conakry, Guinea, hosted the 24th annual meeting of the Francophone Network of Telecommunications Regulation (FRATEL) starting October 29, 2026. The conference, preceded by an ITU workshop, gathered regulators and experts to discuss measures for deploying digital infrastructure. The goal is to harmonize regulatory and economic tools for effective, accessible, and resilient networks across the francophone space.

Mamady Doumbouya, Director General of Guinea's Telecommunications Regulatory Authority (ARPT), highlighted progress since the ACE fiber optic cable arrived 12 years ago. Guinea now has 13 million mobile subscribers, connecting three in four citizens, and nearly 12 million people to mobile internet, or two in three. However, over 600 localities in Guinea still lack network coverage.

To address these gaps, Doumbouya outlined a three-pillar strategy: sharing infrastructure like pylons and fiber to reduce costs, extending coverage to over 600 villages, and ensuring user trust through network quality control and market data publication. The ARPT aims to gather proposals during the two-day meeting.

Why it mattersOver 600 localities in Guinea still lack network coverage, affecting citizens' access to digital services.

Rio Tinto SimFer shows Simandou mine operations

Media professionals toured mining facilities in Beyla, Guinea, on October 28-29, 2026, to see ore processing.

Rio Tinto SimFer hosted a two-day media tour of its Simandou mining blocks 3 and 4 in Beyla, Guinea, from October 28 to 29, 2026. Reporters observed the ore crushing, storage, and transport processes. The visit aimed to showcase the operational realities of the large-scale mining project.

During the immersion, journalists visited the training center, nursery, crushing facilities, and ore storage areas. A training center official explained the practical risk demonstration zone, designed to bridge theory and practice for workers with limited formal education. This facility includes areas for risk identification, health and safety demonstrations, tire assembly simulations, and traffic management.

The tour covered various stages of ore processing, from crushing to transport towards the port. Media delegates interacted with technical managers overseeing the operations. The company highlighted its training and risk prevention measures to enhance worker skills and promote safety.

Why it mattersMedia professionals gained firsthand insight into the operational processes and safety training at Rio Tinto SimFer's Simandou mine in Beyla.

Guinea U-15 team loses in Azerbaijan tournament quarterfinals

The Syli scolaire U-15 team was eliminated from the FIFA World Cup and Festival in Baku after a 2-1 defeat to Greece.

Guinea's Syli scolaire U-15 team has been eliminated from the FIFA World Cup and Festival tournament held in Baku, Azerbaijan. The young Guinean players lost 2-1 to Greece in the quarterfinals, ending their bid for the championship title.

Despite the loss, the team achieved notable victories earlier in the competition, including a decisive 17-0 win against host nation Azerbaijan. They also secured wins against Algeria and Singapore, demonstrating their competitive ability on the international stage.

The Syli scolaire U-15 team will now compete in classification matches, with their next opponent being Colombia. Their objective is to secure fifth place and conclude the tournament on a positive note.

AS Kalombada beats Alu Star with late goal

Kabiné Sakho scored in added time to give AS Kalombada a 1-0 victory over Alu Star.

AS Kalombada defeated Alu Star 1-0 in a Ligue 2 match on Thursday, October 29, 2026, at the Yorokoguia stadium. Kabiné Sakho scored the winning goal in the 90th+3 minute, securing a late victory for the Siguiri club.

The match, part of the 9th round of the Ligue 2 championship, was tense with few clear chances in the first half, ending 0-0. AS Kalombada intensified their pressure after halftime, and Sakho's goal came during a moment of confusion in the Alu Star defense.

This win is AS Kalombada's second of the season, bringing them to 9 points and moving them out of the relegation zone to 12th place. Alu Star, with 12 points, dropped to 5th after their third loss.
