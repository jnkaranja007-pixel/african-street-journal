Guinean court jails 22 former Siguiri officials and 202 illegal gold miners

The former territorial administrators were sentenced to two years each for environmental damage, while the miners face three years and a 10 billion franc fine.

Guinea's Court for Economic and Financial Crimes (CRIEF) on Monday, November 16, 2026, convicted 22 former territorial administrators of Siguiri, a gold-mining town, of complicity in environmental damage, destruction and water pollution. Each was sentenced to two years in prison and a fine of 10 million Guinean francs. The court cleared them of corruption, illicit enrichment and money laundering.

The court also convicted 202 illegal gold miners, several of them foreign nationals, of the same environmental offences. Each received three years in prison and, jointly, a fine of 10 billion Guinean francs. The foreign nationals are also banned from Guinean territory for 10 years, and all convicted parties must pay court costs.

The prosecutor, Alphonse Charles Wright, said he was satisfied that the criminal responsibility of the accused had been established. The administrators, who denied authorising illegal mining, were accused of enabling the miners to operate, which prosecutors said degraded sites and drained streams in the region. The miners had already been released the previous Saturday and taken out of the country.

Why it mattersForeign miners now face a 10-year entry ban, and the former administrators are headed to prison for two years, a sentence that applies to 22 named officials.

Guinea court adjourns trial of prison guard over hospital inmate escape

The Dixinn court postponed the case to 30 November after the prosecution asked for more time to investigate the escape.

The trial of two prison guards and a state nurse, charged over the escape of an inmate from the Sino-Guinean Hospital in Conakry, was adjourned on Monday, 16 November 2026, at the Dixinn Court of First Instance. Two of the three accused were present; the nurse, Yacouba Condé, was absent.

After a judicial visit to the escape site, prosecutors said they had reviewed surveillance footage that set the inmate's movements in motion, but said gaps remained in the file. They asked for more time to question the Telecommunications Regulatory Authority (ARPT) about the inmate.

Guard Lancinet Traoré, who is in detention, asked for provisional release, citing poor health. Prosecutors opposed it because of the seriousness of the charges, and the court refused. The case resumes on 30 November, when Condé is due to appear.

Why it mattersLancinet Traoré remains in detention while the court waits on the ARPT inquiry and the absent nurse's appearance.

18 African countries meet in Conakry on extending social protection

The four-day seminar, organised by the Conférence interafricaine de la prévoyance sociale (CIPRES), brings delegations from 18 African countries together.

A seminar on extending social protection to people who are not yet covered opened in Conakry on Monday, 16 November 2026. The Conférence interafricaine de la prévoyance sociale (CIPRES), a regional body for social security, is hosting delegations from 18 African countries, who will exchange experiences over four days on how to widen protection to populations that are insufficiently covered.

Bakary Sylla, director general of Guinea's Caisse nationale de sécurité sociale (CNSS), the national social security fund, said the seminar would help improve operational plans already under way and that other countries' successes and difficulties would inspire Guinea. He said the system was originally designed for salaried workers, while many citizens work in the informal sector or in rural areas, and that craftspeople, farmers, traders and self-employed professionals should be able to rely on national solidarity against sickness, old age and accidents.

Sylla said the CNSS launched an actuarial study with the International Labour Organization in 2023, which identified the risks to cover as sickness, old age and occupational hazards. Guinea's labour and social protection minister, Ramatoulaye Camara, said coverage across the CIPRES area is about 10 percent of the population, and she described reforms under way, starting with the legal and institutional framework.

Why it mattersInformal workers such as craftspeople, farmers and traders, who are not covered by the salaried-worker system, would gain protection against sickness, old age and accidents under the reforms discussed.

Guinea beats Eritrea 3-1 in Cairo to take provisional lead in Group D

Guinea's Syli National moved provisionally to the top of Group D with nine points after four matchdays of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers.

The Syli National won 3-1 away to Eritrea in Cairo on Monday, 16 November 2026, in the fourth matchday of the qualifiers for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (CAN). Mohamed Soumah, a central defender, opened the scoring in the 28th minute before half-time.

Yakhouba Gnagna Barry, a striker for Horoya AC, scored in the 52nd and 77th minutes, the second on a pass from Sékou Oumar Sylla. Ali Sulieman pulled one back for Eritrea in added time.

Guinea hosts South Africa on 22 March 2027 at the 28 Septembre stadium and plays its final qualifier in Kenya on 30 March 2027. South Africa was due to play Kenya on Tuesday, which could change the top of the group.

Why it mattersGuinea's result puts it provisionally first in Group D, with two remaining qualifiers against South Africa and Kenya on dated fixtures.
