New OGP Leaders Face Union Demands

The new director and deputy director of Guinea's advertising office were presented with a list of urgent employee grievances upon taking office.

Mohamed Bangoura, the new Director General of the Office Guinéen de Publicité (OGP), and his deputy, Alya Camara, have been installed in their roles. The union representing OGP employees immediately presented them with a list of demands. These include the regularization of 174 recruits from 2022, salary increases for 145 permanent employees, and the correction of pay disparities. The union also requested training and scholarships abroad to enhance staff capabilities and the institutionalization of internal social dialogue to prevent future crises.

The union secretary general, Charles Mohamed Kolié, highlighted past internal conflicts and legal issues that have damaged the institution's reputation. He urged the new leadership to avoid favoritism and injustice, which he stated led to the downfall of previous directors. Kolié expressed hope that the new duo's healthy management would lead to their advancement within the state and restore the OGP's national and international standing.

Alya Camara, formerly acting director, assured the new Director General of his full availability to ensure a successful transition and continuity of public service. He noted progress made during his interim period, including increased revenue, controlled expenses, digitalization efforts, and improved human resource management. Camara emphasized the importance of unity and complementarity between the two leaders for the OGP's prosperity.

Why it mattersEmployees at the OGP are affected by demands for better pay, job security, and improved working conditions from their new leadership.

UK Export Finance commits $2 billion for Guinea infrastructure

Guinea's Minister of Planning and International Cooperation announced funding for water, roads, electricity, and education projects.

Guinea's Minister of Planning and International Cooperation, Ismaël Nabé, announced on October 20, 2026, that UK Export Finance will provide 1.8 billion pounds sterling, approximately $2 billion, to support major projects under the Simandou 2040 program. These funds, already signed, are designated for transforming key sectors including water, roads, electricity, and education across the nation. The announcement was made during the opening of the 5th Forum on Trade and Investment (WCAF V) in Conakry, which saw participation from foreign delegations and investors.

Minister Nabé emphasized that these are concrete financial commitments aimed at tangible results, with financing agreements awaiting ratification by the National Assembly. He stressed the importance of moving from commitments to actual project execution, stating, "It's not just about conferences, it's about execution." The forum also included ministerial panels and bilateral meetings to foster discussions with investors.

Further strengthening Guinea's financial capacity, UK Export Finance is expected to announce an additional funding package, though the amount was not specified. Separately, the Minister of Urbanism, Housing, and Spatial Planning, Mohamed Lamine Sy Savané, highlighted the need for ongoing land and urban reforms, including revising relevant texts and developing a national land policy to enhance transparency and citizen access to land titles.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the $2 billion in funding for infrastructure projects that will facilitate business operations and development.

Serhou Guirassy scores twice to boost Borussia Dortmund in Europe

The Guinean forward's brace secured a crucial 2-1 away victory against Sabah FK in the Champions League.

Borussia Dortmund secured a vital 2-1 away win against Sabah FK on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, thanks to a brace from Guinean striker Serhou Guirassy. Guirassy opened the scoring in the tenth minute after a rebound from Felix Nmecha's shot, only for Sabah FK to equalize through Aden McCarthy in the 39th minute. The German club regained the lead in the 53rd minute when Guirassy converted a cross from Joey Veerman.

This victory places Borussia Dortmund provisionally in contention for qualification, with six points from three matches. The win marks a significant turnaround after a previous setback against Bodø/Glimt. Guirassy's two goals bring his Champions League tally to four for the season, solidifying his role as the team's offensive leader.

The win moves Borussia Dortmund to ten points in the standings, pending other matches in the third round of the European competition. The team showed a more determined performance in the second half, ultimately securing the three points on the road in Azerbaijan.

Why it mattersGuinean international Serhou Guirassy is directly affected as his performance and goals are highlighted.

Guinea Teachers Shortage Closes Schools

Two primary schools in Lélouma are shut, leaving over 500 first-year students unable to attend classes due to a critical lack of educators.

The Lélouma prefecture's education system is severely impacted by a shortage of teachers, with over 550 educators missing across all levels. This deficit has forced the closure of two primary schools, preventing more than 500 first-year students from continuing their education. The interim prefectural director of education, Mamadou Oury Diallo, stated that Lélouma has 237 elementary schools and 855 pedagogical groups, but only 395 tenured teachers, requiring an additional 460 for elementary levels alone.

The affected schools are the Hakkou Pellé primary school in Sagalé, with three classrooms, and the Yambou school in the rural commune of Linsan, which has two classrooms. Beyond these closures, hundreds of children enrolled in first grade have been unable to attend school since last year due to the lack of teachers. This situation spans all eleven rural communes within the prefecture.

The shortage also affects secondary education, with 24 establishments struggling. For instance, the Diountou college-lycée lacks mathematics teachers from seventh grade to the final year, and there are no geology professors in any of the prefecture's high schools. Teachers are being asked to be versatile, covering multiple subjects, but this is proving difficult.
