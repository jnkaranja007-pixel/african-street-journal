EDG Sues Ratoma Mayor Over Alleged Illegal Acts

Guinea's electricity company filed a complaint against the mayor of Ratoma for alleged illegal actions, leading to a court summons.

The Société Électricité de Guinée (EDG S.A) has filed a lawsuit against the mayor of Ratoma, accusing him of illegal acts. The complaint was lodged at the Tribunal de Première Instance de Dixinn on Thursday, September 3, 2026. Yaovi Afadoda, EDG's Director of Legal Affairs and Litigation, stated that the company decided to take legal action due to illegal actions planned and executed by the mayor.

Following the complaint, the mayor has been summoned to appear in court on September 15, 2026, at 9 a.m. This legal action follows a recent escalation in tensions between EDG and the Ratoma municipality. After EDG cut power to the mayor's office for non-payment of bills, municipal police reportedly closed an EDG agency in Taouyah, with alleged misconduct occurring during the operation.

Afadoda expressed strong confidence in a conviction, citing the evidence and the mayor's statements. He assured EDG subscribers and the public that the law would be applied rigorously. The legal dispute highlights a significant conflict between a public utility provider and a local government entity over service payments and alleged retaliatory actions.

Why it mattersThe mayor of Ratoma is affected by a lawsuit from EDG for alleged illegal acts, which could lead to legal penalties.

Guinea Government Secures Land for 24 New Hospitals

Health ministry instructs regional inspectors to immediately identify and secure land for hospital construction as part of the Simandou 2040 Program.

The Guinean government is initiating the construction and equipping of 24 new hospitals across the nation's administrative regions. This initiative is part of the National Program for the Extension and Rebalancing of the Hospital Map. A service note from the Ministry of Health and Public Hygiene has directed regional inspectors to begin the process of making land available and securing it for these future healthcare facilities.

This significant program is integrated into the broader Simandou 2040 Program. Successful implementation hinges on the prior availability of serviced, regularized, and legally secured land parcels. The ministry's directive emphasizes the urgency of these preparatory steps to ensure the project's forward momentum and successful development.

The objective is to expand and rebalance the country's hospital infrastructure. By securing the necessary land, the government aims to lay the groundwork for improved healthcare access and services throughout Guinea. This foundational step is crucial for the timely commencement of construction and the eventual operation of the new medical establishments.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the government's directive to secure land for 24 new hospitals, indicating potential infrastructure development and investment opportunities.

CBG Seeks Environmental Study for New Bauxite Exploration

The bauxite producer is looking for a consultant to assess the environmental and social impact of accessing new mining sites.

Compagnie des Bauxites de Guinée (CBG), a major global exporter of metallurgical bauxite, has issued a call for expressions of interest to conduct an environmental and social impact assessment. This study is required for the exploration of plateaus 19, 24, 25, and 66 within its concession area in northwestern Guinea. The company, which ships approximately 16 million tonnes annually, operates under stringent international standards including ASI, ISO 1400, and 45001.

The selected consultant will be responsible for producing a notice of environmental and social impact (NIES) in compliance with Guinean legislation and CBG's subscribed norms. Accessing new plateaus is a periodic necessity for CBG's bauxite operations, and these areas are often inhabited, potentially leading to economic displacement. CBG is registered in Delaware, USA, and in Guinea, with its headquarters and production facilities located within the country.

CBG aligns its practices with IFC performance standards and is committed to sustainable development. The selection of the consultant will be based on demonstrated competence and experience in similar work, as detailed in the accompanying terms of reference. This initiative is part of the company's ongoing efforts to manage its operational expansion responsibly.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the company is undertaking a necessary environmental and social impact study for new bauxite exploration, which is crucial for future operations and compliance.

USTG Pursues Talks Over Central Bank Sanctions

Guinea's main labor union will negotiate with the central bank governor after he suspended the union delegation and fired 12 agents.

The Union Syndicale des Travailleurs de Guinée (USTG) has opted for negotiation to resolve a dispute with the Governor of the Banque Centrale de la République de Guinée (BCRG). Governor Karamo Kaba recently suspended the union delegation and banned all union activities within the institution, warning of legal consequences for any violations. He also dismissed 12 bank employees involved in recent work stoppages that disrupted services for two days.

In response to these actions, which have escalated tensions, the USTG's national executive bureau convened an urgent meeting. The union leadership, including Secretary-General Abdoulaye Camara and representatives from affiliated unions, decided to prioritize dialogue. A source close to the USTG indicated that this inclusive approach will guide their strategy, while maintaining that workers' interests remain paramount and non-negotiable.

The meeting took place at the USTG headquarters under strict confidentiality, away from media presence. The central bank's recent measures represent a significant challenge to the union's operations and its members' rights. The USTG's decision to seek a negotiated settlement aims to de-escalate the situation and find a swift resolution to the ongoing crisis within the financial institution.

Why it mattersWorkers at the Central Bank of Guinea face disciplinary actions and potential job losses due to union activities.
