Guinea dismisses 22 officials over illegal gold mining

President Mamadi Doumbouya removed territorial administrators and officers for facilitating clandestine gold extraction.

Guinea's President Mamadi Doumbouya permanently dismissed 22 territorial administrators and officers on November 3, 2026. These officials, stationed in the Siguiri region, were sanctioned for aiding and covering up illegal gold mining activities. The government cited major land destruction and water pollution as reasons for the dismissals.

Mohamed-Hady Barry, a mining law specialist, described the action as a restoration of state authority. He noted that the dismissed officials included the acting governor, Colonel Fayimba Mara, and several prefects and sub-prefects. Barry called for judicial proceedings to establish criminal responsibility.

The decree, which involved five key ministries, marks a break from past tolerance of administrative misconduct. The affected officials' actions led to the destruction of agricultural land and chemical poisoning of regional water sources.

Why it matters22 Guinean territorial administrators and officers were dismissed for their involvement in illegal gold mining, impacting land and water resources.

Doumbouya holds dual leadership roles in Guinea

Guinea's football federation president also heads the telecom regulator, raising legal questions.

Mamady Doumbouya now leads both the Guinean Football Federation (FÉGUIFOOT) and the Telecommunications Regulation Authority (ARPT). He was elected FÉGUIFOOT president on October 27 and has directed ARPT since May 8, 2023.

This dual role faces scrutiny under Guinean law. Article 18 of the telecommunications law states the ARPT director general cannot hold elected office. FÉGUIFOOT statutes do not explicitly forbid this position.

The legal compatibility of Doumbouya's positions remains unclear. The law prohibits the ARPT director from holding any elected national or local mandate.

Why it mattersMamady Doumbouya's leadership of both the FÉGUIFOOT and ARPT raises questions about compliance with Guinean law, specifically regarding the incompatibility of holding an elected office while serving as director general of a regulatory body.

Urbaine Découverte names young Guinean talents

The third edition of the talent competition featured 50 candidates in music, slam, and modeling.

The third edition of Urbaine Découverte concluded on Friday, October 30, 2026, at the Kofi Annan University of Guinea. Organized by M-Events, the competition showcased 50 young talents across four categories: Miss, Mister, slam, and music.

Moustapha Diallo, CEO of M-Events, expressed satisfaction with the revealed talents and aims to expand the competition nationally next year. He called for support from sponsors and the government to nurture these young artists.

The organizers provided three produced music videos for the top three music contestants and prizes for Miss, Mister, and slam participants. M-Events used its own funds this year, seeking future partnerships to offer more opportunities.

Why it mattersYoung Guineans with talents in music, modeling, and slam are given a platform to showcase their abilities and launch careers.

CRIEF summons officials in 3.135 tonnes gold case

Four personalities, including the current central bank governor, will testify on November 23 regarding alleged gold embezzlement.

The Court for the Repression of Economic Offenses (CRIEF) has summoned four individuals to appear as witnesses in the trial of former central bank governor Lounceny Nabé. Current central bank governor Dr. Karamo Kaba, former vice-governor Abdoulaye Yéro Baldé, legal director Daouda Diané, and Mamady Condé, Affinor's representative in Guinea, are expected in court on November 23.

Lounceny Nabé faces charges of embezzling public funds amounting to 3.135 tonnes of gold, illicit enrichment, and corruption. Nabé denies the accusations, stating the gold, valued at approximately $180 million, remains with Affinor, the company contracted for its refining since 2015. The court ordered the witnesses' appearance at the request of the special prosecutor and the defense to clarify the contract's conclusion and execution.

The case was adjourned to November 23, 2026, for the witnesses' testimony. The trial concerns the alleged diversion of public funds and corruption linked to a gold refining contract.

Why it mattersFour prominent Guinean financial officials are summoned to testify in a high-profile corruption trial involving the alleged embezzlement of gold.
