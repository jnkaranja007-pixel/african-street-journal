Education · 2026-11-12

76.5% of Guinean students fail math threshold

Analysis shows over half of primary students also lack basic reading and math skills.

A 2024 report from the Programme d’analyse des systèmes éducatifs de la CONFEMEN (PASEC) reveals significant learning gaps among Guinean students. Professor Alpha Amadou Bano Barry's analysis shows 52% of primary students finish school without meeting minimum reading competence, and 64% fall short in mathematics.

The situation is worse for students finishing middle school, with 66.8% not reaching the expected standard in reading. In mathematics, a concerning 76.5% of these students are below the required threshold.

These findings highlight widespread deficiencies in foundational skills across Guinea's education system.

Why it mattersGuinean students are not acquiring essential reading and mathematics skills by the end of primary and middle school.

Guinea media regulator suspends journalist

The High Authority of Communication banned a journalist's Facebook page and revoked his press card.

Guinea's High Authority of Communication (HAC) suspended journalist Abdoulaye Kallo in Kankan on November 11, 2026. The media regulator banned his Facebook page and ordered the removal of its publications. Kallo also faces a ban from practicing journalism and the withdrawal of his professional press card.

The HAC's decision N° 047/HAC/P/2026 cited Kallo's Facebook page for spreading shocking images that violated human dignity. The regulator also deemed the posts an incitement to violence likely to cause public disorder, constituting a breach of journalistic ethics and deontology in Guinea.

The HAC has tasked the Post and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority, internet providers, and administrative authorities with enforcing the ban nationwide. The decision prohibits the publication and operation of the "Abdoulaye Kallo" Facebook page across Guinea.

Why it mattersJournalists and media outlets in Guinea are directly affected by the HAC's decision to suspend a journalist and ban his online content.

Politics · 2026-11-12

Siguiri ex-administrators return to prison as corruption trial is adjourned

Trial of 22 former territorial administrators and 202 gold panners resumes on November 16, 2026, after a lengthy day of confrontations.

The corruption trial of 22 former territorial administrators from Guinea's Siguiri administrative region and 202 gold panners has been adjourned to November 16, 2026. The accused face charges including corruption of public agents, illicit enrichment, money laundering, environmental damage and water pollution, and the former officials have been returned to prison.

At the Court of Repression of Economic and Financial Offences (CRIEF), the former administrators, who served as prefects, sub-prefects and interim governors, unanimously denied knowing about foreign nationals from Burkina Faso, Mali and Togo carrying out illegal gold mining in their areas. The gold panners admitted to artisanal mining in Kintinian, Kourémalé and Soumbalakoba but said they did not know the administrators in charge where they were arrested.

Questioning also centred on excavators, known locally as 'poclains', found in the affected localities. The former officials said they inherited already degraded zones and found formal instructions banning the use of such machines in artisanal gold mining. Closing submissions and pleadings are expected next Monday, according to Mosaiqueguinee.

Why it mattersFormer administrators and the 202 accused gold panners face prosecution, with their detention and the next hearing date set by the court.

Business · 2026-11-12

Doumbouya invites investors to Simandou 2040

Guinea's president promises a transformed nation and stable investment rules by 2040.

President Mamadi Doumbouya called on international investors at the Simandou Mining Summit to seize opportunities within the Simandou 2040 program. He aims to make Guinea an attractive investment destination with stable rules, secure investments, and respected commitments. Doumbouya assured investors they would find a different Guinea in 15 years, with a focus on empowering the young population.

The president emphasized a vision of co-construction and national development, regretting the country's current state since independence in 1958. He urged investors to trust Guinea, stating the nation welcomes everyone and respects rules and promises. The Simandou 2040 project is intended to be a global driver for economic and social transformation, using natural resources for sustainable development.

Minister of Public Administration Modernization, Faya François Bourouno, also expressed the government's expectations for commitments from the international gathering. Former Minister Zenab Dramé, recently cleared by the CRIEF, attended the summit and supported the Simandou 2040 vision, hoping for training and jobs for young Guineans and opportunities for local businesses in construction, transport, and logistics.

Why it mattersInvestors and local businesses are offered opportunities for partnerships and contracts within Guinea's Simandou 2040 development program.

Culture · 2026-11-12

Russian embassy displays silk scarves in Conakry

An exhibition of Nina Routchkina's scarf designs opened at Guinea's National Museum on November 10, 2026.

The Russian Embassy in Guinea hosted an exhibition titled "Russia Woven in Silk" at the National Museum of Guinea on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. The event showcased silk scarves created by Russian artist Nina Routchkina, inspired by various regions of the Russian Federation. This cultural exchange aimed to foster closer ties between Conakry and Moscow.

The opening ceremony was attended by Guinea's Minister of Culture, Moussa Moïse Sylla, and the Minister of Higher Education, Djami Diallo, among other dignitaries. Mohamed Amirou Conté, Director General of the National Museum, highlighted the museum's role as a space for cultural dialogue and heritage preservation. He praised Routchkina's artistry for offering a new perspective on Russian cultural heritage.

Conté stated that the exhibition uses silk, color, and motifs to express Russian identity and artistic richness. He emphasized that art transcends borders, serving as a universal language for mutual understanding and cultural connection between peoples. The exhibition seeks to use art as an instrument for dialogue.

Why it mattersGuineans can experience Russian culture through an art exhibition featuring silk scarves.
