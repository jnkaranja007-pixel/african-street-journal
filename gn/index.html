CTG shows over 100 journalists the Morébaya port for Simandou

Media and content creators toured the port's rail link, berths and worker facilities on Monday, November 9, 2026.

More than 100 national and international journalists, bloggers, vloggers and influencers toured the Morébaya port in Séguélén district, Forécariah prefecture, on Monday, November 9, 2026. The Compagnie du TransGuinéen (CTG) organised the visit to show the port's equipment and logistics for the Simandou iron ore project.

The group left the Général Lansana Conté stadium in Nongo at 6:00 a.m. in three buses and reached the port around 9:30 a.m. They first rode the CTG passenger train, which seats 335, for about 50 minutes to Moussaya station and back. Officials said three cars seat 100 each and the VIP car seats 35, reserved for the president and his guests.

Back at the port, visitors saw sports facilities including a gymnasium, basketball court, handball court and synthetic football pitch, along with an infirmary providing on-site health care for workers.

Why it mattersWorkers at the port will have on-site sports and health facilities, according to the tour's presentation.

Guinea court jails eight foreign gold miners and frees one man

The Dixinn court in Conakry sentenced eight Togolese and Burkinabe men to two years in prison and banned all nine from Guinean territory for five years.

On Tuesday 10 November, the Dixinn first-instance court in Conakry convicted eight of nine defendants, two Togolese and seven Burkinabe nationals arrested in Siguiri, for illegal gold panning. The court sentenced each of the eight to two years in prison. The ninth defendant, Adama Tindreogo, was cleared of gold mining and environmental destruction but was jailed for one year for illegal stay and false statements.

Eight of the nine admitted panning gold in the Siguiri area. Most had been in Guinea for less than a year, some for only two weeks. Emmanuel Kemite, a Togolese defendant, told the court the miners sleep in the open between the pits. Harouna Ganame said he left Burkina Faso after jihadist attacks to look for work. Tindreogo said he sells phones and accessories in Conakry and denied any link to the sites.

The court also barred all nine convicted men from the whole of Guinean territory for five years. The prosecutor had asked for five years in prison and a 10-year ban with expulsion. He cited deforestation, polluted rivers and health risks from clandestine gold mining.

Why it mattersThe nine men face two-year and one-year prison terms and a five-year ban from Guinea, so foreign migrant workers in the gold areas face deportation risk.

Guinea hosts regional livestock meeting

Officials met in Conakry to plan the implementation of a 60 million euro regional livestock program.

Guinea hosted the first regional steering committee meeting for the West and Sahel Livestock Development Support Program (PRADEP-AOS) in Conakry on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. The meeting gathered livestock and pastoralism development actors to discuss program priorities and implementation. The program aims to strengthen pastoral resource governance, improve animal health, secure transhumance, and develop competitive value chains.

Guinean Minister of Livestock Alpha Bacar Barry highlighted the sector's economic importance, contributing 36% to Sahelian agricultural GDP and 15% to coastal countries' GDP. He noted persistent challenges including climate change, land pressure, water scarcity, animal diseases, and pastoral mobility conflicts. Regional cooperation is essential to address these cross-border issues, Barry stated, emphasizing the program's role in fostering shared development from pastoral mobility.

The PRADEP-AOS, funded by the European Union for 2025-2030, covers 13 African countries and is coordinated by the CILSS. Enabel is implementing the program's western corridor component. The EU Delegation in Guinea, represented by cooperation chief Gianpiero Borzillo, noted the meeting's timely occurrence for the program's progress.

Why it mattersThe meeting in Conakry involves 13 African countries and aims to improve livestock development and pastoral mobility, directly affecting farmers and rural economies across the region.

Guinea and France honour veterans on Armistice Day in Conakry

Conakry ceremonies marked the 108th anniversary of the 1918 Armistice, with a 100 million Guinean franc aid package for needy veterans.

Guinean authorities and the French diplomatic mission in Conakry marked the 108th anniversary of the November 11, 1918, Armistice on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. The event included tributes to African Tirailleurs, donations to veterans and their families, and a call to revalue Guinean veterans' pensions.

At the Boulbinet military cemetery, French Ambassador Luc Briard laid wreaths for soldiers who died for France, including Guinean Tirailleurs who fought in both world wars. He said African soldiers helped free Europe and France, and he named five French soldiers killed in 2026.

The embassy handed over medicine for a dispensary inaugurated weeks earlier and office supplies for the National Office of Veterans and War Victims. Ambassador Briard said an extra 100 million Guinean francs will be shared among needy veterans by a joint commission with the military pensions directorate.

Why it mattersNeedy Guinean veterans and their widows will receive 100 million Guinean francs in extra aid, split by a joint commission.

General Bangoura and FONIJ director Baldé jailed

Guinea's economic crimes court orders detention for alleged fraud, corruption, and embezzlement.

Conakry: Former Conakry governor General Mathurin Bangoura was jailed on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, at Coyah civil prison. He faces charges including alleged fraud, abuse of trust, and forgery. The general was already under investigation by the CRIEF special prosecutor's office in a related case involving Réma Tip Top Guinea SARL.

Also detained on the same day was Abdourahmane Baldé, the director general of the National Fund for Youth Integration (FONIJ). Baldé, previously banned from leaving the country by the CRIEF prosecutor, is accused of alleged embezzlement of public funds, corruption, and illicit enrichment.

Both personalities were taken to the central prison in Coyah, where they are expected to spend their first night in detention. The judicial proceedings target the management of the FONIJ and its director.

Why it mattersTwo high-ranking Guinean officials are jailed on corruption and fraud charges, impacting public trust and the management of youth integration funds.
