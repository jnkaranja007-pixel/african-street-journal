Conakry authorities clear illegal stalls at Concasseur market

Officials in Gbessia commune cleared the Jakarta area of the market, where residents say drug use and prostitution had taken hold, to make room for a modern butcher shop.

Authorities launched a clearance operation on Saturday, November 14, 2026, at the Concasseur market in the Gbessia commune of Conakry. Local officials, representatives of the Directorate of Land Planning and Urban Development (DATU) and security forces were present to oversee the removal of structures illegally occupied inside and around the market.

Mamadou Aliou Barry, president of the Hamdallaye 1 neighbourhood council, said the area, known locally as "Jakarta", had become a place of prostitution and drug use, and that several young people who took the drug kush ended up in hospital and later died. The site is to host a modern butcher shop. Some occupants said they were given only 72 hours to leave, but Barry said the commune first notified the neighbourhood on October 9 and that residents were warned on site on October 10 and again a week later.

The operation is part of a wider public sanitation campaign to free public spaces, according to local officials. Barry said occupants had a month to prepare, a timeline the complaining occupants dispute.

Why it mattersTraders and occupants who built on the cleared site have lost their stalls, and the area is being reserved for a modern butcher shop.

Millimouno says Guinea's gold mining ban is handled badly

The lawmaker says the state's method has strained relations with neighbouring gold-producing countries, while the ban itself is not disputed.

Guinea's government has banned illegal and unregulated gold mining in Haute-Guinée to halt environmental damage. Faya Lansana Millimouno, a deputy and politician, told Africaguinee.com on Saturday, 14 November 2026, that the ban itself is not disputed, but that the government is going about it the wrong way.

Millimouno said the machine-driven mining that has ruined the environment is mostly run by officials based in Conakry, not by artisanal miners working with hand tools. He said the first step of the ban has put Guinea in difficulty with neighbours, noting that Ghana, Burkina Faso and Mali also have gold mines, where Guineans work too.

Mamadou Bhoye Sall, president of the Nouvelles Forces Démocratiques (NFD), told Mosaiqueguinee.com this weekend that he backs the ban but not the violence. Clashes between security forces and residents in Siguiri over the ban have caused deaths, the outlet reported.

Why it mattersTraders and miners in Siguiri and Haute-Guinée face a crackdown whose methods are now being disputed in parliament and by party leaders.

Guinea launches Simandou Mining Summit

The summit showcased infrastructure projects worth billions to diversify Guinea's economy.

Guinea officially launched its first Simandou Mining Summit on November 11, 2026, in Conakry. The event, which included business meetings over three days, focused on the revival of the Simandou project. Minister of Mines and Geology Bouna Sylla detailed the process, starting with defining state expectations on July 27, 2022, leading to term sheet signings on December 22, 2022.

Sylla explained that negotiations resulted in a shareholders' pact for the Compagnie du Transguinéen (CTG) on March 8, 2023, and the project's relaunch by President Mamadi Doumbouya on March 18, 2023. An agreement to consolidate the shareholders' pact was reached on July 27, 2023, followed by the signing of the mining co-development convention on August 10, 2023, granting the state a 15% non-contributory stake.

The convention allows the state significant management roles in the CTG, aiming to make it an operational entity. Amendments to mining conventions were signed on August 10, 2023, adapting them to the co-development model. The Chinese National Development and Reform Commission approved Baowu's investment on December 29, 2023, enabling financing closure and accelerating construction, with infrastructure work finishing on November 11, 2025, ahead of the December 30, 2025 deadline.

Why it mattersInvestors in the Simandou project have seen infrastructure construction completed ahead of schedule, with the state securing a 15% non-dilutive stake.

Guinea and Djibouti sign carbon cooperation accord at COP31

The two environment ministers will share expertise on sovereign carbon initiatives and the development of carbon markets.

Guinea and Djibouti have signed a cooperation agreement on sovereign carbon initiatives and carbon market development, on the sidelines of the 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Turkey. The accord was signed by Guinea's Minister of Environment and Sustainable Development, Fassou Théa, and his Djiboutian counterpart, Mohamed Ahmed Awaleh.

The two countries say the deal will strengthen technical and institutional cooperation, support the exchange of expertise and promote harmonised sovereign carbon initiatives adapted to national priorities. Djibouti set up what is described as the first such initiative on the African continent in 2023, based on the polluter-pays principle.

Djibouti's scheme requires greenhouse gas emitters in the maritime and aviation sectors to contribute to climate adaptation and the country's energy transition. Gabon in 2025 and, more recently, Liberia have developed similar initiatives, with support from the Africa Sovereign Carbon Registry Foundation.

Maladho Barry launches memoir on entrepreneurship and family in Conakry

The businessman presented "Mémoires Vivantes", published by L'Harmattan Guinée, at a ceremony attended by ministers, deputies and religious leaders.

Mamadou Maladho Barry, a Guinean businessman and entrepreneur, presented his book "Mémoires Vivantes. Le sourire et l'humilité, les assises d'un bâtisseur" in Conakry on Saturday, November 14, 2026. The ceremony drew government members, deputies, magistrates, diplomats and religious leaders, according to Guineematin.com.

Barry, who founded the design firm GUIDE SA and the training firm IGESS SA, said the book was also a way to explain his frequent absences to his family. He said the publisher's founder, Sansy Kaba Diakité, urged him to write after a 2021 testimony at another author's book launch.

The memoir looks at changes in Guinean and African society over recent decades and stresses the passing on of values, entrepreneurship, perseverance and attachment to one's roots. Barry said the launch was first planned for late 2025, around the Africa Cup of Nations, but took place in 2026.

Why it mattersReaders of Guinean business history gain a first-hand account from a founder of several companies and foundations, including his own explanation of his long absences.
