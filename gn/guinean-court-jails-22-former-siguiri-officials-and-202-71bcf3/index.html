Guinean court jails 22 former Siguiri officials and 202 illegal gold miners

The former territorial administrators were sentenced to two years each for environmental damage, while the miners face three years and a 10 billion franc fine.

Sources: Africaguinee, Mosaiqueguinee and Guinee Matin · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Guinea's Court for Economic and Financial Crimes (CRIEF) on Monday, November 16, 2026, convicted 22 former territorial administrators of Siguiri, a gold-mining town, of complicity in environmental damage, destruction and water pollution. Each was sentenced to two years in prison and a fine of 10 million Guinean francs. The court cleared them of corruption, illicit enrichment and money laundering.

The court also convicted 202 illegal gold miners, several of them foreign nationals, of the same environmental offences. Each received three years in prison and, jointly, a fine of 10 billion Guinean francs. The foreign nationals are also banned from Guinean territory for 10 years, and all convicted parties must pay court costs.

The prosecutor, Alphonse Charles Wright, said he was satisfied that the criminal responsibility of the accused had been established. The administrators, who denied authorising illegal mining, were accused of enabling the miners to operate, which prosecutors said degraded sites and drained streams in the region. The miners had already been released the previous Saturday and taken out of the country.

Why it mattersForeign miners now face a 10-year entry ban, and the former administrators are headed to prison for two years, a sentence that applies to 22 named officials.
