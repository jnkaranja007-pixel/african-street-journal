Guinea's labour minister takes lead of African labour administration centre

Ramatoulaye Camara was elected board president for one year at the 45th ordinary session in Conakry, while another Guinean official was named to head the centre for three years.

Sources: Africaguinee and Mosaiqueguinee · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Guinea's minister of employment, labour and social protection, Ramatoulaye Camara, was elected to a one-year term as president of the board of the African Regional Centre for Labour Administration (CRADAT), an intergovernmental body, at the close of its 45th ordinary session in Conakry on Thursday, November 26. Another Guinean official takes over as director-general for three years.

Representatives of the centre's 18 member states took part in the session. Camara said a selection committee reviewed the applications from Gabon, Togo and Guinea before choosing the Guinean candidate. She described the appointments as the first time two women from Guinea hold the centre's top posts: herself as board president, and a labour inspector and magistrate as director-general.

The outgoing board president, Justin Cyprien Ilboudo of Burkina Faso, said the centre trains, advises and researches labour administration across its member states. Guinea's prime minister, Amadou Oury Bah, said changes in the labour market, including artificial intelligence, are raising demand for specialist skills.

Why it mattersGuinea gains direct influence over the centre's training and policy agenda for the next year and three years, as the board and director-general it now leads set its direction for labour administration across 18 states.
