Niamey growers seek flood barriers and cheaper farm inputs

Gamkallé vegetable farmers say recurring floods and rising costs for seeds, manure and irrigation fuel are disrupting production.

Source: Agence Nigerienne de Presse · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Vegetable growers in Gamkallé, a neighbourhood of Niamey, are preparing riverbank plots for the next crops despite recurring floods and rising farm-input costs. They grow mainly lettuce, cabbage, parsley and okra, and sell directly to customers at the gardens.

Abdou Moumouni Abdoul Kadri, a gardener at the site, told Agence Nigerienne de Presse that floods have disrupted normal work for about five years, often arriving after growers have been active for one or two months. With no borehole, they pipe water from the Niger River into storage basins for irrigation. Seeds, manure and fuel for motor pumps have also become costly, he said.

Kadri called on authorities to build protective dikes and make seeds available at affordable prices. He also urged the agriculture ministry to strengthen checks on prices at farm-input shops.

Why it mattersGrowers who depend on the plots for their main income face repeated production interruptions and higher costs for seeds, manure and pump fuel.
