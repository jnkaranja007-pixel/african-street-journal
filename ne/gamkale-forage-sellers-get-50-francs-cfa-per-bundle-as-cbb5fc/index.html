Gamkalé forage sellers get 50 francs CFA per bundle as water hyacinth spreads

Sellers buy each bundle from children for 50 francs CFA and resell it for 100 francs, but an invasive plant is thinning demand.

Source: Agence Nigerienne de Presse · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

In Gamkalé, a neighbourhood of Niamey on the Niger River, people who cut and sell aquatic forage grass earn income alongside fishing and rice farming. Seller Adamou Salou says he buys each bundle from children for 50 francs CFA and resells it for 100 francs.

Salou, who has sold forage for more than 40 years, says sales fluctuate: on strong market days, sellers move between 250 and 750 bundles. He says fewer animals in the city have hurt demand, while invasive water hyacinth is spreading and driving customers away.

The grass feeds livestock, and Salou says fish lay eggs and feed among the plants. He says cutting one stem produces four to five new shoots; workers continue because the trade remains their main source of income.
