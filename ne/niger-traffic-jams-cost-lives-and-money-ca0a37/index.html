Niger traffic jams cost lives and money

Road congestion in Niamey and other cities increases accident risks, fuel waste, and air pollution.

Source: Agence Nigerienne de Presse · 2026-11-05 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Traffic congestion in Niger's major cities, particularly the capital Niamey, is creating significant problems for road safety, the economy, and the environment. According to Police Commissioner Dr. ABOU Mountari, Director General of the Nigerien Road Safety Agency (ANISER), these jams heighten the risk of chain collisions and dangerous driving behaviors.

Dr. Mountari explained that the economic impact includes lost time and increased fuel consumption, while the environmental cost is air pollution. He noted that while congestion exacerbates risks, the primary causes of accidents remain excessive speed, phone use while driving, drunk driving, and ignoring traffic signals. ANISER is considering infrastructure improvements like better roads and traffic lights, alongside stricter vehicle inspections and public transport expansion.

In 2025, road accidents in Niger resulted in 1245 deaths, with human behavior accounting for 99.24% of cases. Speeding was the leading factor at 40.76%, followed by failure to yield at 26.03% and carelessness at 18.28%. ANISER conducts awareness campaigns and joint road checks to enforce traffic laws and improve road signage.

Why it mattersNigeriens face increased risks of accidents, higher fuel costs, and poorer air quality due to traffic congestion.
