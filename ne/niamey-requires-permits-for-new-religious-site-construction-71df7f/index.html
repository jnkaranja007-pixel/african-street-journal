Niamey requires permits for new religious-site construction

An order signed on 17 November also gives existing sites six months to have their situation examined by city services.

Source: Air Info Agadez · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Niamey’s delegated city administrator, Colonel Boubacar Soumana Garanké, signed an order on 17 November regulating construction, expansion, renovation and use of land around mosques and other religious sites in the capital. It covers the city’s five municipal districts.

New construction, extensions and major changes require prior approval. Applicants must document land ownership, submit technical files and meet urban-planning, building and safety rules. Permanent structures on pavements and roads are banned; temporary prayer or festival structures require exceptional permission and must be removable and time-limited.

The order also provides for a census of existing religious sites. Their leaders have six months from publication to ask the relevant services to examine their status; violations can lead to suspended work, removal of unauthorised installations or fines.

Why it mattersLeaders of existing religious sites must have their status examined by the relevant city services within six months of publication.
