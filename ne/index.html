Niger and partners agree 7 points to revive Kandadji dam project

A high-level meeting in Niamey secured commitments for additional funding and the resumption of stalled construction work.

The Nigerien government and its technical and financial partners have agreed on seven points to revive and complete the Kandadji dam program. This agreement was reached at a high-level meeting in Niamey on November 11, 2026, resulting in a joint declaration reaffirming partners' commitment to the two-decade-old project.

Key commitments include additional financing of $561 million to cover a funding gap, with partners urging faster use of already mobilized resources and simplified payment procedures. The declaration also calls for finalizing contractual and financial arrangements to ensure the effective and sustainable resumption of work, with regular payment of approved invoices. A comprehensive approach for resettling 17 populations sites, including compensation, housing, and livelihood restoration, was also agreed upon.

Environmental and social safeguards will continue to be implemented, alongside a security coordination mechanism for construction sites and supply routes. Partners will strengthen coordination under government leadership through a synchronization plan, and a regular technical follow-up mechanism with high-level reviews every two months will monitor progress and commitments.

Why it mattersPartners have committed to providing additional financing of $561 million to bridge a funding gap for the Kandadji dam project, with work set to resume.

World Bank prepares $760 million more for Niger's energy, farming and water

The funding is added to more than $4 billion the World Bank already has committed in Niger, and is meant to support larger projects over the next five years.

Prime Minister Ali Mahamane Lamine Zeine chaired a meeting at his office in Niamey on Thursday, 12 November 2026, with a World Bank delegation led by Vice President for West and Central Africa Ousmane Diagana. The bank said it has prepared about $760 million in additional funding for Niger under a new partnership framework, on top of more than $4 billion it already provides in the country.

Diagana said the money is meant to prepare bigger and more structuring operations over the next five years, drawing on lessons from earlier programmes to move faster and more efficiently. Talks covered three areas: making energy accessible and affordable to all Nigeriens, helping farmers move beyond food insecurity and create small and medium-sized enterprises, and water, which he said is needed for both electricity and farming.

On water, Niger and the bank have drawn up a pact that sets out urgent reforms, public investments and the frameworks needed to bring in private-sector money. Diagana said implementation of the pact on the energy side has already begun.

Why it mattersTraders, farmers and households will depend on whether the planned energy, farming and water reforms reach them over the next five years.

Niger minister inspects Diffa university as construction continues

Higher education minister Mamadou Saidou toured the Hummay Salmana University site, where the administrative block and student dormitories are still being built.

Professor Mamadou Saidou, Niger's minister of higher education, research and technological innovation, visited the Hummay Salmana University (UHS) in Diffa on Thursday, 12 November 2026, a day after presiding over its official naming ceremony. He toured the campus to check how far construction of its academic and social buildings has progressed.

Accompanied by a large delegation that included ministry staff, the rectors of the Hummay Salmana and Maradi universities, and local officials from Diffa, he visited the sites of the administrative block and student dormitories, where work is continuing at a level officials described as only partly satisfactory. The university's infirmary is already finished, and officials briefed the minister on progress, difficulties and completion plans.

The highlight of the tour was the university's agro-pastoral farm, which has run fully since last year and has just recorded its first harvest. The minister expressed particular satisfaction with the results and the farm's potential to help the institution become more self-reliant.

Why it mattersStudents and staff in Diffa will rely on the finished infirmary for health care while the dormitories and administrative block are still unfinished.

20 Nigerien officials and journalists begin three-week China seminar

The seminar, organised by China's Ministry of Commerce, covers Chinese governance, modernisation and China-Africa economic cooperation.

20 information officials and journalists from Niger, drawn from the public and private sectors, are attending a seminar in Beijing, China, that runs for three weeks. The Agence Nigerienne de Presse (ANP) says three of its journalists are among the participants. The sessions were continuing at the Guoyi Hotel on Thursday 12 November 2026.

On Thursday, Sun Jiwen, a former Chinese ambassador to Gabon, spoke on the unification of China under Emperor Qin Shihuang in 221 BC and the building of the Great Wall. In the afternoon, Professor Zhao Yongshen of the Beijing research institute UIBE presented on Chinese modernisation, China-Africa exchanges and the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation (FOCAC).

The Chinese Ministry of Commerce organised the seminar to strengthen friendship with developing countries, including Niger, and to promote cooperation in media and other fields. The organisers say it is also meant to support human resource, economic and social development in developing countries.

Why it mattersNigerien journalists who attend will spend three weeks in Beijing learning how Chinese media and economic cooperation are structured.
