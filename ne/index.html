Niger Minister Meets Media Heads After August Unrest

Communication Minister Adji Ali Salatou met with media leaders to analyze information flow and address misinformation following recent events.

Niger's Minister of Communication and New Information Technologies, Adji Ali Salatou, convened with media executives in Niamey on September 2, 2026. The meeting, also attended by Ibrahim Manzo Diallo, President of the National Communication Observatory (ONC), aimed to improve the national information and communication ecosystem. This gathering followed events that occurred between August 28 and 29, 2026, in Niamey.

The ONC monitored media content from August 28 to September 1, observing traditional, online, and digital platforms. President Diallo noted an exceptional information intensity immediately after the events, leading to a public demand for verified news. He also highlighted the dangers of information vacuums, where unverified or malicious sources can fill the void, transforming uncertainty into perceived fact.

Further observations included divergent descriptions of the events, with official communications calling it a "mood movement" while international media used terms like "mutiny" or "coup attempt." The ONC also pointed to social media's role in accelerating both information and disinformation, with unverified images and claims circulating. The observatory stressed its role is not to dictate narratives but to facilitate investigation and reporting.

Why it mattersThe meeting between the Minister of Communication and media leaders addresses how information was handled during and after the August 28-29 unrest, impacting public understanding and trust in official narratives.

M62 Tahoua Condemns Niger Destabilization Attempts

The M62 Movement's Tahoua section reaffirmed its commitment to national peace and sovereignty following recent unrest in Niamey.

The M62 Movement's Tahoua section has publicly condemned acts of violence and destabilization targeting Niger, expressing strong indignation over events in Niamey on August 29, 2026. In a statement read by its Secretary General, Mohamed Ichéh, the group affirmed its unwavering support for the Nigerien people, emphasizing peace, national unity, sovereignty, and dignity.

The movement urged citizens to remain vigilant, avoid panic and rumors, and report suspicious activities to authorities, stressing that Niger will not be destabilized and its sovereignty will not be compromised. The declaration also extended moral support to the Defense and Security Forces, acknowledging their difficult mission to ensure national security. The Prefect of Tahoua Department, Mamane Nassirou Saidou, praised the initiative, calling for more such actions to support the country's rebuilding efforts.

The M62 Movement highlighted that national unity and patriotism are paramount during this period, stating that commitment to sovereignty includes defending peace, justice, and human dignity. The group called for unity across all eight regions of Niger and urged support for the Defense and Security Forces, asserting that the nation is greater than any internal differences.

Why it mattersThe M62 Movement's statement aims to counter attempts to destabilize Niger, urging citizens to remain united and vigilant against division and disorder.

Niger Health Minister Highlights Breastfeeding's Role in Child Development

Exclusive breastfeeding rates in Niger have risen but remain below global targets, according to the Health Minister.

Niger's Minister of Public Health and Hygiene, Colonel-Major Garba Hakimi, stated that breastfeeding is the primary act of protection, health, and development for children. This year's World Breastfeeding Week, themed 'Breastfeeding for a Sustainable Start in Life: Strengthening What Works,' aligns with national aspirations for the future. The Minister emphasized valuing proven breastfeeding practices and building sustainable support systems for mothers and infants.

Exclusive breastfeeding prevalence in Niger increased from 21.8% in 2022 to 34.1% in 2025. While these gains are significant, they fall short of the World Health Assembly's global target of 50% and the revised goal of 60% by 2030. The Minister noted that many infants under six months still receive other liquids or foods, hindering exclusive breastfeeding and increasing vulnerability. Breastfeeding continuation to two years has also declined to 36.7% from 2022 levels.

The Minister stressed that breastfeeding is crucial, calling it a child's first vaccine and a foundation for optimal physical and cognitive development. It is free, readily available, and meets infants' nutritional and immunological needs, reducing risks of chronic malnutrition, obesity, diabetes, and infections like diarrhea and pneumonia. He also highlighted that breastfeeding requires a collective effort involving families, health professionals, policymakers, media, and society.

Why it mattersChildren under six months in Niger are more vulnerable to malnutrition and disease because many still receive other liquids or foods instead of exclusive breast milk.

King Faisal Prize Opens 2028 Nominations

The international award seeks nominations for its five categories, including medicine and science, with a seven-month application window.

The Secretariat General of the King Faisal International Prize announced on September 1, 2026, the opening of nominations for the 2028 edition. The prize recognizes outstanding contributions across five categories: Service to Islam, Islamic Studies, Arabic Language and Literature, Medicine, and Sciences. This announcement marks a significant milestone in the prize's 50-year history, aiming to acknowledge individuals and institutions whose work has benefited humanity.

For the 2028 cycle, specific themes have been set for several categories. Islamic Studies will focus on "Islamic Heritage in al-Andalus," while Arabic Language and Literature will explore "Arabic Literature from the Perspective of Comparative Studies." In Medicine, the theme is "RNA-based Therapies," and in Sciences, the focus is on "Biology." The Service to Islam award is an honorary distinction for exceptional contributions to Islam and Muslims.

Applications are open to academic and scientific institutions as well as research centers. The nomination period runs for seven months, from September 1, 2026, to March 31, 2027. Submissions can be made via postal mail, email, or through the prize's official nomination portal. Further details regarding the prize and its statutes are available on the official website.

Why it mattersInvestors may be interested in the prize's focus on scientific and medical advancements, potentially indicating areas of future innovation and investment.
