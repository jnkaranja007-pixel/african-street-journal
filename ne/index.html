Niger traffic jams cost lives and money

Road congestion in Niamey and other cities increases accident risks, fuel waste, and air pollution.

Traffic congestion in Niger's major cities, particularly the capital Niamey, is creating significant problems for road safety, the economy, and the environment. According to Police Commissioner Dr. ABOU Mountari, Director General of the Nigerien Road Safety Agency (ANISER), these jams heighten the risk of chain collisions and dangerous driving behaviors.

Dr. Mountari explained that the economic impact includes lost time and increased fuel consumption, while the environmental cost is air pollution. He noted that while congestion exacerbates risks, the primary causes of accidents remain excessive speed, phone use while driving, drunk driving, and ignoring traffic signals. ANISER is considering infrastructure improvements like better roads and traffic lights, alongside stricter vehicle inspections and public transport expansion.

In 2025, road accidents in Niger resulted in 1245 deaths, with human behavior accounting for 99.24% of cases. Speeding was the leading factor at 40.76%, followed by failure to yield at 26.03% and carelessness at 18.28%. ANISER conducts awareness campaigns and joint road checks to enforce traffic laws and improve road signage.

Why it mattersNigeriens face increased risks of accidents, higher fuel costs, and poorer air quality due to traffic congestion.

Niger prime minister signs deal in eastern Libya

Ali Mahamane Lamine Zeine visited Benghazi on November 2, 2026, to boost cooperation with the Haftar camp.

Niger's Prime Minister Ali Mahamane Lamine Zeine visited Benghazi, Libya, on Monday, November 2, 2026. He signed a memorandum of understanding to strengthen cooperation with the Haftar camp, which controls eastern Libya. This move comes as Niamey also maintains ties with the government in Tripoli.

The agreement aims to expand cooperation in various fields between Niger and the Haftar-controlled region of Libya. Zeine's visit follows Algeria's recent announcement of sending military units to Niger. This diplomatic engagement seeks to enhance security along the Niger-Libya border.

This development occurs shortly after Algeria declared it would deploy troops to Niger. The Nigerien Prime Minister's visit to Benghazi highlights a complex regional security strategy for Niamey.

Niamey mayor regulates street vendors

New rules require businesses to maintain a two-meter distance from sidewalks and obtain municipal permits.

The Administrator Delegate of the City of Niamey, Col Boubacar Soumana Garanké, issued an order regulating street vendors on November 4, 2026. The measure aims to protect sanitation infrastructure, ensure pedestrian movement, and enhance road safety.

The regulation applies to kiosks, shops, and temporary structures, prohibiting their installation on sidewalks and drainage canals. Existing businesses have a two-month grace period from November 1, 2026, to comply with the new urban planning requirements.

Commercial activities on public land now require a revocable municipal permit specifying location, size, and activity type. Car and motorcycle wash stations must manage wastewater separately and avoid discharging into public drains.

Why it mattersStreet vendors and businesses operating on public land in Niamey face new regulations and permit requirements.

Niger minister visits AGRHYMET centre

Colonel Bylaly Elhadj Gambobo explored the climate centre's work supporting farmers and pastoralists.

Niger's Minister of Agriculture and Livestock, Colonel Bylaly Elhadj Gambobo, visited the AGRHYMET climate centre in Niamey on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. He aimed to understand the institution's activities and discuss agricultural and pastoral challenges with its leaders.

During the visit, Colonel Gambobo learned about AGRHYMET's training, research, and climate monitoring systems designed to anticipate climate risks, assist producers, and enhance food security. Discussions also covered strengthening collaboration between the ministry and AGRHYMET on data sharing, capacity building, and agricultural policy support.

Colonel Bylaly Elhadj Gambobo praised AGRHYMET's expertise, stressing the need to connect scientific knowledge with the practical needs of farmers and herders. AGRHYMET, established in 1974 and based in Niamey, is a specialized institution of the CILSS, comprising 13 member states.

Why it mattersNigerien farmers and herders will benefit from improved climate risk anticipation and support for their needs.
