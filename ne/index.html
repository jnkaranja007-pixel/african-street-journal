Niger trains energy planners with IAEA support

A two-week workshop in Niamey aims to improve national energy demand forecasting using specialized software.

Niger's High Authority for Atomic Energy (HANEA) is hosting a national training workshop on the Energy Demand Analysis Model (MAED) in Niamey from November 9 to November 20, 2026. The workshop, supported by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), aims to enhance participants' skills in using the MAED software for future energy needs assessment. This initiative is part of the technical cooperation project NER 2007.

The training, led by IAEA international expert Soufiane Hachad, features technical presentations and practical exercises using national data. Niger's Minister of Energy, Sani Maty, opened the workshop, emphasizing the tool's importance for anticipating future energy demands and improving national energy planning. The program follows previous IAEA cooperation, including training on the MESSAGE model.

This effort by HANEA seeks to build national expertise for effective energy planning and electricity supply decisions, contributing to Niger's energy sovereignty and sustainable development. The agency plans further collaboration with the IAEA to develop these national capacities.

Why it mattersNiger's energy planners are being trained to better forecast future energy needs and improve national planning.

Niger accuses Chad of drone strike

Niamey claims a Chadian drone strike on October 24 killed two civilians and injured others.

Niger's government accused Chad of conducting a drone strike on its territory on October 24, which it claims killed two civilians and injured several others. The incident, which occurred around 1 AM, also reportedly damaged property. This accusation followed a sharp diplomatic escalation between the two neighboring nations on October 26.

Niger's President Abdourahamane Tiani is described as unpredictable, with his government issuing a strongly worded statement against Chad after the alleged strike. Despite attempts by Niamey and N’Djamena to de-escalate tensions following the diplomatic row, security and geopolitical disagreements between the leaders make a swift return to normal relations unlikely.

The diplomatic dispute between Niger and Chad highlights ongoing security and geopolitical divergences between the two countries. The packet does not provide further details on the immediate next steps or reactions from either government following the accusations.

Niger sets December 2026 deadline for field clearance

Tillabéri governor Maina Boukar announced the date to prevent farmer-herder conflicts and protect harvests.

The governor of Niger's Tillabéri region, Colonel Maina Boukar, set December 15, 2026, as the deadline for clearing fields. This decision aims to prevent conflicts between farmers and herders and ensure peaceful coexistence. The meeting included administrative, customary, and religious authorities, along with security officials.

Governor Boukar emphasized that clearing fields is a sensitive step in the agro-pastoral calendar, crucial for livestock movement and access to agricultural residue. He noted that Tillabéri's economy relies heavily on farming and herding, contributing to food security and local income. The deadline protects farmers' harvests and interests while allowing herders access to pastures.

The governor urged traditional chiefs to mediate conflicts and called on communal authorities, technical services, farmers, and herders to respect the agreed-upon rules. Mechanisms for surveillance, early warning, and dispute resolution will be strengthened to maintain peace and security in the region.

Why it mattersFarmers and herders in the Tillabéri region will have a clear date to manage their activities and avoid disputes over land use.

Alger accuses France over EU statement on Niger troop deployment

The European Union's foreign affairs spokesperson addressed troop movements in Niger on October 30.

Algeria has accused France of influencing a European Union statement regarding the deployment of Algerian troops in Niger. The EU's foreign affairs spokesperson, Anouar El Anouni, read a statement on October 30 concerning the situation. This diplomatic exchange intensifies tensions between Algeria and France.

Anouar El Anouni, who handles foreign affairs and security policy for the European Commission, was questioned by the press in Brussels. He read a prepared one-minute statement that appeared carefully worded. The EU's position was shared during a daily press briefing.

The diplomatic dispute follows reports of Algerian troop movements within Niger. This incident further strains relations between Algeria and France, dimming hopes for improved diplomatic ties.

Why it mattersAlgeria and France are in a diplomatic dispute over the European Union's statement on Algerian troop deployments in Niger.
