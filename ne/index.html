Niger Establishes Human Rights Watchdog With Investigative Powers

A new independent administrative authority will investigate complaints, monitor detention sites, and advise the government on rights policies.

Niger has established the National Observatory for Human Rights and Fundamental Liberties (ONDH/LF), an independent administrative authority tasked with investigating human rights violations. Signed on October 13, 2026, an ordinance outlines the observatory's missions, composition, and operations. The ONDH/LF, headquartered in Niamey with potential national representations, can receive complaints from victims, their beneficiaries, or human rights organizations, and can also initiate investigations autonomously.

The observatory's mandate includes monitoring detention facilities, preventing torture, guiding complainants, and providing legal assistance, especially to vulnerable individuals. It is empowered to conduct inquiries, gather testimonies, request documents from administrations and private citizens, and seek public force support. The ONDH/LF will also promote human rights through awareness campaigns and advise public authorities on relevant policies and legislation.

Comprised of seven members appointed by presidential decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Human Rights, the ONDH/LF will operate free of charge and ensure adversarial proceedings. However, its jurisdiction excludes cases already before courts, those related to the July 26, 2023 events, and matters concerning state security or operations against armed groups. The observatory will submit an annual report to the President and can be dissolved by presidential decree for operational failures.

Why it mattersThe establishment of the ONDH/LF provides a new avenue for citizens to report and seek redress for human rights violations.

Niger Fuel Shortages Strand Drivers in Capital

Motorists in Niamey face long queues and disrupted daily life due to an ongoing gasoline shortage, with no clear end in sight.

Fuel shortages are increasingly affecting Niger, with the capital city Niamey experiencing long lines at petrol stations. Residents report that the lack of gasoline is hindering their ability to commute to work and perform essential daily tasks. Some drivers have spent days searching for fuel, despite official assurances that the scarcity has been resolved.

Frustration is mounting among Niamey residents who are urging authorities to implement a lasting solution to the persistent fuel problem. "For a week now we've been struggling, we can't even find fuel to get to work," said Harouna Chérif, a resident. Abdoulaye Zakari, another resident, added, "We've been told the shortage is over, yet we still don't see the light at the end of the tunnel."

The exact cause of the ongoing fuel shortages and the timeline for the full restoration of supplies remain unclear. This uncertainty leaves motorists in Niamey with no definite indication of when they will be able to refuel their vehicles without significant delays.

Why it mattersNigerien drivers are affected by being unable to find fuel, disrupting their commutes and daily activities.

Niger reviews plan to end child marriage

Officials and partners assess progress on a five-year strategy aimed at protecting millions of girls from early unions.

Niger's Ministry of Population, Social Action, and National Solidarity launched a mid-term review of its National Strategic Plan to End Early Marriage (PSNME) 2024-2028 in Niamey. The two-day workshop, attended by traditional leaders, experts, and partners like UNICEF and UNFPA, aims to evaluate the plan's first two years, identify challenges and effective approaches, and set priorities for 2026-2028. This review aligns with activities for the International Day of the Girl.

Despite a decrease in early marriages, with 64.9% of women aged 20-24 married before 18 in 2021 compared to 76.3% in 2012, the issue remains a significant challenge. The PSNME, launched in 2024 with UNICEF and UNFPA support, focuses on four key areas: institutional and legal frameworks, community mobilization and social norm change, family dialogue and empowerment of girls, and access to quality education, health, and protection services. The plan aims to protect approximately three million girls aged 10 to 18.

The review seeks to examine the plan's execution between 2024 and 2025, learn from its implementation, and define priority actions for 2026-2028. A UNICEF official noted encouraging progress in girls' rights through combined efforts, emphasizing the need for broad community engagement. Initiatives like "Sultanates and Cantons Friendly to Children" support girls' schooling and prevent early marriage, reflecting a government commitment to child protection and human development.

Why it mattersThe government's strategic plan aims to protect approximately three million girls aged 10 to 18 from early marriage and pregnancy.

Niger Regulates Electronic Media and Social Networks

A new ordinance requires online media and social platforms with over ten thousand followers to register with the National Communication Observatory.

Niger has enacted a new ordinance, number 2026-48, dated September 10, 2026, to govern electronic press activities. The law, comprising fifty-seven articles, outlines conditions for online media operations, digital actor responsibilities, and penalties for infractions. Notably, it extends its reach to social networks with at least ten thousand followers, though personal or non-professional blogs with fewer followers are excluded unless they disseminate information harmful to human dignity, national territory integrity, or state security.

Creating an electronic media outlet in Niger now requires either authorization or a declaration to the National Communication Observatory (ONC). The ONC will determine the specific conditions for authorization, creation, installation, and operation through its deliberations. Nigerien electronic press companies meeting regulatory criteria are eligible for state aid through a press fund, as defined by the regulatory authority.

This new regulatory framework aims to establish clear guidelines for the burgeoning digital landscape in Niger. It seeks to balance freedom of expression with responsibilities, particularly concerning the dissemination of information that could impact national stability and human dignity. The ordinance's broad scope suggests a significant shift in how online content and digital platforms will be managed.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because new regulations require electronic media and social platforms to obtain authorization or make a declaration to a state body, impacting business operations and potentially access to funding.
