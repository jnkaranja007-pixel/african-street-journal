Hajj conference and salon opens in Jeddah

More than 520 entities showcase services for transport, housing, food, and health.

The 6th Hajj Conference and Salon began Sunday, November 8, 2026, in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, with over 520 public, private, and non-profit entities participating. These organizations are exhibiting services and solutions for transport, housing, catering, healthcare, and technology, detailing preparations made months before pilgrims arrive.

The event highlights the complex chain of planning, contracts, and logistics involving multiple bodies and specialties that occur before pilgrims travel, find accommodation, or eat. Services like travel, air transport, lodging, and health are presented alongside infrastructure, telecommunications, digital services, and training, showing interconnected links impacting all Hajj phases.

The conference facilitates mutual needs assessment and partnership exploration among participants. Business meetings, contract finalizations, and agreement signings are taking place. Innovation and technology spaces connect solution providers with pilgrim journey challenges, complementing training programs for field agents.

Why it mattersNigeriens participating in the Hajj will benefit from improved services in transport, housing, and health, as a result of partnerships and solutions showcased at the Jeddah conference.

Niger coach names 25 players for Morocco qualifiers

Nabil Neghiz selected players based on 21 days of observation and analysis of performance and physical data.

Niger's national football team coach, Nabil Neghiz, announced a 25-player squad on Saturday, November 07, 2026, in Niamey for the upcoming 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers against Morocco. The matches are scheduled for November 13 and November 17, 2026, marking the third and fourth rounds of qualification.

The Algerian coach explained his selection process involved 21 days of meticulous observation, focusing on individual and collective temperament, technical, tactical, and physical qualities. The squad includes 15 to 16 regular players whose performance data and physical states were analyzed, with particular attention paid to players from the national championship.

Notable absences from the squad include Mohamed Wonkoye and Zakari Adebayor, a decision Neghiz stated was based on the team's current needs and context rather than a permanent exclusion. Goalkeepers selected are Tanja Mahamadou, Issiaka Boukary Kanta, and Abdoulaye Mainassara.

Why it mattersNigerien football fans will follow the national team's performance in the CAN 2027 qualifiers against Morocco.

Niger revokes nationality of over 20 people

General Abdourahamane Tiani's government has stripped nationality from critics of military rule.

More than 20 people have been stripped of their Nigerien nationality in the past two years through decrees signed by General Abdourahamane Tiani. These individuals, identified as critics of the military authorities and associated with the former overthrown government, are all currently outside Niger. The most recent revocations occurred in September and affected five people, including former Prime Minister Ouhoumoudou Mahamadou.

These actions raise questions about the consequences for those who have lost their citizenship. RFI has sought to understand these implications by speaking directly with affected individuals. The revocations target those perceived as opposing the current military leadership, impacting their legal status within Niger.

The packet states that RFI asked the affected individuals about the consequences of these revocations.

Why it mattersNigeriens who are critics of the military government are being stripped of their nationality, affecting their legal status.

Niger civil society questions EU over uranium exploitation

Arlit civil society group demands answers on environmental and social impacts of 50 years of Orano's uranium mining.

The Civil Society Coordination of Arlit on Sunday, November 8, 2026, strongly reacted to European Union statements regarding military cooperation between Niger and Algeria. The group denounced what it called interference in the affairs of sovereign states. They also called on European partners to address the environmental, health, economic, and social consequences of over 50 years of uranium exploitation by French group Orano.

This intervention follows controversy over remarks attributed to the EU's foreign affairs spokesperson, Anouar El Anouni, concerning Algerian military presence in Niger. The Arlit coordination finds these questions difficult to understand for an agreement between two neighboring African countries. They affirmed Niger and Algeria's sovereign right to define their military cooperation based on their interests and international commitments.

Beyond diplomacy, the group highlighted the legacy of uranium mining in Arlit and Akokan, criticizing France for not sufficiently addressing the impacts of decades of mining on local populations and the environment. Since the early 1970s, companies linked to Orano, formerly Areva, have exploited uranium, contributing to global supply but leaving a significant environmental burden and raising questions about economic benefit distribution.

Why it mattersNigeriens in Arlit are directly affected by the environmental, health, economic, and social consequences of over 50 years of uranium exploitation.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Algerian army to deploy in Niger

Niger's parliament approved the deployment of Algerian soldiers on August 30, 2026, following a request from General Abdourahmane Tiani.

Algeria plans to deploy soldiers from its national army into Niger. Both houses of the Algerian Parliament approved this deployment in late October, following a request from General Abdourahmane Tiani.

The deployment is intended to protect the strategic Trans-Saharan gas pipeline project. Algerian Prime Minister Ali Mahaman Lamine Zeine was seen greeting Algerian soldiers at Niamey airport on August 30, 2026.

This move, which breaks with Algeria's previous doctrine of non-interference, aims to increase Algeria's influence in the Sahel region and is influenced by economic and geopolitical rivalries with Morocco.

Why it mattersAlgerian soldiers will be deployed in Niger to protect a strategic gas pipeline project, a move driven by geopolitical rivalries.
