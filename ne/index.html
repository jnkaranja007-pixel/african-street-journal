Niger forum addresses productive land restoration

The fourth National Tree Forum in Niamey focuses on restoring productive bases to support agricultural and pastoral production and food security.

Niger's fourth National Tree Forum opened in Niamey on Thursday, August twenty-seventh, 2026, initiated by the "Initiative pour l’Arbre" association and overseen by the Ministry of Environment, Hydraulics, and Sanitation. The forum's theme, "the restoration of productive bases, pillar of the Refoundation," is a key mission for the Ministry, according to Colonel Laouali Samaila, Director General of Water and Forests.

Colonel Samaila stated that restoring productive bases is crucial for agricultural and pastoral production, food and nutritional security, improving groundwater recharge, and enhancing biodiversity. He emphasized that the degradation of land fertility, pastures, and water resources directly impacts economic activities and livelihoods, highlighting the tree's role in soil protection, erosion control, and providing useful products.

Despite millions of trees planted annually in Niger, a largely desert country where agriculture is central to most households' economies, there is a persistent issue of inadequate maintenance and protection. The forum aims to address these challenges, recognizing that the disappearance of vegetation disrupts ecological balance and affects the country's future.

Why it mattersThe forum directly affects communities by aiming to restore land fertility and water resources, which are essential for their agricultural and pastoral livelihoods.

Zinder Governor closes 5th International Solidarity Camp for Niger Scouts

Colonel Massalatchi Mahaman Sani, Governor of Zinder, presided over the closing ceremony for the 5th International Solidarity Camp of Niger Scouts, emphasizing youth engagement.

The 5th International Solidarity Camp of Niger Scouts concluded on Wednesday, August 26, at the Abdou Salam Adam Youth Center in Zinder. Governor Colonel Massalatchi Mahaman Sani highlighted the participants' commitment to promoting peace, social cohesion, and environmental protection. The event included activities such as tree planting, public health initiatives, and cleaning of gutters.

The camp addressed themes including responsible citizenship, road safety, entrepreneurship, climate change, cybercrime, social cohesion, and reproductive health. Sporting and cultural competitions were organized to foster fair play and fraternity among the youth. Discussions were also held for young girls on sensitive topics like gender-based violence, early marriage, and reproductive health.

Governor Sani commended the youth's contribution to the CNSP and the government's policy for strengthening youth skills and citizenship education. He also recognized the Association of Scouts of Niger for initiating this annual event and reaffirmed his support for the government and the Prime Minister, Ali Mahaman Lamine Zeine, acknowledging the President's interest in youth initiatives.

Why it mattersThe event directly affects the hundreds of young participants by providing education on critical social and environmental issues and fostering community engagement.

Niamey motorcycle taxi drivers adopt vests to boost security and trust

Motorcycle taxi drivers in Niamey are increasingly wearing vests as a work uniform to better organize the sector and enhance trust between drivers and clients.

Motorcycle taxi drivers in Niamey are adopting vests as a work uniform to improve security and identification. This initiative, led by the Association of Motorcycle Taxis of Commune 4, aims to professionalize the sector and address concerns about theft and incidents involving unidentified drivers. The vests are becoming visible in peripheral neighborhoods and along major roads in the capital.

Anas, president of the Association of Motorcycle Taxis of Commune 4, stated that the initiative responds to the need to better regulate the sector and build confidence. He explained that past incidents of theft or hit-and-run accidents were often attributed to motorcycle taxis, leading to the decision to implement vest wearing. Many drivers support this, believing it helps distinguish authorized operators and attracts more clients.

Clients, who have experienced issues like theft and aggression, welcome the measure, seeing the vests as a way to identify drivers in case of problems. Nafiou, a client, recounted instances of aggression and a driver fleeing with his change. While some drivers resist wearing vests for personal reasons, the association plans disciplinary measures and is encouraging users to choose vest-wearing taxis to reduce risks.

Why it mattersThis initiative directly affects motorcycle taxi drivers by requiring a new uniform and impacts clients by offering increased security and identification during their commutes.

Niger Minister Elected First Vice President of WHO Africa Regional Committee

Niger's Minister of Health, Colonel Major Hakimi, was elected first vice president of the 76th session of the WHO Regional Committee for Africa in Addis Ababa.

Colonel Major Hakimi, Niger's Minister of Health and Public Hygiene, secured the position of first vice president during the 76th session of the WHO Regional Committee for Africa. The session, which began on August twenty-fifth, two thousand twenty-six, in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, brought together delegations from forty-seven member states of the WHO African region.

The election of the president, vice presidents, and rapporteurs was the initial agenda item for the session. The Nigerien delegation, led by the Minister and accompanied by officials from the Nigerien Embassy in Ethiopia, including Chargé d'affaires Harouna Hamidou, participated in the proceedings.

The committee's work continued with a report from the Regional Director on WHO activities in the African region. Discussions also covered topics such as a new era for health in Africa, joint action for Vision two thousand thirty-five, and the joint reform process for the global health architecture.

Why it mattersNigerien citizens are affected by this event as their country gains a leadership role in regional health policy discussions, potentially influencing future health initiatives.

Kiota hosts 74th Maoulid, drawing thousands of faithful

The religious city of Kiota, in the Dosso Region, celebrated the seventy-fourth edition of Maoulid, commemorating the birth of the Prophet Mohammed.

Kiota, Niger, hosted the 74th Maoulid celebration from August 25 to 26, drawing thousands of faithful from across Niger and other Muslim countries including Nigeria, Benin, Togo, Burkina Faso, Ghana, Côte d’Ivoire, and Senegal. The event, held in the religious city of Kiota, focused on readings from the Holy Quran, zikrs, and invocations led by prominent marabouts and cheikhs.

The celebration gathered key figures including Prime Minister Ali Mahaman Lamine Zeine, the Chief of Staff of the President of the Republic, Brigadier General Amadou Ibro Bacharou, members of the National Council for the Safeguard of the Homeland (CNSP), and the Governor of the Dosso Region, Colonel-Major Bana Alhassane. Cheikh Khalifa Moussa Aboubacar presided over the event, which also saw the reading of the Prophet Mohammed's biography by Cheikh Djibril.

Prime Minister Zeine conveyed greetings from the President of the Republic and the CNSP, praying for a fruitful rainy season for Niger and eternal paradise for fallen soldiers. Governor Alhassane urged faithful to continue invocations and collaborate with the Defense and Security Forces to combat terrorism, thanking the Khalifa for the event's organization.

Why it mattersThousands of faithful from Niger and neighboring countries participated in a significant religious event, influencing community cohesion and national discourse on security and prosperity.
