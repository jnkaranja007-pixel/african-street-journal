Niger forces neutralise seven mercenaries

Military operations in October recovered weapons, communication devices, and destroyed armed group bases.

Nigerien security forces neutralised seven mercenaries during operations across the country in the second half of October 2026. The Centre Intégré de Coordination des Operations (CICO) reported that Operation Damissa, near the Nigerian border, saw ground forces destroy enemy bases and shelters.

In the north, two armed bandits were killed near Amazguèr on October 20 after firing on a patrol, with two rifles and ammunition seized. Two days later, in Tabarkat, four AK-47 rifles, two M80 machine guns, and hundreds of communication devices were confiscated. West of the country, one mercenary was neutralised in Tinfagatt, and a logistical base was destroyed.

The CICO report also detailed the October 16 neutralisation of a Boko Haram fighter near N’Guigmi, with one suspect arrested for supplying the group. On October 24, former Boko Haram fighters surrendered in Bosso with their families, handing over two AK-47 rifles and ammunition.

Why it mattersNigerien security forces neutralised seven mercenaries and seized weapons during operations in October 2026.

Algerian fuel trucks arrive in Agadez

60 tankers delivered fuel to Niger under joint military escort, easing supply issues.

About 60 Algerian fuel tanker trucks arrived in Agadez, Niger, on Saturday evening. The convoy was escorted by joint Nigerien and Algerian forces, marking a bilateral cooperation effort to address fuel supply difficulties.

This delivery clarifies the presence of Algerian soldiers in Niger, who were seen escorting the fuel trucks. The official reception of this first shipment is scheduled for Sunday, November 1, in Agadez, with the Nigerien Minister of Petroleum expected to attend.

The initiative continues the cooperation between Algeria and Niger, particularly in the Agadez region. This partnership has historically included health, education, trade, and infrastructure projects, with Algeria also contributing to the Trans-Saharan road and fiber optic projects.

Why it mattersNiger's fuel supply difficulties are eased by this delivery from Algeria.

Niger journalist Moussa Kaka detained for two months

Moussa Kaka, a journalist for radio television Saraounia, has been held incommunicado since August 31.

Journalist Moussa Kaka has been detained in Niger for two months without seeing his family or lawyer. Kaka left his media outlet in Niamey on August 31 and never arrived home. The Nigerien Minister of Justice Alio Daouda confirmed Kaka was alive and detained in early October, according to Reporters Without Borders (RSF).

Sadibou Marong, RSF's director for sub-Saharan Africa, stated that the organization is demanding information and calling for Kaka's immediate release. RSF believes Niger is violating its international commitments by holding the journalist in secret. Kaka was known for reporting on Nigerien news for RFI Afrique.

RSF is calling for Kaka's release and for Niger to uphold its international obligations. The organization has not received further information on Kaka's whereabouts or legal status since the minister's statement.

Why it mattersJournalist Moussa Kaka is being held incommunicado in Niger, affecting his freedom and access to legal counsel.
