Niger counts 1,245 road deaths in 2025 and sets 2026 as a year of road discipline

The transport minister put the 2025 toll at 7,116 accidents, 1,245 deaths and 4,439 serious injuries, ahead of African Road Safety Day.

Transport and Civil Aviation Minister Colonel Major Abdourahamane Amadou announced on Saturday, November 14, that Niger recorded 7,116 traffic accidents in 2025, which killed 1,245 people and seriously injured 4,439 others. He made the announcement in a message on the eve of the 15th African Road Safety Day.

In his speech, the minister cited World Health Organization figures that road crashes kill 1.19 million people a year worldwide, with pedestrians, cyclists, motorcyclists and micromobility users making up 53% of victims. He said the authorities have declared 2026 the year of road discipline, with targets to cut road deaths by 30%, cut accidents by 50% and achieve 100% use of approved helmets nationwide.

The minister named public awareness, mass education, stronger road safety enforcement and reform of the national road safety agency, ANISER, as priorities. He listed excessive speed, driver fatigue, alcohol and drug use, overloading and phone use at the wheel among the main causes of accidents.

Why it mattersDrivers, passengers and vulnerable road users such as motorcyclists face stricter enforcement and helmet requirements aimed at halving accidents this year.

Niger's communication regulator opens November session in Niamey

The National Communication Observatory (ONC) will examine draft rules on private audiovisual and electronic media, public access to state media and complaint handling.

Ibrahim Manzo Diallo, president of the Observatoire National de la Communication (ONC), Niger's media regulator, opened the institution's ordinary November 2026 session on Monday, 16 November, in his office in Niamey. ONC members, secretaries-general, central directors and technical advisers attended.

In his opening speech, Diallo pointed to ordinance no. 2026-48 of 10 September 2026 on electronic press, which he described as a major step for the ONC. He said the text strengthens the legal framework for regulating electronic media and lets the Observatory carry out that role fully. He urged members to show rigour and responsibility in reviewing the files.

The session will examine draft decisions on the conditions for creating and running private audiovisual and electronic media, citizens' and associations' access to public media, and how complaints and appeals are lodged and handled. Members will also consider an opinion requested by the Electronic Communications and Postal Regulatory Authority (ARCEP) on a draft transition plan, along with authorisation requests.

Why it mattersPrivate audiovisual and electronic media operators must now meet the conditions the ONC sets for creating and running their outlets, and citizens and associations will gain a defined route for access to public media.

Niger launches traditional wrestling championship in Niamey

40 wrestlers from across the country are competing as the federation tests a new individual-victory format for Kokowa.

Niger's Minister of Youth, Sports and Culture, M. Sidi Mohamed, launched the national traditional wrestling championship on Monday at the Niamey games arena. 40 wrestlers from regions across the country are taking part.

The event is an experimental phase of the revised code for Kokowa, the national wrestling sport. Under the reforms, victory is decided for each wrestler individually, rather than by region or team. The minister urged referees, technical coaches and wrestlers to show full commitment and a spirit of collaboration so the trial produces useful lessons.

The president of the Nigerien Wrestling Federation called the championship a major opportunity to move from promoting the new code to applying it fully. He urged referees to be rigorous and impartial, and asked coaches to prepare wrestlers under the new rules. General Assoumane Abdou Harouna, head of the Niamey region, said he was proud to host the competition and assured participants of improved hospitality during the event.

Why it mattersWrestlers and clubs must now compete under the new individual-victory rules, and the federation will use this event to judge whether the revised code works.

Nigerien media professionals visit Hunan province

Journalists and information officials from Niger are touring Hunan, China, to explore its industrial and cultural sites until November 21.

Nigerien information officials and journalists arrived in Changsha, Hunan province, on Monday, November 16, marking one week since their seminar began on November 10. Hunan, located over 1,300 km south of Beijing, is the birthplace of communist leader Mao Zedong and features dramatic karst landscapes that inspired the film Avatar.

The visiting media professionals will stay in Hunan until November 21, with planned visits to the SANY heavy industry park, the BROAD Group focused on low-carbon energy, and a hybrid rice research center. They will also explore the Yuelu Academy and the Malanshan audiovisual and cultural creative industrial park.

The delegation's itinerary includes tours of islands, parks, and towers within Hunan province, known for its spicy Xiang cuisine and diverse ethnic groups like the Han, Tuja, and Hmong. The visits aim to provide insights into industrial innovation and cultural heritage.
