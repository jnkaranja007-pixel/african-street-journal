Advisory Council Backs Niger's Leaders After Military Mutiny

The Advisory Council for the Refoundation (CCR) condemned acts of destabilization following a late August mutiny in Niamey.

The Advisory Council for the Refoundation (CCR) issued a statement on September 1, 2026, condemning a military mutiny that occurred in Niamey between August 28 and 29. The council affirmed its unwavering support for Niger's highest authorities and the government, denouncing the actions as an attack on the state's security and the nation's sovereignty. The CCR stated that these events aimed to destabilize the Republic and undermine the progress of Sahelian states reclaiming their dignity.

In its declaration, the CCR strongly condemned the mutiny, calling it an attempt to weaken institutions and compromise national refoundation achievements. The council reiterated its commitment to Niger's hard-won sovereignty, emphasizing it cannot be compromised. It also pledged total, constant, and unfailing support to the President, the National Council for the Safeguard of the Homeland (CNSP), and the Defense and Security Forces, commending their daily efforts to preserve national integrity, security, stability, and sovereignty.

The CCR urged all Nigeriens to remain mobilized, vigilant, responsible, and united to protect national unity and refoundation gains. The council also called for calm and discipline to preserve peace and stability. The statement concluded with condolences to the nation and families of soldiers who died defending the country's honor during the events.

Why it mattersThe Advisory Council for the Refoundation's statement affects Niger's political stability by reaffirming support for the current government after a military mutiny.

Governor Leads Prayers for Peace and Security in Niger

Agadez Governor Ibra Boulama Issa attended a prayer session for national peace and security during the Mouloud celebration.

The Governor of Agadez Region, Major General Ibra Boulama Issa, participated in a prayer and Quranic recitation ceremony for peace and security in Niger. The event, held at Tendé Mosque on September 1, 2026, marked the celebration of the Prophet Muhammad's birth, known as Mouloud.

General Issa expressed his satisfaction with the ceremony, stating that prayers were offered to implore divine mercy for the country during difficult times and to foster peace, security, and solidarity among Nigeriens. He urged all citizens to remain calm and united, emphasizing the need for determination against destructive forces seeking to undermine national sovereignty.

The Governor called on the population of Agadez and all Nigeriens to stay united and resolute, warning against manipulation and urging vigilance regarding national and international events. He also conveyed thanks, on behalf of President Abdourahamane Tiani, to religious scholars for their continuous efforts to ensure peace and security in Niger.

Why it mattersNigeriens are affected by calls for unity and vigilance amidst difficult times and threats to national sovereignty.

Niger Media Director Missing, Ex-Minister Detained Amid Security Concerns

Moussa Kaka, head of SARAOUNIA media group, disappeared after leaving work, while former Defense Minister Kalla Moutari was arrested.

Moussa Kaka, director general of the SARAOUNIA press group and a correspondent for Radio France Internationale (RFI), is missing. He was last seen leaving his Niamey office around 19:00 on Monday, August 31, 2026, and never arrived home. His family has reported him missing and has not received any news. Authorities have not yet released any official statement regarding his disappearance.

Separately, Kalla Moutari, a former deputy and minister of national defense, was apprehended by armed men in Dakoro, Maradi region, on Tuesday, September 1, at approximately 15:00. Witnesses from his circle confirmed the arrest, but the reasons behind it remain unspecified. These events unfold against a backdrop of security incidents that occurred in Niamey on August 28 and 29.

The Nigerien authorities have not officially commented on either the disappearance of Moussa Kaka or the detention of Kalla Moutari. The lack of official communication has heightened concerns for both individuals, particularly given the recent security events in the capital city.

Why it mattersThe disappearance of a media director and the arrest of a former minister raise concerns about the safety of prominent figures in Niger.

Mothers Fear Palpable as Children Suffer Severe Malaria in Dosso

Mothers in Dosso face fear and guilt over delayed treatment and lengthy care for children under five suffering from severe malaria.

Severe malaria, a leading cause of illness and death for children under five in Niger, is a significant concern at the Dosso regional hospital. Mothers at the mother and child reference center are torn between fear, guilt, and hope as their children undergo treatment for the disease. The pediatric ward sees many children admitted in critical condition due to severe malaria, including cerebral malaria and severe anemia.

Nurses at the Dosso hospital describe the emergency protocol for children arriving with malaria. This involves assessing the severity of the illness, checking for convulsions, consciousness, and ability to drink, as well as measuring temperature and blood sugar. Treatment includes stabilizing fever with injectable paracetamol, administering diazepam for convulsions, and providing intravenous glucose and oxygen if needed. The primary treatment involves an injection of Artesunate, followed by close monitoring.

Healthcare teams face numerous challenges, including a shortage of beds, especially during the rainy season, leading to multiple children sharing a single bed. Frequent drug shortages force parents to buy medication outside, which is difficult for those without means. Many children arrive late, often after trying traditional remedies at home. The hospital also struggles with insufficient technical equipment to manage severe cases effectively.

Why it mattersChildren under five in Niger are affected by severe malaria, leading to hospitalizations and potential death, with mothers experiencing fear and guilt over treatment delays and the severity of their children's illness.
