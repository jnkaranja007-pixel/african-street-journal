Niger orders Mahamid Arabs to leave by 31 December

Several thousand members of the community, many settled in the south-east for decades, have been told to leave the country by the end of 2026.

Nigerien authorities have ordered the Mahamid Arab community to leave the country before the end of 2026, according to RFI Afrique. Local authorities announced that several thousand nomadic Mahamid Arabs living in south-east Niger must depart by 31 December.

Many Mahamid have lived in Niger for decades, and the first arrived from Chad and Sudan in the 1970s. Authorities have taken measures against the community for several months, and voices in the country have denounced serious discrimination.

The deadline applies to a community concentrated in the south-east, where it has lived for generations. Its members now have less than six weeks to decide whether and where to go.

Why it mattersMahamid families who have lived in south-east Niger for decades must leave the country by 31 December or face the consequences of the order.

Niger and Algeria advance technical work on trans-Saharan gas pipeline

An Algerian delegation is in Niamey from 17 to 20 November to settle route studies and operational steps for the Nigeria-to-Algeria project.

An Algerian delegation is in Niamey from 17 to 20 November 2026 to advance preparations for the Trans-Saharan Gas Pipeline, which would carry natural gas from Nigeria through Niger to Algeria. Niger’s petroleum ministry secretary-general, Abdoulkarim Mohamed Ali, received the delegation on 17 November on behalf of the petroleum minister.

The talks focused on speeding up technical studies of the pipeline’s route across Niger and setting the next operational steps needed to prepare for construction. The delegation includes officials from Algeria’s hydrocarbons ministry, SONATRACH’s pipeline transport branch and the National Pipeline Company.

Algeria has begun construction on its section, according to the Nigerien news agency. Niger’s project management unit, an interministerial committee and state petroleum-products company SONIDEP are also to support preparations; the government says the project is expected to raise public revenue, create jobs and develop local content.

Why it mattersSONIDEP is to provide technical, logistical and operational support as Niger prepares the route studies and construction steps.

Tiani appoints Aboubacar Issaka Oumarou to lead Niger’s national police

The former head of the country’s narcotics-trafficking office was named police director general by a decree signed on 13 November.

Niger’s President, Gen. Abdourahamane Tiani, appointed police commissioner general Aboubacar Issaka Oumarou as director general of the National Police in a decree signed on 13 November. Oumarou previously led the Central Office for the Suppression of Illicit Drug Trafficking.

Oumarou has held several senior police posts in Niger, including regional police director in Agadez and deputy director of public security. He also served in United Nations peacekeeping missions in Timor-Leste, Burundi and the Democratic Republic of Congo, where he coordinated UN police in the east from Goma.

In Congo, he directed a project to train six rapid-intervention police battalions ahead of the 2011 general elections and led the UN police electoral group. The appointment puts him in charge of Niger’s national police as public security and citizen protection remain challenges, according to Agence Nigerienne de Presse.

Business · 2026-11-18

Niger investment agency visits three firms receiving tax-code benefits

The Niamey delegation reviewed production and jobs at Murex, Nataro Agro and Bénie, which benefit from the investment code.

A delegation from Niger’s Agency for the Promotion of Private Investment and Strategic Projects (ANPIPS) visited three industrial firms in Niamey on 17 November to review operations, production capacity and their contribution to the economy. The firms—Murex SARLU, Nataro Agro SARL and Bénie SARLU—receive benefits under the investment code.

Murex produces A4 paper and employs 16 people, with capacity to make 800 cartons a day, equivalent to half a container of imported paper. Its director, Hachem Ibrahim Khalil, said the agency had advised and supported the factory. The company reported an investment of 641 894 848 FCFA and a tax exemption of 142 233 722 FCFA.

Nataro Agro, a natural-juice producer created by the Belvie group, reported an investment of 1 535 821 223 FCFA and an exemption of 417 166 514 FCFA. Company managers presented investments, production capacity and jobs during the visits; the report gave no figures for Bénie.

Why it mattersMurex employs 16 people and makes 800 cartons of A4 paper a day, while its reported tax exemption is 142 233 722 FCFA.

Sport · 2026-11-18

Tillabéri handball league launches four-day tournament with five teams

The regional league opened the competition on 16 November, with the youth council president linking its peace theme to insecurity in the region.

The Tillabéri regional handball league opened a tournament at the regional stadium on 16 November, bringing together five teams from the city. The competition, sponsored by Abdoul Aziz Maliki, president of the Tillabéri communal youth council, is scheduled to run through 19 November.

Maliki said insecurity had weakened ties between communities and argued that sport could bring young people together. The tournament is intended to showcase local talent and give handball greater visibility in the region. Municipal and traditional authorities attended the opening.

Two matches were scheduled for the first day. In the men’s game reported, AS Dogoney beat AS Le Mafé 16-6; officials urged the teams to show discipline and responsibility throughout the competition.

Why it mattersFive Tillabéri teams are competing through 19 November, with local players seeking greater visibility for handball in the region.
