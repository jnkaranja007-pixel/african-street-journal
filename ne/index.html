Niger Officials and Clerics Pray for Peace

Regional authorities and religious leaders held prayer sessions across Tillabéri for national peace and stability.

Regional Governor Colonel Maïna Boukar attended Quranic readings and supplications for peace and social tranquility in Niger on Friday, September 4, 2026. These events, organized in several mosques in Tillabéri, saw regional authorities collaborating with local clerics. The primary objective was to implore divine protection for Niger against internal and external threats and to seek peace, security, and stability for the nation.

The devotional gatherings continued at the Ganda-Tché Friday Mosque, where congregants performed their weekly prayers and offered invocations for the country. These religious assemblies, initiated by regional authorities, demonstrate their commitment to supporting the nation through prayer, particularly following recent events. Religious scholars addressed attendees, emphasizing the importance of prayer as a means for believers to seek divine protection, peace, and blessings.

During the sessions, religious leaders urged the faithful to increase their supplications, strengthen their faith, and remain united in prayer for Niger's peace, stability, and security. The events were attended by various officials, including the region's two general secretaries, security force representatives, technical service heads, and civil society and youth organization representatives.

Why it mattersRegional authorities and religious leaders organized prayer sessions to seek peace and stability for Niger, affected by internal and external threats and recent events.

Nigerien Regulator Elected Vice-President of West African Media Platform

Ibrahim Manzo Diallo will serve a two-year term as vice-president of the PRA-UEMOA-Guinée, a body overseeing media regulation in the region.

Ibrahim Manzo Diallo, president of Niger's National Communication Observatory, was elected vice-president of the Platform of Audiovisual Regulators of UEMOA Member States and Guinea (PRA-UEMOA-Guinée). The election occurred during the platform's 12th General Assembly in Conakry, held from August 31 to September 1, 2026. Diallo will serve alongside the newly elected president, Boubacar Yacine Diallo of Guinea, for the next two years.

The platform brings together media regulatory bodies from UEMOA countries and Guinea. Discussions during the assembly focused on regulating social media, cybersecurity, and combating content that could harm social cohesion. Participants advocated for harmonizing regulatory frameworks and increasing cooperation with major digital platforms.

Diallo's election takes place as regional regulators grapple with adapting their systems to the evolving media landscape and the rise of digital platforms. The assembly's outcomes signal a regional effort to address these challenges through coordinated regulatory approaches and enhanced collaboration.

Why it mattersNigerien media regulators are affected by the election of Ibrahim Manzo Diallo, who will influence regional approaches to social media and digital platform regulation.

Niger's Cure Salée Festival Dates Shifted to September 25-27

The 59th annual nomadic herders' gathering will now take place in Ingall after careful consideration of organizational challenges.

Niger's Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock has officially set the dates for the 59th Cure Salée festival, the annual gathering of nomadic herders. The event will now be held in Ingall from September 25 to 27, 2026. This decision, communicated via an official correspondence dated September 3, follows a thorough review of organizational constraints, including the mobilization and coordination of various stakeholders.

The ministry stated that the aim is to create optimal conditions for the event's success. Recommendations from the regional authorities of Agadez and Tahoua, favoring the original dates, were carefully examined and considered. The ministry also acknowledged the significant efforts already made by these regions in preparations and took into account potential constraints arising from the date change.

The Cure Salée is a significant event in Niger's pastoral calendar, drawing herders, nomadic communities, and visitors from across the country and the sub-region. It serves as a platform for economic exchange, cultural celebration, and social tension regulation in Sahelian areas. The ministry expressed gratitude for the regional authorities' commitment and support in preparing this edition.

Why it mattersThe date change for the Cure Salée festival affects nomadic herders and communities by potentially altering their travel and participation plans for this significant cultural and economic event.

Minister Oversees Cement Distribution in Niamey

Over twenty thousand tonnes of cement are being mobilized for sale in Niamey under new regulations.

Minister of Commerce and Industry Abdoulaye Seydou visited several cement sales points in Niamey on Thursday, September 3, 2026, to monitor the implementation of new measures for selling cement types 32.5 and 42.5. He emphasized that purchasers must present a sales certificate or deed of transfer. Control teams are tasked with ensuring cement reaches its intended personal use sites, with transport costs ranging from three thousand to seven thousand FCFA per tonne depending on distance.

The operation involves over twenty thousand tonnes of cement mobilized, with more than one hundred trucks deployed on the day of the visit. The Minister stated that between twenty and twenty-five thousand tonnes will be available weekly for Niamey residents. He indicated that the operation might continue if needs persist but expressed hope it would be the final phase as the rainy season concludes, potentially reducing demand.

Security forces and municipal services are supporting the distribution, alongside economic activity control brigades. The Minister urged the public to report any malpractices, as they have done previously. Sales points visited included Francophonie (Chazalè), OPVN of Lazaret (for defense and security forces), Aboubacar Charfo, and various locations in Commune I, including the civil registry and industrial zone.

Why it mattersResidents of Niamey are affected by new regulations and distribution efforts for cement, a key building material.

Dosso candidates compete for Niamey science high school spots

Ten students from the Dosso region, including seven girls, are competing in entrance exams for the prestigious Lycée scientifique de Niamey.

The entrance examination for the Lycée scientifique de Niamey officially began on Friday, September 4, in Dosso. Mamoudou Hassan Hamadou, Deputy Secretary General of the Dosso Region, launched the process at the Saraounia Mangou high school. Ten candidates from the Dosso region, comprising seven girls and three boys, are participating in this competitive examination.

These students, selected from the top performers in their respective schools, aim to gain admission to the scientific high school, an institution known for nurturing Niger's future scientific elite. The first test was in mathematics, followed by French and physics-chemistry over two days. Academic officials, including Mme Boureima Zeinabou, are supervising the examinations.

Hamadou encouraged the candidates to remain calm and confident, emphasizing the importance of careful reading, planning on scratch paper, and adhering to exam rules. He stressed that their performance is crucial for representing the Dosso region and expressed hope to see them all enrolled in the second year at Niamey for the 2026-2027 academic year.

Why it mattersTen students from the Dosso region are competing for a chance to attend the Lycée scientifique de Niamey, an institution that shapes future scientific leaders.
