Niger police dismantle fake medical aid scam

A trader confessed to fabricating distress stories and using his child's photo to solicit funds.

Niger's Judicial Police Directorate announced on November 5, 2026, the dismantling of a scam network that used fake medical aid appeals on social media. The network solicited financial contributions from citizens by exploiting their compassion. The police received a complaint on March 1, 2026, from the General Referral Hospital (HGR) in Niamey about an individual posting alarming messages seeking public financial aid for supposed gravely ill patients.

The investigation identified the account holder and revealed fraudulent activities began in January 2026. Fabricated cases included a 70-year-old woman needing FCFA 10,490,000 for evacuation to Ouagadougou, and an orphan, Abdourahamane, needing FCFA 1,637,000 for a lung infection. The suspect, a merchant with a master's degree in accounting, admitted to creating these stories and altering his own child's photo. He was apprehended with FCFA 548,624 and multiple SIM cards.

The police are urging mobile phone companies to strengthen subscriber identification to prevent fraudulent use of money transfer services. They also advise the public to verify aid appeals with health facilities before sending money.

Why it mattersNigeriens who donated money to fake medical emergencies lost their funds and may be less likely to trust real appeals.

Niger and Mali presidents meet to boost Sahel security pact

Presidents Tiani and Goïta reaffirmed commitment to the Confederation of Sahel States and intensified fight against terrorism.

Niger's President Abdourahamane Tiani and Mali's President Assimi Goïta met in Niamey on Thursday, November 5, 2026, to discuss security challenges within the Confederation of Sahel States (AES). The leaders co-chaired an expanded working session involving delegations from both countries, focusing on counter-terrorism efforts and strengthening the AES.

Niger's Foreign Minister Bakary Yaou Sangaré stated the visit aimed to consolidate existing ties and address shared challenges. He reported that discussions covered advancements in defense, diplomacy, and development, with both presidents expressing satisfaction with the AES Unified Force's successes against terrorism and coordinated diplomatic actions. They also reviewed socio-economic situations and ongoing programs for free movement of people and goods, transport infrastructure, and resource exploitation.

The presidents condemned destabilization attempts by mercenaries and their foreign state sponsors, while acknowledging the resilience of Sahel populations. They reiterated their determination to eradicate terrorism and agreed to continue coordinating diplomatic and economic integration efforts. President Goïta extended an invitation to President Tiani for an official visit to Mali at a later date.

Why it mattersThe presidents of Niger and Mali met to strengthen their security pact and address terrorism, impacting the stability and development of the Sahel region.

Nigerien soldiers sworn in at Dosso training centre

Recruits of the 2025 contingent completed 16 weeks of initial training on November 6, 2026.

New soldiers of the 2025 contingent at the Agali Instruction and Training Centre in Dosso were presented to the national flag on Friday, November 6, 2026. The ceremony, attended by military and civilian officials, marked the culmination of 16 weeks of initial combat training from June 15 to October 15, 2026. The recruits learned fundamental military skills and values like courage, loyalty, and sacrifice.

General Mamane Sani Kiaou, Chief of Staff of the Armed Forces, presided over the event, emphasizing the symbolic importance of the flag in a challenging security context. He paid tribute to fallen soldiers and thanked parents for entrusting their children to the armed forces. The new soldiers are expected to bolster the ranks of the Nigerien Armed Forces engaged in combating terrorism and transnational crime.

The commander of the Dosso training centre, Battalion Chief Harissou Lawali, highlighted the constant attention from military command to training future generations. He stated that the flag represents the history, unity, sovereignty, and honour of the Nigerien people. The 2025 contingent, drawn from across Niger, will serve in various capacities to address national security threats.

Why it mattersYoung Nigeriens are joining the armed forces to defend the nation against terrorism and crime.

Niger and Benghazi authorities seal non-aggression pact

The agreement aims to prevent a proxy conflict, with the UAE and Algeria vying for influence in Fezzan.

Niger and Benghazi authorities have agreed to a non-aggression pact to avoid a proxy conflict. Niger's Prime Minister Ali Mahaman Lamine Zeine landed in Benghazi on Sunday, November 1, 2025, where he met Saddam Haftar, the vice-commander of the Libyan National Army. This meeting followed an impromptu visit that surprised many observers.

The pact seeks to de-escalate tensions in the Fezzan region, where both the United Arab Emirates and Algeria are engaged in a direct struggle for influence. Images from the meeting showed a cordial handshake between the two leaders, signaling a potential shift in regional dynamics.

Niger's President Abdourahamane Tiani had previously awarded Saddam Haftar the Order of National Merit, Niger's highest honor, in Niamey on May 22, 2025. This exchange of high-level diplomatic gestures underscores the complex geopolitical maneuvering in the Sahel.

Why it mattersNiger's Prime Minister met with Libyan military leader Saddam Haftar to establish a non-aggression pact, impacting regional stability and influence by Algeria and the UAE.
