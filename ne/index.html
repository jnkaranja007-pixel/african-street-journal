Niger hostages Eva Gretzmacher and Claudia Abbt freed

The Austrian and Swiss women, kidnapped in Agadez in 2025, arrived in Benghazi after over a year and a half in captivity.

Austrian Eva Gretzmacher and Swiss Claudia Abbt, who were abducted separately in Agadez in 2025, were released and arrived in Benghazi, Libya, on Wednesday, October 21, 2026. Their handover to Austrian and Swiss authorities is scheduled after necessary formalities are completed.

Gretzmacher was taken from her Agadez home on January 11, 2025, and Abbt was kidnapped in the same city in April 2025. Both women were held by the Islamic State in the Sahel for over 18 months. Saddam Haftar, son of Libyan marshal Khalifa Haftar, reportedly played a role in their liberation, though this has not been officially confirmed.

A plane was expected to depart Benghazi on Thursday, October 22, 2026, to take Gretzmacher to Vienna and then continue to Switzerland with Abbt. The exact circumstances of their release remain undisclosed.

Why it mattersEva Gretzmacher and Claudia Abbt were held for over a year and a half in Niger by the Islamic State in the Sahel.

Niger president opens judicial year

President Abdourahamane Tiani presided over the 2026-2027 judicial opening ceremony in Niamey.

Nigerien President Abdourahamane Tiani opened the 2026-2027 judicial year in Niamey on Thursday, October 22, 2026. The ceremony focused on the theme 'What roles should justice play in affirming national sovereignty?'

State Court President Abdou Dan Galadima stated that the judicial opening is a time for assessment and projection, allowing justice to review progress and reaffirm its commitment to the Republic. He emphasized that national sovereignty includes the ability to create and enforce one's own legal order, with justice rendered by local judges according to national laws and values.

Bar Association President Fati Kountché Adji noted that national sovereignty is a legitimate aspiration for controlling destiny and development. She added that sovereignty must be reflected in daily institutional operations, heritage protection, public security, law enforcement, and public trust in the state.

Why it mattersNigeriens will experience how their justice system operates under the theme of national sovereignty.

News · 2026-10-23

two women freed after year held in Niger

Swiss and Austrian nationals were kidnapped separately in Agadez and have now arrived in Libya.

Two European women, a Swiss and an Austrian, have been freed in Libya after being held captive in Niger for over a year. Austrian Eva Gretzmacher, 76, and Swiss Claudia Abbt, 69, were kidnapped separately in Agadez in January and April last year. The circumstances of their release and who held them remain undisclosed.

The Austrian and Swiss governments confirmed the release of their citizens following intensive diplomatic efforts. Gretzmacher had spent many years in Niger as an aid worker, while both women were involved in education and development initiatives. Abbt is reportedly on her way back to Switzerland and receiving medical care.

The kidnappings occurred in Niger's northern Agadez region, an area previously linked to Islamic State militants. The release comes more than a year and a half after the women were abducted. Austrian Foreign Minister Beate Meinl-Reisinger stated that joint efforts with regional, European, and international partners had paid off.

Niger minister calls youth to energy jobs

Colonel Alzouma Seyni urged young Nigeriens to pursue careers in energy research, innovation, and entrepreneurship.

Niger's Minister of Energy, Colonel Alzouma Seyni, called on the nation's youth on October 22, 2026, to engage more in energy sector careers, scientific research, technological innovation, and entrepreneurship.

Speaking during World Energy Day, Seyni highlighted energy's role in economic and social development and national sovereignty. He noted the government's priority on energy sovereignty under President Abdourahamane Tiani and Prime Minister Ali Mahamane Lamine Zeine.

The Ministry of Energy's 2024-2027 action plan aims to strengthen national electricity production, define an energy transition model, and improve energy access. Seyni expressed a desire for a resilient energy sector supporting national development and sovereignty.

Why it mattersNiger's youth are called to invest in the energy sector to bolster national energy independence and economic development.
