Niger hosts CAF mission for 2032 Africa Cup of Nations bid

A CAF expert visited Niamey from November 8-9 to assess Niger's readiness for the 2032 tournament.

Niger hosted a Confederation of African Football (CAF) mission from November 8 to 9, 2026, in Niamey to evaluate the country's bid to co-host the 2032 Africa Cup of Nations with Mali and Burkina Faso. The mission, led by CAF expert Basegeta Avellin, reviewed presentations on Niger's bid dossier and security plans presented by the Nigerien Football Federation (FENIFOOT).

During the visit, the CAF expert inspected hotel infrastructure, including the Radisson Blu, Noom Hotel, Hôtel Gawèye, Grand Hôtel, and Hôtel Bravia, to assess their capacity and amenities. The mission also inspected sports facilities such as the Stade Général Seyni Kountché and the Municipal Stadium, along with the National Technical Centre and the General Reference Hospital. FENIFOOT President Issaka Adamou described the mission's assessment as "satisfactory."

Adamou explained the visit's purpose was to verify the coherence between the bid dossier and the existing infrastructure and plans. He noted that in-person explanations provide more detail than the written bid alone. The mission concluded with a summary of work and an audience with the Minister of Youth, Sports, and Culture.

Why it mattersNiger's sports authorities are seeking to co-host the 2032 Africa Cup of Nations, a major continental sporting event.

Niger president meets Sahel education ministers

Ministers from Burkina Faso and Mali discussed implementing the Alliance of Sahel States' education roadmap.

Niger's President Abdourahamane Tiani met a delegation of education ministers from the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) in Niamey on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. The delegation included Mali's Minister of National Education Amadou Sy Savane and Minister of Entrepreneurship, Employment and Vocational Training Oumou Sall Seck, alongside Burkina Faso's Minister of Basic Education, Literacy and Promotion of Languages, Jacques Sosthene Dingara.

The ministers are in Niamey to implement the AES's second-year roadmap concerning the establishment of a conference of education ministers. Minister Dingara stated they aimed to convey greetings from Burkina Faso's Captain Ibrahim Traoré and Mali's General Assimi Goïta. They also reported to President Tiani on the Niamey conference's progress regarding education within the AES, which comprises Niger, Burkina Faso, and Mali.

The Niamey conference aims to foster patriotism, civic responsibility, and education rooted in cultural realities for the youth of the AES member states. The ministers plan to develop cooperation and resource-sharing programs to create a unified educational space for the confederation's youth to drive development. They expect to adopt foundational documents for the conference on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, following instructions from the heads of state.

Why it mattersThe education ministers' meeting in Niamey aims to shape a new generation of youth across Niger, Burkina Faso, and Mali through culturally relevant education and cooperative programs.

Niger trains energy planners with IAEA help

A two-week workshop in Niamey aims to improve national energy demand forecasting.

Niger's High Authority for Atomic Energy (HANEA) is hosting a national training workshop on the Energy Demand Analysis Model (MAED) in Niamey from November 9 to November 20, 2026. The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) is supporting the event, which aims to equip participants with skills to anticipate future energy needs. The training is part of the NER 2007 technical cooperation project.

The workshop's opening ceremony was led by Mr. Sani Maty, Secretary General of the Ministry of Energy, who highlighted the MAED tool's significance for national energy planning. International IAEA expert Mr. Soufiane Hachad is providing expertise for the training, which includes technical presentations and practical exercises using national data. This initiative builds on previous cooperation between Niger and the IAEA, following a training session on the MESSAGE model.

This training seeks to enhance national expertise and guide strategic decisions for electricity planning and supply. HANE A is committed to continuing its collaboration with the IAEA to develop national capabilities for effective energy planning, supporting Niger's energy sovereignty and sustainable development.

Why it mattersNiger's energy planners will gain skills to forecast future energy needs, aiding national development.

Business · 2026-11-11

Niger energy minister inspects Goroubanda power site

Colonel Seyni Alzouma reviewed working conditions and energy production facilities at the key site.

Niger's Minister of Energy, Colonel Seyni Alzouma, visited the Goroubanda site on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. The visit aimed to assess working conditions at the strategic energy production hub.

Colonel Alzouma stated the visit aligns with the ministry's approach and the country's highest authorities' directives to ensure energy installations function well. He met with Nigelec officials and technical teams to identify improvements for electricity supply to the population, noting Goroubanda's importance in national production and reducing foreign energy dependence.

The Goroubanda site, which supplies the river region, includes thermal, solar photovoltaic, and a 40-megawatt Niger-Algeria Friendship power plant inaugurated in June 2026. The minister pledged to address difficulties faced by the teams working under demanding conditions, including projects like the World Bank's BEST1 battery storage system and the West African Power Pool project.

Why it mattersThe minister's visit to Goroubanda aims to improve electricity supply for Niger's populations and reduce foreign energy dependence.

Politics · 2026-11-11

Niger accuses Chad of drone strike

Niamey claims a Chadian drone strike on October 24 killed two civilians and injured others.

Niger's government accused Chad of launching a drone strike on its territory on October 24, which it claims killed two civilians and injured several others. The incident occurred around 1 AM and also caused damage to property. This accusation led to a diplomatic escalation between the two neighboring countries.

The Nigerien statement, released on October 26, contained strong threats and warnings against Chad, indicating a significant deterioration in relations. Niger's leader, Abdourahamane Tiani, is described as unpredictable, with this action being initiated by him. The drone strike reportedly targeted an area within Niger.

Despite attempts by Niamey and N’Djamena to de-escalate tensions following the diplomatic clash, security and geopolitical disagreements between the leaders of Niger and Chad make a swift return to peaceful relations unlikely. The specific nature of these security and geopolitical divergences remains a barrier to reconciliation.

Why it mattersThe diplomatic dispute between Niger and Chad over a drone strike impacts regional stability and security cooperation.
