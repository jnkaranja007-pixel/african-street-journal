Niger justice minister launches legal document training

Minister Alio Daouda initiated a campaign to improve the creation of legal execution documents by judicial staff.

Niger's Minister of Justice, Alio Daouda, officially launched a campaign in Niamey on October 23, 2026, to train judicial personnel on the proper procedures for creating and issuing legal execution documents. This initiative stems from findings during a February 16 to July 10, 2026, field mission which revealed that judicial services were hindered by the inconsistent or absent establishment of these critical documents.

The minister explained that the campaign addresses a lack of knowledge regarding best practices, which have been neglected. Execution documents, defined as certified extracts from court minutes signed by clerks, are essential for enforcing final criminal decisions, including imprisonment and the collection of fines. They also provide a record of an individual's criminal history through judicial records.

The Director General of Legal Affairs and Seals, Elhj Basshir Mai Moussa, further clarified that these documents are vital for initiating the recovery of penalties and fines, and for applying sanctions. The campaign aims to ensure the reliable maintenance of judicial records and to streamline the payment of fines to the state.

Why it mattersNigeriens affected by judicial decisions will experience more effective enforcement of sentences and fines.

Niger launches campaign against female cancers

Around 395 cases of female cancers were diagnosed in Niger in 2025, prompting a new awareness drive.

Niger launched its "Octobre Rose" campaign to raise awareness about female cancers, with approximately 395 cases diagnosed in 2025. The initiative aims to encourage women to undergo screenings for breast and cervical cancers.

Dr. Ramatoulaye Diop Cissé, Director General of the Afoua clinic, explained that a special space is available for patients and doctors to discuss concerns openly, away from clinical pressure. The clinic has also organized a three-day screening caravan in Harkanassou and an art exhibition on October 24th.

For six years, free screenings for precancerous cervical lesions and breast cancer have been offered, alongside training on self-examination. The campaign utilizes various channels, including media support and involvement from artists and different social groups, to spread its message.

Why it mattersThe campaign aims to increase awareness and screening for breast and cervical cancers among women in Niger.

Algeria's parliament to vote on Niger troop deployment

Algerian lawmakers will convene on Sunday, October 25, to consider sending troops to Niger.

Algerian lawmakers are being urgently summoned to parliament on Sunday, October 25, to potentially approve the deployment of troops to Niger. This move follows a recent military cooperation agreement signed between the two nations on October 21, aimed at strengthening security ties.

The Algerian Minister of Defense met with his Nigerien counterpart in Algiers on October 21, signing a military protocol to enhance cooperation. This agreement comes two months after a mutiny in Niger, with Algiers providing military support to the current Niamey government led by Abdourahamane Tiani.

The Algerian constitution requires parliamentary approval for troop deployments abroad. The specific agenda for the October 25 session has not been officially stated, but sources suggest it concerns validating the dispatch of soldiers to Niger.

Why it mattersAlgeria's parliament will vote on sending troops to Niger, directly involving Niger's military and government.

two European women freed in Libya after Niger kidnapping

Austrian and Swiss nationals were held for over a year and a half before their release.

An Austrian and a Swiss woman kidnapped in Niger more than a year and a half ago have been freed and are in Libya, Bern and Vienna announced Thursday. 69-year-old Swiss national Claudia Abbt and 76-year-old Eva Gretzmacher arrived in the eastern city of Benghazi where they received medical treatment. Their release follows an intervention by Libyan military commander Khalifa Haftar. No group has publicly claimed responsibility for the abductions.

Both women were living in Agadez in northern Niger when they were captured and had been involved in education and development initiatives. They were met in Benghazi by teams from their respective countries. The Swiss government told the BBC that the freed national was on her way back to Switzerland. Gretzmacher was taken on 11 January 2025, while Abbt was captured in April last year.

Many experts and Nigerien media outlets have suggested that the Islamic State in the Sahel jihadist group, or its affiliated groups, could be responsible. Looking tired, they appeared in a broadcast by an eastern-based television channel Al-Masar thanking Haftar and his son, Saddam, for securing their freedom.

Why it mattersTwo European women were kidnapped in Niger and later freed in Libya.

Niger energy ministry hosts students for open day

The Ministry of Energy organized an event in Niamey on October 22, 2026, for students to learn about energy sovereignty.

Niger's Ministry of Energy held an open day in Niamey on October 22, 2026, for students and pupils to discuss energy sovereignty. The event, themed "Energy Sovereignty: Challenges, Prospects, and the Contribution of Nigerian Youth," was part of World Energy Day celebrations. Students from the Scientific College and High School, the Military Prytanée, the School of Mines, Industry and Geology (EMIG), and the Abdou Moumouni University of Niamey attended.

Minister of Energy Colonel Seyni Alzouma praised the youth's engagement and thanked ministry staff for the event's success. He announced that the open day would be repeated in future years due to student interest. "We consider you ambassadors of energy from now on," Colonel Alzouma told the attendees, emphasizing the ministry's goal to provide reliable, low-cost electricity to the population. He highlighted the importance of renewable energy sources, which are cheaper than thermal options like coal, gasoil, or fuel oil.

During the open day, participants attended presentations on the energy sector, the ministry's structure, and the roles of various agencies. These included the National Agency for the Control of Interior Electrical Installations (CONTRELEC), the Nigerien Agency for the Promotion of Rural Electrification (ANPER), the National Solar Energy Agency (ANERSOL), the Nigerien Coal Company (SONICHAR), and the National Company for Mineral Coal Carbonization (SNCC-SA).

Why it mattersStudents and pupils in Niamey learned about energy sector challenges, prospects, and renewable energy options.
