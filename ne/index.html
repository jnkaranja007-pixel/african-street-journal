Niger minister visits AGRHYMET centre

Colonel Bylaly Elhadj Gambobo discussed agricultural and pastoral challenges with centre officials.

Niger's Minister of Agriculture and Livestock, Colonel Bylaly Elhadj Gambobo, visited the AGRHYMET centre in Niamey on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. The visit aimed to explore the institution's activities and discuss agricultural and pastoral issues with its leaders.

During his visit, Colonel Bylaly Elhadj Gambobo learned about AGRHYMET's training, research, and climate monitoring systems. These tools help anticipate climate risks, support producers, and improve food security. Discussions also covered strengthening collaboration between the ministry and AGRHYMET, focusing on data sharing, capacity building, and agricultural policy support.

Colonel Bylaly Elhadj Gambobo praised AGRHYMET's expertise, emphasizing the need to connect scientific knowledge with the practical needs of farmers and herders. AGRHYMET, established in 1974, is a specialized institution of the Permanent Interstate Committee for Drought Control in the Sahel, with its headquarters in Niamey, Niger.

Why it mattersNigerien farmers and herders will benefit from improved agricultural policies and climate risk anticipation through enhanced collaboration between the Ministry of Agriculture and the AGRHYMET centre.

Niger PM signs cooperation deal with Haftar camp

Prime Minister Ali Mahamane Lamine Zeine visited Benghazi on November 2, 2026, to sign an accord with the Haftar camp.

Niger's Prime Minister Ali Mahamane Lamine Zeine visited Benghazi, Libya, on Monday, November 2, 2026. He signed a memorandum of understanding to boost cooperation with the Haftar camp, which controls eastern Libya. Niamey also maintains ties with the government in Tripoli.

The agreement was signed during Zeine's visit to Benghazi, where he met General Saddam Haftar. The stated aim was to strengthen bilateral relations and expand cooperation across various sectors. This development follows Algeria's recent announcement of sending troops to Niger.

The accord aims to enhance security cooperation between Niger and the Haftar-controlled region of Libya.

Niger releases first oil data yearbook

The Ministry of Petroleum compiled statistics from 2011 to 2024 covering exploration, production, and economic contributions.

Niger's Ministry of Petroleum has published its first statistical yearbook, compiling oil sector data from 2011 to 2024. The document synthesizes information on exploration, production, transport, refining, marketing, economic contributions, and future development prospects. This initiative aims to make oil a driver of inclusive growth and sustainable development for Niger's citizens.

The yearbook highlights the government's efforts to ensure the oil sector relies on solid and reliable information for strategic decisions. It serves as a reference tool for public authorities, technical and financial partners, investors, academics, and the public. The ministry stated the document consolidates, structures, and values all sector data to promote good governance and transparency.

This publication supports the government's commitment to transparency and results-based planning in managing oil resources. The ministry emphasized the need for solid and reliable information to guide oil policy, the minister's mission letter, local content strategy, and resource management strategy amid energy and geopolitical challenges.

Why it mattersNigeriens can access a comprehensive overview of the nation's oil sector, aiding transparency and informed decision-making.

Niger road accidents kill 1,245 in 2025

Human behaviour caused 99.24% of crashes, with speeding and priority violations leading the toll.

Road accidents in Niger claimed 1,245 lives in 2025, a 12.56% increase from the previous year, according to Dr. Abou Mountari, Director General of the National Road Safety Agency (ANISER). While the total number of accidents decreased by 11.26% to 7,116, the number of fatalities rose, and serious injuries increased by 3.9% to 4,439.

Dr. Mountari stated that 99.24% of these accidents were attributed to human behaviour, including speeding (40.76%), failure to yield priority (26.03%), and carelessness (18.28%). Other factors like road conditions, vehicle state, and environmental issues contributed minimally. ANISER conducts awareness campaigns and joint controls with security forces to promote safe driving practices and enforce traffic laws.

ANISER is working towards global road safety indicators outlined in the WHO's 2021-2030 action plan, focusing on safe infrastructure, vehicles, users, multimodal transport, and victim care. The agency, established by law, operates as a public administrative body with financial autonomy under the Ministry of Transport and Civil Aviation.

Why it mattersNigerians faced a higher risk of death on the roads in 2025 due to a rise in fatal accidents, despite a drop in overall crash numbers.

Politics · 2026-11-04

Algerian troops arrive in northern Niger

60 tankers carrying fuel crossed the border and reached Agadez with soldiers.

A convoy of about 60 Algerian tankers carrying fuel, escorted by soldiers, arrived in northern Niger this weekend. The troops crossed the border a week after Algerian lawmakers approved their deployment to the neighboring country. Nigerien armed forces welcomed the convoy.

The convoy reached the northern Nigerien city of Agadez on Saturday, according to reports from French radio station RFI. An Algerian military unit is also expected in Niamey. The Algerian parliament agreed to send troops on October 25 to assist Niger in its fight against armed groups along their shared border.

Algeria's deployment aims to protect its energy and economic interests, including the under-construction Trans-Saharan Gas Pipeline. This is Algeria's first military deployment abroad since 1973. The move occurs as the United Arab Emirates and Libya expand their influence in the Sahel region.

Why it mattersAlgerian troops and fuel tankers have entered Niger to assist in fighting armed groups and protect energy interests.
