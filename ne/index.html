World Bank pledges $700 million more for Niger as Kandadji project restarts

The World Bank plans to add about $700 million in public funding to its existing Niger portfolio of more than $4 billion over the next five years.

Niger's President, General Abdourahamane Tiani, met Ousmane Diagana, the World Bank's vice-president for West and Central Africa, in Niamey on Friday, 13 November, at the head of a large delegation. They discussed the Kandadji programme, a new partnership framework between Niger and the bank, agriculture and energy.

Diagana acknowledged that implementation of the Kandadji project had been delayed for various reasons. A roundtable with all partners, held on 11 November under the prime minister's leadership, produced agreements that Diagana said should allow the project to restart quickly. He said the new five-year framework, launched by Niger's finance minister, aims to create more and better jobs, especially for young people and women, and is paired with a World Bank programme giving young people access to credit to start small and medium enterprises.

Diagana said the talks also focused on electricity, agriculture and water as the three priority sectors for accelerating development. He described Niger's progress on electricity under an initiative called Mission 300 as significant.

Why it mattersYoung people and women in Niger are the stated target of new World Bank credit for starting small and medium enterprises.

Niger hospital gets 86 million FCFA solar equipment

The Poudrière Regional Hospital in Niamey received solar infrastructure funded by Istithmar West Africa to ensure reliable power for critical services.

The Minister of Energy, Colonel Seyni Alzouma, handed over solar infrastructure valued at approximately 86 million FCFA to the Minister of Health, Col. Major Garba Hakimi, on Friday, November 12, 2026. The equipment, financed by Istithmar West Africa, is intended for the Poudrière Regional Hospital in Niamey.

Colonel Alzouma stated that the solar installation demonstrates the commitment of the energy and health ministries to use power for life and well-being. He emphasized that reliable electricity is fundamental for the continuity and safety of care in hospitals, especially in maternity and operating rooms, enabling the functioning of medical equipment, lighting, and the preservation of medicines.

The Minister of Energy explained that the permanent availability of energy is crucial for the Poudrière Regional Hospital, which serves hundreds of patients daily, particularly in gynecology and pediatrics. He added that securing the power supply directly contributes to protecting the lives of mothers and newborns, aligning with the government's vision to make energy access a key driver for national sovereignty and public service modernization.

Niger's APME-DIRGA presents pastoralist support project in Niamey

The association gathered state services, development partners, customary authorities and herders to review the draft plan before it is finalised.

The Association for the Promotion and Modernisation of Livestock Farming (APME-DIRGA) held a restitution and validation workshop on Saturday at the general Seyni Kountché stadium meeting room in Niamey. The project aims to strengthen the economic capacity of pastoral households, improve their access to services and markets, and build their resilience to climate and economic shocks.

The association's president, Ahmed Bello, said no development project can succeed without a participatory, transparent and inclusive approach. Insa Hassan, director in charge of monitoring NGOs and development associations at the Ministry of the Interior, said the process would bring public transparency, coordination among actors in the pastoral sector and local ownership of the results.

The meeting runs from November 14 to 15 and lets the project team present its preparatory work, diagnostics and strategic priorities. Participants are asked for observations and recommendations before the document is completed.

Why it mattersHerders and local authorities now get to shape the project's priorities before the final document is set.

Security · 2026-11-15

Niger's mobilisation chief asks soldiers' wives to review national defence plan

General Mamane Souley, head of the General Mobilisation Commission, presented the plan to the wives of Niger's Defence and Security Forces in Niamey on Saturday.

General Mamane Souley, president of the General Mobilisation Commission (COMOG), chaired a meeting on Saturday 14 November 2026 at the Niamey Congress Palace with the wives of Niger's Defence and Security Forces (FDS). The meeting explained the principles and objectives of the general mobilisation for the defence of the homeland.

Souley said the President of the Republic had made mobilisation a strategic option because of persistent hostility from the country's enemies. He said the federation of FDS wives was the structure best placed to support fighters, and he asked them to examine the commission's work and point out where it should be revised so that soldiers feel the nation behind them.

The meeting was attended by the coordinator of the FDS wives, the president of the federation, members of the commission and the Jeunes sentinelles du Sahel. Souley thanked the coordinator and the president of the FDS elders for their support of the mobilisation.

Why it mattersThe wives' feedback is expected to shape how the commission revises its mobilisation plan before it is presented as a defence option.

Culture · 2026-11-15

Women from Zinder's Quranic schools hold prayer sessions for peace

Hundreds of women gathered at Zinder's André Salifou University in a reading of the Quran, held at the request of the First Lady.

Women of the Union of Quranic Schools of Niger, Zinder section, held several sessions of Quran reading on Friday in the amphitheatre of André Salifou University in Zinder. The gatherings were aimed at asking God to restore peace and security in Niger.

The meeting was organised at the request of the First Lady, Hadjia Sabira, with support from Elhadj Mohamed Souley, known as Dan-petit, a special adviser to the President. Governor Colonel Massalatchi Mahaman Sani attended, praised the turnout and passed on greetings from the President and the First Lady.

The governor asked attendees to pray together for the repose of the soul of the President's father. Souley asked for prayers to intensify for peace and security across Niger and the confederal space.

Why it mattersThe prayers are a response to the president's call for religious gatherings to pray for the country against internal and external threats.
