Niger records 7,116 road accidents in 2025, with 1,245 killed

Transport Minister Colonel Major Abdourahamane Amadou announced the figures ahead of the 15th African Road Safety Day, and the government has declared 2026 the year of road.

Niger recorded 7,116 road accidents in 2025, which killed 1,245 people and seriously injured 4,439, according to Transport Minister Colonel Major Abdourahamane Amadou. He gave the figures in a message on Saturday, November 14, the eve of the 15th African Road Safety Day.

The minister said the country's road toll remains very worrying and named excessive speed, fatigue at the wheel, alcohol and drug use, overloaded vehicles, phone use while driving and not wearing seatbelts or helmets as the main causes. Pedestrians, cyclists, motorcyclists and users of micromobility vehicles account for 53% of global road victims, he noted, citing the World Health Organization.

The authorities have declared 2026 the year of road discipline, with targets to cut road deaths by 30%, cut accidents by 50% and reach 100% use of approved helmets nationwide. Awareness campaigns, mass education, stronger road controls and a reform of the road safety agency, ANISER, are the stated priorities.

Why it mattersMotorcyclists and pedestrians, who make up most road victims, now face checks and enforcement, and the approved-helmet target applies across the whole country.

Niger's security forces kill five mercenaries and seize weapons in first half of November

The Centre Intégré de Coordination des Opérations says the operations ran from 1 to 12 November and recovered a cache of rocket launchers in the east.

Niger's Centre Intégré de Coordination des Opérations (CICO) said the country's defence and security forces neutralised five mercenaries between 1 and 12 November, and seized several items of military equipment. One mercenary lookout was killed on a hill in the Samira sector during a patrol carried out under Operation Niya on 3 November, and three were killed in total in the Samira area.

The CICO said a further mercenary was killed in the Almahaou operations zone near Inatès, where a Motorola radio was recovered. In the south, a mercenary camp holding motorcycle spare parts and other equipment was burned, and a mercenary was killed at the weekly market in Télémcès under Operation Shara, where a search of his phone turned up images of alleged terrorist leaders and armed men.

The bulletin also reported that in the east, under Operation Naléwa Dolé near Kablewa, forces dismantled an enemy arms cache on Thursday 12 November, seizing 12 RPG-7 rocket launchers, six rockets and 16 charges. On 1 November in Bosso, two former Boko Haram fighters surrendered to the security forces and handed over an AK-47 rifle and about 50 rounds of ammunition, and the military authorities called on remaining armed group members to follow them.

Why it mattersResidents of the Diffa and Bosso areas are directly affected by the surrender call and the continued operations in the east, which the military says remain under control.

Niger's National Guard seizes rocket launchers meant for Boko Haram

Troops found 12 RPG-7 launchers and 11 rockets hidden in a bush near Bonigrat, in the Diffa region near Lake Chad.

Niger's National Guard seized a cache of weapons and ammunition in the Kablewa sector of N'Guigmi department, in Diffa region, on November 12, 2026. Patrol troops found the cache, which included 12 RPG-7 rocket launchers and 11 rockets, hidden in a bush about one kilometre south-east of Bonigrat. The find was made during the operation known as "Nalewa Dole".

Colonel Mahamadou Daye, commander of the National Guard's regional command in Diffa, said troops followed vehicle tracks to the hiding place after gathering intelligence on the ground. He said security services believed the weapons were linked to members of the Mohamid community and were meant to resupply Boko Haram fighters. Diffa governor General Mahamadou Ibrahim Bagadoma attended the presentation of the weapons to regional authorities on November 15, 2026.

The weapons were displayed at the ceremony as evidence, and the commander said the find showed the value of intelligence work against the networks that move arms to armed groups around the Lake Chad basin.

Why it mattersThe seizure cuts off weapons that were headed for Boko Haram fighters in the Lake Chad basin, which the National Guard says makes the area safer for residents and traders.

Niger launches Russian language training for youth

Hundreds of young Nigeriens will study Russian for one year to access new educational and professional opportunities.

Niger officially launched a Russian language training program in Niamey on Saturday, November 14, 2026. The initiative, organized by the International Russophile Movement in Niger (MIR-Niger) with the Russian House and La Main dans la Main NGO, aims to boost youth skills. The program will provide access to Russian universities, scholarships, and employment opportunities.

Beidou Souley, representing Niger's Minister of Professional and Technical Education, highlighted the language as a key to scientific, technological, and cultural knowledge. He noted that students could pursue studies in cities like Moscow and Saint Petersburg and collaborate with Russian companies operating in Niger. The course spans one year, with six hours of instruction weekly: four in person and two online.

Upon completion, learners will gain language proficiency and familiarity with Russian culture, history, and values. The Russian Ambassador to Niger, Viktor Voropaev, praised MIR-Niger president Dr. Mohamed Alfazazi Issoufou Ballé for promoting friendship and cultural ties. He expressed optimism that the youth's interest in Russian education will broaden their personal and professional horizons.

Why it mattersHundreds of young Nigeriens will study Russian for one year, gaining language skills and cultural knowledge to pursue education and employment opportunities in Russia and with Russian companies in Niger.

Sport · 2026-11-16

Abba Ibrahim wins Niamey centennial Kokowa competition

The national champion defeated his older brother in the final match of the traditional wrestling event.

Lutteur Abba Ibrahim won the Kokowa (traditional wrestling) competition held in Niamey on Sunday, November 15, 2026. The event was part of the city's centennial celebrations. Ibrahim, who was the 2024 national champion, defeated his elder brother Nazifi Ibrahim in the final bout, which lasted over four minutes.

The 48-hour Kokowa competition featured 16 wrestlers selected from Niamey's five districts. Abba Ibrahim received 600,000 CFA francs for his victory, while his brother received 350,000 CFA francs. The Minister of Youth, Sports, and Culture, Sidi Mohamed Almouhmoud, stated that Kokowa was included to give the centennial celebration a special sporting, cultural, and popular dimension.

The Administrator Delegate of Niamey, Colonel Soumana Boubacar Grantché, noted that the city's 100th anniversary is themed 'Niamey, 100 years of history, resilience, and future.' He highlighted that traditional wrestling, as celebrated in this competition, carries collective memory and reflects the continuity of traditions.

Why it mattersAbba Ibrahim received 600,000 CFA francs for winning the traditional wrestling competition.
