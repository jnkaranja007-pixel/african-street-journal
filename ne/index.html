Niger association condemns Chadian drone attack

The 'Kungiyar hadin Kay da cigaba' group criticizes the October 24-25 incident in Emi Lulu and alleges French influence.

The social cohesion association 'Kungiyar hadin Kay da cigaba' condemned a Chadian drone attack in Emi Lulu, Agadez region, on the night of October 24-25, 2026. The group called on Niger's authorities to take appropriate measures against what they termed a betrayal from a friendly nation.

The association described the attack as "abject and perfidious," expressing shock that it came from Chad, previously considered a friend. They criticized current Chadian leaders as "puppets" acting on behalf of France, while praising Niger's authorities for their restraint and strategic vision.

The group highlighted Niger's recent military cooperation agreement with Algeria, including the deployment of Algerian forces, as a measure to secure the region. They noted significant investments in the area, such as the trans-Saharan highway, a gas pipeline, and a planned railway, aimed at restoring Agadez's past importance as a trade hub.

Why it mattersNiger's authorities are called upon to respond to a drone attack and alleged foreign interference impacting regional security and investment.

Niger launches 24th African traditional medicine day

Health Minister Garba Hakimi urged traditional healers to collaborate with health facilities to improve care quality.

Niger's Minister of Health, Colonel-major Garba Hakimi, launched the 24th African Traditional Medicine Day in Niamey on October 27, 2026. The event, themed "Reflection on research and development efforts in traditional medicine for the implementation of universal health coverage in the African Region," aims to promote therapeutic heritage and preserve ancestral knowledge.

Minister Hakimi stressed the importance of traditional medicine as a local resource rooted in socio-cultural realities, passed down through generations. He called for strict adherence to quality standards, safe practices, raw material traceability, and scientific evaluation of therapeutic effects, urging traditional practitioners to work with health structures to enhance sector performance and patient safety.

Dr. Nikiema Jean-Baptiste, World Health Organization representative, noted the celebration commemorates a decision to promote traditional medicine within health systems. He highlighted the WHO's four priorities for traditional medicine's contribution to health: evidence-based research, knowledge documentation, regulation, and collaboration between traditional and conventional practitioners.

Why it mattersNiger's population, particularly those relying on traditional healers, will benefit from improved quality and safety of traditional medicine practices.

Nigerians honored for local resource development

World Food Programme and FAO celebrate rural men and women as builders of food sovereignty.

In Niamey on October 28, 2026, the World Food Programme (WFP) and the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), with Niger's Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock, recognized rural men and women for their work in developing local resources. An exhibition titled 'Faces of Resilience in Niger' showcased these individuals, attended by government members and guests.

Minister of Agriculture and Livestock, Col. Bilaly El Hadj Gambobo, stated these individuals are not just people to be helped but are farmers, herders, processors, and entrepreneurs who possess generational knowledge, innovate with limited means, and create income. He emphasized their role as the primary builders of Niger's food sovereignty and the strength of its people.

WFP Representative in Niger, Guy Adoua, added that resilience in Niger is a daily lived experience, seen in farmers restoring degraded land and mothers working for a better future. He highlighted that these individuals are the primary actors of their own resilience, not merely beneficiaries of interventions.

Why it mattersRural men and women in Niger are being recognized for their active role in building the country's food sovereignty and resilience.

Algeria to send troops to Niger

Algerian parliament approved the deployment of troops to Niger on October 25.

Algeria will deploy troops to Niger, marking the first time since the 1973 "Kippour war" that Algerian soldiers will operate outside their national borders.

The Algerian parliament approved the troop deployment project on October 25. This decision follows assurances regarding the security of the Trans-Saharan gas pipeline.

Hasni Abidi, director of the Center for Studies and Research on the Arab and Mediterranean World, discussed the shift in Algerian military doctrine and the potential establishment of an Algerian military base in Niger.

Why it mattersAlgerian troops will be stationed in Niger to secure the Trans-Saharan gas pipeline.

Algeria sends troops to Niger to protect energy interests

President Tebboune announced the deployment after parliament approved intervention on October 25.

Algeria will send military units to Niger to protect its energy interests and enact a military cooperation agreement. President Abdelmadjid Tebboune announced the deployment following parliamentary approval on October 25.

The move aims to secure Algerian economic interests south of the border and support the Niamey regime. This follows the signing of a military and security cooperation accord on October 21.

The deployment comes after both chambers of the Algerian Parliament voted to authorize the army's intervention in Niger.

Why it mattersAlgerian troops are being sent to Niger to safeguard the country's energy interests.
