Niger frees journalist Serge Mathurin Adou after two years in prison

Adou, a Nigerien-Ivorian journalist, was released on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, after being convicted of endangering state security.

Niger released journalist Serge Mathurin Adou on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, after two years in prison. He had been convicted of endangering state security, a charge linked to accusations made from neighbouring Burkina Faso.

Burkina Faso, Niger's Sahelian ally, had claimed after a terrorist attack there that Adou was involved in a destabilisation plot coordinated by former president Paul-Henri Sandaogo Damiba, with help from Burkinabe soldiers and ministers. Adou, a Nigerien-Ivorian journalist, had been sentenced to two years in prison in November 2025 in the case.

Jeune Afrique reported the release on November 11, 2026, the same day it took place.

Niamey traders close Grand Marché shops over Socogem rent rise

Some shopkeepers at Niamey's Grand Marché stayed shut in recent days to protest a sharp rise in shop rents set by Socogem.

Traders at the Grand Marché in Niamey, Niger, are protesting a sharp rise in the rent on their shops. The increase was decided by Socogem, the company that builds and manages the markets. In protest, some traders did not open their shops in recent days.

The traders say the rent increase lands on businesses already struggling in a difficult economic situation. Niger's Minister of Commerce met both sides early in the week to try to defuse the dispute.

The packet does not report any agreed outcome from that meeting, or any revised rent figures.

Why it mattersTraders renting shops at the Grand Marché in Niamey face higher rent payments to Socogem, the company that manages the market.

Sahel education ministers adopt founding texts

Ministers from three Sahel countries met in Niamey to boost educational cooperation.

Education ministers from the Confederation of Sahel States (AES) adopted foundational documents and recommendations for educational cooperation in Niamey on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. The meeting established a framework for harmonizing curricula, promoting national languages, and improving teacher training.

The first ministerial session, presided over by Burkina Faso's Minister of Basic Education Jacques Sosthène Dingara, adopted the conference's framework document and internal regulations. Dingara stated that education is an essential lever for sovereignty and economic transformation, calling for swift implementation of the decisions.

The adopted documents outline the vision, missions, and priorities for cooperation, including developing technical and vocational training. A technical follow-up committee will be established, and the next ordinary session will be held in Burkina Faso.

CAF mission inspects Niamey venues for Niger's 2032 Cup of Nations bid

Confederation of African Football expert Basegeta Avellin toured hotels, stadiums and a hospital in Niamey on November 8 and 9, 2026, to check the joint bid with Mali and Burkina.

The Confederation of African Football (CAF) sent a mission to Niamey on November 8 and 9, 2026, to assess Niger's bid to co-host the 2032 Africa Cup of Nations with Mali and Burkina Faso. The expert, Basegeta Avellin, was briefed on the bid file and the security plan presented by the Nigerien Football Federation (FENIFOOT).

The delegation visited the Radisson Blu, Noom, Gawèye, Grand and Bravia hotels to check their capacity, then inspected the General Seyni Kountché Stadium, the Municipal Stadium and the National Technical Centre. It also toured the General Reference Hospital before a closing synthesis and a meeting with the Minister of Youth, Sports and Culture.

CAF's expert made no statement at the end of the visits. FENIFOOT president Issaka Adamou called the outcome "satisfactory" and said the aim was to check whether the infrastructure on the ground matched what the bid file describes.

Why it mattersThe visits test whether the hotels, stadiums and hospital named in the bid can actually host the tournament.

Niger journalists attend China information seminar

20 Nigerien officials and journalists are in Beijing for a three-week seminar on Chinese governance and media cooperation.

20 Nigerien information officials and journalists are attending a three-week seminar in Beijing, China, focused on Chinese governance and media cooperation. The event, organized by China's Ministry of Commerce, includes intensive sessions on topics such as China's international communication strategies and Sino-African economic ties. This gathering aims to strengthen friendship and promote cooperation between China and developing nations like Niger.

The seminar's third day featured discussions on China's governance model, its path to modernization, and mutual inspiration between China and Africa. Former Chinese Ambassador to Gabon, Sun Jiwen, presented on China's unification in 221 BC under Emperor Qin Shihuang, highlighting his role in standardizing Chinese characters and initiating the Great Wall's construction. Professor Zhao Yongshen from UIBE Beijing also spoke on Chinese modernization and Sino-African exchanges.

The program includes presentations on the theoretical framework of Sino-African exchanges and case studies from Africa, examining the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation (FOCAC) from 2000 to 2027. The seminar seeks to foster development in human resources, economy, and society within participating countries. Three journalists from the Agence Nigérienne de Presse (ANP) are among the attendees.

Why it mattersNigerien information officials and journalists are participating in a seminar in China to enhance their skills and foster international cooperation.
