Niger Digital Day trains 140 youth in digital skills in Tahoua

The Niger Digital Day initiative launched a training program for 140 young people in Tahoua, focusing on artificial intelligence, web development, and cybersecurity.

The Niger Digital Day (NDD) national tour began a training program for 140 young people in Tahoua on Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, 2026. The training, themed “AI and Digital Skills for Youth,” aims to equip participants with skills in artificial intelligence, web development, cybersecurity, content creation, and digital finance.

Colonel-Major Souleymane Amadou Moussa, Governor of the Tahoua Region, presided over the launch ceremony at the ETC Guidan Fasaha. He praised the non-profit organization Niger Digital for its initiative, stating it aligns with the priorities of Nigerien authorities to train youth for future professions and foster their autonomy.

The Governor emphasized that the training offers a valuable opportunity for young people to create their own activities, generate income, and build their future. He also expressed satisfaction with the inclusion of young girls and women, highlighting their importance for regional development.

Why it mattersOne hundred forty young people in Tahoua are directly affected by gaining new digital skills, which could lead to employment and entrepreneurship opportunities.

President Tiani urges AES parliamentarians to unite for confederation challenges

General Abdourahamane Tiani met with leaders of the Sahel States Alliance (AES) parliamentary bodies, emphasizing the need for cohesion to address the confederation's goals.

General Abdourahamane Tiani, President of Niger, called on parliamentarians from the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) to remain united during a meeting in Niamey on August 25, 2026. Dr. Ousmane Bougouma, President of the Legislative Assembly of Faso, led the delegation, which included the President of Mali's National Transition Council, General Malick Diaw, and the President of Niger's Consultative Council for Refoundation, Dr. Mamoudou Harouna Djingarey.

The meeting focused on reviewing the progress of the AES Parliament, which became operational with the installation of confederal deputies and the adoption of its internal regulations. Three working commissions have been established: Defense and Security, Diplomacy, and Development. Dr. Bougouma stated that the delegation thanked President Tiani for enabling the completion of the AES's architecture.

President Tiani reminded the parliamentarians of the context surrounding the AES's creation, urging them to remain pragmatic and work towards addressing the concerns of their populations. He stressed the importance of fraternity within the AES to build a space of security, development, and well-being for its people, according to Dr. Bougouma.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects citizens of the AES member states by shaping the legislative and developmental direction of their confederation.

Culture · 08/26/2026

Niamey Muslims celebrate Maouloud at Grand Mosque

The Muslim community in Niamey observed Maouloud, commemorating the birth of Prophet Muhammad, with prayers and recitations at the city's Grand Mosque.

The Muslim community in Niamey celebrated Maouloud on Tuesday, August 25, 2026, marking the birth of Prophet Muhammad. The ceremony at the Grand Mosque of Niamey gathered Muslim faithful for prayers, Quranic recitations, and sermons focused on the Prophet's life and teachings.

Members of the National Council for the Safeguard of the Homeland (CNSP), the Governor of the Niamey region, religious authorities, and numerous faithful attended the event. Cheikh Djibrilla Soumaïla Karanta, president of the Islamic Association of Niger (AIN), spoke about the Prophet's values, including truth, generosity, humility, mercy, and justice.

Karanta emphasized the importance of peace, security, and fraternity in Islamic teachings, urging Nigeriens to uphold national unity, peace, and stability. He also called on citizens to cultivate patriotism, respect diversity, and remain vigilant against threats to Niger's security and cohesion.

Why it mattersThe Muslim community in Niamey is affected by this event as it brings them together for a significant religious observance and reinforces calls for national unity and peace.

Alhousseini Chefou promotes Nigerien culture with tea ceremony at AES sessions

Alhousseini Chefou, a consultant to the Consultative Council for Refoundation (CCR), is serving tea to delegates at the confederal sessions of the AES Parliaments in Niamey.

Alhousseini Chefou, known as the "grand master of tea," is actively engaging delegates at the confederal sessions of the AES Parliaments, which are taking place from August twenty-fourth to August twenty-ninth, 2026. He has been present at various locations, including Diori Hamani International Airport and the Mahatma Gandhi International Conference Center, offering tea prepared with traditional ceremony and conviviality.

Chefou, who also serves as CEO of Nomade Crafts and Culture, has previously represented Nigerien culture at international events such as COP27 in Sharm el-Sheikh, the African Union Summit in Niamey, and SITHO-VITHRO 2023 in Ouagadougou. His tea stand, adorned with Nigerien heritage, serves as a showcase for the country's way of life.

He views tea as a symbol of love and respect, recalling instances where representatives from conflicting countries shared tea and shook hands under his tent. Chefou's efforts aim to foster exchange and fraternity among the peoples of the AES through this cultural tradition.

Why it mattersDelegates from Burkina Faso and Mali, along with other attendees at the AES Parliament sessions, are directly affected by Alhousseini Chefou's tea service, which aims to foster fraternity and cultural exchange.

Health · 08/26/2026

MSF opens pediatric unit in Dan Issa to relieve Madarounfa district

Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) established a seasonal pediatric unit, 'Unité Palu,' at the Dan Issa Integrated Health Center to treat severe malaria in children aged one to 59.

The new 'Unité Palu' in Dan Issa aims to alleviate overcrowding at the Madarounfa District Hospital's pediatric ward and provide closer access to adequate care for local patients. This seasonal hospitalization unit operates during the peak malaria season, typically from August to November each year.

The unit, which started in 2009 with 20 beds, has expanded its capacity to 120 beds by 2026. In 2025, it admitted 4,713 urgent patients, achieving a death rate of 2.6%, well below the national standard of up to 10% mortality, according to the Prefect of Madarounfa, Battalion Chief DJIBEROU Mahamadou.

The Prefect noted that MSF fully supports the unit, which employs 13 doctors, 43 nurses, 40 nursing assistants, 11 surface agents, and 5 laboratory technicians. MSF Project Coordinator Joachim Biakenga highlighted that malaria disproportionately affects children under five during the rainy season in Dan Issa.

Why it mattersChildren aged one to 59 months in Dan Issa and surrounding areas are directly affected by improved access to care for severe malaria, potentially reducing mortality rates.
