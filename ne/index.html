Niamey and Konya sign twinning agreement

The mayors of Niger's capital and Turkey's Konya city signed a memorandum of understanding on November 5, 2026.

Niamey and Konya, in Turkey, officially became sister cities on Thursday, November 5, 2026, following the signing of a memorandum of understanding in Konya. Colonel Boubacar Soumana Garanke, mayor of Niamey and president of the Association of Municipalities of Niger, signed the accord with Konya's mayor, Uğur İbrahim Altay.

This agreement aims to strengthen decentralized cooperation between the two municipalities, focusing on urban development, local public service management, and sharing experiences. Mayor Garanke described the partnership as a dream come true, anticipating it will enhance friendship between Nigerien and Turkish peoples. Mayor Altay emphasized using their cities' experiences to benefit residents and promote international cooperation.

The delegation from Niger also visited the Taş Bina Digital Promotion Center in Konya, observing the Turkish city's digital achievements. The partnership intends to foster exchanges of skills and best practices in local governance to improve living conditions for their populations.

Why it mattersThe mayors of Niamey and Konya signed an agreement to strengthen cooperation and share experiences in urban development and public services.

Niger ex-minister Kalla Moutari held for over two months

Kalla Moutari, a former minister and PNDS figure, has not been presented to a judge since his August arrest.

Former minister Kalla Moutari has spent more than two months in the Niamey gendarmerie in Niger. Moutari, a known figure within the PNDS party, was arrested in late August 2026, days after a destabilisation attempt.

Sources indicate that Moutari has not yet been brought before a judge. His detention follows his arrest shortly after a period of attempted destabilisation in the country.

The former minister's continued detention without judicial appearance raises questions about the legal process following his August arrest.

Niger health minister inspects Maradi region facilities

Colonel-major Garba Hakimi visited health centers in Guidan Roumdji and Karambi Saboua on Friday.

Niger's Minister of Public Health and Hygiene, Colonel-major Garba Hakimi, visited the type 2 Integrated Health Centre (CSI) of Karambi Saboua and the District Hospital (HD) of Guidan Roumdji on Friday. The visit aimed to assess the operational conditions, equipment, and patient care at these facilities in the Maradi region.

During his inspection, the minister observed the available equipment and the functioning of both health structures. He acknowledged the efforts of the health personnel and conveyed greetings from President Abdourahamane Tiani and Prime Minister Ali Mahamane Lamine Zeine to the facility managers.

The minister toured various services, including consultation rooms, the operating block, and the emergency unit at the Guidan Roumdji District Hospital. Officials from the visited health centers and the Guidan Roumdji District presented their respective structures to the minister.

Why it mattersNiger's health minister inspected health facilities in the Maradi region to assess their conditions and identify needs.
