Niger fishers seek state help to clear sand and invasive plants from river

The fishers' association says catches have dwindled and fish prices have risen as plants choke breeding areas in the Niger River.

Source: Agence Nigerienne de Presse · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Boubacar Ali, secretary-general of Niger's fishers' association, is calling on the authorities to remove sand and invasive plants from the Niger River, saying the waterway no longer supports fishers as it once did. He said catches have become so scarce that fishers sometimes bring in only two or three fish.

Ali told Agence Nigerienne de Presse that sand has covered rocks where fish found food, while water hyacinth and Tifa obstruct navigation and threaten fish breeding and breathing. He said the resulting fish shortage has also made the product more expensive in markets nationwide.

Ali said existing projects do not sufficiently involve fishers, who he argues understand the river's problems. He proposed removing and treating the plants, which he said could be turned into material used by market gardeners. Fishing and aquaculture support more than 50 000 people directly or indirectly in Niger.

Why it mattersFishers who rely on the river are reporting catches of only two or three fish at times, while fish prices have risen nationwide.
