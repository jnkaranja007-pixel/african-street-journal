Niger doctors urge hiring graduates to ease health-worker shortage

Dr Tankari says hundreds of doctors graduate each year, but many are not recruited as trained staff leave for the West.

Source: Agence Nigerienne de Presse · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Niger’s doctors’ association president, Dr Tankari Abdoulaye, urged the state to recruit more medical graduates, saying the country’s health system lacks staff. He made the appeal in an interview with Agence Nigerienne de Presse during the Global Summit in Dakhla, Morocco, held from 20 to 21 November.

Tankari said the state trains doctors each year at three universities, with hundreds graduating, but many are not hired. He said some doctors leave for the West, while others turn to entrepreneurship. He called for mass recruitment and said the association works with the Health Ministry on national health policy.

Tankari also said health sovereignty requires trained workers and investment from both the state and private sector. He described the Dakhla summit as an opportunity for African countries to share experience and work with Morocco.

Why it mattersNewly trained doctors and patients face a staffing shortfall as graduates go unrecruited and some doctors leave for the West.
