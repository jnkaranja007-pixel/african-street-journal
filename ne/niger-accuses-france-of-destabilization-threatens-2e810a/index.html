Niger Accuses France of Destabilization, Threatens International Justice

The Nigerien government claims to have gathered substantial documentation on a recent destabilization attempt and reserves the right to pursue legal action internationally.

Niger's Council of Ministers has accused France of orchestrating a recent destabilization attempt at Niamey's Base 101, citing a "vast network of complicity" led by the French regime. The government stated that "renegade elements" were tasked with fulfilling President Macron's "unfulfilled designs" in the country. This action is seen as part of a broader effort to undermine Niger's sovereignty and control over its natural resources, with the government also denouncing certain African nations for facilitating these destabilizing actions.

The Council of Ministers announced that significant documentation has been compiled regarding the aggression, and Niger reserves the right to bring actions before international courts. The government expressed gratitude to friendly nations, including AES member states, Algeria, and Russia, for their solidarity. They also called for increased prayers against the nation's enemies and imperialist powers, urging citizens to remain vigilant.

The government extended condolences to the families of civilian and military martyrs and wished a speedy recovery to the injured. They acknowledged the mobilization of social forces and the patriotic spirit supporting the defense and security forces. While the French government has not publicly responded, several named African countries have denied involvement in the events of August 28-29, 2026.

Why it mattersThe Nigerien government is affected by a destabilization attempt it attributes to France, potentially leading to international legal action and impacting national sovereignty.
