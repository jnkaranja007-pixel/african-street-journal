Diaspora group urges Sifuna to fund JKIA expansion without foreign investors

At a New Jersey meeting, a Kenyan in Ohio said overseas Kenyans could pool money and partner with the government to modernise the airport.

Source: Capital FM · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Kenyans living in the United States have urged Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna to seek diaspora funding for the expansion of Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA), rather than bring in foreign investors. The proposal was presented on Saturday at a meeting with Sifuna and other Linda Mwananchi supporters in New Jersey.

Kiemo Mohando, a Kenyan based in Columbus, Ohio, said Kenyans abroad could combine contributions and work with the government on the airport. He pointed to billions of shillings sent to Kenya annually and called for a coordinated funding arrangement, including ways for diaspora members to contribute directly to development institutions. No funding target or commitment was announced.

The proposal follows the government’s 2024 plan to give India’s Adani Airport Holdings a 30-year concession for a project estimated at $1.85 billion. The plan included a second runway and a new passenger terminal; aviation workers and two Kenyan organisations challenged the arrangement.

Why it mattersKenyans abroad who want to fund the airport would need a coordinated contribution mechanism; Mohando cited billions of shillings sent home annually but announced no target.
