High Court summons Mombasa police chief over unexecuted KSh154.4 million debt warrant

Justice Jairus Ngaah ordered OCS Eliud Kimwele to explain why officers have not arrested Finance Executive Evans Oanda under a warrant received in June.

Source: The Standard · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Kenya’s High Court has ordered Eliud Kimwele, the officer in charge of Mombasa Central Police Station, to appear on December 11 and explain why he has not executed an arrest warrant against county Finance Executive Evans Oanda. The warrant concerns an approximately KSh154.4 million debt owed to Kooba Limited under court orders.

Justice Jairus Ngaah issued the summons after Kooba director Richard Bell asked the court to make Inspector-General of Police Douglas Kanja take over enforcement. Kooba says officers received the warrant on June 30, after it was issued on June 26, but have not acted. The judge directed Kimwele to appear personally, rather than submit a report.

The debt stems from a 2022 judgment awarding Kooba KSh151 million over the county’s handling of approvals for a proposed data centre. Interest and costs have since raised the amount to approximately KSh154.4 million. The county’s lawyer asked for 14 days to respond to Kooba’s application.

Why it mattersKooba remains without approximately KSh154.4 million under the judgment, while the police station chief must explain the delay in court by December 11.
