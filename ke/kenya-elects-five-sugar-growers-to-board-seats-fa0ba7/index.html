Kenya elects five sugar growers to board seats

The representatives were chosen by 108,372 registered growers, and will serve three-year terms after the agriculture secretary gazettes their names.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Five sugar farmers were elected to represent growers on the Kenya Sugar Board in nationwide votes across the country’s five sugar-growing regions on November 24. Each region elected one representative, and 108,372 registered growers voted, a turnout of 43.9%.

The Elections Committee said it had sent the successful candidates’ names to the Cabinet Secretary for Agriculture and Livestock Development for gazettement. Once gazetted, they will serve as board directors for three years. The board is expected to oversee the Sugar Act 2024 and monitor millers, with a focus on timely farmer payments and sugar production.

Kenya is projected to produce 900,000 metric tonnes of sugar in 2026, against estimated annual demand of 1.0 million to 1.2 million tonnes, leaving a projected deficit of about 100,000 to 300,000 tonnes.

Why it mattersThe five elected growers will serve three-year terms on the board responsible for monitoring millers and timely payments to farmers, once their names are gazetted.
