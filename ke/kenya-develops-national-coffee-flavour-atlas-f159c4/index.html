Kenya develops national coffee flavour atlas

Around 500 samples from 34 counties will be analysed to document diverse coffee profiles for industry use.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-10-31 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Kenya is creating a national coffee flavour atlas to document the country's diverse coffee profiles, with findings expected in 2027. The Kenya Coffee Flavor Map initiative will analyse approximately 500 samples from 34 coffee-growing counties, bringing together producers, co-operatives, researchers, and sensory professionals. Professional cuppers will evaluate coffee samples blind, linking tasting notes to geographic and production data to create regional flavour profiles and a searchable sensory database.

The initiative aims to connect coffee flavour characteristics with regional origins, varieties, and production practices, according to organisers. Marty Pollack, co-founder of Torch Coffee, stated the project would encourage collaboration between Kenyan and international coffee professionals. The project is supported by the Government of Kenya, the Coffee Board of Kenya, and the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) in Kenya.

The Kenya Coffee Flavor & Origin Expo will be held in Nairobi from November 14 to 16, featuring professional cupping sessions, exhibitions, and networking for producers and international buyers. Organisers expect the atlas to provide a shared reference for understanding regional differences and communicating coffee characteristics. The number of coffee shops in Kenya increased from 231 in 2019 to 791 in 2023.

Why it mattersKenyan coffee producers and exporters will gain a shared reference for understanding and communicating the unique characteristics of their coffees.
