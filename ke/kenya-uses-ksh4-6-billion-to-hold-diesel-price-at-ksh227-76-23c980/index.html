Kenya uses KSh4.6 billion to hold diesel price at KSh227.76

The fuel regulator kept Nairobi’s diesel ceiling unchanged through 14 December after import costs rose 6.7% in October.

Source: Farmers Review Africa · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Kenya’s Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) kept the maximum diesel price in Nairobi at KSh227.76 a litre through 14 December, using KSh4.6 billion from the Petroleum Development Levy Fund. The support blocked a KSh10.54-per-litre increase even as global diesel prices fell.

EPRA data show the landed cost of diesel, meaning the cost of bringing it into Kenya, rose to USD (US dollars) 1,137.09 per cubic metre in October from USD 1,065.38 in September, while the world price fell 6.1%. The regulator’s pricing formula averages cargoes unloaded at Mombasa through the 9th of each month, so cargoes bought earlier at higher prices may still count. The figures do not separate the effects of cargo timing, shipping and insurance.

The fund collects about KSh2.4 billion a month from a levy of about KSh5.40 on each litre of petrol and diesel. Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi said on 3 November that October’s funds were held back because energy demand was expected to rise as Europe entered winter. The International Energy Agency said on 13 November that global oil supply was recovering, but diesel could remain in short supply into 2027.

Why it mattersLorry operators and matatu commuters avoid the blocked KSh10.54-per-litre increase, while the fund’s KSh4.6 billion payment exceeds its reported monthly collections of about KSh2.4 billion.
