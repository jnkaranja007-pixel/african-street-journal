Kenya plans tougher laws against illicit alcohol cartels

The government will introduce stricter legislation and boost enforcement to dismantle criminal networks involved in the production and sale of harmful alcoholic drinks.

Source: Capital FM · 2026-10-22 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Deputy President Kithure Kindiki has declared illicit alcohol a significant threat to Kenya’s future, vowing to implement tougher laws and intensify enforcement against production and sale networks. The government has elevated the fight against illicit brews and drug abuse, with a multi-agency response coordinated by the Deputy President's office. Kindiki stated that the alcohol menace could undermine national progress and emphasized a commitment to successful intervention, distinguishing the crackdown from targeting legitimate businesses.

The campaign involves agencies such as the National Authority for the Campaign Against Alcohol and Drug Abuse (NACADA), the Anti-Counterfeit Authority, the Kenya Bureau of Standards (KEBS), and the Anti-Narcotics Unit. A permanent secretariat has been established at Harambee House Annex to coordinate ongoing enforcement and inter-agency collaboration. Law enforcement agencies are directed to support NACADA's operations, with the government expecting improved results from the intensified crackdown.

Recent coordinated efforts over 10 days led to nine successful raids on premises selling illicit ethanol and 116 raids targeting sellers of illicit brews nationwide. The government is also pursuing amendments to the legal framework governing the manufacture, distribution, and sale of alcoholic drinks to close loopholes exploited by criminal networks and impose tougher penalties.

Why it mattersKenyans, particularly young people, are at risk of severe health consequences and societal disruption from the proliferation of dangerous illicit alcohol.
