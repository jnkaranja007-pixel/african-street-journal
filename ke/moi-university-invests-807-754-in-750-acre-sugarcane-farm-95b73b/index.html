Moi University invests $807,754 in 750-acre sugarcane farm

West Kenya Sugar will supply seed cane and production support, then buy the harvested crop for milling under the university’s partnership.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Moi University has invested KSh105 million (US$807,754) in a sugarcane farm at its main campus in Kesses, Uasin Gishu County. The university has established 750 acres and plans to expand the plantation to 1,500 acres, seeking additional income for academic programmes and operations.

Farm manager Tobias Okeno estimates that establishing the farm costs about KSh70,000 per acre (US$538.50). The university has planted eight fast-growing varieties for research into which suit the North Rift region. West Kenya Sugar Company is supplying seed cane, technical expertise and agronomic guidance, and will purchase the harvested cane for milling.

Moi University expects to finish harvesting the first 750 acres by June 2027. It is targeting an average yield of about 40 tonnes per acre, with output dependent on rainfall, soil fertility, crop management and variety performance.

Why it mattersWest Kenya Sugar is the planned buyer for cane from the 750-acre first phase, which the university expects to harvest by June 2027.
