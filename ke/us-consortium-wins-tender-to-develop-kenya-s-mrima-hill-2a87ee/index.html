US consortium wins tender to develop Kenya's Mrima Hill minerals

Frontier Minerals beat seven bidders for a site estimated to hold more than $60 billion in critical minerals, including niobium.

Sources: BBC Africa, Business Daily Africa and Africanews · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Frontier Minerals, a US-led consortium, won the tender to develop Kenya’s Mrima Hill mining project after beating seven bidders, including companies from China and Australia. The site in Kwale County covers about 390 acres of forest.

The deposit is estimated to contain critical minerals worth more than $60 billion, including niobium, which is used in electric vehicles, aerospace and defence technology. Frontier Minerals brings together Bunker Hill Mining Corp, American Resources Corporation and ReElement Technologies Corporation. Brad Barnett of the consortium said its proposal includes processing and refining minerals in Kenya so the country captures more value.

The project comes as Washington seeks greater access to African minerals and aims to strengthen US supply chains while competing with China. A separate preliminary estimate cited by Business Daily Africa put the site’s rare earth minerals at more than KSh6 trillion.

Why it mattersKenya's share of the Mrima Hill mineral value depends on Frontier Minerals carrying out the proposed processing and refining in-country.
