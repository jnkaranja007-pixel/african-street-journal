Heifer assessment targets year-round feed supply for Kenya’s dairy farmers

The study finds costly, seasonal feed and uneven services constrain productivity, while Kenya produces 5.2 billion to 5.76 billion litres of milk a year.

Source: Farmers Review Africa · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Heifer International Kenya’s Dairy Value Chain Gap Analysis identified feed supply, farm services and market efficiency as constraints on the country’s dairy sector. Findings were presented at a media roundtable in Nairobi on November 19, as Kenya pursues higher productivity under its National Agri-food Systems Investment Plan for 2026–2030.

Across 11 counties, the study held 54 engagements, with detailed assessments in Nyandarua, Embu and Elgeyo Marakwet. Smallholders in Nyandarua get three to 15 litres per cow each day, against 25 to 30 litres on intensive farms. Farmers face high feed costs, seasonal shortages, inconsistent quality and limited mechanisation and conservation services, and the assessment calls for businesses able to supply quality feed throughout the year.

Kenya produces 5.2 billion to 5.76 billion litres of milk annually, against domestic demand of 12.76 billion litres. The investment plan estimates an annual feed gap of 30 million tonnes, based on 2021 figures. Ainabkoi Dairy Cooperative Society chairman Luke Kesei said unpredictable milk prices make it difficult for farmers to plan investments.

Why it mattersDairy farmers face seasonal feed shortages and unpredictable milk prices, while the assessment identifies year-round quality supply as a priority.
