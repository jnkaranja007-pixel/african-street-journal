Kenya's savings co-operatives face money-laundering gaps as grey-list exit effort conti...

Financial sector experts say weak monitoring, member checks and reporting systems leave the societies exposed to abuse and short of compliance capacity.

Source: Capital FM · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Kenyan savings and credit co-operative societies (SACCOs), member-owned lenders, face gaps in transaction monitoring, member verification and reporting of suspicious activity as the country seeks removal from the Financial Action Task Force (FATF), the intergovernmental body that sets anti-money-laundering standards, from its grey list. Financial sector experts raised the concerns at a Nairobi forum on SACCO compliance and digital payments organised by Innovative Software Technologies Limited.

Zachariah Obari, compliance director at ABC Bank, said many SACCOs still need the technology and trained staff to spot unusual transactions and report them to authorities. He called for capacity building, led by the Sacco Societies Regulatory Authority (SASRA), the state regulator, working with SACCO leaders, and said written policies alone were not enough to meet anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorism financing obligations.

The forum brought SACCO representatives and financial-sector participants together to discuss regulatory duties and technology-based responses. Obari said failure to improve safeguards could undermine Kenya's campaign to address deficiencies identified by international financial-crime watchdogs.

Why it mattersSACCO leaders and the regulator must build staff and technology capacity to monitor transactions and report suspicious activity as Kenya pursues grey-list removal.
