Education · 2026-11-16

Mombasa teacher assault not linked to KCSE, says CS

Education CS Julius Ogamba stated the attack on examination supervisor Julius Ndurya was due to personal issues, not exam malpractice.

Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba stated that the assault of a teacher in Mombasa was not connected to the ongoing Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) examinations. Ogamba said the incident was triggered by personal issues and that the teacher was not near any examination centre when attacked. He announced an investigation into why the assault was linked to the examinations.

The incident involved Julius Ndurya, a Kenya National Examinations Council (KNEC) supervisor at Vigurungani Senior School in Kwale County. Ndurya claimed he was abducted and assaulted on November 11, sustaining head injuries and a fractured hand after rejecting a KSh50,000 bribe and confiscating unauthorized examination papers. Police confirmed Ndurya reported the assault and are investigating.

The 2026 KCSE examinations are in their final week, with several attempted cheating cases reported. The Ministry of Education has tightened measures to curb examination malpractice. Ndurya alleged his attackers abandoned him believing he was dead.

Why it mattersJulius Ndurya, a KCSE supervisor, was allegedly assaulted and abandoned after rejecting a bribe and confiscating exam papers.

Security · 2026-11-16

Kenya probes looting of refugee offices in Dadaab

Government offices were vandalized and property looted following protests by Somali refugees.

The Kenyan government is investigating the destruction and looting of Department of Refugee Services offices in Hagadera, Dadaab, which occurred on November 14, 2026. A group of refugee youth allegedly carried out the acts, prompting an appeal for calm from acting Commissioner for Refugee Affairs Mercy Mwasaru. The incident disrupted infrastructure serving both refugees and the host community.

Mwasaru warned against retaliatory attacks and urged refugee and host communities to exercise restraint, stating that the incident threatened peaceful coexistence. She called on community leaders to discourage retaliation and reject inflammatory information, emphasizing that those responsible would be held accountable under Kenyan law. Security agencies are working to recover stolen property and identify those involved.

The Department of Refugee Services is seeking information from the public to aid the investigation, with reports being accepted confidentially by the National Police Service or DRS officials. The government stressed that maintaining peace in Hagadera is a shared responsibility for all residents.

Why it mattersThe government has launched an investigation into the destruction and looting of refugee offices in Dadaab, with security agencies pursuing those responsible.

Business · 2026-11-16

Fire contained at Mavoko garment factory

Property worth millions of shillings was destroyed in a late Sunday night blaze along the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway.

Late on Sunday night, a fire swept through a garment factory in Mavoko, beside the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway, and destroyed property worth millions of shillings. Firefighters, police and volunteers from the Kenya Red Cross Society joined the response, with teams from Mavoko and Machakos County, Nairobi City County, and the National Youth Service (NYS) all taking part.

The Kenya Red Cross said its Machakos Action Team was among the responders and that the blaze had been brought under control. "The fire has been contained, and no injuries were reported," the organisation said in an update. The exact extent of the losses has yet to be established, though the fire destroyed a significant section of the factory.

An assessment of the damage is due to be carried out on Monday to establish the full extent of the losses.

Why it mattersOwners of the garment factory face losses worth millions of shillings, and the full scale of the damage will only be known once Monday's assessment is complete.
