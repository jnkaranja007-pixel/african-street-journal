Two more charged in Sh1.569 billion PROFIT fraud case

Eric Otieno Achila and Susan Wangari Gathiaka appeared before the Anti-Corruption Court in Nairobi, bringing the total number of accused to 13.

Two additional suspects, Eric Otieno Achila and Susan Wangari Gathiaka, have been charged in connection with an alleged Sh1.569 billion fraud involving the Programme for Rural Outreach of Financial Innovations and Technologies (PROFIT). The proprietors of Dangzerina Enterprises, Maxtor Supplies, and Graybar Distributors, respectively, denied the charges before Chief Magistrate Harrison Barasa Owima.

The charges stem from investigations by the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) into the alleged fraudulent disbursement of Sh1,569,582,338.20 from the National Treasury Development Account to the PROFIT Programme. The EACC claims funds were channeled to 23 private entities for unsupplied goods and services, and that an unauthorized Kenya Commercial Bank account was used to launder and embezzle Sh175.3 million.

Achila faces three counts and Wangari four counts. Both were granted a Sh1.5 million bond with a surety of a similar amount, or cash bail of Sh650,000 for Achila and Sh700,000 for Wangari. The prosecution, led by Assistant Director of Public Prosecutions Susan Keli, did not oppose their release but requested conditions including surrendering passports and refraining from witness interference. The case will be mentioned on September 2, 2026.

Why it mattersThe alleged fraud directly impacts the financial integrity of a government program and the individuals involved face legal consequences.

Police to Recommend Violent Political Actors to IEBC for Action

The National Police Service will submit names of individuals promoting political violence to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission for consideration ahead of the.

The National Police Service will recommend individuals deemed unfit to contest elective positions due to their involvement in political violence to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC), according to Deputy Inspector General Gilbert Masengeli. This action is part of intensified efforts by security agencies to prevent politically instigated violence ahead of the 2027 General Election.

Masengeli stated that police would investigate, arrest, and prevent crime related to political violence, dealing firmly with "goons" and their sponsors. The police will submit names of political actors with track records of promoting or perpetrating violence to the electoral commission for consideration, aiming to disqualify them from running for office.

This announcement follows recent violence at a Linda Mwananchi political rally in Homa Bay County on August 16, where 37 suspected gang members were arrested with crude weapons. The incident also resulted in the death of a police driver and injuries to eight officers, highlighting renewed concerns over the use of organized groups in political activities as parties mobilize for the upcoming elections.

Why it mattersPolitical actors and the public are affected as the police aim to curb violence and ensure a peaceful electoral process, potentially impacting who can run for office and the safety of political gatherings.

Experts urge East Africa to commercialize bioeconomy research for profit

Policymakers, scientists, investors, and entrepreneurs at the Africa Bioeconomy Workshop in Nairobi called for linking agricultural production, research, and innovation to markets.

Experts at the Africa Bioeconomy Workshop in Nairobi urged East Africa to transition from bioeconomy strategies and research to building profitable industries. The call emphasizes retaining more value from the region’s biological resources and creating jobs, moving beyond exporting raw materials.

Niall O’Connor, Centre Director of the Stockholm Environment Institute (SEI) Africa Centre, stated that the region possesses the necessary resources, research capacity, and entrepreneurial talent for a competitive bio-based economy. The 2026 State of the Bioeconomy in Eastern Africa report identifies agricultural residues and organic waste as potential feedstocks for various industries, including biofuels and organic fertilizers.

Dr. Abdou Tenkouano, Director General of the International Centre of Insect Physiology and Ecology (icipe), highlighted agriculture's significant contribution to GDP in many sub-Saharan African countries, ranging from 23 to 35 percent. He stressed the opportunity to build industries around biomass and urged governments to strengthen university-industry partnerships to support innovation in agro-processing and bioprocessing.

Why it mattersThis shift affects East African communities by creating local jobs and increasing incomes for families through value addition and processing of biological resources.

Nairobi hosts anime convention, draws thousands of fans

More than 4,000 anime and manga enthusiasts gathered in Nairobi for the Otamatsuri convention, showcasing elaborate costumes made from recycled materials.

The Otamatsuri anime and manga convention in Nairobi recently attracted over 4,000 attendees, transforming recycled materials and imagination into detailed cosplay outfits. This event highlights the growing popularity of Japanese pop culture in Kenya, with fans dressing as characters from franchises like One Piece, Naruto, and Gachiakuta.

Jotham Micah, co-founder of entertainment company Movie Jabber and an organizer of the convention, stated that anime and manga are increasingly crossing cultural boundaries. He noted that these subcultures provide inspiration, fun, and entertainment for many participants.

For Kenyan cosplayers, creating these elaborate costumes often involves using affordable and unconventional materials. Julius Mokaya, a 34-year-old building caretaker and two-time costume competition winner, uses cardboard and other items from recycling bins to craft his outfits, as he cannot afford to import expensive costumes.

Why it mattersThe event provides a platform for creative expression and community building for thousands of anime and manga fans in Kenya.

Nairobi designer revives traditional hand looms for unique clothing

Hillary Mukubwa, founder of Mukabwa studios, uses hand looms to create clothes and fabrics, aiming to preserve Kenya's cultural heritage amidst mass production.

In Nairobi, designer Hillary Mukubwa is turning to traditional hand looms to create clothing, departing from modern mass production methods. Mukubwa, founder of Mukabwa studios, states that he feels like an anomaly in returning to old traditions, believing that modernization often diminishes traditional craftsmanship and work practices.

Mukubwa views the use of hand looms as a way to keep Kenya's cultural heritage alive. He explains that hand-loomed items carry a story and are considered pieces of art, producing items with meaning rather than in mass quantities, unlike electric machines.

Clients appreciate the individuality and quality of the clothes, finding them more interesting than high street offerings. Maylone Ginnette, a client, highlighted the unique nature and pure organic cotton quality of the garments, noting the visible effort involved in their creation.

Why it mattersThis story is important to consumers who seek unique, high-quality clothing and to artisans who want to preserve traditional craft methods in the face of industrialization.
