IEBC Demands Evidence From Gachagua on Bribery Claims

The electoral commission challenges the DCP leader to provide proof for allegations of a Sh200 million house purchase and interference in tender processes for the 2027 polls.

The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) has demanded that Democracy for Citizens Party leader Rigathi Gachagua present evidence for his claims of bribery and interference in the commission's affairs. IEBC Chairman Edung Ethekon stated that allegations of a house being purchased for him to influence decisions were unsubstantiated and urged Gachagua to provide sale agreements, transfer documents, and payment receipts to the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission.

Ethekon also dismissed claims of predetermined awards and political interference in the procurement of electoral technology and ballot papers, noting that tender processes for the Integrated Elections Management System and ballot papers are in early stages and temporarily halted by review requests. He asserted that all procurement decisions will adhere to constitutional and legal frameworks, and refuted claims of misconduct among commissioners, emphasizing the commission's independence.

The IEBC chairperson further rejected allegations of voter registration data manipulation or suppression, stating the Register of Voters is under the commission's custody and undergoes multiple verification processes. He urged stakeholders with concerns to use established legal mechanisms rather than making unsubstantiated public accusations, warning that unverified claims could erode public confidence ahead of the 2027 General Election.

Kenya MPs Back Bill to Link Air Passenger Fees to Aviation Safety and Tourism

A National Assembly bill passed by Kenyan lawmakers aims to directly channel revenue from air passenger service charges towards enhancing aviation safety and promoting tourism.

The Kenyan National Assembly has passed a Bill that could see money collected from air passengers more directly fund aviation safety and tourism initiatives. The Air Passenger Service Charge (Amendment) Bill, 2026, sponsored by Majority Leader Kimani Ichung’wah, seeks to alter how the revenue from this charge is managed and allocated, with a specific focus on bolstering aviation safety and tourism development.

This legislative move follows Parliament's amendment of the Air Passenger Service Charge Act less than a year ago, which replaced the Tourism Promotion Fund with the Tourism Fund as a recipient of the levy. The current charges are Sh600 for domestic passengers and US$50 for international travellers. The proposed changes aim to strengthen the connection between the levy paid by travellers and investments in the sectors crucial for air transport and tourism.

Under the proposed framework, a portion of the collected revenue would support entities responsible for aviation safety and measures to improve air transport operations. Additionally, the changes aim to boost support for tourism promotion, a sector reliant on robust air connectivity. The Bill now proceeds to the Senate for consideration before it can become law, potentially offering travellers greater transparency on how their levies are utilized.

Why it mattersAir travellers will be affected as the fees they pay are now intended to directly fund aviation safety and tourism promotion efforts in Kenya.

Ruto Unveils New Police Uniform Amidst Stalled Reforms

President Ruto is set to introduce a new police uniform, drawing criticism for prioritizing appearance over substantive reforms recommended by a taskforce.

President William Ruto will on Friday unveil a new uniform for General Duty officers at the Kiganjo National Police Training College, with the change scheduled to take effect on August 31. Inspector-General Douglas Kanja stated the uniform update aims to boost officer morale and aligns with public input from a 2023 exercise. This move, however, has sparked debate regarding the government's focus on outward changes while significant operational reforms remain unaddressed.

The timing of the uniform's rollout is notable, occurring after the expiry of a seven-day deadline set by former Chief Justice David Maraga for the release of a comprehensive police reform report. Maraga, who chaired the taskforce, submitted its findings on November 16, 2023, detailing issues such as underfunding, corruption, and leadership problems within the National Police Service, Kenya Prisons Service, and National Youth Service.

The Maraga taskforce's mandate was to examine deep-seated legal, policy, and operational challenges, not just uniforms. Its recommendations aimed to improve accountability and institutional strength. Maraga has accused the government of neglecting crucial reforms necessary to restore police independence and combat systemic brutality and impunity, citing partisan policing and political violence as evidence of a deteriorating security environment.

Why it mattersPolice officers are affected by the government's focus on new uniforms instead of implementing reforms to address systemic issues like corruption and brutality.

Kenyan mothers die from childbirth complications amid weak healthcare

Kilifi County recorded 532 maternal deaths per 100,000 live births in 2022, far exceeding the national average of 355.

Penina Zawadi died after giving birth, a tragedy linked to severe bleeding and delayed transport to a referral hospital. Her family reported she bled heavily for four hours at Malindi General Hospital before being moved to Kilifi County Referral Hospital, an hour away on a poor road. Doctors attempted a cesarean section and later a procedure to remove her uterus, but she died in intensive care.

Sub-Saharan Africa faces the world's highest maternal death rates, accounting for 70% of global fatalities, with around 180,000 deaths annually. Causes include severe bleeding, obstructed labor, high blood pressure conditions, and infections from unsafe abortions. Shortages of medical equipment, transport, and information exacerbate the problem, with some local clinics functioning as basic outposts.

Recent funding cuts from donors have worsened the situation for vulnerable populations. The Kilifi County government reported 81 deaths in the last eighteen months, calling it progress, and noted efforts to increase healthcare facilities. However, many clinics remain poorly stocked, and families struggle with funeral expenses, with one community fundraiser yielding only about a dollar per person.

Why it mattersPenina Zawadi's death highlights the critical lack of timely and adequate medical care for pregnant women in Kenya, leading to preventable maternal mortality.

Kenyan Farmers Use Worms to Combat Soil Degradation

Farmers are turning to vermicompost as an alternative to chemical fertilizers to improve soil quality and boost harvests, driven by rising costs and soil health concerns.

Kenyan farmers are adopting vermicomposting, using redworms to process waste into nutrient-rich fertilizer, as a strategy to reverse soil degradation. This shift is partly a response to increased chemical fertilizer prices in 2026, influenced by the war in the Middle East, and a growing demand for organic produce. George Muturi Kamau, a farmer in Lari, reports that his worm farm has helped rescue his land from degradation caused by previous chemical fertilizer use and has significantly boosted his harvests.

A 2025 soil report by the Heinrich Boll Foundation highlighted that over forty percent of East African soils are degraded, with intensive farming and changing climates contributing to the issue. In Kenya specifically, the report classified thirty-two percent of cropland as strongly acidic. The overuse of chemical fertilizers was identified as a significant factor in this degradation, leading to an estimated annual loss of more than sixty-five billion dollars in productivity across the region.

The search for alternatives like vermicompost is gaining traction among farmers seeking sustainable methods to improve soil health and productivity. By converting farm and food waste into a natural fertilizer, farmers can reduce reliance on expensive chemical inputs. This practice not only aims to restore degraded land but also aligns with the increasing market demand for organically grown food products, offering a dual benefit to agricultural producers.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by the rising cost of chemical fertilizers and the need to reverse soil degradation, making organic alternatives like vermicompost a practical solution for improving their land and harvests.
