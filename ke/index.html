Former Attorney General Paul Kihara dies aged 72

The Judiciary has paid tribute to the distinguished jurist for his enduring contribution to Kenya’s legal institutions.

Former Attorney General Paul Kihara Kariuki died on November 3, 2026, at the age of 72. The Judiciary has paid tribute to the retired Court of Appeal President, describing him as a distinguished jurist.

Chief Justice Martha Koome stated that Kihara's career was marked by dedicated public service and judicial leadership. He served as President of the Court of Appeal from 2013 to 2018, championing reforms to improve efficiency and accessibility. Koome also recognised his role as the founding Director of the Judiciary Training Institute.

Kihara served as Attorney General from 2018 to 2022, acting as the Government’s principal legal adviser. Chief Justice Koome said his legacy would live on through the institutions he helped build and the reforms he championed.

Why it mattersThe death of former Attorney General Paul Kihara affects the Kenyan legal fraternity and the government he served.

ADM opens Nairobi office to boost East African trade

The global nutrition and agricultural company aims to connect local demand with its worldwide supply chain network.

ADM, a global leader in nutrition and agricultural supply chain solutions, has opened a new destination marketing office in Nairobi, Kenya. This move strengthens the company's ability to link customers across East Africa to its global supply chain network. Kenya is a leading trade hub for food and feed ingredients and serves as a gateway to the wider East African region.

The Nairobi office will offer customers access to a wide range of food ingredients, feedstuffs, and animal nutrition products. It will focus on market insights, local merchandising, and storage and distribution. Patrick Heijbroek, Vice President of Global Trade, stated that East Africa's population of over 500 million people represents a high-growth market with increasing demand for quality ingredients and supply chain solutions.

This expansion builds on ADM's decade-long presence in Kenya and complements its existing investments in Africa, including human nutrition labs in Nairobi, a distribution hub in Lagos, Nigeria, and flavor creation capabilities in Johannesburg, South Africa. In 2025, ADM's destination marketing operations handled nearly 31 million metric tons.

Why it mattersFarmers and businesses in East Africa will gain better access to ADM's global supply chain for food and feed ingredients.

Kenya restricts Diwali fireworks to four hours

Mining CS Joho mandates permits and limits shell size for celebrations from November 6 to 8.

Mining Cabinet Secretary Hassan Joho has limited fireworks displays during Diwali celebrations to a four-hour window, from 7pm to 11pm, between November 6 and 8. Organisers must obtain permits and adhere to safety regulations, with fireworks shells capped at four inches in urban areas unless an exemption is granted. These directives aim to regulate displays and minimise public disruption during the Hindu festival.

Joho stated that only qualified, experienced, and sober individuals can handle aerial fireworks, and dealers must sell products only to permit holders. Applications for display authorisations can be made at Madini House or county mining offices. Displays are restricted to approved locations, including hotels, clubs, temples, sports grounds, and school compounds cleared by an Inspector of Explosives.

The new rules follow President William Ruto's pledge in October 2024 to consider Diwali as a national holiday, a proposal that requires parliamentary approval. Contravention of Joho's directives risks a fine not exceeding Ksh10,000 or imprisonment for up to two months, or both.

Why it mattersKenyans celebrating Diwali face restrictions on fireworks displays and potential fines for non-compliance.

Kenya sugar farmers to elect board representatives November 24

The Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock Development confirmed the election date and published the final voters' register and candidate list.

Sugarcane farmers in Kenya will elect five representatives to the Kenya Sugar Board on November 24, 2026. The Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock Development confirmed the date and published the final voters’ register and candidate list on October 30. The election will fill five grower representative positions as provided for under Section 6 of the Sugar Act No. 11 of 2024.

Elections Committee Chairperson Harun Khator signed a notice stating the election would follow the applicable legal framework and court orders. The final voters’ register is available on the Board’s website and displayed at designated facilities. Preparations for the election began on May 12, 2026, with an invitation for applications from members of the public.

The High Court had suspended the elections in September pending a constitutional petition challenging disputed sugarcane catchment boundaries. The November 24 vote will determine who represents sugarcane farmers in Board deliberations on industry issues. Growers should consult official committee communications and the published register to confirm their eligibility.

Why it mattersThe November 24 vote will determine who represents sugarcane farmers in Board deliberations on industry issues.
