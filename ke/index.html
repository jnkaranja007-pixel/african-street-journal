Aviation Workers End Strike After Pay and Dues Deal

Normal operations resume at Kenyan airports following an agreement that includes remittance of union dues and new collective bargaining talks.

Aviation workers have returned to their duties after a two-day strike, ending a standoff that disrupted flights at Nairobi's JKIA and Moi International Airport. The Kenya Aviation Workers Union (KAWU) and the Kenya Airports Authority (KAA) reached a return-to-work agreement, which includes the immediate remittance of agency fees owed to KAWU. Kenya Airways reported delays of over six hours and some flight cancellations due to the industrial action.

The agreement paves the way for new negotiations between KAWU and the Kenya Civil Aviation Authority (KCAA) on a Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA), following the Salaries and Remuneration Commission's (SRC) withdrawal of previous guidance. While KAWU has reservations about new SRC parameters issued on September 1, they will address these during the CBA talks. A separate dispute between KAWU and Jambojet regarding union recognition will await a court determination.

The industrial action, which paralyzed airport operations for two days, has now concluded with workers directed to resume normal duties. The agreement includes a non-victimisation clause protecting striking employees from reprisals. The focus now shifts to negotiations for the KAWU-KCAA CBA, with the outcome potentially impacting terms for aviation workers under SRC remuneration guidelines.

Why it mattersAviation workers and employers resolved a dispute over pay and union dues, allowing for the resumption of normal operations and future negotiations.

Nairobi County Clears Tonnes of Garbage From Drains Ahead of Rains

City officials are removing approximately 3,000 tonnes of waste daily to prevent flooding in vulnerable neighborhoods before anticipated El Niño rains.

Nairobi County is intensifying efforts to clear tonnes of garbage from its drainage systems and waterways in anticipation of El Niño rains. The county government reports collecting about 3,000 tonnes of waste daily, a significant increase from the previous 1,000 tonnes, to address blocked drains and illegal dumping sites. This surge in waste removal highlights a critical city-wide waste management challenge, with garbage accumulating rapidly in flood-prone areas.

Specific areas have seen substantial clean-up operations. Embakasi South has had over 4,500 loads of waste removed since April, with Kware Bridge and English Press in Mukuru Kwa Reuben also reporting thousands of loads cleared. Efforts extend along the Ngong River and Nairobi River, targeting waste that could block drainage infrastructure. Over 40 major waste collection points and illegal dumping sites have been addressed since April.

The county has deployed teams across all 17 sub-counties to remove waste, desilt drains, and clear vegetation, focusing on congested markets and residential areas. This clean-up initiative, running from August 31 to September 6, is part of a broader strategy to mitigate flood risks exacerbated by inadequate waste management and settlements near waterways. The county has also recruited 4,000 Green Army workers and is proposing new waste management facilities.

Why it mattersNairobi residents in flood-prone neighborhoods are at risk of increased flooding due to accumulated garbage in drainage systems, which the county is now working to clear.

Man gets 10 years for vandalizing Sh5.1m telecoms equipment

A court convicted Jacob Mutungi Matolo for tampering with and stealing American Tower Corporation equipment valued at over Sh5.1 million in Taita Taveta.

A man has been sentenced to 10 years in prison for vandalizing telecommunications infrastructure worth more than Sh5.1 million in Taita Taveta County. Jacob Mutungi Matolo was convicted by Senior Principal Magistrate Cecilia Kithinji after the prosecution proved his involvement in the vandalism and theft of equipment belonging to American Tower Corporation. The offences, which included tampering with a telecommunications plant and stealing, occurred between April and July 2025 at the Kidongu Communication Tower.

The prosecution presented evidence that Matolo, along with others, forcibly entered the facility and damaged equipment valued at Sh5,118,546, with the intention of theft. Investigations led to the recovery of the vandalized items from Matolo, and officers from American Tower Corporation confirmed the property belonged to the company. Principal Prosecution Counsel Charles Mogaka led the case, which involved four witnesses and physical evidence.

Magistrate Kithinji found sufficient evidence linking Matolo to the crimes, relying on witness testimony and exhibits. Matolo was charged under the Kenya Information and Communication Act and the Penal Code, facing counts of tampering with a telecommunications plant, stealing, and an alternative charge of handling stolen property. He has 14 days to appeal the conviction and sentence.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the vandalism of telecommunications equipment disrupts services and incurs significant financial losses for companies like American Tower Corporation.

Gachagua links IEBC staff transfers to disputed election tender

Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua claims election commission officials were moved after refusing to support a tender favoring a South Korean firm.

Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua has accused the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) of victimizing officials who resisted a disputed tender for election technology. Gachagua alleges that senior staff in Supply Chain Management and Electoral Management departments were transferred to remote counties after refusing to provide what he called false testimony to the Public Procurement Administrative Review Board (PPARB). He stated that these officials were replaced with "cronies" by the IEBC chairman and vice chair.

The controversy surrounds the IEBC's proposed Integrated Elections Management System (IEMS) tender, advertised on August 11 and temporarily halted by the PPARB due to a challenge from a prospective bidder. Gachagua has repeatedly claimed the tender specifications were tailored to favor Miru Systems, a South Korean firm, and that the process was designed for a predetermined outcome. IEBC chairman Erastus Ethekon has denied these allegations, stating the tender has not been awarded and that specifications are generic and performance-based.

Gachagua connected the procurement dispute to a series of staff changes announced by the IEBC in August. A memo dated August 12 detailed transfers and re-designations affecting officials across several departments, including Supply Chain Management. Several officials were moved from headquarters to county offices, with directives to report by August 17. IEBC chairman Ethekon urged parties with concerns to utilize legal mechanisms within Kenyan law.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected as the integrity of election technology procurement is questioned, potentially impacting future electoral processes.
