Husband arrested over wife's shallow grave burial

Detectives in Kajiado County arrested Shon David Rabassa Kioko after a farmhand reported the incident.

Detectives in Isinya, Kajiado County, arrested Shon David Rabassa Kioko on October 30, 2026, for allegedly murdering his 34-year-old wife, Farida Peter Sangka. Her body was found buried in a shallow grave on their farm in Entarakuet.

The arrest followed a report by farmhand Ndayishimiye Deo, a Burundian national, who stated Kioko instructed him to help bury the body. Police officers secured the scene and exhumed the remains in the presence of a pathologist for forensic examination.

The body was moved to Nairobi Funeral Home pending a postmortem examination. Kioko and Deo remain in custody as the Directorate of Criminal Investigations pursues justice for Sangka’s family.

Why it mattersA woman was allegedly murdered and buried on her farm in Kajiado, Kenya.

Kenya launches pesticide residue monitoring framework

New system aims to improve food safety and protect horticultural exports from rejections.

Kenya has launched its National Pesticide Residue Monitoring Framework (NPRMF) to coordinate pesticide testing and data sharing across the agricultural sector. The framework aims to support regulatory oversight, food safety compliance, consumer protection, and access to domestic and export markets. The Pest Control Products Board, the Kenya Plant Health Inspectorate Service, and the Agriculture and Food Authority helped develop it.

Agriculture Secretary James Wanjohi said at the October 15 Nairobi launch that food control is multisectoral, emphasizing working together from farm to fork. The framework matters for exporters because Kenya's horticultural exports have been rejected at European borders for exceeding pesticide residue limits, particularly for beans and peas. A coordinated system provides a single reference for testing data, enabling exporters to demonstrate compliance and regulators to identify problem areas before shipments reach markets.

The framework's development involved over two years of consultations and stakeholder engagement. A pilot phase in five counties from May to June 2025 identified capacity gaps before the national rollout. Continued support from governments and development partners is sought for sustained funding of laboratory equipment, staff training, and sample collection.

Why it mattersKenyan farmers exporting horticultural products will benefit from reduced rejections in foreign markets.

Kenya develops national coffee flavour atlas

The initiative will document diverse coffee profiles from 34 counties, with findings published in 2027.

Kenya's coffee industry is creating a national flavour atlas to document diverse coffee profiles across the country. The Kenya Coffee Flavor Map initiative will analyze approximately 500 samples from 34 coffee-growing counties, with results expected in 2027. This project aims to connect specific flavour characteristics with regional origins, varieties, and production methods.

Producers, co-operatives, researchers, and sensory professionals are collaborating on the initiative, which involves blind sensory evaluations by Kenyan and international coffee cuppers. The findings will be made freely available to the industry and will be a central feature of the Kenya Coffee Flavor & Origin Expo in Nairobi from November 14-16. Professional cupping sessions are scheduled for November 15 and 16.

The planned atlas will include regional flavour profiles, visual maps, and a searchable database to showcase the diversity of Kenyan coffee. Marty Pollack, co-founder of Torch Coffee, stated the project will foster collaboration between Kenyan and international coffee professionals. The initiative is supported by the Government of Kenya, the Coffee Board of Kenya, and the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) in Kenya.

Why it mattersKenyan coffee producers and exporters will gain a shared reference for understanding and communicating the unique characteristics of their coffees.

CIP and Crop Trust develop climate-resilient potatoes in Kenya

The BOLD project will use wild potato relatives to breed varieties resistant to heat, unpredictable rain, pests, and diseases.

The International Potato Center (CIP) and the Crop Trust have launched a new phase of the BOLD project in Kenya to develop climate-resilient potato varieties. This initiative aims to strengthen seed systems and improve farmers’ access to quality planting material.

The project will harness genetic traits from wild potato relatives to help breeders develop varieties capable of withstanding rising temperatures, unpredictable rainfall, pests, and diseases. Dr. Benjamin Kilian, BOLD Project Coordinator at the Crop Trust, stated that wild relatives contain options agriculture needs as conditions change.

Potatoes are Kenya's second most important staple after maize, supporting 3.3 million jobs and contributing US$324 million annually, but production faces pressure from climate change and diseases. The goal is to release at least one improved variety in Kenya combining climate resilience, disease resistance, and high yields.

Why it mattersKenyan farmers will receive new potato varieties that are more resilient to climate change, pests, and diseases, potentially improving yields and income.
