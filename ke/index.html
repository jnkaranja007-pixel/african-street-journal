Kenya and Saudi Arabia agree three-month pilot to review domestic worker recruitment fees

The pilot will test voluntary recruitment fees based on market cost, following a technical annex signed in Riyadh.

Kenya and Saudi Arabia have agreed a three-month pilot programme to review recruitment fees for Kenyan domestic workers seeking jobs in the Kingdom. Labour Cabinet Secretary Alfred Mutua announced the arrangement after meeting Saudi Arabia's Vice Minister for Labour, Dr Abdullah bin Nasser Abuthnain, in Riyadh.

The two countries signed a letter of intent providing for voluntary recruitment fees based on market cost competitiveness. They also signed a technical annex to the bilateral agreement on domestic workers, first signed on July 5, 2026. The annex provides for streamlined procedures, lower recruitment costs and integrated technology, and tightens rules on contracts between employers and workers.

Mutua said the framework is expected to improve recruitment services and ensure the process complies with approved regulations. The two sides also discussed the pay, welfare and working conditions of Kenyans already employed in Saudi Arabia.

Why it mattersKenyans recruited as domestic workers for Saudi Arabia face recruitment fees that the three-month pilot will review, and the annex sets out how contracts between them and employers are regulated.

College principal butchered in bedroom attack

Unknown assailants broke into Gerishom Wafula Manase's home in Kakamega County and killed him with crude weapons.

Gerishom Wafula Manase, the 51-year-old Deputy Principal in charge of Academics at Navakholo Technical and Vocational College, was killed in his bedroom on November 8, 2026. Unknown attackers scaled a fence, broke a window, and entered his home in Lubao, Kakamega County, attacking him with crude weapons. The assailants stole a television set, two laptops, and two mobile phones before escaping.

Kakamega County Police Commander Tom Achiya stated that detectives are pursuing multiple leads, including an inside job, business dispute, love triangle, or robbery. "For sure, the mission was to kill, and that is why they did not bother with other people who were in the house," Achiya said, adding that no stone would be left unturned. Investigators recovered one stolen television set inside a church compound in the neighbouring Malava constituency.

Theories surrounding the motive include marital problems, a love triangle, a land deal gone sour, or work-related disagreements, though police have not yet established a connection. A cousin claimed Manase had recent relationship controversies at his church and strained marriage, noting the family had moved into the new home only six months prior. A close friend also reported Manase seemed unusually anxious and withdrawn in the week before his death.

Why it mattersUnknown assailants killed Gerishom Wafula Manase in his bedroom, stealing electronics and leaving investigators pursuing multiple theories including robbery and personal disputes.

Kenya seeks safeguards for youth seeking jobs in Germany

Nairobi forum highlights need for ethical recruitment and worker protection amid growing labour mobility.

Kenya is pushing for stronger safeguards as its citizens seek employment in Germany, warning against fraudulent recruitment and exploitation. The government stressed the need for ethical recruitment, transparent contracts, and effective worker protection during the Kenya-Germany Skills and Labour Mobility Forum in Nairobi. Officials raised concerns that labour mobility opportunities must not expose job seekers to unfavourable working conditions.

Moses Ivuto, Secretary for Administration for the Principal Secretary for Labour and Skills Development, stated that prospective workers need adequate time to understand employment contracts before signing. He emphasized that recruitment costs, rights, and working conditions must be clearly communicated. Irene Karari, Director of the Skills and Expertise Division, added that government support must extend beyond securing jobs to protecting workers throughout their migration cycle, including their dignity, safety, and welfare.

German Ambassador to Kenya Sebastian Groth cautioned against fraudulent recruitment agencies exploiting young people with false promises. He called for stronger oversight of agencies to prevent job seekers from being defrauded or facing exploitative arrangements. Groth also noted that language proficiency is an important requirement for vocational training and employment in Germany.

Why it mattersKenyans seeking jobs in Germany must be protected from fraudulent recruitment and exploitation, with clear contracts and accessible complaint mechanisms.
