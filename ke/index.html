IEBC challenges Gachagua to prove bribery and tender interference claims

The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) has demanded evidence from Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP) leader Rigathi Gachagua regarding his allegations.

IEBC Chairman Edung Ethekon dismissed claims that politicians influenced the commission's leadership, including allegations that a house was purchased for IEBC Chairperson Erastus Ethekon to sway his decisions. Ethekon urged Gachagua to provide specific evidence, such as the property's location, sale agreements, transfer documents, payment receipts, and details of the seller and purchaser to the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC).

The commission also rejected allegations of predetermined awards and political interference in the procurement of electoral technology and ballot papers. Ethekon stated that the procurement processes for the Integrated Elections Management System (IEMS) and ballot papers are in early stages, with no companies yet awarded tenders. These processes are currently halted due to review requests filed with the Public Procurement Administrative Review Board.

Ethekon maintained that tender documents were publicly uploaded on August 11, 2026, and technical specifications are generic and open to all qualified suppliers. He affirmed that all procurement decisions will adhere to Article 227 of the Constitution and the Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Act, emphasizing the commission's independence and focus on its constitutional mandate without political influence.

Why it mattersCitizens are affected as the integrity of the electoral process and the upcoming 2027 General Election are called into question by these allegations and the IEBC's response.

MPs back passenger charge changes to fund aviation safety and tourism

The National Assembly passed a Bill to channel Air Passenger Service Charge revenue more directly towards aviation safety and tourism promotion.

The National Assembly has approved changes to how revenue from the Air Passenger Service Charge is administered and allocated. The Air Passenger Service Charge (Amendment) Bill, 2026, sponsored by Majority Leader Kimani Ichung’wah, aims to support aviation safety and tourism development. The Bill now moves to the Senate for consideration before it can become law.

The proposed changes seek to strengthen the link between the levy paid by travelers and investment in the sectors that underpin air transport and tourism. Part of the revenue would support institutions responsible for aviation safety and measures to improve the safety and efficiency of air transport operations. This comes as Kenya works to strengthen its aviation infrastructure and regulatory systems.

The legislation also aims to increase support for tourism promotion, a sector heavily dependent on air connectivity. Improved air links are critical for attracting international visitors and facilitating movement within the country. The charge currently stands at Sh600 for domestic routes and US$50 for international journeys, with the Cabinet Secretary able to vary rates.

Why it mattersAir travelers will see the money they pay through the Air Passenger Service Charge directed more specifically to aviation safety and tourism promotion.

Kenya records decline in skilled birth attendance, family planning uptake

New data reveals a drop in deliveries by skilled attendants and fewer women using family planning services, highlighting persistent gaps in maternal and newborn healthcare access.

Kenya has seen a decline in skilled birth attendance and family planning uptake, raising concerns about maternal and newborn healthcare. Deliveries conducted by skilled birth attendants fell from 69.77 per cent between April and June 2025 to 65.47 per cent between January and March 2026. The number of women using family planning services also decreased during the same period, according to data presented to editors in Nairobi.

Polycarp Oyoo, Programs Advisor for Maternal and Newborn Health at the International Centre for Reproductive Health Kenya (ICRHK), stated that these figures should prompt closer scrutiny into why women are not consistently accessing antenatal and delivery services. He noted that about 15 mothers are lost daily, with dozens of newborn deaths and stillbirths also recorded each day. Oyoo urged investigation into potential weaknesses within the health system.

Delays contribute significantly to maternal and newborn deaths, with 30 per cent linked to deciding to seek care, 25 per cent to reaching a facility, and 45 per cent occurring after arrival due to untimely or poor care. Only 37 per cent of facilities providing delivery services meet basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care standards, and critical medicine shortages are reported, including 48 per cent lacking magnesium sulphate, 47 per cent lacking benzyl penicillin, and 40 per cent lacking oxytocin.

Why it mattersThis decline in maternal health services directly affects women and newborns in Kenya, increasing risks during childbirth and impacting family planning choices.

Politics · 2026-08-27

Kalonzo Musyoka Demands Full Constitutional Implementation in Kenya

Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka called for the protection of Kenya's 2010 Constitution, warning against efforts to weaken devolution, democratic institutions, and electoral.

Speaking on the 16th anniversary of the Constitution's promulgation, Kalonzo Musyoka emphasized the supreme law as a shield for citizens and urged vigilance in defending gains since 2010. He highlighted the Constitution's role in transforming governance through devolution, public participation, and stronger protection of fundamental rights.

Musyoka, who served as Vice President when the Constitution was adopted, specifically defended devolution, describing it as a significant gain that brought resources and decision-making closer to citizens. He expressed concern over delays in releasing funds to county governments, arguing that such delays undermine the constitutional promise of devolution and opposed proposals giving the national government greater control over county finances.

Kalonzo also addressed Kenya's electoral system, stating that electoral integrity is critical for stability. He called for an independent, properly funded, and impartial Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) ahead of the 2027 General Election, linking electoral integrity to avoiding a repeat of the 2007 post-election violence that left over 1,000 dead.

Why it mattersKenyans are affected by potential changes to the Constitution, particularly regarding the independence of county governments and the integrity of future elections, which could impact their daily lives and political stability.

Kenyan mothers die from childbirth complications as healthcare falters

Hundreds of mothers in Kenya, including Penina Zawadi, are dying during childbirth due to severe bleeding, obstructed labor, and inadequate medical facilities and transport.

Maternal deaths continue to rise in Kenya, with Kilifi County reporting 532 deaths per 100,000 live births in 2022, significantly higher than the national average of 355. Penina Zawadi died on June 23 after giving birth, following severe bleeding and a delayed transfer from Malindi General Hospital to Kilifi County Referral Hospital, according to her family.

Zawadi's family stated she bled for four hours at Malindi Hospital while awaiting referral to a larger facility an hour away on a potholed road. Her father, Nixon Charo, said they expected a safe birth but circumstances during labor led to her death. Her mother, Esther Bendera, described the extensive bleeding and the doctors' efforts to save her daughter.

The deaths are attributed to shortages of basic medical equipment, inadequate transport for referrals, and insufficient information for pregnant women. While the Kilifi County government reported 81 deaths in the last year and a half as progress, local clinics often lack more than painkillers. Funding cuts from donors have further exacerbated these challenges for vulnerable communities.

Why it mattersThis story directly affects Kenyan mothers and their families, who face a high risk of death during childbirth due to systemic healthcare deficiencies.
