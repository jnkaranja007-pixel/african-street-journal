Kenya Airports Handle 244 Flights as Strike Ends

Aviation operations rapidly returned to normal after workers called off their strike, with major airports facilitating 244 arrivals and departures within nine hours.

Kenya's aviation operations have returned to normal, with major airports handling 244 flights between 3 a.m. and noon on Tuesday, nine hours after aviation workers ended their strike. The Kenya Airports Authority (KAA) confirmed all airports are operating normally, with Air Traffic Control services fully resumed. Jomo Kenyatta International Airport managed 55 flights, Moi International Airport recorded 23, Wilson Airport handled 140, Kisumu International Airport saw 12, and Eldoret International Airport processed 14 flights.

The swift recovery follows a return-to-work agreement between aviation workers and their employers, ending a strike that had threatened to strand passengers and disrupt operations. The agreement includes the remittance of outstanding agency fees owed to the Kenya Aviation Workers Union (KAWU) by KAA, paving the way for new negotiations on a Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA). These negotiations will proceed after the Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC) issued new parameters on September 1, replacing earlier guidance.

KAWU has expressed reservations about the new SRC parameters but agreed to address these concerns through CBA negotiations rather than continued industrial action. The Kenya Civil Aviation Authority (KCAA) has committed to honoring the outcome of these negotiations. The agreement also addresses a separate recognition dispute between KAWU and Jambojet, with parties agreeing to await the court's determination on the matter.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the resumption of flight operations indicates a stabilization of business activity and a return to normal trade and travel conditions.

Ex-Chief Officer Charged Over Sh1.5 Million Earned Using Forged Certificates

A former Machakos County official faces charges for allegedly using fake academic credentials to secure a senior position and earn over Sh1.5 million.

A former Machakos County chief officer has been charged with fraud after allegedly using forged academic certificates to obtain a senior government position. The Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) stated the official earned Sh1,585,967.90 in public funds during his tenure from January to November 2023. He pleaded not guilty to charges including fraudulent acquisition of public property, uttering a false document, and forgery of academic certificates at the Machakos Law Courts.

The EACC investigation revealed the former Chief Officer for Industry and Innovation presented a fraudulent Bachelor of Commerce degree from the University of Nairobi, purportedly issued in 2004, and a fake Certified Public Accountants (CPA) Final Examination Certificate dated June 2019. These documents were used to secure his appointment. The Director of Public Prosecutions approved the charges following the EACC's inquiry.

The accused was granted cash bail of Sh1 million or an alternative bond of Sh2 million with a surety. The EACC plans to initiate separate civil proceedings to recover the salaries and benefits acquired through the alleged fraudulent qualifications. This case is part of an ongoing EACC crackdown on forged academic papers used by public officials to gain employment.

Why it mattersPublic officials using fraudulent documents to gain employment and earn public funds are affected by this prosecution.

NCIC Summons Governor Lenku Over Ethnic Remarks

Kajiado Governor Joseph Ole Lenku faces arrest if he fails to appear before the National Cohesion and Integration Commission on September 17.

The National Cohesion and Integration Commission (NCIC) has summoned Kajiado Governor Joseph Ole Lenku to appear on September 17, 2026, regarding remarks made on August 22. The commission is investigating allegations that Lenku advocated for Maasai candidates to hold political positions in Kajiado and Narok, warning parties against nominating non-Maasai individuals for what he described as Maasai-reserved seats. Failure to appear could result in an arrest warrant and potential criminal or contempt proceedings.

The investigation centers on comments where Lenku reportedly stated, "Any political party that fields a non-Maasai candidate for a position meant for the Maasai in Kajiado, Narok or elsewhere, we will have no relationship with you." He also cited nominations for women’s assembly seats, arguing that only Maasai candidates should be nominated for positions he considered reserved for the community. The NCIC is examining these remarks under provisions of the National Cohesion and Integration Act relating to hate speech and negative ethnicity.

The NCIC, whose mandate includes promoting national unity and guarding against negative ethnicity and hate speech, has not yet made a finding that Governor Lenku has committed an offense. The summons directs Lenku to appear at Britam Towers in Upper Hill, Nairobi, at 2 p.m. on Thursday, September 17, to assist with the ongoing investigation into conduct capable of undermining peaceful relations among communities.

Why it mattersGovernor Lenku's remarks could affect peaceful relations among communities in Kajiado and Narok by promoting ethnic exclusion in political nominations.

Editor Found Near Dam After Abduction

Standard Group Editor Alex Kiprotich was discovered nine hours after armed men abducted him along the Gilgil–Nakuru Road.

Alex Kiprotich, an editor at The Standard, was found dumped near the Masinga Dam outlet at 2 a.m. on Tuesday. He had been abducted nine hours earlier by armed men along the Gilgil–Nakuru Road.

CCTV footage reportedly shows armed men forcing the journalist to open his car doors on Tuesday evening. The exact circumstances and motive behind the abduction remain unclear.

Kiprotich was found approximately 10 meters away from the dam's outlet. The incident raises concerns about the safety of journalists operating in the region.

Why it mattersJournalists and media professionals are directly affected by this event, facing potential risks to their personal safety and freedom of movement.
