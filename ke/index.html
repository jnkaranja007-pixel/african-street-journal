Saudi Arabia bans travel from Kenya over Ebola fears

The kingdom suspended citizen travel and halted all visa issuances due to nine confirmed cases.

Saudi Arabia has suspended travel for its citizens to Kenya and stopped issuing visas due to concerns over the Bundibugyo virus outbreak. The restrictions, announced on Friday, also ban entry for travellers who have stayed in Kenya or another Ebola-affected country within the 21 days prior to arrival.

These measures were introduced after Saudi health authorities assessed the Ebola situation, according to Saudi Gazette. The Kingdom’s Public Health Authority, Weqaya, stated the restrictions are precautionary to protect citizens, residents, and visitors from transmission risks. Saudi Arabia has previously imposed similar bans on countries like the Democratic Republic of Congo, Uganda, and South Sudan.

Kenya has confirmed nine Ebola cases, including two deaths, as of October 30. The outbreak is linked to a Kenyan man who travelled from the Democratic Republic of Congo. Health officials reported that two of seven patients in isolation tested negative in initial tests and await repeat testing, while the remaining five are stable. Kenya had also gone three days without reporting a new confirmed case.

Why it mattersTravel and visa restrictions imposed by Saudi Arabia directly affect Kenyans seeking to enter the kingdom.

Rai Group plans US$32 million sugar factory in Kenya

The new facility in Nandi County will process 1,500 tonnes of sugarcane daily, creating jobs and boosting local incomes.

Rai Group's subsidiary, West Kenya Sugar Co., will invest US$32 million (Kes 4.2 billion) to build a new sugar factory in Saniak Village, Nandi County. The factory is designed to process 1,500 tonnes of sugarcane per day, with plans to double its capacity. The project's Environmental and Social Impact Assessment report details the facility's components, including a mill house, power house, and an effluent treatment plant.

The investment is expected to create direct and indirect employment and encourage farmers to increase sugarcane production. The assessment report suggests potential improvements in household incomes and food security. Rai Group, which owns Kabras Sugar, has over 30,000 acres under sugarcane cultivation through farmer partnerships.

The proposed factory will increase the group's processing capacity, adding to its existing mills: West Kenya Sugar, Olepito, Sukari, and Naitiri. In May 2025, West Kenya Sugar leased Nzoia Sugar's factory and estate for 30 years. Mills under the Rai family are projected to have a combined daily processing capacity exceeding 18,000 tonnes of sugarcane.

Why it mattersKenyan farmers in Nandi County will have a new market for their sugarcane, potentially increasing their incomes and food security.

Politics · 2026-10-31

IEBC defends CEO shortlist over foreign applicants

The electoral commission states all shortlisted candidates for Chief Executive Officer are Kenyan citizens.

The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) has defended its shortlist for Commission Secretary and Chief Executive Officer, stating all candidates are Kenyan citizens. Concerns were raised by the People’s Coalition on Electoral Reforms, led by former Chief Justice David Maraga, over approximately 44 names on the longlist appearing to be of foreign origin.

IEBC clarified that foreign nationals were eliminated at the preliminary stage for not meeting the citizenship requirement. The recruitment process was managed by KPMG Kenya. The commission explained that publishing all 304 applications was a constitutional requirement for transparency, not an indication of qualification.

Mandatory requirements for the position included Kenyan citizenship, a recognized degree, five years of senior management experience, and adherence to Chapter Six of the Constitution. IEBC stated that verification of national identity documents, citizenship, and academic qualifications confirmed that only Kenyan citizens made the final shortlist.

Why it mattersThe People's Coalition on Electoral Reforms warned that a questionable longlist could undermine public confidence in the recruitment of the commission's top administrative official.

First body recovered from Nairobi building collapse

16 people remain missing after an 18-storey building collapsed in Nairobi's Eastleigh district.

Rescue teams recovered the first body on Friday from the rubble of an 18-storey building that collapsed a week ago in Nairobi, with 16 people still missing.

The body of a woman was found crushed beneath concrete and steel at the construction site in Eastleigh, a busy commercial district. Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja stated the building lacked required construction permits and had illegal commercial rentals before inspection.

Families have gathered at the site for a week, but hopes of finding survivors have faded after the critical 72-hour window passed, with no survivors rescued since Saturday.

Why it mattersFamilies are waiting for news of 16 people missing after a building collapse.

Agriculture · 2026-10-31

Kenya develops national coffee flavour atlas

Around 500 samples from 34 counties will be analysed to document diverse coffee profiles for industry use.

Kenya is creating a national coffee flavour atlas to document the country's diverse coffee profiles, with findings expected in 2027. The Kenya Coffee Flavor Map initiative will analyse approximately 500 samples from 34 coffee-growing counties, bringing together producers, co-operatives, researchers, and sensory professionals. Professional cuppers will evaluate coffee samples blind, linking tasting notes to geographic and production data to create regional flavour profiles and a searchable sensory database.

The initiative aims to connect coffee flavour characteristics with regional origins, varieties, and production practices, according to organisers. Marty Pollack, co-founder of Torch Coffee, stated the project would encourage collaboration between Kenyan and international coffee professionals. The project is supported by the Government of Kenya, the Coffee Board of Kenya, and the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) in Kenya.

The Kenya Coffee Flavor & Origin Expo will be held in Nairobi from November 14 to 16, featuring professional cupping sessions, exhibitions, and networking for producers and international buyers. Organisers expect the atlas to provide a shared reference for understanding regional differences and communicating coffee characteristics. The number of coffee shops in Kenya increased from 231 in 2019 to 791 in 2023.

Why it mattersKenyan coffee producers and exporters will gain a shared reference for understanding and communicating the unique characteristics of their coffees.
