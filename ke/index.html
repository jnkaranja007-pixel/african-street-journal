Police Probe Armed Men After Editor's Abduction

The National Police Service is investigating the identities of armed individuals who allegedly abducted Standard Group Associate Editor Alex Kiprotich along the Gilgil-Nakuru Road.

The National Police Service has launched an investigation into the abduction of Standard Group Associate Editor Alex Kiprotich, who was taken by heavily armed men on Tuesday night along the Gilgil-Nakuru Road. Kiprotich was found safe on Wednesday morning after approximately nine hours. The police spokesperson stated that the Anti-Abduction Unit is actively pursuing the case to identify the perpetrators and ensure they are brought to justice.

The incident has raised concerns about the safety of journalists, with the Media Council of Kenya noting previous cases of press freedom violations that have not resulted in arrests. The council urged the Inspector General of Police to ensure accountability for those responsible for Kiprotich's abduction and to address the documented violations against media practitioners.

The police condemned the abduction, emphasizing that intimidation of journalists or interference with constitutional rights, including press freedom, will not be tolerated. The Anti-Abduction Unit, established specifically for such cases, is investigating the circumstances, including the reported presence of a rogue 60-man squad outside the police command structure.

Why it mattersJournalists are affected by the abduction of Alex Kiprotich, highlighting concerns over press freedom and the effectiveness of security agencies in protecting media practitioners.

Director Charged Over Alleged KSh28 Million Housing Fraud

A director of Gladdens Homes Limited faces charges for defrauding 22 individuals of KSh28 million in a housing scheme.

Bildad Wambua, a director of Gladdens Homes Limited, was arraigned at the Makadara Law Courts on Wednesday, September 2, facing multiple counts of obtaining money by false pretences and money laundering. The Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) alleges Wambua operated a housing scheme between 2024 and 2026, targeting individuals seeking to purchase homes in Nairobi. He is accused of falsely representing that prospective buyers could purchase houses on Land Title No. Nairobi/Block A 40/474, as part of a purported Urban Housing Renewable Development LPP project.

Victims allegedly made payments through Gladdens Homes Agency, a private entity associated with Wambua, after being induced by these representations. The DCI stated that investigations confirmed 22 victims collectively lost KSh28 million through the scheme. The prosecution opposed Wambua's release on bond, with a ruling scheduled for September 3. The case is set for mention on October 15, 2026.

The DCI urged members of the public who may have had similar transactions with Wambua or Gladdens Homes Agency and believe they were defrauded to report to the DCI Starehe Sub-County offices. Detectives also advised Kenyans to conduct due diligence before committing funds to property transactions and to report suspected fraud promptly. The allegations against Wambua have not been proven in court.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the case involves alleged fraud and money laundering in a housing scheme, impacting financial trust and potentially property development.

Chinese miner falls to death in 147-foot Siaya pit

A 59-year-old Chinese national died at a gold mining site in Siaya County after losing consciousness and falling into a deep pit.

A Chinese national, Jiang Minqiao, 59, died Tuesday after falling into a 147-foot pit at the ArmLight Gold Mining Site One in Lumba Village, Siaya County. Police reported that Jiang lost consciousness around 1:39 am as he and another Chinese worker, Zheng Weishui, were ascending from the pit. He sustained severe injuries, including a head injury and fractures to his hands and legs, and died at the scene.

The incident occurred shortly before 2 am, according to a report filed at Ndori Police Station. Jiang had reported for duty at 10 pm on September 1. The site manager alerted authorities, and officers from the Ndori Sub-County Police and Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) processed the scene. The body was transported to Aga Khan Hospital mortuary in Kisumu for preservation pending a postmortem examination.

Police are investigating the death as a sudden death and death of a foreign national to establish the exact circumstances surrounding the fatal fall. The incident highlights the inherent dangers at mining sites, particularly those involving deep pits and the risks associated with working at such depths.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the death of a foreign national at a mining site raises safety concerns and could impact operational regulations and oversight.

Kenyan Aviation Workers End Strike, Flights Resume

A two-day strike by Kenyan aviation workers has been called off after a deal was reached, allowing flights to resume from Nairobi.

Flights from Nairobi resumed on Tuesday after aviation workers ended a two-day strike that caused widespread delays and cancellations. Thousands of passengers were stranded across Kenya on Sunday and Monday due to the industrial action. The strike, a go-slow protest by aviation unions, was the latest in a series demanding a new collective bargaining agreement and an end to the use of volunteer staff.

The Kenya Civil Aviation Authority announced that an agreement had been reached, which includes the payment of agency fees owed to the unions by the Airports Authority. Negotiations on the collective bargaining agreement are set to restart, with the aviation authority committing to uphold the outcome of these future discussions.

Jomo Kenyatta International Airport in Nairobi, a significant regional hub, experienced disruptions that impacted operations. This airport is crucial for Kenya's tourism sector, and the resolution of the strike is expected to restore normal travel and business activities.

Why it mattersThe strike disrupted flights, affecting thousands of passengers and impacting Kenya's vital tourism industry.

Activists Blame Kenya for Ugandan Opposition Figure's Plight

Ugandan and Kenyan activists accuse Kenya of facilitating the illegal rendition of opposition figure Kizza Besigye, whose health is reportedly deteriorating.

A coalition of Ugandan and Kenyan activists on Tuesday accused Kenya of facilitating the illegal rendition of Ugandan opposition figure Kizza Besigye, whose health is reportedly worsening. Besigye, who faces treason charges in Uganda, was allegedly abducted in Kenya in November 2024 and later appeared in court. Activists stated that Kenya's Extradition Act, requiring a lawful request, arrest warrant, and judicial proceedings, was bypassed.

The activists called for protests in Nairobi on Thursday, demanding accountability for those involved in Besigye's transfer. Last year, Kenya's foreign minister Musalia Mudavadi acknowledged that Kenya had cooperated with Ugandan authorities in Besigye's transfer. Hussein Khalid of rights group Vocal Africa stated that those responsible for the rendition, which has allegedly put Besigye on his deathbed, must be held accountable.

The activists are seeking an independent investigation to determine who requested, authorized, facilitated, and concealed Dr. Besigye's rendition. Besigye, a former physician to Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni, has been a prominent opposition figure for over 25 years. He was accused of plotting a conspiracy against Museveni, who has ruled Uganda for more than four decades.

Why it mattersUgandan opposition figure Kizza Besigye is affected by alleged illegal rendition facilitated by Kenya, leading to deteriorating health and treason charges.
