Kenyan cult leader sentenced to 200 years for 191 deaths

Pastor Paul Mackenzie convicted of murder and starvation charges in Shakahola Forest case.

Self-proclaimed Kenyan pastor Paul Mackenzie received a 200-year prison sentence on Friday for orchestrating the deaths of 190 children and one adult. The conviction stems from inciting his followers to starve themselves to death in the Shakahola Forest. Hundreds of bodies have been discovered at the sect's base near Malindi since 2023.

Mackenzie was found guilty last month of murdering 190 children and one adult between January 2021 and September 2023, alongside 28 co-accused. His lawyer announced an intention to appeal the verdict. The court handed down two 100-year sentences, one for homicide and another for starvation charges.

28 accomplices also received lengthy prison terms for their roles in the deaths. Mackenzie faces separate manslaughter and terrorism charges, which he denies. He appeared indifferent in court on Friday.

Why it mattersPastor Paul Mackenzie's cult activities led to the starvation deaths of 191 followers, including children.

MP urges Ruto to treat ODM talks as party-to-party

Kisumu West MP Rosa Buyu warned against framing engagement between UDA and ODM as a deal with the Luo community.

Kisumu West MP Rosa Buyu urged President William Ruto on Saturday to approach proposed talks between the United Democratic Alliance (UDA) and the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) as a partnership between political parties, not an ethnic deal. Speaking in Nyando Constituency, Kisumu County, Buyu stated that ODM is a national party with diverse supporters, not solely representing the Luo community.

Buyu emphasized that ODM's political identity extends beyond its traditional Nyanza region base, asserting that negotiations should be between UDA and ODM as distinct political entities. Senate Majority Leader Aaron Cheruiyot acknowledged ODM as a significant national partner, dismissing claims of its diminished relevance and affirming UDA's readiness for structured discussions to form a strong government.

Cheruiyot stated that UDA recognizes ODM as an equal partner deserving respect, aiming to build a political arrangement that fulfills Kenyans' aspirations. He referenced the founding party leader Raila Odinga's dream for such cooperation. The discussions precede the 2027 General Election, with both parties seeking a formal political arrangement.

Why it mattersThe engagement between Kenya's ruling UDA party and the opposition ODM party could shape future government formations, affecting all Kenyans.

Kenyan experts appointed to African Medicines Agency committees

Two Pharmacy and Poisons Board officials will serve two-year terms on AMA technical committees.

Kenya has secured two key roles on the African Medicines Agency (AMA) technical committees, with Edward Abwao elected chairperson of the committee overseeing clinical trials. The appointments of officials from the Pharmacy and Poisons Board (PPB) aim to harmonise medicines regulation across Africa and improve access to safe medical products. Dr Ali Abdulahi Arale was appointed to the committee that evaluates medicines, vaccines, and biologicals, while Edward Abwao joined the committee for clinical trials oversight and Good Clinical Practice.

These two-year appointments were announced in Kigali, Rwanda, during the AMA's inaugural week. PPB stated the roles recognise the experience of Kenya's medicines regulators and will strengthen the country's scientific and regulatory decision-making on the continent. Dr Arale brings over 25 years of experience in medicines regulation, including health product registration and emergency use authorization. Abwao has over 22 years of experience and helped establish Kenya’s clinical trials regulatory framework.

The committees provide independent scientific advice to AMA on medical product evaluation and clinical trial oversight. PPB said the participation of the Kenyan experts will foster continental regulatory cooperation and contribute to better access to quality-assured medical products across Africa. The AMA technical committees support evidence-based regulatory decisions and the harmonisation of standards among African countries.

Why it mattersKenyan citizens will benefit from improved access to safe and quality-assured medical products across Africa due to these appointments.

UDA delegates back Aruasa for Kitutu Chache South MP

Businessman Kevin Aruasa received endorsement from United Democratic Alliance delegates for the 2027 election.

United Democratic Alliance (UDA) delegates in Kitutu Chache South have endorsed businessman Kevin Aruasa as their preferred candidate for the constituency's parliamentary seat in the 2027 General Election. The endorsement occurred at a delegates’ meeting in the constituency, where supporters cited Aruasa’s grassroots support and campaign strength. Aruasa recently moved from the Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) to UDA and aims to challenge incumbent MP Anthony Kibagendi.

Following the endorsement, Aruasa pledged to focus on development, youth empowerment, education, and economic opportunities if elected. He stated that the delegates' trust signifies the people's desire for better leadership and development in Kitutu Chache South. Delegates highlighted issues such as youth unemployment, infrastructure challenges, and stalled development projects, promising to rally support for Aruasa.

Aruasa's supporters are working to secure his position as UDA's candidate for Kitutu Chache South before party nominations and the 2027 election. This move sets up a potential contest with MP Anthony Kibagendi, who is expected to seek re-election. The endorsement precedes President William Ruto's upcoming development tour of Kisii and Nyamira counties.

Why it mattersResidents of Kitutu Chache South will have a new candidate, Kevin Aruasa, vying for their parliamentary seat in the 2027 election.

Kenya sugar production jumps 56.7pc

Local factories produced 690,521 tonnes between January and September 2026, up from 440,652 tonnes last year.

Kenya's sugar production increased by 56.7 per cent in the first nine months of 2026, with local factories producing 690,521 tonnes. This output is up from 440,652 tonnes recorded during the same period in 2025, representing an increase of 249,869 tonnes.

The rise in production coincided with a drop in the national average retail price of sugar to Sh155 per kilogram in September 2026, down from Sh171 in September 2025. Kenya Sugar Board Chief Executive Officer Jude Chesire is also chairing the International Sugar Organization Council. Kenya aims to build surpluses for exports by next year, projecting 900,000 tonnes against a demand of one million tonnes.

Sugarcane deliveries to factories processed 7.76 million tonnes in the first nine months of 2026, a 56.2 per cent increase from 4.97 million tonnes last year. Sugar imports fell by approximately 31 per cent to 251,690 tonnes between January and September 2026, compared to 364,486 tonnes in the same period of 2025.

Why it mattersKenyan households are experiencing relief from the cost of living due to lower sugar prices, and the country is positioning itself for future sugar exports.
