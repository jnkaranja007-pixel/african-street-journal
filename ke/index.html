Health · 2026-10-21

Kenya confirms ninth Ebola case as United States raises travel advisory

Health officials report the newest patient is a close relative of the first victim, while contact tracing reaches 134 of 135 identified individuals.

Kenya confirmed a ninth case of Ebola on October 19 after a 24-year-old man tested positive while in quarantine. Director General for Health Dr Patrick Amoth stated that the patient is a close relative of the first victim and has started treatment. Eight hospitalized patients are responding well and showing significant improvement under observation.

The Ministry of Health distributed medical supplies to twenty-eight high-risk counties and established a dedicated testing laboratory at Kenyatta National Hospital. Contact tracing has covered 134 of 135 identified people, placing them in quarantine without any transmission recorded inside the facilities.

The United States State Department raised its travel advisory for Kenya to Level three in response to the outbreak. Officials noted that Kenya has not imposed any restrictions on public gatherings, travel or trade, and contacts who remain well will begin returning home from October 25 after completing their twenty-one-day monitoring period.

Why it mattersHealth officials and citizens in Kenya are directly affected by the Ebola outbreak, quarantine measures, and newly implemented medical testing protocols.

Ruto Expands National Internship Programme By 50,000 Places Starting January 2027

President William Ruto announced a massive expansion of the national internship initiative during recent celebrations in Eldoret.

President William Ruto announced plans to scale up the National Internship Programme by adding 50,000 places starting in January 2027. Speaking during Mashujaa Day celebrations in Eldoret, the President stated that the expanded initiative will provide monthly stipends to successful applicants finishing their studies at universities, technical and vocational education and training institutions, and other colleges.

Placements under the upcoming expansion will use a transparent, digital, and merit-based system, supported by incentives for private employers to offer meaningful work experience. The plan addresses the challenge faced by graduates who struggle to find jobs without prior experience.

This internship expansion is part of four measures proposed by the government, which also include changes to income tax, stamp duty on land transactions, and import duty on mobile phones. Administrative and financing arrangements are expected to proceed ahead of the rollout.

Why it mattersYoung people finishing their studies at universities and colleges are affected by gaining access to the expanded internship programme and monthly stipends.

News · 2026-10-21

Kenyan court convicts cult leader Paul Mackenzie of one hundred ninety-one murders

A Mombasa judge found a self-proclaimed preacher and twenty-eight associates guilty of murdering children and adults in a starvation cult.

A Kenyan court found a self-proclaimed preacher and twenty-eight co-defendants guilty of murdering 191 people in the Shakahola Forest starvation cult case. Hundreds of bodies have been discovered since 2023 at the cult base inland from Malindi.

Paul Mackenzie and his associates were convicted after the judge stated the prosecution proved the case for the murder of 190 children and one adult. The accused stood impassive in the Mombasa courtroom while relatives of the victims looked on.

The High Court rejected claims that the victims voluntarily fasted to death to meet Jesus. Mackenzie continues to face separate charges of manslaughter and terrorism.

Why it mattersRelatives of the victims and the general public are affected by the closure and accountability delivered by the court ruling on the mass starvation deaths.

Business · 2026-10-21

Court certifies urgent bid to jail Nairobi officials over Sh76.7m debt

The High Court has certified as urgent an application seeking to jail Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja and three other county officials over unpaid legal fees.

The High Court has certified as urgent an application seeking to jail Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja, current Finance Executive Ibrahim Auma Nyangoya, Chief Officer for Finance Asha Abdi, and former Finance Executive Charles Kerich over an alleged breach of a court-approved payment plan. Kwengu & Company Advocates filed the fresh application to reinstate a three-month jail sentence previously imposed on Kerich and hold the officials accountable for a Sh76.7 million debt.

Justice Francis Gikonyo directed that the application be served and responses filed within seven days, setting the hearing for October 29. The dispute stems from a missed payment of a second instalment of Sh36.7 million due on October 15 under a court-adopted consent order from July 16. The county previously paid an initial instalment of Sh30 million out of the total Sh106.7 million legal fees dispute involving money owed to Foton East Africa Limited.

The law firm wants the court to declare that the consent order has been breached and to summon Governor Sakaja to personally explain why he should not be cited for contempt. The advocates are seeking civil jail terms of six months for the officials unless the outstanding balance of Sh76,736,841 is settled in full.

Why it mattersNairobi county officials face potential civil jail terms and Governor Sakaja faces a personal court summons over an unpaid Sh76.7 million legal fee debt.

Agriculture · 2026-10-21

Kirinyaga Tea Farmers Demand Kes 117 Million Subsidy Refund

Growers at Thumaita Tea Factory want a government refund after delayed fertiliser subsidy reimbursements reduced their annual bonus payments.

Tea farmers at Thumaita Tea Factory in Kirinyaga County are demanding a Kes 117 million refund from the government. The growers blame delayed fertiliser subsidy reimbursements for reduced bonuses and mounting financial pressure.

Factory chairman Richard Magu stated that the facility used money intended for growers to cover outstanding costs because the state failed to remit funds. Senate figures show the government reportedly owes the Kenya Tea Development Agency approximately Kes7 billion.

Farmers also requested promised tea-processing equipment to improve product quality. In September, the government announced a Kes 10 billion allocation for the tea sector to fund subsidies and factory modernisation.

Why it mattersSmallholder tea farmers are affected because delayed government fertiliser reimbursements reduced their bonus payments and strained household finances.
