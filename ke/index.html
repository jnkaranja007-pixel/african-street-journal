Kenya could be declared Ebola-free in 42 days

Health CS Aden Duale said Kenya will be declared Ebola-free after 42 days without new cases, following WHO protocols.

Kenya could be declared free of Ebola after completing 42 consecutive days without a new case, Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale said. Health authorities are intensifying surveillance and preparing to release contacts who have finished their mandatory monitoring period. The countdown follows World Health Organization (WHO) protocols and depends on the country recording no further infections.

Duale stated on Citizen TV that the government had traced all identified contacts linked to the outbreak and was conducting regular tests. He reported a 100 per cent contact-tracing rate, exceeding the 85 per cent benchmark. The infections detected so far were linked to known contacts, not wider community transmission. The WHO reported nine confirmed cases in Kenya, including one death, as of October 20.

People who completed the 21-day monitoring period are undergoing final tests and clinical assessments. The incubation period for Ebola ranges from two to 21 days. Nearly 551 people were tested in the preceding 48 hours, with all samples returning negative results. The initial patient died on October 5, with laboratory confirmation announced the following day.

Why it mattersKenya's population will be declared Ebola-free if 42 consecutive days pass without new cases.

Former Alego Usonga MP Otieno Mak’Onyango dies

Mak’Onyango, who was detained after the 1982 coup attempt, died in a Nairobi hospital on Sunday.

Former Alego Usonga Member of Parliament Otieno Mak’Onyango died on Sunday evening while receiving treatment at a Nairobi hospital. His successor, Samuel Atandi, announced his death, remembering him as a significant figure in the constituency's history.

Mak’Onyango represented Alego Usonga from 1992 to 1997 after winning a seat on a Ford-Kenya ticket following the reintroduction of multiparty politics. Before entering politics, he worked as a journalist but was arrested in August 1982 following a failed coup attempt and detained for approximately four years, during which he claimed he was tortured.

In 2003, Mak’Onyango sued the government for his detention and was awarded Sh20 million in damages in June 2012, though the compensation had not been paid by 2014, according to a newspaper report. His life spanned periods of political repression and the return of multiparty democracy in Kenya.

Why it mattersFormer Alego Usonga MP Otieno Mak’Onyango died in a Nairobi hospital after a period of detention and a legal battle for compensation.

News · 2026-10-26

two rescued from collapsed Nairobi building

search continues for five people still missing after a 16 or 18-storey block crumbled on Friday.

Two people were pulled alive from the rubble of a building that collapsed in Nairobi on Friday, with a search ongoing for five others still missing. The 16 or 18-storey block crumbled, sending a plume of dust into the streets. 66 of the 67 construction workers in the building at the time escaped unharmed, though one suffered non-life-threatening injuries. The five missing people were thought to have been in shops on the building's base floors, where 11 shops had already opened.

Police said on Saturday that a manhunt had begun for the building's owner and the contractor. Dramatic footage showed the bottom floor giving way before the rest of the building collapsed. Dozens of builders working on upper floors managed to get out largely unscathed. Interior ministry official Kipchumba Murkomen confirmed the number of workers and the injured.

The incident has raised concerns about construction standards and regulations in Kenya. Police used tear gas to dispel looters and crowds of onlookers after the collapse. Rescue teams had made contact with some of those trapped beneath the rubble.

Why it mattersTwo people were rescued and five are still missing after a building collapsed in Nairobi.

Kenya Red Cross trains staff after second Ebola death

Kenya has confirmed nine cases of Ebola, including two deaths, prompting emergency preparedness training.

Kenya has recorded its second death from Ebola, bringing the total confirmed cases to nine. The Kenya Red Cross has begun training its staff in Nairobi on safe handling and burial procedures for victims, as these are high-risk transmission periods.

Dr. Paul Olale, Head of public health emergencies at Kenya Red Cross, stated that his teams are being trained in safe and dignified burials to ensure readiness for any cases. The first victim, who travelled from the Democratic Republic of Congo, died in Nairobi on October 5. The health ministry traced 135 contacts of the first case, placing them in quarantine.

The second person to die was a 65-year-old woman, a close family member and direct contact of the initial patient, who died on Friday. The Red Cross's training focuses on preparedness for handling victims and their bodies to prevent further spread of the disease.

Why it mattersKenya's population is directly affected by the confirmation of two Ebola deaths and nine cases, necessitating public health emergency training.
