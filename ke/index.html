Kenya allocates Kes 3.7 billion to modernise tea factories

President Ruto announced the investment to improve productivity and narrow bonus payment gaps for smallholder farmers.

The Kenyan government will invest Kes 3.7 billion (US$28.48M) to modernise tea factories and boost earnings for smallholder farmers, particularly in the West Rift region. President William Ruto announced the funding in Nyamira County, aiming to improve productivity and reduce the difference in bonus payments between farmers in the West and East Rift regions. The government has already released Kes 1.2 billion (US$9.24M), with an additional Kes 2.5 billion (US$19.24M) planned for the next financial year.

Ruto stated that the investment addresses challenges like delayed tea leaves at the Mombasa auction and ageing factory infrastructure that leads to operational inefficiencies. Farmers in the West Rift receive lower average prices, partly due to these issues and higher energy costs. A parliamentary committee inquiry confirmed significant price differences, with East Rift tea fetching US$2.95 per kilogramme compared to US$1.78 for West Rift tea during the 2024/25 financial year.

The western region produces about 70% of Kenya’s tea but faces lower-grade quality and higher production costs. The parliamentary committee recommended replacing old machinery, auditing local hydropower, and expanding market development. New legal frameworks were also called for to stabilise earnings and address structural inequalities within the tea industry.

Senate committee adopts Sh71.1bn county fund report

The Finance and Budget Committee flagged errors in disbursement schedules for additional county funds.

The Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Budget adopted a report on disbursing an additional KSh71.1 billion to Kenya's county governments in the 2026/2027 financial year. The money covers universal health coverage, community health promoters, county headquarters, affordable housing and projects funded by development partners.

The committee, which met at Parliament Buildings on November 10, found mathematical errors and impractically small amounts in the tranche schedules. It proposed revised allocations of KSh194.08 million for Isiolo, KSh107.91 million for Lamu, KSh159.49 million for Tana River, KSh197.03 million for Nyandarua and KSh153.02 million for Tharaka Nithi. Tana River's Third Schedule allocation would be corrected to KSh505.67 million, paid in two instalments of KSh252.84 million on November 15, 2026 and February 15, 2027.

The committee urged the National Treasury to follow the approved cash disbursement schedules strictly, and to expedite assessment of projects tied to performance requirements. The report will now be tabled before the Senate for debate.

Why it mattersFive county governments, including Isiolo, Lamu, Tana River, Nyandarua and Tharaka Nithi, would receive corrected allocations if the Senate approves the report.

Senators question Kasarani Stadium readiness for AFCON 2027

The Senate Committee on Labour and Social Welfare inspected the Nairobi stadium and questioned its accessibility and emergency preparedness ahead of the December 31 deadline.

The Senate Committee on Labour and Social Welfare has raised concerns over the readiness of Moi International Sports Centre, Kasarani, to host matches at the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON). The committee, chaired by West Pokot Senator Julius Murgor, inspected the 48,000-capacity stadium on November 11, 2026, to check the progress of upgrade works before the December 31 handover deadline.

Senators questioned the pace of construction, access for persons living with disabilities and the stadium's emergency preparedness. Nominated Senator Crystal Asige called for sustainable accessibility measures, while Kajiado Senator Seki Lenku questioned firefighting preparedness after Nairobi City County officials reported that fire hydrants had no water during an earlier inspection. The project team, led by Major Daniel Makana, told the committee the stadium would be ready within the stipulated timelines.

Kenya is co-hosting AFCON 2027 with Uganda and Tanzania, and Kasarani and the 60,000-seat Raila Odinga International Stadium in Talanta Sports City are among its designated venues. The Confederation of African Football (CAF) will approve the stadium only if it meets Category 4 requirements covering infrastructure, spectator safety, accessibility and media facilities. In September 2026, Sports Principal Secretary Elijah Mwangi said a recent CAF inspection had returned encouraging results, though he said this was not final certification.

Why it mattersPersons living with disabilities and spectators at the stadium depend on its accessibility and firefighting measures, which senators say remain under scrutiny before the December 31 handover.

Business · 2026-11-11

Kanja Thuku named acting KTDA CEO

Kanja Thuku, with over 34 years at the agency, takes over from retiring Eng Francis Miano.

Kanja Thuku was appointed acting Chief Executive Officer of the Kenya Tea Development Agency (KTDA) on November 10, 2026, replacing Eng Francis Miano who is retiring. Thuku, currently Group Director for Special Programmes, brings over 34 years of experience and institutional knowledge to the role. The KTDA Holding Board Chairman Enos Njeru announced the appointment following a strategic management review.

The board directed the new acting CEO to focus on stabilizing earnings for smallholder tea factories and implementing reforms to reduce production costs. Eng Miano, who served as acting CEO since January 2026, has proceeded on his terminal leave. Thuku holds a Bachelor of Science in Agriculture from the University of Nairobi and has training in management, logistics, and agricultural practices.

This leadership change occurs amidst an ongoing dispute over the position of KTDA Board Chairman Enos Njeru. Njeru is challenging the legitimacy of a recent election that named James Githinji Mwangi as chairman. The governance concerns affect the management of interests for hundreds of thousands of smallholder tea farmers.

Why it mattersThe appointment of an acting CEO and ongoing leadership disputes at KTDA affect the management and earnings of hundreds of thousands of smallholder tea farmers.
