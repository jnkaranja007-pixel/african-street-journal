Wetangula, Kingi lead MPs to visit recuperating Oburu

National Assembly Speaker Moses Wetangula and Senate Speaker Amason Kingi visited ODM leader Oburu Oginga at his Nairobi home on November 5.

National Assembly Speaker Moses Wetangula and Senate Speaker Amason Kingi visited Orange Democratic Movement (ODM) Party Leader Oburu Oginga at his Nairobi home on November 5. They led a delegation of MPs and Senators to wish the Siaya Senator a speedy recovery after his discharge from hospital.

Wetangula advised Oburu to prioritize his health and avoid rushing back to political engagements, commending his courage. Oburu expressed gratitude for the visit, stating that false social media reports declaring him dead while hospitalized had only strengthened his resolve. "Those who wrote me off while I was in hospital by declaring me dead only made me stronger and more determined to fight my sickness," he said.

Oburu acknowledged his illness had temporarily slowed his political activities but vowed to return to parliamentary debates and active discourse soon. Senate Speaker Kingi noted that the Senate fraternity missed Oburu’s contributions to proceedings and national politics. The delegation included Senators David Wakoli, Stewart Madzayo, William Kisang, and Daniel Maanzo, alongside MPs Millie Odhiambo, Mishi Mboko, and Eve Obara.

Why it mattersODM leader and Siaya Senator Oburu Oginga is recuperating at his Nairobi home after hospitalisation and is receiving visits from top government officials.

Kenya macadamia crisis: 30,000 tonnes unsold

Only 5% of expected macadamia nuts shipped before export window closed, leaving farmers and processors facing losses.

More than 30,000 metric tons of raw macadamia nuts remain unsold in Kenya after the export window closed on October 10. The Agriculture and Food Authority (AFA) reported that only 700 metric tons of the expected 14,000 metric tons were shipped before the deadline. This leaves processors and farmers counting significant losses due to the unexported allocation.

Industry stakeholders attribute the low export volume to bureaucratic hurdles and strict deadlines, preventing farmers from accessing international markets and improving farmgate prices. Kenya Macadamia Growers Association Vice Chairman Mureithi Gichabi urged the government to extend the export period, faulting the regulator for not facilitating the process. Nut Traders Association of Kenya Vice Chairman Njiru Kathangu called for predictable policies, including lifting the ban on raw nut exports to China.

Macadamia value chain expert Peter Mwangi warned the current crisis mirrors a similar situation three years ago. In Nakuru, large-estate representative Jacinta Mwai reported over 5,000 metric tons awaiting shipment, citing damage to investor confidence from policy uncertainty. A farmer in Murang’a, Jane Wambui, noted the glut has weakened farmers’ bargaining power, leading to low farmgate prices.

Why it mattersKenyan macadamia farmers and processors face financial losses due to unsold produce and depressed prices.

Njeru Ndwiga appointed Kenya Coffee Board chairperson

Henry Kinyua will lead the Coffee Research and Training Institute as the sector aims to boost annual production to 150,000 metric tonnes.

Njeru Peter Ndwiga began a three-year term as the Non-Executive Chairperson of the Coffee Board of Kenya on October 30, 2026. President William Ruto made the appointment via Gazette Notice No. 16957, replacing Henry Gihuhi Kinyua.

Ndwiga, a former Embu Senator and Cabinet minister, brings experience in public service and the coffee sector, having served on the Senate Committee on Agriculture. He has supported reforms to improve farmer earnings and cooperative marketing.

Henry Kinyua was appointed Chairperson of the Coffee Research and Training Institute for three years, effective October 30. The new institutions aim to increase annual coffee production to 150,000 metric tonnes, from a projected 60,000 tonnes in the 2025/2026 season, by improving varieties and farmer training.

Why it mattersKenyan coffee farmers will benefit from improved earnings and new coffee varieties designed to withstand climate change.
