Kenya plans tougher laws against illicit alcohol cartels

The government will introduce stricter legislation and boost enforcement to dismantle criminal networks involved in the production and sale of harmful alcoholic drinks.

Deputy President Kithure Kindiki has declared illicit alcohol a significant threat to Kenya’s future, vowing to implement tougher laws and intensify enforcement against production and sale networks. The government has elevated the fight against illicit brews and drug abuse, with a multi-agency response coordinated by the Deputy President's office. Kindiki stated that the alcohol menace could undermine national progress and emphasized a commitment to successful intervention, distinguishing the crackdown from targeting legitimate businesses.

The campaign involves agencies such as the National Authority for the Campaign Against Alcohol and Drug Abuse (NACADA), the Anti-Counterfeit Authority, the Kenya Bureau of Standards (KEBS), and the Anti-Narcotics Unit. A permanent secretariat has been established at Harambee House Annex to coordinate ongoing enforcement and inter-agency collaboration. Law enforcement agencies are directed to support NACADA's operations, with the government expecting improved results from the intensified crackdown.

Recent coordinated efforts over 10 days led to nine successful raids on premises selling illicit ethanol and 116 raids targeting sellers of illicit brews nationwide. The government is also pursuing amendments to the legal framework governing the manufacture, distribution, and sale of alcoholic drinks to close loopholes exploited by criminal networks and impose tougher penalties.

Why it mattersKenyans, particularly young people, are at risk of severe health consequences and societal disruption from the proliferation of dangerous illicit alcohol.

Port of Lamu receives second Dangote refinery equipment shipment

The Port of Lamu received 154 pieces of heavy machinery for the proposed Dangote East Africa Refinery, marking progress on the KSh 2.2 trillion project.

The Port of Lamu has received a second shipment of equipment for the proposed Dangote East Africa Refinery. The MV Great Glory docked carrying 154 pieces of heavy machinery, following an earlier delivery in late September. The KSh 2.2 trillion (US$17 billion) refinery is designed to process up to 700,000 barrels of crude oil daily and is expected to become the largest refining complex in East Africa.

These successive shipments signify physical progress for the refinery project, which requires significant logistical capabilities that Lamu has demonstrated. The port's infrastructure, including its 17.5-meter berth depth, allows for direct discharge of heavy modules. The port handled nearly 800,000 metric tonnes in 2025 and plans to add 21 berths by 2030.

The refinery's construction phase is expected to generate substantial cargo volumes through Lamu over the next 40 months, supporting the port's expansion plans. The long-term viability will depend on sustained throughput after construction and securing crude supply arrangements to operate at design capacity.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the ongoing development of the KSh 2.2 trillion refinery, which is receiving equipment shipments and aims to be the largest in East Africa.

Ex-official alleges procurement pressure in Sakaja fallout

Former Nairobi Chief Officer Geoffrey Mosiria claims Governor Sakaja sidelined him and pressured him into questionable procurement deals.

Former Nairobi County Chief Officer Geoffrey Mosiria has accused Governor Johnson Sakaja of sidelining him for nearly three years and pressuring him into questionable procurement deals. Mosiria stated that his powers as an accounting officer were effectively transferred to junior officers on December 28, 2023, through an unprocedural memo. He also alleged that his health budget was drastically reduced from Sh1.2 billion to Sh100 million, hindering his ability to perform duties.

Mosiria claimed the fallout began after he refused to participate in direct procurement arrangements that he believed would benefit individuals close to the governor and his contractors. He recounted a confrontation during a WhatsApp conference call with Chief of Staff Osman Khalifa regarding these procurement issues. Subsequently, Mosiria was transferred to the Environment department, where he felt he had little authority, and later to Public Participation, Citizen Engagement and Customer Service.

Despite facing alleged attacks in official groups, public criticism, and a court case for contempt, Mosiria stated he chose to lead enforcement operations on the streets to serve Nairobi residents. He also claimed to have received information about preparations for a court case intended to jail him, expressing his hope that Nairobians would discern who truly serves them and who belongs in jail.

Why it mattersNairobi residents are affected by potential corruption in procurement and the reduction of health budgets, impacting public services.

KFC launches Kenyan-inspired food and entertainment campaign

The fast-food chain introduces a limited-time menu and retail activations with local creatives in Nairobi.

KFC has launched Kentucky Town 254, a Kenyan-inspired food and entertainment campaign running from October 20 to November 15, 2026. The initiative combines locally influenced menu innovations with retail activations and partnerships with Kenyan creatives. The campaign introduces a limited-time menu at selected KFC restaurants in Nairobi, featuring items like The Colonel’s Mandazi Burger, The Colonel’s Mandazi Pops, The Kentucky Choma Burger, Samosa Crunch, and Catch These Masala Fries, alongside new beverages.

The campaign extends beyond the menu with pop-up experiences at The Junction Mall, The Hub Karen, Galleria Shopping Mall, and Garden City Mall, featuring games, live performances, and music. A limited-edition merchandise collection, developed with Kenyan fashion and design brand JDHAA | home Apparel 254, is also part of the offering. KFC Africa stated that the initiative was developed around local creators, suppliers, and talent, with execution led in Kenya, which is its second-largest market in Africa with 48 restaurants.

This campaign reflects a localisation strategy adapting international quick-service restaurant offerings to regional tastes and creating opportunities to collaborate with local suppliers, designers, and entertainment talent. KFC Africa aims to build a restaurant brand that reflects African consumers, local partnerships, and regional creativity. The approach highlights the potential of limited-time menus to test new flavour combinations, generate customer engagement, and connect global brands with local food cultures.

Why it mattersKenyan consumers are affected by new menu items and entertainment experiences that incorporate local flavours and culture.

Kenyan veterinarian wins global award for wildlife care

Dr. Mukami Ruoro-Oundo recognized for her work treating injured and trafficked animals, strengthening conservation efforts.

Kenyan veterinary doctor Dr. Mukami Ruoro-Oundo has received international recognition, being named Wildlife Vet of the Year at the 2026 International Fund for Animal Welfare (IFAW) Animal Action Awards in London. The Senior Veterinary Officer at the Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS) was honored on October 15, 2026, for her significant contributions to wildlife rescue, veterinary medicine, and conservation.

Dr. Mukami's work involves treating animals with severe injuries and life-threatening conditions, often in remote areas. Her patients have included elephants with fractured limbs, rhinos with infected wounds, lions caught in snares, and pangolins rescued from illegal trafficking. She develops specialised treatment protocols, particularly for pangolins, enhancing their survival rates after rescue from illegal trade.

Her expertise extends to managing complex cases with limited resources, requiring quick decision-making and extensive veterinary knowledge. Beyond individual animal care, Dr. Mukami's development of treatment procedures and sharing of professional knowledge are strengthening wildlife healthcare practices and supporting long-term conservation efforts across Kenya.

Why it mattersWildlife veterinarians like Dr. Mukami are essential for treating injured animals and developing protocols that improve survival rates for endangered species.
