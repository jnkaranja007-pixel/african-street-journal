Nyanza Leaders Urge Governor Orengo to Retire From Politics

Leaders in Nyanza are calling for Governor James Orengo to step down from active politics, citing his long career and the need for new leadership.

Nyanza leaders are urging Siaya Governor James Orengo to retire from active politics, following his recent declaration to run for president in 2027. Ugenya MP David Ochieng stated that Orengo, one of the nation's longest-serving politicians, should follow the example of his peers who have already retired. Ochieng also accused the governor of undermining democratic principles by allegedly restricting the political freedoms of President William Ruto's supporters in Nyanza.

Ochieng made these remarks at a fundraiser for Sega Girls Secondary School, where he challenged Orengo to respect the right of Kenyans to support any candidate. He noted that many politicians from Orengo's generation, including Mukhisa Kituyi and Kiraitu Murungi, have left active politics. The MP also criticized Orengo's stance on political differences with the national government, advocating for a development-focused approach rather than confrontation.

Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi echoed concerns, joining Ochieng in accusing Orengo of verbally abusing President Ruto's supporters during a recent visit to Homa Bay. Mbadi cautioned against turning Nyanza into a political battleground, warning that competition should not lead to violence or the use of armed youth. These calls come amid intensifying political realignments ahead of the 2027 elections, with Nyanza leaders adopting increasingly divergent views on the national administration and regional direction.

Why it mattersNyanza leaders are affected by calls for Governor Orengo to retire, potentially shifting the region's political landscape and leadership.

Governor Sakaja Urges Early Evacuation Ahead of El Niño Rains

Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja warned residents in flood-prone areas to move to safety before the October-December rainy season begins.

Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja has urged residents in flood-prone areas to evacuate before the anticipated El Niño rains begin in October. Speaking in Kakamega, Sakaja stated that weather authorities have forecast a season that could bring difficult conditions. He emphasized the need for early action and personal responsibility, particularly regarding waste management to keep drainage channels clear.

Preparations are underway in Nairobi, involving county disaster-response teams, Kenya Red Cross, and engineers who are clearing and deepening waterways. Sakaja highlighted that while the government is undertaking these measures, citizen cooperation is crucial. He specifically warned against dumping rubbish into drainage systems, which can exacerbate flooding when heavy rains intensify.

The governor also noted that erratic weather could strain households and farmers, especially after a poor agricultural season. He called for affirmative government action to cushion vulnerable populations from the economic impacts of unfavorable weather. Sakaja encouraged those living in vulnerable locations nationwide to heed official warnings and move to safer areas proactively.

Why it mattersResidents in flood-prone areas are directly affected by the warning to evacuate and the potential for difficult conditions due to anticipated heavy rains.

GSU Officer Shoots Woman Dead in Eldoret Dispute

Police are searching for a General Service Unit officer after a fatal shooting at the GSU Mess in Uasin Gishu County on Sunday evening.

A 45-year-old woman, Doris Murei Nandi, was shot and killed during an argument at the GSU Mess in Naiberi, Uasin Gishu County, on Sunday around 5pm. The deceased was reportedly consuming alcohol when she clashed with a police constable identified as PC Nathan Kirwa, who was armed with a rifle. Seven spent 9mm cartridges were recovered at the scene.

Police officers, including county and sub-county commanders, responded to the incident. They processed the scene, recovering a rifle holster and a Toyota Corolla 110, registration KAR 371U, believed to be linked to the suspect. The woman's body was transported to Eldoret Hospital mortuary for a postmortem examination.

The suspect, PC Nathan Kirwa, fled the scene immediately after the shooting and remained at large as of Sunday evening. A manhunt has been launched by the Ainabkoi Sub-County Criminal Investigations Office, which is leading the investigation into the circumstances surrounding the fatal incident.

Why it mattersA GSU officer is sought by police after allegedly shooting and killing a woman during a dispute, impacting local safety and the ongoing investigation.

Wetangula Urges Opposition to Avoid IEBC Ahead of 2027 Polls

National Assembly Speaker Moses Masika Wetangula asked opposition leaders to refrain from criticizing the electoral body and allow it to conduct the upcoming General Election.

National Assembly Speaker Moses Masika Wetangula has urged the opposition to cease interfering with the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) and permit it to independently organize the 2027 General Election. Wetangula also implored opposition figures to stop employing abusive language against the electoral body, asserting that the IEBC has proven its capacity to manage credible elections, citing the recent Ollau by-election as an example.

Speaking at a church service and fundraiser in Narok East Constituency, Wetangula also voiced support for President William Ruto’s development initiatives, highlighting investments in agriculture and the planned construction of an airport in Narok as significant economic advancements. He contended that the government's development agenda requires additional time and that President Ruto should be granted a second term to finalize and solidify his programs.

The event, hosted by Narok East MP Hon. Aramat, also saw the MP caution officials responsible for hiring for the Standard Gauge Railway (SGR) project. Aramat insisted that local residents must be prioritized for employment, warning that failure to do so would compel the community to take action and pursue alternative solutions to address the issue.

Why it mattersOpposition leaders are directly affected by the call to cease criticism of the IEBC, which could impact their electoral strategies and public messaging.
