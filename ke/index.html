Parliament Demands Answers on Stalled Housing Projects

Lawmakers are pressing government officials to explain delays and provide updates on stalled affordable housing initiatives across the country.

Members of Parliament have called on the government to account for delays in the Affordable Housing Programme, demanding detailed updates on stalled projects. The National Assembly Departmental Committee on Housing, Urban Planning and Public Works has challenged the State Department for Housing and Urban Development and the Affordable Housing Board to explain when these projects will be completed and handed over to beneficiaries.

The State Department cited procurement challenges, limited contractor capacity, project management issues, and the transition to an electronic government procurement system as reasons for the delays. Principal Secretary Charles Hinga acknowledged that the program's scale exposed weaknesses in local contractor capabilities and public procurement systems, but stated that lessons learned would improve future implementations. He also noted instances where contractors lacked the financial or technical ability to begin construction after winning tenders.

The committee, chaired by Joseph Tonui, requested a comprehensive status report on stalled projects, including specific challenges and corrective measures. Committee members emphasized the need for project-specific information to understand the reasons for the halts and assess the adequacy of proposed solutions. Calls were also made for stronger monitoring and evaluation mechanisms to ensure projects meet timelines and standards.

Why it mattersParliamentarians are affected because they need to explain project delays and potential mismanagement to constituents and beneficiaries of the affordable housing program.

Brother's Assault Kills Man; Angry Mob Burns House

A 56-year-old man died from injuries sustained in an alleged family dispute, prompting villagers to torch the suspect's home.

Gabriel Makokha, 56, died on August 27 after being assaulted on August 20 in Chenjeni Village, Bungoma County. Police reported that Alfred Makokha, the victim's brother, allegedly attacked Gabriel with a piece of wood and a metal rod following a family disagreement. The incident was not immediately reported to the police. Gabriel was hospitalized but succumbed to his injuries several days later.

Following Gabriel's death, residents reportedly set fire to a two-room semi-permanent house belonging to Alfred Makokha on August 27, destroying unspecified property. Police are investigating both the alleged assault and the subsequent arson. Items believed to have been used in the attack, a piece of wood and a metal rod, were recovered and are being held at the Sichei Area Assistant Chief’s Office.

The deceased's body was taken to Chebukaka Mission Hospital mortuary, where a postmortem examination revealed a facial wound. Authorities are actively searching for Alfred Khisa Makokha, who remains at large. Investigations into the full circumstances of the death and the arson incident are ongoing.

Why it mattersResidents of Chenjeni Village are affected by the death of Gabriel Makokha and the subsequent destruction of property due to an alleged family dispute and retaliatory arson.

Ruto family associates linked to Sh2.8 billion police uniform deal

Questions surround Nalitex Limited, the new police uniform supplier, whose registered address links to businesses associated with the President's family.

Nalitex Limited, the company contracted to supply new police uniforms, has raised questions due to its registered Nairobi office being untraceable and its postal address being shared with an auditing firm and a property business linked to President William Ruto’s family. The government plans to spend Sh2.8 billion to equip 56,000 officers, with Sh1.2 billion allocated for working uniforms and Sh1.68 billion for ceremonial attire. Nalitex was incorporated in May 2024, shortly before the new uniforms were unveiled.

A search revealed Nalitex's registered office at Hifadhi House in Nairobi could not be located, with other government and logistics firms operating from the building. The company's director and sole shareholder, Selpha Chesimi Temoi, is identified as a Senior Teacher at Naisambu Primary School, raising further questions about her capacity to manage such a contract. Her postal address is shared with audit firm Tela Alusala & Company and serviced apartment business Urban Groove.

The shared postal address links Nalitex to Charles Tela Alusala, an accountant who has co-invested with the Ruto family in Amaco Insurance. Public records also associate Rachel Ruto and Charlene Ruto with Urban Groove apartments. While these links exist, no evidence indicates direct ownership or financial interest of the Ruto family in Nalitex itself.

Why it mattersInvestors and the public are affected by questions surrounding the procurement process and the potential for conflicts of interest in a significant government contract.

Nairobi Hospital Board Reconstituted, Onyambu Remains Chairman

The Kenya Hospital Association has re-elected Dr. Barcley Onyambu as chairman following months of governance disputes and scrutiny.

The Kenya Hospital Association (KHA), owner of The Nairobi Hospital, has reconstituted its Board of Management, with Dr. Barcley Onyambu retaining the chairmanship. The changes follow the KHA Annual General Meeting held on August 25, where Onyambu was re-elected, providing continuity after a period of governance disputes. The new 11-member board includes Samson Mbuthia Kinyanjui as vice chairman and Dr. Agnes Gachoki as chairperson of the Medical Advisory Committee.

This reconstitution occurs amidst a protracted leadership battle involving competing claims over the hospital's chairmanship. A faction previously claimed Onyambu had been removed, a claim the hospital rejected, leading to court involvement. The Senate had also been asked to investigate concerns regarding governance, regulation, and service delivery at the hospital, citing disputes over board elections and financial decisions.

The Nairobi Hospital operates under an unusual ownership structure as a membership-based organisation owned by KHA, rather than having conventional shareholders. Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale previously stated the hospital is member-owned. The board's composition aligns with specific articles within the KHA Articles of Association.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the reconstitution of The Nairobi Hospital's board due to ongoing governance disputes that have placed the institution under intense scrutiny and attracted parliamentary attention.
