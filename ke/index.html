Kenyan cult leader gets 200 years for 191 starvation deaths

Paul Mackenzie was convicted of ordering followers to starve themselves and their children to death to meet Jesus.

Self-proclaimed Kenyan pastor Paul Mackenzie received a 200-year prison sentence on Friday for orchestrating the deaths of 191 people, including 190 children and one adult. The sentencing follows his conviction for inciting followers to starve themselves to death at the sect's base near Malindi. Hundreds of bodies were discovered since 2023 at the Shakahola Forest site.

Mackenzie, a former taxi driver, was found guilty on October 19 of all 191 counts alongside 28 co-accused. He ordered followers to starve their children to death in the forest to "meet Jesus." His lawyer stated that he intends to appeal the conviction and sentence. Mackenzie appeared indifferent in court on Friday.

The court imposed 100 years for homicide charges and another 100 years for starvation charges. 28 accomplices also received lengthy prison terms. Mackenzie faces separate trials for manslaughter and terrorism charges, which he denies.

Why it mattersPaul Mackenzie and 28 accomplices have been sentenced to lengthy prison terms for orchestrating the deaths of 191 people through starvation.

Kenya discharges six Ebola patients

The country has recorded 18 days without a new infection, with one patient remaining under treatment.

Kenya has discharged six Ebola patients, bringing the total number of recoveries to six out of nine confirmed cases since the outbreak began on October 6, 2026. The Ministry of Health announced on November 6 that the last of these six patients tested negative, leaving only one person still receiving treatment. This marks 18 consecutive days without any new infections recorded.

Director-General for Health Dr. Patrick Amoth stated that all nine confirmed cases were linked to a single transmission chain, with no new infections reported since October 19, 2026. He attributed the progress to early detection, isolation, treatment, contact tracing, and safe burial practices. 66 contacts have completed their 21-day monitoring period, and 52 more are expected to finish by November 11.

Kenya must observe a 42-day countdown after the final patient's discharge before officially declaring the outbreak over. Screening continues at 26 entry points, where over 760,000 travellers have been checked. Emergency operations centres and rapid response teams remain on alert, and the Ministry urges citizens to seek immediate medical attention for symptoms like fever and bleeding.

Kenya to deploy more police, patrol boats on Lake Victoria by December

Government plans to boost security for fishing communities and the blue economy on the lake.

Kenya will deploy additional security officers and patrol boats on Lake Victoria by the end of the year to combat insecurity affecting fishing communities. Principal Secretary Raymond Omollo announced the plan to strengthen surveillance, particularly near Uganda and Tanzania borders, to protect fishermen. The government is coordinating with the Border Patrol Unit and Kenya Coast Guard Service for this deployment.

Omollo stated that increased personnel and boats would enhance safety for fishermen operating on the lake, addressing concerns like incidents around Migingo Island. He confirmed the government's commitment to improving safety in Kenyan waters before the year concludes. The government is also investing in nine modern fish landing sites around Lake Victoria to improve fish handling and reduce post-harvest losses.

These facilities, including cold storage and hygienic handling areas, aim to increase fishermen's earnings. The Homa Bay facility is complete, with others under construction at Usenge, Mbita, Kendu Bay, and Sena. The blue economy strategy also encompasses water transport and trade, with a public ferry in Kisumu nearing completion to improve connectivity.

Why it mattersFishermen and business communities around Lake Victoria will experience improved safety and infrastructure, boosting incomes.

Kipchimchim Group opens green tea factory in Nandi

The Sangalo Green Tea Factory will process over 70,000kg of green leaf daily for premium international markets.

Kipchimchim Group has opened the Sangalo Green Tea Factory in Nandi County, Kenya, dedicated to processing green orthodox tea. The factory, operated by the group's Tea Division, can process more than 70,000kg of green leaf each day. This new facility expands Kipchimchim's production beyond black CTC and black orthodox teas to target international demand for specialty green tea.

The company stated that the Sangalo factory will allow African tea to compete internationally based on quality. It will also provide smallholder farmers supplying green leaf direct access to premium specialty tea buyers. Kipchimchim Group expects this development to improve farmer returns by connecting them to higher-value markets for specialty tea.

Sangalo is projected to create direct technical jobs at the factory and indirect employment in the agricultural sector. The investment aims to support socio-economic development and infrastructure in nearby communities. Kipchimchim Group, founded in 1999 and based in Kericho, supports approximately 54,000 smallholder tea farmers.

Why it mattersSmallholder outgrowers supplying green leaf will have direct access to premium specialty tea buyers, improving their returns.
