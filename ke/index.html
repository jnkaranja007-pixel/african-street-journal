Kenyan government urges 1.5 million flood-prone residents to move to higher ground

Residents of Nairobi's Mathare informal settlement are clearing rubbish from the Mathare River ahead of heavy El Niño rains forecast for October to December.

Residents of Nairobi's Mathare informal settlement are preparing for heavy El Niño rains, which Kenya's Meteorological Service has warned could fall between October and December. The government has urged around 1.5 million people living in flood-prone areas to move to higher ground. The warning comes nearly two years after floods in 2024 swept away homes, destroyed livelihoods and claimed lives.

Along the Mathare River, residents are clearing rubbish and debris to reduce the risk of flooding. Victor Ojoo, who lost his home and livestock in the previous floods, said residents began the river clean-up because the 2024 rains affected them badly, and that garbage in the river is the first thing that harms residents when it rains.

Nairobi County has identified potential evacuation centres and plans to position rescue personnel, food and other emergency supplies in vulnerable areas before flooding occurs. The government says it is improving access to affected communities and making sure rescue teams and essential supplies are available ahead of the rains.

Why it mattersResidents in flood-prone areas must decide whether to move to higher ground, and Nairobi County is positioning rescue teams, food and supplies in vulnerable areas before flooding occurs.

Six killed as building collapses near Nairobi

An eight-story under-construction building collapsed on Friday, killing six people and sparking a search for survivors.

Six people died when an eight-story building under construction collapsed near Kenya's capital on Friday. Emergency teams are searching the rubble for survivors of the incident, which occurred on November 13, 2026.

The collapse followed a similar deadly incident in Nairobi's Eastleigh area less than a month prior. Rescue operations are currently underway at the site of the collapsed building.

This latest building collapse adds to a series of construction-related tragedies. The exact cause of the collapse has not yet been determined.

Nairobi Water restores supply after pipeline repair

Emergency repairs on a vandalised pipeline from the Kabete Water Treatment Plant are complete, but full pressure may take time.

Nairobi Water has asked residents to be patient as water supply gradually returns after emergency repairs to a vandalised pipeline. The Nairobi City Water and Sewerage Company announced that the Kabete Distribution Mains along Waiyaki Way have been reopened following the repairs. The company stated that restoring adequate pressure throughout the distribution system will take time.

The company noted that residents in elevated areas and those at the network's far ends may experience delays in full supply restoration. Major health facilities including Kenyatta National Hospital, Nairobi Hospital, and Mbagathi Hospital are among the institutions affected by the interruption. The restoration exercise covers numerous Nairobi neighbourhoods such as Westlands, Parklands, Lavington, Riverside, Kilimani, Kileleshwa, Upper Hill, Langata, Karen, Kibera, Nairobi West, and South C.

The pipeline was vandalised, leading to the interruption of water distribution. The company is working to ensure the system stabilises and pressure builds back to normal levels. Residents are advised to conserve water during this period.

Why it mattersResidents in several Nairobi neighbourhoods and major health facilities are experiencing delayed water supply following pipeline vandalism and subsequent repairs.

Business · 2026-11-14

Ruto promises to dual Nairobi-Mombasa Highway within one year

President William Ruto pledged to expand Kenya's busiest transport corridor, a multibillion-shilling project expected to transform transport and reduce costs.

President William Ruto has pledged to dual the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway within the next year, announcing his administration has a plan for the multibillion-shilling infrastructure investment. Speaking in Mombasa, Ruto urged patience as the government finalises arrangements for the expansion of the 461-kilometre highway, which he stated would transform the nation.

The proposed expansion aims to upgrade the existing two-lane highway into a four-lane dual carriageway, enhancing road safety, reducing travel time, and easing congestion. This critical economic artery links the Port of Mombasa to Nairobi and serves as a major transit route for cargo to Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, and the Democratic Republic of Congo.

The National Treasury is pursuing a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) arrangement for the project, with assessments covering engineering, traffic, environmental impact, costs, and financial viability. This commitment comes amid a broader infrastructure programme, including the Sh191 billion Rironi-Mau Summit highway project.

Why it mattersThe government will dual the Nairobi-Mombasa Highway within one year, a project expected to lower the cost of transporting goods across the country and the wider East African region.
