Somali national to be repatriated after passport fraud conviction

A court ordered the repatriation of a Somali national convicted of obtaining Kenyan identity and passport documents fraudulently.

A Somali national, Mohamud Mohammed Roble, will be repatriated to Somalia after being convicted of passport fraud in Kenya. Roble pleaded guilty to three charges at the JKIA Law Courts, including unlawful presence, making a false declaration for a Kenyan passport, and presenting a fraudulent identity card. He was fined a total of Sh370,000 for these offenses.

The court fined Roble Sh150,000 for unlawful presence, Sh150,000 for the false declaration, and Sh70,000 for the identity card offense, with imprisonment as an alternative to payment. Detectives from the Immigration and Citizen Services Investigations Unit arrested Roble at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport after immigration officials handed over the case. Investigations revealed he had secured Kenyan registration documents before using false declarations to obtain a Kenyan passport.

The court ordered the surrender of Roble's passport to the Director General of Immigration Services for destruction. This case comes amid mounting scrutiny of Kenyan identity instruments, including national identity cards and passports, due to several violations.

Why it mattersA Somali national convicted of passport fraud in Kenya will be repatriated to Somalia after paying fines totalling Sh370,000.

Kenya launches pesticide residue monitoring framework

New system aims to improve food safety and boost horticultural exports by coordinating testing and data.

Kenya has launched its National Pesticide Residue Monitoring Framework (NPRMF) to coordinate pesticide residue testing and data analysis across the agricultural sector. The framework, developed with technical support from CABI's PlantwisePlus programme, aims to ensure food safety compliance and improve access to domestic and export markets. The initiative was developed over two years with input from various government bodies, laboratories, exporters, and county governments.

Agriculture Secretary James Wanjohi stated at the October 15 launch in Nairobi that food control is a multisectoral effort, emphasizing the importance of farm-to-fork collaboration. This framework matters for exporters as Kenya’s horticultural exports have faced rejections in Europe due to exceeding pesticide residue limits, particularly for beans and peas. The coordinated system will provide a single reference for testing data, helping exporters demonstrate compliance and allowing regulators to identify issues before shipments are rejected.

A pilot phase conducted between May and June 2025 in five counties identified capacity gaps and informed the final framework. The government acknowledges that sustained funding is required for equipment, training, and sample collection to make the system operational. The framework's success will depend on whether the monitoring data leads to actionable guidance for farmers or simply serves as a compliance record.

Why it mattersKenyan farmers and horticultural exporters are directly affected by this framework, which aims to prevent rejections of their produce in export markets due to pesticide residue limits.

Kenya launches national coffee flavour atlas project

Around 500 coffee samples from 34 counties will be analysed to map regional taste profiles by 2027.

Kenya is creating a national coffee flavour atlas to document the country's diverse coffee profiles, with findings expected in 2027. The Kenya Coffee Flavor Map initiative will analyse approximately 500 samples from 34 coffee-growing counties, connecting flavour characteristics with regional origins, varieties, and production practices. Kenyan and international coffee cuppers will participate in blind sensory evaluations, with results made freely available to the industry.

The initiative, supported by the Government of Kenya, the Coffee Board of Kenya, and the FAO, aims to provide a shared reference for understanding regional differences and communicating coffee characteristics. Professional cupping sessions will be held during the Kenya Coffee Flavor & Origin Expo in Nairobi from November 14-16. Torch Coffee co-founder Marty Pollack stated the project encourages collaboration between Kenyan professionals and international sensory experts.

The atlas will feature regional flavour profiles, visual maps, and a searchable sensory database. This project coincides with growing domestic interest in coffee, as the number of coffee shops in Kenya rose from 231 in 2019 to 791 in 2023. The findings will be accessible to producers, exporters, buyers, roasters, and educators.

Why it mattersKenyan coffee producers and exporters will gain a tool to better communicate the unique qualities of their beans to international buyers.

Kenya and IFAD sign $2.08 million climate resilience grant

The funding will integrate climate information systems into agricultural value chains to help farmers manage climate risks.

Kenya and the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) have signed a 2.08 million US dollar grant to boost climate resilience in agriculture. Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi signed the Climate Smart Agribusiness Partnerships for Resilience (CSAPR) agreement with IFAD's new Country Director, Matteo Marchisio, in Nairobi.

The funding, provided by the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation through IFAD, will support the Integrated Natural Resources Management Programme (INReMP). This initiative aims to integrate climate information systems into agricultural value chains, helping farmers and agribusinesses anticipate and manage climate-related risks.

The programme is expected to improve agricultural productivity by enabling producers to incorporate climate information into their farming and business decisions. Mbadi reaffirmed the government's commitment to deepening cooperation with IFAD, while Marchisio pledged to strengthen the partnership.

Why it mattersKenyan farmers will receive support to use climate data for better decision-making and improved productivity.

Kenya tea factories pay lower bonuses despite higher auction prices

Reduced leaf production and increased monthly payments explain the drop in farmer payouts for the 2025/26 bonus year.

Kenyan tea farmers received smaller bonuses in the 2025/26 bonus year, even though average auction prices in US dollars rose by 3% to USD 2.23 per kilogramme. Many factories paid less despite a stable shilling and higher sales volumes, with Rukuriri factory reducing its bonus from KES 57.50 to KES 50. This occurred as monthly payments increased from KES 25 to KES 30.

EBC Financial Group analyst David Precious stated that the bonus is calculated after costs and monthly payments, meaning higher interim payouts reduce the final bonus. Leaf deliveries fell by 4.34% in the first 11 months of the financial year, with national output down significantly in January and February due to dry weather. Fuel costs also rose, peaking at KES 242.92 a litre in May.

Despite the lower bonus, total farmer pay may not have decreased due to earlier monthly disbursements. The Kenya Tea Development Agency (KTDA) had previously attributed bonus reductions to lower prices and exchange rates, but these factors improved or held steady in 2025/26. Output levels recovered from March onwards, exceeding 2025 levels.

Why it mattersKenyan tea farmers are directly affected by lower bonus payouts, impacting their income despite increased auction prices.
