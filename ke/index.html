Kenya sends condolences to Poland after Krakow shooting

Six people died and 16 were injured in a market square attack on Saturday, November 7.

Kenya has conveyed its condolences to Poland following a shooting in Krakow that killed six people and injured 16. Foreign Affairs Principal Secretary Korir Sing’Oei expressed Kenya’s solidarity with the Polish people after the Saturday attack in Krakow’s Main Market Square.

Sing’Oei stated Kenya was saddened by the incident and stood with Poland as the country mourns the victims, calling it a terror attack. The shooting occurred shortly before 3 pm local time on Saturday, November 7, when a gunman opened fire from his vehicle. The six victims included four Polish nationals, a British man, and an Austrian woman, with 16 others injured, including two police officers.

Polish prosecutors have opened an investigation into the incident as a suspected terrorist offence, with authorities examining the suspect’s possible motivation. Investigators recovered 11 firearms from the scene and the suspect’s home. The suspect, who had no criminal record and held a firearms permit, was shot by police and remained in critical condition.

Kenya to isolate preacher Paul Mackenzie in prison

Interior CS Kipchumba Murkomen vows strict custody to prevent radicalisation of inmates following Shakahola massacre conviction.

Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen announced on Sunday that controversial preacher Paul Mackenzie will be held in exclusive custody to prevent him from radicalising other prisoners. Murkomen stated that the government would provide a secluded area for Mackenzie, who was recently sentenced to 200 years for his role in the Shakahola deaths, to serve his sentence. This measure follows intelligence reports that Mackenzie had attempted to radicalise fellow inmates and police officers.

The decision aims to stop the preacher from spreading extremist religious teachings or influencing vulnerable prisoners, with concerns that they might adopt his methods and establish similar movements. Justice Diana Kavedza-Mochache sentenced Mackenzie to 100 years for 183 counts of murder by starvation and eight counts of direct homicide, to be served consecutively. 28 co-accused also received sentences ranging from 10 to 160 years.

The Shakahola tragedy, which involved the deaths of 191 people including 190 children, came to light in 2023 when mass graves were discovered in Kilifi County. Murkomen commended the ODPP, DCI, and the Judiciary for securing the conviction, asserting that the government will take firm measures against religious extremism while safeguarding freedom of worship. Mackenzie faces separate proceedings for other alleged offences.

Kenya police seize 454kg of meth

Operation targeting international drug networks leads to three arrests and $27m worth of narcotics.

Kenyan police seized 454kg of methamphetamine valued at $27m in an operation against international drug trafficking networks. The haul was discovered in Msambweni on the Kenyan coast.

Three suspects, two Tanzanians and one Ugandan, were arrested following an intelligence-led operation involving Kenyan and Tanzanian anti-narcotics officers. Police also seized two vehicles and a motorcycle.

The investigation began after a suspect was arrested in Tanzania and handed over to Kenyan authorities. The four suspects are in custody awaiting arraignment, and police state the operation is ongoing.
