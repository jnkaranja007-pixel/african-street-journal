50,000 Ethiopians flee to Kenya amid clashes

UNHCR reports up to 50,000 people have crossed the border into Kenya seeking safety since October 22.

Up to an estimated 50,000 Ethiopians have crossed into Kenya seeking safety since October 22, according to the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR). The influx is driven by ongoing clashes on the Ethiopian side of the border. UNHCR is monitoring the situation at the Moyale border crossing, with registration and verification processes underway.

Displaced individuals are staying with relatives, host communities, or in temporary shelters like schools and churches. Fatuma Ahmed, a displaced person in Al Huda camp, shared that she fled after her house was burnt and she was separated from her family, finding safety and basic necessities like water and shelter in Kenya. UNHCR emergency teams in Moyale are coordinating with the Kenyan government, including the Department of Refugee Services, and other humanitarian partners for relief efforts.

Humanitarian partners, including UNHCR, UNICEF, and WFP, have jointly requested $8.9 million to provide lifesaving resources for up to 50,000 people. Urgent needs include food, safe drinking water, sanitation, and healthcare. UNHCR representative in Kenya, Fatima Mohammed-Cole, thanked the Kenyan government for its response and highlighted the efforts to prevent disease outbreaks among the large number of arrivals.

Why it mattersUp to 50,000 Ethiopians are seeking refuge in Kenya, straining resources and requiring humanitarian aid.

Maraga accuses Ruto of hypocrisy over Ebola quarantine U-turn

Kenya begins a 42-day countdown to Ebola-free status after discharging its last patient and releasing final contacts from monitoring.

Former Chief Justice David Maraga has accused President William Ruto of hypocrisy over the government's position on Ebola quarantine facilities. He criticised Health Cabinet Secretary Aden Duale's Wednesday announcement that Kenya would not serve as a quarantine destination for any other jurisdiction, saying the government had earlier defended hosting a facility for foreign nationals exposed to Ebola.

Maraga pointed to the June protests against a planned 50-bed isolation centre for US citizens in Laikipia, where two young Kenyans were reportedly shot dead by police. In a statement on Thursday he said Ruto had sold out Kenya's safety, sovereignty and dignity to serve American interests. The Independent Policing Oversight Authority has documented the deaths but has not publicly updated the status of its investigations.

The Ministry of Health announced that Kenya had discharged its last Ebola patient and released the final 11 contacts after the 21-day observation period. The country now begins a 42-day countdown to being declared Ebola-free, provided no new cases are recorded. The first imported Bundibugyo virus disease case was confirmed on October 6.

Why it mattersResidents of Laikipia and Kenyans facing the government's shifting quarantine stance are left with unresolved questions about police accountability for the June deaths.

Kenya seeks EU election observers for 2027 polls

President Ruto also requested European support to remove Kenya from money laundering watchlists.

Kenya is seeking election observers from the European Union to support its upcoming 2027 general elections. President William Ruto made this appeal, also requesting European backing to help Kenya exit international money laundering watchlists. The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) has given political parties a 14-day ultimatum to comply with new guidelines, with 271 days remaining until the polls.

The IEBC's directive affects 105 political parties that met a previous deadline. Further guidelines will be issued after the current ultimatum expires. President Ruto's administration is also seeking support for African Union peacekeeping operations in Somalia.

The push for election observers and financial reforms comes as Kenya prepares for the 2027 electoral cycle. The country aims to strengthen its electoral processes and improve its international financial standing.

Why it mattersKenya's electoral integrity and financial reputation are at stake ahead of the 2027 elections.

Sifuna's Linda Mwananchi faces court challenge before Friday's delegates conference

A petitioner asks the Political Parties Disputes Tribunal to suspend the November 13 meeting that would formalise the party's transition from The Equitable Party.

The Linda Mwananchi Movement is set to formalise its transition from The Equitable Party (TEP) at a National Delegates Conference on Friday, November 13, 2026. The meeting will consider a proposed change of the party's name and ratify other decisions needed to complete the transition.

Emmanuel Wellingtone Khamala, who describes himself as a registered TEP member, has filed a petition before the Political Parties Disputes Tribunal seeking to stop the meeting. Through his lawyer, he argues that members were excluded from key decisions, that leadership structures were formed without elections, and that delegates were not drawn from duly elected party structures.

The complaint names TEP's National Executive Committee, its National Elections Board, the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission, the Office of the Registrar of Political Parties and the Political Parties Liaison Committee as respondents. Sifuna has been named as an interested party, and Khamala wants the conference suspended until his complaint is heard and determined.

Why it mattersRegistered TEP members and delegates from each county face a possible suspension of Friday's conference, which would delay the party's name change and leadership ratification.
