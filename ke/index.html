Wetangula Warns Politicians Against Interfering With IEBC

National Assembly Speaker Moses Wetangula cautioned politicians against undermining the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) ahead of the 2027 General Election.

National Assembly Speaker Moses Wetangula has urged politicians to refrain from interfering with the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC), stating that such actions could damage public trust in the upcoming 2027 General Election. Speaking in Kakamega County, Wetangula emphasized that the IEBC is an independent constitutional body that must be allowed to operate without political intimidation or influence, particularly concerning its procurement processes for election technology.

His remarks follow allegations by former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua, who claimed the tender for an Integrated Elections Management System (IEMS) was designed to favor South Korean firm Miru Systems Co. Ltd. Gachagua also alleged that IEBC Chairperson Erastus Ethekon was gifted a Ksh200 million house to influence election outcomes. The IEBC has denied these claims, stating the procurement is ongoing and transparent, and has challenged accusers to provide evidence.

Wetangula used a football analogy to stress that politicians seeking office should not attempt to control electoral officials, rules, or the conduct of elections. He called on IEBC officials to remain steadfast, guided by the Constitution and law, and resist political pressure, asserting that politicians should not dictate procurement decisions for items like ballot papers or election kits.

Why it mattersPoliticians and electoral officials are affected by these statements regarding the integrity and independence of the IEBC's procurement processes and its role in the 2027 General Election.

Ruto Raises Police Constable Basic Salary to Sh29,360

President William Ruto announced a three-phase review of police remuneration, increasing entry-level basic pay and addressing housing shortages for security officers.

President William Ruto announced an increase in the entry-level basic salary for police constables, raising it from Sh21,645 to Sh29,360. This adjustment is part of a completed three-phase review of police remuneration aimed at improving the welfare and working conditions of security officers. Salaries across other police ranks have also been adjusted upwards.

The government is also addressing the shortage of police housing through the National Institutional Housing Programme, which includes 47,464 housing units across 165 projects. Over 2,000 housing units are complete, with more than 9,500 units under construction, intended to ease accommodation challenges and improve working conditions.

Ruto stated that better remuneration and access to decent housing are critical for boosting police morale and enabling officers to effectively discharge their responsibilities. These measures are part of broader government efforts to modernise the security sector and enhance the professionalism and operational capacity of the National Police Service. A new health cover under the Social Health Authority also enrolls over 138,000 police officers.

Why it mattersPolice constables are directly affected by the salary increase from Sh21,645 to Sh29,360 and the ongoing housing program.

Sawe Withdraws From Berlin Marathon Due to Injury

Kenya's world record holder Sabastian Sawe will miss the September 27 race, ending hopes of a repeat performance after his historic London Marathon run.

Kenyan marathon runner Sabastian Sawe has withdrawn from the upcoming Berlin Marathon due to an injury, organizers announced. The 31-year-old defending champion was expected to compete on September 27 but will now delay his return to racing as he recovers.

Sawe, who set a record-eligible marathon time of 1:59:30 at the London Marathon in April, was the first man to run under two hours. He also won the Berlin Marathon last year with a time of 2:02:16, making his absence a significant development for the men's competition.

The withdrawal leaves the men's race open, with the women's event expected to feature Ethiopia's Tigst Assefa, who recently set a new women's world record. Sawe expressed disappointment but thanked organizers and fans for their support.

Why it mattersSawe's injury means he will not compete in the Berlin Marathon, affecting the race's competitive field and delaying his return to professional running.

294 Graduates Complete Tourism Fund Upskilling Program

The Eldoret National Polytechnic has graduated 294 trainees from a two-week program focused on enhancing skills in the tourism and hospitality sector, including content creation.

A total of 294 individuals have graduated from the Eldoret National Polytechnic after participating in a two-week upskilling initiative funded by the Tourism Fund. The program trained 262 participants in the Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) and 32 in content creation, alongside 52 hotel managers and owners, aiming to bolster the tourism and hospitality sector's capacity.

The initiative is designed to equip Kenyan youth and tourism workers with practical skills to improve their employability and productivity, thereby supporting a stable tourism industry. According to Tourism Fund Board member Patrick Ngere, over 8,000 workers have already benefited from similar upskilling programs, with more partnerships planned with TVET institutions.

This upskilling effort is part of broader strategies to strengthen Kenya's tourism workforce and aligns with the Government’s Bottom-Up Economic Transformation Agenda (BETA). The Tourism Fund also supports students pursuing tourism-related courses and professionals seeking to advance their careers through its training revolving fund.

Why it mattersInvestors in Kenya's tourism sector may see improved service delivery and capacity due to the upskilling of 294 workers and managers.
