Politics · 2026-11-02

Kuria questions Gachagua's US relocation threat

Former CS Moses Kuria criticises remarks by former DP Rigathi Gachagua about moving to America if President Ruto wins 2027.

Former Cabinet Secretary Moses Kuria has criticised former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua for stating he would move to the United States if President William Ruto wins the 2027 General Election. Kuria questioned Gachagua's remarks, made during an interview on Kiririmbi, arguing that political leaders should consider Kenyans without the means to leave the country. Kuria posted his criticism on X, calling Gachagua's statement disturbing.

Kuria challenged Gachagua's assertion that divisions within the Mt Kenya region could harm the opposition's 2027 prospects. He suggested Gachagua was avoiding the difficulties of managing a coalition of opposition leaders with diverse interests, likening it to a puzzle of transporting a goat, leopard, and grass across a river. Kuria stated that a proposed alliance involving Kalonzo Musyoka, Edwin Sifuna, and Fred Matiang’i would face significant challenges due to competing ambitions.

The exchange occurs as opposition leaders consolidate support for the 2027 election. Gachagua has advocated for opposition unity to challenge President Ruto's re-election bid. His recent remarks have sparked debate about opposition cohesion and the challenges of building an alliance. This criticism highlights emerging disagreements within the opposition and the ongoing contest for political influence in the Mt Kenya region, following Gachagua's fallout with President Ruto.

Why it mattersFormer Cabinet Secretary Moses Kuria and former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua are publicly disagreeing over political strategy and potential outcomes for the 2027 Kenyan General Election.

Politics · 2026-11-02

Kenyans sue over Bayern-Ajax match funding

Six citizens want the High Court to force the government to reveal financial details of the January 5, 2027 football match.

Six Kenyans have filed a petition at the High Court in Nairobi seeking to block the government from using public money for a football match between Bayern Munich and Ajax. The match is scheduled for January 5, 2027, at Talanta Stadium. The petitioners, led by Erick Mankuyu, want the court to compel the government to disclose all financial arrangements for the event.

The petitioners argue that the government has not been transparent about sponsorship deals and potential public financial commitments for the game. President William Ruto announced the match, stating it would be funded through corporate partnerships. However, the petitioners believe assurances are insufficient without full disclosure of government involvement and potential taxpayer exposure.

The lawsuit names the Attorney General, the Sports Cabinet Secretary, and the National Treasury Cabinet Secretary as respondents. The petitioners are asking the court to prevent the release of public funds and the use of public resources, including security and transport, until financial transparency is achieved.

Why it mattersSix Kenyan citizens are concerned about potential undisclosed use of public funds and resources for a football match.

Education · 2026-11-02

25,849 candidates start Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education exams in Kisumu

Kenya National Examinations Council assures integrity of national tests as 246 schools begin examinations on Monday.

The 2026 Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) examinations began in Kisumu County on Monday, November 2, with 25,849 candidates registered to sit the tests in 246 schools.

Kenya National Examinations Council (KNEC) Chairman Professor Julius Nyabundi assured candidates and parents that adequate measures are in place to safeguard the exams' integrity. He oversaw the opening of the examination container at the Kisumu Central Deputy County Commissioner’s office and reported no irregularities so far.

Kisumu County Director of Education Magare Onduso confirmed adequate preparations and security arrangements, including two security officers per school, to prevent malpractice. This is the second-last KCSE examination under the current system.

Why it mattersStudents in Kisumu County are taking their national secondary school examinations.

Agriculture · 2026-11-02

Taita Taveta targets US$4.3M potato industry growth

The county government aims to boost production by improving seed access, farmer training, and market links.

Taita Taveta County plans to grow its Irish potato industry, currently worth US$4.3 million annually, to increase farmer incomes and create jobs. The county cultivates 750 hectares of potatoes, producing 11,250 tonnes each year. Farm-gate prices average US$0.39 per kilogram, offering economic benefits.

The county government is enhancing the sector by improving access to certified seeds, farmer training, research, and reliable markets. Through the National Agricultural Value Chain Development Project, 21 farmer groups received support for seed potatoes, and nine demonstration plots were established. Agripreneur Gift Kisombe connected farmers in Mbale Ward with a seed supplier who bought KSh131,600 worth of certified Markies seed potatoes.

The County Department of Agriculture, with NAVCDP and AGRICOS, is introducing Irish potato farming in Taveta Sub-County and plans to train stakeholders in sustainable practices. The Kenya Agricultural and Livestock Research Organization is identifying suitable potato varieties for the county's diverse ecological zones. County Executive Committee Member for Agriculture, Dawson Katuu Mzenge, stated that potato farming can increase household incomes and strengthen food security.

Why it mattersFarmers in Taita Taveta County will benefit from improved access to quality seeds, training in better farming techniques, and stronger market connections, increasing their household incomes.
