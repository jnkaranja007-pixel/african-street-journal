Kenyan family protest murder suspect extradition delay

Agnes Wanjiru's relatives say postponement until May 2027 is unacceptable and they were not informed.

The family of Agnes Wanjiru, a Kenyan woman allegedly murdered 14 years ago, protested the postponement of an extradition hearing for former British soldier Robert James Purkiss. The hearing, originally scheduled for next month, has been delayed until May 2027. Purkiss, accused of killing the 21-year-old mother in 2012, denies the allegation and is fighting extradition from the UK to Kenya.

Wanjiru’s niece, Esther Njoki, stated the family learned of the delay through the UK embassy and received no official explanation, calling it an exploitation of their patience. The family had already made travel arrangements for the hearing, which would have marked the case entering its 15th year without a trial. A UK court is expected to announce the reasons for the delay next week.

Agnes Wanjiru disappeared in March 2012 near a British army training camp in Nanyuki, about 200km north of Nairobi, and her body was found nearly three months later in a septic tank. A Kenyan inquest concluded in 2019 that she was unlawfully killed by British soldiers. In September 2025, a Kenyan High Court issued an arrest warrant for a British national. The then British Defence Secretary John Healey had pledged government support for justice during a visit to Kenya last year.

Why it mattersThe family of Agnes Wanjiru is seeking justice for her alleged murder by a former British soldier, and the delay in extradition proceedings impacts their pursuit of answers.

rights groups sue state over pre-election violence

Six human rights organisations want the High Court to order investigations into alleged organised attacks on political gatherings.

Six human rights organisations have filed a lawsuit at the High Court in Nairobi seeking to compel state agencies to investigate alleged organised attacks on political, civic, and religious gatherings. The Kenya Human Rights Commission, Katiba Institute, and the Independent Medico-Legal Unit are among the petitioners accusing the state of failing to prevent, investigate, and prosecute politically motivated violence ahead of the 2027 General Election.

The petitioners want the court to order the Director-General of the National Intelligence Service and the Inspector-General of Police to investigate these attacks and the networks behind them. They also seek orders to preserve evidence like CCTV footage and communication records, and to forward investigation files to the Director of Public Prosecutions. The groups want a joint report within 60 days detailing investigation progress.

The lawsuit also asks the court to direct the state to establish a dedicated, funded multi-agency task force within 30 days to investigate and prosecute politically motivated gang violence. This team would include personnel from the National Police Service and the Directorate of Criminal Investigations, working with the DPP. The petitioners seek to ensure accountability for violence that undermines constitutional freedoms.

Why it mattersSix human rights groups are suing the Kenyan state to force investigations into political violence that threatens fundamental freedoms before the 2027 elections.

ADM and Concern Worldwide US expand Kenya LEAF programme

The initiative now supports 17,500 farmers in Tana River County with climate-smart practices and improved market access.

ADM and Concern Worldwide US have expanded their LEAF programme in Kenya's Tana River County, now reaching 17,500 farmers. The programme, in its fourth phase since 2019, provides technical training, better access to inputs, and stronger market connections to help farmers adopt climate-smart practices. Farmers in the programme have produced over 2,000 metric tons of crops and show greater resilience to environmental challenges.

Malnutrition rates in LEAF-supported communities have fallen to 7.3%, significantly lower than the Tana River County average of 11.5%. Since January, the programme has supplied irrigation equipment to over 450 farmers, covering about 330 acres, and established or strengthened 18 fruit tree nurseries. Community health promoters have also screened over 5,100 children for malnutrition and provided nutrition education.

The programme's success is measured by malnutrition rates and crop tonnage, not commercial export volumes. While the Galole processing plant has a mango pulp capacity of 2,880 metric tons, fresh exports require different certifications. Cecelia Achieng, a farmer from Manyatta Village, stated that the programme has helped her village access income and knowledge from production to market.

Why it mattersFarmers in Tana River County are gaining access to climate-smart practices, irrigation equipment, and training to improve crop production and resilience.

Health · 2026-10-28

Kenya confirms nine Ebola cases

The government expects the outbreak to be declared over in less than two months if containment efforts hold.

Kenya has confirmed nine cases of Ebola and two deaths since announcing its first case three weeks ago. The government has trained nearly 5,000 healthcare workers to manage the disease. The first 20 contacts of the initial patient have been released from quarantine.

Health Director-General Patrick Amoth stated that the focus remains on reaching the end of the outbreak, describing the release of quarantined individuals as a "milestone worth celebrating". The outbreak will only be declared over 42 days after the last confirmed patient tests negative twice.

Officials anticipate the outbreak could be declared over in under two months, provided no new cases emerge. This projection depends on the status of the 98 individuals still in quarantine, who are due for release next week. The UN has provided millions to support Kenya's fight against Ebola.

Why it mattersKenya's health system is managing an Ebola outbreak, affecting nine individuals and leading to two deaths.

AfricaBlight Network launched in Kenya to fight potato disease

African-led collaboration will identify priority countries and foster joint research for late blight management.

The AfricaBlight Network was officially launched on October 27 in Naivasha, Kenya, during the 13th World Potato Congress. This African-led initiative brings together researchers, agricultural institutions, and potato sector partners to create a unified response against late blight, a destructive disease impacting potato production across the continent.

Dr. Peter VanderZaag, President of the World Potato Congress, inaugurated the network, which aims to share knowledge and conduct joint research. Late blight causes significant crop losses, estimated globally between USD 3 billion and USD 7 billion annually, and increases production costs through heavy fungicide use, which can be 10-25% of a harvest's value.

The network will focus on countries including Kenya, Rwanda, Ethiopia, and Eritrea, seeking to strengthen farmer training and disease surveillance. Partnerships with institutions like Kenya's Agricultural and Livestock Research Organization (KALRO) are central to connecting scientific advances with farmers' needs for effective disease management.

Why it mattersKenyan farmers and others across Africa will benefit from improved management of late blight, a disease that reduces potato yields and increases crop protection costs.
