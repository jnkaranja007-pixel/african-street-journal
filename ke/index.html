Police to Recommend Violent Political Actors to IEBC for Action

The National Police Service will submit names of individuals with a history of promoting political violence to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission for.

The National Police Service (NPS) announced it will recommend individuals deemed unfit to contest elective positions to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) based on their record of promoting or perpetrating political violence. Deputy Inspector General Gilbert Masengeli stated that security agencies are intensifying efforts to prevent politically instigated violence ahead of the 2027 General Election.

This decision follows a recent incident on August 16 where violence disrupted a political rally in Homa Bay County, leading to the arrest of 37 suspected gang members found with crude weapons. During the incident, one police officer died and eight others were injured, including three injured the previous day during arrests along the Kisumu-Kericho Road.

Masengeli, speaking at the launch of the Roadmap to a Peaceful General Election 2027, warned that individuals involved in organizing or sponsoring violence would face firm action. The NPS is pursuing organizers, facilitators, and sponsors of criminal activities, emphasizing investigations, arrests, and crime prevention as key responses.

Why it mattersThis policy directly affects political actors who may be barred from contesting elections and citizens whose safety and participation in political rallies are at risk due to violence.

Experts urge East Africa to convert bioeconomy research into profitable industries

Policymakers, scientists, investors, and entrepreneurs at the Africa Bioeconomy Workshop in Nairobi called for linking agricultural production, research, and innovation to markets.

Experts at the Africa Bioeconomy Workshop in Nairobi are pushing East Africa to transition from bioeconomy research to building profitable industries. The call emphasizes creating jobs and retaining more value from the region’s biological resources, moving beyond exporting raw materials.

Niall O’Connor, Centre Director of the Stockholm Environment Institute (SEI) Africa Centre, stated that the region possesses the necessary resources, research capacity, and entrepreneurial talent to establish a competitive bio-based economy. The 2026 State of the Bioeconomy in Eastern Africa report identifies agricultural residues and organic waste as potential feedstock for various industries.

Dr. Abdou Tenkouano, Director General of the International Centre of Insect Physiology and Ecology (icipe), highlighted agriculture's significant contribution of between 23 and 35 percent of GDP in many sub-Saharan African countries as a major opportunity. He urged governments to strengthen university-industry partnerships to promote innovation and entrepreneurship in agro-processing and bioprocessing.

Why it mattersThis shift affects East African communities by creating local jobs and increasing incomes through value addition to biological resources, rather than exporting raw materials.

Mombasa Court Acquits Clinical Officer in Patient Rape Case

A Mombasa court acquitted a former Pandya Memorial Hospital clinical officer accused of raping a dialysis patient, citing insufficient evidence to prove the case beyond.

Chief Magistrate Lucas Onyina found Dias Juma Wabwile not guilty of rape and an alternative charge of committing an indecent act. Wabwile was accused of unlawfully penetrating an adult patient, identified as M.T.K., at Pandya Memorial Hospital in Mvita Sub-County around five am on January thirty-one, two thousand twenty-five. The complainant died before the hearing began, and no witnesses observed the alleged assault.

The case involved a used condom found in a hospital dustbin, which government analysis confirmed contained Wabwile’s DNA. Wabwile admitted the condom was his, claiming he had sex with his wife at home before work and brought the used condom to the hospital for disposal. His wife, Nancy Gakii, supported this account, stating they had sex at about three thirty am that morning.

The magistrate questioned Wabwile’s explanation about wrapping the condom in tissue and how the complainant knew where it was discarded. The court noted the patient was connected to a dialysis machine, making disconnection risky, and inferred that Wabwile might have taken advantage of the situation. However, the court stressed this was an inference, not proof, and consent could not be presumed without the complainant's testimony.

Why it mattersThis case affects patients and healthcare providers by highlighting the complexities of legal proceedings in sensitive medical environments and the challenges of proving consent or lack thereof when a complainant is deceased.

Nairobi designer revives traditional hand looms for clothing production

Hillary Mukubwa, founder of Mukabwa studios, uses hand looms to create unique clothing and fabrics, moving away from mass production trends.

In Nairobi, designer Hillary Mukubwa is reintroducing traditional hand looms for clothing production, a departure from modern mass production methods. Mukubwa, who founded Mukabwa studios, stated that he aims to revive old practices where culture lives, viewing hand looms as a way to keep Kenya's cultural heritage alive.

Mukubwa explained that hand looms are traditional and carry a story, producing items with meaning rather than in mass quantities. He feels like an anomaly in returning to these older traditions, noting that modernization often diminishes traditional craftsmanship.

Clients appreciate the individuality and quality of the clothes, which are made from organic cotton. Maylone Ginnette, a client, highlighted the uniqueness of the pieces and the visible effort involved in their creation, preferring them over high street offerings.

Why it mattersThis story affects consumers seeking unique, handcrafted clothing and designers interested in sustainable, traditional production methods.

Kenyan anime fans transform recycled materials into cosplay culture

Over 4,000 anime and manga enthusiasts gathered in Nairobi for the Otamatsuri convention, showcasing elaborate costumes crafted from unconventional materials.

Thousands of anime and manga fans in Nairobi recently transformed recycled materials and craft skills into elaborate costumes for one of Africa’s largest cosplay conventions. The Otamatsuri event, which attracted more than 4,000 attendees, highlights the growing popularity of Japanese pop culture in Kenya, according to organizer Jotham Micah.

For many Kenyan cosplayers, creating these detailed costumes involves finding affordable and unconventional materials. Julius Mokaya, a 34-year-old building caretaker and two-time winner of the convention's costume competition, uses cardboard and other items collected from recycling bins to craft his outfits, as he cannot afford to import expensive costumes.

Micah, co-founder of entertainment company Movie Jabber, stated that anime and manga are increasingly crossing cultural boundaries, providing inspiration and entertainment. The convention featured fans dressed as characters from popular Japanese franchises like One Piece, Naruto, and Gachiakuta, demonstrating a space for creativity, friendship, and self-expression.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects Kenyan anime and manga fans by providing a space for creative expression, community building, and affordable participation in a global subculture.
