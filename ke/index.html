Kenyan judge Phoebe Okowa wins full nine-year ICJ term

Professor Phoebe Okowa secured the required majority votes in both the UN General Assembly and the Security Council.

Kenyan legal scholar Professor Phoebe Okowa has been elected to a full nine-year term as a judge at the International Court of Justice (ICJ). President William Ruto congratulated Okowa, stating her election is a moment of great pride for Kenya and recognizes the country's contribution to international law.

Okowa secured the necessary majority votes in both the United Nations General Assembly and the Security Council during elections held at the UN headquarters in New York on Tuesday, November 3. Her new term runs from February 2027 to February 2036.

She was initially elected in November 2025 to fill a vacancy and previously served on the United Nations International Law Commission since 2021. The ICJ, based in The Hague, Netherlands, is the UN's principal judicial organ, settling legal disputes between states.

Autopsy reveals 86-year-old Kenyan woman died from stab wounds

Dorita Macharia suffered over 20 injuries, including deep cuts to her face and neck.

An autopsy revealed that Dorita Macharia, 86, died from excessive blood loss due to multiple stab wounds to the neck. The examination at Lee Funeral Home on November 4 also found over 20 other injuries, including deep cuts to her face, neck, and arms, and a fracture on her left upper arm. Government pathologist Dr Bernard Midia stated the fatal wounds damaged blood vessels, causing severe bleeding, and indicated a sharp object was used.

The postmortem was attended by Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) detectives, pathologists, and lawyers for the deceased and suspects. Lawyer Philip Murgor, representing the family, said the findings establish the cause of death, allowing investigators to focus on the circumstances and perpetrators. The findings are expected to guide the DCI Gigiri team investigating Macharia's death at her Loresho Ridge Estate home on October 30.

Macharia's granddaughter, Thandie Malaika, her mother Regina Re, her brother Jimmy Macharia, and Immanuel Munyeki were remanded for 10 days by Kibera Law Courts. This allows detectives to complete their investigation into the circumstances of the 86-year-old's death. Investigators are also examining allegations that some relatives may have interfered with the scene by cleaning parts of the house after the body was discovered.

Why it mattersDorita Macharia's death from multiple stab wounds and excessive blood loss is under investigation, with four family members remanded.

ADM opens new Nairobi office to serve East Africa

The global nutrition company aims to connect regional demand with its worldwide origination network.

ADM, a global leader in nutrition and agricultural supply chain solutions, has opened a new destination marketing office in Nairobi, Kenya. The company stated this move reinforces its commitment to serving customers across East Africa through its global supply chain network.

Kenya is recognized as a key trade hub for food and feed ingredients, and the Nairobi office will offer direct access to ADM’s portfolio of food ingredients, feedstuffs, and animal nutrition solutions. Patrick Heijbroek, Vice President, Global Trade and Head of Origination, EMEA, noted that East Africa represents a high-growth market with over 500 million people and rising demand for quality ingredients.

The office will focus on market intelligence, local merchandising, storage, and distribution services. This expansion builds on ADM’s decade-long presence in Kenya and complements existing investments including human nutrition innovation laboratories in Nairobi and a distribution hub in Nigeria. In 2025, ADM's destination marketing volume reached nearly 31 million metric tons.

Why it mattersKenyan farmers and businesses in the food and feed industry will have direct access to ADM's global portfolio and supply chain solutions.

Kenya and Australia discuss farm tech partnership

The two nations are exploring cooperation in research, livestock technology, and youth skills development.

Kenya and Australia are discussing a new partnership to improve agricultural productivity and innovation. The collaboration aims to boost research, livestock technology, and youth skills development in Kenya's farming sector.

Agriculture Cabinet Secretary Sen. Mutahi Kagwe met with Australia's Acting High Commissioner Andrew Barnes to discuss strengthening crop and livestock research. They explored technology transfer, vaccine development, and sexed semen technology, building on existing vaccine exports to 13 countries, including 11 million doses to Uganda.

The partnership also seeks to use Agricultural Training Centres to equip young people with practical farming skills. This initiative is expected to support the modernisation of Kenya's agriculture and increase youth involvement in the sector.

Why it mattersKenyan farmers and young people stand to benefit from enhanced agricultural research, technology adoption, and skills development.

Ethiopians flee clashes, await registration in Kenya

Thousands of displaced people are waiting at Al-huda primary school in Moyale for processing by UNHCR.

Thousands of displaced Ethiopians were expected to be registered in the Kenyan border city of Moyale on Monday, after fleeing clashes between Oromia and Somali region leaders in Ethiopia.

The United Nations refugee agency, UNHCR, has provided temporary international protection status to these individuals, who are currently awaiting processing at Al-huda primary school.

As of October 29, the UN reported that approximately 50,000 people had fled fighting in southern Ethiopia into Kenya, a figure that had jumped from 36,000 just two days prior, according to UNHCR representative for Kenya, Fatima Mohammed-Cole.

Why it mattersDisplaced Ethiopians fleeing conflict in Ethiopia are awaiting registration and aid in Kenya.
