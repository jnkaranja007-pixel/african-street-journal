Kenya holds fuel pump prices with Sh4.6bn subsidy

The Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority says pump prices will not change for 30 days even though the cost of all three fuels rose between September and October.

The Kenyan government has spent Sh4.6 billion from the Petroleum Development Levy Fund to cushion consumers against rising international petroleum prices. The Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA) said in its monthly review on Saturday that pump prices for Super Petrol, Diesel and Kerosene will stay unchanged from November 15 to December 14, 2026.

The decision came despite higher landed costs for all three products between September and October. Diesel rose the most, by 6.73 per cent, with its average landed cost climbing from USD 1,065.38 to USD 1,137.09 per cubic metre. Super Petrol rose 6.11 per cent and Kerosene 0.71 per cent.

EPRA said the Sh4.6 billion intervention would help stabilise pump prices during the latest pricing cycle. Kenyans.co.ke reported that the government has not said whether the fuel subsidy promised by Cabinet Secretary Mbadi is in effect.

Why it mattersMotorists and transport operators pay the same pump prices until December 14, 2026, while the government funds the gap from the levy fund.

Six workers dead as eight-storey building collapses near Nairobi

All 405 workers at the site have been accounted for, but the cause is still under investigation, with formwork failure suspected.

An eight-storey building under construction caved in on the outskirts of Nairobi, Kenya's capital, killing six people and injuring five others. Kenyan authorities said on Friday that the upper section gave way during concreting works, and that all 405 workers at the site had been accounted for.

Patrick Simiyu Were, the county commissioner for the Nairobi East Sub-Region, said preliminary reports pointed to suspected formwork failure as a possible contributing factor. The site has been closed and secured pending structural assessments and investigations by government agencies. Nairobi Regional Police Commander Issa Mohamud said three bodies were recovered in the morning and three more later from the debris, all of them middle-aged men.

The collapse happened at about 9:52 a.m. local time, weeks after an 18-storey building under construction fell on 9th Street in Eastleigh on October 23, killing eight people. Investigations into that collapse are still ongoing, and building experts have repeatedly criticised regulators for weak oversight of construction protocols and routine inspections.

Why it mattersTraders and residents near the closed site will be kept out until engineers finish the structural assessment.

Climate · 2026-11-15

Mathare residents clear river debris ahead of El Niño rains

Kenya's Meteorological Service has warned of potentially intense rainfall between October and December, and residents are clearing their riverside settlement.

Across Mathare, an informal settlement in Nairobi, residents are clearing rubbish and debris from the Mathare River to reduce the risk of flooding from expected El Niño rains. The flood survivors remember the 2024 rains, which overwhelmed homes and swept away property.

Victor Ojoo, a Mathare resident who lost his home and livestock in those floods, said the community started the river clean-ups because the garbage in the river would first affect residents. Before 2024, he said, residents only cleared drainage systems near shops, roads and fields. The government has urged around 1.5 million people living in flood-prone areas to move to higher ground.

Nairobi County has identified potential evacuation centres and plans to position rescue personnel, food and other emergency supplies in vulnerable areas before any flooding occurs. The government says it is improving access to affected communities and ensuring rescue teams and essential supplies are ready before the rains.

Why it mattersAround 1.5 million people in flood-prone areas have been urged to move to higher ground before the rains arrive.
