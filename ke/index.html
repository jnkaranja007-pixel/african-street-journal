Ruto Adds 5,080 Constables, Demands Accountable Policing

President William Ruto urged new recruits to focus on citizen experience as the key measure of police reform, not just equipment or policy changes.

Kenya has inducted 5,080 new Administration Police Service constables, marking the 59th Passing-Out Parade at the National Police College. President William Ruto stated that the success of police reforms will be measured by how officers interact with and protect citizens. The new officers completed nine months of training and will bolster the National Police Service's capacity to address security threats including banditry, terrorism, and organized crime, while also strengthening border presence.

President Ruto challenged the graduating constables to ensure reforms translate into tangible improvements in public service, emphasizing that the true measure of reform lies in the citizen's experience. He stressed that officers must respond when called, enforce laws impartially, reject corruption, and uphold the dignity and rights of all Kenyans. The President highlighted that these officers are assuming their duties during a period of significant transformation within the policing sector.

The government has also implemented a thirty-six percent increase in the entry-level basic salary for police constables, alongside other rank adjustments. Further reforms include expanding police housing, enhancing medical care through Usalama Cover, upgrading equipment, and increasing access to specialized operational assets. Since 2022, the National Police Service has constructed 252 police stations, recruited and trained ten thousand officers, and provided advanced training to over thirty-one thousand serving personnel.

Why it mattersNewly trained police constables will directly affect citizens' daily interactions with law enforcement and the state's capacity to manage security threats.

Nairobi hires 4,500 workers to clear waste before heavy rains

The county government has employed thousands under the Green Army program to tackle the city's growing waste problem and prepare for anticipated El Niño rainfall.

Nairobi's county government has hired approximately 4,500 workers under its Green Army program to clean streets, drainage channels, and public spaces. Governor Johnson Sakaja stated these recruits are on permanent and pensionable terms, marking one of the largest environmental services employment drives in decades. The initiative aims to address both unemployment and the city's deteriorating environment, with Sakaja noting it provides 4,500 households with an income source.

The Green Army is deployed across Nairobi, clearing drainage channels, removing accumulated waste, cleaning estates, and tackling illegal dumping sites. This effort comes as the city grapples with a significant increase in waste, now collecting about 3,000 tonnes daily compared to 1,000 tonnes previously. The county has linked the clean-up campaign to preparations for heavy rains, with drainage clearance being a priority in several areas.

Since April, the county reports clearing over 40 major waste collection points and illegal dumping sites. Specific clean-up operations include removing 4,500 loads of waste in Embakasi South, with 1,538 loads from Kware Bridge and 2,970 from English Press. Other sites like Motherland in Eastleigh South and Kinyago Slums have also seen significant waste removal, alongside drainage clearance in areas such as Dandora, Ruai, and Kibra.

Why it mattersNairobi residents are affected by the increased employment opportunities and the preparatory work to mitigate potential flooding and sanitation issues caused by heavy rains.

Kenya Public Workforce Exceeds 1 Million, Wage Bill Climbs

The nation's public sector employment has reached 1.07 million, increasing pressure on the government's wage bill as costs continue to rise.

Kenya's public workforce has grown to 1.07 million employees, placing increased strain on the government's wage bill. Figures from the Salaries and Remuneration Commission (SRC) show public sector employment rose by 4.6 per cent in 2025, from 1.023 million workers in 2024. The education sector, along with public administration and defense, saw the largest increases in hiring, with the Teachers Service Commission remaining the biggest employer.

The expanding workforce coincides with a projected rise in Kenya's public wage bill to Sh1.287 trillion for the 2025/2026 financial year, up from Sh1.247 trillion the previous year. This increase is attributed to growth in teaching, health, and security employment, alongside salary adjustments. Despite the rise, the wage bill's share of ordinary government revenue has decreased to a projected 40.68 per cent, though it remains above the 35 per cent threshold.

The SRC noted that persistent inflation, with a month-on-month rate of 6.23 per cent in the fourth quarter of 2025/2026, erodes salary purchasing power and may increase demands for higher pay from public employees. This economic backdrop highlights the challenges in managing the rising costs associated with the growing public service sector.

Why it mattersThe growing public workforce and rising wage bill affect the government's fiscal stability and potentially public services due to increased expenditure.

Kenyan Banks and GGGI Partner to Fund Green Projects

A five-year deal between the Kenya Bankers Association and the Global Green Growth Institute aims to bridge the financing gap for climate and biodiversity ventures.

Kenyan banks will increase their involvement in financing climate and biodiversity projects through a new five-year partnership with the Global Green Growth Institute (GGGI). The agreement, signed by the Kenya Bankers Association (KBA) and GGGI, seeks to transform more green businesses into investment-ready ventures by strengthening sustainable finance and green investment.

The collaboration addresses a long-standing issue where entrepreneurs struggle to secure funding for green initiatives, while lenders report a lack of sufficiently bankable opportunities. "This partnership is about closing that gap," stated Nagnouma Kone, GGGI’s Manager for Africa Strategy and Partnerships. The initiative will connect small and medium-sized enterprises, particularly women-led businesses, with financial institutions and public sector actors to facilitate real transactions.

Under the agreement, GGGI and KBA will work on sustainable finance policies, promote green investment, and enhance the capacity of financial institutions to assess and fund sustainable projects. Mechanisms to de-risk green lending and expand the use of instruments like sustainability-linked bonds and green loans will also be explored to channel more private capital into climate and biodiversity initiatives.

Why it mattersThe partnership directly affects businesses seeking funding for green projects and financial institutions looking for bankable opportunities in Kenya's sustainable development sector.

Kenyan defender Sichenje out for season with knee injury

Charlton Athletic player Collins Sichenje has undergone surgery and will miss the rest of the 2026-27 season after suffering a knee injury.

Charlton Athletic defender Collins Sichenje will miss the remainder of the 2026-27 season due to a knee injury. The 22-year-old Kenyan international sustained the injury during a Carabao Cup match against Tottenham Hotspur and has since undergone surgery.

Club manager Nathan Jones stated that the injury is tough for Sichenje and the entire club. He confirmed that the surgery was successful but emphasized that the player faces an extended period on the sidelines. Jones expressed the club's support for Sichenje during his recovery.

Sichenje joined the Addicks in February from Serbian side Vojvodina and had made ten appearances for the club prior to his injury. The club is committed to supporting him through his rehabilitation, with Jones noting Sichenje's resilient character.

Why it mattersKenyan footballer Collins Sichenje is affected by a knee injury that requires surgery and will keep him out of play for the rest of the season.
