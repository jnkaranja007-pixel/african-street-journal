Kenyan farmers dig trenches to guard soil before El Niño rains

Farmers in central Kenya are preparing for heavier-than-usual rains linked to El Niño, after floods washed away fertile topsoil on their farms.

In Limuru, Kiambu County, farmer Elizabeth Itotia is digging trenches across her five-acre farm to control water runoff and reduce soil erosion, after heavy rains washed away much of her fertile topsoil two years ago.

In Karatina, Nyeri County, coffee farmer Moses Muhoro is preparing his land after floods devastated his farm in 2024. He says the loss of topsoil left his land less productive, and the following year his harvest fell to just 30 percent of the previous year's output.

El Niño, associated with warmer-than-average sea surface temperatures in the Pacific Ocean, can intensify rainfall across East Africa. Kenya's Meteorological Department says it is monitoring the situation and issuing updates as necessary.

Why it mattersFarmers who lose topsoil to floods lose the productivity of their land for seasons afterwards, and Muhoro's harvest fell to 30 percent of the previous year's output.

Natembeya's aide in intensive care after night attack

Amos Boaz, a political ally and communication officer of Trans Nzoia Governor George Natembeya, was attacked by unknown assailants on Monday night.

Amos Boaz, a political ally of Governor George Natembeya and the governor's communication officer, is fighting for his life in hospital after being brutally attacked by unknown assailants on Monday night.

The attack on Boaz, a senior aide to the governor of Trans Nzoia County in western Kenya, has not been linked to any suspect in the reports available so far. Kenyans.co.ke reported that Boaz is in intensive care and that a car was set ablaze during the incident.

Trans Nzoia County has, in recent months, been on a political rollercoaster, marred by politically motivated arrests, goon infiltration and scuffles for control of county projects.

Why it mattersBoaz's condition is critical, and his injuries have left the governor's communication team without its officer.

Business · 2026-11-17

Kenya and EU conclude data talks

A deal allowing free data movement between Kenya and the EU is closer after two years of negotiations.

Kenya and the European Union have concluded two years of technical negotiations on a data adequacy arrangement, a deal that could allow personal data to move freely and securely between the two jurisdictions. This agreement, once formally adopted, would position Kenya as the first African nation to achieve such a mutual data adequacy arrangement with the EU. The conclusion of talks was announced on Monday in Brussels.

The negotiations involved Kenya’s Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) and the European Commission. The arrangement aims to reduce regulatory hurdles for Kenyan businesses handling European customer data, particularly in sectors like business process outsourcing, financial services, and digital technology. Principal Secretary for ICT and the Digital Economy John Tanui stated that the conclusion of technical negotiations would create opportunities for businesses in both markets to expand investment.

Both Kenya and the EU must now complete their respective formal adoption processes for the framework to become operational. Immaculate Kassait, Kenya’s outgoing Data Commissioner, also received the Giovanni Buttarelli Award 2026 during the 48th Global Privacy Assembly for her contributions to data protection and international regulatory cooperation.

Why it mattersKenyan businesses will face fewer regulatory hurdles when transferring European customer data once the data adequacy arrangement is formally adopted.

Security · 2026-11-17

Murkomen tours Rome police centre as Kenya and Italy sign security deal

Kenya's interior minister toured Italian surveillance and intelligence systems before signing a cooperation agreement with Rome.

Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen toured the Questura di Roma, Italy's police headquarters, to see how the country's security agencies combine surveillance systems and intelligence in running police operations and responding to incidents. He said Kenya wants to learn from Italy's experience as it modernises policing and reforms its police service.

Murkomen's trip overlapped with bilateral security talks with Italian Interior Minister Matteo Piantedosi, which ended with a security cooperation agreement. Under the deal the two countries will share intelligence, run specialised training and technical assistance, and use technology against modern security threats.

The agreement also commits both governments to work together against terrorism, organised crime, drug and human trafficking, cybercrime and maritime insecurity. Murkomen said the partnership will operate within national sovereignty, the constitution, the rule of law and fundamental human rights.

Why it mattersKenya and Italy have signed an agreement covering intelligence sharing and training against terrorism, organised crime and cybercrime.
