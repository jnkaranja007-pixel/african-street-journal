Two More Charged in Sh1.569 Billion PROFIT Program Fraud Case

Two additional suspects, proprietors of Dangzerina Enterprises and Maxtor Supplies and Graybar Distributors, were charged in Nairobi, bringing the total to 13 individuals facing.

Eric Otieno Achila and Susan Wangari Gathiaka appeared before the Anti-Corruption Court in Nairobi on Tuesday, denying charges related to the alleged fraudulent disbursement of Sh1.569 billion from the National Treasury Development Account to the Programme for Rural Outreach of Financial Innovations and Technologies (PROFIT). The two were among 11 additional suspects identified by the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) during investigations into the alleged loss of funds.

Achila faces three counts, while Wangari faces four counts. Chief Magistrate Harrison Barasa Owima granted Achila a Sh1.5 million bond with a surety of a similar amount, or cash bail of Sh650,000. Wangari received a Sh1.5 million bond with a similar surety, or cash bail of Sh700,000. The prosecution did not oppose their release on bond but requested consideration of the alleged offences' nature and magnitude.

The PROFIT Programme, funded by the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) between the 2013/2014 and 2023/2024 financial years, aimed to promote affordable financing for small-scale farmers and support financial innovation in rural areas. EACC alleges funds were channeled to 23 private entities for goods and services never supplied, with Sh175.3 million received through an unauthorized Kenya Commercial Bank account and subsequently embezzled.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects the Kenyan public by revealing alleged misuse of funds intended for rural development and financial innovation, potentially impacting trust in government programs and financial institutions.

Police to Recommend Violent Political Actors to IEBC for Action

The National Police Service will submit names of individuals with a history of promoting political violence to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission for.

Deputy Inspector General Gilbert Masengeli announced that the National Police Service will recommend individuals deemed unfit to contest elective positions due to their involvement in political violence to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC). This move aims to prevent politically instigated violence ahead of the 2027 General Election, with security agencies intensifying efforts to address the issue.

The announcement follows a recent incident on August sixteen where violence disrupted a political rally in Homa Bay County, leading to the arrest of thirty-seven suspected gang members found with crude weapons. One police officer died and eight others were injured in related incidents, prompting increased police investigations and a warning that organizers, facilitators, and sponsors of criminal activities will be pursued.

Masengeli, speaking at the launch of the Roadmap to a Peaceful General Election 2027, emphasized that police response will include investigations, arrests, and crime prevention. He urged political actors and the public to adhere to their constitutional rights and responsibilities, as concerns over rising political violence ahead of the general elections continue to grow.

Why it mattersPolitical actors and the public are affected by the police's new measure to recommend individuals involved in violence to the IEBC, potentially impacting who can run for office and the safety of political gatherings.

Experts urge East Africa to commercialize bioeconomy research

Policymakers, scientists, investors, and entrepreneurs at the Africa Bioeconomy Workshop in Nairobi called for the region to move beyond research to build profitable industries.

East African experts are pushing for the region to convert bioeconomy research into profitable industries, moving past strategies and projects. The call came at the Africa Bioeconomy Workshop in Nairobi, where attendees urged governments to connect agricultural production, research, and innovation with markets to create jobs and retain more value from biological resources.

Niall O’Connor, Centre Director of the Stockholm Environment Institute Africa Centre, stated that the region possesses the necessary resources, research capacity, and entrepreneurial talent for a competitive bio-based economy. However, he noted that value is still lost through the export of raw materials. The 2026 State of the Bioeconomy in Eastern Africa report identifies agricultural residues and organic waste as potential feedstocks for industries producing biofuels, organic fertilizers, and other bio-based products.

Dr. Abdou Tenkouano, Director General of the International Centre of Insect Physiology and Ecology, highlighted agriculture's significant contribution to GDP in many sub-Saharan African countries, ranging from 23 to 35 percent. He emphasized that this sector offers a major opportunity to develop industries around biomass, urging governments to strengthen university-industry partnerships and support innovation in agro-processing and bioprocessing.

Why it mattersThis story is important for East African communities because it outlines a strategy to create jobs and increase incomes by processing local biological resources instead of exporting them raw.

Kenyan Anime Fans Transform Recycled Materials into Cosplay Culture

Thousands of anime and manga enthusiasts in Nairobi are using cardboard and other collected materials to create elaborate costumes for conventions, fostering creativity and.

More than 4,000 anime and manga fans gathered in Nairobi for the latest Otamatsuri convention, one of Africa’s largest cosplay events. Attendees transformed recycled materials and craft skills into detailed costumes, highlighting the growing popularity of Japanese pop culture in Kenya.

Jotham Micah, an organizer and co-founder of Movie Jabber, stated that anime and manga have increasingly crossed cultural boundaries, providing inspiration and entertainment. The event featured fans dressed as characters from popular Japanese franchises such as One Piece, Naruto, and Gachiakuta.

For many Kenyan cosplayers, creating costumes involves finding affordable, unconventional materials. Julius Mokaya, a 34-year-old building caretaker and two-time costume competition winner, uses cardboard and items from recycling bins to craft his elaborate outfits, as importing expensive costumes is not feasible.

Why it mattersKenyan anime and manga fans are directly affected by the availability of affordable materials and community events that allow them to express their creativity and connect with others.

Nairobi designer revives traditional hand looms for clothing production

Hillary Mukubwa, founder of Mukabwa studios, uses hand looms to create unique fabrics and clothing, moving away from mass production trends.

A Nairobi designer, Hillary Mukubwa, has shifted his clothing production to traditional hand looms, aiming to revive old practices and preserve cultural heritage. Mukubwa, who founded Mukabwa studios, states that modernization often diminishes the role of traditional craftsmen and their methods.

Mukubwa views hand looms as a way to keep Kenya's cultural heritage alive, explaining that these traditional tools carry a story and produce art with meaning, unlike electric machines that focus on mass production. He feels his approach is unusual for a young person but is driven by a desire to restore these practices.

Clients appreciate the individuality and quality of the clothes produced by Mukubwa's studio. Maylone Ginnette, a client, noted the garments are made from pure organic cotton and are unique pieces, highlighting the effort involved in their creation compared to high street offerings.

Why it mattersThis story matters to consumers who are looking for unique, handcrafted clothing options and to artisans interested in preserving traditional craft methods.
