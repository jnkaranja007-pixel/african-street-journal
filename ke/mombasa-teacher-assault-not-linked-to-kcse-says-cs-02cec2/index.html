Mombasa teacher assault not linked to KCSE, says CS

Education CS Julius Ogamba stated the attack on examination supervisor Julius Ndurya was due to personal issues, not exam malpractice.

Sources: Capital FM and Kenyans.co.ke · 2026-11-16 · 1 min read

Education Cabinet Secretary Julius Ogamba stated that the assault of a teacher in Mombasa was not connected to the ongoing Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) examinations. Ogamba said the incident was triggered by personal issues and that the teacher was not near any examination centre when attacked. He announced an investigation into why the assault was linked to the examinations.

The incident involved Julius Ndurya, a Kenya National Examinations Council (KNEC) supervisor at Vigurungani Senior School in Kwale County. Ndurya claimed he was abducted and assaulted on November 11, sustaining head injuries and a fractured hand after rejecting a KSh50,000 bribe and confiscating unauthorized examination papers. Police confirmed Ndurya reported the assault and are investigating.

The 2026 KCSE examinations are in their final week, with several attempted cheating cases reported. The Ministry of Education has tightened measures to curb examination malpractice. Ndurya alleged his attackers abandoned him believing he was dead.
