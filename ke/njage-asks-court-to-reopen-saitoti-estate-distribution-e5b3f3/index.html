Njage asks court to reopen Saitoti estate distribution

The claimant says he is Saitoti’s son and wants the confirmed grant revoked, the estate’s distribution reviewed and administrators to account for its assets.

Source: The Standard · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

James Solomon Njage has asked Kenya’s High Court to revoke the confirmed grant distributing former Vice-President George Saitoti’s estate to his widow and known son. Njage claims Saitoti was his biological father and says he was left out of the succession proceedings.

In court papers, Njage says he was a minor living outside Kenya when the grant was issued and that he was not told about the proceedings. He wants the estate’s distribution reviewed and its administrators ordered to account for rental income, investments, bank withdrawals, transfers, spending, liabilities and distributions.

Saitoti died in a helicopter crash on June 10, 2012. A rectified certificate confirming the grant was issued on November 11, 2019, without naming Njage as a beneficiary, according to his application. Saitoti’s brother, Ronald Leposo Musengi, has filed an affidavit supporting Njage’s claim, citing a DNA test that reported a 99.9999% probability they are biologically related as uncle and nephew.

Why it mattersNjage, who says he was a minor abroad during the succession proceedings, is asking the court for an account of rental income, investments and other estate transactions.
