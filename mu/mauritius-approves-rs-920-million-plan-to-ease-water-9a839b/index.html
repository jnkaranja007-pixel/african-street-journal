Mauritius approves Rs 920 million plan to ease water shortage

The plan adds transfers from three reservoirs and dams, new 30 m³ tanks, borehole pumps and tanker deliveries to the worst-hit areas.

Sources: Defi Media and Ion News · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Mauritius’s Cabinet approved on Friday, 20 November, projects by the Central Water Authority (CWA), the state water utility, estimated to cost Rs 920 million, to address the water crisis. The works include pumping extra water from Mare Longue reservoir to the La Marie treatment plant and transferring water from Tamarind Falls and Sept Cascades dams to the same plant.

The authority is also to acquire additional tanks, each with a capacity of 30 m³, install container-mounted pressure filters on rivers or dams, and equip boreholes with pumps connected to the existing network. The Cabinet said the projects would help maintain water supplies.

The authority has accelerated interventions using its own teams and stocks of materials, pipes and fittings. Water tankers are to serve the areas most affected.

Why it mattersHouseholds in the areas hit hardest by the shortage will be supplied by tanker trucks while the Rs 920 million works are carried out.
