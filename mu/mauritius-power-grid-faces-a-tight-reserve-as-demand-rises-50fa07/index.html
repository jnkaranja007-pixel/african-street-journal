Mauritius power grid faces a tight reserve as demand rises

The CEB estimates 610 MW of available capacity against a possible summer peak, while a 20 MW unit remains out of service.

Source: Defi Media · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Mauritius’s power grid has an estimated 610 MW of available capacity against summer demand that could reach 600 MW, leaving a narrow reserve, Central Electricity Board (CEB) director general Shamshir Mukoon said on the Defi Media programme “Au Cœur de l’Info.” A 20 MW generating unit at Nicolay is out of service.

Mukoon described the position as tight, though not alarming. The unit’s outage further reduces the CEB’s reserve, while two other units—15 MW at Saint-Louis and 20 MW at Fort George—are due to return after maintenance by 10 December. The Nicolay unit entered service in 1992 and has faced difficulties obtaining parts and specialised technicians.

Demand recently rose by 40 to 50 MW from the previous year, compared with its usual annual increase of 15 to 20 MW, with more air conditioners among the reasons. Solar generation without storage is unavailable during the evening peak. The CEB is backing solar installations with batteries; energy specialist Khalil Elahee called for consumption control and efficiency, while consultant Sunil Dowarkasing urged changes to the sector’s centralised model and more varied generation.

Why it mattersThe CEB must manage the evening peak with a narrow reserve while two units are due back on the grid by 10 December.
