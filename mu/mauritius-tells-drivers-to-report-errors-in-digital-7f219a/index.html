Mauritius tells drivers to report errors in digital licences before 1 January

The Kenya-style KOREK! app, which Mauritius calls its digital licence app, shows data drawn from police records, so errors must be corrected by the Traffic Branch.

Sources: Defi Media and Ion News · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Mauritius's information technology ministry has urged driving-licence holders to check their records on the KOREK! app and report errors to the police Traffic Branch before digital-licence rules take effect on 1 January 2027. The ministry issued the notice on Friday, 20 November, after some users reported incorrect information or trouble accessing their digital licences.

The KOREK! app only displays data from the official records kept by the Traffic Branch of the Mauritius Police Force and cannot change them. Drivers should contact the branch about missing, old or incorrect photos, wrong addresses or personal details, absent vehicle categories, or records that cannot be found using a national identity card number. The notice also covers older licences needing updated records and foreign nationals with valid Mauritian licences who cannot access them.

The Traffic Branch at the Casernes can be reached on 212 6159 or by email. Drivers can also call the driving test centres at Argy, in Flacq, or at the Casernes, in Curepipe. Drivers should provide their full name, national identity card number, address and licence number so police can locate their records.

Why it mattersDrivers whose photos, addresses or vehicle categories are wrong, or who cannot find their records, must contact police with identifying details before the rules take effect on 1 January 2027.
