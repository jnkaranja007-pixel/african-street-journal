Bank of Mauritius sells $15 million as consumer advocate questions results

The November 18 sale brought its 2026 currency interventions to about $170 million, while the rupee lost 2.2% against the dollar from January to October.

Source: Ion News · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

The Bank of Mauritius sold $15 million on the domestic foreign-exchange market on November 18, at Rs 46.75, its latest of 12 interventions since the start of 2026. The sales total about $170 million, or nearly Rs 8 billion. Consumer-rights advocate Suttyhudeo Tengur questioned whether the operations have produced concrete benefits for households.

The rupee depreciated 2.2% against the US dollar from January to October, Tengur wrote on Facebook on Friday, November 20. He said occasional dollar injections do not correct underlying imbalances and argued that higher interest rates make credit more expensive, risking further pressure on household purchasing power.

The Monetary Policy Committee raised the Key Rate by 25 basis points to 5.00% on November 11. Annual inflation is projected at 4.7% for December 2026 and 5.5% for December 2027.

Why it mattersMauritian borrowers face more expensive credit after the Key Rate rose by 25 basis points to 5.00%, while the consumer advocate questions benefits to households.
