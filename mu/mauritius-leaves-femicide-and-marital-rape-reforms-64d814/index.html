Mauritius leaves femicide and marital-rape reforms unproclaimed

Parliament passed the Criminal Code changes in July, but they cannot take effect until they are formally proclaimed.

Source: Defi Media · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Mauritius has yet to proclaim Criminal Code amendments passed by Parliament in July 2026 that recognise femicide and remove the marital-rape exception. The provisions cannot take effect until proclamation, leaving their start date pending.

Mokshda Pertaub, a lawyer and president of the women’s rights organisation MPower, said the delay means the reforms remain unable to produce their intended legal effects. The amendments also set out aggravating circumstances for killings of women, including the relationship between the victim and alleged perpetrator, prior domestic abuse and the victim’s vulnerability.

A separate Domestic Abuse Act took effect on 14 September, extending protections to economic, emotional, psychological and digital abuse, as well as controlling behaviour and coercion. It covers current and former partners and replaced the 1997 domestic-violence law. MPower discussed implementation at a workshop in Péreybère on 22 November.

Why it mattersWomen seeking protection under the new criminal provisions must wait for proclamation before those rules can take effect.
