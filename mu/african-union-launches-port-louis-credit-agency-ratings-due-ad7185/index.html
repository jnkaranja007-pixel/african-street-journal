African Union launches Port-Louis credit agency; ratings due by March

The Africa Credit Rating Agency is in talks with 15 governments and plans research in Mauritius, Kenya, Togo, Seychelles and Burundi.

Source: Defi Media · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

The African Union launched the Africa Credit Rating Agency (AfCRA) on October 7. Based in Port Louis, Mauritius, the agency will assess African governments and corporate borrowers using local data and expertise alongside ratings from international agencies.

Interim chief executive Sifiso Falala said AfCRA is discussing its work with 15 governments, with research planned in Mauritius, Kenya, Togo, the Seychelles and Burundi. The agency expects to issue its first sovereign ratings by the end of March.

A 2023 United Nations Development Programme study estimated that subjectivity in sovereign ratings cost African countries $74.5 billion in excessive interest payments and foregone lending. Moody’s has disputed claims of systematic disadvantage, saying a B-rated sovereign had an estimated one-year default probability of about 5% regardless of region.

Why it mattersThe 15 governments in talks with AfCRA, including Mauritius, are awaiting research and the agency’s first sovereign ratings, expected by the end of March.
