Mauritius civic programme teaches 200 students how government works

Students from 106 schools visited the National Assembly and Bank of Mauritius before studying the Constitution and the roles of state institutions.

Sources: Ion News and Defi Media · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

About 200 students took part in a civic education session at Mauritius’ National Assembly on Wednesday, 18 November, learning about the Constitution and how the country is governed. The event was held under the National Initiative for Civic Education (NICE), a programme run under the Prime Minister’s Office.

The students were divided into four groups for a module on the Constitution and governance. Acting National Assembly Clerk Navin Gopall and Clerk Assistant Niteshen Tayelamay explained parliament’s functions and the separate roles of the legislative, executive and judicial branches, followed by questions from the students.

A separate report said the participants, aged 13 to 14 and drawn from 106 schools in Mauritius, Rodrigues and Agalega, also visited the Bank of Mauritius. The session included an educational video on how the Mauritian state operates.

Why it mattersStudents aged 13 to 14 from 106 schools across Mauritius, Rodrigues and Agalega received instruction on the Constitution and visited two national institutions.
