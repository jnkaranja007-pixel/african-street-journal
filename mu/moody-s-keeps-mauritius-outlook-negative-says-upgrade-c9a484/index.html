Moody’s keeps Mauritius outlook negative, says upgrade unlikely soon

The agency wants sustained evidence of lower public debt and improved state finances before it considers stabilising its view.

Source: Defi Media · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Moody’s Ratings kept a negative outlook on Mauritius’s Baa3 sovereign rating, saying an upgrade was unlikely in the short term. The agency announced its position at a virtual press conference with GCR Ratings, a Moody’s subsidiary, on 26 November.

Raphaele Auberty, a Moody’s sovereign-risk official, said the outlook reflected uncertainty over the government’s ability to continue repairing public finances. The agency will watch public debt relative to gross domestic product and the primary balance, while assessing whether budget adjustments can be made without harming long-term growth.

Moody’s cited recent progress in fiscal consolidation, particularly during the last financial year, but said public debt remained a weakness. It does not recommend government policies; decisions on revenue and spending rest with Mauritian authorities.

Why it mattersMauritian authorities must demonstrate lasting debt reduction and stronger public finances, while balancing budget adjustment against long-term growth.
