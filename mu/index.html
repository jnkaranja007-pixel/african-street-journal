Mauritius Ministry of Education Guidelines Waste School Time

New school guidelines on mobile device use are criticized as unenforceable and a drain on instructional time, potentially leading to punitive measures for students.

The Ministry of Education in Mauritius has introduced new guidelines for mobile phone use in schools, aiming for "Educational Focus, Respect and Privacy." While the rules suggest phones should be silent and stored securely, critics argue the policy is impractical. The guidelines empower school heads to implement sanctions ranging from verbal warnings to temporary phone bans or even student suspension for repeated violations. This process requires extensive documentation and administrative meetings, diverting valuable teacher and administrator time away from teaching and student support.

International research suggests that strict phone bans in schools are ineffective. Studies indicate that teenagers check their phones frequently during school hours, and higher usage correlates with lower concentration abilities. One experiment showed that students allowed phone access, even without active use, performed significantly worse on tests due to distraction. The evidence points to bans not improving school safety or academic outcomes.

The guidelines also apply to school staff, requiring them to refrain from using phones in front of students unless for official or educational purposes. This adds another layer of compliance monitoring for educators. Critics argue that the policy is a performative measure that fails to address the root causes of distraction and will ultimately penalize students for issues beyond their control, without fostering better behavior.

Why it mattersStudents and teachers are affected by new school guidelines that critics say are unenforceable and divert time from education to phone policing.

SME Mauritius CEO Ravin Rampersad Suspended Amidst Irregularity Probe

The board of SME Mauritius Ltd has suspended its Chief Executive Officer, Ravin Rampersad, pending the outcome of an investigation into alleged irregularities.

SME Mauritius Ltd's board of directors announced on Friday, August 28, the suspension of CEO Ravin Rampersad. This decision comes as the board investigates irregularities within the organization. The probe aims to establish facts and responsibilities, with further actions to be determined based on its conclusions. Raj Kishore Bunjun, former Permanent Secretary at the Ministry of Industry, has been appointed Officer-in-Charge to ensure operational continuity.

Rampersad, who took the helm of SME Mauritius on June 18, 2018, was previously a director at Innodis Poultry Ltd and served as a consultant at the Small and Medium Enterprise Development Authority (SMEDA) from January to November 2017. His suspension is intended to safeguard the organization's proper functioning while the investigation proceeds.

The board stated that the suspension is a necessary measure to allow for a thorough examination of the identified issues. This process is designed to ensure transparency and accountability within SME Mauritius Ltd. The findings of the inquiry will guide the board's subsequent decisions regarding the matter.

Why it mattersInvestors may be affected by the uncertainty surrounding leadership and potential financial implications arising from the investigation into irregularities at SME Mauritius Ltd.

Bishop Durhone Urges Dialogue on Same-Sex Marriage Debate

The Bishop of Port-Louis calls for listening and dialogue amidst ongoing legal and societal discussions regarding same-sex marriage in Mauritius, while reaffirming the Catholic.

The debate surrounding same-sex marriage in Mauritius is intensifying as legal frameworks are examined. Bishop Jean Michaël Durhône of Port-Louis has urged for listening and dialogue, emphasizing the Catholic Church's stance that marriage is exclusively between a man and a woman. However, his intervention also highlights the need to acknowledge the lived experiences of homosexual individuals and to move beyond a simple clash of principles.

The National Human Rights Commission has raised concerns about the civil status office's refusal to register same-sex unions, suggesting it could constitute discrimination based on sexual orientation. This issue extends beyond symbolic ceremonies, touching upon practical matters such as inheritance, medical decisions, family protection, and spousal rights upon death.

Proponents of legal reform argue that recognition of same-sex couples would have direct impacts on their daily lives, not merely symbolic value. Bishop Durhône's position does not alter Church doctrine but focuses on how dialogue can continue despite differing views on marriage, acknowledging the practical consequences for affected couples.

Why it mattersThe debate over same-sex marriage and its legal recognition directly affects homosexual couples by impacting inheritance, medical decisions, and family protection rights.
