Mauritius inflation climbs to 6.4% in October

Annual inflation in Mauritius accelerated to 6.4% in October 2026, up from 5.7% in September, driven by fuel price adjustments.

Annual inflation in Mauritius reached 6.4% in October 2026, a significant increase from 5.7% in September and 4.1% in October 2025, according to Statistics Mauritius. The Consumer Price Index rose to 114.8 points in October from 114.1 in September, a monthly increase of 0.6%.

The rise in the index was primarily driven by fuel prices, with gasoline alone contributing +0.6 points to the overall variation and diesel adding +0.1 points. The transport sector saw the largest monthly increase at 3.8%. Food prices saw a slight increase of 0.1%, offset by a 0.1% decrease in vegetable prices.

Overall inflation, calculated on a 12-month average ending in October 2026, stood at 4.3%. Core inflation, excluding food and energy, showed mixed trends, with CORE1 rising to 6.8% annually while CORE2 decreased to 4.2% annually.

Why it mattersConsumers face higher prices as annual inflation reached 6.4% in October 2026, with transport costs rising significantly.

99-year-old pedestrian dies five days after motorcycle collision in Port-Louis

Alex Etienne Kon Ng, 99, was hit on Royal Street on Tuesday, November 10, and died early on Sunday, November 15, at Dr A. G. Jeetoo Hospital.

A 99-year-old pedestrian died in Port-Louis early on Sunday, November 15, five days after a motorcycle struck him on Royal Street on Tuesday, November 10. Alex Etienne Kon Ng had been admitted to the intensive care unit of Dr A. G. Jeetoo Hospital with serious injuries.

The 56-year-old motorcyclist was also injured in the crash. A post-mortem by forensic doctor Maxwell Monvoisin attributed the death to a bleed on the head. Police have opened an inquiry to establish the exact circumstances of the accident, and an autopsy was announced to confirm the cause of death.

Kon Ng was the oldest resident of Quatre-Bornes, a town neighbouring the capital. The inquiry is ongoing.

Why it mattersPolice must now establish how the collision happened, which will decide whether the motorcyclist faces charges.

Port-Louis traffic rerouted around Mgr Leen street construction

New traffic plan in Port-Louis aims to ease congestion near ongoing infrastructure work on Mgr Leen street.

Port-Louis authorities have implemented a new traffic diversion plan on Mgr Leen street, effective Monday, November 16, 2026, to address significant traffic congestion. This measure, in place until December 21, aims to improve traffic flow in this vital part of the capital where infrastructure work is underway.

Light vehicles heading towards Labourdonnais street can now use Mgr Leen street from Old Moka Road, but heavy vehicles are prohibited. Drivers must follow a one-way route through G.E.F. Poulain, A.F. Tank Wen, and D’Alembert streets to reach Mgr Leen street and continue to Labourdonnais street. The section of Mgr Leen street past Labourdonnais remains closed except for residents.

Parking is banned on all affected streets to prevent further blockages, and police officers are on site to manage traffic and enforce the new rules. These changes are part of the construction project overseen by the Road Development Authority (RDA) between G.E.F. Poulain and D’Alembert streets.

Why it mattersDrivers in Port-Louis must follow a new one-way route on Mgr Leen street and surrounding roads until December 21 due to infrastructure construction.
