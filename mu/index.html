Mauritius raises key interest rate to 5%

The central bank's move on November 11 sparks public anger over the rising cost of living and increased loan burdens.

The Bank of Mauritius Monetary Policy Committee raised the key interest rate by 25 basis points on Wednesday, November 11, moving it from 4.75% to 5%. This decision has led to widespread criticism on social media, with many citizens expressing frustration over the escalating cost of living and the impact on their bank loans.

Governor Priscilla Muthoora Thakoor's announcement has drawn sharp reactions, with some social media users calling for her resignation. One user, S. M., stated that since her appointment, the only action has been to increase the repo rate, suggesting she does not understand the financial struggles of ordinary people. Another user, K. R., highlighted that individuals with Mutual Aid loans will see their repayment periods extend by five to 10 years due to the rate hike.

The rate increase, occurring two years after the general elections in November 2024, has been met with irony by some who recall electoral promises. Users questioned if this was the promised change, with one commenter sarcastically asking if this was the '60-0 gift' they received. The decision aims to curb inflation and encourage savings, but concerns remain about its effect on investment and the business climate.

Why it mattersIndividuals with bank loans, including those from Mutual Aid, will see their repayment periods extend by five to 10 years following the interest rate hike to 5% on November 11.

Port-Louis municipality threatens street vendors with sanctions

The city council warns of severe penalties for illegal occupation of public spaces and drains ahead of the festive season.

The Port-Louis municipal council is intensifying its crackdown on illegal street occupations as the busy festive season approaches. In an official notice dated November 10, 2026, the council reminded vendors that no stalls or structures will be permitted on streets or in city drains.

The municipality also stated that using parked vehicles as selling points along public roads is strictly forbidden. Established businesses are prohibited from placing structures outside their shops or displaying goods on sidewalks and roadsides, which obstruct pedestrian flow and drainage.

The council warned it would remove illegal installations and confiscated products, threatening severe sanctions against offenders. This action aims to ensure the free movement of pedestrians and the proper functioning of storm drains as summer approaches.

Why it mattersThe Port-Louis municipality will remove illegal installations and products, applying severe sanctions against offenders who obstruct public spaces and drains.

Mauritius MP Ehsan Juman calls for compromise on street vendors

Labour Party MP Ehsan Juman urges a solution that balances vendors' right to work with public order after a police operation in Port-Louis.

Labour Party MP Ehsan Juman defended street vendors after a police operation on Desforges Street in Port-Louis on Wednesday, 11 November. Juman, who is currently abroad, said on his Facebook page on Thursday, 12 November, that he is following the matter closely and is calling for a solution that respects both the right to work and public order.

Juman said he had spoken by phone with the mayor and the Assistant Commissioner of Police for Port-Louis North about the operation. His aim, he wrote, is to avoid penalising people who depend on the daily trade for their livelihoods, while keeping the busy street safe and clear. He added that vendors who work all year should be allowed to work, but in an orderly and disciplined way.

The management of street vendors in the capital remains a sensitive issue, especially as the busy end-of-year shopping period approaches. Juman said he hopes common sense will prevail, favouring consultation over repression.

Why it mattersVendors who rely on the street for daily income face uncertainty over whether they can keep trading there during the busy end-of-year period.

Macondé viewpoint closed for safety reasons

The Tourism Authority has officially announced the temporary closure of the popular Macondé viewpoint in the southwest of Mauritius until further notice.

The Macondé viewpoint in the southwest of Mauritius is temporarily closed to the public and visitors for safety reasons, with no set reopening date. The Tourism Authority issued a statement on Thursday, November 12, announcing the closure until further notice.

Tour operators have been asked to note this closure and inform their clients, especially those with existing reservations that include a stop at the viewpoint. The official communication follows reports earlier in the week of visitors continuing to access the rocky promontory despite warning signs.

The Tourism Authority stated that any updates regarding the site's reopening will be communicated as soon as more information is available from the relevant authorities. They are requesting cooperation from tourism operators to enforce this precautionary measure.

Why it mattersThe popular Macondé viewpoint is closed until further notice due to safety concerns, affecting tourists and tour operators.

FIFA funding of over Rs 1.1 billion to reach Mauritian football

Each of FIFA's 211 member associations, including the Mauritius Football Association, could receive $25 million, with the first tranche expected in early 2027.

Mauritian football could receive a major financial boost from the Fédération internationale de football association (FIFA). Each of the world body's 211 member associations, including the Mauritius Football Association (MFA), is due to receive $25 million, which is more than Rs 1.1 billion.

According to MFA president Samir Sobha, the FIFA Council has already approved the funding. Planned projects include a stadium built to FIFA's international standards and 13 synthetic pitches across the island. Local clubs are also set to receive support over the next four seasons, similar to the FIFA Forward 3.0 aid given over the past three seasons.

Sobha said the money will be managed transparently, with particular attention paid to how it is spent. The first disbursement is expected at the start of 2027, and the funding programme will run over four years.

Why it mattersClubs and the national association would gain stadium and pitch projects, plus four seasons of club support.
