Mauritius poverty rate to fall below 10% by 2028

Projections show a sustained decrease in poverty, despite imported inflation and currency depreciation impacting purchasing power.

Mauritius aims to reduce its poverty rate to 9.8% by 2028, down from an estimated 12% in 2026, according to economic projections. This anticipated decline is supported by the resilience of the nation's economic pillars and the effectiveness of its social protection systems. The international poverty line used for this measurement is $8.30 USD per day in purchasing power parity.

Despite the overall positive trend, Mauritius faces challenges from the gap between GDP growth and high-value job creation, as well as a demographic shift towards an aging population. Households are experiencing an erosion of purchasing power due to a combination of rising global oil prices, increased maritime freight costs, and the continued depreciation of the Mauritian rupee.

The government's social safety nets, including universal and targeted pensions and subsidies, distinguish Mauritius from many emerging economies. However, maintaining this extensive social support in the face of persistent imported inflation places a significant strain on public finances and national debt.

Why it mattersHouseholds in Mauritius are affected by a decrease in their purchasing power due to rising costs of imported goods and a weakening currency.

Labour Congress seeks risk allowance for more workers

The Mauritius Labour Congress is demanding that the risk allowance granted to police officers be extended to other essential service providers.

The Mauritius Labour Congress (MLC) is calling for the risk allowance, recently given to police officers following unrest, to be expanded to other professional groups. MLC president Haniff Peerun stated that ambulance workers, nurses, and doctors also faced challenges during the curfew to maintain essential services.

Peerun argued that the commitment and risks undertaken by these healthcare professionals warrant financial recognition. He believes their exposure to difficulties while ensuring public service continuity should be acknowledged through the same allowance provided to police.

The union's demand highlights a push for broader recognition of frontline workers' contributions during times of crisis. The MLC's stance suggests a desire to ensure equitable compensation for those who have performed critical duties under challenging circumstances.

Why it mattersHealthcare workers and ambulance staff are affected by the MLC's demand for extended risk allowances.

Hungley urges African Charter use amid rising costs

Mauritius's Vice-President Robert Hungley called for greater reliance on the African Charter for Human Rights to address current population challenges.

Vice-President Robert Hungley advocated for a stronger adherence to the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights during its 40th anniversary event at Middlesex University. He stated that Mauritius, despite ratifying the charter in 1992, remains overly reliant on British and European legal frameworks.

Hungley highlighted the need to draw inspiration from the realities of the Global South and the African continent to develop tailored local solutions. This call comes amidst rising living costs, water access issues, recent tensions, and concerns over police conduct, which he believes the charter can help address.

The Vice-President also emphasized the importance of acknowledging the nation's African heritage and the history of slavery, describing these as fundamental to social justice and human dignity.

Why it mattersMauritian citizens are affected by rising living costs, water access problems, and concerns over police conduct, which the Vice-President suggests could be addressed by better utilizing the African Charter on Human Rights.

Mauritius police search for uncle after child sexual abuse video surfaces

Authorities are seeking a 27-year-old man after a video on social media showed the sexual abuse of a six-year-old girl in Mauritius.

Police in Mauritius have launched an investigation into a 27-year-old man accused of sexually abusing his six-year-old niece. The case began on October 20, 2026, when authorities received a report via the 113 hotline regarding a video circulating on Facebook. The child's mother identified the suspect as her brother and confirmed the victim was her daughter.

The Family Support Service of Port-Louis and the Family Protection Brigade have been monitoring the situation since the report. During a psychological evaluation conducted on October 21, the girl stated she was forced to perform sexual acts on multiple occasions. Officials confirmed the suspect is the child's maternal uncle and godfather.

The victim is currently being prepared for admission to a hospital to undergo necessary medical examinations. While the Family Support Service continues to provide assistance to the family, the suspect remains at large and is actively being sought by police investigators.

Why it mattersA six-year-old girl is directly affected by physical and psychological trauma following sexual abuse, requiring medical intervention and state support.
