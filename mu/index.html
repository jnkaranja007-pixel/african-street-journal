Mauritius adopts disaster bill setting work rules for torrential rain

The law bars most employees from being required to work once meteorologists issue a torrential-rain warning and sets higher pay for emergency staff.

Mauritius’ National Assembly adopted the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (Amendment) Bill without changes on Tuesday, 17 November. The law formalises the national protocol for heavy and torrential rain and strengthens protections for workers during severe weather.

Labour Minister Reza Uteem, who sponsored the bill, said it is intended to end differences in how public- and private-sector employees are treated. Once meteorological services issue a torrential-rain warning, employees cannot be required to travel to or remain at work, except emergency-services personnel; the rule does not wait for a National Crisis Committee directive.

During heavy rain, employees are still expected to go to work unless the National Crisis Committee orders or advises them to stay home, shelter or leave. Private-sector emergency staff working on site during torrential rain are entitled to three times their basic hourly rate; those working from home must receive at least twice their usual pay.

Mauritius postpones free school-van project as economic conditions weigh

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam told parliament the plan’s implementation will be reviewed as the economic situation develops.

Mauritius has postponed its planned free school-van service for children, with Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam saying its implementation will be reviewed as the economic situation develops. He announced the delay on Tuesday, 17 November, in response to a question from Rubna Daureeawoo, the MP for Rivière-des-Anguilles/Souillac.

Ramgoolam said the project, included in the Alliance of Change’s election programme, had been delayed because of the economic situation inherited from the previous government. He said ministers recognise the cost school transport places on many parents, while describing school vans as safe transport for young children.

Free transport for students and older people has been in place since 2005 and costs the state Rs 1.6 billion a year, Ramgoolam said; the government will maintain it despite economic constraints. He also said the proposed van service would extend to Rodrigues, which is part of the public transport system.

Why it mattersParents paying for school transport will have to wait for the government’s review of the project as economic conditions develop.

IPCC recommends criminal charges against four Mauritius police officers

The commission sent its completed investigation into Élodie Thomas’s October shooting to the director of public prosecutions on 17 November.

Mauritius’s Independent Police Complaints Commission (IPCC) has recommended criminal charges against four police officers after investigating the shooting that seriously injured Élodie Thomas, 27, at Camp-Levieux on 9 October. The commission sent its findings to the director of public prosecutions on 17 November.

The IPCC gathered more than 25 statements from police officers, members of the Special Mobile Force and others, and reviewed documents and Safe City camera recordings. Thomas, who left hospital on 6 November, gave her account at home six days later; the commission then held further police interviews from 12 to 16 November.

The investigation began after two complaints were filed on 12 October, one by the driver of the minibus Thomas was travelling in and one on her behalf. Sergeant Gowtamsing Taukoor, arrested on the night of the shooting, faces two provisional charges in court.

Why it mattersThe director of public prosecutions must decide whether to act on the IPCC’s recommendation against four officers.

Police investigate assault on 22-year-old guard at Roches-Noires charcoal factory

The guard was found critically injured at his workplace on 16 November after an apparent dispute over a motorcycle engine, police were told.

A 22-year-old security guard was found critically injured at a charcoal factory on Cremation Road in Roches-Noires on the night of Monday, 16 November. Police received an alert at about 23:45 and found him on a dirt path at the workplace.

The guard had serious injuries to his face and left foot, according to Ion News. Information gathered in the case indicated he had been hurt in an altercation with a man over a motorcycle engine. He was taken to SSRN Hospital in Pamplemousses and then treated by a SAMU medical team.

Police have opened an investigation. Defi Media reported that four young people were detained on 17 November in connection with the case; it said the injured guard, whom it described as 20, was receiving artificial breathing after surgery for blood clots.

Why it mattersPolice are investigating the workplace assault, and four young people were detained on 17 November in connection with the case.

Port-Louis waterfront festival returns with more than 30 food stalls

The 20-22 November event caps dish prices at Rs 275 and adds nightly concerts, DJ sets and radio activities.

The Port-Louis Waterfront Food Festival will return from 20 to 22 November for its second edition, with more than 30 food stalls offering Mauritian dishes and food inspired by Indian, Korean, other Asian and Western cuisines. The event is organised by Défi Digital and Radio Plus, with Pocket Island Solutions Ltd and Coca-Cola as partners.

Organisers say prices for dishes will not exceed Rs 275. The stalls will sell options including biryani, curries, noodles, dumplings, street food and seafood, alongside desserts and drinks. The festival follows a first edition held in July.

Concerts, DJ sets and other music performances are scheduled each evening from 19 to 21 hours. Radio Plus will also run games and activities, distribute gifts and bring its presenters to the waterfront during the three-day event.

Why it mattersVisitors can buy festival dishes for no more than Rs 275, with more than 30 stalls operating from 20 to 22 November.
