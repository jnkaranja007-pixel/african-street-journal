Politics · 2026-11-15

MMM examines Local Government Bill implications

Village councils dissolve on November 22 as the MMM party reviews a new legal framework for local elections.

The Mauritius MMM party's central committee met on Saturday, November 14, to discuss the implications of the upcoming Local Government Bill. This new legislation is expected to reform local administration and could alter how village elections are conducted. The dissolution of village councils is scheduled for November 22, potentially before the new bill is enacted.

The meeting also addressed amendments to the Workers’ Rights Act of 2019. These proposed changes aim to provide equal protection for public and private sector employees during dangerous weather conditions. Under the new protocol, employees would not be required to report to work during torrential rain advisories, though emergency service personnel would be exempt.

The MMM will also commemorate Azor Adélaïde, a former docker and party militant, on Wednesday, November 25, at the Saint-Jean cemetery. This event marks 55 years since his death on November 25, 1971, when he was shot in Curepipe. Adélaïde is remembered as a significant figure in the MMM's history and is considered the first victim of a political assassination in Mauritius.

Health · 2026-11-15

Mother transfers daughter to private clinic after public hospital delays

A mother alleges her minor daughter did not receive specialist care and was transferred without her knowledge from a public hospital.

A mother expressed outrage over her minor daughter's medical care at a public hospital, citing a lack of specialist follow-up, an unannounced transfer, and treatment delays. The incident began on November 12 around 10 PM when her daughter, suffering severe abdominal pain, was admitted to one hospital and then transferred to another due to a lack of beds, arriving at the second facility around 1 AM.

The mother stated she waited hours without a specialist examining her child and that the minor was moved by ambulance to another hospital without prior notification. Believing her daughter was not receiving adequate care for persistent pain, the mother decided to take her to a private clinic where blood tests, an ultrasound, and a gastroscopy were performed, revealing esophageal inflammation.

The mother lamented what she perceived as failures in the public health service and incurred significant private medical expenses. She is considering filing a complaint with the police.

Why it mattersA mother incurred significant private medical expenses due to alleged failures in public healthcare and is considering police action.

Mauritius promotes higher education in Madagascar

17 Mauritian institutions showcased study opportunities in Antananarivo.

17 Mauritian higher education institutions participated in the "Study in Mauritius" fair, recently held in Antananarivo, Madagascar. Mauritian Minister of Higher Education, Science and Research, Kaviraj Sukon, led the delegation and presented the various training possibilities available in Mauritius. The fair, organised with the support of Malagasy authorities for security and crowd management, allowed visitors to explore the diverse courses offered by Mauritian institutions. Seven public and 10 private institutions took part in this initiative, significantly boosting the visibility of Mauritius's higher education offerings.

During the mission, Kaviraj Sukon also held bilateral meetings with his Malagasy counterparts responsible for Higher Education and Scientific Research, and Technical Education and Vocational Training. He also met with the vice-president of the University of Antananarivo.

The event was organised in collaboration with the Mauritius-Madagascar Chamber of Commerce and Industry.

Why it matters17 Mauritian higher education institutions are seeking to attract Malagasy students through a recent fair in Antananarivo.
