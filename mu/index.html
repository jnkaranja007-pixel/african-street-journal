Mauritius central bank raises key rate to 5%

The Bank of Mauritius increased its benchmark interest rate by 25 basis points to combat rising inflation.

The Bank of Mauritius (BoM) has increased its key interest rate from 4.75% to 5%. The Monetary Policy Committee decided unanimously on November 11 to raise the rate by 25 basis points to curb inflation. This measure is intended to prevent inflation from remaining above the target range of 2% to 5%.

Inflation has risen, with the 12-month average increasing from 4% in July to 4.3% in October 2026. Annual inflation climbed from 4.4% to 6.4% over the same period. In October, 47% of consumer price index products showed price increases exceeding 5%, up from 33% in January. The BoM now forecasts average annual inflation of 4.7% in December 2026 and 5.5% in December 2027.

The central bank maintained its economic growth forecast at 3% for 2026 and 3.2% for 2027, driven by financial services, insurance, and tourism. However, it warned of potential negative impacts from prolonged geopolitical tensions in the Middle East on imports, foreign investment, and local consumption. The BoM stated it would remain vigilant and intervene if necessary.

Why it mattersBorrowers in Mauritius will face higher monthly loan repayments due to the central bank's decision to increase the key interest rate.

Mauritius police arrest third suspect in murder probe

A man in his thirties is suspected of involvement in a violent robbery and the assault of Selven Iyempermal.

The Major Crimes Investigation Team (MCIT) in Mauritius arrested a third suspect on Wednesday, November 11, in connection with the murder of Selven Iyempermal. The man, in his thirties, is suspected of involvement in a violent robbery in Vacoas and investigators believe he may also be linked to Iyempermal's assault.

Iyempermal was attacked on the night of October 27 in a house in La Caverne, Vacoas, by masked and armed individuals, dying in hospital the next day from hemorrhagic shock. The two other suspects, Imtiyaz Chady and Ridwaan Bauluck, deny involvement and remain in police custody.

The newly arrested suspect is scheduled to appear before the Curepipe court on Thursday. The MCIT continues its investigation into the events that led to Iyempermal's death.

Why it mattersThe arrest of a third suspect in the murder of Selven Iyempermal affects the community in Vacoas and the judicial process in Curepipe.

99-year-old pedestrian hit by motorcycle in Port-Louis

The nonagenarian was admitted to intensive care after the collision on Royal Street.

A 99-year-old pedestrian was struck by a motorcycle on Royal Street in Port-Louis on Tuesday, November 10. The elderly man, a resident of Quatre-Bornes, and the motorcyclist were both taken to the hospital. The nonagenarian was admitted to intensive care for his injuries.

Police have opened an investigation into the incident to determine the exact circumstances of the collision. The report does not specify the condition of the motorcyclist.

The incident occurred on November 10. The police are working to establish the facts surrounding the accident.

Why it mattersA 99-year-old man was seriously injured and admitted to intensive care following a traffic accident.

Port-Louis road closure sparks traffic complaints

Emergency repairs on Rue Mgr Leen, closed since November 10, continue until December 21.

A temporary closure of a section of Rue Mgr Leen in Port-Louis, between Poulin and D’Alembert streets, is causing significant traffic disruption. The emergency roadworks, which began on November 10, 2026, aim to repair damaged pavement and a retaining wall following a landslide. The repairs are scheduled to last until December 21, 2026.

Motorists have complained about severe traffic jams and feeling unprepared for the closure. Activist Suttyhudeo Tengur addressed the criticism on Facebook on November 12, urging patience and stating that road safety must be prioritized over daily traffic inconveniences. He emphasized the necessity of the urgent repairs to prevent further damage and accidents.

Tengur refuted claims that drivers were not adequately warned, citing radio announcements and communiqués made days before the closure. He suggested that residents leave home earlier to mitigate delays, noting that the current school holiday period generally leads to lighter traffic in the capital.

Why it mattersRoad users in Port-Louis face extended travel times due to emergency repairs on Rue Mgr Leen.

Roadworks on rue Monseigneur-Leen snarl traffic into Port-Louis

Police have proposed an alternative route into Port-Louis while work on rue Monseigneur-Leen continues until 21 December.

Traffic into Port-Louis was slow on Wednesday, 11 November, with delays reported on the main roads leading to the capital from both the north and the south. The congestion is caused by roadworks on rue Monseigneur-Leen, where diversions were set up the day before to allow the ground to stabilise.

Sergeant Yumesh Rughoo of the Traffic Branch said motorists can reach rue Labourdonnais by an alternative route via Old Moka Road, the Casernes centrales and the hospital road. He urged drivers to take care and to follow the road signs that have been put in place.

The work is expected to continue until 21 December.

Why it mattersMotorists travelling into Port-Louis from the north and south face delays and must use the diverted route until the works finish on 21 December.
