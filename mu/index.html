Mauritius to introduce business facilitation bill

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam announced the upcoming Business Facilitation Bill to simplify administrative procedures and improve investor coordination.

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam announced the upcoming finalisation of a new Business Facilitation Bill on October 28 at the Maritim Resort & Spa in Balaclava. The bill aims to simplify administrative processes and create a more operational, transparent, and stable business environment. Consultations are underway through the Economic Development Board to identify measures for faster decision-making and improved institutional coordination for investors.

The Prime Minister stated that attracting investments requires strong institutions, respect for the freedom to undertake business, and a rigorous regulatory framework. These elements are essential for maintaining Mauritius's international credibility and competitiveness. The announcement was made during HSBC's 110th anniversary celebration in Mauritius, attended by ministers, diplomats, and private sector leaders.

HSBC's Group Chairman Brendan Nelson affirmed the bank's commitment to serving as a bridge between Mauritian businesses and global markets. HSBC Maurice CEO Hajrah Sakauloo added that the institution will leverage its international network to support Mauritius's growth as a globally connected financial hub.

Why it mattersThe new Business Facilitation Bill aims to make it easier for both local and foreign investors to conduct business in Mauritius.

Mauritius targets pesticide misuse with new rules

Government issues 32 notices and plans QR codes to track pesticide use on farms.

Mauritius announced a crackdown on pesticide overuse this week, issuing 32 Improvement Notices to farmers. Analyses in May and June found seven out of 78 samples exceeded authorized limits. The government is preparing four new regulations under the Use of Pesticides Act 2018 and plans to implement a QR code system with UN support.

These measures aim to improve public health, but the report argues they do not address the root cause: the market. A visually appealing, smooth, and uniformly green vegetable, often achieved with pesticides, sells for a higher price than a less attractive, naturally grown one. This market dynamic forces farmers to choose between economic viability and safe produce.

The government is also forming an interministerial committee and threatening sanctions, including the withdrawal of support measures or land reclamation. Minister of Agro-industry Arvin Boolell stated, "Many people talk, but few make decisions." The current system penalizes farmers who adopt safer practices, making it difficult for them to compete.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected by new regulations and market pressures that penalize the production of healthy, but less visually appealing, produce.

Mauritius PM meets NGOs after unrest

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam led talks with youth and vulnerable family support groups on October 29.

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam chaired a meeting with non-governmental organisations (NGOs) on Thursday, October 29, following recent unrest. The three-hour consultation aimed to identify factors of marginalisation and exclusion affecting young people and vulnerable families.

The meeting included Deputy Prime Minister Arianne Navarre-Marie and ministers Ashok Subron and Annabelle Savabaddy. NGOs were chosen for their direct knowledge of issues such as education, employment, poverty, mental health, drug abuse, domestic violence, discrimination, and artist marginalisation.

This initial discussion with NGOs will be followed by talks with interfaith committees, ministries, and public bodies. The government plans to create a roadmap with clear deadlines and responsibilities to achieve verifiable results and provide opportunities for young people.

Why it mattersThe Prime Minister is initiating dialogue with NGOs to address social issues like poverty and youth marginalisation after recent unrest.

Mauritius plans Airport Holdings dismantling

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam announced the government's intention to dismantle Airport Holdings Ltd.

The Mauritian government plans to dismantle Airport Holdings Ltd and restructure its operations, Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam announced in Parliament on Tuesday. This move aims to address issues with responsibilities and potential conflicts within the company's entities.

Ramgoolam stated that action has already begun to review the current structure. He also addressed the potential re-listing of Air Mauritius Ltd on the Stock Exchange of Mauritius, noting it was delisted during voluntary administration when the company was near bankruptcy under the previous administration.

Air Mauritius is still in a recovery phase after exiting voluntary administration, with the priority being to strengthen its financial foundations and ensure long-term viability. A re-listing on the stock exchange will only be considered once this situation is consolidated.

Why it mattersThe Mauritian government is planning to dismantle Airport Holdings Ltd, affecting its structure and operations.
