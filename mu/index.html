Father's Psychiatric Report Awaited in Infant's Death

Police are awaiting psychiatric evaluation results for a 30-year-old father arrested in connection with the fatal stabbing of his seven-month-old son.

The investigation into the suspicious death of seven-month-old Adriano Kelyano on August 23 in Roche-Bois has reached a critical stage. The Criminal Investigation Division is awaiting the findings of the psychiatric evaluation of the father, Jean Daniel Perrine, 30, who is under surveillance at Brown-Séquard Hospital. The child's mother, Marie Anne-Sophie Milazar, 20, discovered her son's body on a mattress, wrapped in a sheet and bearing stab wounds to the abdomen, after a short absence to do laundry.

An autopsy confirmed a fatal stabbing attack to the abdomen. The suspect, employed at the Roche-Bois landfill, was arrested shortly after and reportedly claimed to have been obeying auditory hallucinations. He has been provisionally charged with murder in the Port-Louis court and was denied bail. His interrogation will resume once medical clearance is granted, ahead of his next court appearance scheduled for August 31.

The case highlights the ongoing efforts by law enforcement to piece together the events leading to the infant's death. The psychiatric report is considered crucial for understanding the suspect's state of mind and for the progression of the legal proceedings. The community awaits further developments as the investigation continues.

Why it mattersThe psychiatric report's findings could influence the legal proceedings and understanding of the events, potentially affecting the family and community.

Court Drops Money Laundering Charge Against Sattar Hajee Abdoula

A provisional charge of money laundering against Sattar Hajee Abdoula has been dismissed by the court following a motion by his lawyer.

The provisional charge of money laundering against Sattar Hajee Abdoula has been struck down by the court. His lawyer, Me Raouf Gulbul, had filed a motion arguing there was no reasonable suspicion to maintain the accusation. The alleged facts dated back to March 31, 2020, at AfrAsia bank in Port-Louis.

Me Gulbul contended that his client was not the signatory for a contract awarded by Air Mauritius to Grant Thornton for advisory services. He also pointed out that Air Mauritius had not initiated any legal action against Grant Thornton to recover any outstanding sum, undermining the basis of the charge.

The court rendered its judgment this Thursday, officially removing the provisional charge. This decision follows the arguments presented by Abdoula's legal representative, who sought the annulment of the provisional accusation based on the lack of sufficient grounds and evidence.

Why it mattersInvestors may see reduced legal uncertainty for business dealings involving companies like Air Mauritius and its service providers.

Mauritius Trade Deficit Widens to Rs 113.1 Billion in Six Months

The nation's trade imbalance grew by 12.5% in the first half of 2026, driven by a surge in energy imports and lagging exports, according to Statistics Mauritius.

Mauritius recorded a trade deficit of Rs 113.1 billion in the first six months of 2026, a 12.5% increase compared to the same period in 2025. Imports reached Rs 167.1 billion, while exports totaled Rs 54 billion. The deficit widened significantly in the second quarter, from April to June, reaching Rs 67.5 billion, up 26% year-on-year, as import growth outpaced export gains.

A substantial portion of the import increase is attributed to energy products. Mineral fuels, lubricants, and related items accounted for Rs 32.9 billion, or 34.1% of total imports in the second quarter, a 71.5% rise from the previous year, largely due to a 75.3% increase in refined petroleum product imports. Meanwhile, overall exports grew only 0.2% in the first half, with declines in machinery and transport equipment, and manufactured goods offsetting gains in chemicals and food products.

Statistics Mauritius projects an annual trade deficit of approximately Rs 215 billion for 2026, an increase from Rs 208 billion in 2025. This projection anticipates imports of Rs 325 billion against exports of Rs 110 billion. The widening trade gap, primarily influenced by rising energy costs and slower export performance, remains a significant concern for the Mauritian economy.

Why it mattersThe widening trade deficit affects investors by signaling potential economic instability and increased costs for imported goods, impacting business operations and profitability.

Health Minister Defends Public Hospital Surgery Amidst Controversy

Minister Anil Bachoo stated his private doctor observed his surgery, performed by a public sector surgeon, and defended his choice to use public facilities.

Health Minister Anil Bachoo addressed controversy surrounding his recent surgery at Dr. A. G. Jeetoo Hospital, asserting that a public sector surgeon performed the procedure. His private physician, who has treated him for over a decade, was present only as an observer. Bachoo explained that he anticipated criticism regardless of his choice, stating that opting for private care would have drawn accusations of having too much money, while using public facilities led to questions about his trust in the system.

The minister also faced questions regarding the protocol applied in his case and whether similar arrangements could be extended to all patients, to which he indicated a forthcoming response in Parliament. Bachoo highlighted that private doctors do participate in public hospitals, noting that over 150 foreign private doctors openly operate in Mauritian public facilities. He emphasized that his primary concern, and the focus of the medical staff, was the patient's life.

Bachoo's defense of his public hospital treatment raises broader questions about transparency and equal access within the healthcare system. While his choice may be seen as a vote of confidence in public facilities, the debate continues regarding whether ordinary citizens would receive the same considerations. The core issue revolves around ensuring rules are understood and consistently applied to all patients, balancing trust, transparency, and equality in healthcare.

Why it mattersThe minister's surgery in a public hospital and the presence of his private doctor raises questions about equal treatment for all citizens seeking healthcare.

Government and NGO Partnership Targets Domestic Abuse Fatalities

A civil society proposal advocates for a formal collaboration between government bodies and non-governmental organizations to enhance early risk identification and intervention.

A new policy proposal from FAM [Fam an Mars] suggests a strengthened, formal partnership between the Mauritian Government and non-governmental organizations (NGOs) to prevent domestic abuse fatalities. The proposal, prepared for government consideration, aims to shift focus from responding to abuse after it occurs to proactively identifying risks, coordinating protection, and ultimately preventing deaths.

Mauritius has updated its legal framework with the Domestic Abuse Act 2026, which broadens the definition of abuse to include psychological, emotional, economic, digital, and coercive forms. The government has also established frontline Family Support Services and continues to train first responders. However, the proposal highlights that legislation alone is insufficient to avert all tragedies.

The core challenge addressed is the ability of government and civil society to collectively identify high-risk families early, share information effectively, and intervene before domestic violence escalates to severe injury or death. The proposed partnership seeks to bridge this gap by formalizing collaboration between the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family Welfare and competent NGOs.

Why it mattersThis initiative aims to protect individuals experiencing domestic abuse by improving early detection and intervention, potentially saving lives.
