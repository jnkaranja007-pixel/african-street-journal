Prime Minister Contradicts Health Minister's Surgery Account

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam stated the surgery was performed by a private practitioner, contradicting his Health Minister's earlier claims.

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam directly contradicted Health Minister Anil Bachoo's account of his recent surgery at Dr. A. G. Jeetoo Hospital. Responding to the opposition leader in Parliament, Ramgoolam stated that the operation was performed by a private practitioner, Dr. Sookha, not the public hospital's surgeon as Bachoo had asserted for ten days. Bachoo had previously claimed his private doctor was merely an observer and that the procedure was standard practice.

The Prime Minister's statement implies Minister Bachoo was unaware of who performed his surgery, as Ramgoolam suggested Bachoo was under anesthesia and thus unable to know the details. This explanation has been met with skepticism, as it suggests Bachoo confidently provided a version of events he could not have personally verified. The contradiction has fueled controversy and calls for clarification on the authorization and public resources used.

The government is now considering a protocol to allow public access to similar arrangements in the future. This announcement raises questions about the legitimacy of the Health Minister's procedure, given that a protocol for such interventions did not exist at the time. The situation has led to accusations that Minister Bachoo misled the public regarding his surgery.

Why it mattersThe public is affected by potential misuse of public resources and a lack of transparency regarding medical procedures for government officials.

Mauritius Reassesses Drug War Metrics Amid Rising Arrests

The nation's drug control strategy now emphasizes reducing harm and demand over solely focusing on enforcement statistics.

Mauritius is shifting its approach to the drug problem, moving beyond a simple measure of success based on arrests and seizures. The National Drug Control Master Plan 2026–2030 introduces a four-pillar strategy: reducing drug supply and demand, improving health and wellbeing, and fostering a coordinated national response. This plan acknowledges that drugs present multifaceted challenges across criminal justice, public health, social, economic, and governance sectors.

In 2024, authorities recorded 4,373 drug-related infractions and made 2,520 arrests, according to data from the National Drug Observatory. While these figures indicate significant enforcement activity, they do not necessarily reflect a reduction in drug use or associated harms. The policy shift aims to evaluate the effectiveness of interventions by measuring tangible outcomes rather than just enforcement actions.

The new framework seeks to answer more complex questions, such as whether drug dependency is decreasing, if major trafficking networks are being dismantled, and if drug-related hospital admissions and deaths are declining. The focus is on achieving measurable reductions in drug-related harm and improving community safety through a comprehensive and coordinated national effort.

Why it mattersThe shift in drug control policy affects all citizens by seeking to reduce drug-related harm and improve public health outcomes.

Absa Warns Customers of Fake Mobile Banking App Scams

Absa Mauritius is alerting clients to fraudulent Facebook pages and websites promoting a non-existent mobile banking application.

Absa Mauritius has issued a warning to its customers regarding fake Facebook pages and websites that are falsely advertising a new mobile banking application. The bank has clarified that it has not launched any new application through these channels, urging clients to be vigilant.

Fraudsters are reportedly using advertisements and messages to trick users into clicking malicious links or divulging personal information. Absa advises customers to only download applications from official platforms and to never share passwords, PINs, or one-time passwords. The bank also stressed the importance of being wary of suspicious links.

This alert aims to protect customers from potential financial fraud and identity theft. By emphasizing the use of official app stores and secure practices, Absa seeks to prevent clients from falling victim to these deceptive online tactics.

Why it mattersAbsa Mauritius customers are at risk of having their personal and financial information stolen through fraudulent mobile banking app promotions.

Wife Recovers Vehicle Seized in Drug Trafficking Probe

Véronique Leu-Govind's GWM Tank 300 was returned after police approved a request from her lawyer.

Véronique Leu-Govind has regained possession of her GWM Tank 300 all-terrain vehicle. The keys were handed over on Tuesday morning, September 1, 2026, at the Western Division police headquarters. This restitution follows a formal request made by her lawyer, Me Shafick Chuttur, to the police and the Director of Public Prosecutions office. The vehicle had been seized by the ADSU of Rose-Hill on July 30 as part of an investigation into alleged zamal trafficking between La Réunion and Mauritius.

The operation that led to the vehicle's seizure also resulted in the arrest of Leu-Govind's husband, Gulshan Govind. Police investigations at the time focused on the discovery of a methadone vial and cannabis at their home, and Govind's potential involvement in drug trafficking. The GWM Tank 300 underwent examination by Scene of Crime Office experts before its return to Leu-Govind.

Gulshan Govind remains in provisional detention. His next court appearance is scheduled for September 7, 2026, at 1 p.m. before the Bambous tribunal. The investigation into the alleged zamal trafficking continues, with authorities examining the extent of the operation and any connections between La Réunion and Mauritius.

Why it mattersVéronique Leu-Govind is affected because her vehicle was seized as part of an investigation into her husband's alleged drug trafficking activities.
