Mauritius economy to grow 3.5% in 2027

SBM Insights forecasts 2.9% growth this year, citing global uncertainty and local constraints.

Mauritius' economy is projected to grow by 2.9% in 2026, with a rebound to 3.5% expected in 2027, according to SBM Insights. This forecast is attributed to an uncertain international climate, geopolitical tensions, and local structural issues. Inflation, which reached 5.7% in September 2026, is expected to stabilize around 5% by year-end, though household purchasing power remains pressured by the high cost of living and production.

Key growth sectors identified include tourism, financial services, information and communication technology (ICT), and hospitality. Public and private investments in infrastructure, renewable energy, and housing are also anticipated to drive recovery. However, export-oriented manufacturing and textiles face challenges from increased international competition.

To secure medium-term growth, SBM Insights outlined six priorities: controlling the cost of living, ensuring energy access and renewable transition, fiscal consolidation, accelerating investments, diversifying growth engines, and strengthening economic resilience. The report warns that escalating Middle East conflicts or supply chain disruptions could negatively impact commodity and food prices.

Why it mattersMauritius' economy faces growth challenges and inflation pressures, impacting household purchasing power and business operations.

Mauritius plans to dismantle Airport Holdings

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam announced the government's intention to restructure Airport Holdings Ltd.

The Mauritian government plans to dismantle Airport Holdings Ltd and reorganize its structure, Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam stated in Parliament on Tuesday. This move aims to address issues with responsibilities and potential conflicts within the company's entities.

Ramgoolam explained that action has already begun to review the current structure. He also addressed the possibility of re-listing Air Mauritius on the Stock Exchange of Mauritius, noting its withdrawal during voluntary administration when the company was near bankruptcy under the previous administration.

Air Mauritius is still in a recovery phase following its exit from voluntary administration. The government's priority is to strengthen its financial foundations before considering a re-listing on the stock exchange.

Why it mattersThe government's plan to dismantle Airport Holdings and reorganize its structure will affect the management and operations of state-owned aviation entities.

Mauritius police sergeant detained until October 30

Sergeant Gautam Singh Taukoor faces charges of using a firearm to intimidate and causing involuntary wounds.

Police Sergeant Gautam Singh Taukoor will remain in custody until October 30 after appearing before the Rose-Hill court on Monday, October 26. Police opposed his release, and he was returned to provisional detention.

The officer is accused of firing a shot towards Élodie Thomas in Camp-Levieux on October 9. He denies pointing his firearm at Rudy Soobrayen and claims the driver did not follow police instructions that day.

Two provisional charges have been brought against Taukoor: 'Using Firearm to Intimidate' and 'Involuntary Wounds and Blows by Imprudence'. He was questioned by the Independent Police Complaints Commission on Friday.

Why it mattersSergeant Gautam Singh Taukoor is detained until October 30 following charges related to a firearm incident.
