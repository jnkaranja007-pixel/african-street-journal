Mauritius election spending probes stall

Two years after the 2024 general elections, investigations into alleged overspending by nine candidates are ongoing.

Investigations into alleged breaches of electoral spending limits from the November 2024 general elections in Mauritius remain unresolved nearly two years later. Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam stated in a written parliamentary response on December 9, 2025, that the Central Criminal Investigation Department (CCID) is continuing its inquiry into seven Members of Parliament. These MPs were named in eight complaints filed in February 2025 concerning their declared election expenses.

The CCID has collected statements from complainants and heard from representatives of the Electoral Commissioner's office, which has provided requested information. Section 74B of the Representation of the People Act allows candidates to seek Supreme Court exemption for inadvertent irregularities, but this does not confirm innocence or resolve police investigations. The exact number of applications to the Supreme Court and their progress are not publicly confirmed.

Questions persist regarding whether the Supreme Court has reviewed these applications and when the CCID's findings will be sent to the Director of Public Prosecutions. The ongoing nature of these investigations highlights the slow pace of action regarding the alleged electoral spending violations from the 2024 polls.

Why it mattersThe public and political bodies are awaiting the conclusion of investigations into alleged electoral spending violations by seven Members of Parliament following the 2024 general elections.

France and EU back Mauritius with Rs 6.3 billion for water crisis

The package combines a 100 million euro AFD loan with a 20 million euro EU grant, pending final AFD board approval.

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam secured a 120 million euro package, roughly Rs 6.3 billion, during an official visit to Paris, according to a statement published on Tuesday, November 10. The funds are meant to transform drinking water supply in Mauritius.

The package includes a 100 million euro loan from the Agence française de développement and a 20 million euro grant from the European Union. The agreement still requires final validation by the AFD board, which is scheduled for the following week. The money will go to renovating water infrastructure, improving production and distribution, and sharply reducing leaks on the national network.

Ramgoolam also held talks with the AFD director general on future cooperation in waste management, renewable energy and the ecological transition from 2027. He expressed strong gratitude to the AFD, the French government and the European Union for the partnership.

Why it mattersResidents served by the national water network stand to gain from upgraded infrastructure and reduced leaks, as the funding targets those areas directly.

Mauritius TV debate to weigh government's two years in power

Murvind Beetun will host trade unionist Reaz Chuttoo and observer Harish Chundunsing on Tuesday, with the government also invited to take part.

Two years after the general elections in Mauritius, the television programme Au Cœur de l’Info will examine what the government has achieved, what challenges it faces and whether it can correct its course before its mandate ends.

The debate, set for Tuesday, 10 November, will be hosted by Murvind Beetun. He will receive trade unionist Reaz Chuttoo and observer Harish Chundunsing, and an invitation has also been sent to the government.

The programme is titled as a live debate on the two years in power, with the question of what the record shows at its centre.

Why it mattersMauritian voters and public-sector workers get a public test of the government's record at the halfway point of its term.

Port-Louis street closed until December 21 after landslide

Rue Monseigneur-Leen in Port-Louis is shut for repairs to a damaged road and retaining wall.

Rue Monseigneur-Leen in Port-Louis is closed to traffic until December 21 following a landslide. The road surface and a retaining wall were damaged, requiring emergency repairs to secure the area.

The closure has caused significant traffic jams in the region for several days. Constable Bartally advised drivers to respect the diversions, noting that those coming from Old Moka Road must now proceed towards Belle-Village and La Butte.

Drivers who usually use Rue Monseigneur-Leen to reach Rue Labourdonnais will need to alter their routes and expect delays.

Why it mattersDrivers in Port-Louis face traffic disruptions and must use alternative routes due to the road closure.

Port-Louis drivers face delays of up to 90 minutes after road closure and crash

Rue Monseigneur Leen will stay shut until December 21 while crews rebuild a road surface damaged by a landslide.

Motorists heading to Port-Louis faced long delays on Tuesday, November 10, after the closure of Rue Monseigneur Leen, which has been shut since November 6. The closure is for reconstruction work after a landslide damaged the road surface, and it is scheduled to last until December 21.

Some drivers from the central regions took up to 90 minutes, and in some cases longer, to reach the capital during the morning peak. A crash on the highway near Hindu House made the congestion worse. In the afternoon, restrictions were also placed on access roads at Bell-Village and Rue Labourdonnais, with some lanes reserved for residents.

The Traffic Branch, through Constable Bartally, is urging motorists to follow the diversions. Rue Monseigneur Leen remains closed to general traffic, and drivers from Bell-Village are directed to Old Moka Road, while those coming from the highway should use the Caudan roundabout and then pass the Casernes centrales.

Why it mattersCommuters from the central regions must allow up to 90 minutes to reach Port-Louis while the Rue Monseigneur Leen closure lasts until December 21.
