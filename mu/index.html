Police await psychiatric report for father in infant's death investigation

The Criminal Investigation Division is awaiting psychiatric evaluation results for Jean Daniel Perrine, 30, father of seven-month-old Adriano Kelyano, who died on August 23.

The investigation into the suspicious death of seven-month-old Adriano Kelyano has reached a critical stage as police await a psychiatric report. Jean Daniel Perrine, the infant's 30-year-old father, is currently under surveillance at Brown-Séquard Hospital, with authorities needing medical clearance before resuming his interrogation.

Adriano's mother, Marie Anne-Sophie Milazar, 20, found her son's body wrapped in a sheet on a mattress with stab wounds after a brief absence. An autopsy confirmed the infant died from a fatal stab wound to the abdomen. Perrine, an employee at the Roche-Bois landfill, was arrested shortly after and reportedly claimed he acted due to auditory hallucinations.

Perrine has been provisionally charged with murder in Port-Louis court and was denied bail. His next court appearance is scheduled for August 31, following the expected psychiatric evaluation conclusions.

Why it mattersThe local community in Roche-Bois is directly affected by the ongoing investigation into the death of an infant and the legal proceedings against the father.

Court strikes money laundering charge against Sattar Hajee Abdoula

A provisional money laundering accusation against Sattar Hajee Abdoula, stemming from alleged events at AfrAsia Bank in Port Louis on March 31, 2020, has been dismissed.

A court has dismissed a provisional money laundering charge against Sattar Hajee Abdoula. The accusation, which alleged events on March 31, 2020, at AfrAsia Bank in Port Louis, was challenged by Abdoula's lawyer, Me Raouf Gulbul, who filed a motion to cancel the charge.

Me Gulbul argued that there was no reasonable suspicion to maintain the provisional charge against his client. He asserted that Abdoula was not the signatory of the contract awarded by Air Mauritius to Grant Thornton for advisory services. This contract was central to the allegations.

Furthermore, Me Gulbul highlighted that Air Mauritius had not initiated any legal action against Grant Thornton to claim any outstanding sum. The court's decision to strike the provisional charge was rendered this Thursday, as reported by Ion News and Defi Media.

Why it mattersThe dismissal of the money laundering charge affects Sattar Hajee Abdoula by clearing him of a serious provisional accusation, impacting his legal standing and public perception.

Mauritius trade deficit expands 12.5% in first half 2026

Mauritius' trade deficit reached Rs 113.1 billion in the first six months of 2026, marking a 12.5% increase compared to the same period in 2025.

Statistics Mauritius reported on Wednesday that the country's trade deficit grew to Rs 113.1 billion in the first half of 2026, up from Rs 45.6 billion in the first quarter. Imports totaled Rs 167.1 billion, while exports reached Rs 54 billion during this period.

The second quarter saw a significant deterioration, with imports rising to Rs 96.4 billion, an 18.6% increase year-on-year. Energy products were a major factor, with mineral fuels, lubricants, and related products accounting for Rs 32.9 billion, or 34.1% of all imports, a 71.5% rise from the second quarter of 2025.

Exports struggled to keep pace, growing only 0.2% to Rs 54 billion over the six months. While chemical products and food and live animals saw increases, these were offset by declines in machinery, transport equipment, and various manufactured articles. Statistics Mauritius projects the annual deficit for 2026 to be around Rs 215 billion.

Why it mattersThe widening trade deficit means the Mauritian economy is spending more on imports than it earns from exports, impacting the national balance of payments and potentially the value of the Mauritian Rupee.

Health Minister Bachoo defends public hospital surgery choice

Health Minister Anil Bachoo defended his decision to undergo surgery at Dr A. G. Jeetoo hospital, addressing union concerns about his private doctor's presence.

Health Minister Anil Bachoo addressed controversy surrounding his surgery at Dr A. G. Jeetoo hospital on Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, two thousand twenty-six. He stated that a public sector surgeon performed the operation, with his private doctor, who has followed him for over ten years, present only as an observer. Bachoo claimed he would have faced criticism regardless of his choice, citing potential accusations of not trusting public hospitals if he went private, or having too much money if he sought care abroad.

The Minister also responded to questions from unions regarding the protocol applied in his case and whether similar arrangements would be available to all patients. He indicated he would address these concerns in Parliament. Bachoo further noted that private sector doctors, including over one hundred fifty from other countries, openly operate in public hospitals.

Bachoo emphasized that his priority remains saving lives, stating, "For me, what is important is that the work gets done." While he asserts he did what was necessary for his health, unions are seeking clarification on whether ordinary citizens would receive the same treatment, raising questions about transparency and equality in the public health system.

Why it mattersThe controversy surrounding the Health Minister's surgery and the role of private doctors in public hospitals directly affects citizens by questioning equitable access to healthcare and established protocols.

Mauritius Government, NGOs propose domestic abuse fatality prevention partnership

A new policy proposal recommends a formalized partnership between the Mauritian Government and non-governmental organizations to prevent domestic abuse fatalities.

A civil society policy proposal, prepared by FAM (Fam an Mars), suggests a stronger partnership between the Mauritian Government and competent NGOs to prevent domestic abuse fatalities. The proposal aims to shift from reactive responses to early risk identification and coordinated protection, particularly in cases with a history of violence, threats, or coercive control.

The Ministry of Gender Equality and Family Welfare is identified as a key government entity in this proposed collaboration. The initiative seeks to address the limitations of legislation alone in preventing tragedies, despite the Domestic Abuse Act 2026 having broadened the definition of abusive behavior to include psychological, emotional, economic, digital, and coercive forms.

The core idea is to enable the Government and civil society to identify high-risk families earlier, coordinate available information, and intervene before violence escalates. This proactive approach intends to prevent serious injury or death by focusing on prevention rather than solely responding after incidents occur.

Why it mattersThis proposal directly affects families at risk of domestic abuse by aiming to provide earlier intervention and protection against violence.
