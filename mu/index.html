Mauritius Lawmakers Propose Stricter Road Traffic Penalties

New legislation targets drunk driving, drug use, and road rage with vehicle seizures and license suspensions.

The Mauritian government is set to introduce the Road Traffic (Amendment) Bill 2026 to the National Assembly, proposing significant changes to traffic laws. The bill aims to curb serious accidents and fatalities by introducing stricter penalties for dangerous driving behaviors. Key provisions include the potential seizure of vehicles and suspension of driving licenses for individuals caught driving under the influence of alcohol, drugs, or other intoxicating substances.

A new specific offense for 'road rage' will be created under the proposed legislation, alongside enhanced sanctions for fatal road accidents. Drivers causing death while operating a vehicle without a valid license, provisional license, or international driving permit will face more severe penalties. The bill also targets road racing and aims to increase penalties for deaths resulting from dangerous or reckless driving while under the influence of intoxicants.

Beyond serious offenses, the bill includes measures for daily traffic management, such as the possibility of a digital driving license and online payment for certain Fixed Penalty Notices, excluding those involving penalty points. Additionally, the legislation will address excessive noise pollution from exhaust systems, introducing provisions to better regulate these emissions.

Why it mattersDrivers in Mauritius face stricter penalties including vehicle seizure and license suspension for offenses like drunk driving, drug use, and road rage.

Mauritius Trade Deficit Widens to Rs 215 Billion

Official projections indicate a record Rs 325 billion in imports by year-end 2026, significantly outpacing stagnant exports.

Mauritius faces a widening trade deficit, projected to reach Rs 215 billion by the end of 2026, according to Statistics Mauritius. Import costs are expected to climb 3.2% to Rs 325 billion, while exports will only reach Rs 110 billion, a slight 1.9% increase. This imbalance means the country will import nearly three times more goods than it exports, highlighting a structural economic issue.

This revised forecast from the August 26, 2026, report follows earlier projections. In February 2026, the deficit was estimated at Rs 210 billion, with imports at Rs 320 billion. By May 2026, export projections were lowered, pushing the deficit forecast to Rs 215 billion. The deficit for the first half of 2026 already stands at Rs 113.10 billion, with imports at Rs 167.09 billion against exports of Rs 53.99 billion.

The trade deficit has been on a downward trend, increasing from Rs 205.87 billion in 2024 to Rs 207.89 billion in 2025. The current trajectory suggests a continued deterioration in the nation's external trade balance, raising concerns about economic management and the country's reliance on imports.

Why it mattersInvestors are directly affected by the widening trade deficit, which signals potential economic instability and impacts the country's financial standing.

Mauritius School Phone Guidelines Spark Bureaucratic Futility

New Ministry of Education guidelines on student mobile device use are expected to waste instructional time and prove difficult to enforce.

The Ministry of Education in Mauritius has introduced new guidelines for mobile phone use on school premises, aiming for "Educational Focus, Respect and Privacy Responsible Use, Security and Safety." While the policy requires phones to be silenced and stored, critics argue it is an unenforceable bureaucratic exercise that will detract from teaching time. The guidelines empower school heads and educators to implement sanctions ranging from verbal warnings to temporary exclusion, each requiring significant administrative effort.

Enforcing these guidelines presents a logistical challenge for teachers and administrators. Each step of the graduated sanction system necessitates documentation, parent meetings, and administrative follow-up, diverting time from educational duties like curriculum development and student support. International research suggests that exclusionary discipline practices, such as rustication, are not effective in improving school safety or behavior and can lead to worse academic and social outcomes for students.

Global research indicates that strict phone bans or restrictive policies in schools are often ineffective. Studies show that teenagers frequently check phones during school hours, and higher frequency correlates with reduced concentration and poorer test performance. The mere presence of phones, even when not actively used, can significantly distract students and negatively impact their academic performance, suggesting that the new guidelines may not achieve their intended educational objectives.

Why it mattersStudents and teachers are affected by new school phone guidelines that critics argue will waste instructional time and prove difficult to enforce.

SME Mauritius CEO Ravin Rampersad suspended pending investigation

The board of SME Mauritius Ltd suspended its CEO, Ravin Rampersad, on Friday, August 28, to allow for an inquiry into irregularities.

SME Mauritius Ltd's board of directors has suspended Chief Executive Officer Ravin Rampersad, effective Friday, August 28. The suspension is pending the outcome of an investigation into irregularities identified by the board. Raj Kishore Bunjun, former Permanent Secretary at the Ministry of Industry, SMEs and Cooperatives, has been appointed Officer-in-Charge to ensure the continuity of operations.

The board stated that the investigation aims to establish facts and responsibilities concerning the noted irregularities. This measure is intended to safeguard the organization's proper functioning while ensuring a thorough examination of the issues. Rampersad had been leading SME Mauritius since June 18, 2018, having previously worked at Innodis Poultry Ltd and as a consultant for SMEDA.

The suspension follows several years of Rampersad's tenure at the helm of SME Mauritius. His prior roles included a consultancy at the Small and Medium Enterprise Development Authority (SMEDA) from January to November 2017. The board will make further decisions based on the investigation's findings.

Why it mattersThe suspension of SME Mauritius CEO Ravin Rampersad affects the organization's leadership and operational continuity due to an ongoing investigation into irregularities.

Mauritius Pays USD 59.5 Million Towards SADC Contributions

The nation has remitted a significant portion of its financial obligations to the Southern African Development Community, following a recapitalization of dues.

Mauritius has paid over USD 59.5 million as a first installment towards its newly established financial contributions for the 2026/27 fiscal year to the Southern African Development Community (SADC). This payment was confirmed by the Council of Ministers, who also reviewed the nation's economic opportunities within the regional bloc, emphasizing integration and green industrialization.

The government highlighted that despite inheriting economic challenges, over USD 108.26 million was paid by January 31, 2025, to regularize past commitments. The total amount due from Mauritius for the 2026/27 period is USD 67,693,993, with the initial payment covering a substantial part of this sum.

These financial transactions occur as Mauritius navigates its role within SADC, having previously announced its withdrawal from the Mutual Defence Pact in March 2025 due to budgetary pressures. Discussions are ongoing with the SADC secretariat and presidency regarding the country's future engagement and integration strategy.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by Mauritius's ongoing financial commitments to the SADC, which indicate its continued participation and potential for regional economic integration and trade.
