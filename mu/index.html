Police sergeant accused of shooting woman remains in custody

Sergeant Gautam Singh Taukoor faces charges of intimidation and involuntary wounding after an incident on October 9.

Police Sergeant Gautam Singh Taukoor will remain in detention until November 6, when a court will decide on his bail request. Taukoor is accused of firing his weapon on October 9 in Camp-Levieux, hitting 27-year-old cashier Élodie Thomas. The incident occurred as Thomas was returning home from her job at the Winner’s supermarket in Tribeca Mall.

Thomas was struck in the neck by a bullet that penetrated the rear windshield of the van she was travelling in. She was taken to the hospital and is reported to be in stable but concerning condition. Taukoor faces provisional charges of using a firearm to intimidate and causing involuntary wounds through imprudence.

The Director of Public Prosecutions intends to oppose Taukoor's bail application. The court of district of Rose-Hill will hear arguments on the bail motion on Friday, November 6.

Why it mattersÉlodie Thomas, a 27-year-old cashier, was injured and remains in stable but concerning condition after being shot.

Air Mauritius reports Rs 4.1 billion profit for 2025-2026

The national airline's positive equity of Rs 1.37 billion returns after being negative since the Covid-19 pandemic.

Air Mauritius announced a profit of Rs 4.1 billion for the 2025-2026 financial year. This result, released on Friday, October 30, 2026, includes accounting adjustments from the Covid-19 pandemic and unrealised gains from monetary asset and liability revaluations.

The profit also accounts for re-evaluated provisions for flight ticket refunds, unused travel vouchers issued during the pandemic, and favourable fuel prices and a strong euro. Air Mauritius's equity has returned to a positive Rs 1.37 billion, a significant recovery from its negative state post-pandemic.

Despite the positive figures, the board urged caution, noting that high fuel prices in 2026-2027 cannot be fully passed on to ticket prices. CEO André Viljoen stated the results are encouraging but called for continued effort, as favourable exchange rates and fuel prices are not guaranteed for the next fiscal year.

Why it mattersAir Mauritius, the national airline, has returned to profitability and positive equity, benefiting shareholders and the company's financial standing.

Mauritius vegetables fail pesticide tests

Official reports show 59% of fruits and vegetables sold between October 2025 and June 2026 did not meet safety standards.

Food safety in Mauritius collapsed between October 2025 and June 2026, with official pesticide residue analysis showing a sharp decline in compliance for fruits and vegetables.

The Pesticides Regulatory Office reported that 59% of produce failed to meet standards, a situation exacerbated by a six-month gap in official publications between November 2025 and April 2026.

Kripalloo Naik, president of the Small Planters Association, stated that natural produce sells for Rs 5 per pound while treated vegetables fetch up to Rs 20 per pound, pushing farmers towards excessive chemical use.

Why it mattersMauritian consumers faced a heightened risk of purchasing contaminated produce due to widespread non-compliance with pesticide regulations.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Mauritius cabinet weighs lowering criminal responsibility age

Government considers prosecuting 12-year-olds after riots, but legal experts cite lack of evidence and system strain.

The Mauritian government is considering lowering the age of criminal responsibility to 12 years old, a move prompted by recent riots where minors were among those arrested. Cabinet discussed the proposal on October 14, but has opted for wider consultation via a parliamentary Select Committee before any decision is made. The current Children's Act 2020, effective since January 2022, sets the minimum age at 14.

Police reported that 16 minors were among the 151 people arrested during the unrest, representing about one in nine arrestees. However, legal experts point out that a lower age limit would only apply to future cases due to constitutional bars on retroactive punishment. The Mauritian justice system already faces significant delays, as highlighted in past Privy Council cases concerning the right to a hearing within a reasonable time.

No Mauritian studies demonstrate that prosecuting younger children deters violence, and the UN Committee on the Rights of the Child advises against lowering the age, warning of potential harm from the criminal justice system. The proposed change would also necessitate adjustments to the existing juvenile court system, which defines a 'juvenile' as someone aged 14 to under 18, requiring staff training and facility modifications.

Why it mattersThe government's consideration of prosecuting 12-year-olds could bring more young people into a justice system that already struggles with delays.
