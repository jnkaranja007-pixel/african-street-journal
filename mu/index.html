Youth Parliament Debates Future of Mauritius

Seventy-five young participants aged 14 to 23 are debating key issues including artificial intelligence, food security, and sustainable development.

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam launched the fifth National Youth Parliament on August 28, bringing together 75 young Mauritians aged 14 to 23. For two days, these participants are stepping into the roles of parliamentarians to discuss critical issues that will shape the nation's future. The agenda includes topics such as artificial intelligence, food security, sustainable development, peace, justice, and strong institutions.

Ramgoolam urged the youth to become creators, innovators, and entrepreneurs rather than just consumers of technology. He also encouraged them to deepen their understanding of democracy, respect diverse opinions, and advocate for robust institutions. Shirin Aumeeruddy-Cziffra highlighted that the initiative allows young people to experience parliamentary operations firsthand while learning to articulate their ideas and respect others'.

This two-day event provides a platform for the younger generation not only to discuss the future but also to actively begin constructing it. The discussions focus on subjects that directly impact the generation that will live with the consequences of today's decisions, offering them a direct role in shaping their nation's trajectory.

Why it mattersYoung people aged 14 to 23 are directly affected as they will live with the decisions made today regarding artificial intelligence, food security, and sustainable development.

Police quell Sable-Noir clashes over man's death

Tensions flared Sunday in Sable-Noir, leading to police intervention after a family contested the drowning death of Adam Stephen Jarome Dubois.

Police units intervened Sunday in Sable-Noir to restore calm after a violent altercation erupted. The unrest stemmed from the August 16 death of 38-year-old Adam Stephen Jarome Dubois, whose body was found in the Pointe-aux-Sables lagoon. His family disputes the official account of drowning, citing unanswered questions and the potential testimony of a witness.

Family members are demanding a full investigation into the circumstances surrounding Dubois' death, believing significant details remain unclear. They have indicated that a witness present at the time of the incident could provide crucial information to advance the inquiry. The situation escalated with reports of stone-throwing, prompting the deployment of the Special Supporting Unit (SSU) to manage the crowd.

Following the police intervention, order was eventually re-established in the Sable-Noir area. The family's persistent calls for clarity regarding the exact cause of Adam Dubois' death fueled the public's anger and the subsequent confrontation.

Why it mattersThe public unrest in Sable-Noir directly affects local residents by disrupting daily life and requiring police intervention.

El Niño, IOD Disrupt Mauritius Rainfall

Farmers face declining yields and water scarcity as the combined effects of El Niño and the Indian Ocean Dipole alter local weather patterns.

Farmers across Mauritius are observing erratic rainfall patterns, with reservoirs and rivers diminishing and soil cracking. This phenomenon is attributed to the combined impact of El Niño, originating in the Pacific Ocean, and the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD). These climate drivers disrupt global rainfall, leading to unpredictable weather events on the island, including prolonged dry spells interspersed with sudden downpours.

The agricultural sector, particularly sugarcane cultivation, is highly sensitive to water stress, leading to reduced yields and impacting both livelihoods and export revenues. Vegetable growers are experiencing crop wilting, and livestock owners are contending with shrinking pastures. This water insecurity extends to households, schools, and hospitals, which are implementing rationing and adjusting operations.

The Central Water Authority is under pressure to maintain consistent supply amidst failing rains, highlighting the fragility of the island's reliance on rainfall for water security. The situation underscores an urgent need for Mauritius to diversify its water management strategies beyond traditional rainfall replenishment of reservoirs and aquifers.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected by declining crop yields and reduced pastureland due to unpredictable rainfall.

Ministry says 2025 domestic violence report did not signal immediate danger

Government services stated that a prior domestic violence report was insufficient to deem a seven-month-old baby in immediate peril.

A seven-month-old baby, Adriano Kelyano Perrine, was found dead at his Roche-Bois home on August 23 after suffering a fatal stab wound. His father, Jean Daniel Perrine, 30, faces a provisional murder charge. The Ministry of Gender Equality and Family Welfare confirmed a domestic violence incident was reported in 2025, involving the Family Support Services (FSS). The mother had initially sought a protection order but later withdrew it.

The ministry stated that no further reports concerning the child's safety were received by the FSS prior to the infant's death. "The FSS of the region had received no information indicating that the baby was exposed to imminent or serious danger," the ministry said, adding that services can only act on information provided. The ministry maintains that the 2025 domestic violence record alone was not enough to conclude the baby was in immediate danger.

The ministry is now reviewing the circumstances to determine what information was available and what actions were taken. This review will examine detection, assessment, follow-up, and coordination mechanisms for domestic violence cases involving children, aiming to identify any gaps in information handling or communication. The incident raises questions about the level of follow-up required for families with a history of domestic violence and the sharing of information between different services.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Gender Equality and Family Welfare's assessment of past reports affects how child protection services evaluate and respond to domestic violence cases.

Internet Arrives on Raphael Island via Satellite

A new contract will bring satellite internet to Raphael Island, improving coastal surveillance, weather forecasts, and economic activities.

Raphael Island in the St Brandon archipelago is set to gain internet access through a new contract signed between the Information and Communication Technologies Authority (ICTA) and Emtel Ltd. The Universal Service Fund (USF) is financing the initiative, which will utilize low-Earth orbit satellites to provide internet speeds of 34 Mbps download and 12 Mbps upload.

This new digital connection is intended for more than just general browsing. It will link the National Coast Guard's coastal surveillance radar system and provide dedicated access for the National Coast Guard, Mauritius Meteorological Services, and Raphael Fishing Co Ltd. The service is expected to be operational by the end of August for a two-year period.

Minister of Information Technology, Communication and Innovation Avinash Ramtohul stated that geographical distance should not prevent outer island communities from accessing digital services and economic opportunities. The installation is currently underway, aiming to enhance maritime security, weather forecasting, emergency communications, and local business operations.

Why it mattersRaphael Island residents and businesses will benefit from improved communication for maritime security, weather services, and economic operations.
