Mauritius cancels Cambridge exams over security concerns

Students face uncertainty as government announces reliance on assessed grades following exam cancellations.

Cambridge exams scheduled for October 13 in Mauritius were cancelled due to recent tensions, leaving students uncertain about their evaluations. Suttyhudeo Tengur stated on Facebook that while student safety was paramount, their academic future requires equal determination. The government announced that "assessed grades" would be used for these candidates, assuring no one would be penalised.

Tengur questioned the official announcement, asking why this solution was presented as final before Cambridge's agreement and demanding the Ministry of Education release Cambridge Assessment International Education's written response. He warned that statistical methods might not reflect individual potential, and small discrepancies could alter university paths. Tengur urged the ministry and the Mauritius Examinations Syndicate to clarify evaluation methods, eligibility, and review procedures.

He also called for a dedicated contact person, clear deadlines, and pedagogical and psychological support. Tengur suggested financial aid if a new session becomes necessary for some candidates. He insisted Cambridge must justify its methods and Mauritian authorities must firmly defend students' interests, ensuring no student faces avoidable academic penalties due to the crisis.

Why it mattersMauritian students face academic uncertainty and potential penalties due to cancelled Cambridge exams.

Mauritius man identifies sabre attacker by voice and eyes

A 23-year-old victim, attacked after his car broke down in Ébène, recognised his assailant despite a mask.

A 23-year-old man was attacked with a sabre on a vacant lot in Ébène during the night of October 20-21. His car had broken down around 00:10 when a masked individual armed with a sabre demanded they open the car door.

The assailant smashed the car window and beat the driver, striking his right thigh with the sabre before stealing the ignition key. The victim, who escaped on foot, told police he recognised the attacker by his eyes and voice, despite the mask. A woman in the car was unharmed.

The victim provided his statement to Rose-Hill police around 4 AM. An investigation has been opened to identify and locate the suspect. The injured man was taken to the hospital for treatment.

Why it mattersA 23-year-old man in Ébène was physically assaulted and injured.

Mauritius M4 highway environmental report due in November

The 30-kilometer M4 highway project's environmental impact assessment will be made public after review.

The M4 highway project, a planned 30-kilometer dual carriageway linking northern and eastern rural regions, was discussed in parliament on October 20. Member of Parliament Joanna Bérenger questioned Minister of National Infrastructure Ajay Gunness about public consultation outcomes and financing terms, including interest rates.

Minister Gunness stated that the environmental impact assessment report, submitted on April 24, 2026, is being revised to include feedback from three public consultations attended by 242 people. The amended report will be submitted to the Ministry of Environment in November 2026 and then released publicly. A $432 million credit line from Exim Bank of India covers the project, with a variable interest rate of approximately 5.87% at signing on July 22, 2026.

The loan has a 15-year term with a five-year capital repayment deferral, though the M4's specific repayment schedule awaits a dedicated credit agreement. Minister Gunness also confirmed that 25% of works and supplies will be reserved for Mauritian businesses, including SMEs and local suppliers.

Why it mattersMauritian citizens will be affected by the M4 highway project's environmental impact and local business participation.

Mauritius police seek uncle in child sexual abuse case

A six-year-old girl has confirmed sexual abuse by her uncle after a video circulated on Facebook.

Police in Mauritius are searching for a 27-year-old man identified as the uncle of a six-year-old girl who was sexually abused. The investigation began on October 20, 2026, after a video of the abuse was reported to the police hotline.

The child's mother recognized her brother in the video and suspected her daughter was the victim, a suspicion the girl later confirmed. Authorities are arranging for the child to be admitted to the hospital for necessary medical examinations.

The Ministry of Gender Equality and Family Welfare has been monitoring the case since October 20. The Family Protection Brigade is providing support to the minor and her family while the suspect remains at large.

Why it mattersA six-year-old girl in Mauritius has been subjected to sexual abuse by her uncle.
