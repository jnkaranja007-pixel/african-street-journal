Mauritius Labour Congress demands risk allowance extension

The union wants essential service workers like paramedics and nurses to receive extra pay for risks taken during curfew.

The Mauritius Labour Congress (MLC) is calling for the risk allowance, recently given to police officers, to be extended to other professions. MLC president Haniff Peerun stated that ambulance workers, nurses, and doctors also faced difficulties during the curfew while providing essential services.

Peerun believes these healthcare professionals deserve financial recognition for their commitment and the risks they encountered. The union's demand follows recent unrest that led to the allowance for police.

The MLC's request highlights ongoing discussions about compensation for essential workers who performed duties under challenging conditions. No specific date has been set for further discussions on this matter.

Why it mattersAmbulance workers, nurses, and doctors in Mauritius are seeking financial recognition for risks taken during curfew.

Mauritius police seek uncle in child abuse case

A six-year-old girl confirmed her uncle sexually abused her after a video circulated online.

Police in Mauritius are searching for a 27-year-old man identified as the uncle of a six-year-old girl, accused of sexual abuse. The investigation began on October 20, 2026, after a video of the abuse was reported to the police hotline.

The child's mother recognized her brother in the video and suspected her daughter was the victim. The girl later confirmed the abuse by her uncle, who was also her godfather. Authorities are arranging for the child to be admitted to the hospital for medical examinations.

The Ministry of Gender Equality and Family Welfare is closely monitoring the case and will provide necessary support to the minor and her family. The suspect remains at large as the police continue their search.

Why it mattersA six-year-old girl in Mauritius was sexually abused by her uncle.
