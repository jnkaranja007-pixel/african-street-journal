Court dismisses charges against Air Mauritius contractor

Magistrate Shaheen Daureeawoo ruled that internal governance failures at Air Mauritius do not constitute criminal offenses for external service providers.

Charges against Sattar Hajee Abdoula, a contractor for Air Mauritius, have been dismissed by Magistrate Shaheen Daureeawoo. The court found that contractual shortcomings and internal governance failures within Air Mauritius could not be automatically translated into criminal offenses for its service providers. The magistrate emphasized that penalizing a business operator for the management errors of its client was a conceptual drift the court would not permit.

The prosecution's case largely hinged on the argument that an "Advisory Services" contract worth Rs 8.5 million awarded to Grant Thornton bypassed Air Mauritius' internal procedures, including the absence of tenders, legal validation, and board approval. While acknowledging these administrative breaches, the court determined that responsibility for these lapses lay solely with Air Mauritius' internal management and could not be imputed to the contractor.

The ruling highlighted that no evidence demonstrated the contractor's involvement in circumventing procedures or knowledge of their bypass. The court noted that negotiating service scope, agreeing on fees, and issuing invoices are standard business practices. Furthermore, Air Mauritius' prolonged inaction in seeking repayment or issuing formal notices was also considered, with the magistrate concluding that these facts did not objectively establish the criminal nature of the payment.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the ruling clarifies that business dealings with state-owned entities do not automatically carry criminal risk for third parties due to the entity's internal mismanagement.

Socialist Leader Faces Pressure on Presidential Primary Bid

Olivier Faure, head of the Socialist Party, has not yet committed to running in the 2027 presidential primary, despite calls from within his party.

Olivier Faure, the first secretary of the Socialist Party, addressed hundreds of militants at the party's summer days in Blois, but did not declare his candidacy for the 2027 presidential election. He instead focused on a message of unity, criticizing President Emmanuel Macron's administration and predicting the collapse of the "central bloc."

Faure stated that the party would be present for the presidential election and defend "the law of the most just." He also pledged to censure the upcoming budget if it reflects the proposals of Gabriel Attal and Édouard Philippe, particularly their plans to reduce social contributions. The party leader expressed skepticism about any compromise between these two figures.

The Socialist Party leader proposed initiatives such as a national fund for reduced grocery prices for low-income families and a public reading service. He also called for a substantial national and European fund for ecological transition investments and suggested suspending dividends for companies not committed to environmental transformation. Faure reminded attendees that François Hollande began his presidential campaign at 3% and urged against dismissing polls, noting his own current 5% standing.

Why it mattersSocialist Party members are seeking a candidate to contest the 2027 presidential election, and Olivier Faure's decision on whether to run will determine the party's direction.

Mauritius School Phone Guidelines Spark Bureaucratic Futility

New Ministry of Education guidelines on student mobile device use are expected to waste instructional time and prove difficult to enforce.

The Ministry of Education in Mauritius has introduced new guidelines for mobile phone use on school premises, aiming for "Educational Focus, Respect and Privacy Responsible Use, Security and Safety." While the policy requires phones to be silenced and stored, critics argue it is an unenforceable bureaucratic exercise that will detract from teaching time. The guidelines empower school heads and educators to implement sanctions ranging from verbal warnings to temporary exclusion, each requiring significant administrative effort.

Enforcing these guidelines presents a logistical challenge for teachers and administrators. Each step of the graduated sanction system necessitates documentation, parent meetings, and administrative follow-up, diverting time from educational duties like curriculum development and student support. International research suggests that exclusionary discipline practices, such as rustication, are not effective in improving school safety or behavior and can lead to worse academic and social outcomes for students.

Global research indicates that strict phone bans or restrictive policies in schools are often ineffective. Studies show that teenagers frequently check phones during school hours, and higher frequency correlates with reduced concentration and poorer test performance. The mere presence of phones, even when not actively used, can significantly distract students and negatively impact their academic performance, suggesting that the new guidelines may not achieve their intended educational objectives.

Why it mattersStudents and teachers are affected by new school phone guidelines that critics argue will waste instructional time and prove difficult to enforce.

Politics · 2026-08-30

Prime Minister Defends Armored Car Purchase Amidst Public Scrutiny

Navin Ramgoolam addressed controversy over a Rs 30 million state vehicle, citing security needs and past precedents.

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam defended the purchase of an armored BMW for Rs 30 million, stating the vehicle was necessary for state protection. He noted that the car, which had broken down three times in 2025, was due for replacement since 2024. Ramgoolam referenced past security needs, recalling that Sir Anerood Jugnauth acquired four armored vehicles in three years following two assassination attempts in 1989.

Responding to a journalist's question about the expenditure, Ramgoolam used dismissive language, questioning the journalist's motives and suggesting critics should leave the country if dissatisfied. He compared Mauritius's stability and public services favorably to South Africa's challenges, including high unemployment and frequent power outages. This approach shifted the debate from the cost of the vehicle to the legitimacy of the inquiry.

The Prime Minister's remarks, including a reference to a "60-0" victory, were interpreted as a dismissal of public accountability and a reflection of his perceived political dominance following the November 2024 elections. The controversy highlights ongoing discussions about governmental transparency and the obligation of leaders to provide clear justifications for public spending.

Why it mattersThe purchase of an expensive armored vehicle and the Prime Minister's dismissive responses affect citizens by raising questions about government spending and accountability.
