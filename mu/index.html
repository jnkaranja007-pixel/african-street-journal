Government Revives Civic Education Program for 200 Students

Mauritius relaunches the National Initiative for Civic Education (NICE) program, aiming to instill patriotism and civic values in young citizens.

The Mauritian government has relaunched the National Initiative for Civic Education (NICE) program, placing it under the Prime Minister's Office and involving the Ministry of Education, Ministry of Youth and Sports, and the Police Force. The program aims to foster patriotism, responsibility, and civic values among youth. The pilot edition, running from November 16 to 28, 2026, will select 200 Grade 8 students aged 12 to 13 from across the country, including Rodrigues and Agaléga.

Over fourteen days, participants will engage in immersive training, with the first week at regional centers and the second in a residential setting. The curriculum focuses on national education, character development, discipline, emergency preparedness, safety, and community volunteering. The program's innovative hybrid format emphasizes experiential learning and communal living, bringing together civil and security entities to promote a sense of community and national cohesion.

This initiative is viewed as a strategic investment in the nation's future, designed to cultivate responsible, supportive citizens aware of their role in national development. Following this pilot phase and subsequent evaluations, the government plans a nationwide expansion starting in 2027. The residential component is intended to create a unique space for cultural and social mixing among young people from different islands and regions.

Why it mattersTwo hundred students aged 12 to 13 will participate in a two-week civic education program designed to foster patriotism and community cohesion.

News · 2026-09-06

Bus stations record 19 injured and two dead in two years

Minister Osman Mahomed reported 19 accidents with injuries and two fatalities at ten bus stations since January 2024.

Nineteen accidents, including two fatal ones, have occurred at ten bus stations and traffic centers across Mauritius since January 2024, according to Transport Minister Osman Mahomed. The minister provided the figures to Parliament on Tuesday, stating that 11 accidents resulted in minor injuries and six in serious injuries. Flacq station reported five accidents, one fatal, while Immigration station had four, three with serious injuries. Curepipe and Rose-Hill each recorded two accidents.

These official figures may not capture the full extent of risks, as accidents occurring near stations but outside their official boundaries are not included in the statistics. Several incidents have been reported near the Gare du Nord and Résidence La Cure. The question arises whether only incidents within administrative limits accurately reflect the danger posed by these transport hubs.

The minister noted that while stations are equipped with safety features like pedestrian crossings and lighting, their maintenance and the safety of immediate surroundings fall to local authorities. This highlights an ongoing challenge in ensuring comprehensive safety, especially as Mauritius saw a 10.9% increase in road accidents in the first half of 2026 compared to the previous year.

Why it mattersBus station users and nearby residents are affected by the 19 reported accidents, two of which were fatal, and the potential for unrecorded incidents near these transport hubs.

News · 2026-09-06

Advocate Seeks High Risk Framework After Child's Death

A civic advocate is calling for a coordinated system to identify and act on domestic violence risk signals following a child's death.

Seven-month-old Adrianno was fatally stabbed by his father in Roche Bois, an event that has prompted calls for a new approach to domestic violence prevention in Mauritius. Civic advocate Dharamraj Deenoo, Secretary General of Fam An Mars, described the tragedy as a failure of protection and a wound on society's conscience.

Deenoo proposed the creation of a High Risk Assessment Framework during a radio appearance. This system would facilitate information sharing among police, social workers, health professionals, and NGOs to identify and address imminent dangers. He argued that such a framework could have detected patterns of abuse or psychological instability before the fatal incident.

The advocate highlighted that domestic violence can escalate rapidly, leaving victims, particularly children, unprotected without a coordinated response. He stressed that the Domestic Abuse Act, while existing, lacks accessibility and that a High Risk Framework is an urgent necessity to prevent future tragedies.

Why it mattersThe death of a child due to domestic violence highlights a societal failure that could be addressed by a new framework for risk assessment.

Health · 2026-09-06

Ambulances Out of Service Hamper Health Ministry Fleet

Twenty-five of sixty-five ambulances are unavailable for service, with five deemed irreparable.

The Ministry of Health's ambulance fleet is operating at reduced capacity, with twenty-five of sixty-five vehicles currently out of service. Twenty ambulances are undergoing repairs or maintenance, while five are considered too old or damaged to be put back on the road. This situation raises concerns about the service's ability to meet demand, particularly during peak periods, as the SAMU handles emergency interventions, inter-facility transfers, and medical evacuations.

Minister of Health and Well-being Anil Bachoo informed Parliament that the twenty vehicles undergoing repair are expected to return to service. However, the five irreparable ambulances will not be replaced immediately. The ministry plans to acquire five new ambulances in the 2026-2027 budget to gradually replace aging vehicles. Bachoo also acknowledged that preventive maintenance had not been consistently performed and that a new system is being implemented to improve vehicle tracking and reduce downtime.

The current fleet reduction impacts the SAMU's capacity, which responded to fourteen thousand, six hundred seventy-three interventions in 2024. The planned acquisition of five new ambulances may not fully address the gap left by the twenty-five immobilized vehicles. The ministry is also reviewing staffing levels, with one hundred forty-four ambulance drivers and one hundred forty ambulance care attendants employed in 2024, to ensure personnel availability matches vehicle readiness.

Why it mattersTwenty-five ambulances are out of service, impacting the health ministry's ability to respond to emergencies and patient transfers.

Politics · 2026-09-06

Candidates Selected for Demographic Ratios, Not Competence

Mauritian political parties prioritize fitting demographic profiles over policy expertise when selecting candidates for constituencies.

Political strategists in Mauritius are reportedly mapping constituencies and selecting candidates based on demographic ratios and community ties rather than policy expertise or executive capability. This practice, often referred to as placing "parachute candidates," means individuals may be chosen to fulfill internal party quotas rather than to genuinely serve the local population's needs.

Instead of relying on qualifications, many parties opt for locally popular figures who are visible on social media and active in community events. However, popularity and competence are distinct skills, and such figures may lack the necessary understanding to address complex issues like public health, infrastructure, or the synthetic drug crisis, which is a significant concern for ordinary Mauritians.

A further concern is that the visibility of these popular figures may be funded by undisclosed interests. Community lobbyists, who appear close to the people, could be aligned with business interests expecting future favors once their chosen candidate secures a political seat. This lack of transparency means voters may not know who is financing campaigns or what their ultimate agenda is.

Why it mattersMauritian citizens are affected because their elected representatives may be chosen for demographic fit rather than for the skills needed to address critical public issues.
