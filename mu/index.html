Mauritius cabinet approves public sector reform bill

The proposed law aims to improve citizen services, streamline disciplinary actions, and correct administrative failures within set timelines.

The Mauritius cabinet approved on Friday, November 6, a draft law to reform the public sector and improve citizen services. The Public Sector Reforms Bill will establish service standards for citizens and create mechanisms to address administrative shortcomings within prescribed periods.

The new legislation will empower the Ministry of Public Service and Administrative Reforms to coordinate human resource management, administrative reforms, and innovation. It will also establish a Central Disciplinary Service and an Administrative Committee to manage disciplinary cases and ensure their resolution within a limited timeframe.

Each ministry will have a Public Service Implementation Desk/Grievance Desk to identify and correct service failures. The bill mandates the creation of citizen charters detailing service standards and commitments, and will revise promotion policies to recognize experience, performance, and continuous training.

Why it mattersMauritius citizens will experience improved public services with defined standards and faster resolution of administrative issues.

US experts to revise Mauritius flood plan

Three American specialists will visit Mauritius from November 8 to 23 to update the Land Drainage Master Plan.

Mauritius will receive technical assistance from the United States to revise its Land Drainage Master Plan, a key document for managing flood risks. The Council of Ministers met on Friday, November 6, to address the island's vulnerability to extreme weather events and the increasing threat of floods across the country.

Three experts from the Ambassador's Water Expert Program will be in Mauritius from November 8 to November 23, 2026. They will conduct intensive work sessions and technical workshops with stakeholders. A two-day workshop on November 19 and 20 will finalize a roadmap for enhancing the nation's flood resilience.

The current plan, which assesses hydrological and hydraulic risks, maps flood dangers, and evaluates drainage infrastructure, requires an update due to rapidly changing weather patterns and new environmental challenges. The specialists will submit a detailed report with recommendations to optimize flood management.

Why it mattersMauritius faces increasing flood risks due to climate change, prompting a review of its drainage master plan with US expert assistance.

Children filmed drinking beer taken to hospital

Family Support Unit intervenes after social media video shows minors consuming alcohol.

Mauritian authorities took children home after they were filmed drinking beer and brought them to the hospital for medical checks. The Family Support Unit of the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family Welfare intervened following the circulation of images on social media showing the minors consuming alcohol under the amused gaze of adults.

The ministry has opened an investigation into the incident to understand the circumstances and assess the situation of the children. This intervention is particularly sensitive as the family has prior history with the ministry, with three children previously removed from their home in 2025 before being reintegrated in December of that year after a family support program.

Medical examinations will determine the children's health status, while authorities work to establish how the video was made and what measures are needed to ensure their safety and well-being. The video is believed to be recent, and the family was already known to the ministry's services.

Why it mattersChildren in Mauritius were taken to the hospital after being filmed drinking beer, prompting a government investigation.

Le Chebel Citizen targets Super League title

The team aims for the top in the 2026-2027 season, reinforcing its squad with key signings.

Le Chebel Citizen finished third in the last Super League season with 36 points and is aiming higher for the 2026-2027 championship. The club's management and coach are determined to challenge the established hierarchy.

To achieve this, the Chebel-based team is focusing on squad continuity while adding new talent, including midfielder Merven Jocelyn, last season's top scorer. This strategy aims to compensate for player departures and strengthen the team.

The Citizens' first major test is scheduled for Monday, November 9, 2026, at 12:30 PM, when they will face ASPL 2000, a strong competitor in the league. This match marks the end of their preparation for the inaugural fixture.

Why it mattersMauritian football club Le Chebel Citizen is preparing for the upcoming Super League season with ambitions of winning the championship.
