MFA youth football league continues Saturday

The Mauritius Football Association's youth championships will feature U15 and U17 boys' matches across six grounds.

The sixth round of the Mauritius Football Association (MFA) youth championships is scheduled for Saturday, November 7. Only the U15 and U17 boys' categories will compete, with the women's teams taking a break. Matches will take place on six fields: La Caverne Grand Bel Air, Bon Accueil, Guy Rozemont, Sir Gaëtan Duval, S. G. D Grand Gaube, and L’Agrément St Pierre.

For each venue, the U15 matches will kick off at 9:30 AM, followed by the U17 games at 12:00 PM on the same field. The fixtures include Vacoas versus Pamplemousses at La Caverne Grand Bel Air, and Flacq against Grand Port at Bon Accueil.

Other U15 and U17 pairings include Quatre Bornes vs Savanne at Guy Rozemont, Curepipe vs BBRH at Sir Gaëtan Duval, Rivière du Rempart vs Port Louis at S.G.D Grand Gaube, and Moka vs Black River at L’Agrément St Pierre.

Why it mattersYoung male footballers in Mauritius will compete in the MFA youth league on November 7.

Mauritius ranks second in African governance index

The nation maintains its strong institutional standing but sees a decline in its overall score over a decade.

Mauritius has retained its second-place position in the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance (IIAG), scoring 75.0 out of 100 for the 2016-2025 period. This score places the island nation significantly above the continental average of 50.1 and the Eastern Africa average of 47.5. It ranks just behind Seychelles (78.6) and ahead of Cape Verde (70.3).

Despite its high ranking, the report highlights a net decline of 1.9 points in Mauritius's governance score over the decade, placing its trajectory in the 'Bouncing Back' category. This overall decrease, which saw Seychelles overtake Mauritius for the top spot after an 8.3-point increase, is attributed to erosions in security, rule of law, participation, rights, and inclusion. Areas like media freedom and civil society space have seen their margins shrink.

The IIAG report reveals that 30 African countries improved their governance scores between 2016 and 2025, with the continent's average rising by 0.8 points to 50.1. However, Mauritius is among six top-ranked nations whose 2025 score is lower than their 2016 score. Advances in telecommunications, internet access, and port infrastructure remain key strengths for the island.

Why it mattersMauritius's governance performance affects its citizens' rights, freedoms, and participation in public life.

Mauritius farmers get 257.9 hectares for climate adaptation

Minister Arvin Boolell announced land allocation and a new roadmap to address irrigation, pesticide use, and labor shortages.

Mauritius agriculture minister Arvin Boolell met with planters on November 3 in Réduit to discuss urgent climate adaptation needs. The session focused on managing irrigation deficits, controlling pesticide use, and addressing daily operational challenges. Minister Boolell stressed the need for local research and practical solutions, encouraging youth to join the sector.

To support agricultural expansion, 257.9 hectares of land will be allocated to planters under a new roadmap for project distribution. A consultant will also assist pig farmers with operational optimization. Discussions with India are underway to expedite the recruitment of agricultural labor.

Planters raised concerns about irrigation, chemical use, and farming practices, requesting a dedicated roundtable for ongoing dialogue. Technical clarifications were provided by ministry officials and the Food and Agricultural Research and Extension Institute (FAREI).

Why it mattersMauritian farmers are directly affected by the allocation of 257.9 hectares of land and new strategies to manage climate change impacts on their operations.

Sergeant refuses to attend shooting inquiry reconstitution

Key witnesses recreated the events of October 9 when Elodie Thomas was shot.

An inquiry into a shooting that critically injured Elodie Thomas on October 9 in Camp-Levieux has been complicated by the refusal of Sergeant Gautam Singh Taukoor to participate in a fact reconstitution. The Independent Police Complaints Commission (IPCC), led by Satish Faugoo and Hisham Oozeer, organised the event on November 4.

Sergeant Taukoor, the suspect in the shooting, declined to attend the reconstitution. Instead, key witnesses, including Rudy Soobrayen, the van driver, and another employee from the Tribeca Winners supermarket, recreated the events for investigators. Elodie Thomas's colleague also provided details of the incident.

The reconstitution aimed to clarify the sequence of events on the night Thomas was shot. The IPCC is continuing its investigation into the incident.

Why it mattersThe investigation into the shooting of Elodie Thomas is ongoing, with a key suspect refusing to cooperate with investigators.

Business · 2026-11-05

Mauritius tourism operators train for sustainable practices

The Tourism Authority and OIF are equipping hotels and guesthouses with tools for environmental and economic resilience.

Mauritius's tourism industry is adopting greener practices following a workshop on sustainable initiatives held on November 4 at the Trou aux Biches Beachcomber Golf Resort & Spa. The Tourism Authority, in partnership with the Organisation internationale de la francophonie (OIF), organised the event to guide accommodation providers in integrating environmental, social, and economic sustainability into their daily operations. Participants received practical tools and knowledge to implement more responsible and efficient management models.

Niven Muneesamy, director of the Tourism Authority, highlighted the essential role of guesthouses, tourist residences, and small hotels in diversifying guest experiences and supporting local economies. He stated that sustainable development is an unavoidable issue facing climate change, environmental degradation, and natural resource pressures. Muneesamy also pointed to global geopolitical tensions, such as the Middle East crisis, and rising costs for fuel, electricity, and food as significant financial pressures on tourism businesses.

The workshop aimed to equip operators with concrete knowledge for more effective and responsible management. The initiative supports the island's tourism sector in navigating contemporary challenges, including environmental concerns and economic strains.

Why it mattersMauritius's tourism businesses are being trained to adopt sustainable practices to cope with climate change and rising operational costs.
