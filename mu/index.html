Mauritius joins three aviation security treaties

The island nation will strengthen its legal framework against aerial threats and onboard misconduct.

The Mauritius Council of Ministers approved joining three International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) legal instruments on October 23. These agreements aim to bolster the nation's legal framework against new threats to international air travel. The adopted texts include conventions on repressing illicit acts against civil aviation, unlawful aircraft seizure, and offenses committed aboard aircraft.

By adopting these legal tools, Mauritius will establish a system to combat air terrorism, onboard crime, and indiscipline. This move ensures full compliance with strict ICAO standards. The island nation reaffirms its commitment to international aviation security and passenger protection.

This adherence aligns Mauritius's legislation with the latest global standards. The decision was made by the Council of Ministers during their meeting on Friday, October 23.

Why it mattersMauritius has joined three international legal instruments to enhance aviation security and combat threats.

Air Mauritius launches 10-point plan for recovery

The airline aims to improve operations, customer experience, and costs with a new strategic roadmap.

Air Mauritius unveiled a 10-point strategic roadmap on October 23, 2026, designed to consolidate operations, enhance customer experience, and control costs. CEO Andre Viljoen presented the plan, titled "Future-Fit Game Changer Strategies," to union representatives and staff. The initial strategy, "Perfecting the Basics," focuses on strengthening the company's foundations before introducing new initiatives.

Viljoen emphasized a sequential approach, starting with repairing foundations and rebuilding company culture. The plan addresses current challenges and positions Air Mauritius for sustainable long-term growth. Employee input was gathered and compiled into a booklet distributed to all staff, including those at overseas locations.

The airline reported initial positive results, with dispatch reliability reaching 98.72% in July 2026, the best monthly figure since 2020. Customer satisfaction rose to 76.1% in August 2026, up from 72.2% in September 2025, and the Net Promoter Score increased from +9.4 to +23.1 over the past 12 months.

Why it mattersAir Mauritius is implementing a strategic plan to improve its financial performance and operational efficiency.

man stabbed in Roche-Bois

Police found a 25-year-old unemployed man bleeding from stab wounds near Roche-Bois garden.

Police responded to an incident near Roche-Bois garden on the afternoon of Thursday, October 22. Officers discovered a 25-year-old unemployed man lying on the ground with significant stab wounds.

The victim was immediately transported to Dr. A. G. Jeetoo Hospital for treatment and observation. His condition has prevented investigators from taking his statement as an inquiry has been opened into the incident.

The attack occurred in the Roche-Bois area. The victim sustained serious injuries from a bladed weapon.

Mauritius sergeant kept in detention over shooting

Sergeant Gautam Singh Taukoor faces charges of intimidation and involuntary wounds after a shooting incident on October 9.

Sergeant Gautam Singh Taukoor appeared in the Rose-Hill court on Monday, October 26, and was ordered to remain in provisional detention until his next court date on October 30. Police opposed his release as investigations continue into a shooting incident.

The officer, who was questioned by the Independent Police Complaints Commission on Friday, denied pointing his firearm at Rudy Soobrayen. He stated that the driver did not follow police instructions at the scene on October 9. Sergeant Taukoor is accused of firing a shot towards Élodie Thomas in Camp-Levieux.

Two provisional charges have been brought against him: 'Using Firearm to Intimidate' and 'Involuntary Wounds and Blows by Imprudence'. His next court appearance is scheduled for October 30.

Mauritius police seize 203 drug-soaked papers

Customs and DHL services assisted anti-drug officers in intercepting a package containing 203 A4 sheets soaked in narcotics.

Mauritian anti-drug officers intercepted a suspicious package at the PATS warehouse in Plaine-Magnien on Wednesday, October 21. The package, examined with customs and DHL representatives, contained 203 sheets of A4 paper soaked in narcotics, wrapped inside a newspaper.

Following the seizure, the ADSU conducted a controlled delivery operation on Friday, October 23, leading to the arrest of a Port-Louis resident, the package's intended recipient. His mobile phone was confiscated, and a search of his home yielded no further illicit substances or incriminating items.

The suspect has been placed in detention as the investigation continues. The ADSU is pursuing further inquiries into the origin and distribution of the seized drugs.

Why it mattersA Port-Louis resident was arrested and detained after receiving a package containing 203 sheets of paper soaked in narcotics.
