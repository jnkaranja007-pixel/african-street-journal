Mauritius targets 25 000 arpents under crops

The Food Security Programme 2026-2030 is due to launch in two weeks and aims to help cut food import dependence.

Source: Defi Media · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Mauritius plans to bring 25 000 arpents of land under crops as part of its Food Security Programme 2026-2030, which is due to launch in two weeks. The government says the programme will contribute to the national “25 by 35” ambition: producing locally at least 25 % of the country’s food needs by 2035.

Agriculture Minister Arvin Boolell said the programme would use 10 strategies spanning smart agriculture, land policy and the blue economy, including a broader, better-defined fishing zone. It will also cover livestock and fishing. Boolell said Mauritius’s food import bill stands at Rs 170 billion. Small Planters Association secretary-general Kreepalloo Sunghoon said growers should help shape the policies, citing water shortages, labour costs and pesticide costs among reasons farmers abandon fields.

Sunghoon also proposed organising growers by region and vegetable variety, alongside investment in processing and marketing. The programme is intended to offer farmers alternatives to maintain production despite climate and water constraints.

Why it mattersSmall planters are being asked to help shape land and farming policies before the programme’s launch in two weeks, as they contend with water shortages and rising labour and pesticide costs.
