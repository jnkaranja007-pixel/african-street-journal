Female artisanal miners report sexual violence in Kono district

Women working in diamond and gold pits in Jaiama Nimikor describe a pattern of rape and harassment while scavenging for income in abandoned mines.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-08 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Women working as artisanal miners in the diamond-rich Kono district of Sierra Leone face high risks of sexual violence while scavenging in abandoned pits. Janet, now 32, reported being raped at age 15 while searching for gold and diamonds in Jaiama Nimikor, a site located 8 miles from Koidu. She stated that she became pregnant following the assault and faced community stigmatization, forcing her to give up her child to a relative.

Other women in the area report similar experiences of abuse. Haja, a 48-year-old widow, said she was raped while working in the mines and fled her community due to shame. She reported the incident to a local camp master, who dismissed her claim by suggesting the perpetrator was her boyfriend. Haja stated that no official action was taken against the man, who eventually left the village on his own accord.

Many women in Kono turned to mining as a primary source of income following the end of the civil war. Janet and Haja both noted that they entered the sector to support their families after losing parents or spouses. Despite reporting incidents to village authorities, the women describe a lack of accountability for the perpetrators of these crimes.

Why it mattersThe story highlights the physical and social dangers faced by women working in the artisanal mining sector in Kono, specifically regarding sexual violence and lack of legal recourse.
