African youth protest deepening political dissatisfaction

Protests erupted in Kenya, Tanzania, Morocco and beyond demanding better governance amid economic hardship.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-03 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

In 2025, Africa experienced significant economic growth of 3.8-4.1% driven by infrastructure and market reforms. However, this progress was overshadowed by widespread youth-led protests across Kenya, Tanzania, Morocco, and other nations. These demonstrations voiced deep dissatisfaction with governance and economic conditions.

The protests highlighted a growing disconnect between leaders' claims of stability and citizens' realities. Young people demanded improvements amid economic hardship, the impact of millions displaced by floods, and social spending cuts due to debt. Violence and crackdowns occurred in Tanzania, and unrest followed disputed elections in Cameroon.

The year also saw fragile transitions in Côte d'Ivoire and Gabon, and Guinea-Bissau returned to military rule, raising regional concerns. Geopolitical competition increased, with the U.S. approach under Trump affecting aid and trade deals like AGOA renewal. The AU's financial independence stalled, and ECOWAS weakened after member states withdrew.

Why it mattersAfrican youth are directly affected by demands for better governance and economic improvements amid hardship and debt.
