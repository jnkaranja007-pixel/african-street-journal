Analyst traces history of vice presidents in Sierra Leone

A public policy analyst examines why the vice presidency has never led to the presidency since independence.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Foday M. Daboh, a public policy analyst and political economist, stated in February that the vice presidency in Sierra Leone has historically functioned as a political balancing mechanism rather than a leadership incubator. According to the analysis, successive governments have used the office to appease party blocs, balance regional and religious considerations, neutralize internal rivals, and reward loyalty.

Historical examples cited in the publication include Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens, as well as Solomon Ekuma Berewa, who lost the 2007 presidential election despite his experience. The text also notes the tenure of Victor Bockarie Foh and the removal and expulsion of Samuel Sam-Sumana under the All Peoples Congress.

The analysis examines whether the pattern of the office has sealed the fate of the current vice president within the nation's political evolution. Constitutionally established as a stabilizing institution second only to the presidency under the 1991 Constitution, the office has consistently left occupants without an independent political base or a clear succession pathway.

Why it mattersReaders across Sierra Leone face ongoing political debates regarding the constitutional role and historical trajectory of the vice presidency.
