Sierra Leone charcoal trade cuts trees and sustains households

A burner says his family relies on the work, while the environmental agency warns that cutting economic trees threatens food security.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Charcoal burning supports households across Sierra Leone, including in Freetown and regional headquarters towns, but depends on cutting trees. Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old burner who has worked in the trade for more than two decades, said his entire family relies on it for a livelihood.

Paul Lamin, the Environmental Protection Agency’s director of natural resource governance, said burners are cutting economic trees such as orange and mango trees. He warned that cleared land is exposed to climate change and that losing those trees threatens food security. Lamin also said trees take a long time to regrow, and acknowledged that people have few alternative energy sources.

The government’s National Energy Compact says 36% of the population has access to electricity and clean cooking access stands at 15%. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates wood energy, including fuel wood and charcoal, emits 1/2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent per year, or 2-7% of total human-caused greenhouse gas emissions.
