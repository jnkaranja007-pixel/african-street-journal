Sierra Leone warns of plastic waste health risks

Government officials highlight environmental and tourism threats from plastic pollution, urging public action.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-08 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Government officials in Sierra Leone have raised serious concerns about the environmental and health dangers posed by plastic waste, directly impacting citizens and marine life. Judith C. Jones, Senior Permanent Secretary at the Ministry of Tourism and Cultural Affairs, stated that plastic waste threatens the nation's beaches, a key tourism asset. The Go Circular 2026 initiative, themed “Green Actions, New Jobs,” aims to reduce, reuse, and recycle plastic waste for a cleaner country.

Jones described the growing enthusiasm for circularity as "heartwarming and deeply encouraging," noting that the movement, which started in 2024, now involves national and international actors. She stressed the need for greener businesses to combat plastic waste and urged schoolchildren to become agents of change. The ministry is collaborating with universities and innovators to establish a mini-recycling facility along the coastline.

The Deputy Minister of Environment and Climate Change, Mima Yema Mimi Sobba-Stephens, encouraged Sierra Leoneans to adopt waste-reducing habits for the benefit of health, tourism, and national pride. She explained that "going circular" means rethinking how everyday items are used and disposed of, emphasizing that individual behavior in responsible waste disposal is essential for protecting the ecosystem and positioning Sierra Leone as an attractive destination.

Why it mattersPlastic waste poses direct environmental and health threats to Sierra Leoneans, affecting livelihoods and the nation's tourism assets.
