Sierra Leone vice presidents face succession challenges

The office has historically served as a political cul-de-sac, with no vice president ever elected president.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

In Sierra Leone, the office of Vice President, constitutionally powerful, has historically been a political dead end, with no holder ever elected president. Since independence, vice presidents have often been appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, rather than to cultivate ambition for higher office. This practice has frequently left vice presidents without a clear succession pathway or an independent political base.

Past vice presidents illustrate this pattern: Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were insiders but not successors, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared, lost the 2007 presidential election despite his experience. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and politically constrained, and Samuel Sam-Sumana was expelled from his party after falling out with the president.

The current Vice President faces a similar challenge, as the office's history suggests it offers proximity to power but not necessarily a path to the presidency. The 1991 Constitution intended the role to ensure continuity and executive depth, but governments have largely used it as a political balancing mechanism, a trend that has persisted and may already have sealed the fate of the current officeholder regarding presidential succession.

Why it mattersThe historical pattern of vice presidents in Sierra Leone failing to attain the presidency affects political ambition and succession planning within the country's highest offices.
