Sierra Leone vice presidents fail to win presidency

No vice president has ever been elected president in Sierra Leone since independence.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-02 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Sierra Leone's vice presidency has historically been a political dead end, not a path to the presidency. Since independence, vice presidents have been appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic factors, appease party factions, or neutralize rivals, rather than to cultivate future leaders. This practice has meant most vice presidents lack a clear succession plan or an independent political base.

Past vice presidents like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were powerful but not groomed for succession, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup attempt. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared during the Kabbah administration, lost the 2007 presidential election decisively. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and politically constrained, and Samuel Sam-Sumana was expelled from his party after falling out with the president.

The current vice president faces a historical pattern where proximity to power has not translated into presidential success. Berewa's 2007 defeat demonstrated that competence alone was insufficient, as voters opted for perceived change. This recurring outcome raises questions about the current vice president's prospects for higher office.

Why it mattersSierra Leonean vice presidents have not been elected president, impacting political ambitions.
