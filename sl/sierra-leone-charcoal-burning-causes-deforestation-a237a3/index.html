Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency confirms practice destroys economic trees and harms air quality.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-10-28 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Charcoal burning for cooking remains common across Sierra Leone, particularly in Freetown, as only 36% of the population has electricity and 15% have clean cooking access. Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old burner for two decades, stated his family relies on the trade for survival. The process requires cutting down trees, including valuable fruit-bearing ones, and emits greenhouse gases.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental hazards, noting that burners now target economic trees like orange and mango. This leaves land treeless, exposed to climate change, and threatens food security, according to the agency. He admitted that alternative energy options are currently limited.

Experts estimate traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions. The Food and Agricultural Organization warns that continued unsustainable charcoal production will worsen climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersCharcoal burning leads to deforestation, habitat loss, and greenhouse gas emissions, impacting the environment and human health in Sierra Leone.
