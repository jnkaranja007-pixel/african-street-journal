Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

The practice of burning wood for charcoal is depleting forests and threatening food security in Sierra Leone.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-14 · 1 min read

Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old charcoal burner in Sierra Leone, relies on the trade for his family's livelihood, a common practice in Freetown and other towns due to limited access to electricity and clean cooking options. The National Energy Compact states only 36% of the population has electricity access, and 15% has clean cooking access.

Charcoal production involves cutting down trees, including economic ones like mango and orange, leading to deforestation, habitat loss, and environmental degradation. Paul Lamin from the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the hazards, noting that treeless land is exposed to climate change effects and threatening food security.

The burning process emits greenhouse gases, contributing to climate change and air pollution, with traditional wood energy emitting 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually. The Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO) warns that continued unsustainable charcoal production will exacerbate climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersDeforestation from charcoal burning exposes land to climate change and threatens food security for households relying on economic trees.
