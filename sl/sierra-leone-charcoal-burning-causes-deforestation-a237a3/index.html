Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency warns of economic tree destruction and greenhouse gas emissions from burning wood for fuel.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-10-24 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

65-year-old Pa Alimamy has burned charcoal for over two decades in Sierra Leone, a trade he says sustains his family and many others across the country. The demand for charcoal as a cooking fuel is high because only 36% of the population has electricity and just 15% have access to clean cooking.

The Environmental Protection Agency in Sierra Leone acknowledged the environmental hazards of charcoal production, noting that burners are now cutting down economic trees like orange and mango. Director Paul Lamin stated this practice leaves land treeless, exposed to climate change, and threatens food security.

Charcoal burning emits significant greenhouse gases, contributing to climate change and air pollution, according to experts. The Food and Agricultural Organization estimates traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions.

Why it mattersCharcoal burning in Sierra Leone leads to deforestation, habitat loss, and greenhouse gas emissions, impacting the environment and threatening food security.
