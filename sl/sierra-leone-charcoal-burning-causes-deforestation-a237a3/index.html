Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency confirms burning of economic trees threatens food security and exacerbates climate change effects.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-10-31 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Charcoal burning, a common practice across Sierra Leone for cooking fuel, is leading to significant deforestation and environmental degradation. Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old burner for two decades, stated his family relies on the trade for survival, highlighting its prevalence in Freetown and regional towns due to limited access to electricity and clean cooking alternatives. The National Energy Compact reports only 36% of the population has electricity access and 15% clean cooking access.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental hazards, noting that burners are now cutting down economic trees like orange and mango. He stated that this practice leaves land treeless, exposed to climate change, and threatens food security. Paul Lamin explained that the slow regrowth of these trees hinders carbon reduction.

Greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production and use are harmful to human health and the environment. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates wood energy, including charcoal, emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, contributing 2-7% of global anthropogenic GHG emissions. Continued unsustainable practices are expected to worsen climate change, impacting forest productivity and future wood energy supplies.
