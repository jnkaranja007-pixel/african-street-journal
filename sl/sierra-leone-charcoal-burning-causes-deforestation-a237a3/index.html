Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Burning wood for charcoal is destroying economic trees and leaving land exposed to climate change effects.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-10-30 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Charcoal burning, a common practice in Sierra Leone for cooking fuel, is leading to widespread deforestation and environmental degradation. 65-year-old Pa Alimamy, a charcoal burner for over two decades, stated his family relies on this trade for survival, highlighting its prevalence in Freetown and other towns due to limited access to electricity and clean cooking options. The National Energy Compact reports only 36% of the population has electricity and 15% clean cooking access.

The process of making charcoal involves cutting down trees, including valuable economic species like orange and mango, which exacerbates environmental hazards. Paul Lamin from the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the practice's negative impact, noting that the slow regrowth of trees reduces the land's capacity to absorb carbon. He also admitted that viable alternative energy sources are currently scarce for the population.

Experts warn that greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production and use harm human health and the environment. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa contributing a third of these emissions. Continued unsustainable charcoal practices are expected to worsen climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersDeforestation and greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal burning threaten the environment and food security for Sierra Leoneans.
