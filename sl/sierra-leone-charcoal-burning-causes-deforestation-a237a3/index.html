Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

The practice of burning wood for charcoal is destroying economic trees and increasing greenhouse gas emissions.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

65-year-old Pa Alimamy has burned charcoal for over two decades in Sierra Leone, a trade that supports his family. Charcoal is widely used for cooking, especially in Freetown and other towns, because only 36% of the population has electricity and 15% has clean cooking access.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental damage, noting that burners are cutting down fruit trees like orange and mango. This deforestation exposes land to climate change and threatens food security, as trees are vital for absorbing carbon dioxide.

The burning process releases significant greenhouse gases, contributing to air pollution and climate change. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates that traditional wood energy emits 2.4 gigatons of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions.

Why it mattersDeforestation from charcoal burning threatens food security and increases greenhouse gas emissions, impacting the environment and livelihoods.
