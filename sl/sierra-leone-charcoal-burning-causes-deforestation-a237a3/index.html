Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency confirms practice destroys economic trees and worsens climate change effects.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-08 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Charcoal burning is a common livelihood across Sierra Leone, providing cooking fuel for families who cannot afford electricity or gas. Pa Alimamy, 65, has burned charcoal for over two decades, stating his family relies on it for survival. The practice is prevalent in Freetown and other towns due to high demand and limited access to alternative energy sources, with only 36% of the population having electricity and 15% clean cooking access.

The burning of wood for charcoal leads to deforestation, habitat loss, and environmental degradation. Paul Lamin, Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency, acknowledged the hazards, noting that burners now cut down economic trees like mango and orange. This leaves land treeless, exposed to climate change, and threatens food security, as the trees cut take a long time to regrow and absorb carbon.

Greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production and use harm human health and the environment. Globally, traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa contributing one-third of these emissions. The Food and Agricultural Organization estimates that continued unsustainable charcoal practices will worsen climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersSierra Leoneans who rely on charcoal for cooking face health risks from greenhouse gas emissions and environmental degradation, while the practice contributes to global climate change.
