Charcoal burning deforests Sierra Leone, threatens food security

Environmental officials warn that the widespread practice of charcoal production is leading to deforestation, habitat loss, and the cutting of economic trees, exacerbating.

Charcoal production in Sierra Leone is driving significant deforestation and environmental degradation, with officials highlighting the cutting of vital economic trees like mangoes and oranges. The Environmental Protection Agency notes that this practice leaves land exposed to climate change effects and threatens food security. Director Paul Lamin stated that the slow regrowth of trees exacerbates the problem, contributing to carbon space reduction.

The demand for charcoal, particularly in Freetown and regional towns, stems from limited access to electricity and clean cooking alternatives. The National Energy Compact reports that only 36% of the population has electricity access, and a mere 15% have access to clean cooking. This reliance on charcoal, a product of burnt wood, fuels the ongoing environmental damage.

Beyond deforestation and habitat loss, the burning of wood for charcoal and its subsequent use as an energy source releases significant greenhouse gases. Experts warn this air pollution is harmful to human health and the environment. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates that traditional wood energy contributes substantially to global carbon dioxide equivalent emissions, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for a third of these emissions.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected as the cutting of economic trees and deforestation threatens food security and land exposed to climate change.

Sierra Leone Vice Presidents Face Political Dead End

The office, constitutionally powerful, has historically served as a political cul-de-sac rather than a pathway to the presidency, raising questions about the current vice.

The Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone, designed by the 1991 Constitution to be a stabilizing force and executive depth provider, has consistently functioned as a political balancing tool rather than a leadership incubator. Vice Presidents have frequently been appointed to appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or balance regional and ethnic considerations, often without a clear succession pathway or independent political base.

Historical examples illustrate this pattern, with figures like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah serving as powerful insiders but never groomed for succession. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered one of the most prepared Vice Presidents, lost the 2007 presidential election despite his experience, demonstrating that competence in the role does not guarantee electoral success. The electorate's desire for change over continuity proved decisive.

More recently, Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and politically constrained, lacking succession ambition. Samuel Sam-Sumana's experience as an elected Vice President who was later estranged, expelled from his party, and politically isolated further highlights the precariousness of the office. Proximity to power has not translated into political security or a clear path to the presidency for many who have held the position.

Why it mattersThe historical pattern of Vice Presidents being sidelined or facing electoral defeat affects current and future officeholders by limiting their perceived chances of ascending to the presidency.

Women Face Rape and Harassment in Sierra Leone Mines

Artisanal miners in Kono district recount sexual violence and exploitation as they seek income from abandoned pits.

Women in Sierra Leone's Kono district are facing rape and sexual harassment while working in abandoned mining sites, a dangerous but often necessary source of income. Janet, 32, recounted being raped at 15 while scavenging for diamonds and gold in Jaiama Nimikor, an experience that led to an unplanned pregnancy and community stigma. She continues to mine to support her child, who was raised by a relative.

Haja, 48, a widow with three children, also shared her experience of being raped in the mines after her husband's death. She fled her village due to shame but later returned after her daughter's plea. The camp master dismissed her report, suggesting the perpetrator might have been her boyfriend, a claim she denied. The man who raped her eventually left the village voluntarily.

These women turned to mining after the civil war, with abandoned pits offering a way to earn a living when other options were scarce. Many had dropped out of school due to poverty, making mining one of the few available means to support themselves and their families. The risks, including sexual violence, are a stark reality of their struggle for survival in the diamond-rich region.

Why it mattersWomen miners are affected by rape and sexual harassment, impacting their safety and livelihoods in the Kono district.

Finance Minister's Policies Withstood Global Shocks, Report States

A consortium's call for the finance minister's dismissal is misplaced, confusing global economic hardship with local mismanagement, according to an analysis.

A statement by the "CSOs Consortium on Public Accountability" calling for the dismissal of Finance Minister Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura has been described as a flawed intervention. The analysis argues that the consortium confuses economic hardship with mismanagement and populist frustration with policy failure. It asserts that global shocks, including COVID-19 supply-chain disruptions and the Russia-Ukraine war, significantly impacted inflation across developing economies, with food inflation in Sub-Saharan Africa exceeding 20-30 percent in many nations.

In contrast to countries like Ghana, Zambia, Nigeria, and Kenya, Sierra Leone did not default on its debt, maintained its IMF program, and preserved access to concessional financing. This outcome is attributed to disciplined macro-fiscal management under Minister Bangura. Data from the Bank of Sierra Leone indicates that inflation peaked following global shocks but subsequently declined due to coordinated fiscal and monetary tightening, with central bank financing of deficits curtailed.

The analysis also refutes claims of reckless debt accumulation, noting that Sierra Leone's debt is predominantly concessional, commercial borrowing has been restrained, and debt management reforms have improved transparency. The country has no Eurobond exposure or hidden debt, and the minister resisted pressure for unchecked borrowing that could have triggered default. This approach, the report suggests, has helped moderate inflationary pressures and managed the economy effectively despite external challenges.

Why it mattersFarmers face challenges managing inflation on imported goods, while investors benefit from fiscal discipline and preserved access to financing, and the diaspora is indirectly affected by economic stability.

SLEWRC Revenue Jumps to NLE 7.4 Million

The Sierra Leone Electricity and Water Regulatory Commission reports a significant increase in revenue from levies and licenses, despite receiving no direct budget allocations.

The Sierra Leone Electricity and Water Regulatory Commission (SLEWRC) generated NLE 7.4 million in internal revenue in 2024, a substantial rise from the NLE 2.3 million collected in 2023. This increase stems from levies and licenses issued by the commission, which regulates electricity and water service providers. Despite not receiving budgeted funds for recurrent expenditures from the Ministry of Finance in both 2023 and 2024, SLEWRC successfully completed key deliverables.

In 2023, SLEWRC sanitized the packaged water industry through monitoring and enforcement, leading to more licenses and improved service quality. The commission resolved an average of 80% of complaints within a week and took enforcement actions against 64 non-compliant water companies. For 2024, despite receiving no allocated funds, SLEWRC expanded its operational network and developed a Multi-Year Tariff Order (MYTO) model for solar mini-grids.

Looking ahead to FY 2025-2027, SLEWRC plans to strengthen its organizational capacity, ensure market entry and universal access, and maintain international quality standards. The commission also intends to roll out a safety and warranty batching system for water products and monitor tariff rates for sustainability. SLEWRC has submitted a budget of NLE 5,590.29 for this period.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the commission's ability to generate revenue and its plans for tariff rate monitoring and market expansion, which could impact sector profitability and investment.
