Sheriff Barrie credits Momoh for 1991 constitution

National Democratic Alliance Acting Chairman Sheriff Barrie stated that former President Joseph Saidu Momoh initiated the constitutional process.

National Democratic Alliance Acting Chairman Sheriff Barrie credited former President Joseph Saidu Momoh with initiating the constitutional process that led to Sierra Leone’s return to multiparty democracy. Barrie made the remarks Tuesday during an interview with Echo Media, describing Momoh as a central figure in the country’s constitutional history. He argued that the former president’s contribution to the 1991 Constitution remains significant in understanding the foundations of Sierra Leone’s present democratic system.

Barrie stated that if Sierra Leone is talking about democracy today, J.S. Momoh is the architect because he brought the 1991 Constitution. Historical records show Momoh established a constitutional review process in October 1990 to reconsider the 1978 one-party constitution. The review commission recommended restoring a multiparty political system, paving the way for the 1991 Constitution.

The proposed constitution passed Parliament in July 1991 before receiving approval in a national referendum in August of the same year. Momoh signed it into law on September 24, 1991, and it came into effect on October 1. The constitutional transition was interrupted by the April 1992 military coup that removed Momoh from power, but Sierra Leone’s first multiparty elections under the 1991 Constitution were eventually held in 1996.

Why it mattersSheriff Barrie's remarks bring renewed attention to Momoh’s role in the constitutional changes of the early 1990s and the historical process that shaped Sierra Leone’s multiparty political system.

Sierra Leone president warns kush drug peddlers

President Bio visits a rehabilitation center and urges immediate cessation of illicit drug trade.

President Julius Maada Bio has ordered sellers of the synthetic drug Kush and other harmful substances to stop their trade immediately. He issued the warning during a visit to the Government’s Harmful Drug Rehabilitation Facility in Hastings on October 30, 2025. The president assured victims that his administration is committed to ending the sale of these drugs.

Bio expressed sadness that some parents are involved in selling Kush and urged them to find honest livelihoods. He called on the police and judiciary to take stronger action against illegal drug traders. Addressing drug users, he stated that their future is essential for Sierra Leone's development and that the government is working to break the drug supply chain. Kush has caused the deaths of many young people since 2019.

The government has established rehabilitation centers in Hastings, Gondama, and Daru, with plans for another in Teko. Parents shared their struggles, including one whose lawyer son lost his career to Kush addiction before receiving free government treatment. A former user, now an ambassador against drug use, credited the Hastings facility for her recovery and return to school. The Presidential Task Force on Drugs and Substance Abuse was launched, and a national emergency declared to combat drugs.

Why it mattersYoung people and their families in Sierra Leone are directly affected by the pervasive Kush drug crisis and the government's efforts to combat it.

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency warns of economic tree loss and habitat destruction due to cooking fuel production.

In Sierra Leone, charcoal burning is a widespread practice providing a livelihood for many, including 65-year-old Pa Alimamy who has worked in the trade for over two decades. The demand for charcoal as a cooking fuel is high because only 36% of the population has access to electricity and just 15% have clean cooking access, according to the National Energy Compact.

The process of making charcoal involves cutting down trees, leading to deforestation and habitat loss. The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental hazards, noting that burners are now cutting down economic trees like orange and mango. This practice leaves land exposed to climate change effects and threatens food security.

Experts state that burning wood for charcoal and using it for energy emits significant greenhouse gases, harming human health and the environment. The Food and Agricultural Organization estimates that traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa contributing one-third of these emissions. Continued unsustainable production could worsen climate change and reduce future wood supplies.

Why it mattersDeforestation and greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production threaten Sierra Leone's environment and food security.

Rina’s Empower Her Foundation offers free breast cancer screening

Women in Freetown’s Mabela community received free breast cancer screening and education on symptoms.

Rina’s Empowered Her Foundation provided free breast cancer screening to women in Freetown’s Mabela community. The outreach initiative, organised during Breast Cancer Awareness Month, aimed to encourage early medical attention and improve disease awareness. Participants also received education on breast cancer symptoms and the importance of seeking professional medical advice.

Foundation founder Marina Terry stated that the organisation addresses challenges affecting women through health, education, and empowerment initiatives. Cancer doctor Dr. Kelfala Marah from Connaught Hospital explained the importance of recognising warning signs, cautioning that a breast lump does not have to be painful to warrant medical assessment. The foundation intends for the initiative to encourage women to take greater responsibility for their health.

A 2026 study abstract highlighted barriers to early breast cancer detection in Sierra Leone, including limited screening access, low awareness, and delayed medical care. The Jennifer Thompson Organization's CEO, Jennifer S. Lahai Thompson, also called for sustained public education and improved access to screening and treatment beyond awareness month.

Female artisanal miners report sexual violence in Kono district

Women working in the diamond-rich pits of Jaiama Nimikor face persistent threats of rape and harassment while struggling to earn a daily income.

Women scavenging for gold and diamonds in the Jaiama Nimikor mining area of Kono district report frequent incidents of rape and sexual harassment. Janet, a 32-year-old miner, stated she was raped at age 15 while working alone in a pit eight miles from Koidu. She became pregnant from the assault and faced community stigmatization, eventually losing custody of her child to a relative.

Haja, a 48-year-old widow and miner, reported being raped by a man in her village while she worked to support her three children. She said the camp master dismissed her complaint by claiming the attacker was her boyfriend, a claim she denies. Haja fled to the village of Guitor to escape the shame of the incident before eventually returning to her home.

Many women in the region turned to artisanal mining as a primary source of income following the end of the civil war. These miners often work in abandoned pits to support their families after losing parents or spouses in the conflict. Victims report that local authorities and camp masters frequently fail to take action when these assaults are reported.

Why it mattersThe report highlights the physical dangers and lack of security for women working in artisanal mining sites in Sierra Leone's Kono district.
