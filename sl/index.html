Charcoal burning destroys forests and emits greenhouse gases

Environmental officials acknowledge the practice's hazards, noting that economic trees are being cut and land left barren.

Charcoal burning, a common practice across Sierra Leone, is leading to widespread deforestation and environmental degradation. The process requires cutting down trees, including valuable economic species like orange and mango, leaving land exposed and threatening biodiversity. Experts warn that burning wood for charcoal also emits significant greenhouse gases, contributing to climate change.

The Environmental Protection Agency acknowledges the environmental damage caused by charcoal production. Director Paul Lamin stated that the trees cut take a long time to regrow and are essential for reducing carbon emissions. He also noted the limited availability of alternative energy sources for the population, making charcoal a necessity for many households.

With only 36% of Sierra Leone's population having access to electricity and clean cooking access limited to 15%, the demand for charcoal remains high. This continued reliance on traditional wood energy contributes to an estimated 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent emissions annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for a third of these emissions from wood energy.

Why it mattersThe widespread deforestation and greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal burning negatively impact the environment and contribute to climate change.

Sierra Leone Vice Presidents Fail to Reach Presidency

The office, constitutionally powerful, has historically served as a political cul-de-sac rather than a pathway to the nation's highest leadership.

Sierra Leone's Vice Presidency, intended by the 1991 Constitution to ensure executive continuity and depth, has consistently functioned as a political balancing tool. Successive governments have appointed individuals to appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or address regional and ethnic considerations, rather than to cultivate future presidential ambitions. This practice has often left Vice Presidents without a clear succession path or an independent political base, hindering their potential rise to the presidency.

Post-independence history illustrates this pattern, with figures like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah serving as powerful insiders but never successors. Minah's execution after a failed coup attempt highlights the vulnerability of the office. More recently, Solomon Ekuma Berewa, despite extensive experience and involvement in governance, lost the 2007 presidential election, demonstrating that competence in the vice presidential role does not guarantee electoral success.

The experiences of Samuel Sam-Sumana and Victor Bockarie Foh further underscore the limitations of the Vice Presidency. Sam-Sumana was removed from office, expelled from his party, and left politically isolated, while Foh's tenure was brief and politically constrained. These cases collectively suggest that the office, while constitutionally significant, often fails to provide the political security or ambition-building platform necessary for a Vice President to transition to the presidency.

Why it mattersThe Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone is a political cul-de-sac, preventing individuals from advancing to the presidency due to its use as a balancing mechanism rather than a leadership incubator.

Women miners face rape and harassment in Sierra Leone's Kono district

Artisanal miners in the diamond-rich Kono district report widespread sexual violence and harassment as they seek income.

Women in Sierra Leone's Kono district are enduring rape and sexual harassment while working in abandoned mines, seeking income after the civil war. Janet, 32, recounted being raped at 15 while scavenging for diamonds in Jaiama Nimikor, an experience that led to an unplanned pregnancy and community stigma. She continues to mine to support her child, who was raised by a relative.

Haja, 48, a widow with three children, also reported being raped by a man in her village after starting gold mining for a livelihood. She fled her community due to shame, leaving her children behind. When she reported the incident, the camp master dismissed her claims, suggesting the perpetrator was her boyfriend.

These women turned to mining as one of the few available income sources following the civil war. Many had to drop out of school due to poverty. The mines, often abandoned pits, offer a chance to find diamonds and gold, but expose women to significant risks of sexual violence and harassment, with little recourse for justice.

Why it mattersWomen in Kono district are affected by rape and sexual harassment while mining for income, facing stigma and lack of support.

Finance Minister Defends Economic Record Amidst Criticism

A civil society consortium's call for the finance minister's dismissal is misplaced, confusing global shocks with local mismanagement, officials state.

A coalition of civil society organizations has called for the immediate dismissal of Sierra Leone’s Minister of Finance, Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura. However, this call is described as a flawed intervention that conflates global economic hardship with local mismanagement. The statement from the “CSOs Consortium on Public Accountability” is criticized for lacking analytical rigor and empirical grounding, potentially amounting to policy vandalism rather than constructive advocacy.

Officials argue that Sierra Leone, like many nations, faced unprecedented global shocks between 2020 and 2024, including COVID-19 disruptions, the Russia-Ukraine war's impact on food and fuel prices, and aggressive global monetary tightening. Despite these challenges, Sierra Leone avoided defaulting on its debt, maintained IMF program engagement, and preserved access to concessional financing, outcomes attributed to disciplined macro-fiscal management under Minister Bangura.

Data from the Bank of Sierra Leone and IMF reports indicate that while inflation peaked due to global shocks, it has since declined through coordinated fiscal and monetary tightening. The Ministry of Finance asserts that in an economy importing over 70 percent of its consumption, inflation can only be managed, not eliminated. Debt sustainability metrics, according to IMF-World Bank analyses, show that while the risk of distress is high, debt is predominantly concessional, commercial borrowing is restrained, and reforms have improved transparency.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the government's management of fiscal discipline and debt sustainability, which influences economic stability and access to financing.

Business · 2026-08-30

SLEWRC Revenue Jumps to NLE 7.4 Million

The Sierra Leone Electricity and Water Regulatory Commission significantly increased internal revenue from levies and licenses in 2024.

The Sierra Leone Electricity and Water Regulatory Commission (SLEWRC) reported a substantial rise in its internal revenue generation, reaching NLE 7.4 million in 2024. This figure represents a significant increase from the NLE 2.3 million collected in 2023. Despite not receiving budgeted funds from the Ministry of Finance for recurrent expenditures in both 2023 and 2024, the commission successfully executed key deliverables.

SLEWRC's achievements in 2023 included sanitizing the packaged water industry through monitoring and enforcement, which led to more licenses and improved service quality. The commission also resolved an average of 80% of complaints within a week and took enforcement actions against 64 non-compliant water companies. For 2024, SLEWRC expanded its operational network and developed a Multi-Year Tariff Order (MYTO) model for solar mini-grids.

Looking ahead to FY 2025, SLEWRC plans to strengthen its organizational capacity, ensure market entry and universal access, maintain international quality standards, and implement a safety batching system for water products. The commission submitted a budget of NLE 5,590.29 for FY 2025 to 2027. SLEWRC's mandate includes issuing and renewing licenses for electricity and water providers and setting guidelines for service rates.

Why it mattersThe Sierra Leone Electricity and Water Regulatory Commission's increased revenue and regulatory actions affect water companies and their operators.
