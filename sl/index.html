Sierra Leone warns of plastic waste health risks

Government officials cite environmental and tourism threats from pollution, urging public action and greener businesses.

Government officials in Sierra Leone have raised serious concerns about the health and environmental dangers posed by plastic waste. Senior Permanent Secretary Judith C. Jones stated that plastic pollution directly threatens the nation's beaches, a key tourism asset. The Go Circular 2026 initiative, themed “Green Actions, New Jobs,” aims to reduce, reuse, and recycle plastic waste for a cleaner country.

Jones described the growing enthusiasm for circularity as encouraging, noting the movement involves national and international actors. She emphasized the need for greener businesses to combat plastic waste and urged school children to become agents of change. The Ministry of Tourism is collaborating with innovators to establish a mini-recycling facility along the coastline, acknowledging that waste management begins with individual responsible disposal.

The Deputy Minister for Environment and Climate Change, Mima Yema Mimi Sobba-Stephens, linked cleanliness to health, tourism, and national pride, urging citizens to embrace new habits. She explained that "going circular" means rethinking how everyday items are used and disposed of, highlighting that changing habits will help clean the country.

Why it mattersPlastic waste poses direct environmental and health threats to Sierra Leoneans and marine life, impacting livelihoods and the nation's tourism assets.

Sierra Leone mandates electronic procurement for 16 institutions

The National Public Procurement Authority requires all 16 institutions to use the e-GP system from November 1, 2026.

16 public institutions in Sierra Leone must now use the Electronic Government Procurement (e-GP) System for all their purchasing activities. The National Public Procurement Authority (NPPA) made this mandatory starting November 1, 2026, to improve how public money is spent.

The directive applies to ministries like Finance, Health, and Education, along with bodies such as the National Revenue Authority and the Bank of Sierra Leone. The NPPA stated this move follows the Public Procurement Act of 2016 and aims to increase transparency, accountability, and efficiency. Institutions needing to procure outside the system must get written approval and justification from the NPPA.

The NPPA's Chief Executive Officer, Fodie J. Konneh, signed the circular, which aims to reduce delays and ensure better value for money. Failure to comply could lead to procurement activities being declared non-compliant with Sierra Leone's laws.

Why it matters16 government institutions in Sierra Leone will now conduct all procurement electronically, affecting how they spend public funds.

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

The practice emits greenhouse gases and destroys economic trees, threatening food security and forests.

Charcoal burning for cooking is widespread in Sierra Leone, particularly in Freetown, as only 36% of the population has electricity and 15% have clean cooking access. This process requires cutting down trees, leading to deforestation, habitat loss, and environmental degradation. The burning itself emits greenhouse gases harmful to human health and the environment.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the hazards, noting that burners are cutting down economic trees like orange and mango. He stated that this leaves land treeless and exposed to climate change effects, threatening food security. The agency admits there are limited alternative energy options available.

Traditional wood energy, including charcoal, emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, contributing 2-7% of total human-caused GHG emissions globally. Sub-Saharan Africa accounts for one-third of these emissions from wood energy. Continued unsustainable charcoal production could worsen climate change, impacting forests and reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersThe widespread charcoal burning in Sierra Leone leads to deforestation and greenhouse gas emissions, impacting the environment and food security.

Sierra Leonean youth tackle e-waste with 17 innovations

17 youth-led teams presented solutions for electronic waste management at the Youth for Circularity 2030 Innovation Challenge.

17 youth-led teams presented innovative solutions to Sierra Leone's electronic waste problem on Thursday, November 5, 2026. The Youth for Circularity 2030 Innovation Challenge, held at the University Innovation Pod at Fourah Bay College, aimed to foster sustainable e-waste management and create economic opportunities for young people.

The event, organized by UNDP Sierra Leone, saw participation from government officials, academics, and development partners. UNDP Sierra Leone Country Representative Fredrick Ampiah encouraged innovations that contribute to community development, promising finalists laptops and devices to support their work. Minister of Technical and Higher Education, Dr. Haja Ramatulai Wurie, highlighted the potential of discarded electronics as economic resources through recycling and reuse.

Winifred Doe of Female Innovation Lab showcased "Second Life Tech," an initiative empowering women to repurpose mobile phone components into educational materials and teaching recycling skills. The competition attracted 39 concept submissions, with 29 meeting eligibility requirements.

Why it mattersYoung innovators in Sierra Leone are developing solutions to manage electronic waste and create economic opportunities.

Culture · 2026-11-07

Sierra Leone signs creative economy deal at CHOGM

The nation joined Kenya in signing a Statement of Intent with The Akuna Group to boost creative industries.

Sierra Leone and Kenya signed Statements of Intent with The Akuna Group at CHOGM 2026, becoming founding champions of the Commonwealth Creative Economy Partnership. Chief Minister Dr David Moinina Sengeh signed for Sierra Leone, Principal Secretary for Foreign Affairs Dr A. Korir Sing’Oei for Kenya, and Sir Idris Elba for Akuna. The ceremony occurred in the presence of President Dr Julius Maada Bio.

The partnership aims to connect talent with skills, investment, enterprise, and markets across the Commonwealth. Sir Idris Elba, Chairman of The Akuna Group, stated the goal is to help turn creativity into opportunity so more people can make a living from their talent. For Sierra Leone, the agreement aligns with President Bio’s human capital agenda, with Chief Minister Sengeh calling young people a resource to invest in.

The collaboration complements Kenya's Talanta Hela initiative, offering youth creative enterprises a route to market. Akuna’s wider work includes a US$1 million Google.org grant for a programme training emerging filmmakers in Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria, Sierra Leone, and South Africa.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's young people will benefit from investment and opportunities to earn a living from their creative talents through this partnership.
