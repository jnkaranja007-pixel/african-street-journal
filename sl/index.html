Sierra Leone secures $30 million for climate resilience

The funds from the Climate Investment Funds' ARISE Programme will support adaptation and sustainable development.

Sierra Leone has secured US$30 million in climate investment support from the Climate Investment Funds (CIF) ARISE Programme. The announcement was made on Thursday, 12 November 2026, at the COP31 conference in Antalya, Türkiye. This funding aims to strengthen the country’s climate resilience and protect vulnerable communities.

The Ministry of Environment and Climate Change welcomed the investment as an opportunity to advance adaptation and promote sustainable economic development. Minister Jiwoh Emmanuel Abdulai stated that Sierra Leone's recognition as a front-runner country under the ARISE Programme affirms the government's commitment to resilience. The investment will focus on building resilient communities, protecting livelihoods, and safeguarding food and water security.

The US$30 million commitment requires clear information on financing conditions, implementing institutions, and intended beneficiaries. Transparency and accountability will be essential for effective resource management. Prioritized interventions include coastal protection, improved urban drainage, climate-smart agriculture, and disaster preparedness.

Why it mattersThe US$30 million climate investment support will focus on practical interventions like coastal protection and climate-smart agriculture to benefit communities threatened by erosion, flooding, and unpredictable rainfall.

ROCKCEM invests US$60 million to expand Sierra Leone cement supply

The manufacturer is building a grinding mill and a new warehouse in Songo to boost distribution nationwide.

ROCKCEM (SL) Ltd, a cement manufacturer, is undertaking a planned investment of approximately US$60 million to strengthen processing, warehousing and nationwide distribution of RC BRAND CEM II 42.5 R Cement in Sierra Leone. The company stated that rising equipment costs, inflation, changing global economic conditions and an expanding scope pushed the investment to that total.

Central to the industrial expansion is a modern cement grinding mill with a projected daily production capacity exceeding 60,000 bags, with each bag weighing 50 kilograms. According to the company, the facility is designed to improve supply consistency for residential, commercial and infrastructure projects.

The firm is also establishing a modern warehouse at Sumbuya in Songo that is scheduled to commence operations in November 2026. The new distribution hub is intended to serve wholesalers, retailers, contractors and construction companies outside Freetown.

Why it mattersConstruction companies and cement dealers outside Freetown can purchase supplies directly from the new Songo warehouse, reducing logistical expenses on fuel, vehicle maintenance and toll-gate charges.

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

The practice of burning wood for charcoal is destroying economic trees and increasing greenhouse gas emissions.

65-year-old Pa Alimamy has burned charcoal for over two decades in Sierra Leone, a trade that supports his family. Charcoal is widely used for cooking, especially in Freetown and other towns, because only 36% of the population has electricity and 15% has clean cooking access.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental damage, noting that burners are cutting down fruit trees like orange and mango. This deforestation exposes land to climate change and threatens food security, as trees are vital for absorbing carbon dioxide.

The burning process releases significant greenhouse gases, contributing to air pollution and climate change. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates that traditional wood energy emits 2.4 gigatons of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions.

Why it mattersDeforestation from charcoal burning threatens food security and increases greenhouse gas emissions, impacting the environment and livelihoods.

Sierra Leone opens West Africa investment summit in Lungi

The three-day WAIIS 2026 gathering, which runs to November 18, has a regional investment pipeline above US$180 billion, according to organisers.

Sierra Leone has opened the inaugural West Africa Integration and Investment Summit (WAIIS) 2026 at the Julius Maada Bio International Conference Centre in Lungi, running from November 16 to 18. President Julius Maada Bio is convening the event, which brings together political leaders, investors, business executives and development partners to explore economic cooperation across the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS).

Across four pillars (energy trade and green industrialisation, strategic minerals and natural resource development, agribusiness and food systems transformation, and digital transformation and connectivity), organisers say the gathering links Heads of State, ministers and development finance institutions with sovereign investors, business leaders and project owners. Barbados Prime Minister Mia Amor Mottley arrived ahead of the opening and attended a youth session with President Bio and Nigerian-born British actor and humanitarian Idris Elba.

A central feature is the WAIIS Deal Room, which is designed to match selected investment-ready projects with investors, financial institutions and strategic operators. The initiative targets participation from 15 West African countries, and the programme includes a commitment process meant to track progress after the event. Further discussions and investment engagements were expected to continue through November 18.

Why it mattersInvestors and project owners now have a three-day window, ending November 18, to pitch projects in the WAIIS Deal Room to financial institutions.

Analyst traces history of vice presidents in Sierra Leone

A public policy analyst examines why the vice presidency has never led to the presidency since independence.

Foday M. Daboh, a public policy analyst and political economist, stated in February that the vice presidency in Sierra Leone has historically functioned as a political balancing mechanism rather than a leadership incubator. According to the analysis, successive governments have used the office to appease party blocs, balance regional and religious considerations, neutralize internal rivals, and reward loyalty.

Historical examples cited in the publication include Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens, as well as Solomon Ekuma Berewa, who lost the 2007 presidential election despite his experience. The text also notes the tenure of Victor Bockarie Foh and the removal and expulsion of Samuel Sam-Sumana under the All Peoples Congress.

The analysis examines whether the pattern of the office has sealed the fate of the current vice president within the nation's political evolution. Constitutionally established as a stabilizing institution second only to the presidency under the 1991 Constitution, the office has consistently left occupants without an independent political base or a clear succession pathway.

Why it mattersReaders across Sierra Leone face ongoing political debates regarding the constitutional role and historical trajectory of the vice presidency.
