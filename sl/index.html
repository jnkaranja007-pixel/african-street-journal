Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental Protection Agency notes that economic trees are being cut, threatening food security and increasing exposure to climate change.

Charcoal burning, a common practice in Sierra Leone for cooking fuel, is leading to widespread deforestation and environmental degradation. Many families, like that of 65-year-old Pa Alimamy who has burned charcoal for two decades, rely on this trade for survival. The government's National Energy Compact indicates only 36% of the population has electricity access, with clean cooking access even lower at 15%.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental hazards, stating that burners are now cutting down economic trees like orange and mango. This practice leaves land treeless, exposed to climate change effects, and threatens food security. Paul Lamin noted that the trees cut take a long time to regrow and are essential for reducing carbon space.

Experts warn that greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production and use are harmful to human health and the environment. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions. Continued unsustainable practices will exacerbate climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's population faces limited energy alternatives, leading to deforestation and environmental damage from charcoal burning.

Sierra Leone passes 11 of 22 MCC indicators

The country improved its performance in economic management, governance, education, and business development.

Sierra Leone passed 11 of 22 indicators on the United States Government’s Millennium Challenge Corporation (MCC) Fiscal Year 2027 Scorecard, an increase from nine indicators in the previous assessment. The Ministry of Information and Civic Education announced the results on Friday, 6 November 2026, noting improvements in economic management, democratic governance, education, business development, and women’s economic participation. These results reflect ongoing reforms under President Dr. Julius Maada Bio aimed at strengthening institutions and promoting development.

The country satisfied mandatory MCC requirements for Personal Freedom and Control of Corruption within the Ruling Justly category. Sierra Leone also made progress in the Economic Freedom category, with inflation falling to 7.6 per cent from over 28 per cent previously, and passing the new Business Start-Up indicator. However, the packet notes further improvements are needed in access to finance, infrastructure, taxation, and the cost of doing business.

The MCC Scorecard assesses countries on Ruling Justly, Investing in People, and Economic Freedom to measure commitment to democratic governance, human development, and policies supporting economic growth. While Sierra Leone performed strongly in Ruling Justly, passing five of six indicators, Government Effectiveness remained below the passing threshold, indicating a need for continued reforms in public institutions and service delivery.

Why it mattersThe government's performance on the MCC scorecard affects its ability to attract investment and promote economic growth.

Politics · 2026-11-10

Sierra Leone vice presidents fail to reach presidency

No vice president has ever been elected president in Sierra Leone's history.

The office of Vice President in Sierra Leone has historically been a political dead end, failing to produce a successor to the presidency. Since independence, vice presidents have been appointed for reasons such as balancing regional or ethnic considerations, appeasing party factions, or neutralizing rivals, rather than for succession planning. This has often left them without independent political bases or clear pathways to higher office.

Past vice presidents have faced precarious political fortunes. Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah served under Siaka Stevens but were never groomed for leadership, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup attempt. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared during the Kabbah administration, lost the 2007 presidential election despite his experience. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and politically constrained, while Samuel Sam-Sumana was expelled from his party after falling out with the president.

The current Vice President's fate may already be sealed by this historical pattern, according to public policy analyst Foday M. Daboh. The 1991 Constitution envisions the vice presidency as a stabilizing institution ensuring continuity, but practice has shown it to be more of a political balancing tool. Berewa's 2007 defeat is cited as empirical evidence that competence in the role can be a liability, as voters opted for perceived change over experience.

Why it mattersSierra Leoneans seeking the presidency through the vice presidency have historically been unsuccessful, impacting political ambition and succession.

Sierra Leone seeks more Russian investment

Presidential Adviser Alhaji Musa Timothy Kabba urged expanded economic cooperation in energy, agriculture, and mining at the Russia–Africa Summit.

Presidential Adviser Alhaji Musa Timothy Kabba represented Sierra Leone at the Third Russia–Africa Summit in Moscow on Saturday, October 31, 2026, calling for increased Russian investment and technology transfer. He specifically sought cooperation in energy, agriculture, mining, and industrial development to transform diplomatic ties into economic partnerships that improve livelihoods.

Alhaji Musa Timothy Kabba, speaking on behalf of President Dr Julius Maada Bio, highlighted Africa’s potential alongside challenges like inadequate infrastructure and food insecurity. He stressed Sierra Leone's determination to attract responsible foreign investment for industrial growth, employment, and value addition, noting the 64-year diplomatic relationship.

The Presidential Adviser welcomed the reopening of the Russian Embassy in Freetown and the establishment of the Russian House as platforms for exchanges. Drawing on his experience as a Saint Petersburg University graduate, he emphasized the need for Russian participation in electricity generation, petroleum exploration, and peaceful nuclear-energy technology to boost industrialization.

Why it mattersSierra Leone aims to attract foreign investment and technology to boost its industrial growth, employment, and value addition.

Business · 2026-11-10

Sierra Leone could gain $108 million from AI

A new study ranks Sierra Leone 33rd out of 56 Commonwealth nations in AI readiness, identifying youth employment opportunities.

Sierra Leone could see its Gross Domestic Product (GDP) rise by US$108 million over the next decade through adopting Artificial Intelligence (AI), according to a new study. The 2026 Commonwealth Youth AI Readiness Index, released on Thursday, November 5, 2026, found that AI adoption could boost economic growth, youth employment, entrepreneurship, and innovation.

The Ayre Think Tank study ranked Sierra Leone 33rd out of 56 Commonwealth countries with a score of 43.6, below the 53-point benchmark. Researchers noted significant opportunities for strengthening the economy by investing in digital infrastructure, practical technological skills, and youth-led innovation, despite challenges like limited electricity reaching only 39.2 percent of the population.

An online survey in March 2026 found 79.3 percent of 122 young Sierra Leoneans aged 18-35 used AI tools for academic support, though this was not nationally representative. Calvin Ayre stated that success in the AI revolution depends on governments providing youth with skills, resources, and opportunities for business and employment.
