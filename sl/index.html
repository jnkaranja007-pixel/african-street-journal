Sierra Leone secures US$30 million under climate resilience programme

The Climate Investment Funds' ARISE programme announced the support at COP31 in Antalya, with financing arrangements and implementation still to be set out.

Sierra Leone secured US$30 million in climate investment support under the Climate Investment Funds' Accelerating Resilience Investments and Innovations for Sustainable Economies (ARISE) programme, announced on 12 November at the 31st Conference of the Parties in Antalya, Türkiye.

Environment and Climate Change Minister Jiwoh Emmanuel Abdulai said the recognition of Sierra Leone as a front-runner country under ARISE affirmed the government's commitment to resilience and adaptation. The announcement does not mean the funds have already entered government accounts; financing terms, project components, allocations and timelines will determine how they are deployed.

The government is expected to provide information on financing conditions, implementing institutions and intended beneficiaries. The article identifies coastal protection, urban drainage, climate-smart agriculture, water infrastructure, disaster preparedness and early-warning systems as possible priorities, with local communities involved in setting them.

Why it mattersThe government must disclose financing conditions, implementing institutions and intended beneficiaries before the US$30 million support is deployed.

Finance minister’s defender rejects call for his dismissal

Writer Foday M. Daboh says Sierra Leone’s economic hardship reflects global shocks, while arguing that Sheku Bangura restrained borrowing and inflation.

Foday M. Daboh has rejected a civil society consortium’s call to dismiss Sierra Leone Finance Minister Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura, arguing that economic hardship does not prove mismanagement. The consortium, identified as the CSOs Consortium on Public Accountability, demanded Bangura’s immediate removal.

Daboh says global disruptions between 2020 and 2024, including supply-chain problems, food and fuel inflation and tighter monetary policy, pressured economies. He argues Sierra Leone avoided default, retained access to concessional financing and stayed engaged with an International Monetary Fund programme under Bangura.

Citing Bank of Sierra Leone statements, Daboh says inflation later declined after fiscal and monetary tightening and reduced central-bank financing of deficits. He also acknowledges that Sierra Leone remains at high risk of debt distress, while saying borrowing is predominantly concessional and commercial borrowing has been restrained.

Why it mattersThe finance minister faces a demand for immediate dismissal, while the country remains at high risk of debt distress, according to the article.

Sierra Leone charcoal burners cut economic trees as demand grows

Environmental Protection Agency official Paul Lamin says the trade leaves land exposed and threatens food security, while 65-year-old Pa Alimamy depends on it for his family’s.

Charcoal production in Sierra Leone is cutting trees as households rely on the fuel for cooking, with 65-year-old burner Pa Alimamy saying the trade has kept him alive for more than two decades. He said his entire family depends on the work for income.

Paul Lamin, the Environmental Protection Agency’s director of natural resource governance, told Politico SL that burners now cut economic trees, including orange and mango, leaving land bare and threatening food security. Lamin said the trees take a long time to grow back, and acknowledged that people have few alternative energy sources.

The government’s National Energy Compact says 36% of the population has access to electricity and clean cooking reaches 15%. The Food and Agriculture Organization estimates traditional wood energy produces 1/2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, or 2-7% of global human-caused greenhouse-gas emissions; sub-Saharan Africa accounts for one third of emissions from wood energy.

Why it mattersPeople who depend on charcoal for cooking and income face scarce alternatives, while Lamin says cutting orange and mango trees threatens food security.

Sierra Leone passes 2027 MCC scorecard

The country met the benchmarks in an assessment of 104 nations, but passing does not guarantee it will receive development assistance.

Sierra Leone passed the Millennium Challenge Corporation’s 2027 Scorecard, meeting the required benchmarks in an annual assessment of national policy performance. The scorecard evaluates countries across 22 indicators covering governance, economic freedom and investment in people.

The corporation said 36 of the 104 countries assessed for fiscal year 2027 passed, while 68 failed to meet the benchmarks. The scorecards feed into a competitive process for development assistance, but the MCC board considers other factors when selecting countries, so Sierra Leone’s pass does not secure funding.

The MCC released the FY2027 country scorecards on Nov. 6, 2026. The assessment measures countries against the corporation’s policy standards.

Why it mattersThe MCC board must consider other factors before selecting Sierra Leone for development assistance, so the pass alone brings no funding guarantee.

Sierra Leone analysis says vice presidency offers no clear path to succession

Political economist Foday M. Daboh argues that the office has not helped any vice president win the presidency, citing Solomon Berewa’s 2007 defeat.

Sierra Leone political economist Foday M. Daboh argues that the vice presidency has not provided a clear route to the presidency, despite its constitutional position as the country’s second-highest office. In an analysis published in February 2026, he asks whether the pattern has already shaped the prospects of the current vice president.

Daboh says successive governments have used the office to balance regional, religious or ethnic interests, manage party factions and reward loyalty. He argues that vice presidents often enter without an independent political base or a defined succession path.

He points to Solomon Ekuma Berewa, who became the Sierra Leone People’s Party candidate for president in 2007 but lost to Ernest Bai Koroma. Daboh also cites former vice president Samuel Sam-Sumana, who was expelled from his party after becoming estranged from the president.

Why it mattersThe current vice president faces a succession question in an office that, Daboh says, has not produced an elected president.
