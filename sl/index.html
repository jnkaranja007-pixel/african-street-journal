Sierra Leone finance minister featured in Forbes Africa

Karefa Ansumana Francis Kargbo discussed economic reforms, fiscal discipline, and attracting foreign investment.

Sierra Leone’s Minister of Finance, Karefa Ansumana Francis Kargbo, was featured in the October/November 2026 Forbes Africa magazine. He detailed the government's economic reform agenda, achievements in macroeconomic stability, and plans to attract foreign investment.

In an interview titled “Securing Stability and Unlocking Sierra Leone’s Potential,” Kargbo highlighted fiscal discipline and domestic revenue mobilisation as key to economic transformation. He noted that the budget deficit narrowed and the primary fiscal balance shifted to a surplus. Inflation declined to 4.4 percent in December 2025.

The minister, who took office in August 2026, also discussed Sierra Leone’s ongoing engagement with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) through the Extended Credit Facility (ECF). He acknowledged past crises, including the civil war and Ebola outbreak, but stressed the country's resilience.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's economic reforms and stability are being highlighted to attract foreign investment and promote sustainable growth.

Shankerdas & Sons signs local content agreement

The company commits to increasing Sierra Leonean employment and skills development.

Shankerdas & Sons (SL) Limited signed a Local Content Plan Implementation Agreement with the Sierra Leone Local Content Agency on October 29, 2026, in Freetown.

The agreement aims to boost local employment, improve technical skills, support women's professional development, and increase Sierra Leonean business participation. Arjay Kumar, Administration Manager at Shankerdas & Sons, highlighted challenges including unreliable electricity and shortages of qualified personnel.

The company is committed to its women's empowerment program to promote inclusion and professional growth, recognizing the need for sustained investment in technical education and workforce development.

Why it mattersSierra Leoneans will benefit from increased job opportunities, skills training, and support for women in the workforce through this agreement.

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency confirms burning of economic trees threatens food security and exacerbates climate change effects.

Charcoal burning, a common practice in Sierra Leone for cooking fuel, is leading to widespread deforestation and environmental degradation. Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old charcoal burner for over two decades, stated his family relies on this trade for survival, highlighting its prevalence in Freetown and regional towns due to limited alternatives. The government's National Energy Compact reports only 36% of the population has electricity access, with clean cooking access at a mere 15%.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental hazards, noting that burners are now cutting down economic trees like orange and mango. This practice leaves land treeless and vulnerable to climate change, threatening food security. He confirmed that alternative energy options for the population are currently limited.

Experts estimate traditional wood energy, including charcoal, emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, contributing 2-7% of total human-caused greenhouse gas emissions globally, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions. The Food and Agricultural Organization warns that continued unsuitable charcoal production and use will worsen climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Sierra Leone lawmaker joins ECOWAS trade assessment

Hon. Abdul Kargbo participated in a mission to Liberia to evaluate a digital customs platform.

Sierra Leonean opposition lawmaker Hon. Abdul Kargbo joined an ECOWAS Parliament mission in Liberia to assess the SIGMAT digital platform for managing goods in transit. The four-day visit aimed to evaluate the platform's implementation and improve cross-border trade facilitation among member states. Kargbo, a member of the ECOWAS Parliament’s Committee on Infrastructure, stressed the importance of parliamentary oversight in regional trade programmes.

The delegation, led by Hon. Amdiatta Diaby of Senegal, visited the Liberia Revenue Authority and the Freeport of Monrovia. They identified challenges including inadequate road infrastructure, unreliable internet, and insufficient technical training hindering SIGMAT's effectiveness. Liberia Revenue Authority Assistant Commissioner William L. Buku noted electronic information-sharing can reduce human intervention and improve transparency.

The SIGMAT platform was officially launched in October 2025 connecting Côte d’Ivoire, Guinea, and Liberia. Sierra Leone is among countries implementing SIGMAT, making the assessment relevant to its regional trade efforts. The mission's findings highlight the need for member states to strengthen infrastructure, technical capacity, and institutional cooperation for digital customs operations.

Why it mattersSierra Leonean opposition lawmaker Hon. Abdul Kargbo participated in a regional assessment of a digital trade platform that affects cross-border movement of goods.

Politics · 2026-11-03

African youth protest deepening political dissatisfaction

Protests erupted in Kenya, Tanzania, Morocco and beyond demanding better governance amid economic hardship.

In 2025, Africa experienced significant economic growth of 3.8-4.1% driven by infrastructure and market reforms. However, this progress was overshadowed by widespread youth-led protests across Kenya, Tanzania, Morocco, and other nations. These demonstrations voiced deep dissatisfaction with governance and economic conditions.

The protests highlighted a growing disconnect between leaders' claims of stability and citizens' realities. Young people demanded improvements amid economic hardship, the impact of millions displaced by floods, and social spending cuts due to debt. Violence and crackdowns occurred in Tanzania, and unrest followed disputed elections in Cameroon.

The year also saw fragile transitions in Côte d'Ivoire and Gabon, and Guinea-Bissau returned to military rule, raising regional concerns. Geopolitical competition increased, with the U.S. approach under Trump affecting aid and trade deals like AGOA renewal. The AU's financial independence stalled, and ECOWAS weakened after member states withdrew.

Why it mattersAfrican youth are directly affected by demands for better governance and economic improvements amid hardship and debt.
