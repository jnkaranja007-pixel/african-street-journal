Sierra Leone delegation seeks Rwandan farm investment

Officials and private sector representatives are in Rwanda for a week to explore agricultural partnerships and technology transfer.

Sierra Leone's Deputy Minister II of Agriculture, Ing. Bryan Smart-Kanu, is leading a delegation to Rwanda to find agricultural investment and build partnerships. The group arrived on October 25, 2026, for a week-long tour to boost the nation's farming sector. This visit supports Sierra Leone's goal to attract investment and speed up the Feed Salone programme.

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) facilitated the trip, allowing Sierra Leonean officials and business leaders to share knowledge with Rwandan counterparts. They are examining farming methods and looking for areas for bilateral investment. The delegation includes people from poultry, cassava, and coffee sectors, discussing value chains, modern techniques, and extension services.

The tour, ending October 31, 2026, aims to identify practical lessons and investment prospects adaptable to Sierra Leone. These efforts are part of the Feed Salone programme, which seeks to increase domestic food production and food self-sufficiency. The experience gained is expected to help promote sustainable agricultural growth in Sierra Leone.

Why it mattersSierra Leonean officials and business leaders are seeking agricultural investment and partnerships in Rwanda to strengthen their country's farming sector and food production.

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency confirms practice destroys economic trees and harms air quality.

Charcoal burning for cooking remains common across Sierra Leone, particularly in Freetown, as only 36% of the population has electricity and 15% have clean cooking access. Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old burner for two decades, stated his family relies on the trade for survival. The process requires cutting down trees, including valuable fruit-bearing ones, and emits greenhouse gases.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental hazards, noting that burners now target economic trees like orange and mango. This leaves land treeless, exposed to climate change, and threatens food security, according to the agency. He admitted that alternative energy options are currently limited.

Experts estimate traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions. The Food and Agricultural Organization warns that continued unsustainable charcoal production will worsen climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersCharcoal burning leads to deforestation, habitat loss, and greenhouse gas emissions, impacting the environment and human health in Sierra Leone.

APC completes elections in 15 districts amid legal disputes

Legal challenges in Kambia and Karene delay the opposition party's internal electoral process, impacting a court directive.

The All People’s Congress (APC) has finished its lower-level elections in 15 of Sierra Leone’s 17 districts. However, unresolved petitions and legal disputes have stopped the party from completing the electoral process in Kambia and Karene. This situation was revealed during High Court proceedings in Freetown on Tuesday, October 27, 2026.

Patrick Hassan Kamara, National Coordinator of the APC’s Independent Elections Management Committee (IEMC), testified that outstanding petitions are the main reason for the delays in Kambia and Karene. He confirmed that disputes involving Wards 220 and 227 in Kambia are before the party’s Independent Electoral Commission (IEC). Kamara stated these issues must be resolved to ensure a credible process and reduce further legal challenges.

The delays raise concerns about the APC meeting a court directive to finish its remaining lower-level elections within 21 days. The court proceedings also highlighted operational issues, such as election personnel arriving late at polling locations. Rerun elections were conducted in parts of Port Loko, the Western Area, and Kono due to earlier challenges.

Why it mattersThe opposition APC party's internal elections are delayed by legal disputes in two districts, affecting their compliance with a court order.

Female artisanal miners report sexual violence in Kono district

Women working in diamond and gold pits in Jaiama Nimikor describe a pattern of rape and harassment that goes unpunished by local authorities.

Women working in the diamond-rich district of Kono in eastern Sierra Leone are reporting incidents of rape and sexual harassment while scavenging for minerals. Janet, a 32-year-old miner, stated she was raped at age 15 while working alone in a pit in Jaiama Nimikor, located 8 miles from Koidu. She reported the assault to her village head, but the perpetrator remained unidentified.

The violence often occurs in abandoned mining sites where women seek income to support their families. Haja, a 48-year-old widow, reported that she was raped by a man in her village while working as a gold miner. She stated that the camp master dismissed her complaint, claiming the assailant was her boyfriend, an allegation she denied. Haja fled to another village due to the resulting shame before eventually returning.

These women turned to artisanal mining as a primary livelihood following the end of the civil war. Many report that their complaints to local village leaders and camp masters have yielded no action against the perpetrators. The victims continue to face social stigma and long-term consequences, including unwanted pregnancies and the displacement of their children.

Why it mattersThe story details the experiences of women in Kono district who face sexual violence while working in artisanal diamond and gold mines.

Africa Finance Corporation and Munich Re launch US$1 billion trade finance programme

The new initiative will provide committed risk-sharing support for trade finance lending to African financial institutions.

Africa Finance Corporation (AFC) and Munich Re have launched a US$1 billion Unfunded Managed Debt Mobilisation Program. This programme will offer committed risk-sharing support for AFC’s trade finance lending to African financial institutions. The initiative aims to provide African banks with increased liquidity to finance intra-African and international trade flows. Munich Re will automatically take a share of eligible AFC transactions based on pre-agreed risk parameters and limits.

The first phase of the programme will focus on commercial letters of credit, trade loans, and trade guarantees, which are essential instruments for banks financing client imports and exports. Initially, it involves 38 financial institutions across 11 African countries. AFC Executive Board Member Banji Fehintola stated that the programme demonstrates how to attract institutional capital at scale to expand financing capacity. Wola Asase, Deputy Director & Head of Syndications, noted that this replaces previous ad-hoc risk sharing with automatic coverage for faster decisions.

This programme is designed to address committed capacity constraints for banks that finance trade, particularly as the African Continental Free Trade Area deepens economic integration. AFC aims to leverage its origination capabilities and structuring expertise to attract private capital. The capacity freed up as transactions mature will be reused to finance new investments, supporting Africa's development.

Why it mattersAfrican banks will receive more trade finance, delivered faster, to support their clients' import and export activities.
