Charcoal burning deforests Sierra Leone, harms environment

Environmental officials acknowledge the practice's hazards, including cutting economic trees and emitting greenhouse gases.

Charcoal burning, a common practice in Sierra Leone, particularly in Freetown, is contributing to deforestation and environmental degradation. The demand for charcoal as a cooking fuel persists due to limited access to electricity and gas, with only 36% of the population having electricity and 15% clean cooking access. This reliance on burning wood for charcoal production leads to habitat loss and biodiversity reduction.

The Environmental Protection Agency in Sierra Leone has acknowledged the significant environmental hazards associated with charcoal production. Director Paul Lamin noted that burners are increasingly cutting down economic trees like orange and mango, leaving land barren and vulnerable to climate change effects, which also threatens food security. He stated that the slow regrowth of these trees exacerbates the problem.

Experts warn that the entire charcoal process, from burning wood to its use as fuel, releases substantial greenhouse gases, impacting both human health and the environment. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates that traditional wood energy contributes 2-7% of total anthropogenic GHG emissions globally, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for a third of these emissions. Continued unsustainable practices are expected to worsen climate change.

Why it mattersThe practice of charcoal burning directly affects farmers by leading to deforestation and the destruction of economic trees, impacting land productivity and food security.

Sierra Leone Vice Presidency Fails to Produce Presidents

The office, constitutionally powerful, has historically served as a political cul-de-sac rather than a pathway to the presidency.

The Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone, though constitutionally second only to the presidency, has consistently failed to elevate its occupants to the highest office. Analysts note that successive governments have utilized the position for political balancing, regional appeasement, or to neutralize rivals, rather than as a leadership incubator. This practice has often left Vice Presidents without a clear succession plan or an independent political base, hindering their presidential ambitions.

Historical examples illustrate this pattern. Vice Presidents like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were influential but not groomed for succession. Minah’s downfall after an alleged coup attempt highlighted the vulnerability of the office. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared during the Kabbah era, lost the 2007 presidential election, demonstrating that competence alone was insufficient against a desire for change.

More recent cases, such as Victor Bockarie Foh's brief and constrained tenure, and Samuel Sam-Sumana's estrangement and expulsion from his party, further underscore the precariousness of the Vice Presidency. These instances suggest that proximity to presidential power does not guarantee political security or a future presidential bid, raising questions about the office's true value for political advancement.

Why it mattersThe Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone is a political liability for its occupants, as historical patterns show they are not groomed for succession and often lack political security.

Women face rape, harassment in Sierra Leone mines

Artisanal miners in Kono district recount sexual violence and exploitation as they seek income from abandoned pits.

Women in Sierra Leone's Kono district are facing rape and sexual harassment while working in abandoned mining sites, according to testimonies from artisanal miners. Janet, 32, recounted being raped at 15 in a mining site in Jaiama Nimikor, an experience that led to an unplanned pregnancy and community stigma. She now sends limited support to the child raised by relatives, while continuing to mine for income after her parents died in the civil war.

Haja, 48, also shared her experience of being raped by a man in her village after her husband's death, which forced her to flee her community and leave her three children behind. She reported the incident to the camp master at the mines, who dismissed her claims, suggesting the perpetrator might have been her boyfriend. Haja eventually returned to her village after her eldest daughter pleaded with her to come back.

These women turned to mining as one of the few available income sources following the civil war, scavenging in already mined-out pits for diamonds and gold. The difficult conditions and lack of security in these sites expose them to significant risks, with many reporting that their claims of assault were not adequately addressed by local authorities or mine management.

Why it mattersWomen miners are affected by rape and sexual harassment, which impacts their safety and livelihoods.

Finance Minister Defends Economic Record Against Civil Society Criticism

A civil society group's call for the finance minister's dismissal is misplaced, conflating global economic hardship with local policy failures.

A coalition of civil society organizations has called for the immediate dismissal of Sierra Leone's Finance Minister, Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura. This call is described as a flawed intervention that confuses economic hardship with mismanagement and populist frustration with policy failure. The statement from the "CSOs Consortium on Public Accountability" is criticized for lacking analytical rigor and empirical grounding, risking policy vandalism instead of legitimate accountability advocacy.

The critique highlights that between 2020 and 2024, global systemic shocks including COVID-19 supply-chain disruptions, the Russia-Ukraine war's impact on food and fuel prices, global monetary tightening, and a strong US dollar led to inflation and capital flight across many economies. While countries like Ghana, Zambia, Nigeria, and Kenya faced debt defaults or severe financial stress, Sierra Leone maintained its IMF program, preserved access to concessional financing, and avoided fiscal collapse due to disciplined macro-fiscal management.

Regarding inflation, data from the Bank of Sierra Leone indicates it peaked due to global shocks but has since declined through coordinated fiscal and monetary tightening, with central bank financing of deficits curtailed. IMF reports acknowledge improved fiscal discipline and reduced central bank financing helping to moderate inflationary pressures. In an economy importing over 70 percent of its consumption, inflation is seen as manageable rather than eliminable, with the minister credited for trend and containment.

Why it mattersThe finance minister's economic management is being questioned by civil society groups, potentially impacting public confidence and policy direction.

SLEWRC revenue jumps to NLE 7.4 million

The Sierra Leone Electricity and Water Regulatory Commission collected NLE 7.4 million in internal revenue in 2024, a significant increase from the NLE 2.3 million generated.

The Sierra Leone Electricity and Water Regulatory Commission (SLEWRC) reported a substantial rise in internal revenue generation, reaching NLE 7.4 million in 2024. This figure represents a notable increase from the NLE 2.3 million collected in 2023, primarily from levies and licenses. Despite not receiving budgeted funds from the Ministry of Finance for both 2023 and 2024, the commission successfully implemented key initiatives.

SLEWRC's Deputy Director, Sama Sandi, explained that in 2023, the commission sanitized the packaged water industry through enforcement actions, leading to more licenses and improved service quality. They also resolved an average of 80% of complaints within a week and took action against 64 non-compliant water companies. For 2024, SLEWRC expanded its operational network and developed a Multi-Year Tariff Order model for solar mini-grids, which is nearing completion.

Looking ahead to FY 2025-2027, SLEWRC has submitted a budget of NLE 5,590.29. The commission's strategic plan includes strengthening organizational capacity, ensuring market entry and universal access, maintaining international quality standards, and rolling out a warranty batching system for water products. SLEWRC is responsible for regulating electricity and water service providers, including issuing and renewing licenses and setting guidelines for service rates.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the regulatory body's increased revenue and operational activities suggest a more robust and regulated market for electricity and water services.
