Women miners face sexual violence in Sierra Leone's Kono district

Female artisanal gold miners in Jaiama Nimikor, Kono district, report experiencing rape and sexual harassment while working in the mines.

Women working in artisanal mines in Sierra Leone's Kono district are encountering sexual violence, according to accounts from female miners. Janet, now 32, shared her experience of being raped at age 15 in a mining site in Jaiama Nimikor, eight miles from Koidu, after her parents died during the civil war. She had turned to mining to support herself and her family.

Janet reported the rape to her village head, but could not identify her assailant. She became pregnant a month later and faced community stigmatization, leading a relative to raise her child. Similarly, Haja, 48, a widow with three children, stated she was raped by a man in her village after starting gold mining for livelihood.

Haja reported her assault, but said no action was taken, and the camp master in the mines dismissed her claim. She fled her community due to shame, leaving her children behind, but later returned at her eldest daughter's request. The man who raped Haja voluntarily left the village.

Why it mattersWomen in mining communities face significant personal safety risks and economic hardship, impacting their ability to earn a living and care for their families.

Charcoal Burning Threatens Sierra Leone's Environment and Food Security

Widespread charcoal production in Sierra Leone, driven by limited access to electricity and clean cooking alternatives, is causing significant deforestation and environmental.

Charcoal burning, a primary energy source for many households in Sierra Leone, is accelerating deforestation and threatening food security, according to Paul Lamin, Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency. The practice involves cutting down trees, including economic varieties like orange and mango, leaving land exposed to climate change effects.

The reliance on charcoal stems from a lack of affordable energy alternatives; only 36% of the population has access to electricity, and clean cooking access is critically limited at just 15%, as stated in the National Energy Compact supported by the World Bank. This high demand for charcoal, especially in Freetown and other regional towns, exacerbates environmental issues.

Experts and a report by the Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO) highlight that charcoal production and use emit large amounts of greenhouse gases, contributing 2-7% of total anthropogenic GHG emissions globally. Continued unsustainable production is expected to worsen climate change and reduce future wood-energy supplies, impacting both human health and the environment.

Why it mattersSierra Leoneans who rely on charcoal for cooking face health risks from emissions and the long-term threat of food insecurity due to environmental degradation.

Sierra Leone Vice Presidents Face Succession Challenges, Analyst Says

A public policy analyst highlights that the Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone has historically been a political cul-de-sac, with no Vice President ever ascending to the presidency.

The Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone, despite its constitutional power, has consistently failed to serve as a pathway to the presidency, according to Foday M. Daboh, a public policy analyst. Since independence, the office has functioned more as a political balancing mechanism than a leadership incubator, often used to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, or reward loyalty.

Historically, Vice Presidents have lacked a clear succession path and an independent political base. Examples include Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens, and Solomon Ekuma Berewa during the Kabbah era, who lost the 2007 presidential election despite being deeply involved in governance. This pattern suggests that competence and continuity have not been sufficient for electoral success.

Even more recently, Victor Bockarie Foh's brief tenure and Samuel Sam-Sumana's removal from office and party expulsion further illustrate the precarious nature of the position. Daboh suggests that proximity to power has not translated into political security or a presidential destiny for those holding the Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone's political evolution.

Why it mattersThe report directly affects politicians and citizens by revealing a consistent pattern where the Vice Presidency does not lead to the presidency, influencing political strategy and public perception of the office.

Mother protects autistic daughter from family and community discrimination

A mother in Sierra Leone has spent 15 years shielding her daughter, Fatima Dumbuya, from family members who believed the child was an evil spirit, leading to a move from Port.

Margaret Dumbuya and her husband moved from Port Loko to Freetown in December 2010 to protect their two-month-old daughter, Fatima, from family members who considered her an evil spirit. Fatima, now 15, was born prematurely in October 2010, and her slow development led to accusations of being a demon, with some family members suggesting she be killed.

Fatima faced constant discrimination and maltreatment in schools, leading her mother to withdraw her after an incident where a corporal punishment resulted in a knee injury. The injury still causes pain and requires surgery, which the family cannot afford. The ongoing challenges caused Fatima to speak to herself, reinforcing the belief among some neighbors and her father that she was evil.

Her uncle intervened, encouraging her father to take her to Kissy Psychiatric Hospital, where she received medication that calmed her and improved her speech. A doctor recommended a school for children with autism. Margaret now travels daily from Waterloo to Calaba Town, staying outside the school to monitor Fatima's progress and treatment, as she cannot afford round-trip transport.

Why it mattersThis story highlights the severe challenges faced by families with autistic children in Sierra Leone, including social stigma, lack of educational support, and financial burdens for medical care.

Finance Minister Bangura avoids default amid global economic shocks

Sierra Leone's Finance Minister Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura maintained IMF program engagement and preserved access to concessional financing, preventing fiscal freefall.

Sierra Leone's Finance Minister, Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura, has been credited with disciplined macro-fiscal management that prevented the country from defaulting on its external debt between 2020 and 2024. This contrasts with Ghana's default in 2022 and Zambia's protracted debt restructuring, according to Politico SL. The minister's actions helped Sierra Leone navigate global economic shocks, including COVID-19 supply-chain disruptions, the Russia–Ukraine war, and aggressive global monetary tightening.

The country also saw improved fiscal discipline and reduced central bank financing, which helped moderate inflationary pressures, as noted by IMF Country Reports for Sierra Leone (2023, 2024). While inflation peaked following global food and fuel shocks, it subsequently declined through coordinated fiscal and monetary tightening. The Bank of Sierra Leone's Monetary Policy Statements indicate that central bank financing of deficits was curtailed.

Furthermore, Sierra Leone avoided reckless debt accumulation by predominantly relying on concessional debt and restraining commercial borrowing. The IMF–World Bank Debt Sustainability Analysis indicates the country remains at high risk of debt distress, but debt management reforms have improved reporting and transparency. The country has no Eurobond exposure, hidden debt, or debt-fueled populist spending, according to Politico SL.

Why it mattersThe stability of Sierra Leone's economy directly affects all citizens by influencing the cost of goods, job security, and the availability of public services.
