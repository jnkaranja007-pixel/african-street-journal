Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

The practice of burning wood for charcoal is depleting forests and threatening food security in Sierra Leone.

Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old charcoal burner in Sierra Leone, relies on the trade for his family's livelihood, a common practice in Freetown and other towns due to limited access to electricity and clean cooking options. The National Energy Compact states only 36% of the population has electricity access, and 15% has clean cooking access.

Charcoal production involves cutting down trees, including economic ones like mango and orange, leading to deforestation, habitat loss, and environmental degradation. Paul Lamin from the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the hazards, noting that treeless land is exposed to climate change effects and threatening food security.

The burning process emits greenhouse gases, contributing to climate change and air pollution, with traditional wood energy emitting 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually. The Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO) warns that continued unsustainable charcoal production will exacerbate climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersDeforestation from charcoal burning exposes land to climate change and threatens food security for households relying on economic trees.

Sierra Leone seeks stronger coordination of national data systems

A national review found more than 70 data initiatives in Sierra Leone, yet only about 30% of national data systems exchange information effectively.

The Ministry of Planning and Economic Development (MoPED) is seeking stronger coordination among government institutions to improve Sierra Leone's national data systems, after concerns over fragmented platforms, limited information sharing and weak investment in data management. The issue was discussed on November 13, 2026, at the inaugural Steering Committee meeting of the Power of Data (PoD) Initiative in the MoPED Conference Hall in Freetown.

A national review presented at the meeting identified more than 70 data initiatives, about 160 stakeholders, over 20 policy and legal frameworks and 10 development partners. Less than 1% of the national budget went to data and statistics, and the country's data protection legislation remains at draft stage. Deputy Development Secretary I, Usman Cherry Conteh, urged government agencies to work together to prevent duplication and produce practical results.

The Minister's statement, read by Conteh, called for existing data platforms to be connected rather than replaced, and recommended an implementation plan with clear responsibilities, timelines, budget allocations and monitoring arrangements. UNFPA Deputy Representative Sibeso Mululuma encouraged the government to increase domestic financing, and Power of Data consultant Bailah Molleh outlined five priority areas under the proposed 2026–2030 Country Implementation Framework.

Why it mattersGovernment agencies must connect existing data platforms and set budgets and timelines, while less than 1% of the national budget currently goes to data and statistics.

Orange Sierra Leone holds talks with government on network expansion

The company's board chairman and chief executive met the speaker of parliament, the chief minister and the telecoms regulator's director general on Thursday, November 12.

Orange Sierra Leone has held talks with senior government officials on improving telecommunications services, expanding network coverage and increasing investment in digital infrastructure. The meetings on Thursday, November 12, brought together the company's board chairman, Brelotte Ba, and its chief executive, Aicha Touré, with representatives of key government institutions.

The delegation met Speaker of Parliament Segepoh Solomon Thomas, Chief Minister David Moinina Sengeh, National Telecommunications Authority (NatCA) Director General Amara Brewah and Minister of Communication, Technology and Innovation Salamata Momonah Bah. Key issues discussed included extending coverage to more communities, improving service reliability and strengthening consumer protection.

The company said it would keep working with government institutions and regulators on innovation, connectivity and digital and economic development. A separate report of the same engagements said Ba and Touré also paid a courtesy visit to President Julius Maada Bio at State House in Freetown, where they reaffirmed the group's investment commitment.

Why it mattersResidents in communities not yet covered by the network stand to gain access if the coverage extension agreed with regulators goes ahead.

Sierra Leone vice presidents rarely become president

No vice president in Sierra Leone has ever been elected president, raising questions about the current vice president's future.

The office of Vice President in Sierra Leone has historically been a political dead end, with no holder ever ascending to the presidency. Since independence, vice presidents have often been appointed to balance political considerations rather than to groom successors.

Past vice presidents like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were powerful but not groomed for succession, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared, lost the 2007 presidential election despite his experience, indicating that competence alone is insufficient for electoral success.

Samuel Sam-Sumana's experience as a vice president who was later estranged from the president, expelled from his party, and left politically isolated, further illustrates the precariousness of the position. Victor Bockarie Foh's short tenure also lacked succession ambition.

Why it mattersThe current Vice President of Sierra Leone faces a historical pattern where the office does not lead to the presidency, impacting future political ambitions.

Culture · 2026-11-14

30 rural women honoured for leadership in Sierra Leone

The Advocacy for Women and Youth Empowerment recognised women for contributions in education, healthcare, politics, and entrepreneurship.

The Advocacy for Women and Youth Empowerment (AWYE) honoured 30 women for their leadership and contributions to national development at its 8th annual awards ceremony on Saturday, November 7, 2026, in Waterloo.

30 women from the Western Area Rural District were picked from 157 nominations over three months, and their work spans education, healthcare, politics, entrepreneurship, women's empowerment and community development. Deputy Opposition Whip in Parliament Kemokoh Conteh urged the awardees to support one another as Waterloo faces growing development challenges.

AWYE Executive Director Ibrahim Marvel Kamara announced the launch of the 2026 Award Magazine and appealed for US$20,000 to secure an operational office. Susue Women's Finance CEO Naasu Genevieve Fofanah, who launched the magazine, encouraged women to embrace entrepreneurship and financial independence.
