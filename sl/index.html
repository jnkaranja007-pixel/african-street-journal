Health · 2026-10-23

sierra leone reviews ebola readiness amid critical gaps

health ministry identifies urgent needs in coordination, isolation capacity, and supplies.

Sierra Leone's Ministry of Health, via the National Public Health Agency (NPHA), has reviewed the nation's Ebola preparedness, identifying critical gaps in emergency response systems. The review, held at NPHA headquarters in Wilberforce, focused on strengthening coordination, hospital isolation capacity, infection prevention, and contact tracing.

Officials stressed the need to activate the incident management structure and assess hospital readiness, while also highlighting the importance of engaging paramount chiefs and community leaders for public awareness. The NPHA called on stakeholders to urgently address identified gaps and ensure adequate supplies for operational readiness.

This review follows reports of seven confirmed Ebola cases in Kenya, linked to an outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Sierra Leone has intensified surveillance at Freetown International Airport and land borders, particularly for travellers from affected countries.

Why it mattersSierra Leone is reviewing its Ebola preparedness due to identified gaps and recent cases in Kenya.

Sierra Leone launches national shipping company with China

The new company, Sierra Leone Ocean Shipping Company, plans to build a fleet of 50 vessels.

President Julius Maada Bio launched the Sierra Leone Ocean Shipping Company on Monday, October 19, 2026, in Freetown. This joint venture between the Sierra Leone National Shipping Agency and China’s Zhejiang Xiehai Group aims to increase the nation's stake in transporting its own goods. The state agency holds a 30% share, while the Chinese partner owns 70%.

The company plans to build a fleet of 50 vessels, with 30 ships in the first phase of development. President Bio stated that this move reduces reliance on foreign carriers for exports and imports. The initiative is expected to strengthen maritime connectivity and facilitate the transport of minerals, agricultural commodities, and other commercial goods.

This venture seeks to position Sierra Leone as a maritime and logistics hub along the West African coast. It is designed to enhance the country's participation in international shipping and ensure greater economic benefits from its trade activities. The government has called for transparency and accountability in the company's operations.

Why it mattersSierra Leoneans will benefit from employment opportunities and greater economic returns from the nation's trade.

Sierra Leone advances open contracting in Africa

The nation showcased progress in transparent public procurement at a Rabat conference, aiming to reduce corruption.

Sierra Leone joined 15 other African nations last week at a conference in Rabat, Morocco, to showcase progress in transparent public procurement. The three-day Open Government Partnership event, themed "Towards an Open Africa," aimed to advance open governance across the continent.

Joseph Munda Bindu, Chairman of NACCED and OGP Focal Point, presented Sierra Leone's approach to open contracting, noting that over 30 percent of the national budget is channeled through procurement. He stated that the country's 2024–2028 National Action Plan embeds open contracting to reduce corruption and improve public service delivery.

Rev. Alusine Diamond Suma, the OGP Civil Society representative, emphasized that open contracting requires making data usable and accessible for citizens. The conference, organised by Morocco's Ministry of Digital Transition and Administrative Reform, also addressed open budgeting and digital technology.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's participation in the Rabat conference highlights its commitment to transparent public procurement and anti-corruption reforms.

Health · 2026-10-23

Sierra Leone seeks UAE funding for medical campus

Health Minister Austin H. Demby discussed a proposed 300-bed referral hospital and training facilities with UAE counterpart Ahmed Bin Ali Al Sayegh.

Sierra Leone’s Minister of Health, Austin H. Demby, met with UAE Minister Ahmed Bin Ali Al Sayegh in Abu Dhabi to seek support for healthcare development. The talks focused on improving medical infrastructure, emergency preparedness, and maternal and child health. Demby highlighted Sierra Leone's progress, including a 32 per cent reduction in maternal mortality during a recent campaign.

Demby presented plans for the Jembe Medical Campus, which would include a 300-bed regional referral hospital and training for medical professionals. He sought strategic partners to finance and construct the facility to expand specialised care and strengthen the national health workforce. The UAE minister affirmed his country's commitment to continued cooperation.

The meeting occurred on the sidelines of the Abu Dhabi Future Health Summit. Sierra Leone's Minister of Health was accompanied by Chief Medical Officer Dr. med Mustapha Sundifu Kabba and Ambassador Rashid Sesay. The UAE has previously supported Sierra Leone with a 100-bed field hospital and medical supplies.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's health sector will benefit from potential UAE investment in a new medical campus and training facilities.

Sierra Leone vice presidents fail to win presidency

No vice president in Sierra Leone has ever been elected president, raising questions about the current officeholder's future.

In Sierra Leone, the office of Vice President has historically been a political dead end, with no holder ever ascending to the presidency. The 1991 Constitution outlines a powerful role, but governments have used the position to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, or neutralize rivals, rather than cultivate future leaders. This practice has often left Vice Presidents without a clear succession path or an independent political base.

Post-independence history shows Vice Presidents like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah were powerful but not groomed for succession, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup attempt. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared, lost the 2007 presidential election despite his experience, demonstrating that competence in the vice presidency did not guarantee electoral success. Victor Bockarie Foh's short tenure and Samuel Sam-Sumana's estrangement from the president and party expulsion further illustrate the precariousness of the office.

The current Vice President faces a historical pattern where proximity to power has not translated into political security or a pathway to the presidency. This recurring national debate questions whether the Vice Presidency is a curse or a blessing, and whether the current officeholder's fate is already sealed by this trend.

Why it mattersThe Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone has historically been a political cul-de-sac, with no Vice President ever being elected President, impacting the career trajectory of current and future officeholders.
