Security guard jailed for stealing CCTV camera

Alie Sesay received a six-month sentence for taking a camera valued at Le2,540 in Freetown.

A 34-year-old security guard, Alie Sesay, was sentenced to six months in prison in Freetown for stealing a CCTV camera. The camera was valued at Le2,540 and was taken from Aberdeen. Magistrate Medissa Massaquoi of Pademba Road Magistrate Court No. 6 handed down the sentence on October 30, 2026, after Sesay pleaded guilty to larceny.

Sesay stole the CCTV camera belonging to Bilal Bah on September 21, 2026, from No. 90 Sir Samuel Lewis Road in Freetown. He admitted the offence without contesting the charge. Sub-Inspector Amiza John prosecuted the case and presented Sesay’s voluntary caution statement. The prosecutor noted Sesay was a first-time offender.

Magistrate Massaquoi considered Sesay's guilty plea and lack of prior convictions as mitigating factors. She acknowledged that his admission saved the court time and resources. Despite these factors, the magistrate ordered Sesay to serve his sentence at the Male Correctional Facility in Freetown.

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency confirms burning of economic trees threatens food security and exacerbates climate change effects.

Charcoal burning, a common practice across Sierra Leone for cooking fuel, is leading to significant deforestation and environmental degradation. Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old burner for two decades, stated his family relies on the trade for survival, highlighting its prevalence in Freetown and regional towns due to limited access to electricity and clean cooking alternatives. The National Energy Compact reports only 36% of the population has electricity access and 15% clean cooking access.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental hazards, noting that burners are now cutting down economic trees like orange and mango. He stated that this practice leaves land treeless, exposed to climate change, and threatens food security. Paul Lamin explained that the slow regrowth of these trees hinders carbon reduction.

Greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production and use are harmful to human health and the environment. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates wood energy, including charcoal, emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, contributing 2-7% of global anthropogenic GHG emissions. Continued unsustainable practices are expected to worsen climate change, impacting forest productivity and future wood energy supplies.

Female artisanal miners report sexual violence in Kono district

Women working in the diamond-rich Jaiama Nimikor area describe systemic rape and harassment while scavenging for gold and diamonds in abandoned pits.

Women working in the artisanal mining sector in Jaiama Nimikor, Kono district, report frequent instances of rape and sexual harassment. Janet, a 32-year-old miner, stated she was raped at age 15 while scavenging for gold and diamonds in abandoned pits located 8 miles from Koidu. She reported the incident to her village head at the time but could not identify her attacker.

The violence has forced women to abandon their homes and families due to social stigma and a lack of official recourse. Haja, a 48-year-old widow, said she fled to the village of Guitor after being raped while working in the mines. She claimed the camp master dismissed her report by suggesting the attacker was her boyfriend, an assertion she denies.

Many women turned to mining as a primary source of income following the end of the civil war. These miners continue to face risks while working in isolated areas to support their families. Haja eventually returned to her original village after her attacker voluntarily left the area.

Why it mattersThe report details the physical and social dangers faced by women working in the artisanal mining sector in Sierra Leone's Kono district.

Politics · 2026-10-31

African youth protest governance amid economic hardship

Protests erupted in Kenya, Tanzania, Morocco and other nations as citizens questioned growth figures.

Youth-led protests demanding better governance swept across Kenya, Tanzania, Morocco and other African nations in 2025. These demonstrations occurred amidst economic hardships, widespread floods displacing millions, and mounting debt that strained social spending.

The protests highlighted a disconnect between official narratives of economic resilience and the lived experiences of citizens. While leaders reported growth around 3.8-4.1% driven by infrastructure and market reforms, many young people felt left behind by persistent social challenges and political dissatisfaction.

The year 2025 also saw high-stakes elections and unrest in several capitals, including violence and crackdowns in Tanzania and disputed victories in Cameroon. Fragile transitions occurred in Côte d'Ivoire and Gabon, while Guinea-Bissau returned to military rule, raising regional concerns.

Why it mattersYoung people across Africa are protesting against their governments due to economic difficulties and a lack of good governance.

Two Sierra Leone police officers to study forensic science in India

The officers will gain advanced knowledge in scientific evidence analysis and modern crime detection techniques.

Two Sierra Leone Police officers are travelling to India for master’s degrees in forensic science, starting Friday, October 30, 2026. This initiative aims to strengthen criminal investigations and improve the force's technical capabilities. The officers received their travel tickets at a ceremony at Police Headquarters in Freetown.

Acting Director of Training, ACP John Fayiah Tumbay, presented the travel documents, urging the officers to represent Sierra Leone and the police with discipline. He stressed the importance of acquiring specialised skills to improve the force's investigative capacity and service delivery upon their return.

The training is expected to enhance forensic investigation practices and the handling of evidence, contributing to more effective investigations and public confidence. Inspector-General William Fayia Sellu’s efforts focus on personnel development and responding to evolving security challenges.
