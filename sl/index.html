Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency confirms practice destroys economic trees and worsens climate change effects.

Charcoal burning, a common cooking fuel source in Sierra Leone, is leading to widespread deforestation and environmental degradation. The practice involves cutting down trees, including valuable fruit-bearing ones like mango and orange, to produce charcoal, leaving land exposed to climate change impacts. This process also emits greenhouse gases harmful to human health and the environment.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the hazards, noting that the slow regrowth of cut trees contributes to deforestation. He stated that limited alternative energy options exacerbate the problem, forcing people to rely on charcoal production. Experts estimate that traditional wood energy, including charcoal, emits 2-7% of total global anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions annually.

The demand for charcoal is increasing, which the Food and Agricultural Organization warns will worsen climate change. This, in turn, could negatively affect forest health and reduce future wood-energy supplies. Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old charcoal burner, stated that his family relies on this trade for their livelihood, highlighting the survival aspect of the practice.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's population faces limited access to electricity and clean cooking, with only 36% having electricity and 15% having clean cooking access, forcing reliance on charcoal.

Sierra Leone finance minister details economic reforms in Forbes Africa

Karefa Ansumana Francis Kargbo discusses fiscal discipline, revenue mobilisation, and attracting foreign investment.

Sierra Leone's Minister of Finance, Karefa Ansumana Francis Kargbo, featured in Forbes Africa's October/November 2026 edition, outlined the government's economic reform agenda. He discussed achievements in macroeconomic stability and plans to attract foreign investment, strengthen public financial management, and foster sustainable economic growth.

In an interview titled “Securing Stability and Unlocking Sierra Leone’s Potential,” Kargbo, who took office in August 2026, highlighted fiscal discipline, domestic revenue mobilisation, international financial cooperation, and private sector development. He noted that the budget deficit narrowed and the primary fiscal balance shifted from a deficit to a surplus.

The minister also reported that inflation declined to single digits in April 2025, falling to 4.4 percent by December 2025, attributing this to fiscal and monetary policy. Sierra Leone continues its engagement with the International Monetary Fund through the Extended Credit Facility.

Why it mattersThe Finance Minister's interview in Forbes Africa details the government's strategy for economic growth and stability, affecting businesses and investors.

Politics · 2026-11-04

Sierra Leone vice presidents lack succession path

The constitution grants power, but political appointments sideline vice presidents from presidential ambition.

Sierra Leone's Vice Presidency, established by the 1991 Constitution to ensure continuity, has historically served as a political balancing tool rather than a leadership incubator. Vice Presidents are often appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, preventing them from developing independent political bases or clear succession pathways.

Past Vice Presidents like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were powerful but not groomed for succession, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup attempt. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared during the Kabbah era, lost the 2007 presidential election, demonstrating that competence alone does not guarantee success. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and constrained, and Samuel Sam-Sumana was expelled from his party after falling out with the President.

The pattern suggests that the office, while constitutionally powerful, has become a political liability for those seeking higher office. This recurring issue prompts debate on whether the Vice Presidency is a curse or a blessing in Sierra Leone's political evolution.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's vice presidents have historically been unable to transition to the presidency due to political appointments and lack of independent bases.

Business · 2026-11-04

UNDP trains Sierra Leone innovators on plastic solutions

20 entrepreneurs will develop scalable businesses to tackle plastic pollution and create jobs.

The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) has launched a three-day training for 20 Sierra Leonean innovators in Freetown. The programme aims to help them scale solutions for plastic pollution into viable businesses. This initiative is part of the Global Plastics Innovation Programme (GPIP), funded by the UN Peace and Development Trust Fund.

The training combines expert sessions, practical exercises, and networking to address challenges like accessing finance and markets. Participants will receive ongoing technical support to strengthen their business models. Innovators are developing solutions for waste collection, recycling, reuse systems, and circular business models across Sierra Leone.

Kevin Petrini, UNDP Deputy Resident Representative, stated that the fight against plastic pollution is a story of innovation and entrepreneurship. Binta Barrie, UniPod Lead, noted the programme equips innovators with knowledge and tools to increase their impact and turn local solutions into sustainable enterprises.

Why it matters20 Sierra Leonean innovators will gain skills to build businesses addressing plastic pollution, creating jobs and improving livelihoods.

Sallieu Bah penalty moves Zorya to fifth

Sierra Leone international Sallieu Bah scored a 55th-minute penalty to give Zorya Luhansk a 1-0 win over Epitsentr.

Sierra Leonean footballer Sallieu Bah scored a penalty in the 55th minute on November 2, leading Zorya Luhansk to a 1-0 victory against Epitsentr. The goal secured three points for Zorya at the Stadion Valeriy Lobanovskyi, moving them to fifth place in the Ukrainian Premier League standings with 18 points from 11 matches.

The Ukrainian Premier League reported that an air raid alert delayed the second half, and another alert later forced players off the field in the 69th minute before play resumed. Bah's penalty extended Epitsentr's winless streak to seven league games, with two draws and five defeats.

Epitsentr remained 10th in the league with nine points after the Matchday 11 fixture.
