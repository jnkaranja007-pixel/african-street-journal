Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Charcoal production is destroying economic trees and contributing to greenhouse gas emissions in Sierra Leone.

Charcoal burning is a common practice in Sierra Leone, particularly in Freetown, to meet cooking fuel demands where only 36% of the population has electricity and 15% have clean cooking access. This process involves cutting down trees, including valuable fruit trees like orange and mango, leading to deforestation and habitat loss. The burning itself releases greenhouse gases harmful to human health and the environment.

Paul Lamin, Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency, acknowledged the environmental hazards, noting that the slow regrowth of trees exacerbates deforestation and reduces the land's capacity to absorb carbon. He also stated that charcoal burners are increasingly cutting down economic trees, threatening food security and exposing the land to climate change effects.

The Food and Agricultural Organization estimates that traditional wood energy, including charcoal, emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa contributing one-third of these emissions. Continued unsustainable charcoal production and use are expected to worsen climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersDeforestation and greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal burning threaten Sierra Leone's environment and food security.

Sierra Leone vice presidents fail to reach presidency

No vice president has ever been elected president in Sierra Leone, raising questions about the office's succession pathway.

The office of Vice President in Sierra Leone has historically been a political cul-de-sac rather than a path to the presidency. Since independence, no Vice President has successfully transitioned to the top job, leading to a recurring national debate about the office's true value and purpose.

The 1991 Constitution outlines a powerful role, but governments have often used the position for political balancing, regional appeasement, or neutralizing rivals, rather than cultivating future leaders. This practice frequently leaves Vice Presidents without a clear succession plan or an independent political base, as seen in cases like Solomon Ekuma Berewa's decisive loss in the 2007 presidential election.

Former Vice Presidents such as Sorie Ibrahim Koroma, Francis Minah, Samuel Sam-Sumana, and Victor Bockarie Foh have all experienced political challenges or limitations despite their proximity to power. The current Vice President's fate is now being questioned against this historical pattern of vice presidential political vulnerability.

Socfin worker dies after falling from moving truck

Sebatu Sengeh, an agriculture carrier, died Friday after attempting to board a utility truck that was in motion.

An agriculture carrier named Sebatu Sengeh has reportedly died following a workplace accident at Socfin Agricultural Company (SAC) in Pujehun District. The incident occurred on Friday, October 23, 2026, at approximately 3:45 p.m. along Road 32G/F in Block 33, Division 3. Sengeh was assigned to the company’s Agriculture Department.

According to a Socfin press release, Sengeh reportedly tried to board a moving utility truck, identified as Howo 45, which was collecting field workers. The company stated that its management was notified shortly after the incident and alerted local authorities and emergency response teams. The exact circumstances are under investigation, with Socfin cooperating with the Sierra Leone Police and safety authorities.

Socfin expressed condolences to Sengeh’s family and is offering support to them. The company operates an oil palm plantation in Malen Chiefdom and stated it adheres to occupational health and safety standards. A final investigation report will be shared if further public communication is required.

Why it mattersSebatu Sengeh, an agriculture carrier, died in a workplace accident.

Female artisanal miners report sexual violence in Kono district

Women working in abandoned diamond and gold pits in Jaiama Nimikor describe a pattern of rape and harassment while seeking to earn a living.

Women working in the diamond-rich district of Kono in Sierra Leone are reporting frequent incidents of rape and sexual harassment at artisanal mining sites. Janet, a 32-year-old miner, stated she was raped at age 15 while scavenging for gold and diamonds in Jaiama Nimikor, a site located eight miles from Koidu. She reported the incident to the village head at the time, but the perpetrator remained unidentified.

Another miner, 48-year-old Haja, said she was raped by a man in her village while working in the mines to support her three children. Haja reported the assault to the camp master, who dismissed her claim by suggesting the attacker was her boyfriend. The social stigma and lack of official response forced Haja to temporarily flee her community to the village of Guitor, leaving her children behind.

These women turned to mining as a primary source of income following the end of the civil war. Many miners in the region are former students who dropped out of school due to poverty. While Haja eventually returned to her village after her attacker left the area, she continues to face the long-term consequences of the assault.

Why it mattersThe report details the experiences of two women, Janet and Haja, who were raped while working as artisanal miners in Kono district.

sierra leone finance minister defends economic management

A civil society group called for the finance minister's dismissal, citing economic hardship and debt.

A coalition of civil society groups, the “CSOs Consortium on Public Accountability,” has called for the dismissal of Sierra Leone’s Finance Minister, Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura. The group attributes current economic hardship and debt levels to mismanagement, but the minister’s defense argues these issues stem from global shocks like the COVID-19 pandemic and the Russia-Ukraine war. The minister points to Sierra Leone’s avoidance of debt default, unlike Ghana and Zambia, as evidence of disciplined macro-fiscal management.

The finance minister’s office stated that inflation, while high, has declined from its peak due to coordinated fiscal and monetary tightening, and reduced central bank financing. Data from the Bank of Sierra Leone and IMF reports support this, noting improved fiscal discipline. Given that Sierra Leone imports over 70 percent of its consumption, the focus is on managing inflation rather than eliminating it, a task the minister claims to have delivered on.

Regarding debt, the minister’s office highlights that Sierra Leone's debt, while at high risk of distress, is predominantly concessional with restrained commercial borrowing. Reforms have improved reporting and transparency, with no Eurobond exposure or hidden debt. The minister resisted pressure for unchecked borrowing to fund subsidies and wage hikes without revenue, a move that could have triggered default.

Why it mattersThe finance minister's actions and the claims against him directly affect the economic policies and financial stability of Sierra Leone.
