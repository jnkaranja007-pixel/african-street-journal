Idris Elba's Akuna Group offers free AI filmmaking course

Aspiring creators in Sierra Leone can apply for a programme using artificial intelligence to develop and produce films.

The Akuna Group, chaired by Sir Idris Elba, has opened applications for a free artificial intelligence filmmaking programme called From Script to Screen with AI. Aspiring creators in Sierra Leone, Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria, and South Africa can apply online until November 15, 2026. The initiative, in partnership with Google and with curriculum from Curious Refuge, aims to teach African storytellers how to use AI tools to create films. Selected projects will receive production funding.

The programme will run until October 2027, starting with a 12-week online academy from February to April 2027. Participants will then move into mentoring, project pitching, and film production stages. All participants will receive foundational training in AI filmmaking before choosing to specialise in fiction, documentary, animation, advertising, or screenwriting. Applicants need to be at least 18 years old and reside in one of the five participating countries, with access to a laptop, internet, and electricity.

Sir Idris Elba stated the programme aims to improve access to education and equip African creators to tell their own stories using AI. Khaled Gad, CEO of The Akuna Group, added that the programme provides a pathway from training to funded films, with creators retaining control of their work. Selected participants will have opportunities to showcase their completed films.

Why it mattersAspiring filmmakers and visual artists in Sierra Leone can gain new skills and funding to produce films using artificial intelligence.

Sierra Leone president warns kush sellers to stop

President Julius Maada Bio visited a rehabilitation facility and urged drug peddlers to cease their illicit trade.

President Julius Maada Bio has ordered sellers of the synthetic drug Kush and other harmful substances to stop their trade immediately. He issued the warning during a visit to the Government’s Harmful Drug Rehabilitation Facility in Hastings on October 30, 2025. The president stated that these peddlers are profiting from an illicit trade that is causing widespread damage in Sierra Leone.

Addressing recovering victims, Bio assured them of his administration's dedication to halting the sale of dangerous drugs. He expressed sadness that some parents are involved in selling Kush and urged them to find legitimate ways to earn a living. The president also called on the judiciary and police to intensify actions against those involved in the illegal drug trade.

President Bio highlighted the government's efforts to protect Kush users by establishing rehabilitation centers in Hastings, Gondama, and Daru, with plans for another in Makeni. He noted that the drug has caused the deaths of an unspecified number of young people since its emergence around 2019. The Presidential Task Force on Drugs and Substance Abuse was launched, and a national emergency declared to combat drug use.

Why it mattersSierra Leonean citizens, particularly young people, are at risk of addiction and death from the synthetic drug Kush.

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental Protection Agency notes burning of economic trees threatens food security and increases climate change effects.

Charcoal burning, a common practice in Sierra Leone for cooking fuel, is leading to widespread deforestation and environmental degradation. Many families rely on this trade for their livelihood, especially in Freetown and other regional towns where access to electricity is limited to 36% of the population and clean cooking access is only 15%. The process involves cutting down trees, including valuable economic species like orange and mango, and emits greenhouse gases.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental hazards, stating that the practice leaves land treeless and exposed to climate change. He noted that the trees cut take a long time to regrow and are essential for reducing carbon space. Experts also highlight that greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production and use are harmful to human health and the environment.

Globally, traditional wood energy, including charcoal, emits an estimated 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent per year, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions. The Food and Agricultural Organization warns that continued unsuitable charcoal production and use could exacerbate climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersDeforestation from charcoal burning threatens food security and exacerbates climate change effects for the population of Sierra Leone.

Brookfields hotel blames ministry over lease dispute

Hotel director Alhaji Ahmed Wurie says Ministry of Tourism and Cultural Affairs failed to review rent as agreed in 2015.

Brookfields hotel executive director Alhaji Ahmed Wurie blamed the Ministry of Tourism and Cultural Affairs for breaching their lease agreement. Speaking at a parliamentary committee meeting on October 29, 2026, Wurie stated the ministry, not the hotel, should initiate rent reviews every five years. He noted the rent was reviewed in 2015 from 40,000 dollars to 60,000 dollars, with a commitment for a 20 percent increase every five years.

Wurie clarified that the hotel did not seize land belonging to Milton Margai Technical University, but was seeking its agreed-upon plot. He explained that a fence erected by a Special Court, not the hotel or government, might have been mistaken for the boundary. The hotel has been pursuing this land issue for 10 years, seeking the remaining part of their designated space.

The hotel, Hilltop Hotels Group Limited, is a Sierra Leonean company that secured investors, including KG and S, to help with rehabilitation starting in 2012. Operations were impacted by the Ebola outbreak. Seven owners are Sierra Leonean, with two British, one Irish, and one Jamaican.

Why it mattersThe lease dispute directly affects Brookfields hotel shareholders and the Ministry of Tourism and Cultural Affairs.

Sierra Leone targets 500,000 youth jobs by 2030

Planning Minister Barlay reaffirmed the goal at a Freetown summit focused on West African economic development.

Sierra Leone aims to create 500,000 jobs for young people by 2030, according to Planning Minister Kenyeh Barlay. She restated this target on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, during a regional summit in Freetown. The government's Youth Employment Scheme is a key part of this plan to address unemployment.

Barlay, speaking for Vice President Dr Mohamed Juldeh Jalloh, stated that youth employment is central to the Medium-Term National Development Plan 2024–2030. She noted that 15,000 young people gained short-term work in 2024 through the National Commission for Social Action, rehabilitating community facilities. The minister acknowledged challenges like low pay and limited access to finance.

The Free Quality School Education programme has increased school enrollment by over one million children since 2019. The Feed Salone initiative also aims to create jobs in agriculture and fisheries. Ngone Diop of the Economic Commission for Africa highlighted that 91.6 per cent of West African employment was informal in 2025.

Why it mattersYoung people in Sierra Leone are directly affected by the government's commitment to creating jobs and developing skills.
