Health · 2026-10-24

sierra leone journalists trained on Ebola preparedness

Health officials and media representatives met in Freetown to improve reporting during public health emergencies.

Journalists in Sierra Leone received training on emergency preparedness and responsible reporting on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, at the National Public Health Agency (NPHA) headquarters in Freetown. The NPHA, working with the Independent Media Commission (IMC), conducted the one-day session to enhance accurate reporting and cooperation during health crises.

NPHA Executive Director Professor Foday Sahr stated that Sierra Leone's preparedness is precautionary, with no current Ebola outbreak, following developments in the Democratic Republic of Congo and Uganda. He reported that the country's overall Ebola preparedness score rose from 51 percent to 83 percent after an October 8 reassessment, with infection control capacity improving to 82 percent and safe burial preparedness reaching 73 percent.

The training aimed to prevent misinformation and improve media coverage of public health emergencies, with IMC Executive Secretary Khalil Kallon presenting guidelines informed by past outbreaks like COVID-19. Professor Sahr urged journalists to verify information and avoid sensationalism, while Kallon stressed professional ethics and protecting vulnerable individuals' privacy.

Why it mattersJournalists in Sierra Leone were trained to report responsibly during potential public health emergencies like Ebola.

Business · 2026-10-24

Sierra Leone launches media campaign for investment summit

Dr. Kandeh Yumkella kicked off promotion for the West Africa Integration and Investment Summit set for November 17-18.

Sierra Leone is preparing to host the West Africa Integration and Investment Summit (WAIIS) 2026 in Freetown from November 17-18. Special Envoy Dr. Kandeh Kolleh Yumkella launched the summit's media campaign on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, in Aberdeen, Freetown. The summit aims to attract international capital and strengthen cross-border trade across West Africa.

Dr. Yumkella, who also chairs the Presidential Initiative on Climate Change, Renewable Energy and Food Security, stated that WAIIS focuses on practical investment mobilization and project implementation, not pledging. He highlighted the Gento Group's proposed port development at Kent and Banana Island as a major infrastructure investment example. The summit will connect investment-ready projects with financial institutions and strategic partners.

The summit is expected to draw African business leaders, international investors, financial institutions, development partners, and Heads of State. Preparations include four thematic pillars to direct investor attention to priority sectors. A Private Sector Advisory Board of influential African business leaders has also been established.

Why it mattersSierra Leone is preparing to host a major regional investment gathering to attract international capital and strengthen cross-border trade.

Climate · 2026-10-24

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency warns of economic tree destruction and greenhouse gas emissions from burning wood for fuel.

65-year-old Pa Alimamy has burned charcoal for over two decades in Sierra Leone, a trade he says sustains his family and many others across the country. The demand for charcoal as a cooking fuel is high because only 36% of the population has electricity and just 15% have access to clean cooking.

The Environmental Protection Agency in Sierra Leone acknowledged the environmental hazards of charcoal production, noting that burners are now cutting down economic trees like orange and mango. Director Paul Lamin stated this practice leaves land treeless, exposed to climate change, and threatens food security.

Charcoal burning emits significant greenhouse gases, contributing to climate change and air pollution, according to experts. The Food and Agricultural Organization estimates traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions.

Why it mattersCharcoal burning in Sierra Leone leads to deforestation, habitat loss, and greenhouse gas emissions, impacting the environment and threatening food security.

Politics · 2026-10-24

Sierra Leone vice presidents lack succession path

No vice president has ever been elected president in Sierra Leone, raising questions about the current officeholder's future.

Sierra Leone's Vice Presidency, constitutionally powerful, has historically served as a political cul-de-sac rather than a route to the presidency. Since independence, vice presidents have often been appointed to balance regional or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, or neutralize rivals, rather than to cultivate ambition for higher office. This has meant most vice presidents lack a clear succession pathway and an independent political base.

Post-independence history shows vice presidents like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah were powerful but not groomed for succession, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup attempt. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared, lost the 2007 presidential election, demonstrating that competence alone was insufficient. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and politically constrained, while Samuel Sam-Sumana's experience highlights the insecurity of proximity to power without presidential backing.

The current Vice President faces a sharp question: has the historical pattern already sealed their fate? The office's design and past outcomes suggest a challenging path for any vice president seeking to transition to the presidency, with electorates often opting for perceived change over continuity.

Why it mattersThe office of Vice President in Sierra Leone has historically not led to the presidency, impacting the career trajectory of those who hold it.

News · 2026-10-24

Female artisanal miners report sexual violence in Kono district

Women working in diamond and gold pits in Jaiama Nimikor describe a pattern of rape and harassment while seeking income in abandoned mining sites.

Female artisanal miners in the diamond-rich district of Kono, Sierra Leone, are reporting frequent incidents of rape and sexual harassment while working in abandoned pits. Janet, a 32-year-old miner, stated she was raped at age 15 while scavenging for gold and diamonds in Jaiama Nimikor, a site located 8 miles from Koidu. She reported the assault to her village head, but the perpetrator remained unidentified.

Another miner, 48-year-old Haja, said she was raped by a man in her village after starting gold mining to support her three children. Haja reported the incident to a camp master, who dismissed her claim by suggesting the attacker was her boyfriend. She subsequently fled to the village of Guitor due to the stigma and shame associated with the assault before eventually returning to her home.

These women turned to artisanal mining as a primary livelihood following the end of the civil war. Many miners report that their attempts to seek justice through local authorities have been ineffective. Janet and Haja continue to work in the sector to provide for their families despite the ongoing risks of violence.

Why it mattersWomen working in artisanal mining sites in Kono district face physical violence and sexual assault while attempting to earn a living.
