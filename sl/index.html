Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency warns of economic tree loss and greenhouse gas emissions from cooking fuel.

Charcoal burning is a common livelihood in Sierra Leone, supporting families like that of 65-year-old Pa Alimamy who has worked in the trade for over two decades. The practice is widespread in Freetown and other towns due to limited access to electricity and clean cooking alternatives, with only 36% of the population having electricity and 15% clean cooking access according to a government energy report.

The Environmental Protection Agency acknowledges the severe environmental hazards, noting that burners are now cutting down economic trees like mango and orange. Paul Lamin, Director of Natural Resource Governance, stated that this deforestation leaves land exposed to climate change effects and threatens food security, while the slow regrowth of trees exacerbates carbon space issues.

Greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production and use contribute significantly to global emissions, with traditional wood energy accounting for 2-7% of anthropogenic GHG emissions annually. Experts warn that continued unsustainable charcoal practices will worsen climate change, potentially reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's environment and food security are threatened by charcoal production, which also contributes to global greenhouse gas emissions.

Female artisanal miners report sexual violence in Kono district

Women working in abandoned diamond and gold pits in Jaiama Nimikor describe a pattern of rape and harassment while seeking income.

Women working in the diamond-rich district of Kono in Sierra Leone report frequent sexual violence while scavenging for gold and diamonds in abandoned pits. Janet, a 32-year-old miner, said she was raped at age 15 while working alone in Jaiama Nimikor, a site located 8 miles from Koidu. She reported the incident to her village head at the time, but the perpetrator remained unidentified.

Another miner, 48-year-old Haja, said she was raped by a man in her village while working to support her three children. Haja stated that when she reported the assault, the camp master dismissed her claim, suggesting the attacker was her boyfriend. The stigma and lack of official response forced Haja to temporarily flee her community to the village of Guitor.

These women turned to artisanal mining as a primary source of income following the civil war. Janet, who dropped out of school due to poverty, continues to work in the mines to support the child born from her assault. Haja eventually returned to her village after her eldest daughter pleaded with her, noting that the man who raped her had left the area.

Why it mattersThe report highlights the physical danger and lack of protection for women working in artisanal mining sites in Kono, where they face sexual assault and social stigma while trying to earn a living.

Sierra Leone vice presidents fail to reach presidency

No vice president has ever been elected president in Sierra Leone since independence.

The office of Vice President in Sierra Leone has historically been a political dead end, failing to produce a successor to the presidency since independence. The 1991 Constitution envisions a powerful role ensuring continuity, but governments have used the position to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, rather than cultivate future leaders. This often leaves vice presidents without a clear succession path or an independent political base.

Past vice presidents illustrate this pattern: Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were insiders but not groomed for succession, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, a seasoned vice president during the Kabbah era, lost the 2007 presidential election decisively, demonstrating that competence was not enough for voters seeking change. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and constrained, and Samuel Sam-Sumana's experience showed how proximity to power offered no political security after estrangement from the president.

The current Vice President's fate may already be sealed by this historical precedent, raising questions about whether the office is a curse or a blessing. The electorate's preference for perceived change over experience, as seen in Berewa's defeat, suggests that the vice presidency can be a political liability. This recurring national debate highlights the office's failure as a leadership incubator.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's vice presidents have consistently failed to ascend to the presidency, indicating a potential political disadvantage for the current officeholder.

Politics · 2026-11-12

Former minister Samura Kamara releases memoir on Sierra Leone's democracy

The former presidential candidate's book covers four decades of public service and the disputed 2018 and 2023 elections.

Dr. Samura Matthew Wilson Kamara, a former Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation and two-time presidential candidate of the All People's Congress (APC), has released a memoir titled "My Circumstances of Life: Service, Betrayal and the Struggle for Sierra Leone's Democracy". The book, now available on Amazon in Kindle, paperback and hardcover, traces his career from his birth in 1951 in Kamalo village to senior posts including Financial Secretary, Governor of the Bank of Sierra Leone and Minister of Finance.

The memoir covers the civil war, post-war reconstruction, economic recovery and the Ebola outbreak. A large part concerns the 2018 and 2023 presidential elections, which the APC challenged after the official results were announced. Kamara recounts a post-election meeting with President Julius Maada Bio, where, he says, discussions included detained individuals and people who died during the unrest.

The book also examines the Agreement for National Unity, which came out of political dialogue between the government and the APC after the 2023 elections.

Why it mattersReaders can see Kamara's own account of the 2018 and 2023 elections and of the talks that followed the 2023 vote, including the detentions and deaths discussed with the president.

Sierra Leone seeks first AFCON qualifier points against DR Congo

The Leone Stars, still without a point in Group E, face DR Congo in Kinshasa in their third 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifier on Thursday, November 12.

Sierra Leone's Leone Stars play the Democratic Republic of Congo on Thursday, November 12, 2026, at the Stade des Martyrs in Kinshasa, in Matchday 3 of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers. Kick-off is at 4:00 PM Sierra Leone time. Sierra Leone is bottom of Group E after losing its opening two matches.

The Leone Stars lost 3-2 to Zimbabwe on September 24, after leading 2-0 through Alie Conteh and Sallieu Tarawallie, and then lost 1-0 to Equatorial Guinea four days later. DR Congo has won both of its opening fixtures, beating Equatorial Guinea 2-0 and Zimbabwe 2-1, and leads the group with six points. The Confederation of African Football has authorised up to 18,000 spectators at the venue.

Coach Didier Gomes Da Rosa has named a 25-man squad that includes captain Osman Kakay and defender Abdulai Juma Bah. The two teams meet again on Tuesday, November 17, in Morocco, where Sierra Leone will play its designated home fixture because its national stadium is not approved to host international matches.

Why it mattersThe Leone Stars must win points in these two November matches to keep their hopes of reaching the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations alive, having lost their opening two Group E games.
