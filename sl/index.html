Female artisanal miners report systemic sexual violence in Kono district

Women working in abandoned diamond and gold pits in Jaiama Nimikor describe a pattern of rape and harassment with little recourse from local authorities.

Women working in the artisanal mining sector in Sierra Leone’s Kono district are facing widespread sexual violence while scavenging for gold and diamonds. Janet, a 32-year-old miner, reported being raped at age 15 while working alone in the pits of Jaiama Nimikor, a town located 8 miles from Koidu. She stated that she reported the assault to her village head at the time, but the perpetrator was never identified.

The violence has left lasting trauma and social stigma for survivors. Haja, a 48-year-old widow, said she fled her home to the village of Guitor after being raped while working in the mines. She claimed that when she reported the incident, the camp master dismissed her complaint by suggesting the attacker was her boyfriend. Haja stated that the man who assaulted her eventually left the village voluntarily after she returned at her daughter's request.

Many women turned to the abandoned mines for income following the civil war, often dropping out of school to support their families. These miners continue to face risks of physical assault and harassment while working in isolated areas. Survivors report that local community leaders and camp officials frequently fail to take action against the perpetrators of these crimes.

Why it mattersWomen working in the artisanal mining sector face ongoing risks of sexual violence and lack of legal recourse from local camp authorities.

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

The practice of burning wood for charcoal is depleting forests and threatening food security, according to environmental officials.

Pa Alimamy, 65, has burned charcoal for over two decades in Sierra Leone, a trade that sustains his family and many others across the country, especially in Freetown. The demand for charcoal as a cooking fuel is high because only 36% of the population has electricity and 15% have access to clean cooking, according to the government's National Energy Compact.

The Director of Natural Resource Governance at the Environmental Protection Agency acknowledged the environmental damage, noting that burners are cutting down fruit trees like mangoes and oranges. This deforestation leaves land exposed to climate change and reduces future wood supplies, as trees take a long time to regrow and help absorb carbon dioxide.

Charcoal production and use emit significant greenhouse gases, contributing to climate change and air pollution. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates that traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for a third of these emissions.

Why it mattersEnvironmental officials warn that charcoal burning is causing deforestation and threatening food security by destroying fruit trees, leaving land exposed to climate change.

Sierra Leone vice presidents face succession challenges

The office has historically served as a political cul-de-sac, with no vice president ever elected president.

In Sierra Leone, the office of Vice President, constitutionally powerful, has historically been a political dead end, with no holder ever elected president. Since independence, vice presidents have often been appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, rather than to cultivate ambition for higher office. This practice has frequently left vice presidents without a clear succession pathway or an independent political base.

Past vice presidents illustrate this pattern: Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were insiders but not successors, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared, lost the 2007 presidential election despite his experience. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and politically constrained, and Samuel Sam-Sumana was expelled from his party after falling out with the president.

The current Vice President faces a similar challenge, as the office's history suggests it offers proximity to power but not necessarily a path to the presidency. The 1991 Constitution intended the role to ensure continuity and executive depth, but governments have largely used it as a political balancing mechanism, a trend that has persisted and may already have sealed the fate of the current officeholder regarding presidential succession.

Why it mattersThe historical pattern of vice presidents in Sierra Leone failing to attain the presidency affects political ambition and succession planning within the country's highest offices.

Politics · 2026-11-16

Finance minister's defenders say calls for his sacking are misplaced

A Politico SL guest column argues that a civil society group's demand to remove Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura confuses global shocks with local mismanagement.

The "CSOs Consortium on Public Accountability" has called for the immediate dismissal of Sierra Leone's Finance Minister, Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura. Writing in Politico SL on Monday, 16 November 2026, guest writer Foday M. Daboh calls the demand flawed, arguing it confuses economic hardship with economic mismanagement.

Daboh says the 2020 to 2024 period brought overlapping shocks, including COVID-19 supply-chain disruptions, Russia-Ukraine war-driven food and fuel inflation, and sharp US dollar appreciation. He says Ghana defaulted on external debt in 2022, while Sierra Leone did not default, kept IMF programme engagement and preserved access to concessional financing.

He cites Bank of Sierra Leone monetary policy statements showing inflation peaked after the food and fuel shocks and then declined as fiscal and monetary tightening took hold. He also quotes an IMF country report saying improved fiscal discipline and reduced central bank financing helped moderate inflationary pressures.

Why it mattersTraders and households face a price environment the writer says depends on imports for over 70 percent of consumption, so inflation can be managed but not eliminated.

Business · 2026-11-16

Freetown dialogue pushes Chinese investment in Sierra Leone industry

Over 100 officials, company representatives and regional bodies met on November 13 to discuss cooperation in mining, infrastructure, energy, agriculture and fisheries.

The China-West Africa Investment and Industrialization Dialogue was held in Freetown on November 13. Zhang Dianbin, the Chinese embassy's chargé d'affaires ad interim, and Wang Peng, its counsellor for commercial and economic cooperation, attended, along with Sierra Leone's trade, mines, fisheries and agriculture officials and the head of the National Investment Commission.

The dialogue brought together over 100 participants, including Sierra Leonean government departments, Chinese and Sierra Leonean enterprises, and regional bodies such as the ECOWAS Economic Commission and the Mano River Union, plus the China-Africa Development Fund. The Chinese delegation said it would act in a market-based and law-based manner and focus on industrial empowerment, while African representatives committed to a fair, transparent and stable business environment.

The event was the first high-level meeting of the West Africa Integration and Investment Summit, and it aims to build a government-business platform and firm up pipeline cooperation projects for that summit.

Why it mattersChinese and Sierra Leonean companies now have a government-business platform to pursue projects in mining, energy and agriculture ahead of the West Africa Integration and Investment Summit.
