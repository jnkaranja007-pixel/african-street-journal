Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Burning wood for charcoal is destroying economic trees and threatening food security, officials say.

Pa Alimamy, 65, has burned charcoal for over two decades in Sierra Leone, a trade that supports his family. Charcoal use is widespread in Freetown and other towns due to limited access to electricity and clean cooking options. The government's National Energy Compact states only 36% of the population has electricity and just 15% have clean cooking access.

The Environmental Protection Agency acknowledges the environmental damage caused by charcoal production. Director Paul Lamin stated that burners are cutting down economic trees like mango and orange, leaving land exposed to climate change and threatening food security. He noted that trees take a long time to regrow and are vital for reducing carbon emissions.

Greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production and use harm human health and the environment. A Food and Agricultural Organization report estimates traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa contributing one-third of these emissions. Continued unsuitable production risks exacerbating climate change and reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersDeforestation from charcoal burning threatens food security and exposes land to climate change effects, impacting the livelihoods of people dependent on agriculture.

Sierra Leone vice presidents lack succession path

The office has historically been used to balance political factions rather than cultivate future leaders.

The Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone has consistently failed to produce a successor to the presidency, raising questions about its true value. Since independence, vice presidents have been appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, rather than to cultivate ambition for higher office.

This pattern has left vice presidents without clear succession pathways or independent political bases, often making the office a political cul-de-sac. For example, Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared, lost the 2007 presidential election, demonstrating that competence in the role did not guarantee electoral success.

Samuel Sam-Sumana's experience as an elected vice president who was later estranged, expelled from his party, and politically orphaned further illustrates the precariousness of the office. The current vice president's fate is now being questioned against this historical backdrop.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's vice presidents have historically been appointed for political balancing rather than groomed for presidential succession, leaving them vulnerable.

Air Sierra Leone fails to restart London flights

The airline promised a late October return but has not resumed operations or provided verifiable evidence of flights.

Air Sierra Leone has not returned to commercial flight operations as promised for the end of October. The airline had announced in August that ticket sales were open for its services between Sierra Leone, The Gambia, and the United Kingdom. However, by November 9, there was no independently verifiable evidence that flights had resumed.

The airline suspended operations on April 30, 2026, after its technical partner Ascend Airways ceased operations. This left Air Sierra Leone without a Boeing 737-8 aircraft chartered from Ascend Airways. The airline stated it was discussing alternative aircraft arrangements and needed time for new aircraft registration.

In August, Air Sierra Leone announced it had secured regulatory approvals from the UK Civil Aviation Authority for its London routes. Reports in September indicated discussions with Corendon Dutch Airlines for a Boeing 737-9 aircraft, but the arrangement was described as under discussion, not finalized.

Why it mattersSierra Leone residents and businesses relying on Air Sierra Leone for travel to the UK face continued disruption.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Sierra Leone minister's actions defended amid calls for sacking

A "CSOs Consortium on Public Accountability" statement calling for the minister's dismissal is called 'policy vandalism'.

A group identifying as the "CSOs Consortium on Public Accountability" has demanded the removal of Sierra Leone’s Minister of Finance, Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura. This call is characterized as a flawed intervention that conflates economic hardship with mismanagement and populist frustration with policy failure. The author contends that while civil society is vital, its advocacy must be grounded in analytical rigor and empirical evidence.

The report attributes Sierra Leone's economic stability between 2020 and 2024 to disciplined macro-fiscal management under Minister Bangura, contrasting the nation's performance with global shocks like COVID-19 and the Russia-Ukraine war. Data from the Bank of Sierra Leone and IMF Country Reports indicate that inflation, driven by import reliance, has been managed through coordinated fiscal and monetary tightening.

Concerning debt, the analysis states Sierra Leone faces a high risk of distress, but its debt is largely concessional with limited commercial borrowing. Reforms have enhanced reporting, and there are no Eurobonds or hidden debts. The minister is credited with resisting pressure for unsustainable borrowing to fund subsidies and wage increases without revenue backing.

Why it mattersThe finance minister's economic management is defended against calls for his dismissal, impacting citizens through inflation and debt management.

Business · 2026-11-09

First Lady attends West African bankers' awards

Dr. Fatima Maada Bio highlighted the banking sector's role in economic growth and development.

First Lady Dr. Fatima Maada Bio attended the 2nd West African Annual Bankers’ Day Celebration and Awards Ceremony in Freetown on Saturday, November 7, 2026. The event, held at the Freetown International Conference Centre, Bintumani, gathered financial experts and policymakers from across the region.

Dr. Bio described the gathering as a recognition of dedication and innovation within the financial industry. She emphasized the banking sector's importance for national and regional development, including supporting businesses and encouraging investment.

The ceremony celebrated excellence and professionalism in West Africa's banking sector. Dr. Bio expressed optimism for the sector's continued growth and embrace of innovation.

Why it mattersThe First Lady of Sierra Leone attended an event celebrating the West African banking sector's contribution to economic growth.
