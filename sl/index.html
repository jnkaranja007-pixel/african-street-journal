Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental agency notes economic trees are cut, threatening food security and increasing climate change effects.

Pa Alimamy, 65, has burned charcoal for over two decades in Sierra Leone, a trade sustaining his family. Charcoal use is widespread in Freetown and regional towns due to limited access to electricity and clean cooking, with only 36% of the population having electricity and 15% clean cooking access.

The Environmental Protection Agency acknowledges the practice's hazards, noting that burners now cut economic trees like mango and orange. Director Paul Lamin stated that deforestation from charcoal burning is severe, as trees take long to regrow and are vital for reducing carbon space.

Greenhouse gas emissions from charcoal production and use contribute to climate change, impacting forest health and future wood supplies. The Food and Agricultural Organization estimates traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa accounting for one-third of these emissions.

Why it mattersDeforestation and the cutting of economic trees by charcoal burners directly affect the environment and food security for people in Sierra Leone.

Sierra Leone vice presidents lack succession path

No vice president has ever been elected president, raising questions about the current officeholder's future.

The office of Vice President in Sierra Leone has historically failed to serve as a pathway to the presidency. Since independence, vice presidents have been appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, rather than to cultivate ambition for higher office.

This pattern means most vice presidents enter office without a clear succession plan or an independent political base. For example, Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared, lost the 2007 presidential election, demonstrating that competence in the vice presidency did not guarantee electoral success.

Samuel Sam-Sumana's tenure also illustrates the precariousness of the position; he was elected vice president but later became estranged from the president, expelled from his party, and left politically isolated.

Why it mattersThe vice presidency in Sierra Leone has not led to the presidency, impacting the political careers of those who hold the office.

Sierra Leone launches US$4.66M waste project

The five-year initiative funded by the Global Environment Facility aims to reduce pollution in Freetown.

A US$4.66 million project funded by the Global Environment Facility (GEF) was launched on 2 November 2026 to transform Freetown’s waste management system.

The initiative, which includes the Environment Protection Agency Sierra Leone and the Freetown City Council, aims to minimise pollution over five years. Dr Abu-Bakarr Massaquoi, Executive Chairman of the Environment Protection Agency, stated that waste should be viewed as a valuable resource.

Freetown is the only city from a least developed country among five participating cities globally in this project, which aligns with the government's Sustainable Waste Management Act and aims to advance Sierra Leone’s circular economy.

Why it mattersThe project aims to improve Freetown's waste management system and minimise pollution, directly affecting residents.

Commonwealth leaders seek partnerships to unlock prosperity

The 2026 CHOGM in Antigua and Barbuda aims to foster collaboration between governments and businesses.

The 2026 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM) opened in Antigua and Barbuda on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, with leaders seeking bold partnerships between governments and businesses to unlock prosperity for the Commonwealth’s 2.7 billion people. His Majesty King Charles III joined leaders in St. John’s for the official Opening Ceremony, which celebrated Antiguan and Barbudan culture. The summit, themed ‘Accelerating Partnerships and Investment for a Prosperous Commonwealth’, is the first under Commonwealth Secretary-General Hon Shirley Botchwey and will include direct engagement with business leaders and investors.

Leaders will focus on mobilising partnerships and investment in scalable projects addressing member country needs, including skills-building, climate finance, and green energy. King Charles III noted the global strain on trust between nations, the impact of conflict, rapid technological change, and the catastrophic reality of climate change. He highlighted the Commonwealth as an opportunity to draw on collective ingenuity to address these challenges.

Secretary-General Botchwey stated the world needs the Commonwealth spirit of finding solutions and remade CHOGM as a summit of action. She called for a new model of partnership and cooperation, discarding the old division between states and business, to address the scale of global challenges. New initiatives are already underway to mobilise investment, boost intra-Commonwealth trade, upskill young people, and expand renewable energy.

Why it mattersSierra Leone, as a Commonwealth member, is affected by the drive for partnerships and investment in areas like skills-building and green energy.

Business · 2026-11-06

Choithrams legacy highlighted by Forbes Africa

The 82-year-old business has expanded from a single shop to operations in 27 countries.

Forbes Africa's October/November 2026 edition featured Choithrams, a business established in Sierra Leone in 1944, highlighting its contributions over 82 years. The company, which began as a small retail shop in Freetown, now operates five supermarkets, a wholesale outlet, an electrical store, and three warehouses in Sierra Leone.

Harish Agnani, Head of Operations, told Forbes Africa that the company expanded across Sierra Leone before entering other African markets. Choithrams also operates two local manufacturing facilities producing candies, bubble gum, and laundry soap, alongside international operations in Dubai, the UK, and India.

Beyond commerce, Choithrams supports Choithram Memorial Hospital in Freetown, offering subsidized and free treatment to disadvantaged patients. A recent medical intervention by seven German doctors, funded by the company, successfully conducted 44 surgeries without patient charges, saving them between US$5,000 and US$10,000 each.

Why it mattersChoithrams' 82-year legacy in Sierra Leone, from retail to manufacturing and healthcare, is detailed in Forbes Africa.
