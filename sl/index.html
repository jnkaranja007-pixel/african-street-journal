Climate · 2026-11-02

Sierra Leone charcoal burning causes deforestation

Environmental Protection Agency notes economic trees are cut, leaving land exposed to climate change.

Pa Alimamy, 65, has burned charcoal for over two decades in Sierra Leone, a trade that sustains his family. Charcoal use is widespread in Freetown and other towns due to limited access to electricity, with only 36% of the population having power and 15% clean cooking access, according to the National Energy Compact.

The Environmental Protection Agency acknowledges the environmental damage from charcoal production, including deforestation and habitat loss. Director Paul Lamin stated that burners are cutting economic trees like orange and mango, leaving land exposed to climate change and threatening food security. Burning charcoal also emits greenhouse gases harmful to health and the environment.

The Food and Agricultural Organization estimates that traditional wood energy emits 2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, with Sub-Saharan Africa contributing one-third of these emissions. Continued charcoal production and use could worsen climate change, impacting forest health and reducing future wood-energy supplies.

Why it mattersCharcoal burning leads to deforestation and greenhouse gas emissions, exacerbating climate change effects in Sierra Leone.

Education · 2026-11-02

WAEC withholds 78-year-old candidate's exam result

Pa Lamin Turay's 2026 WASSCE result is among 47,454 withheld over suspected malpractice.

The West African Examinations Council (WAEC) has withheld the 2026 West African Senior School Certificate Examination (WASSCE) result of 78-year-old Pa Lamin Turay. Turay, who returned to formal education in 2019 at age 71, sat the exam as a regular candidate. His result is one of 47,454 withheld by WAEC for suspected examination malpractice, representing 23.6 per cent of the 200,756 candidates' submitted results.

Minister of Basic and Senior Secondary Education Conrad Sackey confirmed on 27 October that 47,454 candidates, including 26,735 females and 20,719 males, had results withheld due to suspected irregularities. He stated that withholding a result does not confirm guilt and WAEC will investigate each case. Unverified grades circulating on social media suggest Turay obtained C6 in Christian Religious Studies and Mathematics, B3 in Government, C5 in History, D7 in Literature, E8 in Agricultural Science, and F9 in English Language and Health Science.

The 2026 WASSCE results also highlighted a national challenge in English Language performance, with 63.33 per cent of candidates failing and only 9.77 per cent achieving a credit pass. This has raised concerns about English proficiency for higher education. The University of Makeni (UNIMAK) has introduced a special provisional admission for candidates who did not secure a credit in English Language.

Why it mattersThe withholding of results affects 47,454 candidates, including Pa Lamin Turay, and raises concerns about the integrity of the examination system and English language proficiency for students.

Female artisanal miners report sexual violence in Kono district

Women working in the diamond-rich pits of Jaiama Nimikor describe incidents of rape and harassment while scavenging for gold and diamonds.

Women working in the artisanal mining sector in Jaiama Nimikor, Kono district, report frequent incidents of sexual violence while searching for gold and diamonds. Janet, a 32-year-old miner, stated she was raped at age 15 while scavenging in a pit eight miles from Koidu. She reported the assault to the village head, but the perpetrator remained unidentified.

Haja, a 48-year-old widow, said she was also raped while working in the mines. She stated that the camp master dismissed her complaint, claiming the attacker was her boyfriend, an allegation she denied. Haja fled her community to Guitor due to the stigma and shame, leaving her children behind before eventually returning at her daughter's request.

Many women turned to mining in Kono following the civil war as a primary source of income. Janet, who dropped out of school due to poverty, continues to support the child born from her assault. Haja’s attacker eventually left the village voluntarily after the incident.

Why it mattersThe report highlights the specific dangers faced by women in the artisanal mining sector, including sexual violence, lack of institutional support, and social stigmatization.

Politics · 2026-11-02

African nations face youth protests amid economic woes

Growth held steady at 3.8-4.1% in 2025, but citizens saw deepening political dissatisfaction and escalating conflicts.

In 2025, African nations experienced a year of contrasts, with economic growth averaging 3.8-4.1% due to infrastructure and market reforms. However, this economic resilience was overshadowed by widespread youth-led protests in countries like Kenya, Tanzania, and Morocco, demanding better governance amidst economic hardships and social challenges.

The year saw high-stakes elections that stirred anger rather than trust, leading to violence and crackdowns in Tanzania and unrest in Cameroon. Fragile transitions occurred in Côte d'Ivoire and Gabon, while Guinea-Bissau returned to military rule, raising regional concerns over democratic norms. The African Union's financial independence agenda stalled, and its peace operations faced funding shortages.

Security threats multiplied across the continent, with Sudan's war displacing over 11 million people and armed groups expanding in the Sahel. Eastern DRC suffered intense fighting, and Nigeria faced large-scale violence. New actors like the UAE deepened their influence through arms and port deals, positioning themselves as stabilizing powers amid multipolar shifts.

Why it mattersCitizens across Africa, including in Sierra Leone, faced economic hardships and demanded better governance, leading to protests and unrest.

Politics · 2026-11-02

Sierra Leone vice presidents fail to win presidency

No vice president has ever been elected president in Sierra Leone since independence.

Sierra Leone's vice presidency has historically been a political dead end, not a path to the presidency. Since independence, vice presidents have been appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic factors, appease party factions, or neutralize rivals, rather than to cultivate future leaders. This practice has meant most vice presidents lack a clear succession plan or an independent political base.

Past vice presidents like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were powerful but not groomed for succession, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup attempt. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared during the Kabbah administration, lost the 2007 presidential election decisively. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and politically constrained, and Samuel Sam-Sumana was expelled from his party after falling out with the president.

The current vice president faces a historical pattern where proximity to power has not translated into presidential success. Berewa's 2007 defeat demonstrated that competence alone was insufficient, as voters opted for perceived change. This recurring outcome raises questions about the current vice president's prospects for higher office.

Why it mattersSierra Leonean vice presidents have not been elected president, impacting political ambitions.
