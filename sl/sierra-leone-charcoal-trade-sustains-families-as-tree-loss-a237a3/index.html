Sierra Leone charcoal trade sustains families as tree loss threatens food security

A 65-year-old burner says his family depends on the work, while an environmental agency director warns that producers are cutting orange and mango trees.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Pa Alimamy, a 65-year-old charcoal burner in Sierra Leone, has worked in the trade for more than two decades and says it supports his family. Charcoal is widely used in Freetown and regional headquarters towns, where many households cannot afford gas or electricity for cooking.

Sierra Leone’s National Energy Compact says 36% of the population has access to electricity and clean-cooking access is 15%. Paul Lamin, the Environmental Protection Agency’s director of natural resource governance, said burners are cutting economic trees such as orange and mango, leaving land bare and threatening food security.

The Food and Agriculture Organization estimates traditional wood energy emits 1/2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent per year, or 2-7% of total human-caused greenhouse gas emissions. Sub-Saharan Africa accounts for one third of emissions from wood energy, the report says.

Why it mattersHouseholds without affordable gas or electricity depend on charcoal for cooking, while the National Energy Compact puts clean-cooking access at 15%.
