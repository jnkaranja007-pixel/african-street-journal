Sierra Leone civil society group demands finance minister's dismissal

The Consortium on Public Accountability says hardship reflects poor inflation control and reckless borrowing, which a rebuttal disputes.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

The CSOs Consortium on Public Accountability has called for the immediate dismissal of Sierra Leone's Finance Minister, Sheku Ahmed Fantamadi Bangura. In a response published by Politico SL, writer Foday M. Daboh argues the demand confuses economic hardship with policy failure.

Daboh says Sierra Leone avoided default, stayed engaged with an International Monetary Fund programme and retained access to concessional financing under Bangura. He cites central bank statements and IMF reports as showing inflation declined after fiscal and monetary tightening, and says debt remains at high risk of distress but is mainly concessional, with commercial borrowing restrained.

The consortium's statement, as described by Daboh, alleges poor inflation control and reckless debt accumulation. He says the country has no Eurobond exposure or hidden debt; the response does not include the consortium's full case.

Why it mattersThe consortium must now press its case for removing Bangura, while the minister's record is being defended on inflation and debt management.
