Sierra Leone charcoal burners cut economic trees as demand grows

Environmental Protection Agency official Paul Lamin says the trade leaves land exposed and threatens food security, while 65-year-old Pa Alimamy depends on it for his family’s.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Charcoal production in Sierra Leone is cutting trees as households rely on the fuel for cooking, with 65-year-old burner Pa Alimamy saying the trade has kept him alive for more than two decades. He said his entire family depends on the work for income.

Paul Lamin, the Environmental Protection Agency’s director of natural resource governance, told Politico SL that burners now cut economic trees, including orange and mango, leaving land bare and threatening food security. Lamin said the trees take a long time to grow back, and acknowledged that people have few alternative energy sources.

The government’s National Energy Compact says 36% of the population has access to electricity and clean cooking reaches 15%. The Food and Agriculture Organization estimates traditional wood energy produces 1/2.4 GT of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, or 2-7% of global human-caused greenhouse-gas emissions; sub-Saharan Africa accounts for one third of emissions from wood energy.

Why it mattersPeople who depend on charcoal for cooking and income face scarce alternatives, while Lamin says cutting orange and mango trees threatens food security.
