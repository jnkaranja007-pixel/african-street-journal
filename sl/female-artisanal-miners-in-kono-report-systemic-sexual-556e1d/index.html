Female artisanal miners in Kono report systemic sexual violence

Women working in diamond and gold pits in Sierra Leone face persistent threats of rape and harassment while struggling to earn a daily livelihood.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-10-22 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Female artisanal miners in the Kono district of Sierra Leone are reporting frequent incidents of rape and sexual harassment while working in abandoned mining pits. Women scavenging for diamonds and gold in Jaiama Nimikor describe a dangerous environment where they are vulnerable to assault while attempting to support their families.

Janet, a 32-year-old miner, recounted being raped at age 15 while working alone in a pit. She reported the incident to her village head, but the perpetrator remained unidentified. Another miner, 48-year-old Haja, stated she was raped by a man in her village while working to support her three children. She claimed that when she reported the assault to a camp master, he dismissed her complaint by suggesting the attacker was her boyfriend.

These women turned to artisanal mining as a primary source of income following the civil war or the death of spouses. Many report that their experiences have led to social stigma, pregnancy, and the forced separation from their children. Despite these reports, victims say that local authorities and camp leaders have failed to take meaningful action against the perpetrators.

Why it mattersThe story highlights the physical danger and social stigma faced by women working in the informal mining sector in Kono district.
