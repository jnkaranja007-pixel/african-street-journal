Female artisanal miners in Kono report systemic sexual violence

Women working in abandoned diamond and gold pits in Jaiama Nimikor face persistent threats of rape and harassment while seeking a livelihood.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Women scavenging for minerals in the diamond-rich district of Kono, Sierra Leone, are facing frequent sexual violence while working in abandoned mining pits. Janet, a 32-year-old miner, reported being raped at age 15 while searching for gold and diamonds in Jaiama Nimikor, a site located 8 miles from Koidu. She stated that she became pregnant following the assault and faced community stigmatization, forcing her to give up her child to a relative.

Another miner, 48-year-old Haja, described fleeing her community to Guitor after being raped while working in the mines. Haja said she reported the incident to the camp master, who dismissed her claim by alleging the attacker was her boyfriend. She noted that the perpetrator eventually left the village voluntarily, but she faced significant social shame and was separated from her three children during her displacement.

Many women turned to artisanal mining as a primary income source following the end of the civil war. These miners often work alone in remote pits to support their families after losing guardians or spouses. Despite reporting these incidents to village authorities, the women stated that they received little support or justice for the attacks.

Why it mattersWomen working in artisanal mines in Kono face ongoing sexual violence and lack institutional protection, forcing some to flee their homes.
