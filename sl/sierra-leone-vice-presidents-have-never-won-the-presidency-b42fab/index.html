Sierra Leone vice presidents have never won the presidency

An analyst says the office has repeatedly served as a political dead end, and asks whether the current Vice President's path is already closed.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-11 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Foday M. Daboh, a public policy analyst and political economist, argues that the Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone has rarely led to the top job. He writes that no Vice President has ever been elected President, even though the 1991 Constitution makes the office second only to the presidency.

Daboh says successive governments have used the post to balance regional, religious or ethnic interests, appease party blocs, neutralise rivals and reward loyalty, rather than to groom successors. He cites Solomon Ekuma Berewa, widely seen as the best-prepared Vice President, who lost the 2007 SLPP presidential race to Ernest Bai Koroma.

Daboh also points to Victor Bockarie Foh, who served briefly after Samuel Sam-Sumana was removed, and to Sam-Sumana himself, who was later expelled from the party. He asks whether the pattern has already sealed the fate of the current Vice President, a question the article raises but does not answer.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's Vice President and the parties around the office are directly affected, as the analysis argues the post has blocked succession to the presidency.
