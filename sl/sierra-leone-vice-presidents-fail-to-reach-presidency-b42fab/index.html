Sierra Leone vice presidents fail to reach presidency

No vice president has ever been elected president in Sierra Leone since independence.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-12 · 2 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The office of Vice President in Sierra Leone has historically been a political dead end, failing to produce a successor to the presidency since independence. The 1991 Constitution envisions a powerful role ensuring continuity, but governments have used the position to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, rather than cultivate future leaders. This often leaves vice presidents without a clear succession path or an independent political base.

Past vice presidents illustrate this pattern: Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were insiders but not groomed for succession, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, a seasoned vice president during the Kabbah era, lost the 2007 presidential election decisively, demonstrating that competence was not enough for voters seeking change. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and constrained, and Samuel Sam-Sumana's experience showed how proximity to power offered no political security after estrangement from the president.

The current Vice President's fate may already be sealed by this historical precedent, raising questions about whether the office is a curse or a blessing. The electorate's preference for perceived change over experience, as seen in Berewa's defeat, suggests that the vice presidency can be a political liability. This recurring national debate highlights the office's failure as a leadership incubator.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's vice presidents have consistently failed to ascend to the presidency, indicating a potential political disadvantage for the current officeholder.
