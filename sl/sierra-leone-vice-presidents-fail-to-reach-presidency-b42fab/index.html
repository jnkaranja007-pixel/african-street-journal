Sierra Leone vice presidents fail to reach presidency

The constitution grants power, but political appointments sideline vice presidents from leadership.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-01 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

In Sierra Leone, the Vice Presidency, constitutionally powerful, has historically served as a political cul-de-sac. Since independence, no Vice President has been elected President, raising questions about the current officeholder's fate. Vice Presidents are often appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, rather than cultivate ambition.

This practice means most Vice Presidents enter office without a clear succession pathway or an independent political base. Examples include Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens, and Solomon Ekuma Berewa, who lost the 2007 presidential election despite extensive experience. Victor Bockarie Foh's short tenure and Samuel Sam-Sumana's expulsion from his party also illustrate the office's political precarity.

The 1991 Constitution intended the Vice Presidency to ensure continuity and executive depth. However, successive governments have treated it as a political balancing mechanism. This has led to a pattern where proximity to power does not translate into political security or a viable path to the presidency.

Why it mattersThe Vice Presidency in Sierra Leone has been a political dead-end, with no holder ever elected president, suggesting a pattern that affects the current Vice President.
