Sierra Leone vice presidents lack succession path

The constitution grants power, but political appointments sideline vice presidents from presidential ambition.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-04 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Sierra Leone's Vice Presidency, established by the 1991 Constitution to ensure continuity, has historically served as a political balancing tool rather than a leadership incubator. Vice Presidents are often appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, preventing them from developing independent political bases or clear succession pathways.

Past Vice Presidents like Sorie Ibrahim Koroma and Francis Minah under Siaka Stevens were powerful but not groomed for succession, with Minah facing execution after an alleged coup attempt. Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared during the Kabbah era, lost the 2007 presidential election, demonstrating that competence alone does not guarantee success. Victor Bockarie Foh's tenure was short and constrained, and Samuel Sam-Sumana was expelled from his party after falling out with the President.

The pattern suggests that the office, while constitutionally powerful, has become a political liability for those seeking higher office. This recurring issue prompts debate on whether the Vice Presidency is a curse or a blessing in Sierra Leone's political evolution.

Why it mattersSierra Leone's vice presidents have historically been unable to transition to the presidency due to political appointments and lack of independent bases.
