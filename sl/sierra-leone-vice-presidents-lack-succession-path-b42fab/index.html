Sierra Leone vice presidents lack succession path

No vice president has ever been elected president, raising questions about the current officeholder's future.

Source: Politico SL · 2026-11-06 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The office of Vice President in Sierra Leone has historically failed to serve as a pathway to the presidency. Since independence, vice presidents have been appointed to balance regional, religious, or ethnic considerations, appease party blocs, neutralize rivals, or reward loyalty, rather than to cultivate ambition for higher office.

This pattern means most vice presidents enter office without a clear succession plan or an independent political base. For example, Solomon Ekuma Berewa, considered well-prepared, lost the 2007 presidential election, demonstrating that competence in the vice presidency did not guarantee electoral success.

Samuel Sam-Sumana's tenure also illustrates the precariousness of the position; he was elected vice president but later became estranged from the president, expelled from his party, and left politically isolated.

Why it mattersThe vice presidency in Sierra Leone has not led to the presidency, impacting the political careers of those who hold the office.
