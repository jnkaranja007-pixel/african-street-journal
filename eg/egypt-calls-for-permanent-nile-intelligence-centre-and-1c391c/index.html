Egypt calls for permanent Nile intelligence centre and legal track

Water Minister Hani Sewilam says Ethiopia’s dam operations require notification, data sharing and coordination during droughts and floods.

Source: Egypt Independent · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Egypt is urging a shift in how it manages the Nile dispute with Ethiopia, calling for a permanent centre to assess the basin and the Horn of Africa and a sustained legal and technical effort over damage from unilateral dam operations. The proposals are set out in an Egypt Independent commentary published on 17 November.

The commentary says Water Resources and Irrigation Minister Hani Sewilam has adopted a more direct public position on the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam. It says Egypt accepts Ethiopia’s right to development but rejects unilateral control over a shared watercourse, and says Ethiopia remains bound by duties to notify downstream countries, share data and coordinate during droughts and floods.

A newly announced Egyptian-Sudanese committee is tasked with assessing the effects of unilateral operation and pursuing compensation, the commentary says. It also urges Egypt to engage international banks, sovereign funds and engineering firms before new dams are built, and to develop regional economic projects.
