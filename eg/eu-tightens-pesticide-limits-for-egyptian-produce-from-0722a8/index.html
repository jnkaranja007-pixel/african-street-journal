EU tightens pesticide limits for Egyptian produce from January 2027

The rules follow 91 EU notifications on Egyptian shipments from January to August, 81% of them involving pesticide residues.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Egyptian fresh-produce exporters face stricter European Union rules on pesticide residues, packaging and supply-chain compliance from January 2027 through 2030. The first change takes effect on 29 January 2027, when limits for benomyl, carbendazim and thiophanate-methyl fall to the lowest detectable level.

The EU-funded Agrinfo programme, run by Colead, reported 91 notifications on Egyptian shipments between January and August; 81% concerned pesticide residues. September brought nine more alerts, eight involving fresh produce. A spice manufacturer and exporter told an Egypt-EU agrifood dialogue in Cairo that replacing carbendazim requires field trials to test substitutes’ effects on crop protection, yields and quality.

From 5 May, difenoconazole limits will fall on many crops. Stickers on fruit and vegetables must be industrially compostable from 12 February 2028, and the EU will ban single-use plastic packaging for fresh produce under 1.5 kg from 1 January 2030, subject to member state exemptions.

Why it mattersEgyptian growers must test substitutes for carbendazim before the EU limit changes on 29 January 2027, with exporters also facing border alerts on produce.
