Egypt directs $1.5 billion IMF tranche to central bank reserves

The final review clears a combined $2.3 billion disbursement, including $800 million for green, water-management and renewable-energy reforms.

Source: Egypt Independent · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Egypt's final review under its International Monetary Fund programme has cleared the way for $2.3 billion in financing, with $1.5 billion from the Extended Fund Facility (EFF) set for the Central Bank of Egypt. The bank is to use the money to strengthen foreign reserves, fund strategic commodity imports and meet external obligations coming due.

Economics professor Amr Youssef said the local-currency equivalent of the EFF funds would also help cover the state budget's financing gap and reduce reliance on high-interest domestic borrowing. The separate $800 million Resilience and Sustainability Facility tranche is earmarked for reforms in the green transition, water management and renewable energy.

The eighth and final EFF review completes drawdowns under the current arrangement. Egypt's next programme is a national economic plan for 2027–2030, intended to reduce dependence on external borrowing and attract foreign investment and proceeds from state asset sales.

Why it mattersEgyptian households and state spending gain room, since the budget no longer leans as heavily on expensive domestic borrowing to close its gap.
