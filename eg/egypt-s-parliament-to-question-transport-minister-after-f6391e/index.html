Egypt's parliament to question transport minister after train crash

Seven people died and more than 100 were hurt when two passenger trains collided south of Cairo on Sunday.

Source: Egyptian Streets · 2026-11-11 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Egypt's Parliament moved on Monday, 10 November, to press Transport Minister Kamel El-Wazir for answers after a collision between two passenger trains in Ayyat, Giza Province, about 32 miles south of Cairo. The House of Representatives' Transport and Communications Committee will hold an emergency session on Tuesday, 11 November, and its chairman, Wahid Qorqar, approved the request for the meeting.

The crash happened on Sunday, 8 November, when a train bound for Fayoum ran into the back of a train heading to Aswan that had stopped on the same track, and several carriages derailed. Egyptian National Railways identified the trains as Nos. 134 and 2010. The Health Ministry reported seven dead and 125 injured, and the driver of the Fayoum-bound train was among the dead.

President Abdel-Fattah el-Sisi ordered an urgent inquiry and said anyone found negligent must answer for it. The Public Prosecution is examining the technical causes. Early counts had put the dead at four and the injured at 88, according to Agence France-Presse, before the toll rose over the weekend.

Why it mattersPassengers on Egypt's rail network and the families of the seven people killed face a parliamentary inquiry into the minister's responsibility for the crash.
