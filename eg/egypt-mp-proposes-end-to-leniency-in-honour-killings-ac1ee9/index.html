Egypt MP proposes end to leniency in honour killings

Fatma Adel's draft bill seeks tougher penalties for rape and sexual assault, removing honour as a defence.

Source: Egyptian Streets · 2026-11-05 · 1 min read

Member of Parliament Fatma Adel has introduced a draft bill to amend Egypt’s Penal Code, Law No. 58 of 1937, aiming to end leniency in honour killings and sexual violence cases. The proposed legislation seeks to classify killings of a spouse or former spouse, or someone caught committing adultery with a spouse, as premeditated murder. It would also prevent judges from reducing sentences based on surprise, revenge, family honour, or social circumstances.

Adel, representing the Justice Party, stated the bill addresses the role of law in protecting victims of violence against women. The proposal challenges deeply rooted social attitudes by questioning whether cultural expectations or family reputation should ever justify violence. The draft bill aims to introduce tougher penalties for rape and sexual assault.

The proposed amendment to the Penal Code has reignited debate about violence against women. The legislation seeks to restrict the circumstances in which judges can reduce sentences for serious offences. This move directly impacts the legal framework surrounding crimes of passion and sexual assault within Egypt.

Why it mattersEgyptian women and their families are directly affected by proposed changes to laws concerning honour killings and sexual violence.
