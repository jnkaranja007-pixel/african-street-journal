Egypt pays $38.2 billion to service external debt as reserves hit record

Central bank figures show repayments fell by $500 million from the previous fiscal year, even as the external debt stock climbed to $166.7 billion.

Source: Egypt Independent · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Egypt paid $38.2 billion to service its external debt in the 2025/2026 fiscal year, $500 million less than the $38.7 billion it paid the year before, according to data released by the Central Bank of Egypt. The total comprised $30.4 billion in principal installments and $7.8 billion in interest payments.

The external debt stock nevertheless rose 3.3% to $166.7 billion by the end of June 2026, equal to 38.2% of gross domestic product. The central bank attributed the increase to roughly $6.4 billion in net new external borrowing and about $900 million from exchange-rate movements in non-dollar currencies against the US dollar.

Net international reserves climbed to an all-time high of $57.5 billion in October, up $152 million from September's $57.35 billion. The bank said lower interest obligations drove the annual saving in debt-service costs.

Why it mattersEgypt's government must keep meeting $30.4 billion in principal installments and $7.8 billion in interest each year, while its debt stock grows.
