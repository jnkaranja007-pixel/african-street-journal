Egypt Standardizes Entry Visa Fees at $15 for 2027

A new decree establishes a uniform $15 administrative fee for entry and transit visas, alongside other consular services, effective January 1, 2027.

Egypt will implement a standardized $15 administrative fee for entry visas, transit visas, and select consular services provided by its diplomatic missions abroad starting January 1, 2027. This measure, detailed in Prime Minister Decree No. 2553 of 2026 and published in the Official Gazette, aims to unify consular service costs globally. The $15 surcharge will be added to existing base visa charges, meaning the final cost for travelers will depend on the specific visa category.

This move follows a previous adjustment in March 2026, which increased the visa-on-arrival fee for foreign tourists from $25 to $30. Egypt continues to utilize its digital entry system, issuing electronic visas with QR codes for automated border verification. Currently, citizens from 118 nations are eligible for visas on arrival as part of efforts to boost international tourism and simplify arrival processes.

Officials stated that the new standardized fee structure is a significant step toward streamlining consular procedures across all Egyptian missions worldwide before the 2027 implementation. The decree repeals all previous provisions that conflict with this new fee structure, ensuring a consistent application of the administrative charge.

Why it mattersTravelers seeking entry into Egypt will face a new, standardized administrative fee of $15 on top of base visa costs starting in 2027.

Egypt, Saudi Arabia Urge Regional De-escalation to Protect Energy Security

President Al-Sisi and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman spoke by phone Thursday, warning of tension's impact on supply chains and global trade.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah Al-Sisi and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman stressed the necessity of reducing current regional escalation to preserve Middle East stability. The leaders warned of the tension’s impact on energy security, supply chains, and global trade during a phone call on Thursday.

Presidential spokesman Mohamed El-Shenawy stated that Al-Sisi emphasized a "shared destiny" between the two nations, declaring that the security of Saudi Arabia and other Arab states is an extension of Egyptian national security. Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman praised the close coordination between Cairo and Riyadh aimed at achieving peace and stability in the Middle East.

The leaders also discussed intensifying bilateral coordination across various fields, including high-level visits, and advancing cooperation to meet developmental aspirations and improve regional conditions. Al-Sisi extended congratulations ahead of the upcoming Saudi National Day, wishing the kingdom continued stability and prosperity.

Why it mattersRegional de-escalation is urged to protect energy security, supply chains, and global trade, affecting international commerce and stability.

Sisi Declares Nile Water a National Security Matter

President Sisi stated Egypt has the right to defend its water, food, and developmental security along the Nile River.

President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi reiterated that Egypt views the Nile River as a critical component of its national security. During discussions with Italian Deputy Prime Minister Antonio Tajani in Cairo, Sisi emphasized Cairo’s legitimate right to safeguard its water, food, and developmental security. He firmly rejected any unilateral actions within the Nile basin and called for adherence to international law, particularly the principle that prohibits harm to downstream nations.

The meeting also addressed the evolving situation in the Horn of Africa, with both officials agreeing to enhance bilateral coordination to promote regional stability. They stressed the importance of respecting the sovereignty, unity, and territorial integrity of all states in the region. Sisi also highlighted the vital role of the Red Sea in Egyptian national defense, asserting that its security and freedom of navigation fall under the jurisdiction of its coastal states.

Sisi warned against any actions that could destabilize the Red Sea or lead to its militarization and internationalization. The discussions underscored Egypt's commitment to regional stability and its firm stance on protecting its vital water resources and strategic maritime routes.

Why it mattersEgypt views the Nile River as a non-negotiable component of its national security, and the President reiterated Cairo’s right to safeguard its water, food, and developmental security.

Russia's Su-57E Fighter Jet Debuts New Weapons at Egypt Airshow

Rostec will showcase its export-grade Su-57E stealth fighter with an unrevealed armaments package and perform live aerial demonstrations.

Russia's defense sector is set to unveil its export-grade Su-57E fifth-generation stealth fighter at the Egypt International Airshow 2026. State giant Rostec will premiere the aircraft, featuring a previously unseen armaments package, at the event scheduled for September 8–10 in El Alamein. Beyond static displays of its newly integrated weapons systems, which have not been publicly exhibited in Russia or elsewhere, the fighter jet is slated for live aerial demonstrations during the expo.

In conjunction with the fighter jet's debut, Rostec subsidiary High Precision Systems will conduct the first foreign demonstration of its "Bison" anti-drone system. This system is engineered to intercept and neutralize low-altitude aerial threats and unmanned vehicles. The "Bison" system will be presented as a key component of Moscow's defensive systems export portfolio.

The Egypt International Airshow serves as a significant platform for Russia's defense industry to showcase its latest technological advancements to international buyers. The presence of the Su-57E with its new weaponry and the "Bison" anti-drone system highlights Russia's efforts to expand its global defense market share.

Why it mattersThe debut of advanced Russian military hardware at the Egypt International Airshow is relevant to investors interested in defense technology and international arms trade.
