Egypt sets private-sector minimum wage at EGP 8,500 from 2027

The National Wages Council raised the floor from EGP 7,000, a rise of about 21.4 percent that takes effect on 1 January 2027.

Egypt's National Wages Council approved on 15 November a minimum monthly wage of EGP 8,500 for private-sector employees, up from EGP 7,000, effective 1 January 2027. The EGP 1,500 increase is about 21.4 percent. The council also set a minimum net hourly wage of EGP 34 for workers in temporary and part-time jobs.

The decision followed a meeting chaired by Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly, who stressed that the government will ensure private-sector businesses comply with minimum wage requirements. He noted that the sector employs the largest share of Egypt's workforce and said the government is working to improve incomes in line with directives from President Abdel-Fattah El-Sisi.

Private-sector employees will also get an annual periodic increase from the 2027 financial year, set by each establishment's accounting year. It must be at least three percent of the employee's social insurance contribution wage, with a minimum rise of EGP 350 a year.

Why it mattersPrivate-sector staff on the old floor will see their pay lifted to EGP 8,500 a month, and employers must add an annual rise of at least EGP 350.

Egypt captures 60% of Europe fertilizer market

Cairo's natural gas exports doubled fertilizer prices, bringing hard currency amid rising domestic costs for farmers.

Egypt supplied over 60 percent of fertilizer to European markets between March and September, according to Industry Minister Khaled Hashem on Saturday, November 14. The country leveraged a regional crisis into a significant influx of hard currency, despite increased production costs for its own farmers. Hashem stated that the government increased the minimum price of natural gas for factories but avoided rationing, unlike other nations.

Fertilizer export prices nearly doubled during this period, rising from approximately USD 400-450 a ton to nearly USD 900 a ton. The minister also announced the launch of Egypt’s first Industrial Investment Fund, designed to redirect investment from real estate and gold into manufacturing. A new Manufacturer Support Unit will assess struggling factories, with estimates of such plants ranging from 7,400 to 25,000.

The war between the United States and Iran, which began in late February, disrupted shipping and contributed to the market conditions. Hashem made these remarks during an interview on MBC Masr's talk show “Al-Hekaya” with Amr Adib.

Why it mattersEgyptian fertilizer producers are now supplying over 60 percent of Europe's market, with export prices doubling since March.

Egypt puts first two new air-conditioned trains into service on Metro Line 1

The trains are the first deliveries under a EUR 876 million contract with France's Alstom for 55 trains on Cairo's oldest metro line.

Transport Minister Kamel al-Wazir oversaw the entry into service of the first two new air-conditioned trains on Cairo Metro Line 1 on Sunday, 15 November. The trains are the start of deliveries under a contract with the French company Alstom to supply 55 trains for the Helwan–New El-Marg line.

Al-Wazir rode one of the new trains from Saray al-Qubba station to Sadat station, passing eight intermediate stations, and inspected its technical features. French Ambassador Éric Chevallier and National Authority for Tunnels chairman Tarek Goweili attended the ceremony. The trains include surveillance cameras in the driver's cab and carriages, screens showing the next stop, and spaces for wheelchairs.

The contract, signed in 2021, also covers eight years of maintenance, overhauls and spare parts. The line carries about 2.5 million passengers a day, and the new fleet was built at Alstom's plant in Valenciennes, France.

Why it mattersPassengers on the line, which carries about 2.5 million people a day, will get air-conditioned carriages and wheelchair spaces as more of the 55 trains arrive.

DiDi says electric vehicles will come to Egypt's ride-hailing market

The company's international chief says the pace of the shift depends on charging infrastructure, investment and what drivers can afford.

Stephen Zhu, chief executive of DiDi's International Business Group, told Daily News Egypt that the shift to electric vehicles in Egypt's ride-hailing sector is a matter of time rather than a question of whether it will happen. He said the pace will depend on infrastructure readiness, investment and affordability for drivers and passengers.

Zhu said the transition needs partnerships and a supportive ecosystem to be commercially viable, and warned against a rapid rollout that could add financial or operational pressure on drivers and business partners. DiDi plans to work with vehicle manufacturers on suitable electric models, with fleet operators and vehicle owners to bring them into its services, and with local stakeholders on charging and electricity supply.

Zhu put the savings from electric vehicles at 30% to 40% lower daily operating costs than petrol-powered cars, depending on operating conditions. He said vehicle standards must reflect each market, noting that requirements workable where newer, costlier cars are common may be hard to meet where drivers and passengers are more sensitive to cost.

Why it mattersDrivers who switch to electric cars would see daily operating costs fall by 30% to 40%, according to DiDi's chief executive, while those who must replace vehicles early would carry the cost.

Egypt, Germany and EU forum expands youth opportunities

A Cairo forum marked five years of cooperation aimed at boosting young Egyptians' socio-economic participation.

Cairo hosted the Socio-Economic Collaboration Forum (SECF) on Monday at the Hilton Grand Nile, convened by the German development agency GIZ Egypt for the German Government with the Ministry of Social Solidarity. More than 150 representatives from government, civil society, the private sector, academia, media and international development partners attended. They marked five years of work under the Equal Opportunities and Social Development (EOSD) project and discussed how to widen young people's socio-economic participation in Egypt.

The European Union co-finances the project, and its first phase focused on creating an enabling environment for young people, particularly young women and persons with disabilities. Christian Grün, Head of Cooperation at the German Embassy in Cairo, said the partnership between Egypt, Germany and the European Union aims to let young people realise their potential and access opportunities. Marguerite Saroufim, Deputy Minister of Social Solidarity, called the forum an effective model of cooperation tied to Egypt Vision 2030.

Saroufim urged the private sector to offer training aligned with labour market needs, and asked civil society groups to build solutions on community needs. Research institutions were asked to supply scientific evidence to guide decisions and measure impact. Grün said that when young people can take part, societies move forward.

Why it mattersYoung women and persons with disabilities are the groups the forum says the next phase must reach, through training tied to jobs.
