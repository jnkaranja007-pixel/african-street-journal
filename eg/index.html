Egypt reclaims over 3.5 million feddans of desert for agriculture

The state has added millions of feddans of agricultural land through desert reclamation, aiming for self-sufficiency and affordable food.

Egypt's Agriculture Minister Alaa Farouk stated that the nation has reclaimed over 3.5 million feddans of desert land, a significant expansion aimed at boosting food security and rural development. This initiative is part of a broader strategy to increase agricultural area and production through both horizontal land reclamation and vertical expansion utilizing scientific research and digital transformation. The goal is to maximize added value, achieve self-sufficiency, reduce the import gap, and provide safe food at affordable prices for citizens.

The minister highlighted major land reclamation projects including the New Delta (2.2 million feddans), Toshka (1.1 million feddans), North and Central Sinai (456,000 feddans), New Egyptian Rural Development (1.5 million feddans), and projects in South Upper Egypt and the New Valley (650,000 feddans). These efforts have transformed the agricultural sector, demonstrating the state's commitment to securing food and agricultural product needs, even as other nations faced challenges and imposed export restrictions.

Farouk emphasized that while the world loses agricultural land annually to desertification and climate change, Egypt has managed to expand its arable land. This proactive approach supports urban development and the agricultural sector, ensuring the nation's food supply. The "Egypt 2026 Forum" showcases this vision, with wide participation from society, political parties, and decision-makers, reflecting the government's focus on public policies and development programs.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected by the expansion of agricultural land and efforts to increase production and provide affordable food.

Egypt president says military action to defend Saudi Arabia

President Al-Sisi stated Egypt's proposed military effort is solely for defending Saudi territory and not for attacking Yemen.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah Al-Sisi announced on Wednesday that any proposed Egyptian military effort would be directed towards defending Saudi Arabian territory, clarifying that it would not involve an attack on Yemen. He added that Egypt would extend this defense to any other Arab state upon request, emphasizing that the security of Gulf nations is integral to Egypt's own national security. Al-Sisi made these remarks during the Homeland and Citizen Security session at the Egypt Forum 2026, highlighting the indivisibility of Arab national security.

The President also addressed the importance of strengthening Egypt and maintaining public solidarity in the face of numerous risks, including the Ethiopian dam. He reiterated that targeting state institutions, as seen in 2011, is a tactic to destabilize nations. Al-Sisi identified the armed forces and the Interior Ministry as foundational pillars of state security, noting ongoing attempts to undermine the Interior Ministry's role and public perception.

Al-Sisi called for a review of the 2011 events to prevent their recurrence, citing the targeting of security forces, prison breaks, and subsequent consequences as examples of state collapse. He stressed the critical need to preserve state institutions, warning that their failure leads to widespread problems, a situation he noted is evident in the current regional landscape.

Why it mattersEgypt's military stance affects regional security and stability for Arab nations.

Business · 2026-10-22

President asks developer for 200,000 rental homes

President el-Sisi urged businessman Hisham Talaat Moustafa to provide rental apartments as part of a large investment deal.

President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi asked businessman Hisham Talaat Moustafa on Tuesday to help provide between 100,000 and 200,000 rental apartments. The request came during a session on urban development at the Egypt Forum 2026, following an agreement for Moustafa’s company, Talaat Moustafa Group, to develop four mixed-use projects with an investment of EGP 11 trillion. El-Sisi stated that developers undertaking projects of such scale should also contribute to society, questioning the social service aspect of major investments.

Moustafa responded affirmatively, citing the private sector’s contributions to social initiatives. However, el-Sisi clarified that this request was separate from existing programs like the Haya Karima initiative and the Long Live Egypt Fund. He emphasized the scale of his proposal by noting that 1 percent of the announced investment would equal EGP 100 billion.

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly also spoke at the forum, announcing a major housing program that will include rental apartments, social housing, and projects for middle-income households. This initiative, to be rolled out over four years, aims to expand access to affordable housing across different income groups and revive rental housing as a key option.

Why it mattersEgyptian citizens across different income groups will have more housing options, including rental units, as part of a new government initiative.

Greek ambassador sees strong Egypt-Greece partnership

The Greek ambassador to Egypt stated that bilateral ties offer a solid foundation for cooperation, built on shared history and mutual interests.

Greek Ambassador to Egypt Nikolaos Papageorgiou described Egyptian-Greek relations as an advanced model of strategic partnership, citing deep historical, civilizational, and human ties. He noted that the relationship continues to develop, serving the interests of both peoples and supporting regional stability. Papageorgiou made these remarks on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, on the sidelines of the Mediterranean Offshore Conference and Exhibition in Alexandria.

The ambassador emphasized that the strong historical and human foundations of the Cairo-Athens relationship have gained renewed strategic significance over centuries, creating a basis for coordination in various fields. He highlighted that regional challenges underscore the importance of partnerships built on trust and shared interests. Sustained communication and growing cooperation in political, economic, and cultural spheres reflect the vitality of these bilateral relations.

Papageorgiou pointed to promising opportunities for deepening cooperation, particularly in energy and electricity interconnection, leveraging complementary capabilities in the natural gas sector. He also cited Alexandria as a symbol of Egyptian-Greek ties, demonstrating cultural diversity and common ground between civilizations. The city's historical significance supports bilateral relations and strengthens their popular and cultural dimensions.

Why it mattersThe Greek ambassador's statements highlight the potential for enhanced cooperation between Egypt and Greece, impacting regional stability and economic ties.

Education · 2026-10-22

Egypt forum highlights school attendance gains amid teacher shortages

Government officials presented improved school attendance as a success at the Egypt Forum 2026, despite ongoing concerns about teacher and classroom scarcity.

The Egypt Forum 2026, an event mandated by President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi, showcased a reported increase in school attendance as a key achievement in the nation's education sector. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbuly announced that basic education would anchor the forum's opening session, focusing on whether current curricula prepare students for future labor markets. The government framed this attendance boost as a transition point, allowing a shift in policy focus from expanding access to ensuring learning quality.

However, Mada Masr's investigation into the attendance claims suggests that increased regular testing, a recent curriculum change, is a significant driver of the reported gains. This innovation has drawn criticism from families and members of parliament who argue it strains students and teachers without improving educational outcomes, and potentially conflicts with pedagogical research.

Despite these criticisms and a general backdrop of chronic low spending in education, the government used the forum to highlight progress. The Education Minister outlined achievements, emphasizing the rise in school attendance as a foundation for future improvements in learning quality, a narrative presented amidst public inquiries and parliamentary pushback over recent ministry decisions.

Why it mattersStudents and teachers are affected by increased testing and potential burnout due to teacher shortages, despite reported attendance improvements.
