Bus crash kills three on Red Sea highway

25 people were injured, including five foreign nationals, when a bus collided with a sand truck.

Three people died, including a bus driver, and 25 others were injured on Friday, October 30, when a passenger bus crashed into a sand-laden truck on the highway between Hurghada and Ras Gharib. The accident occurred approximately 6 miles past Jabal al-Zeit, heading towards Ras Gharib in the Red Sea Governorate.

Local reports indicate the bus struck the truck from behind, partially obstructing traffic, though photographs showed the front of the bus severely damaged. Among the injured were a French citizen, an Italian, and three Russian nationals. Ambulances transported the injured, with conditions ranging from moderate to critical, to nearby hospitals.

Ismail al-Arabi, the Red Sea Governorate’s health undersecretary, visited injured individuals at Ras Gharib Central Hospital, and critical cases were moved to Hurghada General Hospital. Security and traffic officials worked to clear the wreckage and reopen the road, while prosecutors initiated an investigation into the incident.

Why it mattersThe road accident resulted in three deaths and 25 injuries, affecting local traffic and emergency services.

Egypt central bank raises interest rates by 1%

The Monetary Policy Committee increased key rates to combat inflation and currency pressures.

The Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) unexpectedly raised its key interest rates by 1 percentage point on Thursday. The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) increased the overnight deposit rate to 20.0 percent, the overnight lending rate to 21.0 percent, and the main operation rate to 20.5 percent. The credit and discount rate also moved to 20.5 percent.

Banking expert Hany Aboul Fotouh stated the decision reflects concerns over inflation and global economic shifts. He noted the US dollar approaching EGP 56 as a catalyst for currency pressure, which risks increasing costs for imported goods and production inputs. The CBE aims to preserve local debt appeal and reduce money outflows.

Geopolitical tensions add to market uncertainty, potentially disrupting energy markets and supply chains. The central bank stated the tighter monetary policy targets rising food and energy costs to protect domestic purchasing power in a difficult global economy.

Why it mattersEgyptian citizens face potential increases in the cost of imported goods and production inputs due to currency pressures and rising inflation.

Egypt moves to raise fines for false news

The government is preparing amendments to the Penal Code to increase penalties for spreading false information online.

Egypt’s Cabinet announced on Wednesday, October 28, that it is preparing amendments to the Penal Code to increase fines for false news and rumour-related offences. This action is part of a larger government plan to regulate social media through new digital safety legislation, expanded technical monitoring, and public awareness campaigns.

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly directed the formation of a working group to follow up on recommendations from a committee examining harmful social media practices. Justice Minister Mahmoud El-Sherif presented a report to the Cabinet on measures to safeguard Egyptian family values and social cohesion.

The committee also proposed establishing general rules for digital platforms through forthcoming digital safety legislation and stronger state oversight. The government has not yet specified the proposed fines or announced a timetable for introducing the amendments.

Why it mattersThe Egyptian government is increasing penalties for spreading false news online as part of new digital safety legislation.

EgyptAir adds Birmingham flights

The national carrier now flies four times weekly between Cairo and the UK's West Midlands.

EgyptAir launched direct flights between Cairo International Airport and Birmingham Airport on October 30, 2026, as part of its winter schedule.

The airline will operate the route four times per week using Boeing 737 MAX 8 aircraft, making Birmingham Egypt's third UK destination after London Heathrow and Manchester.

This expansion aims to serve the Egyptian diaspora in the West Midlands and facilitate travel to Africa, the Middle East, and Asia via Cairo.

Why it mattersEgyptAir's new route connects the West Midlands to Cairo, benefiting the Egyptian diaspora and business travelers.

Giza digital billboard hacked with inappropriate footage

An international advertising provider's cyberattack caused the Giza screen hack, leading to broadcast suspensions.

An inappropriate footage hack targeted a digital billboard in Giza on Thursday, October 29, after a cyberattack on an international advertising provider. Egypt’s Ministry of Interior confirmed the incident was linked to a breach of the provider's website, which displayed advertisements for a local company. The hack simultaneously affected digital billboards in several other countries.

The advertising company that owns the Giza billboard has suspended all broadcasts while it works to fix the security breach. The ministry stated that legal actions have been initiated following the incident. October 2026 has seen numerous significant cyberattacks globally, prompting cybersecurity experts to label it "Black October".

The international provider's website was compromised, leading to the unauthorized display of footage. The advertising company is working to restore normal service across its screens. The ministry confirmed legal measures are in place.
