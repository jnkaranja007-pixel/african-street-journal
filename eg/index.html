Egypt Minister Ends Debate on International School Curricula

International schools in Egypt must teach Arabic, history, and religion, with grades counting toward final scores.

Egypt’s Minister of Education and Technical Education, Mohamed Abdel Latif, declared the debate closed on mandatory Arabic, history, and religion classes in international schools. Speaking at the Egypt Forum 2026, Abdel Latif stated that all students in any Egyptian school will study these subjects, and their grades will be included in final scores. He emphasized that treating these subjects as pass-or-fail would diminish their importance.

The minister’s remarks followed a discussion with businessman Ahmad Tarek Khalil, who advocated for prioritizing the Arabic language in international schools. Abdel Latif agreed, confirming the ministry's support for international schools in teaching Arabic. Currently, Arabic and history each contribute 10 percent to the total score for the international certificate, making up 20 percent combined.

The Egypt Forum 2026, held from October 19 to 24, aims to foster dialogue between the government and citizens. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly highlighted that the forum is dedicated to the citizen, who has borne the brunt of economic and structural reforms. The event includes ministers, officials, civil society, experts, and citizens, focusing on constructive dialogue about Egypt's present and future.

Why it mattersInternational schools and their students are directly affected by the mandatory inclusion of Arabic, history, and religion in their curriculum and grading.

ZenHR Opens Cairo Office, Plans EGP 150m Investment

The AI-powered HR platform aims to boost digital transformation for Egyptian businesses with a new regional hub and significant capital injection.

ZenHR, a human resources and payroll platform, has launched its sixth regional office in Cairo, Egypt. This move signifies the company's commitment to the Egyptian market and its goal of supporting the digital transformation of local institutions, companies, and factories. CEO Yousef Shamoun stated that the Cairo office represents a significant milestone after nearly a decade of serving Egyptian clients. Over the next two years, ZenHR intends to invest more than EGP 150m to expand its local team and enhance its service offerings.

The company is targeting medium to large enterprises in sectors such as manufacturing, retail, services, and technology, as well as government entities. ZenHR's platform automates HR processes, aiming to reduce operational costs and improve productivity by complying with Egyptian labor legislation, including the new Labour Law No. 14 of 2025. The platform automatically calculates payroll, factoring in income tax, social insurance, and overtime, and integrates with a bilingual Arabic-English interface and mobile application.

The expansion was announced at a large ZenHR event in Cairo, attended by over 250 HR leaders and business representatives, including diplomatic and government officials. During the event, ZenHR signed a memorandum of understanding with digital insurance broker Amanleek to integrate its services, automating employee insurance activation and termination. ZenHR also honored its long-standing clients and signed new partnership agreements.

Why it mattersEgyptian businesses will benefit from automated HR processes and more efficient payroll calculations, tailored to local legislation.

Egypt Switches to Winter Time Ending Daylight Saving

Clocks will move back one hour on October 29, returning the country to standard time and affecting business operating hours.

Egypt will revert to winter time on Friday, October 30, as daylight saving time concludes. Clocks will be set back one hour at midnight on Thursday, October 29, effectively giving residents an additional hour. This change aligns with Law No. 24 of 2023, which dictates daylight saving begins the last Friday of April and ends the last Thursday of October annually.

The transition to winter time impacts established operating hours for various commercial establishments. Restaurants and cafes are permitted to stay open until midnight, while shops and malls must close by 10:00 PM on most days. Exceptions allow shops and malls to remain open until 11:00 PM on Thursdays, Fridays, public holidays, and festive occasions.

Workshops located within residential areas are subject to a 6:00 PM closing time under the current regulations. These winter operating hours were set by the Ministry of Local Development and are now in effect following the end of daylight saving.

Why it mattersBusinesses and shops will adjust their operating hours due to the time change, affecting daily operations and customer access.

Eni starts Egypt Western Desert gas project's second phase

The Meleiha project's expansion will boost gas processing capacity to 100 million standard cubic feet daily.

Italian energy firm Eni has initiated operations for the second phase of its Meleiha gas project in Egypt's Western Desert. This expansion, conducted through its joint venture Agiba Petroleum Company with the Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation, establishes a new gas processing hub. The facility is designed to handle up to 100 million standard cubic feet of gas per day, significantly increasing the processing capacity for gas produced in the region.

The project's second phase incorporates an integrated infrastructure system, including new development wells, gathering networks, and transmission pipelines that connect to Egypt's existing gas grid. This integration aims to accelerate resource monetization and enhance operational efficiency. Eni, which has been active in Egypt since 1954 and is the country's largest energy producer, views this development as reinforcing its commitment to Egypt's energy security.

The expanded Meleiha project is expected to increase the availability of natural gas for the domestic market. Eni highlighted that the development adheres to high standards of operational excellence, safety, and environmental sustainability. The company's operations in Egypt span exploration, development, and production across multiple regions, including the Western Desert, Gulf of Suez, Nile Delta, and the Eastern Mediterranean.

Why it mattersThis project will increase the availability of natural gas for the domestic market.

Egypt Spent LE31 Billion on Sports Facilities Amid Declining Participation

Minister boasts of infrastructure investment while official data shows fewer matches and teams in rural youth centers.

Sports Minister Gohar Nabil announced an investment of over LE31 billion in youth and sports facilities, including thousands of centers, stadiums, and pitches, during the Egypt Forum 2026. Nabil highlighted the creation of opportunities through these facilities across all 27 governorates and praised a national project for producing 577 athletes for national teams. However, this announcement contrasted sharply with data from the Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics (CAPMAS).

CAPMAS data revealed a significant decline in participation at village youth centers between 2013 and 2024. The number of matches played dropped by 85.5 percent, and sports teams decreased by 50.3 percent, despite a 6.7 percent rise in the number of centers. Player numbers in village centers also fell by 53.4 percent, reducing their share of total registered players from 82 percent to 63 percent.

The decline was also observed in city youth centers, though at a lesser rate, with matches falling by 50.8 percent. Despite the substantial investment and claims of opportunity, Egypt's Olympic medal count halved at the Paris 2024 games, with three medals compared to six in Tokyo 2020. The cost per medal in Paris exceeded LE333.3 million, a significant increase from Tokyo's LE37.1 million per medal.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the significant expenditure on sports infrastructure that has not translated into improved athletic performance or increased participation.
