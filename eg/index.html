Egypt rejects Nile water security compromise

President Al-Sisi stated Egypt will not accept a fait accompli regarding its water security from the Nile.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah Al-Sisi declared on Sunday that Egypt will not accept a fait accompli or compromise its water security, stating that the right to development for Nile Basin countries should not harm others. Speaking at the ninth Cairo Water Week, Al-Sisi emphasized that the Nile provides over 98% of Egypt’s renewable water resources amidst population growth and rising demand.

Al-Sisi asserted that any assumption of Egypt accepting a fait accompli on water security is a losing bet, noting that a river's headwaters do not grant unilateral control. He affirmed Egypt's recognition of Nile Basin countries' right to development if exercised responsibly and in accordance with international law, highlighting past cooperation on energy and agriculture projects built on prior notification and consultations.

Water Resources and Irrigation Minister Hani Sewilam announced Egypt's commitment to water as a "bridge for cooperation" and detailed efforts in water-use efficiency and infrastructure upgrades under the "Water System 2.0" program. The ninth Cairo Water Week, themed "Water as a Catalyst for Peace, Shared Prosperity and a Sustainable Planet," runs until October 29 with 21 ministers participating.

Why it mattersPresident Al-Sisi's statements directly address Egypt's national water security concerns related to the Nile River.

Egypt ranks second in global sugar byproduct exports

The country shipped 889,000 metric tons of beet pulp and sugar cane bagasse worth USD 176 million in 2025.

Egypt has secured the position of the world's second-largest exporter of sugar byproducts, including beet pulp and sugar cane bagasse. In 2025, the nation's exports represented approximately one-fifth of the global trade in these materials. The total volume shipped reached about 889,000 metric tons, generating USD 176 million (EGP 9.7 billion) in revenue.

This export performance shows significant growth, with the value of shipments increasing by 54 percent from 2024. In the previous year, Egypt also held the second rank globally, capturing an 18 percent share of the market and earning USD 114 million (EGP 6.3 billion). Between 2020 and 2025, the value of these exports grew at a compound annual rate of 17 percent, while the volume increased by 11 percent.

China was the primary importer, purchasing USD 46 million (EGP 2.5 billion) of Egypt's sugar byproducts, accounting for 26 percent of the total. Spain followed with USD 24 million (EGP 1.3 billion), and Ireland with USD 21 million (EGP 1.1 billion). South Korea and Italy were also significant buyers, with imports valued at USD 16 million (EGP 881.3 million) and USD 13 million (EGP 716 million) respectively. These top five markets collectively accounted for 68 percent of Egypt's total exports in this category.

Why it mattersEgyptian farmers and sugar producers are earning significant revenue from exporting sugar byproducts.

Egyptian dollar rate unlikely to fall below LE 50

Economist Mohamed Fouad cites foreign investment outflows and economic imbalances for the prediction.

Economist Mohamed Fouad stated that the US dollar exchange rate in Egypt will likely remain around 52 Egyptian pounds and is unlikely to fall below 50 pounds soon. He attributed this to foreign investment outflows, increasing financing needs, and structural economic problems. Fouad appeared on MBC Masr’s “al-Hekaya” program, hosted by Amr Adib.

Fouad explained that approximately $1.8 billion exited Egypt between Monday and Thursday of the previous week. He noted that a flexible exchange rate system makes the market sensitive to capital movements. Global factors, including strong US economic activity and energy market pressures, also contribute to dollar strength worldwide.

Domestically, investors are selling treasury bills and recovering funds from maturing three-month deals. Egypt's balance of payments shows a trade deficit of about $65 billion and a current account deficit of nearly $2.25 billion. These domestic imbalances combined with global financing pressures make the Egyptian economy vulnerable.

Why it mattersEgyptian citizens and businesses will be affected by the continued high exchange rate of the US dollar against the Egyptian pound.

Egypt uses satellite tech to track waterway changes

The Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation is expanding its use of remote sensing to detect illegal construction along canals.

Egypt’s Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation is using satellite imagery and remote sensing technology to detect illegal construction and other changes along waterways. Minister Hani Sewilam reviewed a monitoring model developed by the ministry that uses Google Earth Engine to track changes in and around canals. The model identifies new buildings along water banks and alterations to watercourses, allowing faster detection of potential violations.

Minister Sewilam stressed the importance of satellite imagery for better waterway monitoring and more accurate water management decisions. The ministry is also developing geospatial databases to document encroachments and changes, which will help field teams respond more efficiently. Sewilam directed officials to combine satellite data with field measurements, existing databases, and artificial intelligence for analysis and planning.

The ministry’s initiative aims to improve the protection of water resources. Field teams will use data from these databases to identify and respond to potential violations more efficiently.

Why it mattersEgyptians who rely on waterways for agriculture and daily life will benefit from better monitoring and management of water resources.

Egypt launches patient safety strategy for 2026-2030

The plan aims to reduce medical errors and improve healthcare standards across all levels of care.

Egypt has launched a national quality and patient safety strategy set to run from 2026 to 2030. Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar announced the initiative, which seeks to reduce medical errors and improve healthcare standards nationwide. The strategy also aims to establish systems for reporting adverse events and learning from them.

The strategy integrates quality standards from primary healthcare to intensive care, according to Abdel Ghaffar. A new medical liability and patient safety law has been introduced, alongside a dedicated patient safety department. A higher committee has been formed, uniting public, military, police, and private healthcare providers with civil society organisations.

Egypt's first patient safety report for 2025 will serve as a baseline for future performance. The ministry is also cooperating with the World Health Organization (WHO) on a professional diploma in patient safety, with 70 doctors already enrolled. The General Authority for Healthcare Accreditation and Regulation (GAHAR) has accredited 964 facilities and registered over 122,000 professionals.

Why it mattersEgyptians receiving healthcare will benefit from improved standards and a reduction in medical errors.
