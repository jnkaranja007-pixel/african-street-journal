Egypt FM discusses water, finance with UN, EIB

Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty met UN envoy and EIB vice-president in Cairo on Tuesday.

Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty met UN Special Envoy on Water Retno Marsudi and European Investment Bank (EIB) Vice-President Gelsomina Vigliotti in Cairo on Tuesday. They discussed water security, preparations for the 2026 UN Water Conference, and expanding development financing in Egypt. Abdelatty outlined Egypt’s water-scarcity challenges and measures to address them, including water conservation and irrigation modernisation.

Abdelatty stressed Egypt’s support for cooperation among Nile riparian states and equitable benefit sharing, rejecting unilateral measures that could affect downstream states. In a separate meeting, Abdelatty discussed expanding the EIB’s portfolio in Egypt, which has funded 141 projects since 1979. They also addressed financing for projects in energy, health, transport, water, and food security.

Abdelatty also spoke with Iranian counterpart Abbas Araghchi by phone on Tuesday about regional de-escalation and crisis management. The ministers reviewed options to manage escalating regional risks and discussed expanding regional consultations between Iran and the “R4” ministerial group. Abdelatty reiterated the government’s commitment to economic reforms to improve the investment climate.

Why it mattersEgypt's foreign minister met with UN and EIB officials to discuss water security and development financing, and with Iran's foreign minister about regional de-escalation.

egyptian mp questions stray dog campaign funds

irene said filed a formal request for a briefing on public spending for stray dog control.

Irene Said, a member of Egypt’s House of Representatives, demanded on October 27 that the government explain its spending on stray dogs. Residents continue to report growing numbers of stray animals in streets and residential areas, with packs gathering near schools and hospitals. Said filed a formal request for a briefing to the prime minister and ministers of agriculture, local development, environment, and health.

The lawmaker stated that the dogs have been linked to bites and attacks, posing risks to public safety and health. These incidents impose costs on the state for treating victims and preventing rabies. The government announced a national plan including sterilization and vaccination drives, but Said questioned its effectiveness and the sufficiency of assigned money and personnel.

Said pointed to testimony in June before the House Agriculture Committee regarding the General Authority for Veterinary Services. She asked how actual progress compared with stated targets for the national campaign.

Why it mattersan Egyptian MP is questioning government spending on a national campaign to control stray dogs.

Egypt seeks to boost African exports

Industry Minister Khaled Hashem stated that intra-African trade reached $214bn in 2025, with Egypt accounting for $9.3bn.

Egypt aims to increase its exports to African nations by diversifying markets and strengthening trade partnerships, according to Industry Minister Khaled Hashem. Speaking at a forum organised by AmCham Egypt and Afreximbank, Hashem highlighted Africa's potential for Egyptian industrial, agricultural, engineering, and building-materials products. The event gathered government officials, exporters, and finance specialists to discuss expanding Egypt's commercial reach across the continent.

Hashem noted that intra-African trade totalled approximately $214bn in 2025, a 5.5% annual rise, while total African merchandise trade was about $1.5trn. Egypt's share of this intra-African trade was around 4.4%, or approximately $9.3bn, a figure Hashem described as "abnormal" given Egypt's industrial capacity. He stressed that increasing exports globally is a government objective, but Africa offers significant opportunities for rapid growth.

Egyptian exports to African Union countries reached approximately $7.6bn in 2025, a decrease from $7.8bn in 2024, with imports at around $2bn. Libya was the largest destination for Egyptian exports, receiving $1.5bn in 2025, followed by Algeria at $1.2bn and Morocco at $1.1bn.

Why it mattersEgyptian exporters will benefit from expanded opportunities in African markets.

Egypt's irrigation minister calls for African water law adherence

Hani Sewilam stated that 90 percent of Africa’s surface water is in shared basins, requiring data sharing and consultation.

Egypt's Irrigation Minister Hani Sewilam stated Tuesday that 90 percent of Africa’s surface water is located within shared international basins. He emphasized the need for strict adherence to international water law, data sharing, and prior consultation on transboundary projects. Sewilam spoke at Cairo Water Week during a session on the Africa Water Vision 2063.

Sewilam highlighted that the Nile River provides approximately 98 percent of Egypt’s total water resources. He stressed that Egypt's national water security should not negatively impact other Nile basin nations. The minister also noted that Egypt is reviewing 36 downstream Nile projects.

Ahead of the 2026 UN Water Conference, Sewilam urged for a unified African stance to secure sustainable financing and development for water projects.

Why it mattersEgypt's water security is directly impacted by the Nile River, which supplies 98 percent of its water resources.

Egypt doubles internet package capacity for LE150

The National Telecom Regulatory Authority ordered providers to increase base data from 50 GB to 100 GB starting November 1.

Egypt's National Telecom Regulatory Authority (NTRA) has instructed internet providers to double the capacity of the LE150 base package to 100 GB, effective November 1. This move, without any additional cost, follows presidential directives to make telecommunications services more accessible for citizens. The initiative aims to improve access to digital government services and support the nation's digital transformation.

NTRA CEO Mohamed Shamroukh stated that this order reflects the authority's role in regulating and facilitating the sector. He explained the goal is to allow families to access quality internet services affordably, supporting their integration into digital society. An official source confirmed that companies are committed to implementing the decision without new fees, ensuring the increased data reaches beneficiaries.

This initiative is part of a broader plan to build a 'Digital Egypt' and enhance the competitiveness of the knowledge-based economy. The NTRA will monitor service providers to ensure the new package meets high standards of quality and efficiency. The change is intended to improve the quality of digital life for Egyptians and enable them to utilize various online services.

Why it mattersEgyptian families will receive double the internet data for the same monthly price, improving their access to digital services.
