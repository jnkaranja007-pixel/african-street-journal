Egyptian-American Abdul El-Sayed wins Michigan Senate seat

El-Sayed becomes the first Muslim to serve in the US Senate after a close race against Republican Mike Rogers.

Egyptian-American Abdul El-Sayed won a Michigan Senate seat on Wednesday, November 4, defeating Republican Mike Rogers. El-Sayed, 42, a former public health administrator, is the first Muslim elected to the United States Senate.

The campaign against El-Sayed involved heavy outside spending and attacks, including claims of communism and baseless ties to terrorism. El-Sayed thanked supporters for taking a chance on "the unlikeliest of candidates" after a campaign he described as among the ugliest. The seat was previously held by retiring Democrat Senator Gary Peters.

Egyptian billionaire Naguib Sawiris defended El-Sayed on X, calling him "resilient, sharp, and charismatic" and dismissing accusations of Muslim Brotherhood ties. Sawiris stated that El-Sayed earned his victory on merit, following a viral town hall exchange where El-Sayed disavowed any connection to the group.

Why it mattersEgyptian-American Abdul El-Sayed's historic win as the first Muslim US Senator is commented on by Egyptian billionaire Naguib Sawiris.

Agriculture · 2026-11-06

Egypt and IFAD sign $65M irrigation deals

Agreements aim to modernize water management across 12,600 hectares and train 2,000 people.

Egypt has signed two agricultural financing agreements totalling US$65.32 million with the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) to modernize irrigation and boost climate resilience. The majority of the funds, US$64.85 million, will support the CROWN project, aiming to improve water efficiency and farmer incomes across 12,600 hectares.

The CROWN project is expected to benefit approximately 90,000 rural households by modernizing irrigation and drainage systems and promoting climate-smart farming practices. A separate US$470,000 grant for the PEARL project will enhance agricultural expertise in Egyptian institutions through training and digital tools for about 2,000 individuals.

Egypt faces water scarcity with less than 500 cubic meters per person annually, prompting a focus on efficiency rather than supply. The Nile provides over 98% of the country's water resources, making modernization crucial amid population growth and increasing food demand.

Why it mattersEgyptian farmers will benefit from improved irrigation, access to markets, and climate-smart practices, while institutions will gain enhanced agricultural expertise.

Egypt court hears Giza Pyramids rave ban case

A lawsuit filed by the Egyptian Center for Economic and Social Rights seeks to stop loud music and light shows at the ancient site.

Egypt's State Council Administrative Court has postponed a case seeking to ban rave parties and laser light shows at the Giza Pyramids until January 2, 2027. The Egyptian Center for Economic and Social Rights (ECESR) filed the lawsuit against the Minister of Tourism and Antiquities and other officials. The case challenges the state's alleged inaction regarding commercial entertainment within the Giza Pyramids perimeter.

Lawyers for ECESR argued that loud, low-frequency sound systems pose structural risks to the ancient masonry, and high-powered laser displays violate UNESCO guidelines for World Heritage sites. The suit also demands a halt to temporary earthworks and staging installations within the monument's buffer zone. Prominent preservationists, including Egyptologist Monica Hanna, are supporting the action.

The litigation is grounded in Egypt's Constitution, Antiquities Law #117/1983, and international UNESCO treaties. The ECESR is petitioning for an immediate halt to all event licensing and a full audit of current commercial vendor contracts operating at the Giza Pyramids.

Why it mattersPreservationists and legal groups are suing to protect the Giza Pyramids from structural damage and violations of heritage guidelines caused by commercial entertainment events.

Sport · 2026-11-06

Sondos Sonbol wins historic wushu gold for Egypt

The Egyptian athlete secured the nation's first Youth Olympic medal in wushu with a combined score of 18.660 points.

Egyptian wushu athlete Sondos Sonbol won gold in the women’s Changquan combined event at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games on Tuesday, 3 November. This victory marks Egypt's first Youth Olympic medal in the martial art, which debuted as an official sport in Dakar.

Sonbol achieved a combined score of 18.660 points, surpassing Venezuela's Valeria Frannerys Dudamel Alvarado (18.199) and India's Rajkumari Lanchenbi Chanu (18.039). She led both wushu disciplines, scoring 9.330 points in the staff-based Gunshu and maintaining her lead in Changquan.

Wushu combines traditional combat with choreographed athletic movements, including kicks, jumps, and routines, with some disciplines using weapons. Sonbol's win is a significant achievement for the sport's debut at the Youth Olympic Games.

Tech · 2026-11-06

Egypt to play role in AI governance

Academics and diplomats met at New Giza University to discuss Egypt's potential influence in AI development and global economic shifts.

New Giza University, with the University of Glasgow, hosted a conference on artificial intelligence's growing role in national power and global economics. The event explored how AI is reshaping international influence, with discussions highlighting Egypt's potential to lead in the new order due to its geography and strategic weight.

Participants discussed Egypt's opportunities to increase its involvement in global trade, investment, and production chains, and to help set rules for cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. The forum also addressed the risks of wealth concentrating in a few hands and the necessity for states to regulate markets for equitable benefit distribution.

The conference celebrated New Giza University's 10th anniversary and the 250th anniversary of Adam Smith's "The Wealth of Nations." Speakers cautioned that technology could become a tool for conflict among major global powers.

Why it mattersEgypt is positioned to play an influential role in the emerging international order by developing technological and institutional capabilities in artificial intelligence.
