Bus crash kills three on Red Sea highway

25 people were injured, including five foreign nationals, when a bus collided with a sand truck.

Three people died, including a bus driver, and 25 others were injured on Friday, October 30, when a passenger bus crashed into a sand-laden truck on the highway between Hurghada and Ras Gharib. The accident occurred approximately 6 miles past Jabal al-Zeit, heading towards Ras Gharib in the Red Sea Governorate.

Local reports indicate the bus struck the truck from behind, partially obstructing traffic, though photographs showed the front of the bus severely damaged. Among the injured were a French citizen, an Italian, and three Russian nationals. Ambulances transported the injured, with conditions ranging from moderate to critical, to nearby hospitals.

Ismail al-Arabi, the Red Sea Governorate’s health undersecretary, visited injured individuals at Ras Gharib Central Hospital, and critical cases were moved to Hurghada General Hospital. Security and traffic officials worked to clear the wreckage and reopen the road, while prosecutors initiated an investigation into the incident.

Why it mattersThe road accident resulted in three deaths and 25 injuries, affecting local traffic and emergency services.

Egypt medicinal plant exports reach $852m

Exports of dried herbs, extracts and oils grew from around $400m in previous years to $852m in 2025.

Egypt's exports of medicinal and aromatic plants and related products reached around $852m in 2025, according to Agriculture Minister Alaa Farouk. The government aims to expand local processing and increase value added in the sector. These crops are cultivated on about 80,000 feddans across Egypt, including marjoram, basil, mint, anise, chamomile, fennel, coriander, hibiscus and jasmine.

Farouk stated that exports of dried herbs, extracts and oils reached $852m in 2025, a significant increase from around $400m in previous years. He noted that approximately 85% of the output of some crops in dried-herb form is exported, making the sector a key source of foreign currency and jobs. The ministry is encouraging investment in processing to create higher added value products.

The government is developing an integrated service centre for medicinal and aromatic plants in Samasta, Beni Suef governorate, with support from the UN Development Programme and the European Union. This centre will offer sterilisation, processing, residue-testing, and advisory services. The German Agency for International Cooperation (GIZ) is also supporting a programme to increase small farmers' incomes and promote agricultural innovation.

Why it mattersFarmers and processors in Egypt are affected by increased export earnings and government support for processing and innovation.

Cairo Water Week becomes global dialogue platform

The ninth edition of Cairo Water Week, held October 25-29, featured 137 sessions and broad international participation.

Cairo Water Week has become a key international platform for dialogue and partnership on water challenges, according to Irrigation Minister Hani Sewilam. The ninth edition, held from October 25 to 29, 2026, was themed “Water as a Catalyst for Peace, Shared Prosperity and a Sustainable Planet.”

Minister Sewilam thanked international and national supporters, including the European Union and the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), who co-organized the event. The five-day event concluded on Thursday with 137 technical and scientific sessions and competitions.

The Ministry aims to strengthen cooperation with international organizations, research institutions, and the private sector to implement the second-generation water system (2.0) and support the National Water Resources and Irrigation Strategy 2050.

Why it mattersEgyptian farmers and the agricultural sector are affected by the ministry's commitment to strengthening cooperation for water and food security.

Egypt shops adjust to winter hours

Retailers will close at 10:00 pm, while restaurants can stay open until midnight.

Egypt's shops and restaurants are now operating under new winter hours following the nation's shift to standard time. Retail shops and malls must close by 10:00 pm, with an extended hour on Thursdays, Fridays, and public holidays. Workshops and craft businesses will operate from eight am to six pm, though exceptions exist for businesses on main roads or providing urgent services.

Restaurants and cafés can welcome customers from five am until midnight, with a one-hour extension to one am on holidays. Home delivery services for these establishments are permitted to operate 24 hours a day. These winter operating hours are distinct from the national time change, which set clocks back by 60 minutes.

Grocery stores, supermarkets, bakeries, fruit and vegetable shops, poultry retailers, wholesale markets, and pharmacies are among the establishments exempted from these closing times. These exemptions allow for continuous operation throughout the night where necessary.

Why it mattersEgypt's businesses are adjusting their operating hours due to the seasonal time change.
