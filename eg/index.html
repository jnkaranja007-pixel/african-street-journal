Egypt allocates Red Sea land to cut public debt

President Al-Sisi issued a decree allocating 26,690 feddans to the finance ministry for debt reduction and sukuk issuance.

Egypt has allocated approximately 26,690 feddans of state-owned land in the Red Sea governorate to the finance ministry. President Abdel Fattah Al-Sisi issued a decree on November 11, 2026, to use this land for reducing public debt and issuing sovereign sukuk. This decree amends a previous one that allowed the land's use solely for sukuk issuance.

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly met with Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk and other officials to review progress on debt reduction targets. Madbouly emphasized that lowering debt is a top government priority to free up resources for essential services. The Egypt Forum 2026 set a goal to reduce public debt to about 69.5% of GDP by 2030.

The finance minister reported that the budget-sector debt-to-GDP ratio has been decreasing since the 2023/24 fiscal year, falling by approximately 14 percentage points between June 2023 and June 2026. External debt for these bodies was reduced by about 3.9% of GDP in 2025/26. Madbouly directed ministries to create detailed debt reduction plans for presidential review.

Why it mattersThe government is allocating state land to reduce public debt, aiming to lower the debt-to-GDP ratio to 69.5% by 2030.

Egypt pitches 16 sectors and a faster capital-increase platform to US investors

The investment minister said a new platform called Journey of the Minutes will soon handle company capital increases.

Investment and Foreign Trade Minister Mohamed Farid presented Egypt's investment opportunities to major US and international companies at a meeting hosted by the law firm DLA Piper in Washington. The session was held on the sidelines of the B20 USA 2026 business summit.

Farid presented a new foreign direct investment promotion strategy that targets 16 sectors, including pharmaceuticals, automotive manufacturing, electronics, textiles, chemicals, food industries, technology services, tourism and logistics. He said the strategy aims to attract investment, deepen local manufacturing and boost exports.

Farid said the platform would electronically link relevant authorities and unify procedures for approving minutes of general assemblies and boards of directors, reducing processing times. Separately, Planning and Economic Development Minister Ahmed Rostom met the Deputy Chief Executive Officer of the US International Development Finance Corporation (DFC) and invited it to join a World Bank-backed infrastructure project financing guarantee mechanism.

Why it mattersCompanies seeking to add capital in Egypt will face a platform meant to cut approval times and simplify requirements.

Ahmed Helmy's film Hadouta takes top spot at Egypt's box office

The film took LE 3.614 million from 20,200 tickets sold in 60 cinemas on its opening day, Wednesday.

Hadouta (A Tale), starring Ahmed Helmy, claimed the number-one spot at the Egyptian box office on Wednesday, its opening day in theatres. The movie grossed approximately LE 3.614 million from 20,200 tickets sold across 60 cinemas.

The film is Helmy's return to the big screen after an absence of nearly four years, and pairs him for the first time with actress Amina Khalil. Helmy plays a thug who loses his memory and forgets where he hid a witness he had kidnapped, so he turns to a hypnotherapist, played by Khalil, to recover what happened.

During the promotion, Helmy said he consulted psychiatrists to prepare for the role, particularly around the character's childhood trauma and the loss of his parents at an early age. The film was written by Ahmed Abdel Rahman and directed by Mazen Ashraf, and also stars Rahma Ahmed, Khaled al-Sawy and Maged al-Masry.

Why it mattersCinema operators and producers now have a clear opening-day benchmark for Helmy's first film in nearly four years.

Egypt's prime minister links falling Arabic skills in international schools to national...

Mostafa Madbouly called declining Arabic proficiency among students in international schools a challenge to national identity and national security.

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly said declining Arabic proficiency among students in Egypt's international schools is a challenge to national identity and national security, according to remarks reported by Egyptian Streets.

The writer says the concern is understandable because Arabic carries the country's literature, traditions and humour, but argues that many children who struggle with the language are not rejecting their identity. Many have grown up in a globalised world where English is far more necessary in everyday life.

The article cites Ron Darvin, a professor of language and literacy education, who argues that English can act as social and economic capital, shaping perceptions and opportunities. Attitudes towards English are often shaped by socioeconomic background, with more privileged families giving children greater access to the language.

Angola and Egypt mark 50 years of diplomatic ties

Cairo reception celebrates 51 years of Angolan independence and half a century of bilateral relations.

Angola's ambassador to Egypt, Maquento Sebastião Lopes, hosted a reception in Cairo on Wednesday to mark 51 years of Angola’s independence and 50 years of diplomatic ties with Egypt. Lopes credited Presidents João Lourenço and Abdel Fattah al-Sisi with strengthening relations through regular high-level contact and dialogue. The two countries established diplomatic relations in February 1976.

Ambassador Lopes highlighted the institutionalisation of the Angola-Egypt Bilateral Commission as evidence of a shared will to translate strong political relations into tangible benefits. He noted Egypt's historical support for African liberation movements, rooted in pan-Africanism. The reception, held at a Cairo hotel, was attended by Egyptian government members, the diplomatic corps, academics, students, business people, and the Angolan community in Egypt.

The event, held under the slogan “United for a Democratic, One and Indivisible Angola,” screened a 10-minute documentary on the history of relations. Earlier, Lopes and other diplomats laid a wreath at the monument to Angola’s first president, António Agostinho Neto, in Heroes’ Park.

Why it mattersAngola and Egypt are exploring strategic partnerships in agriculture, industry, infrastructure, transport, energy, water, mineral resources, technological innovation, and training.
