Egyptian phone output and exports could help lower prices, commerce official says

Walid Ramadan said imported components, including RAM, raise costs, while Egypt’s market needs about 20 million phones a year.

Walid Ramadan, vice chairman of the Mobile and Telecommunications Division at the Cairo Chamber of Commerce, said on Monday that higher local production and exports could help reduce mobile phone prices in Egypt. He said the domestic market requires about 20 million phones annually.

Ramadan told the al-Hekaya phone-in programme that most phone components are imported and shortages of parts such as RAM can push prices up. Larger production runs would spread costs across more units, helping manufacturers pass the break-even point and lower unit costs, he said.

He urged the Ministry of Industry and Trade to meet manufacturers about production-cost barriers. Ramadan also proposed energy-cost relief, tax breaks or export rebates, and said exports to countries with which Egypt has trade agreements could help expand output.

Why it mattersPhone manufacturers face imported-part costs, and Ramadan urged the Industry and Trade Ministry to discuss cost barriers with them.

Egypt calls for permanent Nile intelligence centre and legal track

Water Minister Hani Sewilam says Ethiopia’s dam operations require notification, data sharing and coordination during droughts and floods.

Egypt is urging a shift in how it manages the Nile dispute with Ethiopia, calling for a permanent centre to assess the basin and the Horn of Africa and a sustained legal and technical effort over damage from unilateral dam operations. The proposals are set out in an Egypt Independent commentary published on 17 November.

The commentary says Water Resources and Irrigation Minister Hani Sewilam has adopted a more direct public position on the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam. It says Egypt accepts Ethiopia’s right to development but rejects unilateral control over a shared watercourse, and says Ethiopia remains bound by duties to notify downstream countries, share data and coordinate during droughts and floods.

A newly announced Egyptian-Sudanese committee is tasked with assessing the effects of unilateral operation and pursuing compensation, the commentary says. It also urges Egypt to engage international banks, sovereign funds and engineering firms before new dams are built, and to develop regional economic projects.

Why it mattersThe Egyptian-Sudanese committee is tasked with documenting the effects of unilateral dam operations and pursuing compensation.

Misr Life Insurance to offer 15% stake on EGX in December

The sale covers 750 million existing shares held by state-owned Misr Insurance Holding, with the price set through a bookbuilding process.

Misr Life Insurance plans to sell 15% of its shares on the Egyptian Exchange, with subscriptions and trading expected to begin in December 2026. The proposed initial public offering comprises 750 million existing ordinary shares held by Misr Insurance Holding Company, which is wholly owned by the Sovereign Fund of Egypt for Investment and Development.

The offer will include a private placement for qualified investors in Egypt and selected jurisdictions outside the United States, plus a public offer open to investors in Egypt. Both portions will have the same per-share price, set through bookbuilding. The selling shareholder’s remaining shares will be subject to lock-up restrictions under exchange rules and customary market practice.

Misr Life Insurance is seeking approvals from the Financial Regulatory Authority, including approval of the prospectus and registration of the shares, as well as approvals from the exchange. The December timetable depends on regulatory clearance and market conditions. EFG Hermes is the sole global coordinator and bookrunner.

Why it mattersInvestors in Egypt can subscribe to the public offer, while qualified investors can join the private placement; the price will be set through bookbuilding.

Egypt seeks Ramsar funding to restore Lake Qarun wetlands

Environment Minister Manal Awad raised funding with Ramsar chief Musonda Mumba to tackle pollution and improve the lake’s ecological condition.

Egypt’s Environment Minister Manal Awad discussed securing funding to restore wetlands and tackle pollution in Egyptian lakes, with particular focus on Lake Qarun, during talks with Ramsar Convention Secretary-General Musonda Mumba at COP31 in Antalya.

Mumba said the convention’s secretariat was seeking closer cooperation with Turkey, which chairs the Mediterranean Wetlands Working Group, to mobilise money for addressing chemical pollution and improving important lakes. She said Lake Qarun provides habitat for migratory birds and livelihoods and jobs, particularly for fishermen and farmers.

Egypt has four wetlands of international importance under the convention: Lakes Bardawil and Burullus, and the Wadi El-Rayan and Qarun protected areas. The country’s 2024–2030 biodiversity strategy sets 21 national targets, including restoring 20% of degraded ecosystems.

Why it mattersFishermen and farmers around Lake Qarun rely on its livelihoods and employment, while the talks focused on funding to address pollution.

Culture · 2026-11-18

Trevor Noah to close UTH youth festival in Cairo

The two-day event at the Grand Egyptian Museum on 20 and 21 November is built around the theme “Work in Progress.”

UTH, a youth culture platform that produces media, community programmes and events for young Egyptians, will hold its third annual festival at the Grand Egyptian Museum in Cairo on 20 and 21 November. Thousands of young people are expected, and comedian Trevor Noah is scheduled to close the weekend in what the organisers say is his first appearance in Egypt.

The festival targets Egyptians aged 18 to 28 who have not yet settled their careers or lives. Its 2026 theme, “Work in Progress”, or taht el-inshaa in Arabic, asks speakers to show drafts, rejections and changes of direction rather than polished success stories.

UTH began as the Youth Summit. Its first edition in 2024 drew more than 2,000 people to the museum, according to the organisers, and the 2025 edition at the American University in Cairo’s New Cairo campus grew to more than 5,000 attendees and over 100 speakers.

Why it mattersEgyptians aged 18 to 28 can attend the two-day programme at the museum, which is free of any entry cost stated in the source and open to the public.
