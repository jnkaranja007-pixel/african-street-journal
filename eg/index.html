Egypt secures $1 billion World Bank loan for economic reforms

The financing supports economic resilience, private sector growth, and a green economy transition.

Egypt signed a USD 1 billion financing agreement with the World Bank on Sunday, November 1, 2026, for the second phase of its Development Policy Financing (DPF) programme. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly witnessed the signing ceremony at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in the New Administrative Capital.

Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty and World Bank Regional Director Alwaleed Alataban signed the deal, titled “Building Resilience, Creating Opportunities and Enhancing Wealth for Egypt’s Prosperity and Stability.” The programme aims to improve the business environment, attract private investment, and boost economic competitiveness.

The reforms include improving state-owned enterprise governance, removing investment barriers, and promoting fair competition. The second phase also incorporates social protection and environmental measures, such as expanding the Takaful and Karama programmes and developing carbon credit markets.

Why it mattersEgypt's economy will receive a USD 1 billion loan to support reforms aimed at strengthening resilience and encouraging private sector investment.

Egypt parliament approves fund for martyrs' families

Amendments to the law governing the Fund for Honoring Martyrs aim to remove obstacles for beneficiaries.

Egypt’s House of Representatives has given final approval to amendments for the Fund for Honoring Martyrs and Victims of Military and Security Operations. The vote took place during a plenary session on Monday. These changes aim to ensure better support and care for families of those killed or injured in the line of duty.

MP Randa Mostafa, Chair of the Committee on Social Solidarity, stated that the amendments are needed to remove obstacles preventing eligible individuals from receiving entitlements. The changes will refine regulations for disability compensation and other provisions, simplifying access to support and compensation for beneficiaries. The fund recognizes the sacrifices of martyred soldiers.

The legislative intervention addresses financial burdens associated with certain medical services and documentation. It also aims to provide greater organization and clarity in procedures for claiming disability compensation. The Fund's operations will be governed by these adjusted provisions.

Why it mattersFamilies of military and security personnel killed or injured in the line of duty will receive improved support and compensation.

Egypt and Sudan reject Ethiopian dam management

Cairo and Khartoum reaffirm 1959 Nile water rights and warn of threats to their water security.

Egypt and Sudan on Monday rejected unilateral actions in the Eastern Nile Basin, stating Ethiopia's management of its Blue Nile dam without a legal framework threatens their water security. The joint statement followed a "2+2" meeting in Cairo between the foreign and irrigation ministers of both nations.

Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty and Water Resources Minister Hani Sewilam met with their Sudanese counterparts Mohi El-Din Salem and Ismat Qurashi. The two countries declared their water security indivisible and reaffirmed their rights under the 1959 Nile Waters Agreement, calling for Ethiopia to cooperate under international law.

They also rejected involving other Nile Basin countries in the dispute, which they stated concerns only Egypt, Sudan, and Ethiopia. Both nations agreed to support their Permanent Joint Technical Commission for Nile Waters and discussed development projects in the southern Nile Basin, provided they meet technical standards and are agreed upon.

Why it mattersEgypt and Sudan's water security is threatened by Ethiopia's dam management without a binding legal agreement.

Egypt plans spending cuts, reserve boosts amid regional tensions

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly announced measures to rationalise government spending and increase strategic commodity stockpiles.

Egypt is preparing to cut government spending and increase its strategic commodity reserves to counter potential economic fallout from escalating regional geopolitical tensions. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly announced these measures on Monday evening during a meeting of the Central Crisis Management Committee in the New Administrative Capital. The government aims to ease the impact of the crisis on local and global markets by boosting goods and product inventories and reducing state consumption of fuel and energy.

Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk and Planning and Economic Development Minister Ahmed Rostom are finalising a strategy to rationalise public expenditure and maximise state resource efficiency. Minister of Supply and Internal Trade Sherif Farouk stated that Egypt’s strategic reserves of various commodities are sufficient for an average of six months, with new contracts signed to further boost inventories. An initiative to reduce prices of basic goods currently offers 30 commodities at 15% to 25% discounts.

The crisis management meeting included Central Bank Governor Hassan Abdalla, ministers of electricity, agriculture, petroleum, information, and industry, alongside deputy ministers and military officials. The proposed plan guarantees the continuation of economic and productive activities and the availability of financial allocations for basic commodities and petroleum materials.

Why it mattersEgyptians will be affected by government spending cuts and potential price changes on basic goods.

Egypt plans new rules for social media creators

Government met with 70 influencers to discuss a framework for digital content and professional standards.

Egypt's Minister of State for Information Diaa Rashwan met with about 70 content creators and influencers on Sunday, November 1, to discuss regulating the digital content sector. The meeting explored establishing a framework, defining professional standards, and improving access to official information.

This discussion follows recent Cabinet recommendations for new measures against harmful social media practices, including increased fines for false news and a digital safety law. Creators proposed forming an Egyptian umbrella organization to set ethical standards and define professional creators.

Rashwan stated the government will not create a syndicate for influencers but supports their formation of an association. The ministry is exploring ways to support content creators, aiming to distinguish professionals from general users.
