Egypt Launches Digital System for Foster Care Applications

The Ministry of Social Solidarity has introduced a new digital platform to automate and streamline the process for foster family and care institution applications.

Egypt's Ministry of Social Solidarity, in partnership with the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology, has launched the initial phase of an integrated digital services system. This new system automates applications and procedures for alternative foster families and care institutions. Citizens can now submit applications electronically through the ministry’s digital portal and track their progress through vetting and verification stages to the final decision. The initiative aims to enhance digital transformation and citizen services.

Jacqueline Mamdouh, head of the central administration for social care, stated that the digital services were developed in coordination between social care and digital transformation teams to ensure alignment with work procedures and beneficiary needs. Mahmoud Saber, manager of the social care services platform, described the first phase as a significant step in digitizing social care services, re-engineering the citizen’s journey into interconnected digital services with electronic integration to relevant government systems. This simplifies procedures and enhances data accuracy.

Hala Emam, director of social protection programmes, explained that the platform is electronically linked with various state entities through a government digital switch, facilitating electronic data exchange and verification. This reduces the need for paper documents and connects to the digital identity system for secure login. The ministries noted that the system will improve transparency and application tracking efficiency, urging citizens to use the digital services available.

Why it mattersCitizens seeking to become foster families are affected by a new digital system that simplifies and automates their application process, increasing transparency and efficiency.

Central Bank Warns Customers Against Sharing One-Time Passwords

The Central Bank of Egypt issued an urgent warning to bank customers about the risks associated with sharing One-Time Password codes used for transaction verification.

The Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) has alerted bank customers to the dangers of sharing their One-Time Password (OTP) codes, emphasizing that disclosing these security codes can lead to significant risks for their transactions and bank accounts. This awareness campaign was launched via the CBE's official Facebook page, urging users to remain vigilant against fraudulent attempts targeting the verification codes sent to mobile phones, which are essential for confirming payments.

OTP codes are critical security features used in banking transactions, sent to a user's phone to confirm payments. These codes are intended solely for the account holder and are valid for a single use, forming a vital part of transaction security. The CBE highlighted that sharing these codes could allow unauthorized individuals to make purchases, access bank accounts, or transfer funds in the account holder's name, thereby exposing users to scams and financial loss.

The bank stressed the importance of keeping OTP codes confidential, noting that they are sent to the phone number linked to the user's account specifically to confirm payments. Sharing this code with anyone else could enable them to conduct transactions using the account holder's credentials. The CBE's warning aims to prevent users from becoming victims of fraud by ensuring they understand the sensitive nature of OTPs and the potential consequences of their disclosure.

Why it mattersBank customers are affected because sharing their OTP codes can lead to unauthorized transactions, purchases, and fund transfers from their accounts.

Egypt Issues 907 Licenses, Funds National Veal Project

The Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation issued operating permits for livestock and feed projects while allocating new financing for cattle farmers in August.

Egypt's Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation issued 907 operating licenses for livestock, poultry, feed, and dairy collection projects in August. The ministry also approved EGP 59.850m in new financing for the National Veal Project, which will support 47 beneficiaries raising 855 head of cattle. This brings the total funding for the project to over EGP 11.514bn, benefiting 46,398 small breeders, graduates, and women across initiative villages and covering more than 543,417 head of cattle.

Of the 907 licenses, 167 were for small breeders engaged in livestock rearing. The ministry also granted 28 technical approvals for new animal and poultry production projects in desert areas to ensure biosecurity. In addition, 777 registrations for feed mixtures, additives, and concentrates were approved, comprising 416 local and 361 imported registrations, aimed at reducing production costs and improving final product quality. The sector also supervised the destruction of 2,304 tonnes and 1,200 litres of non-compliant imported feed additives.

The ministry organized eight extension seminars and practical training sessions to transfer modern feeding and care practices to breeders. During August, Egypt also exported fish feed, feed additives, frozen poultry, table eggs, and frozen birds to several Arab and foreign markets. Surprise inspection campaigns continued on factories and warehouses to confront monopolies and the withholding of goods.

Why it mattersLivestock farmers and feed producers are directly affected by the issuance of operating licenses, technical approvals, and feed registrations, which aim to reduce costs and improve quality.

Egypt Explores Solar Power and Green Finance for Development

The Ministry of Local Development and Environment is considering expanding solar power and integrating energy efficiency into key sectors.

Egypt is exploring the expansion of renewable energy, particularly solar power, and the integration of energy-efficiency technologies into its agricultural, industrial, and service sectors. These initiatives are part of a broader strategy to reduce energy consumption and emissions, according to the Ministry of Local Development and Environment. Discussions involving government officials, civil society, the private sector, and academic institutions focused on clean energy investment, green financing, and support for small and medium-sized enterprises.

Local Development and Environment Minister Manal Awad emphasized that a just energy transition must encompass more than just clean energy sources. It requires greater energy efficiency, reduced consumption, and stronger local community participation, while maintaining a balance between environmental, economic, and social factors. Awad highlighted the crucial role of civil society organizations in promoting energy-saving practices and building local capacity due to their direct engagement with communities.

Assistant Minister for Sustainability and Social Responsibility Saber Osman noted that rising temperatures linked to climate change are increasing energy demand, underscoring the need for more efficient and resilient energy systems and wider adoption of renewable technologies. He stressed that collaborative efforts among government, businesses, civil society, universities, and research institutions are vital for developing affordable, scalable local solutions. Participants called for a move from identifying challenges to implementing practical interventions.

Why it mattersEgypt's plans for expanding solar power and green finance will affect local development by integrating energy efficiency into key sectors.

Egypt Meteorological Authority Forecasts Temperature Drop and Fog

Temperatures will range from 30C to 42C across Egypt until September 3, with fog expected on morning roads.

Egypt will experience a drop in temperatures across most regions from Saturday until Thursday, September 3, according to the Egyptian Meteorological Authority (EMA). Slightly hot and humid conditions are anticipated during early morning hours nationwide. The EMA predicts maximum temperatures on the northern coast will be between 30-31C, while Cairo and Lower Egypt will see highs of 34-35C. Northern Upper Egypt is expected to reach 35-37C, with southern Upper Egypt experiencing the highest temperatures, ranging from 39-42C.

Throughout the day, the weather will be hot and humid across most of the country, becoming very hot in southern Upper Egypt. Nights will remain slightly hot and humid. Weather maps indicate fog formation between four and eight am on roads leading to and from northern Egypt, including Greater Cairo, Suez Canal cities, central Sinai, and northern Upper Egypt. Drivers are advised to exercise caution due to reduced visibility.

Occasional wind activity is expected on Sunday across most of the country, potentially stirring up sand and dust in southern Egypt. There is also a chance of light rainfall on Sunday in parts of the northern coastal governorates and northern Lower Egypt. The EMA stated it is monitoring weather developments and urged citizens to follow daily updates.

Why it mattersFarmers may be affected by potential fog reducing visibility during early morning fieldwork and light rainfall in northern areas.
