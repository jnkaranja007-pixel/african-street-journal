Health · 2026-08-27

Health Ministry confirms no Salmonella outbreak in Egypt

Dr. Khaled Abdel Ghaffar, Minister of Health, stated that Egypt has not recorded any unusual Salmonella outbreaks despite international incidents.

The Egyptian Ministry of Health confirmed today that there is no Salmonella outbreak in the country, according to Minister of Health Dr. Khaled Abdel Ghaffar. The ministry is actively monitoring local and international health conditions through event-based epidemiological surveillance systems, specifically targeting foodborne illnesses like Salmonella.

Dr. Amr Kandil, Deputy Minister of Health, further noted that the Preventive Medicine and Public Health sector tracks daily changes in infection patterns and assesses reports based on established scientific standards. He confirmed that no unusual Salmonella outbreaks connected to recent international events have been recorded nationally.

This confirmation comes as the ministry reviews international incidents, including over 306 cases of Salmonella Enteritidis in Belgium and 207 confirmed cases in the United Kingdom, which led to product recalls. The ministry advises the public to follow essential food safety practices, such as thorough hand washing, separating raw and cooked foods, and purchasing items from trusted sources.

Why it mattersEgyptian residents are directly affected by the confirmation that there is no current Salmonella outbreak, ensuring public health and safety regarding foodborne illnesses.

Egypt targets doubling oil and condensate output, advances $4bn refinery projects

Egypt aims to double its crude oil and condensate production through a five-year energy strategy while implementing six refinery projects with investments exceeding $4bn.

Egypt's presidency announced a new five-year energy strategy to double the country's crude oil and condensate output. President Abdel Fattah Al-Sisi reviewed the plans with Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly and Petroleum and Mineral Resources Minister Karim Badawi in New Alamein. The strategy includes seismic surveys, advanced technologies, and new contractual frameworks to develop discoveries and strengthen Egypt's position as a regional hub for petroleum product trading, gas liquefaction, and exports.

The government is also advancing six refinery projects with investments over $4bn to modernize existing facilities and increase domestic production of higher-value petroleum products. This initiative aims to reduce the country’s fuel import bill. Badawi also outlined plans for new wells expected to come online by December 2026 and development drilling in the Mediterranean.

Egypt seeks to attract further investment from international energy companies, having cleared overdue payments to foreign partners and maintaining regular payments on current obligations. Al-Sisi directed the government to provide more incentives for international partners to expand exploration and field development, and to increase the mining sector's contribution to the national economy.

Why it mattersThe energy strategy and refinery projects aim to increase domestic production of petroleum products, which affects the availability and cost of fuel for businesses and consumers.

Climate · 2026-08-27

Egypt urges global drought protocol ahead of COP18 presidency

Egypt's Water Resources and Irrigation Minister Hani Sewilam called for a legally binding international drought protocol at UNCCD COP17 in Mongolia.

Egypt has advocated for a legally binding international protocol on drought, stating that land degradation, desertification, and drought are direct threats to water and food security. Minister Hani Sewilam made this call during the ministerial segment of the 17th Conference of the Parties to the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD COP17) in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia.

Sewilam emphasized that drought poses a serious threat to countries with acute water scarcity, such as Egypt, where available water resources are below the absolute water scarcity threshold and over 95% of the country is desert. He urged a shift from crisis response to prevention and preparedness, including stronger early-warning systems and integrated water and land resource management.

The minister also called for increased financing, clearer institutional mechanisms, and innovative financial instruments to support countries and communities most affected by drought and land degradation, particularly developing and African nations. He stressed the importance of restoring degraded land to strengthen food security, protect biodiversity, and preserve livelihoods.

Why it mattersThe call for a global drought protocol directly affects countries like Egypt by seeking to establish international frameworks and financial support for water and food security, which impacts the daily lives of its citizens.

British University in Egypt hosts All-Africa University Games torch relay

The British University in Egypt (BUE) in El Shorouk City hosted the torch relay for the 12th All-Africa University Games, “Cairo 2026,” on Wednesday.

The torch relay ceremony at BUE featured Olympic champion and university graduate Feryal Abdelaziz carrying the flame. This event is part of a national journey for the “Cairo 2026” torch, which started in the New Administrative Capital and is visiting major universities across Egypt, including The American University in Cairo and Cairo University.

The Games, held from 8 to 16 September 2026, mark the first time Egypt has hosted the continent’s largest multi-sport university competition. Approximately 2,000 student-athletes from 59 universities across 16 African countries will compete in 18 sports, with para-athletes included for the first time.

The ceremony was led by President and Vice-Chancellor Professor Mohamed Loutfi MBE, and attended by Professor Ashraf Sobhy, President of the Federation of Africa University Sports. The relay aligns with directives from Egyptian President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi to showcase Egypt's standing in university sport.

Why it mattersThe event directly affects university students and athletes across 16 African countries by providing a major competitive platform and promoting sports alongside academic pursuits.

Sport · 2026-08-27

Egypt orders legal action over wrestler's escape in Rome

The Egyptian Ministry of Youth and Sports referred wrestler Mohamed Mustafa Ali El-Shami's defection during a Rome layover to the Public Prosecution for legal action.

The Egyptian Ministry of Youth and Sports has initiated legal action against wrestler Mohamed Mustafa Ali El-Shami following his defection during a transit layover in Rome. This referral to the Public Prosecution comes after El-Shami slipped away from his delegation while traveling from Taranto to Cairo. The delegation leader's attempt to retrieve him failed, leading the team coach to remain in Rome while the rest of the team returned home.

A separate case involving wrestler Ziad Haggag, who defected during the World Junior Wrestling Championships in Slovakia, has also been forwarded to prosecutorial authorities. The Ministry had previously instructed the Egyptian Wrestling Federation to investigate Haggag's disappearance and take appropriate legal measures. These actions follow preliminary investigations that indicated negligence and inadequate supervision of delegation members.

In response, the Egyptian Olympic Committee suspended Ibrahim Adel Ibrahim Mustafa Al-Attar, head of the wrestling delegation, and referred him to the Ethics Committee. Ministry officials emphasized that these incidents should not overshadow Egypt's strong performance at the Mediterranean Games, where the national wrestling team secured seven medals, including gold medals for Samar Hamza and Mohamed Hassan.

Why it mattersEgyptian athletes and sports officials are affected by the legal and disciplinary consequences of defection, impacting their careers and reputations.
