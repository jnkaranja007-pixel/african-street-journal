Egypt captured most of Europe's fertilizer market as its gas kept flowing

Egyptian factories supplied more than 60 percent of the fertilizer that reached European markets during the March to September season.

Industry Minister Khaled Hashem said on Saturday, 14 November, that Egypt supplied more than 60 percent of the fertilizer that entered European markets during the last agricultural season, which ran from March to September. Export prices for fertilizer roughly doubled over that period, rising from about USD 400 to 450 a ton to nearly USD 900, which the minister's own figures put at EGP 48,151 a ton.

Hashem made the remarks in an interview on Amr Adib's talk show Al-Hekaya on MBC Masr. Cairo kept natural gas supplies to factories running and raised their minimum gas price, without rationing it as several other countries did. Egyptian farmers, meanwhile, paid more to grow their crops.

The minister also announced Egypt's first Industrial Investment Fund, drawing on state and private-sector investors, aimed at moving money out of real estate and gold and into factories. A new Manufacturer Support Unit will review struggling plants one by one, and estimates of how many are struggling range from about 7,400 to 25,000.

Why it mattersFactories that depend on gas must now pay a higher minimum price, and the new fund and support unit will decide which struggling plants get help, starting with the review process the minister outlined.

MP questions Red Sea land handover to Finance Ministry

Mohamed Fouad says 112 million square meters of Red Sea land has been handed to the ministry to cut public debt, despite no clear results from an earlier Ras Shuqair allocation.

Mohamed Fouad, a member of parliament's Economic Affairs Committee and an economist, said on Saturday that state land should not be allocated to cut public debt without a clear plan. Speaking on the TV show al-Hekaya, hosted by Amr Adib, he questioned whether the practice actually solves the problem or only delays it.

Fouad said a presidential decree issued on June 4, 2025, gave the Finance Ministry 174 million square meters at Ras Shuqair to reduce debt and support sovereign Sukuk, or Islamic bonds. 17 months later, he said, no operational plan or preliminary framework exists. The newly allocated Red Sea area, at 112 million square meters, is about 60 percent the size of the Ras Shuqair land.

Fouad has submitted a formal inquiry seeking details on the Red Sea allocation, the status of Ras Shuqair, and the funds generated so far. He also asked how Sukuk returns would be paid, whether from land revenue or general public resources, and said the House of Representatives can hold the government and the minister accountable.

Why it mattersParliament can now demand answers from the minister on how 112 million square meters of Red Sea land will be used and how any bond returns will be paid.

Egypt starts service of first two Alstom trains on Cairo Metro Line 1

The French-built trains are the first deliveries under a contract to supply 55 trains for the country's oldest metro line.

Two new air-conditioned trains have entered service on Cairo Metro Line 1, the first deliveries under a contract with the French firm Alstom for 55 trains. Transport Minister Kamel Al-Wazir rode one of them from Saray El-Qobba station to Sadat station, passing eight intermediate stations, to check its technical specifications and passenger facilities.

The trains come with secure connections between carriages, CCTV cameras in the driver cabins and passenger carriages, LCD passenger information screens and spaces for wheelchair users. Al-Wazir said the trains are a major step towards modernising the line and improving efficiency, comfort and safety. The contract, which covers 495 carriages in total, also includes eight years of maintenance and spare parts.

Line 1 runs 44 kilometres between Helwan and New Marg and serves 35 stations. Alongside the new trains, 23 existing trains are being rehabilitated with a Spanish company, and a consortium of Egyptian and French companies is upgrading signalling, communications, track and power supply. The ministry is preparing to tender works for a new Sheikh Mansour station between Ezbet El-Nakhl and New Marg.

Why it mattersCairo Metro Line 1 passengers will ride the new trains as the rest of the 55-train fleet is delivered over the coming years under the contract.

Egypt and Kuwait sign agreements at joint committee

The 14th session of the Egyptian-Kuwaiti Joint Committee ended with several agreements and memoranda of understanding signed in Kuwait on Sunday.

Egypt's Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty and his Kuwaiti counterpart, Sheikh Jarrah Jaber Al-Ahmad Al-Sabah, co-chaired the 14th session of the Egyptian-Kuwaiti Joint Committee in Kuwait on Sunday. The session ended with the signing of several agreements and memoranda of understanding in priority areas of co-operation, though the foreign ministry did not detail them.

Abdelatty called for more trade, two-way investment and closer ties between the two business communities, and said Egypt would remove obstacles faced by Kuwaiti investors. In a separate meeting, he met Kuwait's Crown Prince Sheikh Sabah Khalid Al-Hamad Al-Sabah. Egypt's foreign ministry spokesman, Ambassador Nader Zaki, confirmed the meeting.

The two delegations signed the political and technical minutes of the committee, which the ministry described as a road map for co-operation until the next session, due in Cairo in 2028. The two sides also discussed the Palestinian issue, the crises in Sudan and Yemen, and the security of navigation in the Gulf, the Red Sea and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, and agreed to keep co-ordinating.

Why it mattersKuwaiti investors and Egyptian workers now have a framework of signed agreements, with the next joint session set for Cairo in 2028.

Spotted hyena returns to Egypt's Red Sea Governorate

Villagers in Abu Ramad found mutilated sheep, signaling the return of a predator unseen for over five millennia.

In the village of Abu Ramad, near Jabal Elba in Egypt’s Red Sea Governorate, mutilated sheep have signaled the return of the spotted hyena. This predator, absent from the region for over five millennia, reappeared in 2024. Local shepherds discovered two sheep killed and partially eaten, noting the large bite marks and a predator footprint larger than a dog's.

Faisal Ohag, an English teacher in Halaib, reported the incident, which prompted shepherds to rally locals. They tracked the predator's footprints by car, motorcycle, and camel to a cave in Jabal Elba. Aldoushy Mahdy, an Associate Professor at Al-Azhar University, stated there have been no further sightings since 2024.

The return of the spotted hyena, once hunted by ancient Egyptians, presents a challenge for modern villagers who no longer know how to coexist with such wildlife. The large predator's presence has raised alarms in the community.

Why it mattersVillagers in Abu Ramad are now facing the challenge of living alongside a predator not seen in their region for over five millennia.
