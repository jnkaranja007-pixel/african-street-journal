Cyberattack caused Cairo billboard porn

Cybersecurity expert Haitham Salah-Eddin said a Brazilian national likely hacked the system managing ads in Egypt and Brazil.

A cybersecurity expert revealed that pornographic content displayed on a Cairo billboard was the result of a cyberattack on an international company managing advertising systems, not a breach of the billboard itself. Haitham Salah-Eddin explained on Saturday that the Egyptian company owning the screen had leased it to an international firm that manages advertising across four countries, including Brazil. The hacker gained remote control over content displayed on screens in these nations.

Salah-Eddin stated on MBC Masr that recent information suggests a Brazilian national is behind the hack. He noted this type of attack has occurred in Germany and England this year, with motives varying from seeking attention to demanding financial rewards. Some attacks, like one in France, carry political messages intended to erode public trust in government safety measures.

The expert warned that cyberattacks are expanding beyond websites to internet-connected devices, citing an incident in France where 700 smart refrigerators were hacked, causing food spoilage. This incident affected stores across France and other European countries, following a similar event in August.

Why it mattersEgyptians saw pornographic content on a Cairo billboard due to a cyberattack on an international advertising management system.

Egypt to power 7,000 factories with 1,000MW solar energy

The 'Shams El-Senaa' initiative aims to cut industrial electricity costs and boost competitiveness.

Egypt plans to install 1,000 megawatts of solar power capacity to supply approximately 7,000 factories through the "Shams El-Senaa" initiative. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly reviewed the plan, which aims to expand renewable energy use and lower industrial electricity expenses.

Madbouly stated that President Abdel Fattah Al-Sisi supports the initiative as part of efforts to diversify energy sources and reduce production costs. Minister of Electricity and Renewable Energy Mahmoud Esmat said the programme will lower factory electricity bills and natural gas use, improving production efficiency.

The initiative also intends to support local manufacturing of renewable energy components and increase local content. This plan aligns with Egypt's national energy strategy to boost renewable sources in its energy mix and improve electricity consumption efficiency.

Why it mattersAround 7,000 factories will see reduced electricity costs and improved production efficiency.

Egypt launches free rabies vaccination for stray dogs

The week-long campaign aims to eliminate rabies nationwide by 2030 and includes public awareness activities.

Egypt’s General Authority for Veterinary Services began a nationwide campaign on Sunday, November 1, to vaccinate stray dogs against rabies at no cost.

The initiative, running for one week across all governorates, is part of Egypt’s strategy to eliminate rabies by 2030. Field teams will work with authorities to vaccinate dogs and conduct public awareness activities about rabies risks and safe interaction with dogs.

The authority urged citizens to assist vaccination teams, report suspected rabies cases or bites, and seek immediate medical attention. Citizens can also submit reports via local veterinary directorates or by calling the hotline at 19561.

Why it mattersThe campaign directly affects public health by working to eliminate rabies transmission from stray dogs.

Amr Adib questions Egypt's stray dog crisis response

Media personality highlights ongoing debate and lack of solutions after a child's death.

Media personality Amr Adib on Sunday addressed Egypt's stray dog problem, days after a young girl was mauled to death by dogs in late October. On his MBC Masr program 'al-Hekaya,' Adib questioned the lack of progress in finding a solution to the persistent issue.

Adib described a societal split over the stray dogs, with one side opposing culling and the other viewing the animals as a danger, particularly to children. He asked if a middle ground or a clear solution exists, noting that funds for management are seen as wasted until a tragedy occurs.

The debate over stray dogs in Egypt has resurfaced following the fatal attack on the girl. Adib's program highlighted the ongoing public discussion and the absence of a definitive resolution to the problem.

Why it mattersEgyptian citizens, especially children, are at risk of fatal dog attacks.
