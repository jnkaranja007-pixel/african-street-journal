Egypt train crash kills seven, injures 125

Two passenger trains collided in Al-Ayyat, Giza, on Sunday, leaving seven dead and 125 injured.

Seven people died and 125 were injured when two passenger trains collided in Al-Ayyat, Giza, on Sunday. Train number 134, a Tahya Misr service from Giza to Fayoum, struck the rear of train number 2010, which was travelling from Cairo to Aswan, between Kafr Ammar and El Reqqa stations, causing several carriages to derail.

Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar reported that 72 ambulances participated in the evacuation, taking 132 injured individuals to hospitals. Al-Wasta Central Hospital admitted 87 cases, including three fatalities, while Al-Ayyat Central Hospital received 34 and Atfih Central Hospital took 11, including four deaths. The public prosecution has taken possession of the bodies.

Islam Azzam, chairman of the Financial Regulatory Authority, ordered the railway insurance pool to speed up compensation payments, raising the payout for deaths and total permanent disability from EGP30,000 to EGP50,000. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly directed officials to provide full support to the victims’ families. Emergency teams worked to clear wreckage and restore services on the Cairo-High Dam line.

Why it mattersSeven people were killed and 125 injured in a train collision in Egypt's Giza governorate, with compensation payouts increased for victims.

Egypt border guards killed in eastern desert operation

Two officers died during a raid targeting illegal gold mining in the Eastern Desert.

Two Egyptian military officers were killed during operations against illegal gold mining in the Eastern Desert. Egyptian Military Spokesperson Colonel Ahmed Othman Ali announced the deaths on Thursday, 5 November.

Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Jordan, along with the Arab League, issued condemnations of the attack on Egyptian border guard forces. Statements released on Friday, 6 November, expressed solidarity with Egypt and offered condolences to the families of the deceased officers.

The Arab League affirmed its support for Egypt's efforts to protect its security and territorial integrity, calling Egypt's stability a pillar of Arab national security. Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs also affirmed its solidarity with Egypt in confronting security threats.

Why it mattersThe deaths of two officers during security operations in the Eastern Desert were condemned by Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan, and the Arab League.

National Bank of Egypt raises certificate yields

The bank increased annual rates by one percentage point on Platinum certificates to 18.75% and 18.85%.

The National Bank of Egypt (NBE) has increased the annual yield on its fixed-rate Platinum certificates of deposit by one percentage point. The new rate for monthly payouts is 18.75 percent, up from 17.75 percent, and for quarterly payouts it is 18.85 percent, up from 17.85 percent. NBE CEO Mohamed al-Etreby announced the Asset and Liability Committee approved the adjustment, which is effective Sunday, November 8.

The higher yields are available on Platinum certificates with monthly interest payouts, now yielding LE 18,750 annually on an LE 100,000 investment, and on those with quarterly payouts, yielding LE 18,850 annually on the same investment. Certificates with quarterly payouts offer LE 100 more per LE 100,000 annually, while monthly payouts provide more frequent access to returns.

This move follows the Central Bank of Egypt’s Monetary Policy Committee decision on October 29 to raise key interest rates by 100 basis points. The CBE increased its overnight deposit rate to 20 percent, its lending rate to 21 percent, and its main operation and discount rates to 20.5 percent.

Why it mattersEgyptian savers will earn more interest on their Platinum certificates of deposit.

Egypt's Kayan University to open February 2027

The new institution will accommodate 25,000 students across six faculties, including computing and AI.

Egypt's Kayan University will open its doors in February 2027, with President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi announcing the new institution will have capacity for approximately 25,000 students. The university, first announced by the Egyptian Armed Forces in July 2026, aims to expand educational opportunities in scientific and technological fields. It will offer six faculties: medicine, dentistry, physiotherapy, pharmacy, engineering, and computing and artificial intelligence.

President El-Sisi stated the university will seek partnerships with international institutions to enhance its academic programmes and provide students with global expertise. The Tahya Misr Fund will cover tuition for academically outstanding students who maintain their performance. The Minister of Higher Education and Scientific Research was instructed to consider allowing the top 10 Thanaweya Amma students to transfer to Kayan University.

The university's expansion follows significant civilian interest, according to the president. El-Sisi made these remarks on Sunday, 8 November, at the third medical conference of the Armed Forces College of Medicine in the New Capital.

Why it mattersEgyptian students will have new higher education opportunities in scientific and technological fields, with potential for international collaboration and financial aid for top performers.

Egypt's Industrial Development Bank posts first EGP 1bn profit

The lender's restructuring plan, launched mid-2024, led to a 47% rise in profit before tax and provisions.

Egypt’s Industrial Development Bank (IDB) achieved a net profit of EGP 1bn in 2025, a first for the institution. Shareholders will receive bonus shares for the first time in 20 years following the approval of the 2025 accounts. Management attributed this success to a restructuring plan initiated in mid-2024 aimed at strengthening the bank's industry financing role.

Chief executive Hussein Refaie stated that the bank has transitioned from addressing structural issues to achieving sustainable growth, supported by a clear strategy. Total assets grew by 31% to EGP 64.9bn, while customer deposits increased by 44% to EGP 52.1bn by the end of 2025. Operating income rose 33% to EGP 3.6bn.

Non-performing loans decreased to 8.1% of total lending from 13.9% a year prior, with provision coverage increasing to 126%. IDB plans to finance strategic projects in industry, energy, urban development, and clean power to diversify income. The bank also plans new branches and technology upgrades, investing EGP 500mn in 2025 as part of a five-year EGP 3bn technology program.

Why it mattersEgypt's Industrial Development Bank achieved its first EGP 1bn profit in 2025 due to a restructuring plan, leading to increased assets, deposits, and loans.
