Egypt showcases water scarcity solutions at UN desertification conference

Egypt's Water Resources and Irrigation Minister Hani Sewilam presented the country's strategies for efficient water use and climate adaptation at COP17 in Mongolia.

Egypt is sharing its methods for addressing water scarcity and adapting to climate change at the 17th Conference of the Parties to the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD COP17) in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. Minister Hani Sewilam highlighted the interconnectedness of water, land, climate, and food security, advocating for integrated policies that maximize natural resource benefits and support sustainable management.

Sewilam emphasized moving beyond crisis response to strengthen preparedness and resilience against climate pressures. He detailed Egypt's experience, including efforts to improve water-use efficiency, expand agricultural drainage water reuse, and implement climate adaptation projects. The country is also increasing its use of technology and innovation to manage its limited water resources.

During the conference, Sewilam is scheduled to participate in several ministerial dialogues to present Egypt’s approach to drought and the links between water, land, and climate. Egypt's participation also aims to mobilize financing and investment for vulnerable countries to strengthen their resilience and implement adaptation measures against drought, land degradation, and water scarcity.

Why it mattersThe event directly affects countries vulnerable to drought and land degradation by seeking to mobilize financing and investment for adaptation measures.

Egypt regulator suspends consumer finance firm over data breach

Egypt's Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) has initiated criminal proceedings and imposed a one-month ban on a consumer finance company for exploiting parents' data to issue.

The Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) has taken action against a consumer finance company, revoking a chief executive’s license and suspending the firm from new contracts for one month. This follows an investigation into reports of the company exploiting parents’ data to issue unauthorized loans for an international school, affecting parents' credit ratings.

The FRA's intensive four-day investigation, triggered by circulating reports and official complaints, found the company in breach of regulatory decisions regarding customer data inquiries, corporate governance, credit controls, and anti-money laundering protocols. Technical teams conducted on-site inspections and direct inquiries, leading to the cancellation of financial and credit liabilities wrongfully attached to affected parents.

In addition to the one-month ban on new contracts, the company is suspended from dealing in finance products related to school fees and club memberships until a comprehensive regulatory review is completed. The FRA also applied penal measures against several key employees and ordered the company’s general assembly to convene to address violations and enforce stricter internal controls.

Why it mattersThis event affects consumers by protecting them from unauthorized financial liabilities and impacts businesses by enforcing stricter data protection and corporate governance standards.

Egypt overtakes European nations in Mediterranean Games medal standings

Egyptian athletes secured 13 medals, including four gold, four silver, and five bronze, placing the country fifth overall at the Mediterranean Games in Taranto.

Egypt has climbed to fifth place in the overall medal standings at the Mediterranean Games in Taranto, surpassing Spain, France, Croatia, and Serbia. The Egyptian delegation, comprising 196 athletes, has earned 13 medals across swimming, fencing, and wrestling, demonstrating a focus on a strong rather than large delegation.

The 13 medals include four gold, four silver, and five bronze. Notable performances include gold medals from Abdelrahman Sameh in 50m butterfly, Mohamed al-Sayed in épée, Mohamed Hamza in foil, and Mohamed Hassan in 67kg wrestling. Egypt also maintains its lead in the Arab medal standings, five medals ahead of Tunisia.

This performance is attributed to close coordination between Olympic champion and Minister of Youth and Sports Gohar Nabil, Egyptian Olympic Committee President Yasser Idris, and national sports federations. The strategy emphasizes producing champions across multiple disciplines, rather than simply increasing the number of participating athletes.

Why it mattersEgyptian athletes and sports federations are directly affected by the results and recognition from these international competitions.

Private Sector Secures 60% of Investments as Economic Risk Falls

Egypt's Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk announced that the private sector now accounts for nearly 60% of total investments, driven by improving economic indicators.

The private sector in Egypt now contributes almost 60% of the country's total investments, according to Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk. He reported on Monday that international risk assessments have reached their lowest levels since 2014, reflecting positive economic changes. Kouchouk briefed Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly on the finance ministry's portfolio, highlighting the impact of state-led economic reforms on investor confidence and the overall investment climate.

The minister noted strong activity in the industrial, telecommunications, information technology, and tourism sectors, positioning the private sector as a key partner in economic development. The government plans to offer additional tax and customs incentives to support corporate expansion, stimulate production, and boost employment rates. These measures aim to enhance investment flows and attract foreign capital.

To improve tax administration, Kouchouk outlined plans for expanding premium tax centers and accelerating digital integration. A new “Real Estate Dispositions” application has launched, allowing citizens to complete property transactions online, from data registration to tax payment and clearance, reducing the need for physical office visits. The ministry is committed to modernizing tax systems and partnering with the business community to improve service quality and compliance.

Why it mattersBusinesses and individuals in Egypt will experience changes in tax procedures and investment opportunities due to new government incentives and digital services.

Egypt and WFP launch market efficiency index to enhance food security

Egypt's Ministry of Supply and Internal Trade and the World Food Programme have agreed to a joint action plan until July 2027 to develop a comprehensive market efficiency index.

The Ministry of Supply and Internal Trade and the World Food Programme (WFP) have launched a joint action plan to develop a comprehensive market efficiency index, shifting state market monitoring from basic observation to proactive data analysis. Minister Sherif Farouk stated the ministry is upgrading its tools to rely on more accurate and integrated data, enabling it to monitor price movements, commodity availability, and regional disparities.

This new index will expand the ministry’s focus beyond mere price tracking to measure commodity availability, ease of access for citizens, regional price variations, and quality levels. This broader scope aims to provide a comprehensive market view, enhancing the ministry’s ability to detect early signs of disruptions and make timely decisions. The agreement was discussed during talks between Farouk and WFP Country Director Rawad Halabi.

The action plan also includes developing an integrated system for citizen complaints, tracking issues from receipt to resolution, and analyzing data to identify recurring problems and improve services for subsidy beneficiaries. Specialised training programmes on data and indicators will be conducted in cooperation with the Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics to evaluate the subsidy system’s contribution to meeting nutritional needs.

Why it mattersThis initiative affects Egyptian citizens by aiming to improve food security, stabilize prices, and enhance the efficiency of the subsidy system, particularly for vulnerable groups.
