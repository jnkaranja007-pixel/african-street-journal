Minister Inspects High-Speed Rail Ahead of Trial Operations

Egypt's Transport Minister inspected progress on the Ain Sokhna to Alamein high-speed rail line, preparing for upcoming trial runs.

Transport Minister Kamel al-Wazir inspected the first line of Egypt’s high-speed electric rail network on Sunday, August 30, 2026. The 660 km line connects Ain Sokhna on the Red Sea to Matrouh on the Mediterranean. Al-Wazir reviewed progress on the section between Ain Sokhna station and Central Capital station, focusing on final finishing work, passenger flow, and administrative and commercial use of station space. He also inspected a rail connection to Ain Sokhna Port designed to facilitate freight transport.

The minister visited a 259,000 square meter maintenance facility and a main workshop covering 578 feddans, which will service all three planned high-speed lines. He also reviewed track sections being prepared for ballast, rails, and overhead catenary systems by the Siemens-Orascom Construction-Arab Contractors consortium. Trial operations without passengers are expected to begin on a section of the first line in the coming months, with further trials to follow the project's timetable.

Egypt is developing a 2,250km high-speed electric rail network, aiming for a combined capacity of approximately 4 million daily passengers across railways and high-speed trains by 2030. The first three lines, spanning 2,000km, are under construction, including the Ain Sokhna–Marsa Matrouh line. A fourth line is planned in partnership with the private sector. The existing 10,000km conventional railway network is also undergoing upgrades.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the development of a new 2,250km high-speed rail network, which is part of a larger mass transit strategy aimed at increasing capacity and efficiency.

Egypt to Host UN Desertification Conference COP18 in 2028

The UN Convention to Combat Desertification unanimously selected Egypt to host the 2028 conference following diplomatic efforts and previous hosting experience.

Egypt will host the 18th Conference of the Parties (COP18) to the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification in 2028, the Foreign Ministry announced. The decision was made during the closing session of COP17 in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. This selection follows extensive diplomatic coordination between Egypt's Foreign Ministry and other government bodies, including the Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation.

The convention, established in 1996, addresses desertification, land degradation, and drought mitigation, issues closely tied to water and food security, particularly in Africa. Egypt has a history of hosting significant international environmental gatherings, having previously hosted COP27 on climate in Sharm El-Sheikh in 2022 and the COP14 biodiversity conference in 2018.

Yasmine Fouad, who has led the UN Convention to Combat Desertification since May 2025, was instrumental in the diplomatic efforts. The convention stems from the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro and focuses on critical environmental challenges that impact vulnerable regions globally.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected as the conference will focus on combating desertification and land degradation, issues critical to agricultural productivity and food security.

Egypt Orders Tighter Electricity Bill Oversight

New directives mandate accurate meter readings, prompt replacement of faulty meters, and standardized installment plans for consumers.

Egypt's Minister of Electricity and Renewable Energy, Mahmoud Esmat, has instructed electricity distribution companies nationwide to enhance oversight and auditing of electricity bills. This includes ensuring accurate consumption readings and improving customer complaint mechanisms. The measures align with President Al-Sisi's call for direct supervision of bills and facilitating the settlement of outstanding customer debts.

During a meeting with distribution company heads, Esmat reviewed billing and accounting systems, emphasizing the need for immediate replacement of malfunctioning meters within 72 hours of a complaint. He also directed the standardization of meter assessment prices and the implementation of a unified installment system for financial settlements, allowing customers flexible payment options.

Further directives require bills to be itemized by consumption month, with meter readings accurately recorded and photographed. Companies must strengthen inspection committees, increase technical support teams, and continuously monitor the complaints system, reporting on response rates and customer service. Coordination with control centers for reliable power supply, especially during peak demand and emergencies, was also stressed.

Why it mattersElectricity consumers are affected by new rules requiring accurate billing, faster meter replacements, and flexible payment plans for outstanding amounts.

Egypt Prioritizes Worker Training for Foreign Markets

Labor Minister Hassan Raddad stated that enhancing skills through training is key to preparing Egyptians for jobs in Arab and European nations.

Egypt's Labor Minister Hassan Raddad announced on Sunday that the ministry is prioritizing worker training to equip young Egyptians for opportunities in Arab and European labor markets. Raddad emphasized that the state is committed to empowering its workforce with high-level skills that align with current market demands. He highlighted the government's success in reducing the unemployment rate from over 13 percent in 2013-2014 to approximately eight percent currently.

The minister also noted that the government has implemented investment incentives and established a dedicated committee within the ministry to support investors and resolve any challenges they might encounter. These measures are part of a broader program aimed at fostering a stable work environment and encouraging investment. The introduction of unified labor regulations for private sector companies, applicable to both small and large establishments, is intended to enhance job security for workers.

New regulations require establishments with 10 or more employees to issue and get approval for their labor regulations from labor offices. Smaller businesses with fewer than 10 workers can adopt simplified regulations covering working hours and weekly rest periods. These initiatives collectively aim to promote greater stability in the work environment and boost worker job security through standardized labor practices across different business sizes.

Why it mattersEgyptian workers are directly affected by the government's focus on training to improve their skills and employability in international markets.

Egypt Exports 172,000 Tonnes of Food Products in One Week

The nation shipped a variety of goods, including vegetables and fruits, to 156 countries while importing more than it exported.

Egypt exported approximately 172,000 tonnes of food products across 3,516 shipments between August 22 and 28, 2026, according to the National Food Safety Authority (NFSA). These exports, comprising nearly 700 product varieties from about 1,300 companies, included significant volumes of vegetables like sweet potatoes, onions, and potatoes, as well as fruits such as strawberries, mangoes, and citrus. Sudan was the primary destination, with Egyptian products reaching 156 countries globally.

The NFSA facilitated these exports by issuing 1,300 health certificates and 908 export permits for agricultural products. Safaga Port led in export consignments with 645 shipments, followed by Alexandria and Damietta ports. Meanwhile, Egypt imported about 218,000 tonnes of food products, including wheat and edible oils, through 1,546 consignments from 80 countries, with Russia being the largest supplier.

The authority also managed the re-export of four rejected food consignments to maintain market integrity. Export permits for natural casings of animal origin were issued for European Union markets after successful compliance testing. The activity highlights ongoing trade in Egyptian food products internationally, alongside substantial imports to meet domestic demand.

Why it mattersEgyptian food producers and exporters are directly affected by the volume and destinations of their weekly exports, as well as the administrative processes involved.
