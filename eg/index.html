Two Egyptian Border Guards killed in Eastern Desert

Two officers died during military operations targeting illegal gold mining in southern Egypt.

Two Egyptian military officers died during operations against illegal gold mining in the Eastern Desert. Egyptian Military Spokesperson Colonel Ahmed Othman Ali announced the deaths on Thursday, 5 November.

Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Jordan, along with the Arab League, condemned the attack on Egyptian border guard forces. Saudi Arabia expressed solidarity with Egypt and offered condolences to the families of the deceased officers. Qatar reiterated its rejection of violence and terrorism, while Jordan conveyed support for Egypt.

The Arab League affirmed its support for Egypt's security and territorial integrity, calling Egypt's stability a pillar of Arab national security. The condemnations were issued on Friday, 6 November, following the military's announcement of the officers' deaths.

Egypt launches investor self-service kiosks

New kiosks allow investors to obtain company documents 24 hours a day without staff interaction.

Egypt has launched pilot self-service kiosks for investors to independently obtain company documents 24 hours a day. Minister of Investment and Foreign Trade Mohamed Farid announced the initiative at the Cairo International Technology Exhibition and Conference (Cairo ICT 2026). This move aims to digitalise government services and simplify business operations.

The kiosks allow investors to issue and print certified true copies of documents like articles of association and certificates of incorporation. Transactions will use electronic identity verification, including national ID cards and passports with biometric and facial recognition technology. This initiative is part of the ministry's plan to improve investment services through electronic delivery and simplified procedures.

The pilot is a practical step towards expanding electronic service delivery and integrating government authorities. The ministry is also developing platforms for capital increases and economic entities, and preparing amendments to the Companies Law. These changes aim to reduce duplicated procedures and improve the business environment.

Egypt and Bahrain agree to boost trade and investment

Egyptian Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk met with his Bahraini counterpart to discuss expanding cooperation in manufacturing, exports, and technology.

Egypt and Bahrain aim to expand trade, increase investment, and strengthen economic cooperation, Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk stated on Thursday, November 5, 2026. His comments came during the third meeting of the Bahrain-Egypt Government Committee for Trade, Economic, Scientific and Technological Cooperation, which took place in Egypt’s New Capital.

Kouchouk urged greater Bahraini investment in Egypt’s productive, financial, and technology sectors, and called for measures to ease trade and improve market access. The meeting, co-chaired by Kouchouk and Bahrain’s Minister of Finance and National Economy, Sheikh Salman bin Khalifa Al Khalifa, reviewed opportunities for private sector involvement in manufacturing, exports, and technology.

Both ministers agreed to work on removing investment barriers and transforming existing bilateral agreements into projects that support economic growth. Egypt also expressed interest in sharing expertise with Bahrain on public financial management and digital transformation, while Sheikh Salman bin Khalifa Al Khalifa called for increased private sector participation in bilateral trade.

Why it mattersEgyptian and Bahraini ministers met to strengthen economic ties, aiming to increase investment and trade between the two nations.

Sandoz appoints Dragos Toma to lead Egypt and Libya

The global medicine company names Dragos Toma, with over 21 years in the sector, to head its operations in Egypt and Libya.

Sandoz, a global leader in generic and biosimilar medicines, has appointed Dragos Toma as Country Head for Egypt and Libya. Toma brings over 21 years of pharmaceutical industry experience, including more than 13 years at Sandoz, to the role.

Toma, who most recently headed the Biosimilars and Prescription Medicines Business Unit at Sandoz Romania, will focus on advancing Sandoz's established position in Egypt. This includes leading in biosimilars, offering a broad portfolio of medicines, and advancing local manufacturing and technology transfer.

Dragos Toma stated that Egypt and Libya present significant opportunities to expand Sandoz's existing presence. He expressed his intention to collaborate with teams, authorities, and healthcare professionals to enhance local capabilities and improve patient access to medicines.

Why it mattersDragos Toma's appointment as Country Head for Egypt and Libya by Sandoz will impact patient access to medicines and local manufacturing capabilities.

Egyptian chemical exports to France climb 10.1%

Shipments reached $333m in nine months, with fertilisers forming nearly half of the total.

Egyptian exports of chemicals and fertilisers to France reached $333m in the first nine months of 2026, a 10.1% increase from the same period in 2025. This growth places France seventh among destinations for Egyptian chemical exports.

The Chemical and Fertilisers Export Council reported that fertilisers accounted for $153m of the total exports to France, with organic chemicals following at $85.4m. Council Chairperson Khaled Abou El Makarem stated that Egyptian producers are competitive and meet European market requirements.

The council will organise a trade mission to France from February 1 to 6, 2027, to foster business-to-business meetings and market studies. This initiative aims to help Egyptian manufacturers build partnerships and increase their European market share.

Why it mattersEgyptian businesses exporting chemicals and fertilisers to France are seeing increased sales and are being supported by their export council to expand further.
