China's Xi arrives in Egypt to expand defence, tech and trade ties

President Xi Jinping's visit aims to deepen a strategic partnership encompassing new debt-swap programs, defense cooperation, and technology.

Chinese President Xi Jinping arrived in Cairo for a three-day visit aimed at expanding a comprehensive strategic partnership with Egypt. The visit, his first in a decade, seeks to enhance cooperation in areas including new debt-swap programs, defense ties, and alignment on Middle East security. Discussions between the leaders will focus on implementing their 2014 Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Agreement and advancing a joint development cooperation strategy for 2025–2029.

The visit marks an evolution in bilateral relations from traditional commercial cooperation to a long-term strategic partnership that includes politics, defense, technology, industry, and energy. For Egypt, closer ties with Beijing offer wider diplomatic maneuverability to diversify international partnerships and leverage China’s diplomatic weight in the U.N. Security Council. China views Egypt as a vital artery for global trade, with Beijing already Egypt’s largest trading partner.

Ahead of his arrival, Xi stated China stands ready to pursue mutual benefit and set a benchmark for joint development, calling for greater synergy in development strategies and high-quality Belt and Road cooperation. He also urged both nations to proactively expand pragmatic collaboration in infrastructure and technology, and deepen exchanges in education, tourism, science, health, sports, and archaeology.

Why it mattersEgyptian businesses and the government are seeking to leverage Chinese investment and development experience to advance national priorities.

Egypt, China Celebrate 70 Years of Diplomatic Ties

Both nations mark seven decades of relations, highlighting historical trade routes and shared civilizational achievements.

Egypt and China are celebrating seventy years of diplomatic relations, a milestone marked by the visit of Chinese President Xi Jinping. Egypt was the first Arab and African nation to establish ties with China in 1956, building on deep historical and civilizational connections. The two countries share ancient roots, symbolized by the Nile and Yangtze rivers, which fostered advancements in fields like medicine, engineering, and astronomy.

These ancient civilizations contributed significantly to human progress, laying foundations for later technological revolutions. Both Egypt and China aspire to participate fully in contemporary technological advancements for sustainable development. Historically, a trade route connected China's Silk Road to Egypt's Red Sea and Suez Canal, facilitating the exchange of goods, cultures, and ideas between East and West for centuries.

Egyptian ports historically served as crucial commercial hubs for trade among Asia, Europe, and Africa. This exchange strengthened the bonds between the two peoples, paving the way for extensive commercial and cultural interactions that continue to shape their relationship today.

Rashwan Pushes for Swift, Accurate Information to Counter Rumors

Minister of State for Information Diaa Rashwan stressed the need for coordinated government communication to prevent the spread of false reports.

Egypt's Minister of State for Information Diaa Rashwan highlighted the critical need for government spokespersons and media outlets to ensure the rapid dissemination of accurate information. He stated that providing official accounts of events and decisions is essential to close information gaps and combat rumors that could undermine state efforts and social stability. Rashwan spoke at a meeting with 35 government spokespersons and media advisers, emphasizing that improving their performance aligns with presidential directives to enhance Egyptian media's role in public awareness.

Rashwan urged government officials to respond promptly to media inquiries to present facts and institutional views. Participants noted challenges such as the proliferation of fabricated reports on social media and some media outlets' over-reliance on these platforms. Concerns were also raised about some news sources not adequately using official information. The attendees called for clearer roles for spokespersons, better institutional access to information, and advanced training programs.

The ministry plans to develop spokespersons' performance and is preparing training programs for various media sectors. Rashwan announced coordination with government bodies and media organizations to establish cooperation mechanisms for timely information flow and to prevent contradictory messaging. Efforts are also underway to accelerate a freedom of information law requiring government bodies to share data with citizens and the media.

Why it mattersGovernment officials and media professionals are affected by the push for timely and accurate information to combat rumors and improve public awareness.
