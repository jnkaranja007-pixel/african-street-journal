Egypt's parliament to question transport minister after train crash

Seven people died and more than 100 were hurt when two passenger trains collided south of Cairo on Sunday.

Egypt's Parliament moved on Monday, 10 November, to press Transport Minister Kamel El-Wazir for answers after a collision between two passenger trains in Ayyat, Giza Province, about 32 miles south of Cairo. The House of Representatives' Transport and Communications Committee will hold an emergency session on Tuesday, 11 November, and its chairman, Wahid Qorqar, approved the request for the meeting.

The crash happened on Sunday, 8 November, when a train bound for Fayoum ran into the back of a train heading to Aswan that had stopped on the same track, and several carriages derailed. Egyptian National Railways identified the trains as Nos. 134 and 2010. The Health Ministry reported seven dead and 125 injured, and the driver of the Fayoum-bound train was among the dead.

President Abdel-Fattah el-Sisi ordered an urgent inquiry and said anyone found negligent must answer for it. The Public Prosecution is examining the technical causes. Early counts had put the dead at four and the injured at 88, according to Agence France-Presse, before the toll rose over the weekend.

Why it mattersPassengers on Egypt's rail network and the families of the seven people killed face a parliamentary inquiry into the minister's responsibility for the crash.

Egypt FM discusses de-escalation with G7 counterparts

Badr Abdelatty met French, Ukrainian, and Canadian ministers in Paris on Tuesday.

Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty met with his French, Ukrainian, and Canadian counterparts in Paris on Tuesday to discuss regional de-escalation and the war in Ukraine. Abdelatty also addressed food security and economic cooperation with the ministers on the sidelines of a G7 meeting.

During discussions with French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot, Abdelatty highlighted the "strategic partnership" between Egypt and France and explored financing for development projects like Cairo Metro Line 6. He warned that wider conflict could threaten global energy and food security and economic stability.

Abdelatty met Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha to discuss trade, energy, and Egypt's interest in hosting a global grain hub. With Canadian Foreign Minister Anita Anand, they explored trilateral cooperation in renewable energy and healthcare, and addressed developments in Gaza and tensions involving Iran.

Why it mattersEgyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty engaged in diplomatic talks with G7 nations regarding regional stability and economic cooperation.

Egypt reviews $16 million Cairo waste plant and electric bus plans

Officials and a World Bank delegation reviewed preparations for an integrated waste facility in 10th of Ramadan City that will process 15,000 tons a day.

Local Development and Environment Minister Manal Awad met a World Bank delegation on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, to review progress on the Greater Cairo Air Pollution and Climate Change Management Project. The talks, held at the ministry's headquarters in the New Capital, covered preparations for a $16 million integrated waste management facility in 10th of Ramadan City.

Awad welcomed 20 new electric buses added to the Bus Rapid Transit system, funded with nearly $7 million from the World Bank Group. The facility, covering 1,228 feddans, is designed to process about half of the waste generated in Greater Cairo and to serve the region for more than 30 years under a public-private partnership.

The meeting also addressed closing the Abu Zaabal dumpsite and developing the Amiriyah bus depot to house 100 electric buses. Officials said the waste facility is expected to cut greenhouse gas emissions by 30 percent and create more than 3,500 direct and indirect jobs.

Why it mattersResidents of Greater Cairo and workers in the waste and transport sectors stand to see the new facility and buses, with more than 3,500 jobs expected to be created.

Egypt's private sector drives 80% of GDP, minister says

Planning Minister Ahmed Rostom told a Washington roundtable that the private sector also supplies most investment and jobs as the country pursues a 2027-2030 reform plan.

Egypt's economic policy is moving towards a private sector-led growth model, Minister of Planning and Economic Development Ahmed Rostom said at a roundtable organised by the American Chamber of Commerce in Washington, DC, on the sidelines of the B20 Summit 2026. He said the sector accounts for about 80% of gross domestic product.

Rostom said the private sector makes up 60.8% of total investment and employs 83% of the labour force. The meeting was attended by Investment and Foreign Trade Minister Mohamed Farid and members of the Egyptian-American Business Council. Rostom called on US businesses to move from strategic dialogue to economically viable projects.

Rostom outlined the National Economic Transformation Programme 2027-2030, which contains 185 reform measures across five sectors that together account for 40% of GDP. It targets growth of 6.2-6.8% and inflation of 5-7% by 2030. He also pointed to a Infrastructure Finance Guarantee Facility set up with the World Bank for renewable energy, water desalination and urban development.

Why it mattersUS firms are invited to join projects in clean energy, AI, cloud computing and data centres, with Egypt's 2027-2030 programme setting growth and inflation targets.

Egypt takes gold for culture and heritage at Wanderlust awards

Readers of the British travel magazine Wanderlust voted Egypt the top prize, with a record 8.2 million votes cast by more than 250,000 people.

Egypt won the Gold Award for culture and heritage at the 25th annual Wanderlust Reader Travel Awards, a prize decided by readers of the British magazine. Ahmed Yousry, chief executive of the Egyptian Tourism Promotion Board, collected the award at the Painted Hall in Greenwich on 4 November, in front of about 500 travel executives.

Wanderlust said the Grand Egyptian Museum had newly showcased the country's ancient treasures. Sherif Fathy, Egypt's tourism and antiquities minister, said the award reflects the country's heritage, its museums and ongoing restoration work meant to preserve sites for future generations. Yousry said the recognition would support campaigns aimed at first-time and returning visitors.

Egypt drew a record 19 million visitors in 2025, and 14.4 million arrived in the first nine months of 2026, up 4 percent from a year earlier. The Grand Egyptian Museum, which opened in November 2025, has drawn more than 6.5 million visitors. Officials now want travellers to stay longer and spend more, while keeping a goal of 30 million annual visitors by 2030.

Why it mattersEgyptian tourism operators and the Egyptian Tourism Promotion Board are the parties directly tied to the award and its visitor campaigns.
