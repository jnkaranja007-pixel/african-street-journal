Egypt and China Celebrate 70 Years of Partnership

President Sisi hailed the enduring relationship as a model for global cooperation and mutual prosperity.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi marked the 70th anniversary of diplomatic relations with China by sending a message to an event in Cairo. He described the bilateral ties as a model of partnership built on respect, solidarity, and shared interests. The President highlighted that joint efforts with Chinese President Xi Jinping have elevated relations to a comprehensive strategic partnership, fostering increased trade, investment, tourism, and cultural exchanges, particularly among youth.

The celebration comes as President Xi Jinping arrived in Cairo for a significant visit, with major roads across the capital adorned with the leaders' images and national flags. This visit signals an intention to expand cooperation, with discussions pointing towards new investments and partnerships, including in artificial intelligence. The two nations are also conducting their second large-scale joint air force exercise.

Former officials emphasized that Egypt aims to leverage its deepening ties with China to advance its own development agenda and national security priorities, without compromising its strategic relationship with other partners like the United States. Chinese investments in Egypt have already surpassed $10 billion, and officials are committed to expanding this economic cooperation.

Why it mattersEgyptian businesses and the national economy are directly affected by the expansion of trade, investment, and economic cooperation with China.

Bus Overturns in South Sinai, Killing 22

A passenger bus carrying Egyptian and Jordanian travelers overturned on the Dahab-Nuweiba road, resulting in 22 fatalities and 28 injuries.

Twenty-two people died and 28 were injured when a passenger bus overturned on the Dahab-Nuweiba road in Egypt’s South Sinai on Wednesday morning. The bus, en route from Cairo to the port of Nuweiba for transit to Jordan, carried both Egyptian and Jordanian passengers. The South Sinai governorate stated the vehicle overturned in the Al-Saada area between Dahab and Nuweiba.

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly has directed the formation of a technical committee to investigate the crash. This committee, comprising officials from the transport and interior ministries, the South Sinai governorate, and the Administrative Control Authority, will assess the vehicle's safety, the driver's condition, and road conditions. Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar activated a central operations room and dispatched 20 ambulances, while South Sinai Governor Ismail Mohamed Kamal increased hospital readiness.

The Minister of Social Solidarity, Maya Morsy, reported coordination with relief teams to provide urgent assistance to those affected. Prime Minister Madbouly also instructed the governor to ensure accurate legal procedures, clear the crash site, and offer necessary support to the victims' families. He extended condolences to the deceased's families and wished the injured a swift recovery.

Why it mattersTwenty-two people were killed and 28 injured in a bus crash, affecting passengers and their families.

Egypt Exports to China Jump 199.8%

Egyptian exports to China reached $840.8 million in the first half of 2026, a 199.8 percent increase from the previous year.

Egypt's exports to China saw a dramatic increase of 199.8 percent in the first half of 2026, reaching $840.8 million compared to $280.5 million in the same period of 2025, according to the Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics (CAPMAS). This surge contributed to a total bilateral trade volume of $11.3 billion between the two nations during the first six months of 2026, an increase of 21.5 percent from the prior year.

Despite the significant rise in Egyptian exports, the trade deficit in favor of China continued to widen. Egyptian imports from China grew by 14.3 percent to $10.4 billion in the first half of 2026, up from $9.1 billion in the first half of 2025. China's primary exports to Egypt during this period were electrical machinery and automotive sectors.

Key Egyptian exports to China included fuels and mineral oils ($494 million), vegetables and fruits ($150.6 million), and cotton ($76.4 million). Remittances from Egyptians working in China also saw an increase, reaching $23.3 million in the 2024/2025 fiscal year.

Why it mattersEgyptian businesses exporting goods to China are directly benefiting from a significant increase in demand and export value.

Egypt and UK University Forge Partnership for First International Campus

Prime Minister Madbouly reviewed progress on establishing the University of Exeter's first international campus in Egypt through a partnership with Ain Shams University.

Egypt's Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly has advanced plans for the University of Exeter's first international campus in Egypt. The initiative involves a partnership between Ain Shams University, Ain Shams National University, and the UK's University of Exeter. This collaboration aims to expand international educational partnerships and attract reputable global institutions to offer programs locally, aligning with Egypt's Vision 2030 for sustainable development.

A document outlining the partnership's principles and terms was signed in August, with Ain Shams National University set to host the campus. This makes Exeter the first UK Russell Group university to establish an international campus in North Africa. The partnership will combine Ain Shams University's academic expertise, Ain Shams National University's modern infrastructure, and the University of Exeter's international standards.

The planned academic programs will focus on future-oriented disciplines such as computer and data sciences, engineering, business, management, and health sciences. Prime Minister Madbouly directed officials to finalize procedures for the partnership's success and explore similar agreements with other international universities to bolster Egypt's higher education and research strategy.

Why it mattersEgyptian students and the local labor market will benefit from access to international academic programs and degrees in future-oriented disciplines.

Egypt, IOM Establish Migrant Health Unit

A new health unit will oversee migrant healthcare access and referrals under an agreement with the International Organization for Migration.

Egypt's Health Ministry will establish a dedicated migrant health unit to manage healthcare access and coordinate referrals for treatment. This initiative is part of a new agreement with the International Organization for Migration (IOM) aimed at enhancing health services for both migrants and host communities. The memorandum of understanding includes provisions for early detection and treatment of HIV, the distribution of medicines, and field screening campaigns conducted by the IOM at specialized hospitals.

The partnership extends to vaccination campaigns, public health programs, mental health and addiction treatment services, and the provision of equipment and supplies for blood donation. The IOM will cover the costs associated with screenings and provide technical and financial support for healthcare expenses and service improvements. Deputy Health Minister Amr Kandil and IOM Egypt Chief of Mission Carlos Oliver signed the agreement in the presence of Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar.

The new unit will monitor the agreement's implementation and manage referrals for migrants needing medical care. Training for healthcare providers, including specialized courses and cardiopulmonary resuscitation, is also included, alongside infrastructure improvements for health facilities. Both parties will collaborate on addressing health challenges related to population pressures and migration flows, including emergency cases, and will establish regular program evaluation mechanisms.

Why it mattersMigrants and host communities will receive expanded health services, including HIV treatment, vaccinations, and mental health support.
