El-Sisi in Moscow for Russia-Africa Summit

Egypt and Russia will discuss economic cooperation, investment, and food security at the summit.

President Abdel-Fattah El-Sisi arrived in Moscow on October 28 for the third Russia-Africa Summit, where he will meet Russian President Vladimir Putin. The summit, running from October 28-29, aims to expand cooperation and economic opportunities between African nations and Russia. Discussions are expected to cover trade, investment, energy, agriculture, security, and technology.

Egypt and Russia have strengthened economic ties, with bilateral trade reaching approximately USD 10.5 billion in 2025, more than double the 2021 figure. Key exchanges include Russian wheat and metal products for Egyptian fruit and vegetables. Russia's state nuclear company, Rosatom, is also constructing the El Dabaa nuclear power plant in Egypt.

This meeting follows previous summits in Sochi in 2019 and St. Petersburg in 2023. Egypt is a significant economic partner for Russia in Africa, with cooperation extending to industrial development and energy supplies.

Why it mattersPresident El-Sisi is meeting with President Putin to advance economic cooperation, investment, and food security discussions.

Egypt eyes floating nuclear reactors with Russia

Cairo is discussing the deployment of floating and small modular nuclear reactors with Rosatom.

Egypt is in talks with Russia's Rosatom to potentially deploy floating and small modular nuclear reactors (SMRs). These floating plants can generate electricity and thermal energy while also providing large-scale seawater desalination. The discussions signal a potential expansion of Egypt's nuclear strategy beyond the El-Dabaa project.

Rosatom Chief Executive Officer Alexey Likhachev confirmed active discussions on floating nuclear power plants and compact land-based reactors. Floating nuclear power plants are compact reactor units on vessels or offshore barges, designed for coastlines and remote regions without extensive onshore infrastructure. Russia operates the world's first floating nuclear power plant, Akademik Lomonosov, powered by two KLT-40S reactors.

These mobile reactors, potentially based on Rosatom's RITM-200 series, would supply reliable power and desalinated water to Egypt's coastal municipalities and industrial zones. This initiative supports national energy security and carbon reduction goals. Interest in floating nuclear technology is growing globally due to rising baseload power demand from industrial electrification and data centers.

Why it mattersEgypt's coastal regions and industrial zones would receive reliable power and desalinated water from floating nuclear reactors.

Egypt moves to raise fines for false news

The government is preparing amendments to the Penal Code to increase penalties for spreading false information online.

Egypt’s Cabinet announced on Wednesday, October 28, that it is preparing amendments to the Penal Code to increase fines for false news and rumour-related offences. This action is part of a larger government plan to regulate social media through new digital safety legislation, expanded technical monitoring, and public awareness campaigns.

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly directed the formation of a working group to follow up on recommendations from a committee examining harmful social media practices. Justice Minister Mahmoud El-Sherif presented a report to the Cabinet on measures to safeguard Egyptian family values and social cohesion.

The committee also proposed establishing general rules for digital platforms through forthcoming digital safety legislation and stronger state oversight. The government has not yet specified the proposed fines or announced a timetable for introducing the amendments.

Why it mattersThe Egyptian government is increasing penalties for spreading false news online as part of new digital safety legislation.

Egypt's pensions fall short for 10.2 million elderly

Retirees say modest, unevenly distributed benefits do not cover living costs as the older population grows.

Egypt's elderly population has reached 10.2 million people, a rise from 9.8 million a year ago, according to the national statistics agency. These 10.2 million citizens aged 60 and older face modest and uneven social protections, with many retirees finding pensions insufficient for a dignified life. The government has introduced new measures including pensions, a cash program for the poor, a rights law, and health insurance reforms.

Despite government efforts, critics including unions, lawmakers, and researchers argue that pension payments are too low and eligibility criteria are becoming stricter. The minimum pension for new retirees increased to EGP 1,755 (USD 31) in January, and existing pensions saw a 15 percent rise on July 1, costing an estimated EGP 70 billion (USD 1.25 billion) annually. However, these amounts are considered inadequate by many.

The older population in Egypt is projected to constitute roughly 14 percent of the total by 2050. Unions and researchers point out that some promised benefits remain largely on paper. The state's minimum wage for public-sector workers is also a point of contention in discussions about adequate support for the elderly.

Why it matters10.2 million elderly Egyptians are directly affected by insufficient pension amounts and uneven social protection.

Suez Canal Authority seeks Greek maritime investment

Egypt's canal authority chairman met with Greece's ambassador to discuss trade and logistics cooperation.

Suez Canal Authority Chairman Ossama Rabiee met with Greece’s Ambassador Nikolaos Papageorgiou at SCA headquarters in Ismailia to discuss strengthening bilateral cooperation in trade and logistics investment. Rabiee invited Greek commercial enterprises to engage more actively with Egypt’s maritime corridor and cooperate with the SCA, highlighting the authority's developed industrial base and new maritime services.

Rabiee stated the Suez Canal has managed crises and safeguarded global supply chains by working with the maritime community. Papageorgiou praised the canal's progress as an ongoing success story and an indispensable artery of global trade, expressing hope for regional geopolitical stability. He noted steady shipping through the canal is vital for global supply chains.

The meeting aims to attract more investment in projects in the canal region, particularly for Greek shipowners who control one of the world's largest merchant fleets. SCA facilities could offer ship repair and dry-docking as alternatives to Greek and Turkish yards, while bunkering services could generate recurring revenue. Suez Canal revenue reached $567.1 million in August 2026, with 1,358 vessels transiting.

Why it mattersGreek shipowners, who control a large merchant fleet, are being invited to invest in Suez Canal facilities for ship repair and services.
