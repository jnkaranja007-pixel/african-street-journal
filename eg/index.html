Egypt seeks Japanese investment for airport upgrades

Minister Sameh Elhefny discussed private sector management and operation of several airports with the Japanese ambassador.

Egypt's Minister of Civil Aviation Sameh Elhefny met with Japanese Ambassador Fumio Iwai to discuss Japanese investment in airport development. The plan includes offering several airports to the private sector for management, operation, and development, in cooperation with the International Finance Corporation (IFC). This meeting occurred at the ministry's headquarters as the ambassador concluded his tenure in Cairo.

The programme aims to use international expertise and technology to improve airport efficiency and services. Elhefny noted growing cooperation between Egypt and Japan in civil aviation, specifically mentioning JICA's support for the new passenger terminal at Alexandria International Airport. The terminal design includes environmental sustainability standards.

Ambassador Iwai expressed appreciation for the cooperation and stressed continued coordination. He highlighted the importance of knowledge exchange to strengthen bilateral relations and expand civil aviation cooperation. Elhefny wished the ambassador success and commended his contributions to strengthening relations between the two countries.

Why it mattersEgyptian airports are seeking private sector involvement and international expertise to improve operations and services.

Egypt moves to raise fines for false news

The government is preparing amendments to the Penal Code to increase penalties for spreading false information online.

Egypt’s Cabinet announced on Wednesday, October 28, that it is preparing amendments to the Penal Code to increase fines for false news and rumour-related offences. This action is part of a larger government plan to regulate social media through new digital safety legislation, expanded technical monitoring, and public awareness campaigns.

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly directed the formation of a working group to follow up on recommendations from a committee examining harmful social media practices. Justice Minister Mahmoud El-Sherif presented a report to the Cabinet on measures to safeguard Egyptian family values and social cohesion.

The committee also proposed establishing general rules for digital platforms through forthcoming digital safety legislation and stronger state oversight. The government has not yet specified the proposed fines or announced a timetable for introducing the amendments.

Why it mattersThe Egyptian government is increasing penalties for spreading false news online as part of new digital safety legislation.

Beyti invests over EGP 7bn in Egypt

The food and beverage company plans EGP 8.7bn spending for 2025-2027 and sources 67% of materials locally.

Beyti has invested more than EGP 7bn in Egypt and plans EGP 8.7bn spending for 2025-2027, the company announced. The food and beverage producer highlighted its contribution to local manufacturing and employment. Beyti provides around 6,000 direct jobs and 19,000 indirect jobs.

The company sources 67% of its raw and packaging materials locally, exporting to more than 45 countries. These figures were shared during an awareness session organised by Beyti, an Almarai subsidiary. Nermine Abou El Atta, Adviser to the Minister of Industry, and Saber Osman, Assistant to the Minister of Local Development, attended the event.

Beyti aims for a 30% reduction in carbon dioxide emissions by partially switching to natural gas. In 2021, the company invested in a 7.6-megawatt solar power plant, the largest in Egypt's food and beverage sector, supplying 20% of energy needs. By 2028, Beyti targets increasing this share to 30%.

Why it mattersBeyti's investment and spending plans in Egypt directly affect the country's manufacturing sector, job creation, and economic development.

Egypt's 10.2 million elderly face modest pensions

The number of Egyptians aged 60 and older grew to 10.2 million, but social protections fall short of living costs.

Egypt's population of people aged 60 and older reached 10.2 million, an increase from 9.8 million a year prior, according to the national statistics agency. This demographic is projected to constitute 14 percent of the population by 2050. The government has implemented pensions, a cash program for the poor, a new rights law, and health insurance reforms to support this group.

Despite these measures, unions, lawmakers, and researchers argue that pension payments are insufficient and eligibility criteria are becoming stricter. The minimum pension for new retirees was raised to EGP 1,755 (USD 31) in January, and existing pensions saw a 15 percent increase on July 1, costing an estimated EGP 70 billion (USD 1.25 billion) annually. Critics contend these amounts do not cover the cost of a dignified old age.

Pensions currently support approximately 11.5 million people, representing the primary source of financial aid for older Egyptians. However, concerns persist that some promised benefits remain largely on paper, failing to provide tangible support to those in need.

Why it mattersEgypt's growing elderly population faces inadequate social protection and pensions that fall short of covering living costs.

Egypt graduates 12 green businesses at GreenShift Summit

The GreenShift Programme supported 12 scaleups in Greater Cairo and the Delta with EGP 57.5m in investment.

Nahdet El Mahrousa hosted the GreenShift Summit on Tuesday, October 27, in Egypt, bringing together over 150 industry leaders and entrepreneurs. The summit celebrated the graduation of 12 green scaleups from Greater Cairo and the Delta that received acceleration support and EGP 57.5m in investment through the GreenShift Programme.

The programme, a partnership between Nahdet El Mahrousa and GIZ, co-funded by the European Union and the German Federal Government, aims to help green enterprises commercialise their solutions. Mostafa Ashraf, private sector adviser at GIZ, noted that access to equipment financing is a key challenge for scaling businesses, a challenge the programme addresses by financing machinery.

The graduating scaleups operate in sectors including climate action, the circular economy, and waste management. Tarek Makram, founder of Extractor Co., credited the GreenShift Programme with providing financial support and expert guidance to overcome challenges in establishing his factory. The programme received 1,353 applications and trained 46 fellows.

Why it matters12 Egyptian green businesses received investment and support to scale their operations in sectors like waste management and the circular economy.
