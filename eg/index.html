Egypt and India discuss boosting startup and financing ties

Egyptian minister met with Indian ambassador to explore cooperation in innovative financing and support for startups.

Egyptian Minister of Planning and Economic Development Ahmed Rostom met with Indian Ambassador to Cairo Suresh K. Reddy on Sunday, November 8, 2026, to discuss strengthening cooperation in innovative financing and supporting startups. Rostom praised India's experience in digital infrastructure and fostering innovation, stressing the importance of learning from their financing mechanisms. He highlighted entrepreneurship as a key driver of economic development and job creation, noting Egypt's plan to develop the sector and encourage unicorn companies.

The Indian ambassador shared that India has over 240,000 startups and sees Egypt as a regional hub for African and European market expansion. He proposed including FinTech and digital solution providers in investment delegations to Cairo. Arrangements are underway for a high-level Indian investment delegation of over 150 companies to visit Egypt. Indian companies currently have nearly $5 billion invested in Egypt across 55 factories, employing over 35,000 Egyptians.

In a separate meeting, Minister of Investment and Foreign Trade Mohamed Farid discussed export financing, startup support, and investment zone development with the CEO of the International Islamic Trade Finance Corporation (ITFC). Farid emphasized the importance of a Fintech Sandbox for developing innovative solutions and attracting startups. He also proposed the Sovereign Fund of Egypt act as a central financing institution for export-related factoring operations to boost foreign currency inflows.

Why it mattersEgyptian officials are seeking to enhance economic and investment cooperation with India and international financial institutions to foster startup growth, create jobs, and boost exports.

Cairo opens first stray dog shelter

The 5,000 sq m facility in El Tebbin can house up to 700 dogs and includes operating theatres and a veterinary pharmacy.

Cairo opened its first dedicated shelter for stray dogs on Monday in El Tebbin, south of the city. The 5,000 sq m facility, located east of the Autostrad road, was inaugurated by the minister of local development and environment, the agriculture minister, and the Cairo governor.

The shelter can accommodate approximately 700 dogs in 112 rooms, with each room housing up to three animals. It features two operating theatres, a veterinary pharmacy, isolation rooms for sick animals, feeding areas, and administrative and security rooms. Outside, there are green spaces, an exercise area, and two pools for treatment and cooling.

A team of veterinarians will provide medical examinations, sterilization, surgery, and vaccinations, including for rabies. The shelter aims to manage stray dogs using scientific and sustainable methods, balancing public health with animal welfare. The governorate plans to replicate the project in other districts if this first phase proves successful.

Why it mattersCairo residents' complaints about stray dogs on the streets prompted the governorate to establish this shelter.

Egypt permanently lists Muslim Brotherhood as terrorist entity

A decision published in the Official Gazette on Monday, November 9, ends the possibility of the group’s listing expiring.

Egypt has permanently designated the Muslim Brotherhood as a terrorist entity, according to a decision published in the Official Gazette on Monday, November 9. The Office for Regulating Lists of Terrorist Entities and Terrorists at the Public Prosecution issued the decision, which also adds three individuals to Egypt’s terrorist list following final court convictions.

The three individuals named are Abdel-Rahman Hassan Mohamed Meligy, Gamal Abdel-Hamid Gomaa Abdel-Hamid, and Osama Ibrahim Mohamed Mostafa El-Rakh. This permanent designation follows several previous court decisions that extended the Brotherhood’s classification as a terrorist organisation, most recently in December 2025 for another five years.

Under Egypt’s Law No. 8 of 2015, listing an organisation prohibits its activities, closes its premises, bans meetings, and freezes its assets. The law also prohibits providing financial support or collecting resources for the organisation, directly or indirectly, including for jointly held assets.

Why it mattersThe Muslim Brotherhood and three individuals have been permanently added to Egypt's terrorist list, leading to prohibitions on their activities and asset freezes.

Egypt renews bid for ITU Council seat

Minister Raafat Hendy seeks African representation at the Doha plenipotentiary conference.

Egypt’s Minister of Communications and Information Technology Raafat Hendy announced in Doha that Egypt is renewing its candidacy for a seat on the International Telecommunication Union’s Council representing Africa. Hendy addressed the Plenipotentiary Conference (PP-26) and asked member states to support Egypt’s bid for the council seat.

Hendy stated that Egypt believes affordable, high-quality, and secure communications are fundamental for development, aligning with the ITU’s goals of digital inclusion and sustainable digital transformation. He highlighted Egypt's expansion of fibre-optic networks, 5G rollout, and efforts to connect rural areas, alongside implementing an IPv6 strategy and a 2026-2030 spectrum strategy.

On the sidelines, Hendy met with Saudi Arabia’s Minister of Communications and Information Technology Abdullah Alswaha to discuss closer cooperation, particularly in AI, building on a 2019 memorandum of understanding. They reviewed areas including AI, digital transformation, and business partnerships, agreeing to coordinate more closely in international telecoms forums.

Why it mattersEgypt is seeking to influence international telecommunications policy by renewing its bid for a seat on the ITU Council.

Egypt wins gold for cultural tourism destination

Egypt's tourism authority CEO accepted the award in London, recognizing the nation's heritage sites and museums.

Egypt secured the gold award for "Most Desirable Cultural and Heritage Destination" at the 25th Wanderlust Reader Travel Awards. The win, based on reader votes, placed Egypt ahead of other global tourist spots. Egyptian Tourism Authority CEO Ahmed Youssef accepted the award at a London ceremony.

The Wanderlust awards reflect traveler preferences and experiences, with winners chosen by the magazine's readers. Prince Harry, founder of the Travalyst initiative, spoke at the event about promoting sustainable tourism. Minister of Tourism Sherif Fathy stated the award highlights Egypt's unique cultural assets and preservation efforts.

Fathy added that the recognition supports the ministry's strategy to showcase Egypt's diverse tourism offerings and boost global competitiveness. Youssef noted the award strengthens international recognition of Egypt's tourism sector and its appeal to those seeking cultural and historical experiences.
