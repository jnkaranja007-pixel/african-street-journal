Egypt lawmakers summon minister after fatal train crash

Seven people died and 125 were injured when two trains collided south of Cairo on Sunday.

Egyptian lawmakers will summon Transport Minister Kamel El-Wazir for an emergency session on Tuesday, November 11, following a deadly train collision on Sunday, November 8. The crash in Ayyat, Giza Province, killed seven people and injured 125. Train Nos. 134 and 2010 collided south of Cairo when a train heading to Fayoum struck the rear of a train stopped on the same track bound for Aswan.

The accident occurred about 32 miles south of Cairo, derailing several carriages. The driver of the Fayoum-bound train was among the seven fatalities. The Health Ministry confirmed the latest casualty figures after initial reports indicated four dead and 88 injured. President Abdel-Fattah el-Sisi has ordered an urgent inquiry into the incident.

The Public Prosecution is investigating the technical causes of the collision. The House of Representatives’ Transport and Communications Committee chairman, Wahid Qorqar, approved the request for the minister's summons.

Why it mattersSeven Egyptians died and 125 were injured in a train collision, prompting a parliamentary inquiry.

Egyptian tax system collects LE 14 billion

A new risk-assessment system generated LE 14 billion from voluntary disclosures and identified non-compliance.

Egypt's Tax Authority has collected LE 14 billion through its new tax risk-assessment system, announced Rasha Abdel-Aal, the authority's head. The system received approximately 750,000 voluntary disclosures, contributing to a total tax yield of LE 97 billion.

The risk-assessment system uses big data and artificial intelligence to electronically screen taxpayer files, classifying them by compliance level without human intervention. This approach directs inspection teams only to high-risk files, sparing compliant taxpayers from routine audits.

This digital transformation aims to balance public treasury resources with the interests of the business community. The Tax Authority is also introducing tax facilitations to encourage informal sector businesses to regularize their status and integrate into the formal economy.

Why it mattersThe Egyptian Tax Authority's new system is improving tax collection and aims to foster a fairer business environment.

Egypt allocates Red Sea land to cut public debt

Egypt's finance ministry will use about 26,690 feddans of state land in the Red Sea governorate to reduce public debt and issue sovereign sukuk.

President Abdel Fattah Al-Sisi has issued a decree allocating about 26,690 feddans of state-owned land in the Red Sea governorate to the finance ministry, to be used for reducing public debt and issuing sovereign sukuk. The decree, published in the Official Gazette, amends Presidential Decree No. 222 of 2026, which had allocated the same plot, about 112.1 million square metres, only for sovereign sukuk.

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly met Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk in the New Administrative Capital to review progress on debt targets. Kouchouk said the ratio of budget-sector debt to GDP had fallen by about 14 percentage points between June 2023 and June 2026, and that external debt of budget-sector bodies was cut by the equivalent of about 3.9% of GDP in 2025/26. Madbouly called debt reduction a top government priority.

The Egypt Forum 2026 recommendations set a path to bring public debt down to about 69.5% of GDP by 2030, with fixed ceilings on public debt, external debt and sovereign guarantees. Madbouly directed officials to draw up a detailed plan assigning specific tasks to each ministry and government body, to be presented to the president.

Why it mattersMinistries and government bodies must now each receive specific debt-reduction tasks under a detailed plan to be presented to the president.

Egypt minister links food security to global peace at G7 meeting

Badr Abdelatty warned that conflicts and climate change are driving up food prices, impacting developing nations.

Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty stated that food security is a fundamental pillar of international peace and stability. He warned that conflicts, geopolitical tensions, climate change, and supply chain disruptions are increasing food prices and pressuring developing countries. Abdelatty spoke on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, at a G7 ministerial meeting session in Paris focused on Middle East stability.

The minister outlined Egypt’s efforts to bolster food security through increased agricultural production, diversified import sources, and improved storage and distribution. He stressed that political solutions and dialogue are essential for resolving regional disputes, citing the dire situations in the West Bank and Gaza. Abdelatty rejected using food as a political weapon and called for protecting civilians and ensuring humanitarian aid access.

Abdelatty highlighted Egypt's upcoming hosting of UNCCD COP18 in 2028 as an opportunity to address drought and promote the water-food nexus. He also emphasized the need for climate adaptation financing for developing nations without increasing their debt. The foreign minister noted that worsening food security challenges in Africa and the Arab region are exacerbated by conflicts, displacement, and water scarcity.

Why it mattersEgyptian citizens and businesses are affected by global food price fluctuations and the country's efforts to secure stable food supplies.

Angola marks 51 years of independence at Cairo reception

Angola's ambassador to Egypt credits both presidents with strengthening ties, citing the Angola-Egypt Bilateral Commission as proof of the commitment.

Angola's ambassador to Egypt, Maquento Sebastião Lopes, hosted a reception in Cairo on Wednesday to mark the 51st anniversary of Angola's independence and 50 years of diplomatic ties with Egypt. Lopes credited Presidents João Lourenço and Abdel Fattah al-Sisi with strengthening relations in recent years through regular high-level contact and dialogue.

Lopes said the two leaders had deepened political trust and identified new areas of co-operation, pointing to the institutionalisation of the Angola-Egypt Bilateral Commission as evidence of the will to turn political ties into benefits for both peoples. The reception, held at a Cairo hotel, drew members of the Egyptian government, diplomats, academics, students, business people and members of the Angolan community in Egypt.

The embassy screened a 10-minute documentary, "Angola and Egypt: 50 Years Building the Future", and Lopes and other Angolan diplomats laid a wreath at the monument to António Agostinho Neto, Angola's first president, in Heroes' Park in Zamalek. Lopes said the two countries were well placed to build partnerships in agriculture, industry, infrastructure, transport, energy, water and mineral resources.

Why it mattersMembers of the Angolan community in Egypt and Egyptian business people were among those invited to the reception marking the anniversary.
