Egypt Health Ministry Denies Salmonella Outbreak

The Ministry of Health confirmed no unusual Salmonella outbreaks are currently detected nationally, despite ongoing monitoring of international incidents and supply chains.

Egypt's Ministry of Health has confirmed that no unusual Salmonella outbreaks are currently occurring within the country. Minister of Health Dr. Khaled Abdel Ghaffar stated that the ministry actively monitors local and international health conditions using epidemiological surveillance systems, particularly for foodborne illnesses. This proactive monitoring, which includes continuous risk assessments, is a precautionary measure and does not indicate an immediate risk within Egypt.

Deputy Minister of Health Dr. Amr Kandil emphasized that the Preventive Medicine and Public Health sector tracks daily infection patterns and assesses reports based on scientific standards. He confirmed that no unusual Salmonella outbreaks connected to recent international events have been recorded nationally. The ministry is tracking specific international incidents, including over 306 cases in Belgium and 207 cases with one death in the United Kingdom, which have led to product recalls in those regions.

The Ministry of Health is implementing response protocols that rely on swift case identification, epidemiological investigation, and supply tracing. They are also advising the public on essential food safety practices, such as thorough hand washing, separating raw and cooked foods, cooking food to safe temperatures, and storing perishables properly. The ministry reaffirmed that its epidemiological surveillance infrastructure remains proactive and operational to safeguard public health.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Health's confirmation of no domestic Salmonella outbreaks means the public can continue to trust local food supplies without immediate health concerns.

Egypt Targets Doubling Oil Output Amid Refinery Projects

The nation's energy strategy includes advancing six refinery projects with investments exceeding four billion dollars to boost production and regional trade.

Egypt aims to double its crude oil and condensate output within five years, according to a presidential announcement. This ambitious target is part of a broader energy strategy that also involves advancing six refinery projects valued at over four billion dollars. The plan, reviewed by President Al-Sisi, includes enhancing production through seismic surveys, advanced technologies, and new contractual frameworks, with a focus on developing previously untapped discoveries.

The strategy seeks to bolster Egypt's position as a regional hub for petroleum product trading and gas liquefaction. Minister Karim Badawi presented plans for exploration, development, and production, including new wells expected to become operational by December 2026. The government is actively working to attract international energy companies by ensuring timely payments for past dues and current obligations.

In the refining sector, the six projects aim to modernize facilities, increase the domestic supply of higher-value petroleum products, and reduce the country's reliance on fuel imports. President Al-Sisi has urged the government to offer further incentives for international partners to expand exploration and field development, emphasizing the need for measurable implementation programs and increased value generation from mineral resources.

Why it mattersFarmers could be affected by shifts in fuel availability and cost impacting agricultural inputs and transportation.

Egypt Demands Binding Drought Protocol Amidst Water Scarcity

Minister Hani Sewilam calls for international action on drought, warning it threatens water, food security, and socioeconomic stability, especially for nations like Egypt.

Egypt is pushing for a legally binding international protocol on drought, citing its escalating threat to water and food security, livelihoods, and socioeconomic stability. Water Resources and Irrigation Minister Hani Sewilam stated at the UNCCD COP17 in Mongolia that drought is a critical issue for countries with severe water scarcity, like Egypt, where desert land constitutes over 95% of the nation.

Sewilam advocated for a shift from reactive crisis management to proactive prevention and preparedness, emphasizing enhanced early-warning systems and integrated water and land resource management. Egypt seeks concrete progress on a drought protocol to facilitate collective international action and bolster the resilience of vulnerable nations against drought impacts. He also urged increased financing and innovative financial tools for developing and African countries most affected by land degradation.

The minister also highlighted Egypt's "Water System 2.0" initiative, which incorporates advanced monitoring technologies like satellite imagery and drones, infrastructure upgrades, and digital solutions for water management. Discussions with the French Ambassador explored investment, financing, and joint projects, including the Fourth National Drainage Programme and pumping station rehabilitation.

Why it mattersEgypt's call for a drought protocol directly impacts farmers by addressing water scarcity and land degradation, which are crucial for agricultural production and livelihoods.

British University in Egypt Hosts Torch Relay for All-Africa Games

Olympic champion Feryal Abdelaziz carried the flame at the El Shorouk City campus as part of national preparations for the continental university sports competition coming.

The British University in Egypt hosted a torch relay ceremony on Wednesday for the 12th All-Africa University Games, branded "Cairo 2026." Olympic champion and university graduate Feryal Abdelaziz carried the flame across the El Shorouk City campus. The event is part of Egypt's national preparations to host the continent's largest multi-sport university competition, which will take place from September 8 to 16, 2026.

The "Cairo 2026" torch began its journey at the New Administrative Capital and is visiting major Egyptian universities, including The American University in Cairo and Cairo University, before the Games. The All-Africa University Games, organized every two years by the Federation of Africa University Sports, will bring together approximately 2,000 student-athletes from 59 universities across 16 African nations, competing in 18 sports. This year marks the first inclusion of para-athletes.

The ceremony, held under the slogan "One Flame, One Continent," was led by University President and Vice-Chancellor Professor Mohamed Loutfi MBE and attended by Professor Ashraf Sobhy, President of the Federation of Africa University Sports, and Dr Ahmed Kamel Mahdy, Chair of the Organising Committee. The event aligns with directives from Egyptian President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi to showcase Egypt's leading role in university sport.

Why it mattersThe event highlights Egypt's role in hosting a major continental university sports competition, involving 2,000 student-athletes from 59 universities across 16 African countries.

Egypt Pursues Legal Action Over Wrestler Defections

The Ministry of Youth and Sports has referred two wrestler defections, one in Rome and another in Slovakia, to the Public Prosecution and suspended a delegation head.

Egypt's Ministry of Youth and Sports is pursuing legal action following the defections of two wrestlers, Mohamed Mustafa Ali El-Shami in Rome and Ziad Haggag in Slovakia. El-Shami disappeared during a transit layover in Rome while returning from the Mediterranean Games. Haggag defected during the World Junior Wrestling Championships in Slovakia. Preliminary investigations suggest negligence and inadequate supervision of the delegations.

In response to the incidents, the Egyptian Olympic Committee has suspended Ibrahim Adel Ibrahim Mustafa Al-Attar, the head of the wrestling delegation, and referred him to the Ethics Committee. The ministry had previously ordered an inquiry into Haggag's disappearance. Despite these defections, ministry officials highlighted the national wrestling team's strong performance at the Mediterranean Games, where they secured seven medals, including two gold.

The circumstances of El-Shami's disappearance involved him slipping away from the group during a layover in Rome. Attempts to retrieve him failed, leading the team coach to remain in Rome while the rest of the delegation returned to Cairo. The ministry stressed that these events should not detract from the team's overall success at the games.

Why it mattersThese defections and subsequent legal actions may impact the reputation and perceived stability of Egyptian sports delegations, potentially affecting international perceptions and future participation.
