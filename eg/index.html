Egyptian pound hits historic low against US dollar

The Egyptian pound closed at 55.35 per dollar on Thursday, a record drop against the US currency.

The Egyptian pound reached a historic low against the US dollar on Thursday, closing at 55.35 pounds per dollar in commercial bank trading. This rate surpassed the previous record of 54.75 pounds set in April. The dollar’s selling rate reached 55.37 pounds at Al Baraka Bank, and between 55.33 and 55.35 pounds at other major Egyptian banks.

Central Bank of Egypt Governor Hassan Abdalla stated that the exchange rate moves dynamically with economic conditions and that fluctuations are normal. He urged the public not to worry about currency movements, emphasizing the banking system's resilience. Abdalla explained that a fixed exchange rate would be more concerning than a flexible one amid changing circumstances.

The pound has depreciated by approximately 13.8 percent against the dollar since February 27, when the official rate was around 47.72 pounds per dollar. The dollar’s buying and selling rates rose by between 0.90 and 0.92 pounds across five banks on Thursday.

Why it mattersEgyptian citizens and businesses are affected by the Egyptian pound's historic drop against the US dollar.

Egypt rejects Turkey's maritime deal

Cairo insists on international law for Mediterranean zones during talks with Ankara.

Egypt's Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty met his Turkish counterpart Hakan Fidan in Istanbul on Friday. Abdelatty rejected Turkey's 2019 memorandum on maritime zones in the Mediterranean. He stressed adherence to the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea for exclusive economic zones and continental shelves.

The Egyptian minister also emphasized support for Libya's unity, sovereignty, and stability during the talks. He stated Egypt's commitment to Libyan institutions that serve the people's interests. Fidan acknowledged Egypt's concerns about Libya and agreed on continued consultation.

The meeting occurred on the sidelines of Quad mechanism discussions with three European nations. Both ministers agreed to coordinate actions to support security and stability in Libya.

Why it mattersEgypt's stance on maritime zones affects its exclusive economic zone and continental shelf in the Mediterranean.

Egypt health minister cites spending gains

Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar highlighted 16 presidential initiatives benefiting 95 million citizens with LE44 billion.

Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar stated on Tuesday that Egypt's leadership support for the healthcare sector is unprecedented. He spoke at the Egypt Forum 2026, recalling a significant disease burden and inadequate budgets in 2014. The minister said state development plans have improved the sector, pointing to 16 presidential initiatives that cost LE44 billion and reached 95 million citizens.

Abdel Ghaffar stressed that healthcare success is measured by citizen experience, not just spending. However, government data shows a decline in public hospital beds from 98,291 in 2013 to 84,225 in 2024, while private beds increased. The total number of hospital beds nationwide decreased from 124,300 in 2013 to 120,239 in 2024.

Egypt has not met the constitutional mandate of allocating at least three percent of GNP to public healthcare since 2014, with spending rarely exceeding 1.7 percent. For the current fiscal year, the government allocated 1.51 percent of GNP. A new calculation method, including off-budget entities like police and military hospitals, is now used to claim the minimum spending threshold is met.

Why it mattersEgyptians are affected by a decrease in public hospital beds despite government claims of healthcare spending success.

Egypt interior minister promises social media complaint results

Minister Mahmoud Tawfiq stated the public has a right to know how online complaints are investigated and resolved.

Egypt's Minister of Interior Mahmoud Tawfiq announced on October 21 that citizens have the right to know the outcomes of investigations into complaints shared on social media. Speaking at the Egypt Forum 2026, Tawfiq emphasized the ministry's commitment to balancing transparency with individual privacy.

Tawfiq explained that online complaints can become matters of public concern, especially if they relate to public security, but acknowledged some may be fabricated. The ministry plans to publish verified findings on its official social media pages, alongside the original complaint, to demonstrate how issues are addressed.

This approach aims to build trust between citizens and security authorities by showing the speed of response to public concerns. The ministry will continue to present these findings transparently while protecting personal privacy.

Why it mattersEgyptian citizens will be informed about the results of their complaints made on social media.

Israeli forces arrest cancer patient and daughter returning to Gaza

Amal Ghazy al-Rify and her daughter were detained at Karam Abu Salem checkpoint after two years of medical treatment in Egypt.

Israeli forces arrested a cancer patient, Amal Ghazy al-Rify, and her daughter Yasmin Rifaat al-Rify on Tuesday evening as they returned to Gaza from Egypt. The two women had been in Egypt for nearly two years seeking medical treatment for Amal's cancer. They were detained at the Karam Abu Salem checkpoint after passing through the Rafah crossing, which had reopened that day.

A relative, Haitham al-Rify, stated that Amal's health had deteriorated, prompting their decision to return to Gaza despite incomplete treatment. He condemned their arrest, emphasizing that neither woman is involved in political or military activities. The family has not been informed of their whereabouts and has contacted the International Committee of the Red Cross for details.

The family appealed on Wednesday for urgent intervention from human rights organizations to uncover the fate of Amal and Yasmine and secure their release. 29 patients and 42 companions had left Gaza on Tuesday for treatment in Egypt, including critical and chronic disease cases.
