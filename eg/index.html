Egypt condemns Israeli strike on Gaza hospital that killed at least six

Cairo calls for an immediate halt to Israeli violations of the ceasefire agreement after the Nasser Medical Complex was hit.

Egypt has condemned an Israeli airstrike on the Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis, southern Gaza, which struck shortly after midnight on Thursday, 12 November, and killed at least six Palestinians while injuring dozens more.

Gaza's Health Ministry said the blast hit the hospital's surgical building and killed patients, medical staff and their companions, including a doctor and a child, and caused extensive damage to several medical departments. The Israeli military confirmed the strike and said it had targeted a senior Hamas commander.

Egypt's Ministry of Foreign Affairs said attacks on healthcare facilities breach international humanitarian law and threaten the ceasefire. Cairo urged the international community to enforce the truce and stop further attacks so the peace plan can move forward.

Why it mattersPatients, doctors and families inside Gaza's hospitals now face strikes on the medical buildings they rely on, and Egypt has asked the international community to enforce the ceasefire.

Egypt plans to use Red Sea coast land to cut public debt

The Ministry of Finance will offer state land on the coast through long-term leases, joint ventures and debt-for-investment swaps.

Egypt's Ministry of Finance is turning prime state-owned land along the Red Sea coast into a source of revenue for reducing public debt. Under a new master plan for the region, the government will offer land under usufruct, a long-term lease, launch joint ventures with private developers and carry out debt-for-investment swaps rather than sell the land outright.

Proceeds from these deals will go directly toward paying down public debt and easing debt-service costs, the ministry said, freeing budget space for social protection, healthcare and education. Officials say major investment deals along the coast will be key to accelerating the fall in debt.

The push builds on a fiscal tightening campaign that has cut the debt-to-GDP ratio from 96 percent to 81.9 percent over three years. A primary budget surplus reached 4.9 percent of GDP in the 2025/2026 fiscal year.

Egypt strips Habib brothers of citizenship over foreign nationality

Two Cabinet decisions revoke the Egyptian nationality of Anas and Tarek Habib for taking another nationality without government approval.

Two Cabinet decisions revoking the citizenship of Anas and Tarek Habib were published in the Official Gazette on Thursday, 12 November. The brothers acquired foreign nationality without the permission that Egyptian law requires, and the orders were signed by Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly on 1 and 2 November.

Decision No. 90 of 2026 removed the citizenship of Tarek Ibrahim Abdel-Rahman Habib, born in Beheira Governorate in September 1994. Decision No. 93 covered Anas Ibrahim Abdel-Rahman Mohamed Habib, born in Saudi Arabia in July 1998. Both followed a recommendation from the Minister of Interior and Cabinet approval, under Nationality Law No. 26 of 1975.

Anas Habib lives in the Netherlands and drew public attention in July 2025, when he filmed himself padlocking the gates of the Egyptian Embassy in The Hague during protests over aid restrictions on Gaza. The decisions do not say which foreign nationality either brother took.

Why it mattersEgyptians who take another nationality without ministerial permission can now lose their citizenship by Cabinet decision, as these two cases show.

Business · 2026-11-14

Egypt unlocks EU digital signature trust

Egypt is the first non-European nation to align with the EU's electronic signature framework, streamlining cross-border digital trade.

Egypt has become the first country outside Europe to reach full technical alignment with the European Union’s electronic signature and seal framework. The Information Technology Industry Development Agency (ITIDA) and the European Commission signed an administrative arrangement that places Egypt on the EU’s Advanced Electronic Signatures (AdES) Trust List. Egyptian and European companies and government bodies can now verify digital contracts, stamps and official documents across borders without the paperwork that used to slow them down.

Cairo is positioned as a digital bridge between Europe, the Middle East and Africa. ITIDA, which acts as Egypt’s root digital trust authority, licenses, regulates and audits local e-signature providers against international cybersecurity and encryption standards. Under the deal, the European Commission will add Egypt’s trusted providers to its third-country list, and Egypt will mirror the EU’s trusted list domestically.

The agreement delivers a flagship digital pledge made at the October 2025 EU-Egypt Summit in Brussels. The report says it is meant to cut red tape, speed up deal closings and make Egyptian exporters and tech firms more competitive in e-commerce.

Why it mattersEgyptian exporters and tech firms can close cross-border deals faster, with digital contracts verified without the old paperwork.

Tech · 2026-11-14

Cairo Airport launches AI photo kiosks

New interactive booths allow travelers to capture souvenir portraits with historical backdrops.

Cairo International Airport has launched free, interactive photo kiosks across its terminals to allow travelers to capture souvenir portraits. The artificial intelligence-equipped booths let passengers select historical eras to customize their backdrop and instantly save or share the digital keepsakes.

This initiative by the Cairo Airport Company aims to enhance the passenger journey by adding playful, modern amenities. Airport operators are increasingly investing in digital entertainment and hospitality to meet global aviation standards, moving beyond just check-in speed and transit logistics.

The passenger experience upgrades coincide with sustained growth in air traffic, with Cairo International Airport handling 2,781,163 passengers and 19,927 flights in October 2026. The airport continues to modernize its infrastructure and services.

Why it mattersTravelers passing through Cairo International Airport can now create digital keepsakes using new AI photo kiosks.
