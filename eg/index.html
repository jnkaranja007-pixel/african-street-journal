Egypt condemns Saudi airport attack, reaffirms solidarity

Cairo expresses strong condemnation of the Dammam airport attack, stating Saudi security is integral to Egyptian national security.

Egypt's Ministry of Foreign Affairs strongly condemned an attack on King Fahd Airport in Dammam, Saudi Arabia, on Wednesday evening. The ministry stated on Facebook that the attack violated international law and targeted air navigation, facilities, and civilians. Egypt rejects such practices that escalate tension and destabilize the region.

The attack resulted in two civilian deaths and eight injuries, according to the Saudi General Authority of Civil Aviation. The deceased included a Saudi citizen and an Indian resident. The injured comprised individuals from Saudi Arabia, India, Yemen, and Nepal. Egypt reaffirmed its full solidarity with Saudi Arabia in defending its territory and citizens.

President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi stated on Wednesday that Egypt’s proposed military effort was aimed at defending Saudi Arabian territory, not attacking Yemen. He added that Egypt would defend any other Arab state if asked, emphasizing that Arab national security is indivisible and the stability of Gulf states is a matter of national security for Egypt.

Why it mattersEgypt's Ministry of Foreign Affairs issued a statement condemning an attack on Saudi Arabia's King Fahd Airport.

Egypt ends GERD dam negotiations with Ethiopia

Minister Hani Sewilam stated Cairo exhausted all negotiation paths over the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam dispute.

Egypt has concluded all negotiation efforts with Ethiopia regarding the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD) dispute, according to Water Resources and Irrigation Minister Hani Sewilam. Speaking at the Egypt Forum, Sewilam stated that Cairo exercised patience but received no response from Ethiopia that would lead to an agreement. He added that Egypt participated in negotiations and signed the 2015 Declaration of Principles to demonstrate good intentions and seek peaceful coexistence.

Sewilam explained that Ethiopia failed to implement the agreed principles, leading to ongoing disputes over dam filling and operation rules, especially during drought. He accused Ethiopia of evading signature on a balanced draft agreement, reached during Washington-hosted talks with World Bank participation, which aimed to protect water rights. The primary contention remains the lack of a binding agreement for dam management during droughts and mechanisms for data sharing.

The GERD project began in 2011, with Ethiopia asserting its right to utilize water resources for development, while Cairo demands a legally binding agreement to safeguard its water security. Sewilam also noted progress in Egypt's relations with other Nile Basin countries, with 10 of the 11 nations agreeing to reopen discussions on the Nile Basin Cooperative Framework Agreement.

Why it mattersEgypt's water security is threatened by the lack of a binding agreement on the operation of the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam.

Egypt signs EGP 11 trillion development deal with Talaat Moustafa Group

President Sisi announced the agreement for four mixed-use projects and directed the construction of hundreds of thousands of rental housing units.

Egypt has agreed an EGP 11 trillion (USD 202 billion) deal with the Talaat Moustafa Group to develop four major mixed-use projects. The agreement was announced on Tuesday, October 20, during Egypt Forum 2026. President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi also directed the construction of hundreds of thousands of new rental housing units.

The projects include developments north of Madinaty and an 11.3-million-square-metre site in central Alexandria, alongside an industrial and logistics project in Abu Qir and a North Coast destination. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly stated that the government aims to ensure citizens across income groups can access suitable housing over the next four years, combining rental and ownership options.

Madbouly said the government seeks to revive rental housing, which declined from the 1960s and 1970s, as a key option alongside ownership. He noted that rental housing accounts for about 50% of the market in many developed economies. The government has implemented, is implementing, or is completing around two million housing units in recent years.

Why it mattersEgyptians will have access to new rental and ownership housing options over the next four years.

Education · 2026-10-23

egypt forum 2026 claims attendance rise

government says higher school attendance allows focus on learning quality despite teacher shortages.

Egypt Forum 2026, mandated by President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi, showcased improvements in school attendance on Monday. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbuly announced that basic education would anchor the forum, with a session titled “What Do Our Children Learn… and Does It Prepare Them for the Future?”. The government presented increased attendance as a success, enabling a policy shift from expanding access to ensuring learning quality.

Education Minister Mohamed Abdel Latif stated that the forum aimed to transition focus to learning quality, citing a video on education "challenges and achievements." Mada Masr found that increased regular testing, a recent curriculum change, is a driver of higher attendance. This policy faces criticism from families and MPs who argue it strains students and teachers without improving outcomes.

The forum followed public and parliamentary backlash against the ministry's decisions, including compulsory weekly assessments and curriculum shakeups. Over 15,000 public inquiries were submitted on education ahead of the event, with the Cabinet staging the narrative. The Egyptian Initiative for Personal Rights noted that the focus on testing conflicts with pedagogical research.

Why it mattersEgyptian students and teachers face increased strain due to new compulsory testing policies implemented by the Ministry of Education.

Hapag-Lloyd plans more Suez Canal transits

The shipping company discussed resuming services with the Suez Canal Authority chairman in Ismailia.

German shipping giant Hapag-Lloyd is planning to increase its Suez Canal transits, adding to the two services already operating between Asia and Europe. Suez Canal Authority Chairman Ossama Rabiee met with Hapag-Lloyd Senior Managing Director Juan Pablo Richards in Ismailia to discuss the potential return of more services.

Hapag-Lloyd is monitoring Red Sea safety and maritime indicators to adjust sailing schedules as traffic gradually resumes through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and the Suez Canal. Rabiee affirmed the canal's readiness to accommodate returning services, highlighting the SCA's fleet modernization and service development. Discussions also included potential cooperation on training for ship captains at the Damietta Alliance Container Terminal.

The move by Hapag-Lloyd reflects a broader industry trend, with companies like Maersk also transitioning services back to the Suez route. This route saves 10 to 14 days on Asia-Europe voyages compared to the Cape of Good Hope. In the third quarter of 2026, 166 containerships transited the canal, up from 16 in the same period of 2025, with tonnage rising to 29 million net tons.

Why it mattersThe Suez Canal Authority expects to generate US$5.56 billion in revenue in 2027 from increased vessel transits.
