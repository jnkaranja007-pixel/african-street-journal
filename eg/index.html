Egypt raises $500 million from dollar bonds

The Finance Ministry sold additional bonds maturing in 2030 and 2033 to diversify funding.

Egypt raised USD 500 million on Tuesday by selling more bonds under two existing dollar issues, according to the Finance Ministry. The sale added USD 150 million to a bond maturing in 2030 and USD 350 million to one maturing in 2033. This reopening strategy allows the government to raise funds without creating new securities.

The Finance Ministry stated the sale is part of a plan to diversify funding tools and markets, attract new investor types, and extend the average maturity of Egypt's debt. The ministry noted the sale proceeded despite global economic challenges, geopolitical tensions, and increased uncertainty.

The ministry renewed its commitment to reducing external debt for state budget entities by USD 1 billion to USD 2 billion annually through greater repayment than borrowing abroad. In the last fiscal year, this debt was reduced by approximately USD 2.4 billion.

Why it mattersEgypt's government is raising funds through international debt markets to manage its finances and diversify borrowing.

Egypt targets 100,000 electric vehicles by 2032

Government plans to boost local manufacturing and reduce import dependence for automotive components.

Egypt aims to produce up to 100,000 electric vehicles annually by 2032, a move designed to cut import reliance and boost domestic manufacturing. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly reviewed the government's plan to localize the automotive industry, emphasizing its importance for industrial growth and export potential to neighboring markets.

Industry Minister Khaled Hashem detailed partnerships to increase local production of automotive components, including an agreement for 8 million metal parts annually. A vocational school for mould and die manufacturing is also planned. Discussions are underway for local battery supply for buses and vehicles, and for electric motors.

A major international automaker plans to start producing three electric vehicle models in Egypt in 2027, targeting 100,000 vehicles annually by 2032. This company aims to increase local component content from 10% to 27% by 2032. China's Dongfeng has already begun electric vehicle manufacturing in partnership with SN Automotive and El Nasr Automotive.

Why it mattersEgyptians will have access to locally manufactured electric vehicles and components, potentially lowering costs and increasing availability.

Egypt seeks port integration with Slovenia

Cairo also wants a labour migration pact to send Egyptian workers to meet Slovenian demand.

Egypt aims to connect its national seaports with Slovenia’s Port of Koper and secure a labour migration agreement for Egyptian workers, Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly announced on Wednesday. Madbouly proposed expanding cooperation between the Port of Alexandria and the Port of Koper to include Egypt’s Ain Sokhna and Damietta ports. This integration seeks to boost Egyptian exports to the European Union and aid Slovenia’s access to African markets.

The discussions occurred during a meeting with Slovenian President Nataša Pirc Musar in Cairo, coinciding with an economic event featuring a 25-company Slovenian trade delegation. Madbouly stated Egypt's intention to formalise a labour migration agreement to supply workers for sectors Slovenia prioritises. He also outlined Egypt’s energy infrastructure plans, including an electricity interconnection with Greece and linking Cypriot gas fields to Egyptian facilities.

President Pirc Musar identified the automotive industry as a key area for expansion, with Egypt prioritising localised electric vehicle production. Cooperation opportunities were also noted in tourism and sciences like artificial intelligence. Madbouly proposed sending Egyptian students to Slovenian institutions, citing existing ties between Cairo University and the University of Ljubljana.

Why it mattersEgyptian workers could find employment in Slovenia, and Egyptian exports may gain easier access to the EU market.

Raya Cyber Solutions debuts 45+ cybersecurity services in Egypt

The Raya IT subsidiary is showcasing its cybersecurity portfolio for the first time at Cairo ICT 2026.

Raya Cyber Solutions, a subsidiary of Raya IT, debuted at Cairo ICT 2026 with over 45 cybersecurity services. The company is exhibiting at the Egypt International Exhibition Centre from November 7 to 10, aiming to expand its reach in Egypt's expanding cybersecurity market.

Egypt MP proposes end to leniency in honour killings

Fatma Adel's draft bill seeks tougher penalties for rape and sexual assault, removing honour as a defence.

Member of Parliament Fatma Adel has introduced a draft bill to amend Egypt’s Penal Code, Law No. 58 of 1937, aiming to end leniency in honour killings and sexual violence cases. The proposed legislation seeks to classify killings of a spouse or former spouse, or someone caught committing adultery with a spouse, as premeditated murder. It would also prevent judges from reducing sentences based on surprise, revenge, family honour, or social circumstances.

Adel, representing the Justice Party, stated the bill addresses the role of law in protecting victims of violence against women. The proposal challenges deeply rooted social attitudes by questioning whether cultural expectations or family reputation should ever justify violence. The draft bill aims to introduce tougher penalties for rape and sexual assault.

The proposed amendment to the Penal Code has reignited debate about violence against women. The legislation seeks to restrict the circumstances in which judges can reduce sentences for serious offences. This move directly impacts the legal framework surrounding crimes of passion and sexual assault within Egypt.

Why it mattersEgyptian women and their families are directly affected by proposed changes to laws concerning honour killings and sexual violence.
