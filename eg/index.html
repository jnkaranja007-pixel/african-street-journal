Climate · 2026-11-07

Egypt water ministry denies Sinai dam collapse reports

Viral social media claims of floodwaters breaching Nuweiba dams are false, officials state.

Egyptian water officials have dismissed social media reports claiming flood protection dams in Nuweiba, South Sinai, failed during recent storms. The Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation confirmed on November 6, 2026, that all primary dams successfully contained massive rainwater surges. Viral images of eroded ground were clarified to show damage near a coastal bridge support, not dam infrastructure.

While primary dams remained intact, localized secondary barriers sustained damage. A concrete barrier 300 meters from the asphalt and a gabion barrier 600 meters out require substantial maintenance. A third barrier near the Arab Hamdan area is undergoing technical evaluation for necessary repairs. The flood defense system in Wadi Watir basin captured approximately 13 million cubic meters of rainwater during the October 27 downpour.

The ministry has invested LE 660 million in the Wadi Watir basin's infrastructure, which includes 14 dams and 6 lakes. A LE 10 billion expansion plan is set to increase the total capture capacity to 76.37 million cubic meters. This future phase includes 9 new dams and 15 lakes, with work commencing after joint committees audit regional water structures.

Why it mattersResidents of South Sinai and travelers on the Sharm El-Sheikh–Taba highway are assured that critical flood protection infrastructure remains operational despite damage to secondary barriers.

Egypt arrests 691 in illegal gold mining crackdown

Two military officers died in exchanges of fire during operations targeting armed criminal groups in the Eastern Desert.

Egypt’s Armed Forces apprehended 691 individuals, including 151 Egyptians and 540 foreign nationals, in a campaign against illegal gold mining in the Eastern Desert. Military Spokesperson Colonel Ahmed Othman Ali announced on Thursday, 5 November, that the operation also seized 34 weapons, 6,307 rounds of ammunition, 317 vehicles, and 916 pieces of technical equipment. The campaign also resulted in the removal of 4,488 tents and makeshift shelters.

Colonel Othman stated that the operations targeted armed criminal groups involved in unauthorised gold extraction, some linked to cross-border weapons trafficking, drug smuggling, and illegal migration. The first phase of the campaign took place near Marsa Alam, with the second targeting the Jabal Eiqaat area. During the operations, two military officers, Colonel Ahmed Samir and Lieutenant Colonel Ahmed Gouda, were killed in exchanges of fire.

The fourth phase of the campaign was scheduled to begin on Saturday, 7 November, following three previous phases conducted between 14 June and 29 October. Colonel Othman also reported that an additional 6,614 people had voluntarily surrendered before the operations commenced.

Why it mattersThe crackdown on illegal gold mining and associated criminal activities directly affects the 691 people arrested and the families of the two military officers killed.

Agriculture · 2026-11-07

Egypt and IFAD sign $65M irrigation deals

Two agreements aim to modernize irrigation, strengthen climate resilience, and boost farmer incomes across 12,600 hectares.

Egypt and the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) signed two financing agreements totalling US$65.32 million to modernize irrigation and improve agricultural resilience to climate change. The majority of the funds, US$64.85 million, will support the Climate-Resilient On-Farm Water Management in the Nile Valley (CROWN) project.

The CROWN project will modernize irrigation and drainage systems, promote climate-smart farming, and enhance farmers’ access to markets and finance, according to IFAD. This initiative is expected to benefit around 90,000 rural households and improve water management across 12,600 hectares of farmland. A separate US$470,000 grant will fund the Positioning Egypt for Agricultural Resilience and Leadership (PEARL) project.

Egypt faces water scarcity with less than 500 cubic meters per person annually, half the threshold for scarcity, as the Nile provides over 98% of its water. The CROWN project focuses on efficiency through climate-smart practices and improved market access. The PEARL project will build institutional expertise through training and digital tools, benefiting approximately 2,000 people.

Why it mattersEgyptian farmers will benefit from modernized irrigation, climate-smart practices, and improved market access, potentially increasing productivity and incomes.

Politics · 2026-11-07

Egypt condemns missile attack on Medina

Cairo reaffirms solidarity with Saudi Arabia and its measures to defend its territory and citizens.

Egypt on Friday strongly condemned an attempt to target Medina with a ballistic missile launched by the Houthi group. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs stated the attack violated international law and threatened regional security and peace efforts.

The Coalition to Restore Legitimacy in Yemen announced on Thursday the interception of the missile. Saudi Civil Defense had issued an alert for a potential threat in Yanbu Governorate.

Egypt reaffirmed its full solidarity with Saudi Arabia, stating that the Kingdom's security is closely linked to Egyptian national security and is a pillar of regional stability.

Why it mattersEgypt's condemnation of the missile attack and reaffirmation of solidarity with Saudi Arabia directly involve Egypt in regional security matters.

Sport · 2026-11-07

Egypt hosts 13th World Golf Awards 2026

Madinaty Golf Club will host the international ceremony for the first time in Africa on November 7.

Egypt is hosting the 13th edition of the World Golf Awards (WGA 2026) on Saturday, November 7, at Madinaty Golf Club. International golf media highlighted Egypt's first time hosting the ceremony on the African continent. This event strengthens Egypt’s international golf tourism presence and its standing as a destination for global sporting events.

Golf Today, Golf Business News, and Golf Industry Asia were among the specialised outlets covering the awards. They noted Madinaty Golf Club's courses meet international standards and its facilities offer top-quality amenities. Omar Hesham Talaat, CEO of Business Development at Talaat Moustafa Group (TMG), expressed pride in Madinaty Golf Club hosting the WGA 2026.

TMG's Madinaty Golf Club has been named Egypt’s Best Golf Course five times and Africa’s Best Golf Course three times at the World Golf Awards. Talaat stated the event is a milestone for showcasing TMG's sports and golf facilities. Hosting the ceremony in Africa reflects the group's vision for integrated destinations combining sport with lifestyle.

Why it mattersEgypt's hosting of the World Golf Awards 2026 will boost its profile as a destination for international golf tourism and sporting events.
