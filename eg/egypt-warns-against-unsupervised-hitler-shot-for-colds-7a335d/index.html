Egypt warns against unsupervised “Hitler Shot” for colds

Health ministry spokesperson Hossam Abdel-Ghaffar says the injection can cause harm and that antibiotics do not work against viruses.

Source: Egypt Independent · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Egypt’s Health Ministry warned on Saturday against using the so-called “Hitler Shot” or “triple injection” to treat the common cold, saying it lacks a sound scientific basis and can harm health. The mixture typically contains an antibiotic and painkillers, and sometimes cortisone, the ministry’s spokesperson, Hossam Abdel-Ghaffar, said.

Abdel-Ghaffar said antibiotics are ineffective against viruses and that unnecessary use contributes to antimicrobial resistance. He advised people to receive treatment under professional supervision. For influenza, he said vaccination is available year-round, though it is best given before winter; it reduces the severity of symptoms and complications, particularly for children, older people and those with chronic illnesses or weakened immune systems.

The spokesperson said artificial intelligence and telephone consultations cannot replace an in-person examination, which may require clinical assessment and medical tests. He also denied shortages of chronic-disease medicines, including treatments for hypertension and diabetes, saying alternatives with the same active ingredient, dose and method of administration are available when some brand-name drugs are disrupted.

Why it mattersPeople considering the injection are told to seek supervised treatment, while influenza vaccine is available throughout the year.
