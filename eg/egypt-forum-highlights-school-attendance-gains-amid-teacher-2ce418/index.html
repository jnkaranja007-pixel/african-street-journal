Egypt forum highlights school attendance gains amid teacher shortages

Government officials presented improved school attendance as a success at the Egypt Forum 2026, despite ongoing concerns about teacher and classroom scarcity.

Source: Mada Masr · 2026-10-22 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The Egypt Forum 2026, an event mandated by President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi, showcased a reported increase in school attendance as a key achievement in the nation's education sector. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbuly announced that basic education would anchor the forum's opening session, focusing on whether current curricula prepare students for future labor markets. The government framed this attendance boost as a transition point, allowing a shift in policy focus from expanding access to ensuring learning quality.

However, Mada Masr's investigation into the attendance claims suggests that increased regular testing, a recent curriculum change, is a significant driver of the reported gains. This innovation has drawn criticism from families and members of parliament who argue it strains students and teachers without improving educational outcomes, and potentially conflicts with pedagogical research.

Despite these criticisms and a general backdrop of chronic low spending in education, the government used the forum to highlight progress. The Education Minister outlined achievements, emphasizing the rise in school attendance as a foundation for future improvements in learning quality, a narrative presented amidst public inquiries and parliamentary pushback over recent ministry decisions.

Why it mattersStudents and teachers are affected by increased testing and potential burnout due to teacher shortages, despite reported attendance improvements.
