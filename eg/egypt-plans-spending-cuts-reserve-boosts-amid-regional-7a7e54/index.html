Egypt plans spending cuts, reserve boosts amid regional tensions

Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly announced measures to rationalise government spending and increase strategic commodity stockpiles.

Source: Daily News Egypt · 2026-11-03 · 1 min read

Egypt is preparing to cut government spending and increase its strategic commodity reserves to counter potential economic fallout from escalating regional geopolitical tensions. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly announced these measures on Monday evening during a meeting of the Central Crisis Management Committee in the New Administrative Capital. The government aims to ease the impact of the crisis on local and global markets by boosting goods and product inventories and reducing state consumption of fuel and energy.

Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk and Planning and Economic Development Minister Ahmed Rostom are finalising a strategy to rationalise public expenditure and maximise state resource efficiency. Minister of Supply and Internal Trade Sherif Farouk stated that Egypt’s strategic reserves of various commodities are sufficient for an average of six months, with new contracts signed to further boost inventories. An initiative to reduce prices of basic goods currently offers 30 commodities at 15% to 25% discounts.

The crisis management meeting included Central Bank Governor Hassan Abdalla, ministers of electricity, agriculture, petroleum, information, and industry, alongside deputy ministers and military officials. The proposed plan guarantees the continuation of economic and productive activities and the availability of financial allocations for basic commodities and petroleum materials.

Why it mattersEgyptians will be affected by government spending cuts and potential price changes on basic goods.
