Egypt warns it will act under 1976 defence pact to back Sudan

Cairo says it will take every step international law allows to protect Sudan's sovereignty and what it calls its red lines, as Sudan's war enters a fourth year.

Source: Egyptian Streets · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

On Tuesday, 24 November, Egypt said it was prepared to take every measure permitted by international law and by its joint defence pact with Sudan, in a statement issued as Sudanese Prime Minister Kamil Idris ended a visit to Cairo. The warning repeats a message Egypt first gave nearly a year ago.

The statement defined Egypt's red lines as Sudan's sovereignty, territorial integrity, resources and state institutions, and rejected any parallel entities. Both governments condemned drone attacks on power stations and water facilities, which they said rebel militias launched from an unnamed neighbouring country, and reaffirmed support for the Sudanese Armed Forces.

The two prime ministers also pledged to defend their water rights under the 1959 Nile Waters Agreement and criticised Ethiopia's unilateral construction of its dam. Egypt already shelters more Sudanese refugees than any other country.

Why it mattersSudanese power stations and water facilities are under drone attack, and Egypt has now put its defence pact on the table as a response.
