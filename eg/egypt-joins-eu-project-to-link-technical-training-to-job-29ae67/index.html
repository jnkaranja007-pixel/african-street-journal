Egypt Joins EU Project to Link Technical Training to Job Market

The AlignVET project, funded by the EU's Erasmus+ programme, aims to develop vocational curricula based on industry needs.

Egypt's ready-made garments and home textiles sector is participating in the EU-funded AlignVET project, which seeks to align technical and vocational education with labor market demands. Workshops held in Sweden brought together Egyptian and European partners to create curricula based on skills identified by industry leaders. The Ready-Made Garments and Home Textiles Chamber at the Federation of Egyptian Industries is a key participant, contributing to the identification of necessary job skills.

Mohamed Abdel Salam, Chairperson of the Ready-Made Garments and Home Textiles Chamber, emphasized that manufacturers are best positioned to define the skills needed in factories, making their input crucial for graduate preparedness. He noted the sector's rapid technological changes necessitate continuous workforce development to boost competitiveness and exports. Alaa Ashmawy, Chairperson of the National Authority for Quality Assurance and Accreditation of Education (NAQAAE), stated the project connects educational institutions, practical training, and employers.

The AlignVET project will integrate modern skills like artificial intelligence and data analysis into vocational programs, according to Jan Henry Hanna, Acting President of Beni Suef Technological University. The first curriculum for the garments sector is expected by June 2027. The initiative aims to create a sustainable model where employers play a central role in shaping future training programs to ensure graduates possess market-relevant capabilities.

Why it mattersEgyptian technical education programs are being updated to better match the skills required by the ready-made garments and home textiles industry, potentially improving graduate employability.
