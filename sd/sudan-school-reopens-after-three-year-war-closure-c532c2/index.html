Sudan school reopens after three-year war closure

Community efforts and local donations revive Sango Mixed Secondary School in South Darfur, now serving more than 115 students.

Source: Radio Tamazuj · 2026-11-16 · 1 min read

Sango Mixed Secondary School in South Darfur's Radom Locality has resumed classes, three years after it closed because of the war between the Sudan Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces that broke out in April 2023. The school had suffered extensive damage to its buildings and a shortage of teaching materials during the closure.

Headteacher Mustafa Al-Toum said community-led initiatives and contributions from local philanthropists and residents made the reopening possible. The school now has 12 teachers and more than 115 students. Al-Toum urgently appealed to government authorities, humanitarian organisations and people from Radom locality for help to rehabilitate the buildings and supply teaching materials.

Fatima Babiker, a third-year student, said students in their final year of secondary school are determined to keep learning despite the destruction the war has caused. Sango is located in Radom locality, which borders the Central African Republic.

Why it mattersMore than 115 students in Radom locality can resume secondary schooling that the war interrupted for three years, but the school still needs repairs and teaching materials.
