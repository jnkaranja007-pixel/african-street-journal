21 countries meet in Geneva to discuss Sudan war

UN envoy Pekka Haavisto says prisoner exchanges and exam coordination are first steps toward a truce.

Source: Radio Dabanga · 2026-10-26 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Intensive international and UN efforts are underway to end the war in Sudan, starting with a Geneva meeting of 21 countries. The talks highlighted the need for immediate confidence-building measures. UN secretary-general’s personal envoy Pekka Haavisto stated that prisoner exchanges and coordination on Sudan’s secondary school certificate examinations would be the first steps.

The Quad nations—Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and the United States—held "extremely productive" meetings in Geneva. Discussions focused on securing a humanitarian truce and paving the way for a permanent ceasefire. US officials stated that there is no military solution to the conflict and urged all parties to engage in negotiations for an inclusive dialogue leading to civilian rule.

Political analyst Mohamed Torshin described the Geneva meeting as a "pivotal step." He believes successful implementation of prisoner releases and exam coordination could lead to a three-month ceasefire. Torshin noted the greatest challenge remains the military track, including the future of the Rapid Support Forces and the integration of the Juba Peace Agreement.

Why it mattersSudanese secondary school students and prisoners of war are directly affected by the proposed confidence-building measures.
