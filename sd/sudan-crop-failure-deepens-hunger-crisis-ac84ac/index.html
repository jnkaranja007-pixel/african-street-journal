Sudan crop failure deepens hunger crisis

Displaced families face soaring prices and malnutrition as food aid is cut and harvests fail in East Darfur.

Source: Radio Dabanga · 2026-10-23 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Displaced families across Sudan are struggling to find food as cuts to World Food Programme (WFP) assistance coincide with a near-total crop failure in East Darfur. Residents of Sheairia report soaring prices, worsening malnutrition, and shortages of medicines and therapeutic food for children.

The WFP's acting executive director, Carl Skau, stated the agency has the capacity to help millions but is running out of money. The General Coordination for Displaced People and Refugees warns that reduced food assistance could force families to skip meals and sell possessions. Spokesperson Adam Rijal said funding shortages could deprive families of essential meals.

Farmers in East Darfur harvested almost nothing of their main crops, including groundnuts, millet, sorghum, and okra, depriving households of food and income. A sack of millet costs about SDG 350,000, and a pound of onions costs around SDG 4,500. Pregnant women cannot obtain adequate diets, and children suffer from malnutrition.

Why it mattersDisplaced families and vulnerable communities in Sudan face worsening hunger, malnutrition, and desperation due to aid cuts and crop failure.
