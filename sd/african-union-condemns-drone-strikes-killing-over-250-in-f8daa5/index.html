African Union condemns drone strikes killing over 250 in Sudan

The AU calls for an immediate humanitarian ceasefire amid reports of civilian deaths in West Kordofan, South Darfur, Khartoum, and Abyei.

Source: Radio Dabanga · 2026-10-23 · 2 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The African Union has condemned drone attacks that killed over 250 people across Sudan and the disputed Abyei area in recent days. AU Commission chairperson Mahmoud Ali Youssouf called for an immediate humanitarian ceasefire and expressed deep concern over the civilian toll, displacement, and destruction of livelihoods. Reports indicate approximately 80 people died in El Dibb, West Kordofan, on October 18, while 23 died in Nyala, South Darfur, on October 16, and three children were killed in Khartoum on October 19.

In the disputed Abyei region, drone strikes on Amiet market on October 19 reportedly killed over 150 people, including Sudanese, South Sudanese, and foreign nationals. Sudan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs denied any involvement by its armed forces in the Abyei attack, while the RSF-aligned Government of Peace has called for an international investigation. The ministry accused the RSF of other attacks, including one on a UN peacekeeping camp in Kadugli in December 2025.

The RSF-aligned Government of Peace has filed a complaint with the Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for Sudan seeking an investigation into the Abyei market attack. Youssouf urged all parties to protect civilians and humanitarian personnel, ensure unhindered humanitarian access, and respect Sudan’s sovereignty. He also reaffirmed support for mediation efforts to secure a ceasefire and launch an inclusive, Sudanese-led political process.

Why it mattersCivilians in Sudan are directly affected by deadly drone strikes that cause deaths, displacement, and disruption of essential services.
