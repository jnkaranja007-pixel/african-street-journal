Amnesty urges war-crimes inquiry into RSF drone strikes in El Obeid

The rights group says nine attacks killed 37 civilians and injured 10 between June and August 2026, hitting homes and civilian infrastructure.

Source: Radio Tamazuj · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Amnesty International called for an investigation into Rapid Support Forces drone strikes in El Obeid, the capital of Sudan’s North Kordofan State, as possible war crimes. The group said it examined nine strikes in the city and its surroundings between June and August 2026, which killed 37 civilians and injured 10.

Amnesty said three strikes hit residential areas, four hit civilian infrastructure including the power plant, fuel stations and main market, and two targeted moving vehicles. The group said it found no military objectives near the strike sites at the time. It based its findings on interviews with 29 people from El Obeid, conducted between July and September 2026.

Amnesty Secretary General Agnès Callamard urged the United Nations and African Union to propose and implement civilian protection measures, including a protection force in Sudan. She also called for the United Nations Security Council to extend the arms embargo covering Darfur to the rest of Sudan and include drones.

Why it mattersEl Obeid residents and users of its power, fuel and market services face strikes that Amnesty says killed 37 civilians and injured 10.
