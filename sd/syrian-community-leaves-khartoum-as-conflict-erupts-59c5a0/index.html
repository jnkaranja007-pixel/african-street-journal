Syrian community leaves Khartoum as conflict erupts

Syrian families who settled in Sudan from the 1870s departed their homes in April 2023.

Source: African Arguments · 2026-11-07 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Syrian families who settled in Sudan from the 1870s departed their homes in April 2023. The community, whose members arrived from Greater Syria, had established significant commercial and agricultural enterprises by the 1950s. Their departure followed the outbreak of fighting between the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) on April 15, 2023.

Architect and photographer Arwa Ahmed observed 10 trucks carrying men in civilian dress on March 18, 2023, less than a month before the conflict began. The UNHCR has described the resulting humanitarian crisis as the world's most devastating. The Melkite Catholic Church on Jumhuriya Street, once a social and cultural hub for the community, was central until their departure.

The earliest Syrian settlers in Sudan, arriving in the 1870s, were merchants from Aleppo involved in interregional trade. A later wave in the 1900s included professionals recruited from the Syrian Protestant College. By the 1950s, businessmen like Nicola Malouf, who also developed a large-scale agricultural enterprise, played a central role in the Sudanese economy.

Why it mattersSyrian families who had lived in Sudan for generations were forced to leave their homes and businesses due to the conflict.
