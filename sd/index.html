Sudan power cut sparks price surge

A drone attack on a substation has left Rabak without electricity for two weeks, driving up costs for basic goods.

Residents of Rabak, in Sudan’s White Nile state, have endured a 14-day power blackout following a drone attack on the Mashkour electricity substation at the end of October. The outage has caused sharp price increases for essential services, with a block of ice now costing SDG 60,000 and charging a mobile phone rising to SDG 5,000.

Milling 3.5 kilogrammes of grain now costs SDG 10,000 as mills operate on generators, leading to long queues for flour. Water supplies have resumed at SDG 3,000 per container after pumps were restarted with generators. Children's education has halted after dark, forcing families to use charcoal irons for school uniforms, which cost SDG 3,000 per bag.

The prolonged power outage has made daily life a struggle in Rabak, with rising costs for food, water, refrigeration, and communications. There is no clear indication from officials on when electricity will be restored to the city.

Why it mattersResidents of Rabak face increased costs for essential goods and services due to a prolonged power outage.

Darfur faces famine after 90 per cent crop failure

19 emergency rooms report drought has devastated crops, forcing thousands of families from their homes across the region.

19 agricultural emergency rooms across Darfur say the current farming season has failed, raising fears of famine. Drought and irregular rainfall have devastated crops, and Justice Africa Sudan estimates harvest losses of around 90 per cent in key sorghum-producing regions. Jalal Eddin Abkar Yousif told Radio Dabanga that 2026 is one of the worst years Sudan has experienced, because of low rainfall and drought across very large parts of the country.

The emergency rooms first aimed to cultivate more than 1,800 feddans, but the target fell to 1,100 feddans because of limited resources, insufficient rainfall and inadequate funding. Intermittent rain caused poor germination and weak growth, and drought killed crops in some cultivated areas. Thousands of families have fled Mershing for Tawila, Shangil Tobaya and Nierteti, while others have crossed into Chad.

Adam Rijal, an official spokesperson for the body coordinating displaced people and refugees in Darfur, said about 538 families, totalling 3,227 people, arrived between 15 October and 6 November, according to field monitoring. Justice Africa Sudan is calling for an immediate, unconditional nationwide ceasefire and unhindered humanitarian access, warning that more than 75 per cent of Sudanese face acute food insecurity while aid has declined.

Why it mattersThousands of Darfur families have lost their harvests and livelihoods, and about 3,227 people from 538 families have arrived in new areas since mid-October.

South Darfur harvest fails due to drought

Mershing locality faces severe food shortages as insufficient rainfall decimates crops.

Civilians in Mershing locality, South Darfur, are experiencing dire conditions after a sharp decline in agricultural production. Insufficient rainfall, shrinking cultivated areas, and encroachments on farmland have led to a near-total failure of the summer harvest. Local authorities are appealing for urgent humanitarian support to expand health, water, and education services.

Oqba Abdel Hamid, the executive director of Mershing locality, stated that only between 40 and 50 per cent of farmers cultivated land this year, with crop production estimated at around 20 per cent. He attributes the decline to poor rainfall, economic difficulties, and farmers’ fears of herders encroaching on their land. These factors have caused many residents to abandon farming or move elsewhere.

Families are now moving towards displacement areas like Tawila and Shangil Tobaya, some influenced by misleading campaigns about available assistance. Abdel Hamid estimates 30 to 40 families recently travelled to Tawila. Previous movements to Shangil Tobaya saw families return after conditions did not meet expectations.

Why it mattersResidents of Mershing locality face increasing food needs and potential displacement due to failed harvests.

RSF accused of abuses and market closure in West Kordofan

Civilians in Foga and Wad Banda face beatings, levies, and confiscation of communication equipment.

The Rapid Support Forces (RSF) stand accused of committing abuses against civilians in Sudan’s West Kordofan State, specifically in the Foga and Wad Banda areas. The Dar Hamar Emergency Response Room reported that RSF personnel subjected civilians to beatings and humiliation at the mining mills market in Foga. The group also accused the RSF of imposing levies on mill operators, demanding a barrel of diesel fuel per mill, and looting the entire market.

Azzam Hamid, spokesperson for the Dar Hamar Emergency Response Room, stated that such conduct has become routine in northern West Kordofan localities, including En Nahud and Wad Banda. Hamid further alleged that RSF personnel confiscated Starlink satellite internet terminals, hindering residents' communication access amidst a deteriorating security situation. Fierce fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the RSF has also occurred near Foga over the past two days.

The RSF also shut down the market in Wad Banda. The confiscation of Starlink equipment restricts residents' ability to communicate and access telecommunications services. This follows two days of fierce fighting between the SAF and RSF in the vicinity of Foga.

Why it mattersResidents of West Kordofan face violence, economic disruption, and restricted communication due to alleged RSF actions.

Vinícius Jr sends video message to Sudanese boy who lost both legs

The Real Madrid winger spoke to 13-year-old Zaher in Arabic, a boy who lost both legs in a drone strike.

Real Madrid winger Vinícius Júnior sent a surprise video message in Arabic to 13-year-old Zaher, who lost both legs in Sudan's civil war. In the clip, the footballer addresses the boy directly with the words "Zaher, mahaba", meaning hello Zaher, while Zaher watches on a television screen in a dark room in Cairo.

Zaher lost his legs in a drone strike and had little access to specialist treatment. He had dreamed of walking and playing football again. A reporter first met him a year ago in the ruins of Khartoum, and on this visit the family greeted the reporters warmly in their Cairo apartment.

The United Nations children's agency, Unicef, says more than 17 million children in Sudan need humanitarian assistance, and that at least 4,300 have been killed or maimed since fighting between the army and the Rapid Support Forces began in 2023.

Why it mattersThe message reached a 13-year-old boy who lost his legs in Sudan's war and is now living in Cairo with his family.
