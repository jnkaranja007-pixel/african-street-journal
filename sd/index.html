Displaced in Nyala Face Critical Shortages Amid Rains

Hundreds of families in South Darfur shelters lack food, water, medicine, and adequate shelter as the rainy season exacerbates already dire conditions.

Emergency health centers in Nyala, South Darfur, report a critical decline in humanitarian conditions within displacement shelters. Hundreds of families face severe shortages of basic necessities, including food, water, medicines, and shelter materials. Nibras Abdullah of Nyala’s emergency room stated that the situation is particularly dire with the onset of the rainy season, forcing some families to live in the open or in damaged classrooms where rainwater leaks through.

Specific shelters like El Jir, housing 214 families, and El Hamaira, with 274 families, are in urgent need of food, water, hygiene supplies, and medicines for chronic conditions. Other shelters, including El Zahra, Mahira School, El Savana, and the airport, face similar widespread shortages. Many displaced individuals are sharing overcrowded classrooms, with some housing up to ten families, and require urgent repairs to roofs and toilets for basic privacy and protection.

While some organizations are providing aid, Abdullah described the scale of assistance as insufficient to meet the growing needs of displaced families. Rising prices further complicate efforts to provide adequate support. Abdullah called for immediate intervention from humanitarian organizations and authorities to supply essential items and improve living conditions to prevent further deterioration as the rains continue.

Why it mattersDisplaced families in Nyala shelters are directly affected by critical shortages of food, water, medicine, and shelter, worsened by the rainy season.

Fighting Disrupts Trade, Raising Famine Fears in Kordofan

The Kordofan Observatory reports families are eating animal feed as food prices soar and aid is cut off due to conflict and road closures.

Fears of imminent famine are growing in parts of Sudan's Kordofan region, with families in El Nahud, Wad Banda, El Khuwei, and Muglad localities resorting to eating ambaz, a residue normally fed to animals. The Kordofan Observatory attributes this crisis to soaring food prices, a sharp decline in incomes, and the cutoff of humanitarian aid. The price of a malwa of millet has reached 35,000 Sudanese pounds, placing it out of reach for many.

The observatory links the worsening food situation to recent fighting around El Obeid and a military build-up by both sides. This conflict has disrupted trade routes, preventing essential goods from reaching affected areas. Farmers are also struggling to access and cultivate their land, compounded by a late arrival of rains this year. The observatory warns that continued fighting and restricted humanitarian access could lead to a widespread catastrophe.

The Kordofan Observatory is calling for immediate action to open and secure trade and humanitarian routes, urging all parties to ensure food and aid reach civilians. It also requests that authorities reverse decisions hindering the transport of goods to areas controlled by the Rapid Support Forces, and calls on the RSF to remove checkpoints and obstacles contributing to rising prices. Humanitarian organizations are urged to prioritize the food and health situation with urgent aid.

Why it mattersFarmers are unable to cultivate land, and families are forced to eat animal feed due to disrupted trade and soaring food prices.

UN Council Debates Expanding Sudan Arms Ban

The UN Security Council convened to discuss civilian protection and the humanitarian crisis in Sudan, with proposals to extend an arms embargo beyond Darfur.

The UN Security Council held an emergency session Monday to address the escalating humanitarian situation and civilian protection in Sudan. Denmark convened the meeting, which focused on transforming aid into a tool for pressure and commerce. A key proposal discussed was amending Resolution 1591 to extend the existing arms export ban from Darfur to the entire country, a move aimed at strengthening international humanitarian law.

The United States, supported by Denmark, Britain, and France, proposed extending the arms embargo to include drones and their components, citing the involvement of over a dozen countries in providing military support that prolongs the conflict. However, China, Russia, and Sudan's representative openly opposed this expansion. Sudanese official Abdelfattah El Burhan downplayed the potential impact of new sanctions and criticized those opposing his government, while denying claims of a no-fly zone.

Reactions to the proposed arms embargo expansion were divided. The Sudan Liberation Movement and the Sumud alliance welcomed the idea as a means to protect civilians and complement peace efforts. Conversely, the Democratic Bloc strongly rejected the proposal, arguing it infringes on Sudan's sovereignty and unfairly equates the legitimate army with militia forces. Analyst Yasser Arman criticized the proposal as a war management tactic rather than a resolution.

Why it mattersThe proposed expansion of the arms embargo affects the Sudanese army and militia forces by potentially restricting their access to weapons, impacting the ongoing conflict.

Sudanese Crisis Drives Trade and Refugee Flows to Abéché

Abéché, Chad's second city, has become a key hub for Sudanese refugees, aid workers, traders, and soldiers, situated less than 200 kilometers from the Darfur region.

Abéché, the capital of Chad’s Ouaddaï province, is experiencing a significant influx due to its proximity to Sudan's Darfur region. Located less than 200 kilometers away, the city is now a primary haven for those displaced by the Sudanese crisis. This has created a dynamic environment where refugees, aid workers, traders, and soldiers converge, highlighting the deep historical and ongoing connections between the two areas.

The city's role as a haven is underscored by the constant movement of people and goods. Traders are a notable presence, suggesting that commercial activities continue despite the regional instability. The presence of soldiers also indicates a security dimension to the city's evolving status as a transit and support point for those affected by the crisis.

This cross-border activity is facilitated by the long-standing ties between Abéché and Darfur. While these historical connections have historically fostered relationships, they now also bring the tensions and challenges associated with the Sudanese crisis directly to Chad's doorstep. The city serves as a critical junction for humanitarian efforts and economic interactions.

Why it mattersThe city's role as a haven and transit point for traders and aid workers directly impacts economic activity and the flow of goods and services.
