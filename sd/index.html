Agriculture · 2026-11-18

White Nile sets diesel at SDG 69,842.50 a gallon as farmers report shortages

The state also priced petrol at SDG 53,365 a gallon, while farmers say scarce diesel is disrupting irrigation in Um Rimta.

Authorities in Sudan’s White Nile State raised diesel and petrol prices on Monday, setting diesel at SDG 69,842.50 a gallon and petrol at SDG 53,365 in Kosti, Rabak, Al-Qitaina and Al-Duweim. The increases drew criticism from residents and farmers.

Hammad Musa, a farmer in Um Rimta, said a fuel container cost between SDG 600,000 and 800,000 and that farmers in the Al-Bugha agricultural schemes needed more diesel after three rounds of irrigation. He warned that shortages threatened their harvests and accused unidentified parties of diverting fuel to the black market. Gismallah Al-Tayyib, another farmer, alleged that fuel was being sold from filling stations on the black market with security forces involved.

The state’s Public Transport Regulation and Development Council also set a transport surcharge of SDG 20 per gallon of commercially priced fuel in Um Rimta, Tendelti, Rural Al-Duweim and Al-Jabalain, and SDG 100 per gallon in Al-Salam. Farmers called on authorities to prioritise fuel supplies for agriculture.

Why it mattersFarmers in Um Rimta say they need more diesel to irrigate crops, while the council has set added charges of SDG 20 or SDG 100 per gallon in named localities.

Security · 2026-11-18

Drone strikes drive most civilian deaths in Sudan, UN rights office finds

The UN Human Rights Office counted more than 2,230 civilians killed in Sudan from January to October, with over 1,630 of those deaths caused by drones.

Drone strikes accounted for more than 80 per cent of the 1,390 civilian deaths recorded in Sudan between January and June 2026, a UN Human Rights Office report published on Wednesday shows. The fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces, the Rapid Support Forces and allied armed movements has been documented across the country, with the office describing drone attacks as a defining feature of the war.

Another 840 civilians were killed in the following four months, close to 520 of them in drone strikes. The office said the figures it could confirm are likely lower than the true toll. Kordofan bore more than two-thirds of documented casualties, and Darfur most of the rest, while White Nile recorded at least 41 civilian deaths.

Over the first half of 2026, the office recorded at least 39 drone strikes on markets, 20 on civilian vehicles, 17 on fuel tankers and depots, and 16 on health facilities. High Commissioner Volker Türk said the findings describe serious violations of international humanitarian law that may amount to war crimes.

Why it mattersResidents of Kordofan, who carried more than two-thirds of documented civilian casualties, face the heaviest recorded toll of the war.

Business · 2026-11-18

GERD operations leave Sudan’s Roseires dam exposed to sudden flow changes

A study says releases from Ethiopia’s newly opened dam can alter levels at Sudan’s irrigation hub within hours, affecting water supplies and farms.

A study published in Water International says operational decisions at Ethiopia’s Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam increasingly shape Sudan’s water security, with changes in releases affecting levels at Roseires Dam within hours. The study was written by water experts Yasser Abbas Mohamed, Azza Mustafa and Saif El-Din Hamad Abdullah.

The researchers cite a 2020 first filling without prior notice that lowered Blue Nile levels and temporarily disrupted Khartoum’s water purification plants. They also say unannounced releases in September 2025 contributed to flooding in agricultural and residential areas north of Roseires.

Negotiations between Ethiopia, Sudan and Egypt have lasted more than 15 years without producing a legally binding operating agreement. The researchers say regulated flows could reduce severe seasonal floods and improve hydroelectric efficiency, but that cooperation is needed to achieve those benefits.

Why it mattersFarmers and residents north of Roseires faced flooding after unannounced releases in September 2025, while Sudan’s irrigation hub can see water levels change within hours.

Security · 2026-11-18

Amnesty urges war-crimes inquiry into RSF drone strikes in El Obeid

The rights group says nine attacks killed 37 civilians and injured 10 between June and August 2026, hitting homes and civilian infrastructure.

Amnesty International called for an investigation into Rapid Support Forces drone strikes in El Obeid, the capital of Sudan’s North Kordofan State, as possible war crimes. The group said it examined nine strikes in the city and its surroundings between June and August 2026, which killed 37 civilians and injured 10.

Amnesty said three strikes hit residential areas, four hit civilian infrastructure including the power plant, fuel stations and main market, and two targeted moving vehicles. The group said it found no military objectives near the strike sites at the time. It based its findings on interviews with 29 people from El Obeid, conducted between July and September 2026.

Amnesty Secretary General Agnès Callamard urged the United Nations and African Union to propose and implement civilian protection measures, including a protection force in Sudan. She also called for the United Nations Security Council to extend the arms embargo covering Darfur to the rest of Sudan and include drones.

Why it mattersEl Obeid residents and users of its power, fuel and market services face strikes that Amnesty says killed 37 civilians and injured 10.

Education · 2026-11-18

Sudan's Tasis Government opens 2027 admissions at nine universities

Applicants must pay a SDG 20,000 fee, and children of people killed in the conflict will receive priority consideration.

The Admissions Committee of the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research under the Tasis Government announced on Monday that applications are open for 2027 academic year places at nine Sudanese higher education institutions. Applicants must pay a fee of SDG 20,000 through Future Bank.

Committee chairman Dr. Musa Al-Amin said the arrangements are designed to ensure that every student who has completed the required examinations can secure a place. The institutions together have about 65 faculties offering more than 120 undergraduate degree and diploma programmes, with a combined capacity above 6,000 students. The University of Al-Salam will admit students through only seven faculties because the rest are not yet operationally ready and sustained damage.

Applications will first be processed through designated university admissions advisers, while an online platform will publish details on faculties, programme codes and procedures. The University of El Daein has completed preparations across all 11 of its faculties.

Why it mattersFamilies paying the SDG 20,000 fee must apply through Future Bank, and places at the University of Al-Salam are limited to seven faculties.
