SAF Drone Strike Kills Dozens in Sudanese Market

The Sudanese Armed Forces are accused of a drone attack on a crowded market near the Chadian border, while fighting intensifies in Kordofan and Blue Nile states.

The Western Darfur Civil Administration accused the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) of killing and injuring dozens of civilians in a drone strike on the weekly Adekong market near the Chadian border. The administration stated this was the fifth bombing of the market this year, occurring during peak trading hours. A subsequent fire reportedly charred bodies, and the administration emphasized the market's role as a trade and humanitarian hub, warning that repeated attacks threaten cross-border commerce and aid delivery.

Separately, the Emergency Lawyers Group reported that at least eight civilians were killed and dozens injured in North Kordofan on Thursday. An initial drone strike destroyed four vehicles, and the aircraft allegedly returned to target individuals attempting to rescue the wounded in the Umm Shro area. The group condemned the attacks, stating they violate international humanitarian law and could constitute war crimes, calling for an independent investigation and an immediate halt to civilian targeting.

In Blue Nile State, forces from the Sudan Founding Alliance, including the RSF and SPLM-N, claimed to have captured the strategic Surkum and Bakuri bases, announcing a "crushing defeat" for the Sudanese army. The army, however, stated it had repelled an attack in the Kili area. These escalations occur amidst a protracted conflict that has displaced millions and seen widespread use of drones.

Why it mattersCivilians in western and northern Sudan are directly affected by drone strikes on markets and transportation routes, leading to deaths, injuries, and disruption of trade and aid.

Sudan Lifts Two-Year Curfew in Khartoum Amid Ongoing War

Authorities have eased restrictions in the capital, allowing residents to move and work around the clock, though security remains tight.

Sudan has lifted a two-year night-time curfew and state of emergency in Khartoum, a move authorities say will enable residents to move and work without time restrictions. The decision was announced by the Security Affairs Coordination Committee, chaired by Khartoum Governor Ahmed Othman Hamza. Despite the easing of these measures, security forces will maintain army patrols and checkpoints throughout the capital. Residents are still advised to carry identification documents when traveling at night.

The curfew was initially imposed in May 2024 as fighting intensified between Sudan’s army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces. The conflict, which began in April 2023, has resulted in tens of thousands of deaths and displaced approximately 13 million people, according to UN and international estimates. Military authorities regained control of Khartoum 18 months ago, but the conflict persists in other regions.

Authorities have also implemented new restrictions for foreign nationals entering Khartoum State. Entry by public or private transport, including trains and buses, will be prohibited unless travelers possess valid visas or permits. These measures indicate a continued focus on security and control within the capital, even as broader restrictions are being lifted.

Why it mattersResidents of Khartoum can now move and work around the clock, but foreign nationals face new entry restrictions.

Floods Displace 138 Families in South Kordofan

Heavy rains and flooding in Abu Kershola locality destroyed 138 homes and damaged 133 others, forcing residents to seek temporary shelter.

One hundred thirty-eight families have been displaced from villages in Abu Kershola locality, South Kordofan state, following heavy rains and floods. The International Organization for Migration reported on August twenty-fifth, 2026, that the severe weather destroyed 138 houses completely and partially damaged 133 homes. The affected families have relocated to temporary open-air shelters within the Abu Kershola locality.

The villages from which families were displaced include Fajuj Al-Baidha, Hajer Sinina, Al-Rusayris, Al-Managil, Al-Joud, Jabruna, Al-Rakab, Al-Bubaya, and Al-Faid Umm Abdullah. Field teams reported the damage on Thursday, August twenty-seventh, 2026, detailing the extent of the destruction caused by the intense rainfall.

The flooding in Abu Kershola has directly impacted the livelihoods and shelter security of these 138 families. The International Organization for Migration provided the figures for displacement and structural damage, highlighting the immediate needs of the affected population who are now in temporary accommodations.

Why it mattersOne hundred thirty-eight families are affected by the floods, losing their homes and being forced to seek temporary shelter in open areas.

Politics · 2026-08-30

Sudan Forces Take Key Blue Nile Military Areas

The Rapid Support Forces and allied groups seized strategic locations in Blue Nile and South Kordofan after intense fighting with the Sudanese Army.

Forces allied under the "Tasis" coalition, including the Rapid Support Forces and the SPLM-North, have captured the Bakuri and Sarkam military areas in Sudan's Blue Nile region. The capture of the 13th Infantry Brigade headquarters in Bakuri, a strategic stronghold before reaching Damazin, followed fierce battles. The forces also took Sarkam in the Karmak area after direct engagements with the Sudanese Army, utilizing strategic drones in the assault.

Video shared by RSF fighters on social media showed their control over Sarkam military area, with vows to advance towards Damazin and Port Sudan. The coalition also reported capturing the Al-Faid Um Abdullah and Tabbesa areas in South Kordofan after violent clashes, seizing significant military equipment. Tabbesa is considered the first line of defense for Al-Abbassia city.

The Sudanese Army stated its withdrawal from Tabbesa was tactical, for redeployment after heavy fighting, and claimed to have killed the attack's commander, Mohammed Abdulrahman, and other senior leaders. Meanwhile, in West Darfur, at least 13 people were killed and dozens injured in a drone strike, suspected to be by the Sudanese Army, on the Adokong border market near Geneina.

Why it mattersThe capture of strategic military areas by the Tasis coalition affects the ongoing conflict and territorial control within Sudan.
