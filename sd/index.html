Sudan army detains ex-ruling party leader Ahmed Haroun

Ahmed Haroun, wanted by the ICC, was detained in Khartoum two days after a televised podcast interview.

The Sudanese army detained Ahmed Haroun, chairman of the former ruling National Congress Party (NCP), on Sunday in Khartoum. Military Intelligence officers arrested Haroun, who is wanted by the International Criminal Court (ICC) for alleged crimes in Darfur. His detention occurred two days after he made his first public appearance since the war began in April 2023, during a televised podcast.

In the podcast, Haroun discussed the conflict, the RSF, and Islamists' role, denying they started the war and stating they joined after the military called for mobilization. He claimed the NCP and the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) share battlefield interests against the RSF despite potential disagreements. Haroun dismissed claims of formal ties between Islamists and the military leadership.

Opposition politician Khalid Omar Yousif described the arrest as an attempt to deflect criticism after Haroun's public appearance. Yousif alleged Haroun had been in Sudan with General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan's knowledge and protection since April 2023, and accused him of participating in the conflict.

Sudan fuel shortage damages maize crop

Irrigation pumps are idle in El Gezira as petrol prices reach SDG600,000 per four-gallon container.

Fuel shortages are damaging agricultural crops in Sudan’s El Gezira state, with petrol prices reaching SDG600,000 for a four-gallon container. A farmer named Mohammed reported that large areas of maize crop were damaged because irrigation pumps could not operate without petrol. He is searching for fuel outside the state to save the rest of his crop and warned that declining production could worsen food insecurity.

Food prices have also risen sharply across El Gezira markets, with a sack of maize now costing between SDG800,000 and SDG820,000. Essential commodities like wheat, lentils, and sesame have also seen significant price increases, severely affecting residents' purchasing power. Transport costs have surged, with goods transport in Omdurman exceeding SDG2 million.

In El Obeid, North Kordofan, all bakeries have gone on strike over a dispute with local authorities regarding bread prices and rising production costs. Journalist Omar Mohammed Omar stated that the strike has disrupted bread supplies across the city. Previously, bread prices reached SDG2,000 to SDG3,000, with demands for prices to reflect the actual weight, size, and nutritional value of each loaf.

Why it mattersFarmers in El Gezira are losing crops due to a lack of fuel for irrigation, and all residents face soaring food prices and disrupted bread supplies.

Sudanese artist preserves memory in Nairobi

Waleed Mohammed uses painting and diverse materials to explore Sudan's past and present transformations.

Sudanese visual artist Waleed Mohammed is documenting the country’s collective memory through art created in his Nairobi studio. He uses painting and diverse materials to explore transformations in Sudanese lives since the April 2023 war and memories from before it.

Mohammed’s work draws inspiration from old photographs and family albums, including archives of Sudanese photographers like Rashid Mahdi and Gadalla Gubara. He reinterprets these images, transforming them into paintings that acquire new meanings relating to identity and collective memory.

His approach engages with the concept of post memory, exploring how a later generation relates to trauma it did not experience directly but which is transmitted through images and stories. Duality of colour characterises his work, with monochromatic grey bodies set against saturated fields of indigo blue, crimson red, and earthy yellow.

Why it mattersSudanese artist Waleed Mohammed is preserving collective memory through his art in Nairobi, Kenya, exploring themes of war and displacement.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Sudanese army downs fourth drone in West Kordofan

The military reported capturing two towns and destroying RSF vehicles during intensified fighting.

Sudan’s army announced on Sunday that its air defence units downed a fourth drone in West Kordofan state in the past week amid escalating clashes with the Rapid Support Forces (RSF). The military stated on Saturday that it destroyed a hostile FH-95 drone east of Wad Banda around midday.

Army troops and allied forces destroyed several RSF combat vehicles near Al-Khuwei, inflicting heavy losses, according to sources. The Joint Force of Armed Movements reported maintaining offensive operations for a third day, advancing on multiple fronts. The army also announced capturing Ayal Bakhit and Umm Marahik in Al-Khuwei district following engagements with RSF fighters.

Military personnel posted footage on social media showing destroyed RSF vehicles and recent advances around Al-Khuwei, a strategic town connecting central Sudan with West Kordofan. The conflict, which began in April 2023 between the army and RSF, has killed tens of thousands and displaced millions, with fighting intensifying in the Kordofan region.

Why it mattersFighting between the Sudanese army and the RSF in West Kordofan has intensified, displacing millions and worsening a humanitarian crisis.

Sudan drone strikes kill 306 in October

At least 42 drone strikes hit Sudan since October, with only one targeting military sites.

Since October, at least 306 people have died in drone strikes across Sudan, with only one strike targeting a military site out of 42 monitored. In the last 10 days, warring parties hit 28 civilian targets including power stations, markets, and a hospital.

On Monday, drones struck two power stations in Kassala and a sub-station in Merowe, causing outages. Last week, a market strike in Nyala killed 102 civilians and injured 113, according to Emergency Lawyers. Strikes have focused on markets in Darfur and Kordofan, and power infrastructure in north, east, and central Sudan.

Economic expert Hossam El-Deen estimates losses from bombed electricity substations at $4 to $5 million, with each transformer costing at least $250,000. Repairs take at least four months, increasing living costs for citizens and cutting revenue for the electricity company. In Shendi, a farmer lost nearly $150,000 on a farm now barren due to lack of pumped water.

Why it mattersSudanese civilians are directly affected by drone strikes on markets, power stations, and infrastructure, leading to deaths, injuries, and economic losses.
