Sudanese Forces Reject AU Dialogue Initiative

Anti-war groups warned that backing El Burhan's plan could legitimize military rule and deepen Sudan's division.

Sudanese anti-war political and civil forces have rejected the African Union’s endorsement of a proposed “Sudanese-Sudanese Dialogue Initiative.” They submitted a memorandum on September 2 to the AU, warning that the process could legitimize military rule and deepen Sudan’s division. The groups criticized the AU for backing an initiative proposed by Sovereignty Council President Lt Gen Abdelfattah El Burhan, whose October 2021 military coup resulted in Sudan’s suspension from AU activities.

The memorandum stressed that while dialogue is not the problem, any credible political process must be genuinely Sudanese-owned, with participants, agenda, and timing agreed by Sudanese stakeholders. They called for the humanitarian crisis and cessation of hostilities to remain central. The groups also demanded the exclusion of the National Congress Party, the Islamic Movement, and affiliated forces, accusing them of playing a role in starting and prolonging the war. They warned that dialogue without a prior ceasefire or broad participation could entrench fragmentation.

The AU Commission stated its support was based on the need for an inclusive process involving Sudanese civil, political, and societal actors, aiming for a united Sudan governed by legitimate civilian institutions. However, the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces aligned-government, Sudan Founding Alliance (Tasees), also rejected the AU endorsement, describing it as biased support for the army's position.

Why it mattersSudanese anti-war forces are affected because they believe the AU-backed dialogue initiative could legitimize military rule and deepen the country's division.

Cholera Outbreak Kills 10 in Sudan's West Kordofan

The World Health Organization has flagged West Kordofan as the latest cholera hotspot after 10 deaths and over 40 infections were recorded in El Idai locality.

At least ten people have died and over 40 have been infected with cholera in Sudan’s West Kordofan region, according to the Kordofan Human Rights Observatory. The cases were reported in El Idai locality over Sunday and Monday. The World Health Organization (WHO) has identified West Kordofan as the latest outbreak hotspot, with more than 2,600 cholera cases reported across several Sudanese states up to early August.

Mortada Ahmed, a spokesperson for the Kordofan Human Rights Observatory, stated that the outbreak is spreading widely in El Idai, with numbers expected to rise. He highlighted a severe lack of medicines and life-saving supplies, including intravenous fluids, and noted that ongoing fighting between the army and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has created access difficulties for medical aid and food. Environmental factors from the rainy season, such as stagnant water and increased insect populations, are also contributing to the spread.

The observatory is calling for urgent humanitarian intervention and pressure on warring parties to open safe corridors for essential supplies. The WHO is conducting vaccination campaigns for 928,000 people in West Darfur and West Kordofan, with preventive measures also underway in South and North Darfur. The UN and its partners are intensifying efforts to combat cholera through treatment, vaccinations, and distribution of medical supplies.

Why it mattersCholera is spreading in West Kordofan, affecting civilians with a lack of medical supplies and access to aid due to conflict and environmental conditions.

WFP Food Aid Destroyed in South Kordofan Air Attack

An airstrike outside Deleng destroyed over 50 metric tons of food aid destined for thousands facing severe food insecurity.

The World Food Programme (WFP) condemned an aerial attack on two of its food aid trucks outside Deleng in South Kordofan on August 30, 2026. The strike destroyed more than 50 metric tons of food, enough to support 5,800 people for a month. The trucks were en route to Kadugli, the state capital, where WFP supports approximately 58,000 individuals experiencing acute food insecurity.

Local authorities reported over 9,800 people arriving in Al-Abbasiya locality, South Kordofan, fleeing renewed fighting. Food and essential household items are among their most urgent needs. Similarly, over 8,700 families from 45 villages have arrived in Shikkan locality, North Kordofan, after fleeing conflict zones. These ongoing displacements strain already overcrowded sites and basic services.

The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) reiterated calls for all parties to protect civilians, humanitarian workers, and supplies, and to ensure rapid, safe, and unhindered access for humanitarian assistance. The Sudan Founding Alliance condemned the attack, calling it a blatant disregard for civilian lives and humanitarian principles.

Why it mattersCivilians in South Kordofan are directly affected as the destruction of food aid exacerbates severe food insecurity and hinders life-saving support.

Juba City Council Approves SSP73.4B Budget for 2026/2027

The Juba City Legislative Council passed a 73.48 billion South Sudanese Pound budget to fund administration and service delivery for the upcoming fiscal year.

The Juba City Legislative Council has approved a budget of 73.48 billion South Sudanese Pounds for the 2026/2027 financial year. This funding aims to support administration and the delivery of services within the capital city. The budget, totaling 73,479,045,500 South Sudanese Pounds, was passed during the council’s fifteenth sitting.

Council Chairperson Dina Keji stated that the approved budget will establish a clear financial framework for the council’s committees and departments. She emphasized that the budget provides clarity, stability, and direction for implementing the council’s priorities and strengthens their responsibility to Juba’s residents. Keji expects financial committees and departments to manage public resources within the approved allocations.

Juba City Mayor Christopher Serafino Wani expressed optimism that the new budget will enhance the city administration’s ability to improve services. Deputy Mayor Jennifer Yabu assured the public that the budget would be implemented according to approved plans, committing to using allocated resources for their intended purposes amidst increasing demands for better public services.

Why it mattersThe Juba City Council's approval of a 73.48 billion South Sudanese Pound budget affects residents by aiming to improve public services and resource management within the capital.
