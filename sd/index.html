South Darfur shelters face critical shortages as rainy season worsens conditions

Displaced families in Nyala's shelters are experiencing severe shortages of food, water, medicine, and shelter materials, with the rainy season exacerbating their living.

Humanitarian conditions in several displacement shelters across Nyala, the capital of South Darfur, have deteriorated significantly, according to the city's emergency room and health centers. Hundreds of families lack basic necessities such as food, water, medicines, shelter materials, and hygiene supplies. The onset of the rainy season has made the situation critical, with many families living in the open or in damaged classrooms where roofs leak.

Nibras Abdullah, a member of Nyala's emergency room and health centers, highlighted specific needs at various shelters. El Jir neighborhood shelter, housing about 214 families, requires food, water, shelter materials, and toilet repairs. At El Hamaira shelter, home to about 274 families, urgent needs include food, water, hygiene supplies, and medicines for chronic conditions like diabetes and high blood pressure.

Other centers, including El Zahra (115 families) and Mahira School (457 families), face similar shortages. Abdullah noted that some families are sharing overcrowded classrooms, with up to 10 families in a single room, and many classrooms need roof repairs and doors for privacy. While some organizations provide assistance, the aid is insufficient to meet the growing needs and rising prices, prompting calls for urgent intervention.

Why it mattersHundreds of displaced families in South Darfur are directly affected by the lack of basic necessities and adequate shelter, impacting their health and safety.

Kordofan Observatory warns fighting restricts food access, raises famine fears

The Kordofan Observatory reports that ongoing conflict and trade route closures are forcing families in several localities to consume animal feed as food prices soar and aid is.

Families in El Nahud, Wad Banda, El Khuwei, and Muglad localities are now relying on ambaz, a groundnut residue typically used for animal feed, as food prices increase and humanitarian aid is restricted, according to the Kordofan Observatory. The price of a malwa of millet has reached 35,000 Sudanese pounds, making it unaffordable for many households. The growing demand for ambaz has also driven its price to about 10,000 Sudanese pounds per malwa.

The observatory attributes the worsening food crisis to recent fighting around El Obeid and a military build-up by both sides of the conflict. This security situation has disrupted trade and prevented goods from reaching parts of Kordofan. Additionally, farmers' ability to access and cultivate their land has been affected, and rains have arrived late this year.

The Kordofan Observatory urges the warring parties to agree on arrangements to open and secure trade and humanitarian routes, ensuring food and aid reach civilians. It also calls on authorities in Khartoum and Northern State to reverse decisions that prevent goods from being transported to areas controlled by the Rapid Support Forces, and asks the Rapid Support Forces to facilitate trade movement and remove levy checkpoints.

Why it mattersResidents of Kordofan are directly affected by the inability to access food and the rising cost of basic necessities, forcing them to consume animal feed.

UN Security Council debates Sudan arms embargo expansion

The UN Security Council discussed extending the arms export ban from Darfur to all of Sudan, including drones and their components, following a proposal from the US.

The UN Security Council convened an emergency session in New York on Monday to address civilian protection and the humanitarian situation in Sudan. Denmark initiated the meeting, and the US proposed expanding the arms export ban, currently limited to Darfur under Resolution 1591 from 2005, to cover the entire country. This expansion would include drones and their components, as more than twelve countries are reportedly providing military support.

Denmark, Britain, and France supported the US proposal, while China, Russia, and Sudan's representative openly opposed it. In Sudan, Abdelfattah El Burhan downplayed the impact of additional sanctions in remarks to the police. He also criticized those opposing his government and urged people to ignore claims about a no-fly zone.

Local reactions varied, with Abdelrahman El Nair of the Sudan Liberation Movement and Khalid Omer Youssef of the Sumud alliance supporting the proposal for civilian protection and as part of a comprehensive peace package. Conversely, Dr. Mohammed Zakaria of the Democratic Bloc rejected it, arguing it equates the legitimate army with the Rapid Support Forces militia and violates Sudan's sovereignty.

Why it mattersThe debate over expanding the arms embargo directly affects the military capabilities and international standing of the Sudanese government and other armed groups, influencing the ongoing conflict.

WFP warns global conflict drives Sudan deeper into hunger crisis

The World Food Programme (WFP) reports that global conflict and disruptions to fuel and trade have worsened Sudan’s hunger crisis, with basic food costs rising by almost 40.

WFP Acting Executive Director Carl Skau addressed the UN Security Council on Monday, stating that Sudan is the world’s worst hunger crisis. He highlighted that conflict beyond Sudan’s borders is increasing food prices for Sudanese families, citing that "one oil tanker anchored in the Strait of Hormuz can mean one less meal a day for a child in Sudan."

Skau urged the international community to act before a famine is officially declared, noting that earlier intervention could have prevented suffering. He criticized the international response to warnings about atrocities in El Fasher, stating that the international community "failed to act on those warnings."

Recalling a visit to Tawila in Darfur, Skau described "700,000 people in a super city of grass huts" who arrived with nothing and now depend on limited humanitarian aid. He emphasized that waiting for famine to be declared is "a total failure of duty" and called for stronger protection of civilians, food systems, and humanitarian access.

Why it mattersSudanese families are directly affected by rising food costs and the lack of humanitarian aid, which threatens their access to basic necessities and safety.
