RSF drones hit 12 power stations across Sudan

Rapid Support Forces attacks since Monday have damaged transformers and fuel installations, causing widespread blackouts.

Rapid Support Forces (RSF) drone attacks have crippled Sudan’s electricity network since Monday, knocking out power stations in at least six states. The strikes have damaged transformers, transmission equipment, and fuel installations from eastern Sudan to the capital, Khartoum. The Sudan Electricity Holding Company reported that the RSF targeted vital substations including Singa, Al-Hawata, Al-Faw, Mashkur, and Al-Geneid, causing extensive destruction and a complete blackout across the southeastern grid.

Witnesses reported explosions at the Abu Hamad facility, and the attacks have affected installations hundreds of kilometers from RSF ground positions. The electricity company stated these "treacherous terrorist attacks" pose a direct threat to the national grid's stability. The bombardment includes strikes on the Al Markhiyat substation northwest of Omdurman, cutting power to large parts of Khartoum.

Attacks have also hit Al Managil, Haya, and Rabak, following an earlier disruption at Tendelti electricity station. In Al Faw and Al Hawata, strikes affected facilities supplying towns and agricultural communities, impacting water pumping, irrigation, and commercial activities.

Why it mattersSudanese citizens are experiencing widespread power outages due to RSF drone attacks on electricity infrastructure.

Sudan leader risks repeating Bashir's Russia gambit, analyst warns

Journalist Rasha Awad says Abdel Fattah al-Burhan's overtures to Moscow echo failed policies of the past.

Sudanese journalist Rasha Awad warned that military leader Abdel Fattah al-Burhan is repeating former president Omar al-Bashir's strategy by seeking Russian political and economic support. Burhan's recent engagement with Moscow, including proposals for investment in mining and infrastructure, mirrors al-Bashir's past efforts to deepen ties with Russia. Al-Bashir visited Russia in November 2017 and sought protection against American actions.

Awad told Radio Tamazuj on Saturday that al-Bashir's previous reliance on Russia did not prevent his overthrow in April 2019. She stated that Burhan's current diplomatic moves are a reaction to his failure to achieve peace and his lack of cooperation with international efforts led by the United States. Awad believes Burhan is using the prospect of closer ties with Moscow as a political maneuver to counter Western pressure and consolidate his power.

However, Awad cautioned that this strategy is unlikely to succeed due to competing international and regional interests. She questioned Russia's ability to provide the expected support, citing Russia's military presence in Syria which did not prevent Bashar al-Assad's rule from collapsing in December 2024. Awad noted that Moscow's broader interests with the United States and Gulf countries could limit its willingness to offer unconditional backing to Sudan's military authorities.

Why it mattersSudan's military leader Abdel Fattah al-Burhan is seeking political and economic support from Russia amid ongoing war and international isolation.

Russia raises South Sudan scholarship places to 40

President Vladimir Putin announced the increase for the 2026-2027 academic year during talks with President Salva Kiir in Moscow.

Russian President Vladimir Putin announced on Friday that Moscow will offer 40 scholarship places to South Sudanese students for the 2026-2027 academic year. Putin made the announcement during talks with South Sudanese President Salva Kiir in Moscow, aimed at expanding bilateral relations.

Putin stated that Russia will continue to support the training of South Sudanese personnel, calling the increased quota a way to strengthen cooperation. The announcement offers more opportunities for South Sudanese citizens to pursue higher education and professional training in Russia, though specific fields of study were not detailed.

The two leaders met following the third Russia-Africa Summit and discussed expanding bilateral trade and economic cooperation, noting that the potential of their relationship had not been fully realised. Russia's ambassador to South Sudan presented his credentials in January.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese students will have increased opportunities for higher education and professional training in Russia.

South Sudan election order reversed

Elections are now scheduled for December 22 under the transitional constitution, reversing the agreed order for a permanent constitution first.

South Sudan's planned elections, scheduled for December 22, will now proceed under the transitional constitution. This decision reverses the order previously set by the 2018 peace agreement, which stipulated elections should occur after a permanent constitution was established.

Regina Adyeng Amaylek Amuch, who fled to Khartoum in 2013 and returned to Malakal this year, stated that lasting peace is what she needs most. She expressed this in August to the United Nations, highlighting the fragility of her rebuilt life and the constant threat of displacement.

The current approach has been criticized for asking citizens to choose election referees before agreeing on the rules of the game. Previous attempts to settle power-sharing, including agreements in 2005, 2015, and 2018, focused on dividing offices rather than defining the country's governance terms.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens, like Regina Adyeng Amaylek Amuch, face continued displacement and insecurity due to unresolved issues regarding the country's governance and the terms of power.

Sudan displaced face food, medicine shortages

Around 27,400 people in Blue Nile region lack basic supplies amid disease and calls for aid.

Displaced people in Sudan’s Blue Nile region are facing severe shortages of food and medicine, alongside disease outbreaks and a lack of educational and employment opportunities. The Blue Nile Humanitarian Aid Commissioner, Gisma Abdel-Karim, stated that efforts are underway to provide immediate aid, including food and shelter, in coordination with humanitarian partners.

The World Food Programme is supplying food baskets, ADRA is providing cash assistance, and Islamic Relief is distributing hygiene kits to approximately 10,600 pregnant and breastfeeding women. However, Issa Khamis, displaced from Balaro, reported that the assistance is insufficient, with ongoing struggles due to food scarcity and disease spread.

Al-Radhiya Arbab, a mother of 12 displaced from Kurmuk locality, called for improved healthcare and medicine availability, and for children to be enrolled in school. Displaced families from Kurmuk are experiencing worsening hunger, and a committee member requested employment for young people to alleviate family financial pressures.
