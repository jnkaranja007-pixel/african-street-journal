Sudanese army drone strike kills 36 in Nyala market

Médecins Sans Frontières reported 150 wounded and widespread devastation after two strikes hit a busy market and a civilian neighborhood.

A Sudanese Armed Forces drone strike hit a busy market in Nyala, South Darfur State, on Monday, October 26, killing 36 people and injuring over 150. A second strike hit a civilian neighborhood in the city around 8 p.m. local time. Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) stated that the Nyala Teaching Hospital, which it supports, received the bodies, with 21 victims identified as civilians.

MSF project coordinator Dale Koninckx described patients arriving at the hospital with shrapnel wounds and burns, transported from the market by tuk-tuks and donkey carts. "Medical staff worked around the clock treating patients, including women and children," Koninckx said, questioning why markets, essential for accessing food, are being targeted. He added that markets, homes, and civilian infrastructure are not legitimate targets and all conflict parties must protect civilians.

This attack follows a similar incident on October 16 in Nyala, where a drone strike on Al-Mawashi market killed at least four people. MSF noted that such incidents expose civilians to violence and limit access to food and supplies amid growing malnutrition concerns. The organization also reported that Rapid Support Forces carried out drone strikes on electricity infrastructure in other states recently.

Why it mattersCivilians in Nyala are directly affected by drone strikes on markets and neighborhoods, leading to deaths, injuries, and limited access to essential food and supplies.

UN, France condemn escalating drone attacks in Sudan

Drone strikes on civilian infrastructure are disrupting essential services and killing civilians, including children.

The United Nations and France have condemned escalating drone attacks across Sudan, warning that strikes on civilian infrastructure are disrupting essential services and killing civilians, including children. The French embassy in Sudan called the recent escalation of air strikes entirely unacceptable, citing attacks in Darfur, Khartoum, Abyei, the northern Nile Valley, Kordofan, and Blue Nile. These attacks have killed hundreds of civilians, including many children, within a few days.

The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) also expressed concern over continuing drone attacks, noting damage to vital civilian infrastructure and disruption of essential services for millions. A major electricity substation serving Khartoum state was struck on Tuesday, October 27, following strikes on power stations in El Gezira, Sennar, and El Gedaref states on Monday, October 26. Local authorities reported extensive damage, threatening the national electricity grid.

The UN’s Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for Sudan warned that expanding drone attacks and repression in El Obeid put civilians at increasing risk. Drone strikes attributed to the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) killed and injured civilians, damaged schools and displacement sites, and disrupted access to healthcare, electricity, and water in North Kordofan’s capital. The mission chair noted the conflict is becoming more geographically extensive, technologically sophisticated, and dangerous for civilians.

Why it mattersCivilians across Sudan are at increasing risk from drone attacks that damage essential infrastructure and cause civilian casualties.

Sudan war displaces 27,400 families in Blue Nile

Intense fighting between the Sudanese army, RSF, and SPLA forces has driven thousands to seek shelter in overcrowded camps.

Fresh fighting between the Sudanese army, Rapid Support Forces (RSF), and the Sudan People’s Liberation Army (SPLA) has driven nearly 27,400 families from their homes in Sudan's Blue Nile state. Displaced people are fleeing into and across the state, adding to the strain on existing shelters. Many are escaping conflict in El Kurmuk, Geissan, and Wad El Mahi localities, while others are returning from South Sudan.

Humanitarian Aid Commissioner Qisma Abdel Karim Deldoum reported that Damazin locality alone hosts 12 camps, with El Karama (5) sheltering 4,814 families and the Agricultural Research Centre accommodating 3,913. The International Organization for Migration (IOM) stated in September that nearly 100,000 people had been displaced in Blue Nile between January and August, with children making up 47% of this number.

The number of internally displaced people in Blue Nile has risen by 21% since the August update, according to the IOM. Camps like Al-Karama 3 have not received food aid for four months, according to its manager.

Why it mattersIntense fighting in Blue Nile state has caused widespread displacement, putting pressure on shelters and leading to a severe deterioration of humanitarian conditions for nearly 27,400 families.

China deploys anti-drone systems in Abyei after attack

Chinese peacekeepers reinforced UNISFA after a drone attack killed UN personnel in the disputed region.

Chinese peacekeepers deployed anti-drone detection and jamming equipment in the disputed Abyei region on October 28, following a drone attack on a market on October 19 that killed members of a United Nations peacekeeping force. China’s Ministry of National Defence stated the specialised equipment aims to detect and jam drones and strengthen security to prevent further attacks. A Chinese peacekeeping rapid-response force returned to its camp after completing a security reinforcement mission.

The United Nations Interim Security Force for Abyei (UNISFA) requested the Chinese peacekeepers’ assistance to reinforce a Pakistani infantry battalion after the October 19 attack. Upon arrival, the Chinese force deployed anti-drone systems and strengthened defensive measures against potential aerial attacks. The security operation included using surveillance drones to monitor the market and secure the surrounding area.

UNISFA’s leadership praised the Chinese peacekeepers for their swift response and professionalism, stating their intervention helped protect lives and property and strengthen stability in the region. The reinforcement mission concluded after the security situation stabilised, allowing the Chinese force to return to its camp.

Why it mattersCivilians and UN peacekeepers in the disputed Abyei region are affected by increased security measures following a deadly drone attack.
