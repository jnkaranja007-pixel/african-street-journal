Air strike destroys WFP food aid trucks in Sudan

Two trucks carrying food for 5,800 people were destroyed in an aerial attack outside Dilling on August 30.

The UN World Food Programme (WFP) condemned an aerial attack that destroyed two trucks carrying food aid to families in South Kordofan. The trucks, clearly marked with WFP banners and flags, were hit outside Dilling on August 30 while en route to Kadugli. More than 50 tonnes of food, including cereals, yellow split peas, vegetable oil, and salt, were destroyed in the strike. These supplies were intended to provide life-saving assistance to 5,800 people for one month.

The WFP stated that this attack is the latest incident to disrupt humanitarian operations in Sudan. The agency has recorded eight aerial attacks this year alone that have damaged its premises and hindered its work. In Kadugli, the WFP is currently supporting nearly 58,000 individuals facing emergency levels of food insecurity or worse with essential food aid.

The WFP has urged all parties involved in the conflict to adhere to international humanitarian law, emphasizing the need to protect civilians, humanitarian workers, and vital aid supplies. The agency stressed that attacks on aid workers and humanitarian operations are unacceptable and must cease immediately. This incident highlights ongoing challenges for humanitarian agencies operating within Sudan.

Cholera Outbreak Kills 10 in Sudan's West Kordofan

The World Health Organization flags West Kordofan as the latest cholera hotspot, with over 40 infections reported in El Idai locality.

At least ten people have died and over forty have been infected with cholera in Sudan’s West Kordofan, according to the Kordofan Human Rights Observatory. The cases were recorded over Sunday and Monday in El Idai locality, which the World Health Organization now describes as “the latest outbreak hotspot.” The WHO has also announced a campaign to vaccinate 928,000 people against cholera in West Darfur and West Kordofan.

Mortada Ahmed, a spokesperson for the Kordofan Human Rights Observatory, stated that cholera is spreading widely in El Idai, with numbers expected to rise. He noted a severe lack of medicines and medical supplies, including intravenous fluids, and highlighted that fighting between the army and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has created blockades, hindering the delivery of aid and food. Environmental deterioration due to the rainy season, with stagnant water and increased insects, is also contributing to the health risks.

The observatory calls for urgent humanitarian intervention and for pressure on warring parties to open safe humanitarian corridors for medicines and food. The WHO reports over 2,600 cholera cases across several Sudanese states up to early August. UN spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric confirmed that the UN and its partners are intensifying efforts to combat cholera through treatment, vaccination campaigns, and distribution of medical supplies.

Why it mattersResidents of El Idai locality in West Kordofan are at high risk of death and further infection due to a cholera outbreak and a severe lack of medical supplies and access.

Climate Pressures Intensify Sudan's Rural and Displacement Crises

Intermittent rainfall and expanding agriculture are fueling resource conflicts, while displaced families face ongoing shortages.

Sudan's pastoralists and smallholder farmers are increasingly challenged by climate change, with unpredictable rainfall shortening grazing periods and forcing earlier migrations into farmland. This, combined with agricultural expansion into pastoral areas through mechanization and land privatization, is escalating resource conflicts. The UN Development Programme (UNDP) is developing climate-risk management tools, including weather-index insurance for farmers and pastoralists, designed to trigger automatic payouts based on dry-spell thresholds to help cover essential needs like feed and water.

The UNDP views the equal targeting of farmers and pastoralists with climate finance as a pioneering approach that could yield substantial peace dividends. Success, however, hinges on building a comprehensive enabling environment for climate risk management, which includes effective climate observation infrastructure and a robust regulatory framework for climate-risk financial services. These efforts build on lessons from a past project focused on sustainable and climate-resilient rainfed farming and pastoral systems.

Meanwhile, in South Kordofan's Abu Jubeiha locality, approximately 57,880 displaced people are living in 62 locations, straining local services. Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) reports that families in camps like El Safa face persistent needs for water, sanitation, and healthcare, often living in temporary shelters with limited space, where women and children form a significant portion of the population.

Why it mattersPastoralists and farmers face increased resource conflicts due to changing rainfall patterns and agricultural expansion, while displaced families struggle with access to essential services.

Sudan Gold Producer Sends Exports to UAE Amid War Funding Accusations

The nation's largest gold producer, Alliance Mining, continues to export to the UAE, a country accused by Sudan's army of funding paramilitary forces.

Sudan's largest gold producer, Alliance Mining and Kush for Exploration and Production Company, is sending its output to the United Arab Emirates, despite the Sudanese army accusing the UAE of funding the Rapid Support Forces (RSF). The company, owned by UAE-based Emiral Resources Ltd, operates in army-controlled territory near the Red Sea and River Nile states. Researchers and local miners indicate that senior military members are involved in running these mining operations.

The Sudanese government filed a genocide case against the UAE in March 2025, alleging support for the RSF. Emiral Resources Ltd denies any connection to the UAE government or funding military activities. Gold exports, whether smuggled or shipped openly, predominantly reach the UAE, with earnings fueling the purchase of sophisticated weaponry for both the RSF and the army.

Despite the ongoing conflict, Sudan's gold mining sector is expanding in army-controlled areas. In 2024, production reached 71 tons, an increase from 46 tons two years prior, generating $1.57 billion in tax revenue for the Central Bank of Sudan. This revenue aids both warring factions in acquiring advanced armaments.

News · 2026-09-05

Sudanese Refugees Risk Sea Crossings for Europe

Desperate to escape dire camp conditions, Sudanese youth are undertaking perilous journeys across the Mediterranean Sea.

Refugees in Adré camp, located on the Chad-Sudan border, describe their existence as "simply existing" and feeling like "prisoners." Adam Abkar Adam, a refugee, expressed his determination to travel through Libya to Europe, seeking a better life. Many others share this sentiment, driven by the lack of freedom and dignity within the camps.

The perilous journey has already claimed many lives. In late August, 37 Sudanese migrants died when their boat capsized en route from Libya to Greece. The International Organization for Migration (IOM) reported that at least 821 people died attempting to cross the sea into Europe in just four months of 2026.

Dar al-Salam Issa, Head of Women’s Affairs in Adré camp, stated that the camps are pushing people to leave due to their restrictive nature. She highlighted that children, young people, women, and the elderly are departing in search of a better life, often facing death in the desert or on the Mediterranean, mirroring the loss of life experienced in the ongoing war.

Why it mattersSudanese refugees are affected by the loss of life and the desperation driving them to dangerous sea crossings in search of safety and dignity.
