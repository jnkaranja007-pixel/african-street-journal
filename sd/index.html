UN food chief urges end to Sudan war amid famine crisis

Hundreds of thousands face starvation as the World Food Programme cuts aid due to funding shortfalls.

Luke J. Lindberg, the new executive director of the United Nations' World Food Programme, has called for an end to the conflict in Sudan, where hundreds of thousands of people are facing famine. Lindberg visited the Goz Al-Salam Camp for Internally Displaced Persons and urged increased financial support for the WFP to expand its humanitarian aid.

The WFP provided food to 4 million people in Sudan during August, but funding constraints forced the organization to cut assistance, leaving 900,000 people without help. The WFP is seeking $520 million to feed 5.4 million people through April 2027.

Sudan's war began in April 2023 between the military and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), creating the world’s largest humanitarian crisis. Approximately 34 million people, nearly two out of every three Sudanese, require assistance, with deadly drone attacks carried out by both rival forces.

Why it mattersThe World Food Programme is seeking $520 million to feed 5.4 million people through April 2027 as funding cuts leave 900,000 without aid.

Sudanese army retakes key West Kordofan town from RSF

The army and its allied Joint Force announced recapturing Al-Khuway after heavy fighting, seizing territory vital for military and commercial routes.

On Friday, Sudan's army and its allied Joint Force announced they had retaken the strategic town of Al-Khuway in West Kordofan state after heavy fighting with the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF). The Joint Force, an alliance of former Darfur rebel movements fighting alongside the army, said its troops now control Al-Khuway, Sarif Ankoush, Um Labana, Al-Dawdiya and several other areas.

The town sits on road networks that link North and West Kordofan with parts of Darfur. In a statement, Joint Force spokesperson Major Mutwakil Abu Ja said the fighting cost the RSF heavily, including the death of a commander, and claimed 20 RSF combat vehicles were destroyed and 40 seized. Darfur regional governor Minni Arko Minnawi welcomed the recapture, saying the town matters for further military advances and the return of displaced people. The claims could not be independently verified.

The RSF had seized Al-Khuway in May 2025, displacing 2,715 households between May 29 and 30 that year, according to the International Organization for Migration. The army and Joint Force also retook Sarif Ankoush and nearby areas earlier on Friday, and troops are now searching the area and pursuing remaining RSF fighters.

Why it mattersResidents of Al-Khuway and the surrounding villages, many of whom were displaced when the RSF took the town in 2025, now face a new front line as the army and its allies consolidate control.

Business · 2026-11-14

Sudan war financing evades international sanctions

Targeted sanctions by Australia, UK, and UN have failed to stop warring parties from funding their campaigns.

Despite targeted sanctions and travel bans from Australia, the United Kingdom, and a UN Security Council resolution, Sudan's warring factions continue to finance their conflict. Since April 15, 2023, these measures have aimed at commanders, companies, and facilitators, but have not stopped the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) from exploiting resources for private gain and military campaigns.

Research by the Sudan Transparency and Policy Tracker (STPT) indicates that while sanctions have increased costs and disrupted some financial channels, they have not deprived either side of necessary resources or external relationships. Designated businesses, like the SAF-aligned Red Rock Mining Company, have shifted operations through unsanctioned subsidiaries and new commercial relationships, maintaining a global footprint.

Red Rock Mining Company, facing sanctions from the UK, EU, and Switzerland, has leveraged international corporate vehicles to continue operations, boasting a global presence by late 2025. Similarly, the military-affiliated firm Zadna relies on Egypt-based partnerships and domestic dominance to render Western penalties ineffective, securing lucrative contracts without significant blowback, according to STPT.

Why it mattersBusinesses linked to Sudan's warring factions continue to operate and secure contracts despite international sanctions, allowing conflict financing to persist.

Politics · 2026-11-14

Sudan's veteran political leader al-Mirghani dies at 90

Mohamed Osman al-Mirghani, leader of the Democratic Unionist Party and head of the Khatmiyya Sufi order, died in Egypt.

Veteran political and religious leader Mohamed Osman al-Mirghani died in Egypt at the age of 90. Al-Mirghani led Sudan’s Democratic Unionist Party (DUP) and headed the influential Khatmiyya Sufi order. Sudan’s Council of Ministers described him as a prominent national and religious figure who significantly contributed to the country’s political life and efforts for unity and consensus.

Al-Mirghani, who drew support from both the DUP and the Khatmiyya Sufi movement, will be buried on Monday at the Sayed Ali Al-Mirghani Mosque in Khartoum North. He inherited leadership of the Khatmiyya order in 1968 and the main DUP faction in 1982. His family has a long history of influence in Sudanese public life.

In November 1988, al-Mirghani met with John Garang, leader of the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement (SPLM), resulting in the Mirghani-Garang Agreement. This peace initiative aimed to end Sudan’s second civil war with a ceasefire and a constitutional conference, but was overtaken by the 1989 military coup.

Why it mattersMohamed Osman al-Mirghani, a key figure in Sudanese politics and a leader of a major Sufi order, has died, affecting his party and followers.

Education · 2026-11-14

White Nile State reports 96% pass rate in intermediate exams

Shafaq Mustafa Suleiman Mohamed, a student at a private girls' school in Rabak, scored a perfect 280 out of 280 marks.

The Ministry of Education in Sudan's White Nile State has announced a 96 percent pass rate in the intermediate school examinations. A total of 23,026 students sat the exams across the state.

Shafaq Mustafa Suleiman Mohamed, a student at Al-Manhal Al-Azab Private Girls' School in Rabak locality, was the state's top performer with a perfect score. State Education Minister Ismail Ali Adam said all nine localities recorded pass rates above 90 percent, with little difference between them, despite a compressed academic year.

Governor Gamar Abdel-Mawla credited teachers and education staff for their efforts through difficult conditions that had disrupted schooling, calling the results an outstanding achievement.

Why it mattersStudents in every one of the state's nine localities now have their intermediate results, with all of them passing above the 90 percent mark.
