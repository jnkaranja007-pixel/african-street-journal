News · 2026-10-28

drone strike kills 102 civilians in nyala market

health minister in RSF-aligned government reports 113 others injured in attack on Tanqong Market in Dumaya neighbourhood.

At least 102 civilians died and 113 were injured in a drone strike on Tanqong Market in Nyala, South Darfur, on Monday evening. The market is in the Dumaya neighbourhood, west of Nyala, the administrative capital of Tasis, a government aligned with the Rapid Support Forces (RSF).

Dr. Alaa al-Din Nugud, health minister for the RSF-aligned Tasis government, stated that the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) carried out the attack. He said a medical team was dispatched immediately to rescue the wounded and that hospitals were on alert. Eyewitnesses reported the drones struck while the market was crowded with shoppers.

Adam Musa Obama, Executive Director of the Darfur Victims Organization, said his group documented 14 civilian deaths, including women and children. He called for international action to protect civilians and ban drone use against them. The war between the SAF and RSF began in April 2023.

Why it mattersCivilians in Nyala, South Darfur, were killed and injured in a drone strike on a market.

Politics · 2026-10-28

Sudan's Burhan travels to Moscow for Russia-Africa summit

Sudan's leader will meet President Putin and discuss cooperation amid ongoing conflict.

Sudan's Sovereignty Council head Abdel Fattah al-Burhan departed for Moscow on Tuesday to attend the third Russia-Africa summit on October 28-29. His delegation includes ministers of foreign affairs and finance, and the heads of intelligence and defence industries. Al-Burhan is scheduled to address the summit and hold bilateral talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Sudan's ambassador to Russia, Mohammed al-Ghazali al-Tijani Siraj, stated that the visit signifies growing bilateral ties and international cooperation, noting Russia's support for Sudan's institutions, including the armed forces fighting the RSF. Siraj also mentioned Russia's past UN Security Council actions defending Sudan's sovereignty and territorial integrity.

Siraj suggested Russia could assist Sudan's post-war reconstruction in sectors like energy, mining, agriculture, and infrastructure, referencing past economic and trade cooperation agreements. The summit agenda includes declarations on artificial intelligence and a 2027-2029 action plan for Russia-Africa partnership, covering political, economic, security, and educational cooperation.

Why it mattersSudan's leader is seeking international support and potential reconstruction aid from Russia while his country is engaged in internal conflict.

Politics · 2026-10-28

UK sanctions 15 Sudan commanders over atrocities

The UK also pledged £15 million in aid for survivors and humanitarian operations in Sudan.

The United Kingdom has sanctioned 15 senior commanders from Sudan’s Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) for alleged atrocities, including sexual violence. The UK announced this action on the first anniversary of El Fasher’s fall, alongside a pledge of £15 million in new support for survivors and humanitarian efforts.

The sanctions, announced by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, impose travel bans and asset freezes on the targeted individuals. Among those sanctioned are RSF Colonel Hussein Abu Jalak and Al-Nour Ahmed Adam, identified for their roles in the siege and attack on El Fasher. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband stated that UK-funded investigations documented the use of rape and starvation as weapons of war, calling for accountability for perpetrators.

The £15 million aid package includes £5 million for survivors of conflict-related sexual violence, providing healthcare and psychosocial support. An additional £10 million will fund humanitarian operations, such as clean water and sanitation in Tawila displacement sites and health services in El Obeid. This follows reporting by the Centre for Information Resilience on systematic starvation and sexual violence by both sides.

Why it mattersSudanese civilians face continued atrocities and lack of essential services due to the ongoing conflict.

Agriculture · 2026-10-28

delayed rains kill over 420 sheep in north darfur

livestock perished from thermal shock and dehydration after a sudden downpour followed months of drought.

In mid-September 2026, delayed rains in Jebel Issa, North Darfur, caused the deaths of over 420 sheep in Wadi Saleh alone. The downpour, which arrived 12 September after a prolonged dry spell, struck livestock weakened by starvation and heat. Unrecorded livestock deaths also occurred in neighbouring valleys.

Hassan Ahmed, representative of the Jebel Issa Emergency Response Room, explained that animals made exhausting daily journeys for scarce pasture and water. He stated that when the sudden downpour and temperature drop hit, their weakened bodies could not withstand the shock. These 420 sheep were collectively managed by 16 caretakers, representing the accumulated wealth for dozens of households.

Regional climate assessments indicate extreme rainfall variability across the northern Sahelian belt, with shorter, erratic rainy windows and intense dry spells. The Food and Agriculture Organization estimates that regional cereal production in vulnerable belts in Darfur has sharply declined due to delayed rainfall and conflict.

Why it mattersLivestock owners in Jebel Issa lost their accumulated wealth and financial safety net when over 420 sheep died.

Education · 2026-10-28

eight million children out of school in sudan

sudanese teachers warn of education system collapse as pupil numbers fall

Eight million children are now outside Sudan’s education system, the Sudanese Teachers’ Committee reported. The committee warned that the public education system faces collapse as teachers struggle with low wages and pupil numbers decline, especially in poorer regions.

Committee member Ammar Youssef stated that the crisis is worsening due to families' inability to afford schooling and public schools' operational difficulties. Spokesperson Sami al-Baqir noted that the minimum wage is estimated at SDG 12,000 and called for higher pay and payment of outstanding dues.

A strike in Khartoum state, now in its second day, has seen strong participation. The committee warned of serious threats to public education's future, citing a significant drop in student numbers in peripheral areas. Some teachers cannot afford transport to reach schools, reflecting their deteriorating living conditions.

Why it mattersEight million Sudanese children are being deprived of educational opportunities.
