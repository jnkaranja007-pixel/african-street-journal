Ethiopian Base Buildup Signals Imminent RSF Attack on Sudan's Ed Damazin

Satellite analysis reveals a significant increase in military vehicles and equipment at an Ethiopian base near the Sudan border, raising concerns of a major Rapid Support Forces.

Researchers at Yale's Humanitarian Research Lab have detected a substantial military build-up at an Ethiopian National Defence Forces base in Asosa, western Ethiopia, between August 23 and 28. The analysis identified approximately 100 new light technical vehicles, some armed, and around 15 armored personnel carriers, suggesting a force capable of equipping a regiment to brigade-sized unit. This escalation, which also includes new cargo containers and a likely vehicle storage shed, coincides with renewed fighting in Sudan's Blue Nile state.

The build-up is believed to be linked to a potential Rapid Support Forces (RSF) attack on Ed Damazin, the capital of Blue Nile state. The RSF has claimed capturing two Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) bases in the region recently. Previous satellite analysis by Yale documented conflict damage in areas like Kurmuk hospital and the A40 bridge, and SAF sources have alleged the RSF uses Asosa airport for resupply during attacks.

Yale researchers warn that the military activity at Asosa heightens the risk of a significant and imminent RSF assault on Ed Damazin, potentially leading to civilian casualties and displacement, possibly spilling into Ethiopia. The build-up may also aim to draw SAF forces away from North Kordofan, increasing the prospect of simultaneous fighting there. The threat to civilians is considered elevated in both Blue Nile and North Kordofan states.

Why it mattersCivilians in Sudan's Blue Nile and North Kordofan states face heightened risks of casualties and displacement due to a potential RSF attack linked to military build-up across the Ethiopian border.

Khartoum Residents Face Economic Hardship Amidst Slow Recovery

Returning residents and Khartoum's population confront power cuts, rising prices, and unemployment as the city attempts to rebuild after years of conflict.

Khartoum is struggling with a slow recovery from war, leaving residents and returnees facing daily hardships. Eyewitnesses report significant challenges in housing, health, water, livelihoods, security, and education. Frequent power and water cuts persist, hindering the restoration of normal life, while rising transport costs and movement restrictions after dark add to the burden. The accumulation of rubbish due to the rainy season has also created an environmental crisis by blocking drainage channels.

The economic situation in Khartoum remains bleak, with widespread unemployment as most commercial activities have ceased. Only government employment and a limited range of commercial operations are functioning, depriving many families of their primary income sources. Fluctuations in goods prices further strain household budgets. The education sector is particularly affected, with teacher wage crises leading schools to impose fees on pupils or rely on parental contributions, resulting in increased educational loss and school dropouts.

Healthcare services in Khartoum are severely impacted by the destruction of facilities and a decline in service quality due to shortages of medical supplies and ongoing power outages. The proliferation of private and commercial schools, many lacking basic conditions and qualified staff, exacerbates the educational crisis. Repeated teachers' strikes and the displacement of students have disrupted academic progress, with some students facing significant financial demands to catch up on missed lessons.

Why it mattersResidents of Khartoum are directly affected by the economic collapse, facing unemployment, rising costs, and a lack of essential services like power and water.

US Arms Embargo Proposal Faces Opposition

A US proposal to extend an arms embargo across Sudan is met with support from Western allies but strong opposition from Russia, China, and Sudan.

The United States has proposed expanding the existing arms embargo on Darfur to encompass all of Sudan, a move aimed at pressuring conflict parties to reduce their capacity for war and limit weapons flow. Denmark, Britain, and France have backed the US initiative, while China, Russia, and Sudan's representative have openly opposed it. Political and human rights activist Nemat Koko stated that reducing the warring parties' access to weapons could limit violence against civilians, particularly sexual and gender-based violence, which disproportionately affects women.

Koko highlighted that the widespread availability of weapons creates an environment of fear and restriction for women, hindering their ability to access essential services like markets, water, and education. The targeting of infrastructure further burdens women, especially female heads of households, who shoulder increased responsibilities as services collapse. She emphasized that the war impacts women's social and economic roles within families and communities, not just their individual safety.

From a feminist perspective, Koko argued that protecting women requires addressing the root causes of violence, including the proliferation of weapons and the collapse of protection and justice institutions. She believes that women's involvement in discussions about an arms embargo should extend beyond symbolic participation to substantive engagement in shaping policies that create a safer environment and prevent violations.

Why it mattersWomen are disproportionately affected by the ongoing conflict and the proliferation of weapons, facing increased risks of sexual violence and restricted movement.

Sudan Liberation Movement Rejects El Burhan Dialogue, Backs Quad Initiative

Abdelwahid El Nur's movement met UN envoy Pekka Haavisto in Nairobi, Kenya, to outline its stance on peace efforts.

The Sudan Liberation Movement, led by Abdelwahid El Nur, has rejected a dialogue proposed by Sovereignty Council chair Abdelfattah El Burhan, viewing it as an attempt to legitimize a coup. The movement met UN envoy Pekka Haavisto in Nairobi, stating that such dialogues will not resolve Sudan's crisis. They advocate for genuine, inclusive dialogue only after the war ends, excluding the National Congress Party, the Islamic Movement, and their affiliates.

The SLM-AW welcomed the international Quad's initiative, finding it laid sound foundations for addressing the situation in Sudan. However, the movement expressed reservations about the performance of the international Quintet, with a delegation sending a letter detailing concerns and proposals. Meanwhile, civil forces in eastern Sudan, the Alliance of Civil Forces for Eastern Sudan, welcomed the "Peace We Want" document from anti-war civilian and political groups.

The eastern Sudan alliance supports the Quad initiative's principles, including preserving Sudan's unity, ending the war, and placing civilians in charge of the political process. Their spokesman, Saleh Ammar, stressed the need to protect the peace process from warring parties and external influences. The alliance also called for international sanctions against Ali Karti's group, emphasizing that the continuation of war benefits its parties at the expense of civilians and national resources.

Why it mattersThe rejection of El Burhan's dialogue by a significant movement and the backing of alternative initiatives by civil forces indicate a divided political landscape that could prolong the conflict and humanitarian suffering.

Military Intelligence Detains Public Health Specialist Attia Abdullah Attia in Gadarif

The military intelligence in Gadarif arrested public health specialist Attia Abdullah Attia while he was conducting routine leishmaniasis surveys.

Military intelligence forces in Gadarif state arrested Dr. Attia Abdullah Attia, a public health specialist and the general secretary of the preparatory committee for the Sudan Doctors Syndicate, in the latter half of August 2026. The arrest occurred while Dr. Attia was engaged in routine leishmaniasis surveys in the state, in collaboration with the Regional Institute for Endemic Diseases at Gadarif University.

Human rights advocates stated that security authorities in Gadarif are imposing strict restrictions on all groups working to defend ordinary citizens needing urgent humanitarian aid. These measures aim to suppress freedoms of movement and work in the region. Another defender from Gadarif told the Sudan War Monitor that security authorities across all eastern Sudanese states are monitoring individuals and groups who criticize local authorities, labeling them as belonging to "Tasis" or "Sumud."

This labeling exposes targeted individuals to harassment, arrests, and fabricated charges. The Sudanese Doctors Syndicate's preparatory committee confirmed Dr. Attia's arrest in Gadarif by military intelligence, highlighting the challenging environment for health professionals and human rights defenders operating under current security restrictions.

Why it mattersPublic health specialist Attia Abdullah Attia was arrested by military intelligence in Gadarif state while conducting routine leishmaniasis surveys, impacting health services and humanitarian aid efforts.
