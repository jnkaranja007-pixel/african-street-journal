Drone strike kills eight civilians in North Kordofan

An independent rights group reported that a drone strike in Um Sharu area of Sodari locality destroyed four vehicles and killed eight civilians, with dozens injured.

Eight civilians died and dozens were injured in a drone strike in Sudan’s North Kordofan state on Thursday, according to the independent Emergency Lawyers group. The group stated that a drone struck civilians traveling through the Um Sharu area of Sodari locality, destroying four vehicles. The drone reportedly returned and struck again as people attempted to evacuate the wounded.

Emergency Lawyers reported no military presence or armed clashes were occurring in the area at the time of the attack. The group condemned the strike as a violation of international humanitarian law that could amount to a war crime, noting it would worsen civilian suffering and restrict medical access. Neither the Sudanese army nor the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) immediately commented on the incident.

North Kordofan is a major front in the ongoing war between the army and the RSF, which began in April 2023. Drone attacks have become a significant feature of the conflict, with the United Nations reporting that drones were responsible for 80% of civilian deaths in the first four months of 2026. The conflict has displaced approximately 13 million people.

Why it mattersEight civilians were killed and dozens injured in a drone strike, impacting rural livelihoods and potentially restricting access to medical care.

Displaced in Nyala Shelters Face Critical Humanitarian Conditions

Emergency health centers in Nyala warn of worsening conditions in displacement shelters due to shortages of food, water, medicine, and shelter materials, exacerbated by the rainy.

Emergency room and health centers in Nyala, South Darfur, are sounding the alarm over deteriorating humanitarian conditions in city displacement shelters. Hundreds of families face inadequate basic needs, with critical shortages of food, water, medicines, shelter materials, and hygiene supplies. Nibras Abdullah, a member of Nyala’s emergency room, stated that conditions have become extremely critical, especially with the onset of the rainy season, forcing families to live in the open or in damaged classrooms where rainwater leaks through.

Specific shelters are experiencing severe deprivation. El Jir, housing approximately 214 families, requires urgent repairs to toilets and latrines, alongside a need for food, water, and shelter materials. El Hamaira shelter, accommodating about 274 families, urgently needs food, water, hygiene supplies, and medicines, particularly for chronic conditions like diabetes and high blood pressure. Other shelters, including El Zahra (115 families) and Mahira School (457 families), report similar widespread shortages.

Abdullah described the current aid as inadequate compared to the growing needs of displaced populations, noting that rising prices further complicate efforts to meet demand. While some organizations provide assistance, the scale does not match the increasing number of families in shelters. She called for immediate intervention from humanitarian organizations and authorities to supply essential items and improve temporary accommodations to prevent further deterioration as the rainy season continues.

Why it mattersDisplaced families in Nyala shelters are facing severe shortages of food, water, medicine, and adequate shelter, with conditions worsening due to the rainy season.

Fighting Disrupts Trade, Raising Famine Fears in Kordofan

Residents in several Kordofan localities are resorting to animal feed as food prices skyrocket and aid is cut off due to ongoing conflict and road closures.

The Kordofan Observatory has warned of imminent famine in parts of the region, citing a sharp decline in incomes and soaring food prices. Families in El Nahud, Wad Banda, El Khuwei, and Muglad are now consuming ambaz, groundnut residue typically used as animal feed, as living conditions worsen and humanitarian aid becomes inaccessible. The price of a malwa of millet has reached 35,000 Sudanese pounds, placing it beyond the reach of many.

This escalating food crisis is attributed by the observatory to recent fighting around El Obeid and a military build-up, which have disrupted trade routes and prevented essential goods from reaching affected areas. The deteriorating security situation has also hampered farmers' ability to cultivate their land, compounded by a late arrival of rains this year. The observatory cautions that continued conflict and restricted humanitarian access could precipitate a widespread humanitarian catastrophe.

The Kordofan Observatory is urging all parties to the conflict to establish secure trade and humanitarian routes, and to prevent the use of aid as a pressure tactic. It also calls on authorities in Khartoum and Northern State to reverse transport restrictions to Kordofan areas controlled by the Rapid Support Forces, and for the Rapid Support Forces to facilitate trade and remove obstacles contributing to rising prices.

Why it mattersResidents in Kordofan are facing famine conditions because fighting has disrupted trade, blocked aid, and made basic food items unaffordable.

Sudan Lifts Two-Year Curfew and Emergency in Capital

Authorities announced the removal of night-time restrictions and a state of emergency in Khartoum, aiming to allow round-the-clock movement and economic activity, though security.

Sudan has lifted a two-year night-time curfew and a state of emergency in the capital, Khartoum, a move authorities stated will enable residents to move and work without time limitations. The decision was made by the Security Affairs Coordination Committee, chaired by Khartoum Governor Ahmed Othman Hamza. Despite this easing of restrictions, security measures remain in place, with army patrols and checkpoints continuing their operations throughout the city. Residents are still advised to carry identification when travelling at night.

The curfew, initially imposed in May 2024 as fighting escalated between the Sudanese army and the Rapid Support Forces, had been in effect for two years. The broader conflict in Sudan began in April 2023, resulting in tens of thousands of deaths and displacing approximately 13 million people, according to UN and international estimates. Military authorities regained control of Khartoum 18 months ago, but fighting persists in other regions.

Further restrictions have been placed on foreign nationals entering Khartoum State. Entry by public or private transport, including trains and buses, is now prohibited unless individuals possess valid visas or permits. These measures indicate a continued focus on security and control within the capital, even as some civilian movement restrictions are relaxed.

Why it mattersThe lifting of the curfew and state of emergency in Khartoum allows for increased movement and economic activity, potentially benefiting businesses and trade.

Kassala Gold Traders Strike Over Unregulated Street Vendors

Gold merchants closed shops in Kassala, demanding authorities address fraud and theft by itinerant sellers that damages the market's reputation and customer trust.

Kassala gold traders have extended their strike, initiated on Monday, until authorities resolve issues caused by itinerant gold sellers. The head of the goldsmiths’ association, Abboud Ahmed Issa, stated that the association has repeatedly alerted police and security agencies to improper practices by these vendors, which include fraud, theft, and transactions that harm customers and the trade's reputation. Established shop owners face accountability, while untraceable itinerant vendors leave the association to handle unresolved complaints.

The problem of unregulated street vendors has persisted since the Covid-19 pandemic and has worsened recently, causing frustration and damaging the city's gold market reputation. Traders report that the proliferation of itinerant sellers has created an unregulated parallel market, involving manipulation of gold weight and prices, and trading without official invoices. This practice distorts market prices and disadvantages shop owners who incur costs for taxes, fees, and security.

The strike began after an incident near the market on Monday escalated into clashes, injuring a bystander. Traders insist they are not trying to bypass authorities but are seeking market regulation to prevent further harm to customers and maintain market stability. The association expressed readiness to cooperate with any official arrangements to protect both customers and traders, emphasizing the strike will continue until a clear solution is implemented.

Why it mattersGold traders in Kassala are striking because unregulated itinerant vendors engage in fraud and theft, impacting customer trust and the established market.
