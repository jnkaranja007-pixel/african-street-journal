Sudan aid cuts worsen hunger, malnutrition

World Food Programme cuts and crop failure leave families struggling for food as prices soar in Darfur.

Displaced families and vulnerable communities across Sudan are facing a deepening hunger crisis due to cuts in food aid and widespread crop failure. The World Food Programme (WFP) has reduced assistance, while East Darfur has experienced a near-total crop failure, leaving households without food and income. Residents in Sheairia report soaring prices, worsening malnutrition, and shortages of essential medicines and therapeutic food for children.

Carl Skau, the WFP's acting executive director, stated the agency has the capacity to help millions but is running out of money. The General Coordination for Displaced People and Refugees warns that reduced aid could force families to skip meals and sell possessions. Spokesperson Adam Rijal said funding shortages threaten essential meals and could lead to desperate measures like withdrawing children from school.

Farmers in East Darfur harvested almost nothing of their main crops, including groundnuts and millet, leading to food insecurity. A sack of millet now costs around SDG 350,000, with prices for onions, cooking oil, and sugar also significantly high. Pregnant women struggle to get adequate diets, and children suffer from malnutrition, with limited supplies of therapeutic food available.

Why it mattersDisplaced families and vulnerable communities in Sudan face increased hunger and malnutrition due to reduced food aid and crop failures.

African Union condemns deadly Sudan drone strikes

The AU chairperson calls for an immediate humanitarian ceasefire after over 250 reported deaths.

The African Union has condemned drone attacks that killed scores of civilians across Sudan and the disputed Abyei area. Reports indicate over 250 people have died in strikes over several days. The AU Commission chairperson, Mahmoud Ali Youssouf, called for an immediate humanitarian ceasefire.

Drone attacks on Monday in Abyei reportedly killed dozens of civilians, including women and children, near Amiet and Todach markets. Media reports citing local and medical sources say about 80 people were killed in El Dibb, West Kordofan, on October 18, while 23 died in Nyala, South Darfur, on October 16. At least three children were killed in Khartoum on October 19.

In Abyei, a drone attack on Ameet market on October 19 has reportedly killed more than 150 people. Youssouf urged all parties to protect civilians and humanitarian personnel, and ensure unhindered humanitarian access. He also stressed the need to respect Sudan’s sovereignty and territorial integrity.

Why it mattersDrone attacks have killed civilians, caused displacement, and disrupted access to food and healthcare in Sudan.

Khartoum denies drone strike role in Abyei market attack

Sudan's government rejected accusations of involvement in an October 19 drone strike that killed at least 153 people in Abyei.

Khartoum has denied its armed forces carried out a drone strike on Amiet Common Market in the disputed Abyei region on October 19. Sudan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs stated on Thursday that the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) had no connection to the attack, which killed at least 153 people and wounded 19. South Sudanese officials, however, blamed Sudan’s military for the incident.

The ministry expressed condolences to the victims’ families and wished the wounded a speedy recovery, but provided no evidence to support its denial. The victims included Sudanese, South Sudanese, and foreign nationals. The attack has increased tensions between Sudan and South Sudan, leading South Sudan to summon Sudan’s ambassador to Juba.

Meanwhile, the RSF-aligned Government of Peace has called for an international investigation into the attack. Its foreign minister, Ammar Amoun Deldoum, told Radio Tamazuj on Thursday that a complaint was filed with the Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for Sudan on October 19. Deldoum stated that a local committee was also established to gather information from traders and survivors.

Why it mattersSudanese citizens were among the 153 people killed in a drone strike in the disputed Abyei region.

Business · 2026-10-24

Sudan lifts ban on 46 imports amid rising inflation

The Sudanese currency reached SDG 10,300 against the dollar as annual inflation hit 28.29%.

Sudan has lifted import restrictions on 46 goods as the dollar climbed to SDG 10,300 and annual inflation reached 28.29%. Sovereignty Council chairman Abdelfattah El Burhan cancelled a cabinet decision that had banned these imports, aiming to ease pressure on consumers struggling with soaring food prices.

Finance minister Jibril Ibrahim stated the decision considers consumer needs while protecting domestic producers. Traders reported an immediate price fall for some goods following the announcement. The National Importers’ Chamber welcomed the move but requested a clear cancellation of Resolution No. 74, which had imposed the ban and caused losses.

Foreign-exchange dealers reported the dollar rose to SDG 10,200 for buying and SDG 10,300 for selling on Thursday, an increase of SDG 100. Annual inflation rose to 28.29% in September from 26.14% in August, with food and drink prices up 36.81% annually.

Why it mattersConsumers face higher prices due to rising inflation and a depreciating currency.

Politics · 2026-10-24

South Darfur mobilization committee backs army

New committee pledges to organize public support and resources for the Sudanese Armed Forces in the 'Battle for Dignity'.

The Higher Committee for Mobilization and Popular Resistance in South Darfur State has begun its work, promising to increase support for the Sudanese Armed Forces and the 'Battle for Dignity'. The committee aims to defeat rebel militias and protect civilians.

South Darfur State Governor Bashir Mursal stated the committee will unite public and official efforts to support the military in this battle. Lieutenant Colonel Yassir Adam Mohammed, head of the committee, said their plan includes organizing public mobilization and directing logistical support to frontline positions.

The committee urged the people of South Darfur to reject rumors and stand with the armed forces. They believe the Sudanese people and their army are the strongest guarantee of national sovereignty and unity.

Why it mattersThe new mobilization committee in South Darfur State will organize public support and resources for the Sudanese Armed Forces.
