Drone strike kills two traders in North Darfur

A vehicle travelling from Northern State to Al-Malha locality was hit by a drone on Sunday morning.

Two civilians died on Sunday when a drone struck a private vehicle in Wadi Majrur, North Darfur. The Al-Malha Emergency Monitor reported that a drone, allegedly from the Sudanese Armed Forces, hit a 1988 Toyota Hilux around 11 a.m. The vehicle was carrying three people and goods from Al-Dabba locality in Northern State towards Al-Malha.

Saddam Idris, spokesperson for the Al-Malha Emergency Monitor for Human Rights, stated that two of the three passengers were killed. The victims were identified as trader Mohamed Musa Bakhit Tikras and Ali Ismail. The third passenger, Idris Ismail, survived the strike but suffered from exposure to toxic substances and dust from the blast.

The Al-Malha Emergency Monitor stated that targeting civilians violates international humanitarian law. Mohamed Musa Bakhit Tikras was from the Umm Bayada area, while Ali Ismail was from Al-Hara. The survivor, Idris Ismail, had been working in mining areas in Northern State and had lost his kiosk business before the incident.

Why it mattersTwo traders, Mohamed Musa Bakhit Tikras and Ali Ismail, were killed when a drone struck their vehicle in North Darfur.

Rebel coalition captures Sali town in Blue Nile

Rapid Support Forces and allied fighters advanced from two directions to take the army-held town on Tuesday.

The Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and allied Sudan People’s Liberation Movement-North (SPLM-N) fighters captured Sali in southern Blue Nile State on Tuesday. The fighters advanced from two directions, moving north from Kurmuk and eastward from Sirkum, after repeated attempts to take the army-held town. RSF soldiers were seen outside the Sali health centre, confirming their entry into the town.

The Sudan Founding Alliance (Ta’sis), which includes the RSF and SPLM-N, announced its forces had captured the strategic Sali military base. The statement claimed that fighting left a large number of army personnel dead or captured, with weapons and military equipment seized. Ta’sis coalition forces stated their commitment to protecting civilians in liberated areas and urged residents to stay away from military operations.

The Ta’sis coalition called these victories a significant shift in the fighting and a strategic step towards ending the dominance of extremist groups. They reaffirmed their determination to continue advancing until remaining strongholds are liberated. The coalition stated these victories represent another step towards the dawn of liberation and the construction of a Sudanese state founded on justice, equality and the rule of law.

Why it mattersFighting in Blue Nile State has resulted in rebel forces capturing the town of Sali and seizing military equipment.

Sudan women journalists warn of rising impunity

A network reports escalating violations against female reporters amid the ongoing war and widespread weapons.

Sudan’s Women Journalists Network has warned of increasing violations and crimes against journalists, particularly women, in Sudan. The network issued a report on the International Day to End Impunity for Crimes against Journalists, highlighting a growing culture of impunity due to the ongoing war, weapon availability, and weakened state institutions.

The report states the current war has worsened the justice crisis, making crimes against journalists a sign of broader failures. Access to justice is militarised, and law enforcement and judicial bodies struggle to operate independently, prosecute offenders, or provide redress. Displacement and social pressures also prevent women journalists from filing complaints and pursuing legal action.

The Women Journalists Network calls for an end to the war, disarmament of militias, and investigations into crimes against journalists. They urge that those responsible be held accountable and victims receive justice and redress. The network also appealed to the UN Fact-Finding Mission to intensify investigations into crimes against Sudanese women journalists.

Why it mattersWomen journalists in Sudan face escalating violations and crimes, with limited access to justice and redress due to the ongoing war and societal pressures.

El Fasher police stations resume operations

Interior Minister and Prime Minister attend ceremony marking return of Federal Police services after war disruptions.

Police stations in El Fasher, North Darfur State, have resumed operations after years of disruption. Interior Minister Lt. Gen. Suleiman Sandal Haqar and Prime Minister Mohamed Hassan Al-Taishi attended a ceremony on Monday marking the resumption of Federal Police services in the city. Police forces largely disappeared from public view after the war began on 15 April 2023, halting most services.

The entire Founding Government Council travelled from Nyala to El Fasher to assess conditions and reopen the stations. Minister Haqar called the event historic, stating the city is recovering despite war and destruction. Prime Minister Al-Taishi conveyed that the police are a fundamental pillar of the government's project to build a new Sudan respecting citizen dignity.

Al-Taishi added that the police must be equipped, trained, and paid monthly to enforce law and protect civilians. Restoring normal life in El Fasher, including providing essential services and rehabilitating institutions, is a priority. A resident confirmed police units are operational, receiving complaints, and conducting investigations, with a court complex also having reopened the previous week.

Why it mattersResidents of El Fasher can now access police services and the justice system following the reopening of police stations and a court complex.

Drones strike Sudan power stations

Fires broke out at electricity facilities in New Halfa and Aroma after drone attacks early Monday.

Drones attacked power stations in Sudan’s Kassala State cities of New Halfa and Aroma early Monday, causing fires at the sites. Eyewitnesses reported that multiple drones struck electricity and power facilities in the two cities. In New Halfa, three drones attacked around 2 a.m., firing missiles that caused explosions and flames, according to witness Mohamed Salih.

The extent of the damage remains unclear, but no casualties have been reported from the attacks. Two drones were intercepted, while a third reportedly completed its strike. In Aroma, drones targeted the Haya transformer station and the Al-Sahil fuel depot, prompting a civil defense team to respond to extinguish fires.

Kassala State Governor retired Major General Al-Sadiq Al-Azraq visited the attacked sites to assess the damage. Firefighting operations were ongoing as authorities worked to determine the full impact of the strikes on the power infrastructure.

Why it mattersPower infrastructure in Kassala State was damaged by drone attacks, affecting electricity supply.
