Sudan drone strikes killed 306 in three weeks

UN human rights chief Volker Türk reported hundreds injured as attacks hit civilian sites and infrastructure across the country.

At least 306 people died and hundreds more were injured in Sudan during the first three weeks of October due to drone strikes. The United Nations Human Rights Office documented these attacks, which were attributed to both the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF). Civilian areas including homes, markets, hospitals, and displacement sites were hit, along with vital infrastructure like power stations and water sources.

UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, Volker Türk, called the escalating drone attacks deeply disturbing, noting they have caused the majority of conflict-related civilian deaths in Sudan this year. He highlighted that scores of civilians were killed and essential services destroyed, worsening the humanitarian crisis and driving displacement. The UN urged warring parties to adhere to international humanitarian law and avoid targeting civilians and civilian objects.

Specific incidents included alleged SAF drone strikes on shelters in West Kordofan between October 6 and 10, killing at least 25 civilians. In Blue Nile state, an alleged RSF strike on October 9 near a gold mining site killed at least 31 displaced people, mostly women and children. On October 18, a transport truck was struck in a double-tap drone attack in Dibab, West Kordofan, killing at least 139 people.

Why it mattersCivilians in Sudan were killed and injured by drone strikes hitting homes, markets, hospitals, and displacement sites.

UK-led partners warn of famine risk in Sudan

19-point-five million Sudanese face acute hunger as conflict and aid obstruction worsen food insecurity.

A UK-led international warning highlights the rising risk of famine in Sudan, where 19.5 million people face acute hunger. The joint ministerial statement, issued by the UK and 31 partners on October 23, states that 5 million are experiencing emergency levels of food insecurity. The number of children suffering severe acute malnutrition could exceed 825,000 if the current trend continues.

The statement attributes Sudan's hunger crisis primarily to conflict, which has displaced millions, damaged infrastructure, and disrupted food production and markets. It also cites systematic obstruction of humanitarian aid and the effects of El Niño, including reduced rainfall and drought. The signatories urge warring parties to lift aid restrictions, protect aid workers, and allow air access into Darfur.

The parties to the conflict are called upon to remove all obstacles to humanitarian assistance and ensure safe, unhindered access. The statement also requests international community to increase humanitarian funding urgently. The number of areas affected by food insecurity is expected to double from the initial assessment of 14.

21 countries meet in Geneva to discuss Sudan war

UN envoy Pekka Haavisto says prisoner exchanges and exam coordination are first steps toward a truce.

Intensive international and UN efforts are underway to end the war in Sudan, starting with a Geneva meeting of 21 countries. The talks highlighted the need for immediate confidence-building measures. UN secretary-general’s personal envoy Pekka Haavisto stated that prisoner exchanges and coordination on Sudan’s secondary school certificate examinations would be the first steps.

The Quad nations—Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and the United States—held "extremely productive" meetings in Geneva. Discussions focused on securing a humanitarian truce and paving the way for a permanent ceasefire. US officials stated that there is no military solution to the conflict and urged all parties to engage in negotiations for an inclusive dialogue leading to civilian rule.

Political analyst Mohamed Torshin described the Geneva meeting as a "pivotal step." He believes successful implementation of prisoner releases and exam coordination could lead to a three-month ceasefire. Torshin noted the greatest challenge remains the military track, including the future of the Rapid Support Forces and the integration of the Juba Peace Agreement.

Why it mattersSudanese secondary school students and prisoners of war are directly affected by the proposed confidence-building measures.

News · 2026-10-26

RSF invasion of El Fasher causes mass displacement and famine risk

Over 1.19 million people fled into Chad, with 107,000 displaced in initial weeks, facing severe hunger.

The Rapid Support Forces’ (RSF) invasion of El Fasher on October 26, 2025, led to catastrophic loss for hundreds of thousands, with survivors now facing famine and displacement.

Hekmat Al-Hafiz described her husband's death in a drone strike and her journey to Ad-Dabba, while Reem Sabil recounted cramming 300 women and children into a truck after the city fell.

The International Organization for Migration reported over 62% of El Fasher’s population fled, with 1.19 million reaching Chad, where UNICEF warned over 130,000 children faced grave dangers including malnutrition.

South Sudanese youth pledge peace at Dakar Olympics

A six-member delegation will travel to Senegal for the 2026 Youth Olympic Games to promote nonviolent conflict resolution.

Six South Sudanese youth from the Upper Nile region are traveling to Senegal for the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games, which run from October 31 to November 13. The delegation pledged to promote nonviolent conflict resolution and stated that violence should not define their country's future.

The French Embassy in Juba is financially supporting the 10-day visit, facilitated by the Nonviolent Peace Force. French Ambassador Philippe Michel-Kleisbauer urged the youth to use the international platform to promote peace and demonstrate commitment to South Sudan's development, calling them potential agents of change.

Ambassador Michel-Kleisbauer also highlighted a cooperative development project along the White Nile, supported by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and local non-governmental organizations. He stated the youth are expected to help implement this project among local communities and authorities to ensure tangible results and hope for future generations.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese youth participating in the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games are pledging to promote peace and nonviolent conflict resolution.
