US proposes Sudan-wide arms embargo, faces UN Security Council division

A US proposal to expand the arms embargo on Darfur to cover all of Sudan and include drones has generated opposing reactions among UN Security Council members.

The United States has proposed extending the arms embargo currently limited to Sudan's Darfur region to the entire country, including drones and their components, to stem the flow of weapons fueling the conflict since April 2023. This initiative, presented at a UN Security Council session on Monday, aims to strengthen the monitoring capacity of the panel of experts responsible for sanctions.

Denmark, the UK, and France have publicly supported the US proposal. Denmark's foreign minister, Lars Løkke Rasmussen, also called for the International Criminal Court's jurisdiction to be extended across Sudan. However, China, Russia, and Sudan's UN representative oppose the expansion, with Russia's Vasily A Nebenzya arguing it would harm the Sudanese army.

Massad Boulos, the US president’s senior adviser for Arab and African affairs, told the Security Council that more than twelve countries are providing military support to the warring parties, prolonging the conflict and increasing civilian casualties. Sudan's army chief, Abdelfattah El Burhan, dismissed the potential impact of any additional sanctions on the country.

Why it mattersThe proposed arms embargo directly affects the Sudanese people by influencing the availability of weapons in a conflict that has caused civilian casualties and destabilized the region.

UN Relief Chief Reports Sudan Crisis Suffers from 'Lack of Action'

More than two-thirds of Sudan's population requires lifesaving assistance, with approximately 1,400 civilians killed in the first half of 2026, primarily due to drone strikes.

Tom Fletcher, the U.N. under-secretary-general for humanitarian affairs, informed the Security Council that about 1,100 civilian deaths were caused by drone strikes. These attacks have targeted homes, markets, and vital infrastructure in areas like El Obeid in North Kordofan, where fighting has displaced about 15,000 families since mid-July, straining existing services.

Humanitarian operations face significant challenges across Sudan, including insecurity, bureaucratic restrictions, and drone attacks. In West Kordofan, the epicenter of a recent cholera outbreak, aid efforts remain limited. Intensifying violence in the Nuba Mountains has cut off communities already suffering from chronic food insecurity, with aid workers detained and facilities looted.

Despite these obstacles, aid agencies reached nearly 11 million people with at least one form of assistance by mid-year, representing about 52% of their prioritized target. While food assistance is on track to reach 11.4 million people, other sectors like education, health, shelter, and water are behind targets, according to Fletcher.

Why it mattersSudanese civilians are directly affected by ongoing violence and a lack of humanitarian action, leading to displacement, deaths, and limited access to essential services.

News · 08/25/2026

Blue Nile civil society warns of deepening crisis as thousands flee violence

Thousands of people are fleeing renewed fighting in Sudan's Blue Nile state, while authorities have restricted civil society activities and closed emergency response centers.

Displacement is worsening in Sudan's Blue Nile state as thousands of people flee fighting in Geissan locality, near the Ethiopian border. Ali Hajo, secretary-general of the Blue Nile Civil Society Initiative, stated that over 15,000 individuals displaced from Geissan have moved to areas including Bakuri, Tandaru, Wad Al-Mahi, and Al-Roseires localities. The International Organization for Migration reported last week that nearly 14,000 people were displaced from Geissan and Kurmuk localities in August.

Security authorities have restricted civil society activities, according to Hajo, by shutting emergency-response kitchens and suspending emergency response rooms that provide assistance to displaced people. He also alleged that military intelligence arrested or pursued several activists involved in humanitarian and community work. These figures could not be independently verified.

Displaced people are sheltering in schools and makeshift camps, where heavy rains are worsening already difficult living conditions. Hajo warned that these restrictions on civilian initiatives and local aid centers, combined with rising displacement and limited humanitarian assistance, could further worsen conditions for civilians affected by the conflict.

Why it mattersCivilians in Blue Nile state are directly affected by increased displacement, restricted access to humanitarian aid, and worsening living conditions due to ongoing conflict and government actions.

Sudan's short-stature football team battles war for World Cup dream

Sudan’s short-stature football team is preparing for the 2026 World Cup in Morocco despite ongoing conflict, displacement, and limited resources.

Sudan's short-stature football team continues to train in Khartoum, aiming for the 2026 World Cup in Morocco. The team faces significant challenges, including war, displacement, and a lack of resources, as they prepare for the international competition.

The team, officially established and recognized in 2020 through the efforts of volunteers and sports journalists, brings together players from various parts of Sudan. Many players have been displaced by the conflict but continue to travel and train together when possible, despite security concerns and limited facilities.

Soccer expert Mohammed Abdullah praised the players' perseverance, stating they represent Sudan with the same dedication as any other national team. Player Ibrahim Abdalla, who faced discrimination due to his stature, hopes to prove that these barriers do not define his abilities on the pitch, especially after Sudan missed the inaugural World Cup for people of short stature due to funding and travel difficulties.

Why it mattersThe story directly affects Sudanese short-stature footballers by highlighting their ongoing efforts to compete internationally despite significant national hardships.
