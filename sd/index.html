Sudan power cut sparks price surge

A drone attack on a substation has left Rabak without electricity for two weeks, driving up costs for basic goods.

Residents of Rabak, in Sudan’s White Nile state, have endured a 14-day power blackout following a drone attack on the Mashkour electricity substation at the end of October. The outage has caused sharp price increases for essential services, with a block of ice now costing SDG 60,000 and charging a mobile phone rising to SDG 5,000.

Milling 3.5 kilogrammes of grain now costs SDG 10,000 as mills operate on generators, leading to long queues for flour. Water supplies have resumed at SDG 3,000 per container after pumps were restarted with generators. Children's education has halted after dark, forcing families to use charcoal irons for school uniforms, which cost SDG 3,000 per bag.

The prolonged power outage has made daily life a struggle in Rabak, with rising costs for food, water, refrigeration, and communications. There is no clear indication from officials on when electricity will be restored to the city.

Why it mattersResidents of Rabak face increased costs for essential goods and services due to a prolonged power outage.

Drone attacks cripple Sudan's power stations

UNICEF warns of dire consequences for children as drought worsens existing crises.

Drone attacks on Sudan's power stations are severely impacting children's access to water, education, and healthcare, according to UNICEF Senior Adviser James Elder. In late October, up to 15 power stations were struck, disrupting essential services. Elder described the combined effect of infrastructure attacks and drought as unprecedented for the country.

The destruction of power infrastructure halts water pumps and treatment, forcing families to use unsafe alternatives and increasing disease spread. Cold storage for vaccines is threatened, and farmers lose irrigation access, exacerbating rising food prices. An estimated 825,000 children under five are expected to suffer severe acute malnutrition this year.

Elder stated that armed drones have caused two in three child casualties in Sudan this year, bringing war to previously safe areas. He highlighted the plight of children like Burie, a teenager who lost both hands in an explosion and now learns online and picks grass with assistance. Elder urged the world to notice Sudan's existential threat.

Why it mattersChildren in Sudan face severe malnutrition, lack of water, and disrupted education due to drone attacks on power infrastructure and ongoing drought.

Nine civilians killed in Blue Nile drone strike

Displaced families fleeing fighting in Bani Shako village were targeted on Tuesday.

Nine civilians died and several were injured in a drone strike on Tuesday in the Bani Shako area of Sudan’s Blue Nile region. Kurmuk Locality Commissioner Abdel Ati Mohamed Al-Faki stated that the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) carried out the attack. The victims were reportedly fleeing Bani Shako village towards Damazin, the regional capital.

Local activists and community sources confirmed the drone attack on displaced civilians. Ahmed Khalid, a civil society activist, called the incident a crime and a violation of international humanitarian law, demanding an investigation. Witness Mohamed Hassan described the situation as dire, with civilians fleeing intense drone activity and facing humanitarian hardship.

The strike occurred amid escalating military operations and displacement in the Blue Nile region, particularly in Kurmuk, Bau, and Geissan localities. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) warns that drone strikes continue to kill and injure civilians across Sudan as humanitarian needs grow.

Why it mattersCivilians in Sudan's Blue Nile region are directly affected by drone strikes during ongoing conflict.

Sudan gold exports earn $3.5 billion

Windfall fails to stabilise economy or lower prices for ordinary people.

Sudan's gold exports generated USD 3.5 billion in revenue over 10 months, according to a report by the Ministry of Minerals and the Central Bank of Sudan. This significant income has not improved the country's collapsing economy or reduced market prices for citizens. The report noted a rise in export earnings entering the formal banking system, with expectations that this proportion will exceed 80 per cent soon.

Despite this revenue, which the Sudanese Mineral Resources Company and Central Bank consider an achievement, the Sudanese pound continues to fall against the dollar. Economists point to a large gap between reported export figures and economic stability, noting that revenues could address the crisis if properly directed. Spending on the war that began on April 15, 2023, remains the priority, impacting public services.

Economic expert Dr. Haitham Mohamed Fathi stated that increased gold production and export figures, even with smuggling, offer a lifeline for Sudan's economy. Gold has been Sudan's most important export since South Sudan's secession. Production dropped to 6.5 tonnes in 2023 but rose to 64 tonnes in 2024.

Why it mattersOrdinary Sudanese citizens are not benefiting from the gold export earnings due to the ongoing war and economic instability.

14,000 returnees receive aid in North Darfur

Displaced people return to 24 villages in Dar Al-Salam locality after fleeing attacks.

Approximately 14,000 displaced people have voluntarily returned to 24 villages in Sudan’s North Darfur State, in the Dar Al-Salam locality. They had fled their homes amid attacks and looting by armed groups, seeking refuge in Dar Al-Salam Town. Residents found that authorities had restored water services and were rehabilitating healthcare facilities.

Sheikh Abdel-Naim Mohamed Ahmed of Salougi village stated that residents of 24 villages in the Wadi Dagash area returned after requesting the Government of Peace and Unity, headed by the leader of RSF, Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, to provide security. He noted that they were displaced in 2024 during military operations around El Fasher army headquarters. Sheikh Abubakar Ibrahim Mohamed of Ja’aour village expressed appreciation for food baskets distributed by the Tasis government.

The food assistance included sugar, flour, tea, pasta, milk, and cooking oil, alongside healthcare, education, and drinking water services. Ikram Hassan Mabrouk, a resident of Daw Al-Bait village, confirmed receiving sufficient food supplies after villagers lost belongings, including livestock, to looting in 2025. Mabrouk expressed determination to rebuild their communities despite the devastation.

Why it mattersReturnees are receiving essential food, water, and healthcare services after fleeing violence and looting.
