US proposes Sudan arms embargo expansion; China, Russia oppose

The United States seeks to broaden the existing Darfur arms embargo to cover all of Sudan, including drones, but faces opposition from China and Russia.

A US proposal to expand the arms embargo on Darfur to include all of Sudan and cover drones has met with mixed reactions at a UN Security Council session. Denmark, Britain, and France supported the US initiative, while China, Russia, and Sudan's UN representative opposed it. The US aims to curb the flow of weapons and drones fueling the conflict that began in April 2023.

Massad Boulos, the US president’s senior adviser for Arab and African affairs, told the Security Council that over 12 countries are providing military support to the warring parties, prolonging the conflict and increasing civilian casualties. The US proposals include clarifying that the embargo covers drones, their components, and related technologies, and strengthening the monitoring capacity of the expert panel.

Sudan’s army chief and head of the Sovereignty Council, Abdelfattah El Burhan, downplayed the potential impact of additional sanctions. Russia's representative, Vasily A Nebenzya, stated that his country opposes expanding sanctions, arguing it would harm the Sudanese army and that the Rapid Support Forces are primarily responsible for indiscriminate drone use against civilian infrastructure and populations.

Why it mattersThe proposed arms embargo expansion directly affects the ongoing conflict in Sudan by attempting to limit the supply of weapons and drones to the warring factions, potentially altering the course of the war and impacting the safety of civilians.

UN warns Sudan war intensifies, spreads with external support

The UN's Under-Secretary-General for Political and Peacebuilding Affairs, Rosemary DiCarlo, reported to the Security Council that Sudan's conflict is becoming harder to stop.

Sudan's conflict is intensifying and spreading, according to Rosemary DiCarlo, the UN’s Under-Secretary-General for Political and Peacebuilding Affairs. In her briefing to the Security Council on Monday, August 24th, DiCarlo stated that after more than three years of fighting, the war is becoming more technologically sophisticated and drawing in a complicated array of armed groups and outside interests. The conflict has caused 13m Sudanese to flee their homes, including 8.7m displaced within the country.

The UN documented around 1,400 civilian deaths between January and June this year, with 1,100 of these caused by drone strikes. Drones operated by both the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) have repeatedly hit markets, schools, fuel stations, and water infrastructure. The fighting has become more geographically dispersed, with heavy drone attacks and limited ground combat concentrating in North Kordofan, West Darfur, North Darfur, and Blue Nile.

DiCarlo warned that the sophisticated weaponry used points to ongoing external support for both parties, allowing them to sustain a level of warfare neither could maintain alone. The proliferation of local militias is also fragmenting the battlefield, making a ceasefire and political settlement more difficult. Insecurity near the Ethiopian border in Blue Nile could strain relations and generate further refugee flows.

Why it mattersThe ongoing conflict directly impacts the safety and stability of Sudanese citizens, leading to displacement and loss of life, and making peace harder to achieve.

Port Sudan detainees begin hunger strike over trial delays

Eighty-three detainees at Port Sudan Central Prison launched an indefinite hunger strike today, protesting what they describe as systematic obstruction of their constitutional.

The detainees, facing charges of collaborating with the Rapid Support Forces under articles 50 and 51 of the 1991 Criminal Act, began their strike at 06:00. They claim their cases have been stalled for years due to a single judge handling all 83 defendants, leading to repeated adjournments and lengthy gaps between hearings, according to a memorandum seen by Radio Dabanga.

The charges, which carry penalties of life imprisonment or death, have resulted in some detainees being held for almost two years without even being referred to court. They argue that pre-trial detention has become a prolonged punishment, overturning legal standards, Radio Dabanga reported.

Families of the detainees also report suffering, particularly those living outside Red Sea state who incur significant travel and accommodation costs to visit relatives and follow cases, in addition to lawyers’ fees. One family described struggling to attend hearings due to repeated adjournments, feeling punished alongside their imprisoned relatives.

Why it mattersDetainees and their families are directly affected by the lack of timely judicial process, leading to prolonged imprisonment and financial strain for those seeking justice.

UN Relief Chief Reports Sudan Crisis Suffers from 'Lack of Action'

More than two-thirds of Sudan’s population requires lifesaving assistance, with approximately 1,400 civilians killed in the first half of 2026, primarily due to drone strikes.

Tom Fletcher, the U.N. under-secretary-general for humanitarian affairs, informed the Security Council that about 1,100 civilian deaths were caused by drone strikes. These attacks have targeted homes, markets, and critical infrastructure in areas like El Obeid in North Kordofan, where 15,000 families have sought refuge since mid-July, straining existing services.

Humanitarian operations face significant challenges across Sudan, including front lines, drone attacks, insecurity, and bureaucratic restrictions. In South Kordofan, towns like Dilling and Kadugli are accessible despite deteriorating roads from heavy rains, but West Kordofan, the epicenter of a recent cholera outbreak, has limited aid access. Intensifying violence in the Nuba Mountains has cut off communities from aid.

Renewed fighting in Blue Nile state has displaced thousands in the past two weeks, with the state now hosting over 370,000 displaced people. Despite these obstacles, aid agencies reached nearly 11 million people with at least one form of assistance by mid-year, achieving 52% of their prioritized target, though falling behind in education, health, shelter, and water assistance.

Why it mattersThe ongoing conflict and lack of humanitarian access directly affect Sudanese civilians who are displaced, face violence, and lack essential services like food, water, and healthcare.

News · 08/26/2026

Civil society warns Blue Nile crisis deepens as thousands flee violence

Displacement is worsening in Sudan’s Blue Nile state as thousands flee fighting in Geissan locality, while authorities restrict civil society groups and close emergency response.

More than 15,000 people displaced from Geissan have moved to areas including Bakuri, Tandaru, Wad Al-Mahi, and Al-Roseires localities, according to Ali Hajo, secretary-general of the Blue Nile Civil Society Initiative. The International Organization for Migration reported last week that nearly 14,000 people were displaced from Geissan and Kurmuk localities in August, with 12,470 fleeing Geissan town and 12 villages between August 11 and 18.

Displaced individuals are sheltering in schools and makeshift camps, where heavy rains are exacerbating already difficult living conditions. Hajo stated that security authorities have restricted civil society activities, including shutting emergency-response kitchens and suspending emergency response rooms that provide assistance to those displaced.

Hajo also alleged that military intelligence has arrested or pursued several activists involved in humanitarian and community work. He warned that these restrictions on civilian initiatives and local aid centers, combined with rising displacement and limited humanitarian assistance, could further worsen conditions for civilians affected by the conflict.

Why it mattersCivilians in Blue Nile state are directly affected by increased displacement, restricted access to humanitarian aid, and worsening living conditions due to ongoing conflict and heavy rains.
