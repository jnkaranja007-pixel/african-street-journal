African Union condemns drone strikes killing over 250 in Sudan

The AU calls for an immediate humanitarian ceasefire amid reports of civilian deaths in West Kordofan, South Darfur, Khartoum, and Abyei.

The African Union has condemned drone attacks that killed over 250 people across Sudan and the disputed Abyei area in recent days. AU Commission chairperson Mahmoud Ali Youssouf called for an immediate humanitarian ceasefire and expressed deep concern over the civilian toll, displacement, and destruction of livelihoods. Reports indicate approximately 80 people died in El Dibb, West Kordofan, on October 18, while 23 died in Nyala, South Darfur, on October 16, and three children were killed in Khartoum on October 19.

In the disputed Abyei region, drone strikes on Amiet market on October 19 reportedly killed over 150 people, including Sudanese, South Sudanese, and foreign nationals. Sudan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs denied any involvement by its armed forces in the Abyei attack, while the RSF-aligned Government of Peace has called for an international investigation. The ministry accused the RSF of other attacks, including one on a UN peacekeeping camp in Kadugli in December 2025.

The RSF-aligned Government of Peace has filed a complaint with the Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for Sudan seeking an investigation into the Abyei market attack. Youssouf urged all parties to protect civilians and humanitarian personnel, ensure unhindered humanitarian access, and respect Sudan’s sovereignty. He also reaffirmed support for mediation efforts to secure a ceasefire and launch an inclusive, Sudanese-led political process.

Why it mattersCivilians in Sudan are directly affected by deadly drone strikes that cause deaths, displacement, and disruption of essential services.

Sudan crop failure deepens hunger crisis

Displaced families face soaring prices and malnutrition as food aid is cut and harvests fail in East Darfur.

Displaced families across Sudan are struggling to find food as cuts to World Food Programme (WFP) assistance coincide with a near-total crop failure in East Darfur. Residents of Sheairia report soaring prices, worsening malnutrition, and shortages of medicines and therapeutic food for children.

The WFP's acting executive director, Carl Skau, stated the agency has the capacity to help millions but is running out of money. The General Coordination for Displaced People and Refugees warns that reduced food assistance could force families to skip meals and sell possessions. Spokesperson Adam Rijal said funding shortages could deprive families of essential meals.

Farmers in East Darfur harvested almost nothing of their main crops, including groundnuts, millet, sorghum, and okra, depriving households of food and income. A sack of millet costs about SDG 350,000, and a pound of onions costs around SDG 4,500. Pregnant women cannot obtain adequate diets, and children suffer from malnutrition.

Why it mattersDisplaced families and vulnerable communities in Sudan face worsening hunger, malnutrition, and desperation due to aid cuts and crop failure.

Sudan lifts ban on 46 imports amid rising inflation

The Sovereignty Council chairman cancelled a cabinet decision as the dollar climbed to SDG 10,300 and annual inflation hit 28.29%.

Sudan has lifted import restrictions on 46 goods as the dollar reached SDG 10,300 and annual inflation rose to 28.29%. Sovereignty Council chairman Abdelfattah El Burhan cancelled a cabinet decision that had banned these imports, aiming to ease pressure on consumers facing soaring food prices.

Finance minister Jibril Ibrahim stated the decision considers consumer needs while protecting domestic producers. Traders reported an immediate price fall for some goods. The National Importers’ Chamber welcomed the move but requested a clear decision to cancel Resolution No. 74, which had imposed the ban and disrupted markets.

Foreign-exchange dealers reported the dollar rose to SDG 10,200 for buying and SDG 10,300 for selling on Thursday, an increase of SDG 100. Annual inflation reached 28.29% in September, up from 26.14% in August, with food and drink prices rising 17.26% monthly.

Why it mattersSudanese consumers face continued pressure from rising prices and a depreciating currency.

Politics · 2026-10-23

West Darfur rallies back Tasis government

Leaders and citizens in El Geneina voiced support for the Transitional Peace Government's agenda for change.

Rallies in El Geneina, West Darfur, saw leaders and citizens express strong support for the Tasis administration and its reform agenda. Participants, including many young people and women, said the turnout showed recovery, stability, and popular backing for the government. The Sudan Founding Alliance (Tasis) is a coalition formed by the RSF and other groups.

Tijani Al-Tahir Karshoum, head of West Darfur's civil administration, stated the rallies rejected the old Sudan marked by injustice and resource plunder, signaling recovery and strengthening security. He condemned attacks on civilians in Darfur and Kordofan, urging international protection and an investigative commission. Ibrahim Fadl, head of the Qimam Alliance, called the event a continuation of Sudan's revolutions, rejecting the current government's actions against civilians.

Finance Minister Carlo John said the participation of youth and women showed genuine recovery in West Darfur. He outlined the Tasis Alliance's economic plan, which focuses on reducing living costs, improving essential services, and providing free education. The economic program emphasizes fiscal decentralization, granting regions more financial and resource management powers, a key part of dismantling the centralized system in place since 1956.

Why it mattersResidents of West Darfur are affected by the Tasis government's agenda for change, which includes economic reforms and calls for civilian protection.

News · 2026-10-23

UNICEF condemns killing of 12 children in Sudan

Nine children died in a displaced persons camp in West Kordofan and three in Khartoum.

UNICEF has condemned the reported killing of at least 12 children in separate attacks in Sudan’s West Kordofan and Khartoum states. Nine children aged between five and 10 were killed on Saturday, October 17, in an attack on a camp for displaced families east of Sanakit in West Kordofan. Two days later, an armed drone struck a residential area in Khartoum, reportedly killing three children aged between two and six, along with their father.

Angela Kearney, UNICEF’s acting representative in Sudan, stated that neither displacement camps nor residential neighbourhoods are safe for children. She noted that families fleeing to camps or returning to their homes are not protecting their children. Kearney urged all parties to the conflict to protect civilians and civilian infrastructure under international humanitarian law.

The deaths come amid an escalation in drone attacks across Sudan, where the war between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces has forced millions from their homes. UNICEF warned that children continue to face deadly violence after more than three years of conflict.

Why it mattersChildren in Sudan are being killed in attacks on both displacement camps and residential areas.
