Sudan faces deepening misery as aid funding plummets

Millions need aid, but only 47% of required funds have been raised, leaving many without shelter.

Sudan's humanitarian crisis is worsening as international aid funding has been severely cut, leaving nearly 34 million people in need. War and drone strikes are severely hindering the delivery of essential supplies across the country.

The International Organization for Migration (IOM) and the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) warn of critical funding gaps for emergency shelter and household goods. Without flexible funding, more families will face inadequate protection from extreme weather, violence, and exploitation in the coming months.

The UN's Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan requires $2.9 billion to aid 20.4 million people, but only 47% has been raised. Aid organizations have only been able to assist 10% of the 15.4 million people requiring emergency shelter and basic items, despite the ongoing conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF).

Why it mattersMillions of Sudanese people are at risk of lacking adequate shelter and protection due to a severe shortfall in humanitarian aid funding.

Storm wrecks shelters of Sudanese refugees in Uganda's Kiryandongo camp

Sudanese communities in Kampala are raising money and collecting tarpaulins for families left homeless after a violent storm swept through the settlement.

Kiryandongo Refugee Settlement in Uganda has been hit by a violent storm that tore apart homes and belongings, leaving many Sudanese refugee families with no roof over their heads. Women, children, older people and the sick are now left exposed to heavy rain, strong winds and high humidity.

Mu’tasim Ahmed Mohammed Abu Bakr, a Sudanese refugee leader in Kiryandongo, told Radio Dabanga that most temporary shelters, built from tarpaulins and wooden poles, could not withstand the wind, which pulled off the coverings and collapsed large sections. He said Sudanese refugees lost more than some other nationalities living there. In Kampala, Abdelaziz Fadl Hassan, who heads the Sudanese refugee community in Kawempe, said the drive began because families whose homes had been destroyed urgently needed tarpaulins.

The first target was about 100 families, but donations have already topped 100 tarpaulins, with organisers now expecting around 200 or more. Donors have also sent clothes, shoes and food, along with cash. A first delegation was due to travel to Kiryandongo on Thursday, 12 November, to deliver the goods.

Why it mattersFamilies in Kiryandongo who lost their shelters are relying on tarpaulins and other goods gathered by Sudanese donors in Kampala and beyond.

Sudan's veteran political leader al-Mirghani dies at 90

Mohamed Osman al-Mirghani, leader of the Democratic Unionist Party and head of the Khatmiyya Sufi order, died in Egypt.

Veteran political and religious leader Mohamed Osman al-Mirghani died in Egypt at the age of 90. Al-Mirghani led Sudan’s Democratic Unionist Party (DUP) and headed the influential Khatmiyya Sufi order. Sudan’s Council of Ministers described him as a prominent national and religious figure who significantly contributed to the country’s political life and efforts for unity and consensus.

Al-Mirghani, who drew support from both the DUP and the Khatmiyya Sufi movement, will be buried on Monday at the Sayed Ali Al-Mirghani Mosque in Khartoum North. He inherited leadership of the Khatmiyya order in 1968 and the main DUP faction in 1982. His family has a long history of influence in Sudanese public life.

In November 1988, al-Mirghani met with John Garang, leader of the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement (SPLM), resulting in the Mirghani-Garang Agreement. This peace initiative aimed to end Sudan’s second civil war with a ceasefire and a constitutional conference, but was overtaken by the 1989 military coup.

Why it mattersMohamed Osman al-Mirghani, a key figure in Sudanese politics and a leader of a major Sufi order, has died, affecting his party and followers.
