Sudan journalists warn of rising attacks amid war

Women journalists face escalating violations and impunity, with justice systems weakened by conflict.

Sudan’s Women Journalists Network has warned of escalating violations and crimes against journalists, particularly women, amid the continuing war and widespread availability of weapons. The network issued a report marking the International Day to End Impunity for Crimes against Journalists, calling for an end to the war and accountability for those responsible for crimes against media workers.

The report states that the current war has deepened the crisis of justice and the rule of law, making crimes against journalists evidence of wider failures. Access to justice has become militarised, and law enforcement and judicial institutions struggle to operate independently. Displacement and social pressures also prevent many women journalists from filing complaints and seeking justice.

The Women Journalists Network stresses that accountability and justice must underpin any political change or legal reform in Sudan. They call for institutional safeguards to protect the justice system from interference and for journalists to be involved in peace processes, ensuring respect for freedom of expression and protection for media workers.

Why it mattersWomen journalists in Sudan face increased danger and a lack of justice due to the ongoing conflict and weakened state institutions.

Drones strike Sudan power stations

Fires broke out at electricity facilities in Kassala State after multiple drone attacks on Monday morning.

Drones attacked power stations in New Halfa and Aroma cities in Sudan’s Kassala State early Monday, causing fires. Eyewitnesses reported that three drones struck New Halfa around 2 a.m., firing missiles and causing explosions. One drone successfully hit its target after two were intercepted.

In Aroma, drones targeted the Haya transformer station and the Al-Sahil fuel depot. A civil defense team, including the Kassala State police chief, responded to extinguish the fires. The extent of the damage is still being assessed, and no casualties have been reported.

Kassala State Governor retired Major General Al-Sadiq Al-Azraq visited the attacked sites to assess the damage. At least 16 power stations across Sudan have been targeted in drone attacks within the past week, leading to widespread outages.

Why it mattersResidents in Kassala State experienced power disruptions and fires due to drone attacks on electricity infrastructure.

South Darfur trains Ebola response teams

Rapid response teams in Sudan's South Darfur State received training to detect Ebola at borders with Central African Republic and South Sudan.

South Darfur State in Sudan last week trained rapid response teams to guard against Ebola outbreaks at its borders with the Central African Republic and South Sudan. Teams from Um Dafuq, Al-Sunta, Buram, and Al-Radom localities attended the workshop to strengthen surveillance and early detection. Dr. Abbas Hassan Shams al-Din, director of the Emergency and Epidemic Control Department, stated the trained staff must maintain high preparedness.

The state health ministry organized the training due to Ebola's spread in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda, according to Dr. Mustafa Al-Barmaki, a ministry director. He described Ebola as a viral hemorrhagic fever that spreads through close contact. The ministry prioritizes epidemic prevention through surveillance and early detection, urging trainees to report suspected cases promptly.

Dr. Badr al-Din al-Sharif, the WHO coordinator in Darfur, noted Ebola was first identified in 1976 in what is now South Sudan. He mentioned that while preventive vaccines exist, new variants may emerge. The current strain lacks an established treatment, though one is in trials in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The disease has killed about 45 percent of infected individuals, with healthcare workers at high risk.

Why it mattersSouth Darfur State is taking precautions against the spread of Ebola from neighboring countries.

Sudan Rapid Support Forces strike three power stations

Attacks in Kassala and Northern states cut electricity to entire regions and damage vital infrastructure.

Rapid Support Forces drones attacked three electricity distribution stations in Sudan's Kassala and Northern states on Monday, destroying two in Kassala and cutting power to the entire state. The attacks also hit the Merowe station in the Northern state. This brings the total number of power stations targeted to over 17 in the past week.

Local sources reported that the Aroma and Halfa Al Jadida stations in Kassala were destroyed by drones early Monday, causing a complete power outage for the state. The Merowe station, near the Merowe Dam, was also targeted. These attacks come amid escalating strikes on electricity infrastructure across various states, including Gadarif, Khartoum, and Gezira.

The repeated attacks have led to widespread power outages, disrupting essential services like water pumping and hospitals. The Aroma station is a strategic hub for eastern Sudan, supplying power to Aroma city and surrounding villages. The government has lodged a complaint with the UN Security Council and the African Union Peace and Security Council, accusing Ethiopia of involvement in attacks on civilian sites and power stations.

Why it mattersSudanese citizens are experiencing widespread power outages that disrupt essential services like water and healthcare.

SPLM team arrives in Eastern Equatoria to reorganize party

A high-level committee will engage members and organize structures from bomas to payams and counties.

A high-level committee from the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement (SPLM) Party arrived in Eastern Equatoria State on Friday to reorganize the party’s structures from the grassroots.

Mary Paul Unde, secretary of the organizing committee, stated the mission aims to strengthen internal organization before primary elections, which will nominate governors and MPs. Akile Mboya Peter, the SPLM state secretary for Eastern Equatoria, welcomed the committee’s arrival as an important step for the party’s congress programme.

The committee's stay duration depends on the congress process progress across the state, covering boma, payam, and county levels. This exercise marks the beginning of the SPLM's internal reorganization in Eastern Equatoria as it prepares for future political activities.

Why it mattersThe SPLM party is reorganizing its internal structures and preparing for primary elections to nominate political leaders.
