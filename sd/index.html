Commodity prices surge in Damazin as military conflict spreads

Essential goods become scarce and household incomes dwindle as fighting expands to Bau locality.

Residents of Damazin, the capital of Sudan's Blue Nile Region, face soaring commodity prices and severe hardship as military operations spread to Bau locality. The Jebel Al-Tin and Mayful areas came under attack on 13 November by forces affiliated with the Tasis Alliance, expanding hostilities that already encompass Kurmuk, Geissan and Wad Al-Mahi localities.

Rami Hassan, a local resident, stated that livelihoods have been disrupted and sources of income have virtually disappeared. Market surveys by Radio Tamazuj established that a kilogram of beef increased from SDG 32,000 to 48,000, and a liter of milk rose from SDG 2,500 to 4,000.

A liter of cooking oil climbed from SDG 10,000 to 12,000, while a malwa of onions rose from SDG 16,000 to 20,000. A sack of sugar increased from SDG 415,000 to 450,000, and a 50-kilogramme sack of flour went from 367,000 to SDG 397,000.

Why it mattersResidents in Damazin face severe price hikes, with a sack of sugar rising to SDG 450,000 and beef reaching SDG 48,000 per kilogram amid spreading military confrontations.

Sudanese band Aswat al-Madina reunites in Cairo after war scatters members

The band that soundtracked Sudan's 2018 uprising is recording and performing again in the Egyptian capital.

The Sudanese band Aswat al-Madina, whose name means Voices of the City, has reunited in Cairo to resume recording and live performances after war scattered its members across several continents. Mohamed al-Mustafa, the band's guitarist and co-founder who goes by Timon, told AFP that being back together still feels unreal.

The band was founded in Khartoum in 2014 and became the soundtrack to the 2018 uprising against longtime ruler Omar al-Bashir, performing for thousands of protesters demanding democracy. Two days before fighting broke out between the army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, the musicians were debating setlists for their next performance.

The war has killed tens of thousands of people and displaced millions more across Sudan. The reunion has taken place in a Cairo studio, where the musicians revisit old songs and stories from Khartoum while the fighting continues back home.

Why it mattersThe reunion gives the band's members a place to work together again, after years of displacement across several continents.
