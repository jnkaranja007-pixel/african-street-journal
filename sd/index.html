Sudan offers Russia Red Sea investment, mining

Sudan's military leader proposed a free economic zone and opportunities in gold, copper, and other minerals to Russian investors.

Sudan's military leader Abdel Fattah al-Burhan proposed establishing a free economic zone on the Red Sea coast and offered Russian investors opportunities in gold, copper, and other mineral resources during talks with President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Friday.

Burhan also proposed Russian participation in developing Sudan’s railway infrastructure, including a planned extension from Port Sudan towards Chad, and said the economic zone could host Russian fuel and wheat storage facilities.

Putin described Sudan as an important Russian partner in Africa, stating that bilateral trade had more than doubled in 2025 and increased by 25% between January and August 2026, making Sudan one of Russia’s 10 largest trading partners on the continent.

Why it mattersSudanese citizens and the nation's economy are directly affected by the proposed economic zone and investment in mineral resources.

RSF drones hit Sudan electricity network

Rapid Support Forces drone attacks have knocked out power stations from eastern Sudan to Khartoum.

Sudan’s Rapid Support Forces (RSF) launched drone attacks on electricity stations across several states since Monday, leaving army-controlled areas without power. The strikes damaged transformers, transmission equipment, and fuel installations from eastern Sudan to Khartoum. The bombardment began with attacks on five electricity stations in Al-Jazirah, Al-Qadarif, Sennar, and White Nile states, followed by strikes on the Al Markhiyat substation northwest of Omdurman.

The Sudan Electricity Holding Company stated that the RSF carried out "treacherous terrorist attacks" using drones, targeting vital substations including Singa, Al-Hawata, Al-Faw, Mashkur, and Al-Geneid in Al-Jazirah State. These attacks caused extensive damage and widespread destruction, resulting in a complete blackout across the southeastern electricity grid and power outages in Sennar, Al-Qadarif, White Nile, and Al-Jazirah states.

The attacks, which reached installations hundreds of kilometers from RSF ground positions, pose a direct threat to the national electricity grid's stability. Drones also struck the Al Junaid transformer station near a sugar factory in eastern Al Jazirah State on October 26, disabling the station and interrupting electricity supplies to the surrounding area.

Why it mattersThe RSF drone attacks have disrupted electricity across multiple states in Sudan, affecting power supply to cities and agricultural communities.

Yale report finds El Fasher empty after RSF takeover

Satellite imagery shows vegetation growth in El Fasher, indicating a near-total absence of civilian life.

A Yale University report reveals El Fasher is largely deserted one year after the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) captured the North Darfur capital in October 2025. Satellite imagery analysed by Yale’s Humanitarian Research Lab shows civilian activity confined to about five square kilometres, with widespread damage to markets and hospitals. The report documents evidence consistent with the destruction or concealment of human remains.

Researchers used satellite data to measure environmental changes between January and March 2026, finding a 9.02% increase in vegetation growth across the city. This increase is interpreted as evidence of the cessation of daily activity and the disappearance of social and civilian life. Eight of nine major markets showed no human or animal presence, with many structures damaged or destroyed.

The First Degree neighbourhood remains the main area where RSF activity and the city’s residual population are concentrated. This neighbourhood’s central market was the only one showing visible expansion and activity. The report tracked 19 healthcare facilities across El Fasher, with imagery raising concerns over human remains.

Why it mattersResidents of El Fasher and surrounding displacement camps are affected by the devastation, displacement, and potential destruction of human remains following the RSF takeover.

UAE court jails eight for smuggling arms to Sudan

Eight individuals received life sentences for using companies and forged documents to move military equipment to Port Sudan.

An appeals court in Abu Dhabi sentenced eight people to life in prison for smuggling weapons and ammunition to the Sudanese army. The court also gave three-year sentences to others, acquitted two, and fined six companies involved in the operation. Authorities ordered the confiscation of funds and equipment, including ammunition and communication devices, used in the illegal deals.

The case involved an attempt to transport military supplies, including Kalashnikov rifles and grenades, valued at $13 million, to Port Sudan. Investigators found that $3 million of this amount was allocated for illicit commissions. A second transaction used over $2 million from the first deal to buy more ammunition, with part of the shipment entering the UAE disguised as humanitarian aid.

Emirati authorities uncovered plans to smuggle an additional five million rounds of machine-gun ammunition through six more operations. The prosecution presented financial records, bank transfers, correspondence, and confessions to prove a network used companies and commercial fronts to hide the nature of the deals and fund movements. The convicted individuals will be deported after serving their sentences.

Why it mattersEight people and six companies were convicted for illegally trading military equipment and smuggling arms to the Sudanese army, impacting the ongoing conflict.
