Sudan fuel crisis paralyses daily life

Drone attacks on infrastructure and rising global prices worsen fuel shortages and increase costs for residents.

Daily life across Sudan is paralysed by a deepening fuel crisis, with long vehicle queues at the few operating petrol stations becoming a daily occurrence. Domestic refining has been crippled by drone attacks targeting infrastructure and energy sources amidst the ongoing civil war.

Osman Faisal, a Khartoum resident, reported queuing for petrol since seven in the morning and waiting for over two hours, unable to fill up for a week. The price of gasoline has recently increased to about $58 per gallon, a rise of over $8 in several days. The Sudanese pound's depreciation against the US dollar further increases the cost of imported shipments.

Adnan Osman, another Khartoum resident, expressed hope for the stabilisation of the exchange rate soon. The conflict in the Middle East since late February has also contributed to significant fluctuations in international energy market prices.

Why it mattersResidents are facing severe fuel shortages and increased prices, with gasoline costing $58 per gallon, impacting daily commutes and essential services.

Sudan report documents alleged atrocities in Al-Jazira

A new report details killings, displacement, and property destruction in Kambo Taiba, warning of potential war crimes.

A human rights report launched on Thursday in Kampala, Uganda, documented alleged atrocities against civilians in Sudan’s Al-Jazira State, specifically the Kambo Taiba area. The report, titled “Identity, Colour and Violence in Sudan,” details alleged killings, summary executions, home burnings, looting, and forced displacement during attacks in January 2025. The Darfur Victims Advocacy Organization collaborated with the Kanabi Conference and the Centre for Truth and Knowledge to compile the findings.

Adam Musa Ishaq, director of the Darfur Victims Advocacy Organization, stated that the attacks, which occurred between January 9 and 14, 2025, claimed the lives of 43 civilians, including many children and elderly people. He accused the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and allied groups, such as the Sudan Shield Forces and the Al-Bara bin Malik Brigades, of responsibility. Approximately 5,000 families were forcibly displaced, with attackers using around 25 military vehicles, heavy weapons, artillery, and drones.

The report documents three waves of attacks involving artillery, arson, armed incursions, and summary executions, leading to the destruction of about 5,000 head of livestock. Under the report’s warning, the targeting of communities by ethnic identity and skin colour may qualify as war crimes or crimes against humanity, and it raises concern about possible ethnic cleansing. Musa called for formal recognition of Kanabi communities’ rights and guaranteed access to essential public services.

Why it mattersAttacks in Al-Jazira State have resulted in 43 civilian deaths and the forced displacement of 5,000 families, with concerns raised about war crimes.

Business · 2026-11-16

Sudan's Tasis government plans to route western trade through Togo's Port of Lomé

The administration, formed by the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and allied factions, says the port will serve western Sudan in place of Port Sudan, which it cannot use.

The Sudan Founding Alliance, also known as the Tasis government, has agreed to use the Port of Lomé in Togo for imports and exports serving western Sudan, as an alternative to Port Sudan. Trade Minister Mohamed Ismail Arkan presented the outcome of the delegation's visit to Togo to Prime Minister Mohamed Hassan Al-Taishi in Nyala on Thursday.

Dr Yagoub Nurein, a Foreign Ministry representative in the delegation, told Radio Tamazuj that the visit covered trade, political and diplomatic cooperation, and that the delegation met Togolese officials and banking executives. He said the delegation also reached an understanding with Togolese banks to open branches in areas under Tasis control.

Traders and producers in western Sudan have suffered heavy financial losses since a 2024 decision barred goods transport to Port Sudan, which remains under the control of the Sudanese Armed Forces. The delegation also asked the Togolese president to launch a diplomatic initiative to end the war in Sudan.

Why it mattersWestern Sudanese traders and producers, who have lost money since the 2024 ban on trucking goods to Port Sudan, would gain a new route to export markets through Lomé.

Education · 2026-11-16

Sudan school reopens after three-year war closure

Community efforts and local donations revive Sango Mixed Secondary School in South Darfur, now serving more than 115 students.

Sango Mixed Secondary School in South Darfur's Radom Locality has resumed classes, three years after it closed because of the war between the Sudan Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces that broke out in April 2023. The school had suffered extensive damage to its buildings and a shortage of teaching materials during the closure.

Headteacher Mustafa Al-Toum said community-led initiatives and contributions from local philanthropists and residents made the reopening possible. The school now has 12 teachers and more than 115 students. Al-Toum urgently appealed to government authorities, humanitarian organisations and people from Radom locality for help to rehabilitate the buildings and supply teaching materials.

Fatima Babiker, a third-year student, said students in their final year of secondary school are determined to keep learning despite the destruction the war has caused. Sango is located in Radom locality, which borders the Central African Republic.

Why it mattersMore than 115 students in Radom locality can resume secondary schooling that the war interrupted for three years, but the school still needs repairs and teaching materials.
