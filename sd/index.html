UN and France condemn escalating drone attacks in Sudan

Drone strikes on civilian infrastructure are disrupting essential services and killing civilians, including children.

The United Nations and France have condemned escalating drone attacks across Sudan, warning of their deadly impact on civilians and damage to critical infrastructure. The French embassy called the recent air strikes entirely unacceptable, wherever they occurred, including Darfur, Khartoum, Abyei, the northern Nile Valley, Kordofan, and Blue Nile. These attacks have killed hundreds of civilians, including many children, within a few days.

The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) reported that a drone struck a major electricity substation serving Khartoum state on Tuesday, October 27. This followed strikes on power stations in El Gezira, Sennar, and El Gedaref states on Monday, October 26. OCHA cited local authorities reporting extensive damage that threatened the stability of the national electricity grid.

The UN’s Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for Sudan warned that expanding drone attacks and a crackdown on civil liberties in El Obeid put civilians at increasing risk. The mission said drone strikes attributed to the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) had killed and injured civilians, damaged schools and displacement sites, and disrupted access to healthcare, electricity, and water in the North Kordofan state capital.

Why it mattersCivilians across Sudan, including children, are being killed and injured by drone attacks that also damage essential infrastructure like electricity and water facilities.

Sudanese army drone strike kills 36 in Nyala market

Médecins Sans Frontières reported 150 wounded and bodies of 21 civilians among the dead from the October 26 attack.

A Sudanese Armed Forces drone strike hit a busy market in Nyala, South Darfur State, on Monday, October 26, at around 8 p.m. local time, causing devastation. A second strike hit a civilian neighborhood. Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) stated that Nyala Teaching Hospital received 36 bodies, with 21 identified as civilians.

MSF project coordinator Dale Koninckx described patients arriving at the hospital with shrapnel injuries and severe burns, some carried from the market on tuk-tuks and donkey carts. Medical staff treated over 150 wounded patients, including women and children, who were shaken by the event. Koninckx questioned why markets, essential for accessing food, were being targeted.

This attack follows a similar incident on October 16 in Nyala, which killed at least four people. MSF noted that these attacks expose civilians to violence and limit access to food and supplies amid growing malnutrition concerns. The organization also called for stronger security measures for its work in Central Darfur, requesting authorities remove commercial sites from around Zalingei Teaching Hospital.

Why it mattersCivilians in Nyala are directly affected by drone strikes on markets, leading to deaths, injuries, and limited access to food.

Sudan war displaces 27,400 families in Blue Nile

Intense fighting between the Sudanese army, RSF, and SPLA forces has driven thousands to seek shelter in overcrowded camps.

Fresh fighting between the Sudanese army, Rapid Support Forces (RSF), and the Sudan People’s Liberation Army (SPLA) has driven nearly 27,400 families from their homes in Sudan's Blue Nile state. Displaced people are fleeing into and across the state, adding to the strain on existing shelters. Many are escaping conflict in El Kurmuk, Geissan, and Wad El Mahi localities, while others are returning from South Sudan.

Humanitarian Aid Commissioner Qisma Abdel Karim Deldoum reported that Damazin locality alone hosts 12 camps, with El Karama (5) sheltering 4,814 families and the Agricultural Research Centre accommodating 3,913. The International Organization for Migration (IOM) stated in September that nearly 100,000 people had been displaced in Blue Nile between January and August, with children making up 47% of this number.

The number of internally displaced people in Blue Nile has risen by 21% since the August update, according to the IOM. Camps like Al-Karama 3 have not received food aid for four months, according to its manager.

Why it mattersIntense fighting in Blue Nile state has caused widespread displacement, putting pressure on shelters and leading to a severe deterioration of humanitarian conditions for nearly 27,400 families.

Drone strike cuts Khartoum power

Al-Markhiyat transformer station damaged, affecting electricity in capital and other states.

A drone strike on the Al-Markhiyat transformer station in Omdurman on Tuesday, October 27, has disrupted electricity supplies across large parts of Khartoum and River Nile State. The Ministry of Energy and Petroleum confirmed the facility sustained extensive damage, taking it out of service. Technical teams are assessing the site to determine repair needs.

The power outages worsen living conditions for residents already facing shortages of basic services. Khartoum resident Abdel-Gadir Al-Sheikh described the situation as extremely difficult, with prolonged electricity and water shortages, fuel scarcity, and bakery closures. The Sudanese Electricity Company accused the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) of targeting power stations in multiple states.

This strike followed similar drone attacks on electricity facilities in Al-Jazirah, Sennar, Al-Qadarif, and White Nile states on Monday, October 26. The United Nations has expressed concern over continued drone attacks and damage to civilian infrastructure. Sudan has been at war since April 2023 between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the RSF.

Why it mattersResidents of Khartoum and River Nile State face worsening living conditions due to prolonged electricity outages affecting essential services.

China deploys anti-drone systems in Abyei after attack

Chinese peacekeepers reinforced UNISFA after a drone attack killed UN personnel in the disputed region.

Chinese peacekeepers deployed anti-drone detection and jamming equipment in the disputed Abyei region on October 28, following a drone attack on a market on October 19 that killed members of a United Nations peacekeeping force. China’s Ministry of National Defence stated the specialised equipment aims to detect and jam drones and strengthen security to prevent further attacks. A Chinese peacekeeping rapid-response force returned to its camp after completing a security reinforcement mission.

The United Nations Interim Security Force for Abyei (UNISFA) requested the Chinese peacekeepers’ assistance to reinforce a Pakistani infantry battalion after the October 19 attack. Upon arrival, the Chinese force deployed anti-drone systems and strengthened defensive measures against potential aerial attacks. The security operation included using surveillance drones to monitor the market and secure the surrounding area.

UNISFA’s leadership praised the Chinese peacekeepers for their swift response and professionalism, stating their intervention helped protect lives and property and strengthen stability in the region. The reinforcement mission concluded after the security situation stabilised, allowing the Chinese force to return to its camp.

Why it mattersCivilians and UN peacekeepers in the disputed Abyei region are affected by increased security measures following a deadly drone attack.
