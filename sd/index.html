Displaced in Nyala Face Critical Shortages Amid Rainy Season

Hundreds of families in South Darfur shelters lack food, water, medicine, and adequate shelter as rains worsen conditions.

Emergency rooms and health centers in Nyala, South Darfur, report a critical decline in humanitarian conditions across displacement shelters. Hundreds of families face inadequate basic needs, including severe shortages of food, water, medicines, shelter materials, and hygiene supplies. Nibras Abdullah, a member of Nyala’s emergency room, stated that conditions have become extremely dire, especially with the onset of the rainy season, forcing some families to live in the open or in damaged classrooms where rainwater leaks through.

Specific shelters like El Jir, housing approximately 214 families, are in urgent need of food, water, and shelter materials, along with repairs to toilets and secure fencing. El Hamaira shelter, with about 274 families, requires immediate supplies of food, water, hygiene items, and medicines, particularly for chronic conditions. Other shelters, including El Zahra (115 families) and Mahira School (457 families), along with numerous others across the city, face similar widespread shortages.

While some organizations provide assistance, Abdullah described the scale of aid as insufficient compared to the growing needs. Rising prices exacerbate the difficulty in meeting demands, as the support does not match the increasing number of displaced families. Abdullah called for urgent intervention from humanitarian organizations and authorities to provide essential supplies and improve temporary accommodations to prevent further deterioration during the ongoing rainy season.

Why it mattersHundreds of displaced families in Nyala shelters are experiencing critical shortages of food, water, medicine, and shelter materials, with conditions worsening due to the rainy season.

Sudan lifts two-year curfew and state of emergency in Khartoum

Authorities announced the easing of restrictions to allow residents to move and work around the clock, but security remains tight with ongoing patrols and checkpoints.

Sudan has lifted a two-year night-time curfew and state of emergency in the capital, Khartoum, a move authorities say will permit residents to move and work without time restrictions. The decision was announced by the Security Affairs Coordination Committee following a meeting chaired by Khartoum Governor Ahmed Othman Hamza. Despite the easing of restrictions, security remains tight with army patrols and checkpoints continuing to operate across the capital.

Residents travelling late at night are still urged to carry identification documents. Authorities have also imposed new restrictions on foreign nationals entering Khartoum State, prohibiting entry by public or private transport, including trains and buses, unless travellers possess valid visas or permits. The curfew was initially introduced in May 2024 as fighting intensified between Sudan’s army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces.

Sudan’s war has been ongoing since April 2023, resulting in tens of thousands of deaths and displacing approximately 13 million people, according to UN and international estimates. Military authorities retook control of Khartoum 18 months ago, though conflict persists in other areas.

Why it mattersThe lifting of the curfew and state of emergency in Khartoum allows residents to move and work around the clock, potentially impacting daily life and economic activity.

Fighting Disrupts Trade, Raises Famine Fears in Sudan's Kordofan

The Kordofan Observatory warns of imminent famine as fighting closes roads, halts aid, and drives up food prices beyond reach.

The Kordofan Observatory reports signs of impending famine in multiple areas, including El Nahud, Wad Banda, El Khuwei, and Muglad. Residents are resorting to ambaz, a groundnut residue normally fed to animals, as living conditions worsen, food prices skyrocket, and humanitarian aid diminishes. Testimonies indicate a severe drop in income sources, with a malwa of millet now costing 35,000 Sudanese pounds, making it unaffordable for many families.

This crisis is exacerbated by recent fighting around El Obeid and military build-ups, which have disrupted trade routes and prevented essential goods from reaching Kordofan. Farmers struggle to access and cultivate their land, compounded by a late rainy season. The observatory attributes the escalating food insecurity to continued conflict, closed trade routes, and restricted humanitarian access, warning of a potential widespread catastrophe.

The Kordofan Observatory urges warring factions to establish secure trade and humanitarian corridors and to refrain from using aid as leverage. It also calls on Khartoum and Northern State authorities to reverse transport restrictions to areas controlled by the Rapid Support Forces, which disproportionately burden civilians. The observatory further requests the Rapid Support Forces to facilitate trade and remove checkpoints contributing to inflated prices.

Why it mattersFamilies in Kordofan are facing famine due to fighting that has disrupted trade, cut off aid, and made basic food items unaffordable.

Kassala Gold Traders Strike Over Unregulated Street Vendors

Gold merchants in Kassala have closed their shops indefinitely, demanding authorities address fraud and market distortions caused by itinerant sellers.

Kassala's gold traders began an indefinite strike on Monday, shutting down their established shops to protest the growing problem of unregulated itinerant gold sellers. Abboud Ahmed Issa, head of the goldsmiths’ association, stated the strike will continue until authorities intervene to resolve issues like fraud, theft, and market distortions. He explained that established traders are held accountable for customer complaints, but unregistered vendors disappear, leaving the association to manage the fallout.

The issue, which has worsened since the Covid-19 pandemic, involves itinerant sellers engaging in practices such as manipulating gold weight, offering inflated prices, and trading without official invoices. This creates an unregulated parallel market that bypasses taxes and fees borne by legitimate shop owners. Traders report that recent confrontations between established merchants and itinerant sellers have led to injuries, prompting the current widespread closure.

Issa expressed frustration that the phenomenon damages the reputation of Kassala's gold market and creates tension. The association is calling on police, prosecutors, and security agencies to fulfill their regulatory role. They are prepared to cooperate with any official measures to protect both customers and legitimate traders, emphasizing that the strike will persist until a clear solution is implemented to ensure fair market practices.

Why it mattersGold traders in Kassala are striking because unregulated itinerant sellers are engaging in fraudulent practices that harm customers and distort the market, impacting their businesses.

South Sudan Summons Envoys Over Election Statement

Foreign Minister James Pitia Morgan summoned the ambassadors of Germany, Japan, Norway, and the United States to protest a joint statement urging political dialogue ahead.

South Sudan has summoned the ambassadors of Germany, Japan, Norway, and the United States following a joint statement that urged the country's leaders to revive political dialogue and strengthen accountability before December elections. Foreign Minister James Pitia Morgan expressed the government's concern, describing the statement as inappropriate and divisive during the election preparation period. The ministry stated that Morgan stressed the importance of national ownership and non-interference in internal affairs, suggesting sensitive political matters should be handled through diplomatic channels.

The summoned diplomats had issued their joint statement after meeting with Vice President Rebecca Nyandeng de Mabior in Juba. In their statement, they called for "urgent action" and "authentic inter-party dialogue," urging leaders to renounce violence and ensure public revenues are used for services and accountability. They suggested these steps would foster private investment, security, and the rule of law, contributing to a future of peace and prosperity.

This diplomatic summons follows recent public remarks by Vice President Nyandeng, who questioned the country's direction and the effectiveness of current leadership in improving citizens' lives. She also noted infrequent meetings with President Salva Kiir, highlighting internal political discussions that preceded the international envoys' intervention.

Why it mattersThe government summoned four ambassadors, viewing their statement on political dialogue and accountability as an inappropriate interference in South Sudan's internal affairs ahead of elections.
