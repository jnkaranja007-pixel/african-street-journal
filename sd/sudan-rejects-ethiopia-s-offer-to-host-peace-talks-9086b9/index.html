Sudan rejects Ethiopia's offer to host peace talks

Khartoum says Ethiopia lacks neutrality, accusing it of backing the Rapid Support Forces and allowing attacks on Sudan from its territory.

Sources: Radio Dabanga and Anadolu Africa · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Sudan's government has turned down an offer by Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed to host and facilitate talks between Sudan's military, political and civilian forces, saying Ethiopia cannot act as a neutral host. Abiy announced the proposal on Tuesday, aiming to end the war and reach a national consensus.

In a statement seen by Radio Dabanga, Khartoum accused Ethiopia of supporting the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, particularly on the Blue Nile front. It also charged that drones have been launched from Ethiopian territory to attack Sudan, a claim Ethiopia rejects. The government wants an independent inquiry and has asked the African Union and partners to exclude Ethiopia from facilitation.

The government said Sudanese parties should agree by consensus on the venue, agenda and facilitators. The Sudan Founding Alliance and the Civil Democratic Alliance of Revolutionary Forces, known as Sumoud, welcomed Abiy's proposal.

Why it mattersSudan's government wants an independent investigation and the exclusion of Ethiopia from any facilitation of talks, leaving Sudanese parties to set the venue, agenda and facilitators.
