UN condemns Blue Nile drone strikes that killed 17 civilians

The UN says the attacks came amid escalating fighting and displacement, with more than 6,700 families recently displaced at two sites in El Roseires.

Sources: Radio Dabanga and Anadolu Africa · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

The United Nations condemned drone strikes in Sudan’s Blue Nile state that reportedly killed 17 civilians in Ed Damazin and injured 20. Local sources said more than 40 suicide drones struck several locations on Tuesday, including the state capital and El Roseires. The Blue Nile Civil Society Initiative revised its initial toll of 15 dead and 15 injured.

The initiative blamed the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces and the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement-North, and called for an investigation. It said strikes hit homes in El Saliha and El Roseires, as well as the Okasha Club public viewing hall. UN Secretary-General spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric said the UN condemned the loss of life and called for safe, sustained humanitarian access.

A UN assessment found more than 6,700 recently displaced families at two sites in El Roseires, in need of food, clean water, healthcare and emergency shelter. Aid groups are distributing shelter materials and food and water; the UN allocated nearly $36 million this year for humanitarian programmes in Blue Nile.
