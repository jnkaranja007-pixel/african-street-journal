Fighting worsens oil pollution as RSF prepares to inspect three Sudan fields

Civil society groups report damaged wells and pipelines near Heglig and say insecurity has blocked tests linking pollution to illness.

Source: Ayin Network · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Fighting between Sudan’s armed forces and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has intensified in West Kordofan, worsening longstanding concerns about oil pollution, local civil society groups told Ayin Network. The groups report damage to oil wells and pipelines, with residential areas near the Heglig oil field among those at particular risk.

A civil society source documenting the problem said weak safeguards that existed before the war began in April 2023 have largely disappeared. The source reported more cases of foetal deformities and infertility, as well as some cancers, but said insecurity had prevented definitive tests to establish direct links to pollution. Residents have tried to seal damaged wells and repair pipelines, sometimes getting injured.

The RSF controls an estimated 85% to 90% of the state and announced plans to revive extraction at three sites. Local news reports said a technical delegation had been sent to inspect the Neem, Defra and Kanar oilfields.

Why it mattersResidents near Heglig face reported pollution and health problems, while insecurity has prevented local groups from testing whether the two are directly linked.
