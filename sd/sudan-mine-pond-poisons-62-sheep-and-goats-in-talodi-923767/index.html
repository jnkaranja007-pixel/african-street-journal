Sudan mine pond poisons 62 sheep and goats in Talodi

The Human Rights and Development Organisation says the livestock died after drinking from a gold-processing pond at the Mridan mine in South Kordofan.

Source: Radio Dabanga · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

62 sheep and goats died after drinking from a gold-processing pond at the Mridan gold mine in Talodi, South Kordofan, on 7 November, according to the Human Rights and Development Organisation, known as the HUDO Centre. The animals belonged to Ismail Hussein Hamed, who filed a complaint with Talodi police and the Sudanese Mineral Resources Company.

The HUDO Centre said both authorities had been notified but had taken no decisive action. According to the mine committee, the company suspended the pond operator in September 2026 and fined him SDG 150 million, equivalent to USD 15,000, for breaching mining regulations. The operator then handed the site to the company, which left the pond open without treating its water.

The HUDO Centre has called on the company to rehabilitate the mine site and remove the contaminated ponds. Activists in the Talodi area have warned that toxic substances could reach reservoirs that supply the town.

Why it mattersHamed's herd is lost, and the mining company is now under pressure to close the open ponds at the Mridan mine.
