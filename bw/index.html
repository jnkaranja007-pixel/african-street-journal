Botswana banks remain resilient, council finds

The Financial Stability Council reported on November 11 that Botswana's financial system is sound and supports economic activity.

Botswana's financial system is resilient and supports economic activity, according to the Financial Stability Council (FSC). The council delivered its verdict in Gaborone on November 11, finding that the nation's banks and non-bank financial institutions are safe and sound.

The assessment is based on robust market infrastructures and effective supervisory machinery, regulators stated. The FSC concluded that the financial system is a sturdy vessel holding steady in increasingly choppy waters.

The council's findings indicate a stable financial environment, though the report cautioned that this calm is not self-sustaining. Further details on the sustainability of this financial resilience were not immediately available.

Botswana policy shift on Taiwan and Ukraine draws fire

The government's foreign policy, pledged to human rights, has been questioned over its stances on China's reunification efforts and Russia's war in Ukraine.

Botswana's foreign policy has faced scrutiny following its recent declarations on Taiwan and its abstentions on Ukraine resolutions. In Beijing on October 13, Botswana stated its opposition to Taiwanese independence and support for China's reunification efforts. Less than two weeks later, Foreign Minister Phenyo Butale confirmed that Batswana citizens had been recruited to fight for Russia in Ukraine, opting for dialogue over a formal protest to Moscow.

President Duma Boko and Minister Butale have described their foreign policy as centred on human rights and economic diplomacy, acknowledging Botswana's need for markets, jobs, and investment. However, critics argue that the government's commitment to principles is tested when defending them might complicate trade or investment discussions. The October communiqué with Beijing explicitly endorsed China's reunification efforts without conditions for peaceful resolution or Taiwanese consent, a move rejected by Taipei.

The government's voting record at the UN on Ukraine has also drawn criticism. While Botswana co-sponsored a resolution deploring Russia's aggression in 2022, it has since abstained on multiple resolutions concerning peace, territorial sovereignty, and the return of Ukrainian children. Opposition MP Duma Saleshando questioned these abstentions in Parliament, particularly regarding the children's resolution, while Minister Butale spoke of opening an embassy in Moscow and inviting Russian investment.

Why it mattersThe government's foreign policy decisions on Taiwan and Ukraine raise questions about its commitment to human rights when economic interests are involved.

Botswana youth demand 30 percent parliamentary quota

A democracy forum report released in November highlights youth calls for greater political influence following the 2024 general elections.

Young Batswana are demanding a 30 percent quota in Parliament for citizens under 35, according to a Democracy International report. This push aims to give the younger generation a greater stake in national decision-making.

The report, titled ‘Beyond Elections: What We Learned Together in Botswana,’ emerged from the 13th Global Forum on Modern Direct Democracy held in Gaborone from October 7–10. It suggests that political change alone, such as the 2024 election that saw the Umbrella for Democratic Change (UDC) take power, may not be enough to address youth representation.

Discussions at the forum also explored alternative methods for youth influence, including children's participation and the contribution of Kgotla ya Basha. The proposal for a 30 percent parliamentary seat reservation for those under 35 was a specific suggestion put forward during the international gathering.

Why it mattersYoung people in Botswana are seeking a 30 percent quota in Parliament, a proposal discussed at a recent international forum in Gaborone.

Aterian pinpoints four copper-silver targets in Botswana's Kalahari belt

Drone surveys and AI-assisted geophysics flagged favourable geological structures across a 20-kilometre licence corridor, though mineralisation is not yet confirmed.

London-listed exploration company Aterian has identified four priority copper-silver targets within a 20-kilometre corridor at prospecting licence PL199/2025 in Botswana's Kalahari Copper Belt. The targets came out of drone-based magnetic surveys and three-dimensional geophysical interpretation, which revealed structures the company considers favourable for mineralisation.

Geological modelling points to fold structures between 10 and 200 metres deep, in rocks believed to belong to the D'Kar Formation, which hosts several established copper and silver deposits elsewhere in the belt. Charles Bray, Aterian's executive chairperson, called PL199 an emerging copper-silver opportunity, with the company's work with Lithosquare, an artificial intelligence partner, paying for the programme in full.

Aterian plans induced polarisation and resistivity surveys to sharpen the structures and rank the strongest targets before drilling. Some targets are interpreted as relatively shallow. The company has stressed that mineralisation has not yet been confirmed, and identifying a target is not the same as finding an economically viable deposit.

Why it mattersShareholders of Aterian would gain substantial value only if a commercially significant copper or silver discovery is made at PL199, which the company says is not yet confirmed.
