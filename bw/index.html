Botswana graduates face job scarcity due to slow business growth

The nation's economy struggles to absorb educated youth as businesses lack expansion and innovation.

Botswana's recent graduation ceremonies celebrate many new university and college graduates entering the job market. However, a significant concern exists regarding the economy's capacity to create enough jobs for these skilled young people. The primary issue is not solely with graduates' preparedness but with the insufficient growth of businesses to absorb them.

While some argue for curriculum changes to better match industry demands, the core problem may lie with the demand side of the labour market. Businesses in Botswana are reportedly stagnant, undercapitalised, and lack the ambition to expand, invest, or innovate. This prevents them from creating the necessary opportunities for graduates.

Economic growth relies on enterprises that produce more, enter new markets, and employ more people. Botswana's private sector has struggled to become dynamic enough to drive this expansion. Without growing businesses, the potential of educated graduates cannot be translated into productive work and economic progress.

Why it mattersGraduates face unemployment because the economy is not creating enough jobs for them.

Botswana ranks fourth in African governance but judicial impartiality slides

The 2026 Ibrahim Index places Botswana fourth of 54 African countries, with strong health and education results but sharp falls in judicial and anti-corruption scores.

Botswana ranks fourth among 54 African countries in the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, scoring 67.5 out of 100, according to the Mo Ibrahim Foundation. Only Seychelles, Mauritius and Cabo Verde rank higher. The index describes Botswana's trajectory as "Bouncing Back", an improvement since 2021 after a longer decline.

The country leads Africa in Education Quality with 89.9 points, up 20.5 points over the past decade, and ranks first for Access to Healthcare with 88.0. It also earned a perfect 100 for the Absence of Armed Conflict. Health Quality, however, scored just 18.3, placing Botswana 50th of 54.

Judicial impartiality fell 18.3 points over the decade to 45.0, ranking 24th. Public perceptions of accountability dropped 10.7 points, and the Absence of Undue Influence indicator lost 25 points. Public Procurement Procedures scored 22.2, placing 41st, while Anti-Corruption Mechanisms scored 30.4 and ranked 30th.

Why it mattersDeclines in judicial impartiality, accountability and procurement scores point to weaker oversight of public spending and contracts, which bears on companies that bid for government work.

Botswana ranks 45th in youth AI readiness despite strong connectivity

A new Ayre Index study places Botswana 45th of 56 Commonwealth nations, with its youth scoring far lower on job access than on technology access.

A new study places Botswana 45th out of 56 Commonwealth nations for youth artificial-intelligence readiness. The Ayre Index, launched on Tuesday at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda, gives the country 38.9 points out of 100, below a readiness benchmark of 53.

The country scored 7.2 out of 10 for meaningful access to technology but only 2.9 for decent work, enterprise and transitions. The study records youth unemployment at 39.6 per cent of the labour force, with many of those without work searching for more than a year.

The index also found that 19.4 per cent of young respondents had installed software and 3.8 per cent had written a computer program. Applying International Monetary Fund scenarios, the researchers estimate that artificial intelligence could add 289 million United States dollars to Botswana's GDP after 10 years under a moderate scenario.

Why it mattersBotswana's youth face high unemployment and limited digital skills despite strong access to technology.

Police spymaster Busang Lesola reportedly bids to lead Botswana's DIS

Deputy Commissioner Busang Lesola, who runs Crime Intelligence, is reportedly among those considered to succeed Peter Magosi at the Directorate of Intelligence and Security.

Deputy Commissioner of Police Busang Lesola, who runs Crime Intelligence at the Botswana Police Service, is reportedly angling to become Director General of the Directorate of Intelligence and Security (DIS). The agency has been led almost without exception by military figures since it was created in 2008 under former President Ian Khama. Information reaching Weekend Post indicates Mr Lesola is among those under consideration to succeed Peter Magosi, whose tenure ended last month in a departure the paper describes as controversial and unceremonious.

Those familiar with the process say Mr Lesola's name has been weighed with favour, his appeal resting on a grounded temperament, a steady character and experience in gathering intelligence. Several other names have reportedly been put before the Office of the President, including former DIS officials who left after Mr Magosi was installed by former President Mokgweetsi Masisi in 2018, and several candidates with military backgrounds.

Within the governing Umbrella for Democratic Change, one faction is understood to favour a police candidate, arguing fresh leadership could steady an institution battered by controversy. Another wants to keep the tradition of drawing the agency's head from the armed forces. Two UDC sources who spoke anonymously told Weekend Post the succession should be used to rethink the agency's mandate, leadership and culture, and conceded demands for reform were not unreasonable.

Why it mattersThe contest pits a police candidate against the military tradition that has led the DIS, and the choice will decide who runs the agency's intelligence work.

Botswana medicines regulator destroys 2.8 tonnes of illegal drugs

The Botswana Medicines Regulatory Authority condemned more than 20,000 units of unauthorised medicines after a multi-agency campaign in late October.

Botswana's Medicines Regulatory Authority, known as BoMRA, has condemned and destroyed 2.8 tonnes of illegal and unauthorised medicinal products worth more than P500,000. The seizures were announced on 5 November and capped a campaign that ran from 23 to 30 October at border posts and trading points, including Martin's Drift Border Gate, Palapye, the Mamuno Border Gate, Kasane, Kazungula and Pandamatenga.

BoMRA worked alongside Port Health, Environmental Health, the Botswana Unified Revenue Service and the Botswana Police Service, checking trading facilities, street vendors and vehicles including trucks, public transport and private cars. More than 20,000 units were destroyed, among them prescription-only medicines, skin-lightening creams, tablets, soaps, oils and codeine-containing cough syrups. BoMRA oversaw the destruction to keep the products out of the supply chain.

BoMRA stressed that medicinal products for human or veterinary use must be registered and separately authorised for import before they can be legally imported, distributed or supplied. Registration alone does not authorise importation, and the authority said unauthorised products are risky because their quality, safety and efficacy have not been properly established.

Why it mattersBotswana's registered medicine importers and distributors must obtain separate import authorisation, and unauthorised products are being seized and destroyed.
