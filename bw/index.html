Botswana backs Tamamyan for WHO leadership

President Boko nominated Dr Gevorg Tamamyan for Director-General, citing a need for a more effective World Health Organization.

President Duma Boko has formally nominated Dr Gevorg Tamamyan for the World Health Organization’s Director-General position, proposing him to the WHO Executive Board for appointment at the May 2027 World Health Assembly. Boko stated Botswana’s endorsement stems from Tamamyan’s commitment to underserved communities and the necessity of a more effective global health body.

In a nomination letter, Boko explained that Botswana’s own health system challenges highlight the need for strong global health leadership. He wrote that multilateral institutions facing strain require a demonstrably effective WHO, not a diminished one. Boko met Tamamyan and was satisfied his commitment to those furthest from care was genuine.

Tamamyan is one of seven candidates announced by the WHO on October 29. Contenders will engage with member states at a forum on November 18-19 in Geneva. The Executive Board will select up to three nominees in early 2027 for member states to consider for appointment on August 16, 2027.

Why it mattersBotswana's President is advocating for a specific candidate to lead the World Health Organization, impacting global health policy.

Gaborone launches electric bus service

CEDA and Ministry of Transport launch electric Metrobus service in Gaborone operated by Tana Holdings.

Gaborone has launched an electric Metrobus service operated by CEDA subsidiary Tana Holdings. The initiative aims to provide more accessible, efficient, and environmentally sustainable public transport in the capital city.

The success of the electric bus service hinges on policy and network reforms first proposed 15 years ago. Without a coordinated plan and an accountable passenger transport authority, ad hoc projects risk underperformance.

The September 2011 draft Integrated Transport Policy White Paper outlined measures to increase patronage, reduce private car use, and provide accessible infrastructure. These include bus priority schemes and financial support for socially necessary services.

Why it mattersGaborone residents will experience more sustainable public transport, but the service's long-term success depends on government implementing overdue policy reforms.

Bank of Botswana holds interest rates at 5.5 percent

Inflation eased to 7.8 percent in September, but remains above the central bank's target range.

The Bank of Botswana maintained its main interest rate at 5.5 percent on Wednesday, a decision by the Monetary Policy Committee chaired by Governor Lesego C. Moseki. This move aims to balance controlling inflation with supporting a fragile economic recovery, keeping borrowing costs steady.

Consumer price inflation fell to 7.8 percent in September from 9.3 percent the previous month, though this decrease was partly due to a statistical base effect from fuel prices in September 2025. Underlying inflation remains above the bank's 3-to-6 percent comfort zone, with projections for 2026 averaging 8.1 percent.

The economy grew 3.7 percent in the year to June 2026, driven by mining, particularly diamonds. However, growth is considered weak due to global demand and productivity issues. Policymakers anticipate inflation will return to the target band by the second quarter of next year, but risks from fuel prices, electricity tariffs, and global conflicts persist.

Why it mattersThe Bank of Botswana's decision to hold interest rates affects borrowing costs for businesses and consumers, impacting the economy's recovery and the cost of living.

Botswana struggles against human trafficking

United States report places Botswana on Tier 2, citing exploitation of women, children, and migrants.

Botswana has intensified its fight against human trafficking compared to last year but still falls short of global standards, according to the 2026 Trafficking in Persons Report from the United States Department of State. The report categorizes Botswana as Tier 2, acknowledging its serious efforts to combat trafficking while noting it has not yet met minimum eradication standards. The country is identified as a source, destination, and transit point for trafficking, with women, children, and migrants most vulnerable to sex trafficking and forced labor.

Traffickers increasingly use social media to lure victims with false job offers abroad, funneling them into agriculture, domestic servitude, and the sex trade. In the Ghanzi district, cattle ranchers reportedly exploit children in labor trafficking. At Dukwi Refugee Camp, residents from countries including the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Somalia face movement restrictions and lack of livelihoods, making child refugees susceptible to sex trafficking, with foreign truck drivers named as buyers.

The report also notes approximately 80 Cuban workers in Botswana may have been subjected to forced labor under bilateral agreements. Russian officials and illicit recruiters are accused of fraudulently drawing young women between 18 and 22 from Africa, including Botswana, for vocational training that leads to work in hazardous drone production facilities with exploitative conditions.

Why it mattersWomen, children, and migrants in Botswana face exploitation through sex trafficking and forced labor, with vulnerable populations in refugee camps and those lured by false job offers being particularly at risk.

Botswana firm lists on Australian Securities Exchange

Falcon Metal Resources Botswana becomes the first citizen-owned company to list on the ASX, raising capital for exploration.

Falcon Metal Resources Botswana (FMRB) became the first 100% citizen-owned company to list on the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX) this Wednesday. The listing is part of a capital-raising effort to expand exploration across its mining licences in Botswana’s mineral belts. FMRB's initial public offering took place alongside those of international companies like Israel’s Identifai Genetics.

The listing is managed through FMRB's Australian partner, Jacaranda Natural Resources (JNR), enabling them to combine resources for exploration and development in both countries. Both companies operate in jurisdictions highly rated for mining policy, including political stability and infrastructure, according to the Fraser Institute. FMRB is exploring for copper, gold, diamonds, and iron ore in Botswana.

FMRB has already originated and advanced eight exploration assets in Botswana through the JNR listing pathway. The company is now focused on building its independent portfolio, including the PL 245/2025 licence on the Kalahari Copperbelt and the Moiyabana iron ore project on PL 119/2025. Its diamond interests are in the Orapa Kimberlite Field under licence PL 181/2024.
