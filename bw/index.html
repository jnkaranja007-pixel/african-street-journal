Botswana economy grows 3.7 percent but households struggle

The economy grew to 3.7 percent in the second quarter of 2026, but household consumption fell by 7.2 percent.

Botswana's economy grew by 3.7 percent in the second quarter of 2026, rebounding from two years of contraction, according to Econsult. This growth was largely driven by mining, while private sector activity and bank lending remained weak.

Despite the overall GDP increase, real household consumption dropped by 7.2 percent in the same quarter. The non-diamond private sector saw only 1.6 percent annual growth, and agriculture contracted by 5 percent due to Foot and Mouth Disease restrictions, causing livestock production to fall by 48 percent.

Bank lending contracted by 2.2 percent in the year to July 2026, with household credit down 4.7 percent, attributed to high borrowing costs. Inflation eased to 7.8 percent in September, down from 10.7 percent in June.

Why it mattersHousehold consumption fell by 7.2 percent in the second quarter of 2026, indicating continued economic pressure on citizens.

Botswana adds intelligence vetting for Special Economic Zone investors

SEZA Botswana has signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the Directorate of Intelligence and Security to screen applicants before licences are issued.

SEZA Botswana, which runs the country's Special Economic Zones, has brought the Directorate of Intelligence and Security (DIS) into its investor vetting process. The two institutions signed a Memorandum of Understanding on Wednesday that lets them collaborate on screening and vetting prospective investors.

The arrangement adds intelligence-based security assessments to the checks applicants already undergo. It is meant to flag financial crime, integrity, reputational and national security risks before investors gain a foothold in the zones. SEZA Botswana Acting Chief Executive Officer Goabaone Masike said cooperation with the DIS would strengthen due diligence and uncover risks that may not be visible from information submitted by applicants.

The DIS will provide an added security assessment where SEZA's own verification capabilities fall short. Masike said investors and entities operating within the SEZ framework may also be scrutinised during the course of their operations where necessary.

Why it mattersApplicants to operate in Botswana's Special Economic Zones will now have their backgrounds checked by intelligence officers before a licence is granted.

Masisi says a man named as father must care for the child

The president told a children's law workshop that a DNA test is the only way for a named man to avoid the duty of care.

President Mokgweetsi Masisi said again this week that if a woman says a man is the father, he is the father and must take care of the child. He made the remark at a workshop in Botswana convened by UNICEF and the Ministry of Child Welfare to overhaul the country's children's legislation.

Advocate Duma Boko, who spoke at the same workshop, explained the legal idea of a putative father: a man presumed to be the father until a DNA test proves otherwise. Boko said only such a test can release him from the duty of care, and that the presence of a father figure in a child's life remains essential.

The workshop followed a proposal raised in the House of Chiefs to make DNA testing compulsory. Boko warned that testing might reveal that some men who reject paternity have fathered many children they never acknowledged.

Why it mattersMen named as fathers in Botswana must provide care for the child unless a DNA test shows otherwise, according to the president.

Unions demand more funding for Botswana schools

Teachers' unions say government spending has not improved overcrowded classrooms or school infrastructure.

Teachers' unions in Botswana have launched an international campaign demanding that the government increase public investment in school infrastructure, teacher support and learning resources. The Botswana Sectors of Educators Trade Union (BOSETU), the Botswana Teachers' Union (BTU) and the Trainers and Allied Workers Union (TAWU) presented a country report at the Education International (EI) Southern Africa Sub-Regional Workshop.

Among the problems the unions listed are poor sanitation, classrooms packed beyond capacity, ageing boarding facilities and school buildings that are falling apart for lack of upkeep. Despite rising government spending on education, the unions say those commitments have not yet reached classrooms or the people working in them.

The report argues that a larger budget alone will not produce quality public education unless money actually reaches schools and communities that need it most. It states that schools must be safe and properly maintained and that teachers must receive adequate support.

Why it mattersLearners in overcrowded classrooms and teachers working in deteriorating buildings are the people the unions say are still waiting for the funding that government has promised.

Sport · 2026-11-13

Young Zebras face Guinea without top scorer Ratshukudu

Botswana's Under-23 side lose the forward who scored three goals against Seychelles, as he is called up to the senior squad for a Uganda qualifier.

Botswana's Under-23 team, the Young Zebras, face Guinea in the second round of qualifying for the U-23 Africa Cup of Nations, and the first leg is set for November 11 at Gaborone National Stadium, with kick-off at 19:00. Coach Michael Mogaladi has left out leading scorer Losika Ratshukudu, the Croatia-based forward who scored three goals over the two-legged tie against Seychelles, including a brace in the opening match.

Ratshukudu has not been dropped for injury or form. He has been called up to Botswana's senior national team for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifier against Uganda, which takes place at Hoima City Stadium on November 14, three days after the home match against Guinea. Mogaladi must now rely on his remaining forwards and midfield to create and finish chances.

The second leg is scheduled for Tuesday, November 17, at 16:00 at Stade du 28 Septembre in Conakry, giving the two sides only six days between matches. A narrow win or a failure to score in Gaborone would leave Botswana with a harder task in the return fixture.

Why it mattersFans and supporters will see the Young Zebras' attack depend on new scorers, with a six-day gap before the Conakry return leg.
