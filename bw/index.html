Bank of Botswana holds interest rate at 5.5 percent

Inflation cooled to 7.8 percent in September, but remains above the central bank's target range.

The Bank of Botswana kept its main interest rate at 5.5 percent on Wednesday, deciding against a change even as inflation eased. The Monetary Policy Committee, led by Governor Lesego C. Moseki, voted unanimously to maintain the rate. This decision aims to balance controlling inflation with supporting a fragile economic recovery.

Consumer price inflation fell to 7.8 percent in September from 9.3 percent the previous month. However, much of this decrease was due to a statistical base effect from a fuel price increase in September 2025. Underlying inflation remains above the Bank's 3-to-6 percent comfort zone, with rising fuel and electricity costs expected to keep it elevated.

Policymakers now forecast inflation to average 8.1 percent for 2026, falling to 5.4 percent in 2027, with a return to the target band anticipated in the second quarter of next year. Risks to this outlook include potential increases in fuel and electricity prices, drought affecting food costs, and global oil price volatility due to the Middle East conflict.

Why it mattersThe Bank of Botswana's decision to maintain interest rates affects borrowing costs for businesses and consumers, impacting economic activity.

Makhadzi held in Botswana over P500,000 dispute

South African musician's passport surrendered to deputy sheriff pending court hearing on November 5.

South African musician Makhadzi is unable to leave Botswana due to a P500,000 legal dispute with promoter Gilbert PP Seagile. A court order obtained by Seagile bars her departure until at least November 5, 2026, when her lawyers will challenge the restriction.

Seagile claims Makhadzi owes him P500,000 for failing to appear at a One Woman Show on September 12, 2026, causing financial and reputational damage. While no formal contract was signed, Seagile stated a conversation acknowledged the event, and Makhadzi's passport was surrendered to the deputy sheriff.

The promoter confirmed the court order, which reportedly included provisions for Makhadzi's passport surrender and potential imprisonment, though he stated they opted not to pursue imprisonment while negotiations were ongoing.

Botswana minister rejects IMF wage cut plan

Ndaba Gaolathe proposes tackling waste and project costs before reducing public sector jobs.

Botswana's Vice President and Finance Minister Ndaba Gaolathe has proposed an alternative to International Monetary Fund (IMF) recommendations for cutting the public sector wage bill.

Speaking in Selebi-Phikwe, Gaolathe argued that the government should first address waste, inflated project costs, and inefficient administration before considering job cuts. He urged officials to justify project expenses, questioning why a project might cost three billion pula when it could be done for 500 million.

This debate occurs as Botswana faces fiscal pressure from a downturn in the diamond industry, prompting the government to seek sustainable public finances through spending containment, revenue collection, and institutional efficiency.

Why it mattersBotswana's government is reassessing its spending and revenue strategies due to fiscal pressures.

Sport · 2026-11-04

Dolphins reach Road to BAL Elite 16

Botswana's first basketball club to advance after finishing Lusaka campaign with three wins.

Dolphins Basketball Club has become Botswana’s first basketball club to reach the Road to BAL Elite 16. The team secured this progression by finishing their Group E campaign in Lusaka with three wins and a 3-2 record. FIBA had identified Dolphins as being on the brink of history before their final match.

The Botswana champions achieved this progression despite a final match loss to Ferroviário da Beira on Tuesday. Earlier in the campaign, Dolphins secured a 77-72 victory over Zimbabwe’s Harare Kings, with Dallan Coleman scoring 19 points. They also achieved a comeback win against Malawi’s Bravehearts, winning 78-74 after trailing at halftime.

Dolphins experienced a 96-56 defeat against hosts Munali Suns in Zambia. However, they responded with a 63-56 victory over South Africa’s Soweto Panthers. This win lifted their record to 3-1, putting qualification within reach before the final match.

Botswana remains tier 2 for human trafficking

US report credits government efforts but notes continued exploitation of women, children, and migrants.

Botswana is still designated Tier 2 for human trafficking, according to the 2026 Trafficking in Persons Report from the United States Department of State. The country has increased its efforts compared to last year but has not met global minimum standards for eradicating the crime. The report identifies Botswana as a source, destination, and transit corridor for trafficking, with women, children, and migrants most vulnerable to sex trafficking and forced labor.

Traffickers are increasingly using social media to lure victims with false job offers abroad, funneling them into agriculture, domestic servitude, and sex work. In the Ghanzi district, cattle ranchers reportedly exploit children in labor trafficking. At Dukwi Refugee Camp, residents from countries including the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Somalia are at high risk due to movement restrictions and lack of livelihood, with child refugees allegedly drawn into sex trafficking by foreign truck drivers.

The report also flagged approximately 80 Cuban workers in Botswana, suggesting the Cuban government may have subjected them to forced labor through bilateral agreements that confiscate wages. Additionally, Russian officials and recruiters are accused of fraudulently recruiting young African women, including from Botswana, for vocational training, only to place them in hazardous military drone production facilities with exploitative conditions.

Why it mattersWomen, children, and migrants in Botswana face exploitation through sex trafficking and forced labor, including in agriculture and domestic servitude.
