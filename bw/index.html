Botswana Parliament Approves National Health Insurance Policy

A ten-year partnership with Abu Dhabi Health Data Services will support the new policy covering medicines and digital health systems.

Parliament has unanimously adopted the National Health Insurance Policy on August 19, 2026, marking a significant step towards universal healthcare. This policy aims to ensure that all citizens can access quality medical care, irrespective of their location or financial status. The Minister of Health, Dr. Stephen Modise, has championed the initiative, framing it as a matter of dignity and equity for the nation.

Following the policy's adoption, the Ministry of Health announced a decade-long collaboration with Abu Dhabi Health Data Services (ADHDS). This partnership will focus on improving the supply chain for medicines and medical products, alongside broader health system enhancements. Key areas of cooperation include the development of digital health infrastructure and advancements in genomics.

The agreement with ADHDS is expected to provide crucial support for the successful implementation of the National Health Insurance scheme. By leveraging international expertise in health data management and supply chain logistics, Botswana aims to build a robust and efficient healthcare system capable of meeting the needs of its population. This collaboration signifies a commitment to modernizing healthcare delivery.

Why it mattersThe National Health Insurance Policy and its partnership with ADHDS will affect all Batswana by aiming to provide equitable access to quality healthcare services.

Botswana Government Seeks P85 Billion Bond Ceiling

The Ministry of Finance has asked Parliament to increase the government bond issuance ceiling from P55 billion to P85 billion to enhance financing flexibility.

Parliament has been asked to raise Botswana’s government bond issuance ceiling from P55 billion to P85 billion. Finance Minister Ndaba Gaolathe stated that the proposed increase is intended to provide greater financing flexibility for the government, rather than signal an intention to borrow more.

Gaolathe clarified to legislators that the new ceiling should not be viewed as a borrowing target or an indication that the full amount will be utilized. Instead, it represents the maximum value of government securities that can be outstanding at any given time.

This move aims to provide the government with more options for managing its finances and ensuring it has the capacity to issue securities up to the higher limit when needed, without necessarily committing to borrowing the full sum.

Why it mattersThe government's proposed increase in the bond issuance ceiling affects investors by potentially offering more government securities for purchase.

Bank of Botswana Holds Rate at 5.5% Amid Elevated Inflation

The Monetary Policy Committee decided against further tightening, keeping the key rate at 5.5 percent despite inflation exceeding the central bank's target range.

The Bank of Botswana has maintained its Monetary Policy Rate at 5.5 percent. This decision was made by the Monetary Policy Committee on Thursday, who opted against further tightening measures. Inflation has recently fallen from 10.7 percent in June to 9.4 percent in July, but remains significantly above the Bank’s medium-term objective of 3–6 percent.

This marks the second consecutive period the rate has been held steady. The committee's decision reflects a cautious approach to monetary policy, balancing the need to curb inflation with potential impacts on economic growth. The current rate is substantially higher than the upper limit of the bank's desired inflation range.

The Bank of Botswana's target for inflation is between 3 and 6 percent. The July figure of 9.4 percent indicates that price pressures are still a concern for policymakers. The committee's assessment considered recent economic data and forecasts before concluding to hold the rate.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the decision to hold the bank rate at 5.5% indicates a stable, albeit high, cost of borrowing and potentially signals the central bank's ongoing concern with inflation.

Millers warn wheat shortfall deepens import dependence

Botswana's wheat production has declined by 10 percent, leaving millers increasingly reliant on foreign supplies to meet demand.

Botswana's milling industry faces a deepening reliance on imports as local wheat production continues to fall short of demand. Bokomo chief executive Werner de Beer stated that wheat output has decreased by 10 percent, making it insufficient to significantly impact the country's needs. This shortfall forces millers to depend more heavily on international markets to maintain factory operations and supply chains.

The declining local production means that millers are unable to source a substantial portion of their wheat domestically. This situation directly impacts their operational capacity and necessitates a greater allocation of resources towards securing foreign wheat supplies. The trend indicates a growing gap between what Botswana can produce and what its industries require.

This increasing dependence on imported wheat has significant implications for the stability of supply and potentially for the cost of flour and related products within the country. Millers are navigating a challenging environment where local agricultural output is not keeping pace with industrial consumption, a situation that requires careful management of international procurement.

Why it mattersMillers are affected by a 10 percent decline in local wheat production, forcing them to increase their reliance on imports to keep factories supplied.

Township Rollers Faces New FIFA Bans Over Unpaid Player Debts

Despite recent sponsorships totaling millions of pula, the football club faces two new transfer bans due to outstanding payments to former players.

Township Rollers, a popular Botswana football club, is again facing transfer bans from FIFA due to unpaid debts owed to former players. The club recently settled a P1.2 million debt to former coach Abdelaziz Karkache, which had resulted in a previous ban. This payment allowed them to prepare for player registration, but two new FIFA bans, effective July 14 and August 28, have now halted those plans.

These latest bans stem from outstanding payments to former players Thabiso Mokenkoane and Ntambwe Wafauna Djo. The club is now compelled to pay these debts to lift the bans and register players for the ongoing season. Failure to do so means the team will have to compete without necessary new or returning players, impacting their competitiveness.

Team chairman Thapelo Pabalinga stated that the funds intended for team preparation and strengthening are being diverted to settle inherited debts from past investors and executive committees. Another former player, Thabo Rakhale, has also issued a demand letter for unpaid dues, threatening further escalation to FIFA if not resolved within ten days.

Why it mattersThe football club Township Rollers is affected by new FIFA bans, forcing them to use newly acquired sponsorship money to pay off inherited debts to former players and coaches instead of strengthening their team for the league.
