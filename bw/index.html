Chief Justice warns Law Society over Foot-and-Mouth Disease legal intervention

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe cautioned the Law Society of Botswana against interfering in a high-profile legal dispute related to the FMD crisis.

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe has warned the Law Society of Botswana (LSB) against intervening in a significant legal dispute stemming from the Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) crisis. This warning followed Ketlogetswe's dismissal of an urgent application by the LSB, which sought to reverse his decision to allow South African Senior Counsel Reginald Willis to practice in Botswana on an ad hoc basis.

Willis had been granted permission to represent JS Beef (Pty) Ltd and Primefast (Pty) Ltd in urgent litigation. This litigation concerns the government's management and the economic consequences of the FMD outbreak, indicating ongoing legal challenges related to the disease's impact.

The Chief Justice's stance highlights a judicial pushback against the LSB's involvement in a case that directly affects businesses dealing with the economic fallout of the FMD crisis, signaling potential implications for legal representation in such high-stakes disputes.

Why it mattersBusinesses like JS Beef (Pty) Ltd and Primefast (Pty) Ltd are directly affected as their legal representation in a critical FMD dispute is being challenged, impacting their ability to pursue claims regarding economic losses.

Botswana fertility rate falls below replacement level

Botswana's total fertility rate has dropped to 1.96 children per woman, falling below the 2.1 replacement level benchmark, according to the 2024 Vital Statistics Report.

Botswana is experiencing a demographic shift as its total fertility rate has fallen below the population replacement level. The 2024 Vital Statistics Report from Statistics Botswana indicates the rate is now 1.96 children per woman, which is below the 2.1 benchmark needed to maintain a stable population.

This change suggests a future challenge for the country, which has historically focused on creating jobs for a growing youth population. The gradual decline in births, occurring one family at a time, points to a potentially significant long-term demographic transformation.

The shift implies that Botswana may soon face a different problem: a shortage of young people. This contrasts with decades of effort to address youth unemployment, signaling a reversal in demographic trends that could impact various sectors of the economy and society.

Why it mattersBotswana's population structure is changing, which will affect the availability of young workers and consumers for all businesses and public services.

Government seeks P3.1 billion in August debt auction

The Ministry of Finance aims to raise P3.1 billion from the domestic market on August twenty-eighth to fund the budget deficit, continuing its reliance on monthly debt auctions.

The government plans to secure P3.1 billion from the domestic market on August twenty-eighth through a debt auction. This move continues the government's strategy of using monthly debt auctions to finance its budget deficit, as reported by the Sunday Standard.

The Bank of Botswana will conduct the auction on behalf of the Ministry of Finance. The offering is part of the existing P55 billion domestic note programme and includes P1.5 billion in three-month Treasury bills due on December two, 2026, and P1 billion in six-month bills maturing on March three, 2027.

Additionally, the auction will offer P600 million in reopened longer-term government bonds. This financing effort highlights the government's ongoing need for capital from the domestic market to meet its financial obligations.

Why it mattersThe government's reliance on domestic debt auctions affects all citizens as it funds public services and influences the national economy.

Sefalana subsidiary faces food safety alert after dead rodents found in products

Foods Botswana (Pty) Ltd, a subsidiary of Sefalana Holding Company Limited, is under scrutiny after dead rodents were reportedly discovered inside packaged Tsabana products.

An urgent food safety alert has been issued following reports of dead rodents found within packaged Tsabana products, a government-supplied food item manufactured by Foods Botswana (Pty) Ltd. This discovery has raised serious concerns regarding food safety controls at the Serowe-based manufacturing facility.

A considerable number of children are feared to have been exposed to this food safety risk. Tsabana is a product provided by the government, indicating a broad distribution and potential impact on vulnerable populations who rely on these provisions.

The incident has prompted questions about the oversight and quality assurance processes at Foods Botswana (Pty) Ltd, a key subsidiary of Sefalana Holding Company Limited. The company's manufacturing practices are now under intense scrutiny due to the reported contamination.

Why it mattersChildren who consume government-supplied Tsabana products are directly affected by the potential health risks posed by rodent-infested food.

Botswana citizenship-by-investment program stalls, leaving applicants in limbo

Botswana's Impact Citizenship Program, intended to offer citizenship for financial contributions, has not launched as scheduled, leaving prospective investors unable to apply.

Botswana's planned citizenship-by-investment program, marketed as the Impact Citizenship Program, has failed to open on schedule, according to DirectCitizenship.com. The program, which was promoted as a fast-track entry to citizenship in exchange for a financial contribution, is not yet operational and is not accepting applications.

The delay means that would-be investors are currently in limbo. The government is still working to finalize the legal, institutional, and administrative machinery required to launch the scheme, which has raised questions about its readiness for international investors.

What was initially presented as a quick path to Botswana citizenship has instead become a prolonged delay, impacting those who had anticipated applying to the program.

Why it mattersInternational investors who planned to apply for Botswana citizenship through the Impact Citizenship Program are directly affected by its failure to launch, as they cannot submit applications.
