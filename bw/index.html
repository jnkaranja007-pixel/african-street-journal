Botswana businesses struggle to create jobs

The nation's economy is not growing fast enough to absorb new graduates.

Botswana's recent graduation ceremonies celebrate many new graduates, but questions linger about their future employment prospects. The nation's economy faces a significant challenge in creating jobs at the same rate educated young people are entering the workforce.

While some argue universities produce inadequately prepared graduates, the primary issue may be the economy's demand side. Businesses need to grow, invest, and innovate to absorb the talent and creativity of graduates.

A stagnant private sector, undercapitalised and unwilling to expand, will perpetuate unemployment regardless of curriculum changes. Economic growth, driven by enterprises producing more goods and services, is essential for job creation.

Why it mattersGraduates face unemployment because Botswana's businesses are not expanding and creating enough new opportunities.

Botswana skills strategy falls behind schedule

The African Development Bank reports delays in a 10-year plan to address unemployment and diversify the economy.

A 10-year National Human Resource Development Strategy aimed at tackling graduate unemployment and reducing diamond dependence is behind schedule. The African Development Bank flagged incomplete work, inadequate participation from youth and businesses, and weaknesses in project administration.

The strategy, intended to cover 2026–2036, aims to transform human capital development and align skills with Vision 2036 and a knowledge-based economy. The African Development Bank's report noted that the finalisation of the draft strategy is now scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2026, a revision from the original timeline.

The delays raise concerns about Botswana's readiness to address unemployment and the persistent gap between educational outputs and employer demands. The African Development Bank's evaluation of the previous strategy (2009–2022) has been completed, but the development of the successor strategy has faced setbacks.

Why it mattersThe National Human Resource Development Strategy's delays impact Botswana's efforts to reduce unemployment and diversify its economy away from diamonds.

Botswana's President says fathers must care for children named by mothers

President Duma Boko says a man named as father is presumed to be the father until a DNA test proves otherwise.

Botswana's President, who is also Advocate Duma Boko, a lawyer, said this week that when a woman says a man is the father of her child, he is the father and must take care of the child. He made the remarks at a workshop with UNICEF officials and the Ministry of Child Welfare, convened to overhaul the country's children's legislation.

The workshop discussed a proposal raised in the House of Chiefs to make DNA testing compulsory. Boko described a man named by a mother as a putative, or presumed, father, and said only a DNA test proving otherwise can release him from the duty of care. He warned that compulsory testing could show some men have fathered children they never acknowledged.

Paternity tests compare short tandem repeat markers in a child's DNA with those of a potential father, excluding the mother's contribution. Laboratories typically report a probability of paternity of 99.99 percent or higher when the markers match. Boko said online that no child should suffer because of circumstances beyond their control.

Why it mattersMen named as fathers by mothers would be presumed responsible for child support unless a DNA test shows otherwise, under the reform Boko described.

Business · 2026-11-12

Botswana seeks 30% De Beers stake for tech access

President Duma Boko says the deal offers exposure to synthetic diamond applications and AI infrastructure.

Botswana plans to increase its ownership in De Beers from 15 percent to 30 percent, a move President Duma Boko described as an investment in technology. This expanded stake aims to give the country access to synthetic diamond applications beyond traditional gemstones.

Boko stated that the increased shareholding would make Botswana the largest shareholder in De Beers, granting greater influence over its strategic direction. He highlighted Element Six, a De Beers subsidiary developing synthetic diamond technology for industrial and technological uses, as a key part of the investment case.

The President noted that demand for high-technology synthetic diamonds is expected to rise with the expansion of artificial intelligence infrastructure and data centres. He also revealed that the US government has approached Element Six for a proposed US$600 million joint facility in Georgia, potentially anchoring a technology partnership between Botswana and the United States.

Why it mattersBotswana's planned increase in De Beers ownership is positioned as a strategic investment in technology, particularly synthetic diamond applications for AI and data centres.

Sport · 2026-11-12

Young Zebras miss Ratshukudu for U-23 Cup qualifier

Botswana's Under-23 team faces Guinea without their top scorer, Losika Ratshukudu, who joins the senior squad.

Botswana's Young Zebras will attempt to reach the U-23 Africa Cup of Nations without their leading scorer, Losika Ratshukudu, for their second-round qualifier against Guinea. The first leg match is scheduled for November 11 at Gaborone National Stadium, kicking off at 19:00. Coach Michael Mogaladi's squad selection omits Ratshukudu, who scored three goals in the previous round against Seychelles.

Ratshukudu's absence is due to his call-up to Botswana's senior national team for a 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifier against Uganda on November 14. This promotion signifies progress for Botswana football but presents a challenge for the Under-23 side, which must now find alternative attacking strategies against stronger opponents. Mogaladi aims to develop a more collective attacking effort, with remaining forwards and midfielders needing to increase their responsibility.

The return match against Guinea is scheduled for November 17 in Conakry. With only six days between the two legs, the coaching staff has limited time to address any tactical issues. The Young Zebras' qualification hopes may depend on their ability to adapt quickly to playing without their key finisher and secure a positive result in the home leg at Gaborone National Stadium.

Why it mattersThe Young Zebras' qualification hopes for the U-23 Africa Cup of Nations are directly impacted by the absence of their top scorer, Losika Ratshukudu, for crucial matches against Guinea.
