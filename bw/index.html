BIDPA says Botswana budget is only moderately geared toward jobs

The policy institute says lasting employment depends on private-sector hiring, while unemployment stands at 27.6 per cent.

Botswana’s 2026/27 budget is only “moderately” employment-oriented, according to a policy brief by the Botswana Institute for Development Policy Analysis (BIDPA). Researchers Masedi Motswapong and Lillian Mookodi say the budget’s ability to sustain jobs depends on whether public spending can lead to durable, privately financed employment.

The country’s labour force has reached roughly 1.17 million, while unemployment rose to 27.6 per cent from 25.9 per cent in 2023. The brief counts 287,060 people actively seeking work; including 76,418 discouraged people who have stopped looking lifts the extended unemployment rate to 32.5 per cent.

Youth unemployment reached 34.4 per cent in 2023 for people aged 15–35, and the rate for those aged 15–24 is estimated at 40–44 per cent. Nearly 41.3 per cent of young people are not in education, employment or training, the brief says.

Why it mattersThe 287,060 people actively seeking work and the 76,418 who have stopped looking are counted in the brief’s employment assessment.

Botswana's Financial Stability Council says banking system remains resilient

The council warns that the calm in the financial system is not self-sustaining and depends on continued supervision and safe banks.

Botswana's Financial Stability Council concluded on 11 November in Gaborone that the country's financial system remains resilient and supports economic activity. The council based its verdict on the safety and soundness of banks and non-bank financial institutions, the strength of market infrastructures and supervisory arrangements that regulators now describe as effective.

The council's assessment rests on concrete evidence rather than sentiment, according to the report published by the Weekend Post. Its message was carefully hedged: the stability it found is not self-sustaining and requires continued vigilance from regulators and institutions alike, as the broader financial environment grows more uncertain.

The full assessment is available to subscribers only, so further detail on specific measures, figures or next steps is not available in this report.

Why it mattersBanks, lenders and investors in Botswana depend on the council's finding that the system is sound, and its warning that this stability must be maintained means supervision will remain central to their operations.

Gobotswang challenges PEEPA-led Land Board recruitment in court

The Tswapong South MP says the process lacks legal authority over tribal land administration.

Tswapong South MP and Botswana Congress Party (BCP) Shadow Minister for Lands and Agriculture Kesitegile Gobotswang has filed an urgent application at the Gaborone High Court challenging the government's recruitment of Land Board members. The process, led by the Public Enterprises Evaluation and Privatisation Agency (PEEPA), offered three-year appointments to national and local "Land Governance" structures.

In an affidavit sworn on 28 October 2026, Gobotswang argues the recruitment bypasses the Tribal Land Act of 2018 and that PEEPA lacks authority to appoint members who administer tribal land. The exercise began weeks before Land Board members' terms were due to expire in November and closed without an explanation of its legal basis, he says. He also says the criteria give too little weight to the knowledge of local farmers and community members.

Gobotswang says he asked the Lands Minister on 11 October to explain PEEPA's role and called for the process to be suspended, but received no response by his 14 October deadline. He asks the court to treat the silence as evidence that the government cannot justify the exercise. He warns that appointments made through the process could expose land allocations, grants and title cancellations to legal challenge.

Why it mattersLand Board members whose terms expire in November face replacement through a recruitment process Gobotswang says lacks legal basis, and land allocations, grants and title cancellations by improperly constituted boards would be open to challenge.

Aterian identifies four copper-silver targets in Botswana

The targets lie along a 20-kilometre corridor in the Kalahari Copper Belt, where the company plans more surveys before choosing drilling sites.

London-listed exploration company Aterian has identified four priority copper-silver targets across a 20-kilometre corridor at prospecting licence PL199/2025 in Botswana’s Kalahari Copper Belt. Drone-based magnetic surveys and three-dimensional geophysical interpretation helped identify geological structures the company considers favourable for mineralisation.

Aterian said it has not confirmed mineralisation. Its geological modelling places possible fold structures 10 to 200 metres underground in rocks believed to belong to the D’Kar Formation. Executive chairperson Charles Bray said the four targets give the company several prospects to investigate.

Aterian is preparing induced polarisation and resistivity surveys to better define the structures and rank areas for drilling. The exploration programme is fully funded by the company’s artificial intelligence joint venture with Lithosquare; survey results will guide the selection of drilling targets.

Why it mattersThe planned surveys will determine which of the four targets Aterian prioritises for drilling, with the programme funded by its Lithosquare joint venture.
