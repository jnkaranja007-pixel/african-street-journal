BDC targets P5.5bn in investments and 25,000 jobs by 2031

Botswana has over P158 billion in pension assets, but only 45% of it is invested at home.

The Botswana Development Corporation (BDC) plans to deploy P5.5 billion into priority sectors and support the creation of 25,000 jobs by 2031. Managing Director Oteng Keabetswe set out the strategy at the BDC Investment Forum on Thursday.

Keabetswe described a "capital paradox" in which Botswana has plenty of savings but little capital reaching productive businesses. Private sector credit stands at about 30% of GDP, against 55% in comparable regional economies, and less than 5% of capital goes into private equity and venture capital.

Requirements for pension funds to invest about 50% of their assets domestically by 2027 could channel roughly P8 billion back into the economy, Keabetswe said. The BDC's targets depend partly on unlocking that domestic capital for businesses able to expand production.

Botswana adds intelligence vetting for special economic zone investors

Investors seeking licences in Botswana's Special Economic Zones will now be assessed by the Directorate of Intelligence and Security before approval.

SEZA Botswana has brought the Directorate of Intelligence and Security (DIS) into its investor vetting process, adding intelligence-based security assessments to the checks that prospective investors already undergo in the country's Special Economic Zones. The two institutions signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) on Wednesday.

The arrangement is designed to identify financial crime, integrity, reputational and national security risks before investors gain a foothold in the zones. SEZA Botswana Acting Chief Executive Officer Goabaone Masike said cooperation with the DIS would strengthen due diligence and uncover risks that may not be visible from information submitted by applicants.

Under the deal, the DIS will provide an added security assessment where SEZA's own verification capabilities are not sufficient. Masike said investors and entities operating within the SEZ framework may also be subjected to scrutiny during the course of their operations.

Unions demand more funding for Botswana schools

Teachers' unions say government spending has not improved overcrowded classrooms or school infrastructure.

Teachers' unions in Botswana have launched an international campaign demanding that the government increase public investment in school infrastructure, teacher support and learning resources. The Botswana Sectors of Educators Trade Union (BOSETU), the Botswana Teachers' Union (BTU) and the Trainers and Allied Workers Union (TAWU) presented a country report at the Education International (EI) Southern Africa Sub-Regional Workshop.

Among the problems the unions listed are poor sanitation, classrooms packed beyond capacity, ageing boarding facilities and school buildings that are falling apart for lack of upkeep. Despite rising government spending on education, the unions say those commitments have not yet reached classrooms or the people working in them.

The report argues that a larger budget alone will not produce quality public education unless money actually reaches schools and communities that need it most. It states that schools must be safe and properly maintained and that teachers must receive adequate support.

Why it mattersLearners in overcrowded classrooms and teachers working in deteriorating buildings are the people the unions say are still waiting for the funding that government has promised.
