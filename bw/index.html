Botswana secures South African beef market access

The agreement allows for exports of processed beef and live cattle, providing farmers with a new market amid disease and drought concerns.

Botswana has successfully negotiated access to the South African market for its beef and live cattle exports. This development offers a crucial new avenue for the country's livestock farmers, who are currently facing pressure from foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) and the threat of drought.

Director of Veterinary Services Dr. Kobedi Segale confirmed that discussions with South African authorities commenced in August. The resulting agreement encompasses both processed beef and live cattle, provided they meet the necessary veterinary and export requirements stipulated by South Africa.

This market access comes at a critical time for Botswana's agricultural sector. The dual threats of FMD and potential drought have created an urgent need for alternative markets to support livestock producers and ensure the stability of the sector.

Why it mattersBotswanan farmers are affected by gaining a new market for their beef and live cattle exports.

Botswana ranks third in Africa on global capitalism index

The nation scored 72.7 out of 100, but faces persistent barriers to private-sector growth.

Botswana has been ranked as Africa 's third-highest-performing capitalist economy, according to the Global Capitalism Index. The country achieved a score of 72.7 out of 100 in the index released by the University of Virginia 's Democracy and Capitalism Lab. This places Botswana behind Mauritius and Seychelles, which ranked first and second respectively in Africa.

Globally, Botswana secured the 52nd position. Namibia followed in fourth place among African nations with a score of 69.7. Despite its strong overall performance, the index highlighted existing weaknesses in business formation and capital markets.

These identified weaknesses suggest that persistent barriers continue to hinder the growth of Botswana's private sector. The index aims to measure various aspects of capitalist economies, with scores reflecting their overall performance and health.

Why it mattersInvestors may find Botswana's high ranking on the global capitalism index appealing, but persistent barriers to business formation and capital markets could affect private sector growth.

Nurses union condemns clinic's 24-hour service rollout

The Botswana Nurses Union says Sefhophe Clinic's expansion to round-the-clock care is unsafe due to insufficient staff.

The Botswana Nurses Union (BONU) has voiced strong objections to the recent transition of Sefhophe Clinic to a 24-hour facility, citing significant concerns over patient safety and staff working conditions. BONU argues that the service expansion was implemented prematurely, without the necessary personnel to manage continuous operations and emergency responses effectively.

According to the union, the clinic is currently staffed by only five nurses and a single midwife. This limited team is deemed inadequate to meet the demands of round-the-clock healthcare, including attending to emergencies and fulfilling the medical needs of the surrounding communities. BONU believes this staffing level compromises the quality of care provided.

The union's statement highlights a critical gap between the operational requirements of a 24-hour health service and the available resources at Sefhophe Clinic. BONU's condemnation suggests a potential risk to both patients and the existing healthcare staff due to the strain of inadequate staffing.

Why it mattersResidents of communities served by Sefhophe Clinic face potential risks to their health due to inadequate staffing at the newly expanded 24-hour facility.

Botswana Housing Corporation reverses P101.7mn loss

The corporation anticipates a P49.58 million profit before tax for the 2025/2026 financial year, marking a significant turnaround.

Botswana Housing Corporation (BHC) expects to return to profitability in the 2025/2026 financial year, projecting a P49.58 million profit before tax. This follows a substantial P101.741 million loss recorded in the previous financial year. The anticipated improvement represents a P151.321 million turnaround, according to a cautionary statement issued by the corporation on October 13.

The turnaround is attributed to internal adjustments and improved financial management within the corporation. BHC has been undergoing restructuring aimed at enhancing operational efficiency and service delivery. The corporation's statement indicates a positive outlook for its financial performance in the coming fiscal period.

This financial recovery is crucial for BHC's ongoing projects and its ability to meet housing demands across the country. The projected profit will enable further investment in infrastructure development and housing initiatives, supporting the government's broader economic and social development agenda.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the Botswana Housing Corporation's return to profitability, which signals improved financial health and potential for future returns.

Botswana inflation eases to 7.8 percent

Botswana's annual inflation rate fell by 1.5 percentage points in September, offering some relief from rising costs.

Botswana's annual inflation rate declined sharply to 7.8 percent in September from 9.3 percent in August. The decrease of 1.5 percentage points offers some relief to consumers and businesses facing elevated operating costs. Statistics Botswana's Consumer Price Index report for September 2026 detailed the fall, noting that transport costs remained the primary driver of price increases across the economy.

The easing inflation rate provides a welcome respite for households and companies that have been struggling with the financial impact of rising prices. While the overall inflation has decreased, the persistent increases in transport costs continue to affect economic activity and household budgets.

The September figures indicate a positive trend in price stability, although the underlying causes of transport cost increases require ongoing attention. This development is a key indicator for economic health and consumer confidence in Botswana.

Why it mattersConsumers and businesses are affected by the reduced rate of price increases, though transport costs remain a significant factor.
