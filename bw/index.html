Botswana fails global tax transparency test

The OECD's 2026 peer review found Botswana lacks required tax information exchange arrangements for multinationals.

Botswana has failed a global tax transparency test, according to a new report by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD). The country has not established the necessary bilateral arrangements to exchange tax reports from multinational companies, years after the issue was first raised.

The OECD's 2026 Country-by-Country Compilation of Peer Review revealed Botswana's ongoing transparency gap. This failure means the nation cannot properly share tax information on large corporations operating within its borders.

This situation leaves Botswana facing a longstanding transparency deficit. The report highlights the absence of key international agreements required for such exchanges.

Why it mattersBotswana has failed a global tax transparency test due to a lack of required tax information exchange arrangements for multinational companies.

Botswana receives one billion Pula diamond dividend

The newly established Diamond for Development Fund received a one billion Pula transfer from De Beers.

Botswana's new Diamond for Development Fund received one billion Pula, about USD 73 million, last week. Both President Duma Boko and former President Mokgweetsi Masisi had reasons to be satisfied with the transfer.

The fund's creation was a vision of former President Masisi, and President Boko is overseeing the financial asset's arrival under his administration. The transfer occurred without political opposition.

The relationship between Botswana's leaders and De Beers is described as having "frenemy energy." The fund's establishment is a result of this engagement.

Why it mattersBotswana's government secured one billion Pula for the Diamond for Development Fund.

Global Fund ends Botswana HIV and TB funding by 2030

Botswana must finance its HIV and tuberculosis programmes entirely from domestic resources after the Global Fund's final grant cycle.

The Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria has designated its latest grant cycle as Botswana's final allocation for HIV and TB programmes. The country is expected to finance these health services entirely from domestic resources by the end of 2030.

Funding for HIV programmes has been reduced, with Botswana's allocation falling from US$22.88 million under Grant Cycle 7 to US$13.12 million under Grant Cycle 8. This represents a decline of 42.6%, or approximately US$9.75 million.

This decision means Botswana will need to secure its own funding for HIV and TB treatment and prevention efforts moving forward. The Global Fund's Grant Cycle 8 is the last for these specific disease programmes in the country.

Why it mattersBotswana's HIV and TB programmes will need domestic funding by 2030.

Signet and De Beers deepen Botswana diamond ties

The two companies are strengthening their partnership across mining, manufacturing, and retail to boost natural diamond sales.

Signet Jewelers and De Beers are reinforcing their partnership in diamond mining, manufacturing, and retail, with Botswana's economic development, local craftsmanship, and responsible sourcing at the core of efforts to revive consumer demand for natural diamonds.

This collaboration was highlighted at a reception in Gaborone on Friday, celebrating Botswana’s 60th independence anniversary and its role in the global diamond industry, following a visit by Signet executives to the Jwaneng mine.

The companies aim to center their strategy on Botswana's economic transformation and responsible sourcing to counter weak demand in the natural diamond market.

Botswana investors access Dangote IPO via BDRs

Access Bank Botswana issues BDRs for Dangote Refinery IPO, opening African market access on the BSE.

Investors in Botswana and other SADC markets can now participate in the Dangote Petroleum Refinery & Petrochemicals FZE initial public offering through Botswana Depository Receipts (BDRs). Access Bank Botswana Limited is issuing these BDRs as an unsponsored depository, marking a first for the Botswana Stock Exchange.

The offering, referred to as “The People’s IPO,” allows Botswana-based investors to become shareholders in the refinery from their local exchange. The final days to invest in this first generation of public shareholders are currently underway.

This structure provides a new avenue for regional investment into Africa’s industrial sector.

Why it mattersBotswana investors can now access a major African industrial opportunity through their local stock exchange.
