Botswana launches P1 billion fund for industry

The Diamonds for Development Fund aims to convert mineral wealth into sustainable enterprises and jobs.

Botswana has launched its P1 billion Diamonds for Development Fund to create industries and jobs, moving beyond its reliance on diamond revenue. The fund received its initial P1 billion from De Beers Group. This initiative aims to transform wealth from the country's soil into diversified resources that generate value long after diamonds are depleted.

Established through a partnership between the Government of Botswana and De Beers Group, the fund intends to attract investment, nurture businesses, and expand the nation's export base. Chairman Dr. Akinwumi A. Adesina stated that the fund will convene government, business, and financial institutions to develop high-potential opportunities into investable projects.

The fund's strategy is to act as a catalyst, de-risking and packaging opportunities to attract private capital. This approach addresses the common challenge in Africa where promising ventures fail to become viable projects due to a lack of structure or funding.

Why it mattersBotswana's young population faces a challenge of formal employment not keeping pace with ambitions, and this fund aims to create jobs.

IDM Botswana graduates Class of 2026

The Institute of Development Management will hold its 20th Joint Graduation Ceremony on October 30, 2026, in Tlokweng.

The Institute of Development Management (IDM) Botswana is hosting its 20th Joint Graduation Ceremony on Friday, 30 October 2026, at the Royal Aria Convention Centre in Tlokweng. The event will celebrate the academic and professional achievements of the Class of 2026 from Botswana, Lesotho, and Eswatini.

Minister for State President, Defence and Security, Honourable Moeti Ceasar Mohwasa, will deliver the keynote address. George, Earl of St Andrews, Chancellor of the University of Greater Manchester, United Kingdom, will confer postgraduate and undergraduate degrees awarded by his university.

The ceremony theme is “Thriving as a Continuous Learner in an Ever-Changing Environment,” highlighting lifelong learning and adaptability. IDM reaffirms its commitment to developing management and professional capacity across the region.

Why it mattersGraduands from Botswana, Lesotho, and Eswatini will receive degrees and celebrate their achievements.

Botswana secures $503.75 million loan from AfDB

The new financing offers budget relief but raises questions about future revenue and debt repayment.

Botswana has secured a US$503.75 million loan, equivalent to P6.62 billion, from the African Development Bank (AfDB) approved last Friday, October 23. This new facility provides financial relief and is roughly a quarter of the P26.35 billion deficit projected for the 2026/27 financial year.

The latest loan follows a US$304 million facility approved on May 14, 2025, for the 2025/26 fiscal year, which was fully disbursed by June 2026. Finance Minister Ndaba Gaolathe had previously outlined a five-year grace period followed by 15 years of repayments for that loan, with a variable interest rate of 5.51 percent.

As of June 2026, Botswana's public debt stood at approximately P95 billion, or 32.12 percent of GDP, including government guarantees. Direct government debt, excluding guarantees, was P86.33 billion, with successive loans carrying their own repayment schedules.

Why it mattersBotswana's government is borrowing more money, increasing the national debt and raising concerns about its ability to repay.

Business · 2026-10-30

Botswana diamond workers allege exploitation

The Botswana Diamond Workers Union claims some firms pay meagre wages and force punishing hours on staff.

The Botswana Diamond Workers Union (BDWU) has accused some diamond cutting and polishing factories of exploiting workers, particularly Indian migrants. Allegations include meagre wages, precarious contracts, punishing hours, and intimidation against union organizing.

These claims emerge as Botswana's diamond trade faces soft global demand, sliding prices, and competition from lab-grown stones. The BDWU's accusations strike at the country's reputation for producing ethical diamonds, a brand built over nearly six decades.

Botswana sought to keep more value from its diamonds by developing local cutting and polishing, aiming to create jobs and transfer skills. However, operating costs in Botswana are higher than in established hubs like Surat, India, potentially leading some firms to cut labour costs.

Why it mattersWorkers in Botswana's diamond polishing factories face allegations of exploitation including low pay and long hours.

Ukraine demands guarantees over captured Motswana

Botswana faces diplomatic hurdles in securing the release of a citizen fighting for Russia.

Ukraine wants assurances that a captured Motswana will not rejoin Russian forces, complicating Botswana’s negotiations to secure the citizen’s release.

Minister of International Relations Phenyo Butale told Ntlo ya Dikgosi on Monday that negotiations with Ukrainian authorities were progressing, but Kyiv remained concerned the captured person might return to the Russian military.

Butale was responding to a question from Kgosi Ian Khama of GaMangwato, explaining that Ukraine fears foreign nationals released after capture could rejoin Russia’s war effort. Botswana faces difficulty providing a firm guarantee about a citizen’s future conduct, and continued recruitment of Batswana into the Russian military further complicates efforts to reassure Ukraine.
