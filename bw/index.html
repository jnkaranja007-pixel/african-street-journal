Chief Justice warns Law Society over Foot-and-Mouth Disease legal intervention

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe cautioned the Law Society of Botswana against interfering in a high-profile legal dispute concerning the Foot-and-Mouth Disease crisis.

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe issued a warning to the Law Society of Botswana (LSB) regarding its involvement in a significant legal case stemming from the Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) outbreak. This caution followed Ketlogetswe's dismissal of an urgent application from the LSB, which sought to reverse his decision to allow South African Senior Counsel Reginald Willis to practice in Botswana on an ad hoc basis.

Willis had been granted permission to represent JS Beef (Pty) Ltd and Primefast (Pty) Ltd in urgent litigation. This litigation specifically addresses the government's management and the economic consequences of the FMD outbreak, indicating ongoing legal challenges related to the disease's impact.

The Chief Justice's stance underscores a judicial effort to manage the legal proceedings surrounding the FMD crisis, directly affecting companies involved in the beef industry and the broader economic implications of the outbreak.

Why it mattersThe legal dispute directly affects JS Beef (Pty) Ltd and Primefast (Pty) Ltd, which are involved in urgent litigation concerning the economic impact of the Foot-and-Mouth Disease outbreak.

Botswana's fertility rate drops below replacement level

Botswana's total fertility rate has fallen to 1.96 children per woman, indicating a potential future shortage of young people, according to Statistics Botswana.

Botswana faces a demographic shift as its total fertility rate has dropped below the replacement level of 2.1 children per woman. The 2024 Vital Statistics Report from Statistics Botswana indicates the rate now stands at 1.96 children per woman.

This decline suggests a future challenge for the country, which has historically focused on job creation for its youth. The slow, family-by-family change could lead to a different problem: a scarcity of young people in the population.

The long-term implications of this trend could affect the labor force and economic growth, shifting the national concern from youth unemployment to a potential shortage of young workers and consumers in the coming decades.

Why it mattersBotswana's declining fertility rate means fewer young people will be available for the workforce and as consumers, impacting economic growth and social structures.

Government seeks P3.1 billion in August debt auction

The government plans to raise P3.1 billion from the domestic market on 28 August to finance its budget deficit, continuing its reliance on monthly debt auctions.

The government will conduct a debt auction on 28 August, aiming to raise P3.1 billion from the domestic market. This action continues the government's heavy reliance on monthly debt auctions to finance its budget deficit, according to the Sunday Standard.

The Bank of Botswana will manage the auction on behalf of the Ministry of Finance. The offering is part of the existing P55 billion domestic note programme and includes P1.5 billion in three-month Treasury bills maturing on 2 December 2026, and P1 billion in six-month bills maturing on 3 March 2027.

Additionally, the auction will include P600 million in reopened longer-term government bonds. This move highlights the government's ongoing strategy to secure funds from within the country to cover its financial obligations.

Why it mattersThe government's reliance on domestic debt auctions to fund its budget deficit directly affects citizens through the allocation of public funds and the potential impact on interest rates.

Foods Botswana faces scrutiny after rodent discovery in children's food

A food safety alert has been issued after dead rodents were reportedly found inside packaged Tsabana products, a government-supplied food for children.

Dozens of children are feared to have been exposed to a food safety risk following reports of dead rodents discovered within packaged Tsabana products. Tsabana is a government-supplied food product manufactured by Foods Botswana (Pty) Ltd, a subsidiary of Sefalana Holding Company Limited.

The discovery has prompted an urgent food safety alert and raised significant questions regarding the food safety controls at the Serowe-based manufacturer. The incident highlights potential vulnerabilities in the production and packaging processes of essential food items.

This event has drawn attention to the critical need for stringent quality assurance in food manufacturing, especially for products intended for vulnerable populations like children. The full extent of the exposure and its implications are under investigation.

Why it mattersChildren who consume government-supplied Tsabana products are directly affected by the potential health risks posed by rodent contamination.

Botswana's citizenship-for-investment program stalls, leaving applicants in limbo

The Impact Citizenship Program, designed to offer fast-track citizenship for financial contributions, has failed to launch on schedule and is not accepting applications.

Botswana's attempt to enter the global citizenship-by-investment market has stalled, with the much-promoted Impact Citizenship Program failing to open as scheduled. This delay has left prospective investors in an uncertain state, as the program, marketed as a gateway to citizenship in exchange for a financial contribution, remains non-operational.

According to DirectCitizenship.com, the government is still working to finalize the necessary legal, institutional, and administrative frameworks required to launch the scheme. What was advertised as a rapid entry into Botswana citizenship has instead become a prolonged delay, raising doubts about the program's readiness for international investors.

The program's inability to accept applications means that individuals who planned to invest in exchange for citizenship are currently unable to proceed. This situation contrasts sharply with the initial marketing of a fast-track process, creating uncertainty for those interested in the scheme.

Why it mattersWould-be investors are directly affected as they cannot apply for citizenship through the program, leaving their plans in limbo.
