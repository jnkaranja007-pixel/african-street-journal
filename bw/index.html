Chief Justice Warns Law Society Over Foot-and-Mouth Legal Fight

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe dismissed an urgent application by the Law Society of Botswana seeking to rescind his decision allowing a South African lawyer to appear.

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe has cautioned the Law Society of Botswana (LSB) against interfering in a significant legal case related to the Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) crisis. The Chief Justice issued this warning after rejecting an urgent application by the LSB. The LSB had sought to overturn his earlier ruling that permitted South African Senior Counsel Reginald Willis to practice in Botswana on an ad hoc basis.

Willis was admitted to represent JS Beef (Pty) Ltd and Primefast (Pty) Ltd in urgent legal proceedings. These proceedings concern the government's handling of the FMD outbreak and its economic consequences. The Chief Justice's dismissal of the LSB's application signifies a setback for the society's attempt to intervene in the dispute.

The FMD crisis has had substantial economic impacts, and the legal battles surrounding its management are high-profile. The Chief Justice's firm stance indicates a desire to maintain the integrity of the legal process and prevent external bodies from unduly influencing ongoing litigation concerning critical economic issues.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the legal dispute concerns the management and economic impact of the Foot-and-Mouth Disease crisis, potentially influencing business operations and regulatory environments.
