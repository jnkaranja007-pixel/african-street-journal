Botswana UDC government reverses free education pledge

Diamond revenue collapse forces the Umbrella for Democratic Change to retain secondary school fees.

Botswana's Umbrella for Democratic Change (UDC) government has reversed its pledge to provide free public education due to a collapse in diamond revenue. The decision means secondary school fees will be retained, sparking a dispute over election promises and national finances.

The UDC's manifesto, themed 'Inclusive Growth, Quality Lives,' had committed to guaranteeing quality education as a human right. This was widely understood as a promise to end cost-sharing school fees, which have been in place since 2006.

The government faces mounting financial constraints as the diamond mining sector, vital to Botswana's economy, experiences a macroeconomic crisis. The UDC government is now engaged in a war of words over the state's public finances and its campaign commitments.

Why it mattersThe UDC government's reversal on free education directly impacts households that relied on the promise to reduce school costs.

Botswana boxers win three medals in DRC

The team secured two bronze medals and a chance at gold in their return to the IBA Africa Elite Championships.

Botswana boxers returned to the IBA Africa Elite Men’s & Women’s Boxing Championships in Kinshasa, Democratic Republic of Congo, after a four-year break, securing three medals. Treasure Moremi and Trecier Thipe each won a bronze medal, with Thipe's bout stopped due to a shoulder injury. Kobamelo Molatlhegi was Botswana's sole finalist, competing for a gold medal against the host nation's boxer.

BoBA Publicity Secretary Kabelo Seleka stated the mission was to compete for medals and make a strong statement on the continental stage. "We are not going to Kinshasa simply to participate, we are going there to compete," Seleka had declared before the event. Moremi lost her semifinal by split decision, while Thipe's injury ended her medal hopes. Molatlhegi faced Bofenda Mabala in the final.

Seleka noted that while the medal count is not comparable to previous achievements, the campaign signifies a return to the continental stage and reaching medal positions. He acknowledged room for improvement, stating, "We must be able to compete consistently across all weight categories and convert more of our participation into victories and medals." Seleka also highlighted the need for consistent investment in preparation and athlete development.

Gaborone mortgages near 94% of property value

A P1 million property with a P937,000 mortgage leaves owners with only P63,000 equity.

The average mortgage loan-to-value ratio in Greater Gaborone has climbed to 93.7 percent, according to the Bank of Botswana’s 2025/26 Household Indebtedness Survey. This means a P1 million property financed with a P937,000 mortgage leaves its owner with just P63,000 in housing equity.

A modest fall in the property’s value could wipe out this financial cushion, illustrating emerging exposure in Greater Gaborone.

The survey highlights the growing risk for homeowners in the region.

Why it mattersHomeowners in Greater Gaborone face significant risk as their mortgage debt approaches the full value of their properties.

Business · 2026-11-02

Cheetah Cement plans 800,000-tonne Gaborone plant

The expansion could boost domestic cement production and reduce reliance on imports.

Cheetah Cement plans to build a new manufacturing plant in Gaborone with an annual capacity of 800,000 tonnes. The company highlighted this expansion during a visit from the Botswana Investment and Trade Centre (BITC) to its existing Matsiloje facility. Cheetah Cement, which began operations in 2021, currently employs 41 Batswana.

Newly appointed chief executive Wang briefed the BITC delegation, led by chief executive Keletsositse Olebile, on the company's performance and challenges. Olebile reaffirmed BITC's commitment to assisting Cheetah Cement with regulatory and infrastructure hurdles. The agency also stressed the importance of investment aftercare for established businesses.

This proposed plant aligns with government efforts to diversify Botswana's economy beyond diamonds and create private-sector jobs. Increased local cement production could improve supply reliability and offer competition to imported products. The plant's ultimate impact depends on implementation, production costs, and competitiveness.

Why it mattersThe proposed Gaborone plant aims to increase domestic cement production, potentially lowering costs for construction projects and reducing the need for imports.
