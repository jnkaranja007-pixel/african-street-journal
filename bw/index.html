Chief Justice warns Law Society over FMD legal intervention

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe cautioned the Law Society of Botswana (LSB) against interfering in a high-profile legal dispute concerning the Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD).

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe issued a warning to the Law Society of Botswana (LSB) after dismissing its urgent application. The LSB sought to reverse Ketlogetswe's decision to allow South African Senior Counsel Reginald Willis to appear in Botswana on an ad hoc basis.

Willis was admitted to represent JS Beef (Pty) Ltd and Primefast (Pty) Ltd in urgent litigation. This litigation addresses the government's management and the economic impact of the Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) outbreak.

The Chief Justice's warning highlights judicial concern over the LSB's involvement in a case with significant economic implications for companies affected by the FMD crisis.

Why it mattersCompanies involved in the beef industry are directly affected by the legal proceedings and the government's handling of the FMD outbreak, which impacts their operations and financial stability.

Botswana faces youth population decline as fertility rate drops below replacement

Botswana's total fertility rate has fallen to 1.96 children per woman, below the 2.1 replacement level, indicating a future shortage of young people.

Botswana is experiencing a demographic shift, with its total fertility rate (TFR) dropping below the level needed to maintain a stable population. The TFR has reached 1.96 children per woman, according to the 2024 Vital Statistics Report from Statistics Botswana, which is below the widely accepted replacement benchmark of 2.1.

This decline suggests that the country, which has historically focused on creating jobs for a growing youth population, may soon face the opposite challenge: a shortage of young people. The change is described as gradual, occurring one family at a time, but the statistical data points to a significant long-term impact.

The implications of this trend could be profound for Botswana's economy and social structure, as a shrinking youth demographic affects the workforce, consumer base, and the dependency ratio in the coming decades.

Why it mattersThis demographic shift affects all residents of Botswana by changing the future availability of workers, consumers, and the overall age structure of the population.

Government seeks P3.1 billion in August debt auction

The government plans to raise P3.1 billion from the domestic market on 28 August to finance its budget deficit, continuing its reliance on monthly debt auctions.

The government will conduct a debt auction on 28 August, aiming to raise P3.1 billion from the domestic market. This action continues the government's heavy reliance on monthly debt auctions to finance its budget deficit, according to the Sunday Standard.

The Bank of Botswana will manage the auction on behalf of the Ministry of Finance. The offering includes P1.5 billion in three-month Treasury bills maturing on 2 December 2026, P1 billion in six-month bills maturing on 3 March 2027, and P600 million in reopened longer-term government bonds.

This auction is part of the existing P55 billion domestic note programme. The government uses these funds to cover its financial obligations, indicating a consistent need for domestic borrowing to support its budget.

Why it mattersThe government's continued reliance on domestic debt auctions directly affects taxpayers and the national budget, as it indicates how public funds are being managed and financed.

Foods Botswana faces scrutiny over rodent-infested Tsabana products

A food safety alert has been issued after dead rodents were reportedly found inside packaged Tsabana products, a government-supplied food for children.

Dozens of children are feared to have been exposed to a food safety risk following the discovery of dead rodents in packaged Tsabana products. Tsabana is a government-supplied food product manufactured by Foods Botswana (Pty) Ltd, a subsidiary of Sefalana Holding Company Limited.

The reported discovery has triggered an urgent food safety alert. This incident raises serious questions about the food safety controls at the Serowe-based manufacturer, Foods Botswana.

The situation highlights potential lapses in quality control for a product intended for vulnerable populations, prompting concerns about the integrity of the manufacturing process and the safety of the food supply chain for government-distributed items.

Why it mattersChildren who consume government-supplied Tsabana products are directly affected by the potential health risks associated with rodent-infested food.

San community group demands police brutality investigation

The Botswana Khweedom Council has engaged Police Commissioner Dinah Marathe regarding alleged police brutality and victimization against the San community.

The Botswana Khweedom Council has called for an independent, impartial, and transparent investigation into alleged police brutality, according to a letter dated August 17, 2026. This action follows a recent incident involving Baleki Kelentse, a middle-aged man from the San community in Dukwi village, who was reportedly beaten and injured by police during a stock theft investigation.

The Council, an organization advocating for Basarwa rights, is demanding that Commissioner Marathe make findings on the circumstances that led to Kelentse's injuries. The group characterizes the incident as part of perpetual victimization by police officers.

The engagement with the Police Commissioner highlights concerns about law enforcement conduct affecting the San community, particularly in cases involving investigations such as stock theft. The Council seeks accountability and clarity regarding the alleged assault.

Why it mattersThe San community is directly affected by the alleged police brutality and the subsequent demand for an investigation into the incident.
