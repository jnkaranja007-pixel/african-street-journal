Chief Justice Warns Law Society Over Foot-and-Mouth Disease Legal Fight

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe has cautioned the Law Society of Botswana against interfering in a significant legal case concerning the Foot-and-Mouth Disease outbreak.

Chief Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe has issued a stern warning to the Law Society of Botswana (LSB) regarding its involvement in a critical legal dispute stemming from the Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) crisis. The Chief Justice's admonishment followed his rejection of an urgent application filed by the LSB. The LSB had sought to overturn his previous decision that permitted South African Senior Counsel Reginald Willis to practice in Botswana on an ad hoc basis.

Reginald Willis was admitted to represent JS Beef (Pty) Ltd and Primefast (Pty) Ltd in urgent legal proceedings. These proceedings are directly related to the government's handling of the FMD outbreak and its subsequent economic repercussions. The LSB's attempt to intervene and rescind the Chief Justice's decision highlights a significant disagreement over legal representation in this high-profile case.

The Chief Justice's dismissal of the LSB's application underscores the court's stance on the matter, reinforcing the initial decision to allow external legal expertise. This legal battle is unfolding against the backdrop of the ongoing FMD crisis, which has significant implications for the affected companies and potentially the broader agricultural sector.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the legal dispute involves companies engaged in urgent litigation concerning the economic impact of the FMD outbreak, potentially influencing business operations and market stability.
