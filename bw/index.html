Botswana nominates Gevorg Tamamyan for WHO top job

President Duma Boko cited Botswana's own health challenges as a reason for supporting effective global health leadership.

President Duma Boko has formally proposed Dr. Gevorg Tamamyan for the World Health Organization's Director-General position, with the nomination letter sent to the WHO Executive Board chairperson. The World Health Assembly will consider the appointment in May 2027.

Boko explained Botswana's decision by stating that the country's health-system challenges highlight the need for effective global health leadership. He met Tamamyan and was satisfied with his commitment to underserved communities, leading Botswana to put his name forward.

Tamamyan is one of seven candidates announced by the WHO on October 29. A candidates' forum is scheduled for November 18–19 in Geneva, and the Executive Board will narrow the field to five in early 2027, with the new Director-General taking office on August 16, 2027.

Why it mattersBotswana's President is seeking a more effective World Health Organization to address global healthcare needs, particularly for those struggling to access care.

Botswana Vaccine Institute faces export threat

Namibia and South Africa plan domestic vaccine production, potentially reducing demand for Botswana's foot-and-mouth disease exports.

Botswana Vaccine Institute (BVI) faces a potential long-term threat to its export business as Namibia and South Africa develop their own foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) vaccine manufacturing capabilities. Namibia plans to establish its own plant, aiming to reduce reliance on imported vaccines, despite a recent three-year deal for over 3.8 million doses from Botswana valued at P50.43 million.

Ben Haraseb, Namibia's director for agricultural production, outlined the plant plans in October at an expo in Windhoek. While Botswana is Namibia's current main supplier, the immediate risk to BVI is limited due to the existing supply agreement and the fact that Namibia's facility is not yet operational. However, successful local production could eventually decrease Namibian orders.

South Africa also resumed domestic FMD vaccine production in February 2026 through the Agricultural Research Council (ARC), after a two-decade hiatus. The ARC handed over its first locally produced batch on February 6. These developments in key export markets raise concerns for BVI's future regional sales.

Batswana approval of China's influence halves in a decade

Afrobarometer survey shows favourable views dropping from 74% to 37% between 2014 and 2024.

Batswana's positive views on China's influence have fallen by half over the past decade, according to a new Afrobarometer survey.

The proportion of citizens seeing China's influence as positive dropped from 74 percent in 2014 to 37 percent in 2024, a decrease of 37 percentage points. Only 50 percent of Batswana now believe China's economic activities significantly influence the national economy, down from 76 percent in 2014.

Men and urban residents are more likely than women and rural dwellers to view China's influence as substantial and beneficial.

Why it mattersBatswana citizens' perceptions of China's influence on their country have significantly declined.

Botswana women's football faces funding gap

A new 20-team league launched this year requires more sponsorship to sustain its growth and player development.

Women's football in Botswana is expanding with the introduction of a 20-team National First Division, split into northern and southern conferences. However, the Botswana Football Association (BFA) warns that a lack of sponsorship and suitable infrastructure threatens the league's progress. Clubs need financial backing for transport, equipment, coaching, and facilities to support the increased opportunities for players.

Tsholofelo Setlhoko, BFA Head of Women’s Football, stated that while the new league represents significant growth, clubs are struggling with funding shortages. She met with representatives from the African Club Association (ACA), an independent body aiming to support clubs across the continent, to explore potential partnerships. Setlhoko, also a FIFA Women’s Football Development Expert, highlighted the need for dependable backing to meet weekly operational costs.

The BFA is seeking ways to build on the momentum of the new division, aiming to provide a more secure foundation for players and clubs. Regional competitions, like the existing women's club tournament, offer chances for teams to improve skills and raise their playing standards by competing against diverse styles. BFA president Tariq Babitseng is also discussing a regional men's club tournament.

Why it mattersWomen's football players and clubs in Botswana face challenges in securing necessary funding and infrastructure to sustain the newly launched 20-team league.

Politics · 2026-11-06

4,274 foreigners gained Botswana citizenship via marriage

Zimbabweans and South Africans married to Batswana citizens acquired citizenship between 2018 and 2026.

Government figures show that 4,274 Zimbabwean and South African nationals became Botswana citizens through marriage between 2018 and 2026. Of this total, 2,947 were Zimbabweans and 1,327 were South Africans, with Zimbabweans making up about 69 percent of the beneficiaries.

Minister of Labour and Home Affairs Pius Mokgware stated that geographical proximity and shared borders facilitate relationships leading to these marriages. He explained that cross-border movement has contributed to relationships where foreign spouses seek Botswana citizenship. Marriage to a Motswana does not automatically grant citizenship; applicants must meet naturalisation requirements.

The Citizenship Act requires foreign spouses to have resided in Botswana for at least five years and lodged a declaration of intention to apply for naturalisation two and a half years before their application. Minister Mokgware disclosed these figures in response to a question in Ntlo ya Dikgosi.

Why it mattersZimbabwean and South African nationals married to Batswana citizens gained Botswana citizenship after meeting residency and application requirements.
