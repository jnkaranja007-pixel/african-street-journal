Bank of Botswana holds interest rate at 5.5 percent

Inflation cooled to 7.8 percent in September, but remains above the central bank's target range.

The Bank of Botswana kept its main interest rate at 5.5 percent on Wednesday, deciding against a change even as inflation eased. The Monetary Policy Committee, led by Governor Lesego C. Moseki, voted unanimously to maintain the rate. This decision aims to balance controlling inflation with supporting a fragile economic recovery.

Consumer price inflation fell to 7.8 percent in September from 9.3 percent the previous month. However, much of this decrease was due to a statistical base effect from a fuel price increase in September 2025. Underlying inflation remains above the Bank's 3-to-6 percent comfort zone, with rising fuel and electricity costs expected to keep it elevated.

Policymakers now forecast inflation to average 8.1 percent for 2026, falling to 5.4 percent in 2027, with a return to the target band anticipated in the second quarter of next year. Risks to this outlook include potential increases in fuel and electricity prices, drought affecting food costs, and global oil price volatility due to the Middle East conflict.

Why it mattersThe Bank of Botswana's decision to maintain interest rates affects borrowing costs for businesses and consumers, impacting economic activity.

Botswana VP offers alternative to IMF wage cuts

Vice President Ndaba Gaolathe proposed tackling waste and project costs before reducing public sector jobs.

Botswana's Vice President Ndaba Gaolathe proposed a different approach to managing the public sector wage bill, suggesting that the government focus on eliminating waste and inflated project costs before considering job cuts. Speaking in Selebi-Phikwe, Gaolathe argued that ministries must justify the expense of their projects, questioning why a project might cost billions when it could be delivered for a fraction of that. He urged officials to scrutinize procurement and demand evidence of value from public spending.

Gaolathe's remarks come as Botswana faces fiscal pressure due to a downturn in the diamond industry, which has reduced government revenue. The International Monetary Fund has previously recommended reducing the wage bill to 11 percent of GDP, a target that could necessitate cuts to the civil service. The government is currently evaluating strategies to maintain essential services and fund development while ensuring financial sustainability.

The government is also pursuing tax reforms, including extending value-added tax to remote services and introducing electronic invoicing, to broaden its revenue base. Efforts are underway to encourage private sector investment and diversify the economy beyond diamonds through the Botswana Economic Transformation Programme, though these initiatives are expected to take time.

Why it mattersBotswana's public finances are under pressure, and the government is deciding how to manage spending and revenue.

Botswana struggles against human trafficking

The 2026 Trafficking in Persons Report keeps Botswana on Tier 2, citing progress but persistent exploitation.

Botswana has intensified its fight against human trafficking compared to the previous year, yet it still falls short of global standards. The 2026 Trafficking in Persons Report from the United States Department of State classifies Botswana as Tier 2, acknowledging serious efforts to combat the crime but also its failure to meet minimum eradication standards. The report identifies Botswana as a source, destination, and transit country for trafficking, with women, children, and migrants most vulnerable to sex trafficking and forced labor.

Traffickers are increasingly using social media to lure victims with false overseas job offers, funneling them into agriculture, domestic servitude, and the sex trade. In the Ghanzi district, cattle ranchers exploit children for labor. At Dukwi Refugee Camp, residents from countries including the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Somalia are at high risk due to movement restrictions and lack of livelihood opportunities, with child refugees reportedly drawn into sex trafficking by foreign truck drivers.

The report also flags approximately 80 Cuban workers in Botswana, suggesting they may have been subjected to forced labor by the Cuban regime through bilateral labor agreements. Russian officials and illicit recruiters are accused of fraudulently drawing young women from Africa, including Botswana, for vocational training, only to place them in hazardous military drone production facilities under exploitative conditions.

Why it mattersBatswana citizens, refugees, and foreign workers within Botswana are at risk of exploitation through forced labor and sex trafficking.

Botswana reaffirms opposition to Taiwan independence

Gaborone's stance on Taiwan's sovereignty draws criticism and prompts questions about self-determination.

Botswana has restated its support for Beijing's position on Taiwan, opposing Taiwanese independence and backing China's reunification efforts. This stance was met with sharp condemnation from Taiwan, which accused Beijing of coercing other governments into statements that undermine Taiwan's sovereignty. The decision comes as Botswana prepares to celebrate its independence, raising questions about the principle of self-governance.

Taiwan, which has been governed separately from mainland China since 1949, has developed its own democratic institutions and economy, including a significant semiconductor industry. 83 percent of Taiwanese supported autonomy in a September 2026 survey. Taiwan possesses a permanent population, territory, functioning institutions, and the capacity for international relations, according to the report.

The report argues that Taiwan's economic success, particularly in semiconductors, demonstrates its capacity to manage its own affairs, removing any suggestion of lacking the means for self-governance. It questions whether Botswana believes Taiwan's people have the right to self-determination, drawing a parallel to Botswana's own independence in 1966, which was not conditional on wealth or power.

Why it mattersBotswana's foreign policy decision on Taiwan's sovereignty affects the nation's international relations and its stated principles on self-determination.

Ramaotwana asked to probe Masisi-linked trust dispute

Acting chief Kgosi Oscar Mosielele seeks investigation into alleged mismanagement of the Ipelego Community Trust.

Acting chief Kgosi Oscar Mosielele has asked Justice Minister Nelson Ramaotwana to direct the Master of the High Court to investigate a multimillion-pula dispute concerning trustees linked to former President Mokgweetsi Masisi. Mosielele filed complaints alleging that the trustees of the Ipelego Community Trust, founded by Masisi, had failed to follow legal requirements for trusts. The trustees include Masisi's sister, Boitumelo Phadi Mmutle, and the trust's chair, Kgosi Kgabosetso II Mosielele.

In a letter dated September 24, Mosielele stated the trustees failed to submit required reports, maintain proper accounts, and prepare financial statements. He accused them of not acting in good faith and running the community trust like a private entity. The chair reportedly acknowledged due process was not followed and agreed to prepare documents for community transfer, but trustees later refused to participate in proceedings.

Mosielele raised concerns about financial records, noting omissions from August 24, 2016, to March 31, 2018, and a lack of donor identification for 5,847,768 pula in donations. The trust also allegedly failed to provide bank statements or details on transactions totaling 259,775.94 pula during a transition period. A handover meeting was scheduled for March 6, 2026.

Why it mattersThe dispute affects beneficiaries of the Ipelego Community Trust, founded by former President Masisi, due to alleged mismanagement by its trustees.
