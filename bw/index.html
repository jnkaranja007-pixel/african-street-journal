FNB Botswana profit climbs 12% on transaction surge

Higher foreign-exchange earnings and fee-based business boosted FNB Botswana's profit before tax to P2.117 billion in FY2026.

FNB Botswana reported a 12 percent increase in profit before tax, reaching P2.117 billion for the financial year 2026. This growth was driven by increased transaction volumes and foreign-exchange earnings, which offset slower credit demand.

The bank's non-interest revenue jumped 30 percent to P2.314 billion, largely due to a 113 percent surge in foreign-exchange income to P970 million. Acting Chief Financial Officer Orapeleng Senwelo and Treasurer Tshepiso Mokgethi-Magapa highlighted the resilience of the bank's diversified business model.

Management attributed the financial performance to strong foreign-exchange income, increased digital transaction volumes, and continued momentum in fee-based services. Prudent lending practices and sound liquidity management also contributed to the results.

Botswana nominates Gevorg Tamamyan for WHO top job

President Duma Boko cited the nation's health system challenges as a reason for backing the candidate for Director-General.

President Duma Boko has formally nominated Dr. Gevorg Tamamyan for the World Health Organization's Director-General position, with the appointment to be decided at the World Health Assembly in May 2027. Boko stated that Botswana's own health system struggles highlight the need for effective global health leadership, emphasizing access to healthcare and institutional effectiveness in his nomination letter to the WHO Executive Board chairperson.

Boko explained that Botswana supports a stronger WHO, not a weaker one, during a time when multilateral institutions face strain. He met Dr. Tamamyan and confirmed his commitment to underserved communities is genuine, leading to Botswana's endorsement. The President pledged that both Botswana and Tamamyan will adhere to the election's code of conduct.

Dr. Tamamyan is one of seven candidates announced by the WHO on October 29. A candidates' forum is scheduled for November 18-19 in Geneva. The Executive Board will select five candidates in early 2027, with the final appointment made by member states in May 2027, and the new Director-General taking office on August 16, 2027.

Why it mattersBotswana's citizens will be affected by the effectiveness of the World Health Organization's leadership in addressing healthcare needs, particularly for those struggling to access care.

Stanbic launches P1.37 billion IPO on BSE

The bank offers 20 percent of its shares for sale, aiming for a 7 December 2026 listing.

Stanbic Bank Botswana has launched an Initial Public Offering (IPO) to sell 20 percent of its shares, valued at approximately P1.37 billion. The offer, which began on 30 October, involves up to 319.36 million shares priced at P4.30 each and is scheduled to close on 27 November.

This move by Stanbic signals a potential end to a nearly nine-year drought of new equity listings on the Botswana Stock Exchange (BSE) since CA Sales Holdings listed in November 2017. The bank anticipates its Main Board listing on 7 December 2026.

Stanbic's IPO follows Kwa Nokeng Oil Holdings, which is set to list on 10 November after its offer was oversubscribed. Continental Reinsurance Holdings also has a P2.13 billion public offer with an expected listing on 10 December, making it a busy period for the BSE.

Why it mattersBotswana's stock exchange is experiencing a surge in new company listings, with Stanbic Bank Botswana's P1.37 billion IPO being a significant event.

Botswana women's football faces funding gap

The launch of a 20-team Women's National First Division is threatened by a lack of sponsorship and infrastructure.

Botswana's women's football is growing, but a funding shortfall could halt progress. Clubs need more sponsorship and better playing facilities, according to Tsholofelo Setlhoko, Head of Women’s Football at the Botswana Football Association (BFA). The recent introduction of a 20-team Women’s National First Division, split into northern and southern conferences, provides a structured platform for more clubs and players.

Setlhoko stated that sustaining this development requires clubs to secure necessary resources. She met with African Club Association (ACA) representatives to explore support avenues. The BFA official highlighted challenges in sponsorships, infrastructure, and suitable playing environments for women players. Increased league size raises costs for transport, equipment, coaching, facilities, and player welfare.

The BFA president, Tariq Babitseng, who also leads COSAFA, has discussed a regional men's club tournament. Women's football already has regional club competitions that allow teams to compete beyond domestic leagues. Setlhoko believes these opportunities sharpen players' skills and raise the standard of play.

Business · 2026-11-05

Botswana households spend 63% of income on debt

Bank of Botswana survey shows debt repayments absorb earnings as total household debt rose to P70.3 billion in 2025.

Botswana households are now dedicating 63 percent of their income to debt servicing, according to the Bank of Botswana’s 2025/26 Household Indebtedness Survey Report released on October 22, 2026. This high ratio reflects the pressure from rising borrowing costs, stagnant wages, and ongoing living expenses on consumers.

The survey found that total household debt grew by 2.5 percent to P70.3 billion in December 2025, representing 26.1 percent of the country's gross domestic product. While the central bank does not currently view this as a systemic threat, it noted that borrowers are increasingly vulnerable to financial shocks. Debt-to-income ratios for both men and women have risen, with those aged 40-49 facing the highest average ratio at 52.7 percent.

Signs of repayment stress are evident, as the average default rate reported by banks more than doubled from 2 percent in 2024 to 4.7 percent in 2025. The Bank of Botswana stated that household leverage has increased relative to income, reducing resilience to income, interest rate, and cost-of-living shocks.

Why it mattersBotswana households are struggling with high debt repayments, impacting their financial resilience.
