Zotus Group clears international money-laundering checks for Kalahari City

The Botswana Investment and Trade Centre says the developer's accreditation is not yet final project approval.

Zotus Group, the developer behind the proposed Kalahari City, has been screened against international anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing databases, the Botswana Investment and Trade Centre (BITC) told The Botswana Gazette. Officials from the centre, Botswana's High Commission in London and security agencies also visited the company's offices in Manchester, United Kingdom.

BITC said the checks, run through the World-Check system, looked for litigation, sanctions and reputational risks. The project is estimated to draw between US$30 billion and US$50 billion in investment and create more than 20 000 jobs, with Zotus City Development Group holding 51 percent, government 10 percent and strategic investors 39 percent.

The centre stressed that its accreditation of the project as a strategic investment initiative is not final approval. It said the project has moved into a structured facilitation and due diligence phase under government oversight.

Why it mattersInvestors in the project face a multi-stage process before any final approval, with the government holding a 10 percent stake under the proposed structure.

Botswana documentary Paradise in Peril earns two festival nominations

The film, which examines wildlife conservation and the communities living alongside it, is up for Best Editing and Best Cinematography at the 2026 Kingdom in the Sky Conservation.

The local documentary Botswana: Paradise in Peril has been nominated for Best Editing and Best Cinematography at the 2026 Kingdom in the Sky Conservation International Film Festival (KITS-CIFF). The nominations put a Botswana-made film about conservation in front of an international audience.

Producer Moabi Mogorosi said in an interview that the nominations give the film visibility, which he called good for the work and for the people featured in it. The documentary was commissioned by the Botswana Wildlife Producers Association (BWPA) and produced by the local company ABI Films. It looks at wildlife conservation successes alongside the challenges faced by communities living with wildlife.

The film examines regulated sustainable hunting in marginal areas and how rural communities benefit from those programmes. It also takes up the international debate over hunting as a conservation tool, challenging calls from anti-hunting lobby groups for blanket bans. Mogorosi said the recognition should encourage filmmakers to keep making advocacy productions that speak for the people.

Why it mattersRural communities that take part in regulated sustainable hunting programmes are the people the film presents, and the festival recognition gives their side of the wildlife debate a wider audience.

Botswana singers Mpho Sebina and Nnunu Ramogotsi head to Sandton

The two artists will perform their original music live at Artistry in Sandton on March 26, 2027, under the Music & Diplomacy initiative.

Botswana singers Mpho Sebina and Nnunu Ramogotsi are scheduled to perform at Artistry in Sandton, Johannesburg, on March 26, 2027. The showcase is meant to put Botswana's sound in front of a new audience in one of the region's hardest music markets.

Ramogotsi told Time Out that the invitation was a major step for them as they try to break into the South African market, which she described as very hard to penetrate. The appearance falls under the Music & Diplomacy initiative, which uses music to connect artists, audiences, cultural institutions and businesses across borders. Both artists will perform original material live.

A joint collaboration is not confirmed, but Ramogotsi said collaborations are being lined up at home and abroad. She is currently promoting her album Sephonono, which won Best Jazz Album of the Year 2026 and earned her the Best Achievement Award under BOMU.

Why it mattersSebina and Ramogotsi gain a showcase before a South African audience, a step toward building a fan base beyond Botswana.
