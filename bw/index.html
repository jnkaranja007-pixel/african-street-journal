Boko backs Tamamyan for WHO leadership role

President Boko nominated Dr Gevorg Tamamyan, citing a need for effective global health leadership.

President Duma Boko has nominated Dr Gevorg Tamamyan for the World Health Organization's top position. Boko formally proposed Tamamyan for the role of Director-General at the World Health Assembly in May 2027 in a letter to the WHO Executive Board chairperson.

The President stated Botswana's health-system challenges underscore the need for effective global health leadership. He wrote that multilateral institutions under strain require a demonstrably effective WHO, not a diminished one. Boko's endorsement centres on Tamamyan's commitment to underserved communities and improving healthcare access.

Boko linked the nomination to Botswana's experience confronting its own health system limitations. He framed the endorsement around the WHO's ability to respond to countries' healthcare needs, particularly for those struggling to obtain care.

Why it mattersPresident Boko's nomination of Dr. Gevorg Tamamyan for WHO Director-General aims to improve global health leadership and healthcare access for underserved communities, reflecting Botswana's own health system challenges.

4,274 foreigners gained Botswana citizenship via marriage

Zimbabweans and South Africans married to Batswana citizens acquired citizenship between 2018 and 2026.

A total of 4,274 Zimbabwean and South African nationals became Botswana citizens through marriage between 2018 and 2026.

Minister of Labour and Home Affairs Pius Mokgware announced the figures in Ntlo ya Dikgosi, stating that 2,947 were Zimbabweans and 1,327 were South Africans. Mokgware attributed the numbers to geographical proximity and shared borders facilitating relationships.

Foreign spouses must meet naturalisation requirements, including at least five years of residence and lodging a declaration of intention to apply for citizenship two and a half years prior.

Katlego Twala wins Tokyo art award

Botswana artist Katlego C.L. Twala receives Sponsor Award at inaugural TTC African Art Award in Tokyo.

Botswana artist Katlego C.L. Twala has won the Sponsor Award at the first Toyota Tsusho Corporation (TTC) African Art Award ceremony held in Tokyo, Japan.

The award recognises Twala's artistic excellence and her work connecting African perspectives with the global art scene. She was among several young African artists from countries including Botswana, South Africa, and Zimbabwe who exhibited their work in Tokyo.

TTC established the award to support artists across Africa and provide a platform for them to showcase their creations in Japan. Twala previously won the New Emergence Art Prize in 2024 and had a sold-out solo booth at Latitudes Art Fair in 2025.

Why it mattersThe award puts a Motswana artist's creative vision into an international conversation.

Botswana cookbook named global finalist

Kenneth Middleton's debut cookbook, Paradise, is one of eight finalists for the 2026 Gourmand World Cookbook Awards.

Kenneth Middleton's cookbook, Paradise, has been named one of eight global finalists for the 2026 Gourmand World Cookbook Awards' First Cookbook category. The book, published by Penguin Random House South Africa, blends local Botswana dishes such as seswaa with international recipes including Vietnamese pho and Korean fried chicken.

Middleton expressed optimism about the award, thanking his publishers and supporters. "Until then, this office remains optimistically disposed towards a favourable verdict and we place our fate in the hands of providence," he stated. The cookbook celebrates indulgent, guilt-free cooking with an expansive identity that keeps regional flavours prominent.

The winners of the Gourmand World Cookbook Awards will be announced in May 2027. Paradise has already secured a place among the world's standout debut cookbooks, showcasing Botswana's culinary creativity internationally.

Why it mattersBotswana's culinary talent is recognised internationally through Kenneth Middleton's cookbook.
