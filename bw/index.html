Botswana raises petrol, diesel prices

Petrol and diesel prices increase on Friday due to global fuel market tensions and shipping costs.

Botswana will see higher fuel prices starting Friday, October 23, after midnight. Petrol 95 will rise by 154 thebe per litre to 1,975 thebe, and diesel 50ppm will increase by 283 thebe per litre to 2,376 thebe. Illuminating paraffin prices will remain unchanged.

Botswana Energy Regulatory Authority CEO Dr Never Tshabang announced the price hikes, attributing them to rising international fuel market costs. Global oil prices climbed due to Middle East tensions, impacting refined product prices, freight, insurance, and exchange rates for Botswana, which imports refined fuel.

The International Energy Agency reported increased shipping costs from the Middle East Gulf to Asia, more than five times pre-conflict levels. This affects diesel prices significantly, impacting transport, farms, mines, and construction companies, with potential ripple effects on food and building material costs.

Why it mattersBotswana households, transport operators, and businesses face increased costs for petrol and diesel.

Botswana warns citizens against joining Russian military

Government says Batswana who enlist in foreign conflicts risk prosecution under the Foreign Enlistment Act.

Botswana's government has warned its citizens against knowingly joining the Russian military, stating that those who enlist risk prosecution upon their return. Minister of International Relations Phenyo Butale informed Ntlo ya Dikgosi on Monday that some Batswana had been lured by job offers, while others travelled to Russia with the intention of military service.

Butale explained that voluntary involvement in foreign armed conflicts could violate Botswana’s Foreign Enlistment Act, which prohibits participation without lawful authority. He confirmed that 27 Batswana had been linked to Russian military recruitment since June 25, with eight intercepted before departure, four repatriated, and 15 still unaccounted for.

Authorities are concerned that some prospective recruits may be avoiding monitored travel routes, potentially using buses through Zambia to reach Russia. This makes interception more difficult for officials.

Why it mattersBotswana citizens who join foreign militaries face prosecution at home.

Botswana gospel singers collaborate with Dr Tumi

Hosanna Gospel Singers and South African artist Dr Tumi recorded a live album to boost Botswana's music scene.

Botswana's Hosanna Gospel Singers joined forces with South African artist Dr Tumi this past weekend at the GICC for a live album recording called The Encounter.

The recording featured 23 choir members and a six-piece band, aiming to promote cross-border collaboration and elevate Botswana's gospel music on a larger stage, according to Hosanna convener Nico Matlapeng.

Dr Tumi stated his involvement was driven by the artists' passion and his desire to foster a two-way exchange, enabling South African audiences to consume Botswana's music and vice versa.

Why it mattersBotswana's gospel music artists are seeking wider regional recognition and collaboration.

Culture · 2026-10-28

book captures botswana's dance heritage

Thalefang Charles' new book documents six landmark Mophato Dance Theatre productions with photography and artist accounts.

Thalefang Charles has released a new book, Mophato: Botswana’s Story, Told in Dance, that preserves Botswana’s cultural memory through photography and storytelling.

The book covers six Mophato Dance Theatre productions, including PULA, BORWA, and TEEMANE, exploring themes like drought, migrant labour, and national identity through dance.

Charles drew on the voices of over 40 artists to reveal the creative process and vulnerability behind the performances, offering readers a behind-the-scenes look.

Why it mattersThe book documents Botswana's cultural heritage through dance productions.
