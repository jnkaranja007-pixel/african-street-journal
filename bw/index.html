Botswana eyes 25% De Beers stake as luxury firms bid

Global luxury giants and diamond industry players are pursuing majority control of the struggling miner.

Botswana is positioning itself to acquire a 25 percent stake in De Beers as global luxury investors prepare to take control of the struggling diamond producer. The proposed ownership structure could see private investors collectively controlling 51 percent of De Beers, with Botswana, Namibia, and Angola sharing the remaining 49 percent.

The Global Diamond Consortium (GDC), led by former De Beers CEO Gareth Penny, is the preferred bidder for Anglo American’s 85 percent shareholding. Bloomberg reported that Botswana and GDC are nearing an agreement to increase the government's existing 15 percent stake to approximately 25 percent, making Botswana the largest individual government shareholder.

Swiss luxury conglomerate Richemont, owner of Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels, is in advanced discussions to join the Penny-led consortium. Individual stakes for Namibia and Angola remain undisclosed, and the allocation of shares among private investors has yet to be finalized.

Why it mattersBotswana's government is set to increase its stake in the diamond producer De Beers.

Botswana to host regional trading for Dangote refinery IPO

The Botswana Stock Exchange will act as a regional hub for investors seeking access to the US$1.6 billion refinery share offering.

The Botswana Stock Exchange (BSE) will serve as the regional trading hub for the US$1.6 billion Dangote Petroleum Refinery initial public offering (IPO). This arrangement, coordinated by the Committee of SADC Stock Exchanges (CoSSE), aims to simplify investor access to the Nigerian refinery's shares.

CoSSE chairman Collen Tapfumaneyi announced last Wednesday in Gaborone that regional exchanges will concentrate trading through Botswana, allowing investors to use domestic brokers. Access Bank Botswana will issue Botswana Depository Receipts, each representing one Dangote Refinery share, facilitating an offering of up to US$500 million with trading expected to start on 14 December 2026.

This move follows a Reuters investigation highlighting regulatory hurdles and restricted distribution channels for foreign investors in the original Nigerian IPO prospectus. Concentrating trading via Botswana could improve liquidity and retain local brokerage activity, although participating markets must still meet their own regulatory requirements.

Why it mattersBotswana's financial sector is facilitating regional access to a significant international investment opportunity.

Botswana proposes P60 billion to restore public assets

A P60 billion restoration programme aims to repair schools, hospitals, and infrastructure built with diamond wealth.

A proposed P60 billion restoration programme, dubbed Restoration Botswana, suggests repairing public assets built with diamond wealth before commissioning new projects. Statistics Botswana figures show approximately P744 billion was invested in gross fixed capital formation between 2006 and 2024, including private investment. This programme aims to prevent the squandering of diamond-era wealth through the deterioration of infrastructure like railways and classrooms.

The plan estimates a P60 billion allowance over five years, or P12 billion annually, with lower and higher scenarios ranging from P40 billion to P90 billion. These figures are provisional and require validation against asset inventories and condition surveys. The strongest published sector anchor is the approximately P7 billion needed to bring public schools to minimum acceptable standards.

The 2026/27 Budget proposed P1.85 billion for maintaining and rehabilitating existing public assets, falling short of the estimated P10 billion needed annually. The proposed strategy suggests making asset management the first claim on 50% of the development spending envelope, shifting resources from new construction to preserving existing assets.

Why it mattersBotswana's public assets, including schools and hospitals, are at risk of deterioration, impacting public services.

University of Botswana to award three honorary doctorates

Roy Sesana, Matshidiso Moeti and Justice Oagile Dingake will be honoured for their work in human rights, public health and justice.

The University of Botswana (UB) will award honorary doctorates to three prominent figures during its 44th graduation ceremony from October 21-23. Roy Sesana, Matshidiso Moeti, and Justice Professor Oagile Bethuel Key Dingake are recognised for their significant contributions to human rights, public health, and justice.

Roy Sesana will receive a Doctor of Letters for his advocacy for the Basarwa communities' rights and cultural identity, particularly concerning land rights in the Central Kalahari Game Reserve. Dr. Matshidiso Moeti, former WHO Regional Director for Africa, will be awarded a Doctor of Science for her work in public health across the continent. Justice Dingake, a judge in Papua New Guinea, will receive a Doctor of Laws for his contributions to the judiciary, legal scholarship, and human rights.

The university stated these honours acknowledge the recipients' dedication to advancing societal well-being and justice. Moeti's tenure at the WHO, from 2015 to 2025, focused on regional health challenges and preparedness. Dingake's judicial career includes service on the Residual Special Court and work on constitutionalism and access to justice.

Why it mattersThree leaders will receive honorary doctorates from the University of Botswana for their contributions to human rights, public health, and justice.

Botswana reaffirms one-China stance amid Taiwan rebuke

Taiwan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs accused China of spreading false statements and warned Botswana against blindly following Beijing.

President Duma Boko reaffirmed Botswana's recognition of Beijing as China's sole legitimate government last Thursday, a stance that has drawn sharp criticism from Taiwan.

Taiwan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs accused China of using bilateral talks to spread false statements that denigrate Taiwan's sovereignty. The ministry warned Botswana to maintain objectivity and neutrality and not to underestimate the potential threat China's actions pose to regional peace.

On Tuesday, October 13, Botswana and China issued a joint declaration elevating relations to a comprehensive strategic cooperative partnership, which describes Taiwan as part of China and rejects any form of Taiwanese independence.

Why it mattersBotswana's reaffirmation of the one-China principle and its joint declaration with China have led to a diplomatic rebuke from Taiwan.
