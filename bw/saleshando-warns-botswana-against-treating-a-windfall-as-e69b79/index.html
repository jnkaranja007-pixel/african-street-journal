Saleshando warns Botswana against treating a windfall as reform

The opposition leader said a P7.3 billion central-bank distribution accounted for most of the deficit improvement, while debt is projected to reach 44.66% of GDP by March.

Source: Weekend Post · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Dumelang Saleshando, Botswana's Leader of the Opposition and Maun North MP, urged the government to tackle immediate economic pressures in his response to the State of the Nation Address in the National Assembly on 11 November. He argued that stronger growth figures reflected a recovery in diamond mining, while households were still under pressure.

Of the deficit's fall from P26.35 billion, or 8.9% of GDP, to roughly P9.3 billion, or 3.1%, Saleshando said P8.1 billion came from a higher revenue estimate. Most of that, he said, was a P7.3 billion one-off distribution from the Bank of Botswana, which he called good fortune rather than reform. He also cited a 7.2% fall in real household consumption over the year, attributing that figure to Econsult.

Saleshando warned that the country's public debt is projected to climb to 44.66% of GDP by March 2027, past the 40% ceiling Parliament was asked to raise to 60. He added that diamonds still provide about a third of government revenue and three-quarters of foreign exchange.
