Botswana's budget rated moderately job-friendly, with private firms key to lasting work

A Botswana Institute for Development Policy Analysis policy brief says the 2026/27 budget's job impact depends on whether private firms turn public spending into durable.

Source: Weekend Post · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

The Botswana Institute for Development Policy Analysis (BIDPA) rates the 2026/27 national budget as "moderately" employment-oriented. In Policy Brief No. 22, researchers Masedi Motswapong and Lillian Mookodi assess whether spending weighted toward concrete and wages can produce durable, privately financed jobs.

The brief puts the labour force at roughly 1.17 million, with 287,060 people actively seeking work. Unemployment stands at 27.6%, up from 25.9% in 2023, and the extended rate, which includes 76,418 discouraged workers who have stopped looking, is 32.5%.

Youth unemployment for ages 15–35 rose to 34.4% in 2023, and nearly 41.3% of young people are not in education, employment or training. The brief says the budget can invite, but not command, the private-sector hiring on which lasting job creation depends.

Why it mattersJobseekers, especially young people, depend on private firms hiring, which the brief says the budget can encourage but not guarantee.
