Aterian pinpoints four copper-silver targets in Botswana's Kalahari belt

Drone surveys and AI-assisted geophysics flagged favourable geological structures across a 20-kilometre licence corridor, though mineralisation is not yet confirmed.

Source: Weekend Post · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

London-listed exploration company Aterian has identified four priority copper-silver targets within a 20-kilometre corridor at prospecting licence PL199/2025 in Botswana's Kalahari Copper Belt. The targets came out of drone-based magnetic surveys and three-dimensional geophysical interpretation, which revealed structures the company considers favourable for mineralisation.

Geological modelling points to fold structures between 10 and 200 metres deep, in rocks believed to belong to the D'Kar Formation, which hosts several established copper and silver deposits elsewhere in the belt. Charles Bray, Aterian's executive chairperson, called PL199 an emerging copper-silver opportunity, with the company's work with Lithosquare, an artificial intelligence partner, paying for the programme in full.

Aterian plans induced polarisation and resistivity surveys to sharpen the structures and rank the strongest targets before drilling. Some targets are interpreted as relatively shallow. The company has stressed that mineralisation has not yet been confirmed, and identifying a target is not the same as finding an economically viable deposit.

Why it mattersShareholders of Aterian would gain substantial value only if a commercially significant copper or silver discovery is made at PL199, which the company says is not yet confirmed.
