Botswana's diamond naming campaign faces US regulatory hurdle

Lab-grown diamond producers are invoking the US Federal Trade Commission's existing guidance as Botswana seeks to distinguish mined stones from laboratory-made alternatives.

Source: The Botswana Gazette · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Botswana's effort to reserve the word "diamond" for natural stones faces a hurdle in the United States, where the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), the consumer regulator that oversees jewellery advertising, is scheduled to review its Jewelry Guides in 2028. The report describes the US as the world's largest diamond consumer market.

The Diamond Producers Council, which represents lab-grown diamond manufacturers, told JCK Online, an international jewellery publication, that President Duma Boko's position conflicts with US consumer protection principles. The council said Botswana cannot set the terminology for diamond sales in the United States.

The dispute follows the November 10 signing of the Paris Declaration on Natural Diamonds between Botswana and France. The FTC's 2018 revision removed "synthetic" from its recommended descriptions for laboratory-manufactured diamonds.

Why it mattersThe Diamond Producers Council is contesting Botswana's terminology campaign, while the FTC's scheduled 2028 review will consider the guidance governing US jewellery advertising.
