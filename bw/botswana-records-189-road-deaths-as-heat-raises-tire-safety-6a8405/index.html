Botswana records 189 road deaths as heat raises tire-safety questions

The government reports heat waves above 40 degrees Celsius, but the available figures do not establish a link between rising temperatures and tire-related deaths.

Source: Weekend Post · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Crashes killed 189 people and seriously injured 448 in Botswana between January and September 2026, according to figures cited by assistant transport and infrastructure minister Letlhogonolo Barongwang. The period included 165 fatal crashes. The figures have not established whether rising temperatures contributed to tire-related deaths.

Barongwang named speeding, drunk driving, distraction and failure to obey traffic rules as major contributors. The report gave no separate count of crashes involving tire failure. Botswana’s government reports recurring heat waves above 40 degrees Celsius, while research shows heat speeds the chemical aging of tire components.

Low pressure and excessive loads can increase tire strain and heat, according to tire manufacturer Continental. The U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration warns that poor maintenance can lead to blowouts or tread separation.

Why it mattersThe transport ministry’s road-safety response must address 165 fatal crashes recorded in nine months, while no separate tire-failure count is available.
