Botswana’s WHO nominee sets out Africa health priorities

Dr Gevorg Tamamyan told African health ministers that stronger systems should curb outbreaks and narrow gaps in children’s cancer survival.

Source: The Botswana Gazette · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Dr Gevorg Tamamyan, Botswana’s nominee for director-general of the World Health Organization (WHO), called for stronger health systems and fairer access to treatment in a presentation to African health ministers on Monday. The Armenian paediatric oncologist was one of seven candidates presenting their priorities at the Conference on Public Health in Africa as the race ahead of the 2027 election gathers momentum.

Tamamyan said that by 2032, Ebola outbreaks should no longer pose a major threat and children with cancer in Africa should have survival rates closer to those in high-income countries. He cited current rates of 20-30% in Africa, compared with 80-85% in high-income countries, and linked the difference to health-system capacity.

He said treating childhood cancer depends on diagnostic services, specialist training, access to medicines and sustainable health financing. Ethiopian Health Minister Dr Mekdes Daba Feyssa had asked the candidates what three things they could offer Africa.

Why it mattersTamamyan’s campaign must persuade African health ministers ahead of the 2027 WHO leadership election, with his stated targets extending to 2032.
