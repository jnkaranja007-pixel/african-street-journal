Gobotswang asks court to halt disputed Land Board recruitment

The Botswana MP says the government bypassed the Tribal Land Act by using PEEPA to recruit members for land-governance bodies.

Source: Weekend Post · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Kesitegile Gobotswang, a Botswana Congress Party lawmaker and shadow minister for lands and agriculture, has asked the Gaborone High Court to rule on a government recruitment process for Land Boards. His urgent application challenges an expression of interest published by the Public Enterprises Evaluation and Privatisation Agency (PEEPA) on 7 October for three-year appointments to national and local “Land Governance” structures.

In a founding affidavit sworn on 28 October, Gobotswang argues that the process has no legal basis and that PEEPA has no authority to recruit members of bodies that administer tribal land. He says he wrote to the Minister of Lands on 11 October seeking the legal authority for the process, gave a deadline of 14 October and called for it to be suspended, but that the government did not respond before the recruitment closed.

He also argues that the criteria put too much weight on professional qualifications and too little on the knowledge of local farmers and community members. The MP warns that appointments made through the process could leave land allocations, grants and title cancellations open to legal challenge.
