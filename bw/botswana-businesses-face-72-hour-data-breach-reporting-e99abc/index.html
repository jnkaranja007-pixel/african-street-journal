Botswana businesses face 72-hour data breach reporting window

Under the Data Protection Act, controllers must alert the commission without undue delay, and high-risk breaches also require notice to affected people.

Source: Weekend Post · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Under Botswana’s Data Protection Act, 2024, the organisations that control personal data must alert the Information and Data Protection Commission without undue delay, and within 72 hours of awareness where that is feasible. Where a breach is likely to pose a high risk to people’s rights and freedoms, those people must be told too, subject to the Act’s exceptions.

Air Botswana’s suspected ransomware incident, identified on November 3, 2026, has put the rules in focus. The airline said affected internal systems held employee and internal information, while passenger booking, aircraft and flight operations were unaffected. It said it had activated its response procedures and engaged the relevant authorities.

The Act took effect on January 14, 2025. Section 63 places the duty to notify the commission on the controller, the organisation that decides why and how personal data is used, not on a service provider that processes data for it.

Why it mattersSmall businesses holding customer, staff or supplier data face a 72-hour clock to notify the commission once they become aware of a breach, where feasible.
