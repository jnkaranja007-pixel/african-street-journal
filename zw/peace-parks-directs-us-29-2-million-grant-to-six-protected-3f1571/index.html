Peace Parks directs US$29.2 million grant to six protected areas

The Rob Walton Foundation funding will support conservation management across 82,000 square kilometres in Zimbabwe and three other countries.

Source: Pindula News · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Peace Parks Foundation secured a US$29.2 million grant from The Rob Walton Foundation for conservation management in six protected areas, including Zimbabwe’s Greater Mana Pools Ecosystem in Mashonaland West Province. The funding was announced on Wednesday, 19 November 2026.

Across Zimbabwe, Angola, Malawi and Mozambique, the grant will support work covering 82,000 square kilometres. Peace Parks said the investment will strengthen park management, biodiversity protection and human-wildlife coexistence, and help establish sustainable financing to attract further public, private and philanthropic investment.

The other beneficiaries are three parks in Mozambique, the Nyika-Vwaza Complex in Malawi and Luengue-Luiana National Park in Angola. Five of the six areas already have long-term partnership agreements between Peace Parks and national governments.

Why it mattersPeace Parks’ Zimbabwe conservation work is included in funding intended to build sustainable financing for the Greater Mana Pools Ecosystem.
