Zimbabwe High Court orders ZIMRA to refund unlawful duty on US$130,000 car

The court ruled the returning resident met the ownership test and gave the revenue authority 30 days to calculate any refund due.

Source: Pindula News · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Zimbabwe’s High Court ordered the Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (ZIMRA) to determine and refund, within 30 days, customs duty unlawfully charged on Tererai Moyo’s US$130,000 Dodge Ram TRX. Justice Regis Dembure overturned the customs commissioner’s refusal to grant her returning-resident duty relief.

Moyo had paid US$148,200 in duty, value-added tax and surcharges under protest to avoid losing the vehicle. The court found she met the legal test: the car existed and had been fully paid for at least six months before she returned to Zimbabwe on 12 July 2025.

Moyo had lived in South Africa for 31 years. ZIMRA rejected her application, first citing the absence of a registration book in her name and later arguing the seller retained ownership because it appeared on shipping documents.

Why it mattersMoyo is owed a determination of any duty not legally payable, with ZIMRA required to calculate and refund it within 30 days.
