Zimbabwe's Constitutional Court postpones two challenges to the CAB3 amendment

The cases contest changes that shift the presidential vote to Parliament and extend terms from five years to seven, delaying the next polls to 2030.

Source: Pindula News · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Zimbabwe's Constitutional Court postponed two challenges to the Constitution of Zimbabwe Amendment (No. 3) Act, known as CAB3, on Wednesday, 25 November, after both sides sought more time. One case was brought by the Movement for Democratic Change (MDC), the main opposition party, and the other by two war veterans; both contest the amendment's validity.

MDC president Douglas Mwonzora is among the challengers. Their lawyers argue that the law undermines voting rights and requires a referendum, while the State insists proper processes were followed. The MDC's lawyer, Professor Lovemore Madhuku, said he had asked for more time to supplement his papers. He said the State then used that delay to seek a postponement of the war veterans' case.

The amendment, signed by President Emmerson Mnangagwa in July, replaces a direct public vote for president with a parliamentary electoral college and extends presidential terms from five years to seven, delaying the next polls to 2030. Madhuku said the war veterans' case argues that removing the right to choose the president breaches the Constitution's political rights provision. No new hearing date was reported.

Why it mattersThe delay leaves the change to the presidential vote and the 2030 election timetable in force while the court considers the challenges.
