Zimbabwe miners challenge RBZ over 30% foreign-currency surrender

The dispute pits mining companies seeking greater access to foreign currency against the central bank’s exporter-retention system.

Zimbabwe’s mining industry is pressing the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ) for greater access to foreign currency, challenging a system that requires exporters to surrender 30% of their earnings.

Under the policy, exporters retain 70% of foreign-currency earnings and hand the remaining 30% to the central bank. The dispute reflects opposing positions: miners want more access to the currency they earn, while the RBZ is determined to preserve the current arrangement.

The Zimbabwe Independent reported the dispute on 16 November. The account provides no details of a proposed policy change or a next step by either side.

Why it mattersMining companies seeking foreign currency face the existing rule to surrender 30% of export earnings to the RBZ.

Harare extends settlement regularisation deadline to 31 December

The city says thousands of properties have been recorded as it moves to a second phase focused on infrastructure and land rights.

The City of Harare has extended the Phase 1 deadline for residents, developers and beneficiaries of stands in dysfunctional and illegal settlements to 31 December 2026. Acting Town Clerk Warren Sean Chiwawa announced the extension in a public notice on 17 November, replacing the previous deadline of 17 November.

The council said the extra time followed a large number of people coming forward and requests from residents’ associations, civic organisations and development partners. It reported that thousands of properties had been recorded, accounts created and development compliance assessments carried out.

The city said Phase 2 would begin immediately, shifting from paperwork to physical development in areas that meet regularisation requirements. Planned work includes water and sewer infrastructure, roads and council-supervised planning; the phase also covers land tenure, value and use, with the council seeking to secure recognised ownership and address multiple allocations and land baronism.

Why it mattersResidents and developers have until 31 December 2026 to meet council requirements, while Phase 2 covers infrastructure and recognised land tenure.

Zimbabwe forecasters warn of storms and possible hail through Friday

The Meteorological Services Department expects scattered thunderstorms, strong winds and localised downpours across the country, with the wettest conditions from Sunday to Friday.

Across the country, from Sunday through Friday, the Meteorological Services Department (MSD) has forecast scattered thunderstorms, strong winds and possible hailstorms, with localised downpours at their heaviest.

Harare Metropolitan, Matabeleland North, Manicaland and all Mashonaland provinces face the first wave on 15 and 16 November, and the system then reaches every area from 16 to 20 November, according to an advisory issued with the Department of Civil Protection. The MSD warned of lightning, flash floods in low-lying areas, river basins and urban centres, and winds that can damage crops, roofs and structures.

People are told to shelter indoors during lightning, avoid sailing in storms and postpone trips where possible. Households are urged to secure loose items, repair rooftops and keep away from windows and glass doors.

Why it mattersFarmers whose crops and roofing lie in the path of the strong winds are told to secure loose items and repair damage before the storms reach them.

Former Highlanders vice-chairman survives two road accidents in four days

Modern Ngwenya escaped injury in both crashes, including one near Kwekwe while he was travelling to Harare on Monday morning.

Modern Ngwenya, a former vice-chairman of Zimbabwe’s Highlanders Football Club, survived two road accidents within four days, escaping physical injury in both. The first happened on Thursday on the Gweru-Bulawayo Road as he and veteran journalist Ezra “Tshisa” Sibanda returned from Lower Gweru; their vehicle hit a stray animal.

On Monday morning, about 30 km outside Kwekwe, Ngwenya was driving to Harare when he swerved to avoid an oncoming vehicle and lost control, Sibanda said in a Facebook post. The car left the road, returned to the highway and veered off again, hitting small trees before stopping about 50 metres from where Ngwenya first lost control. He was travelling at about 100 km/h.

Sibanda said Ngwenya was shaken and traumatised, and expressed relief that he had survived both crashes. The incidents come amid deaths in the Highlanders community this year, including four club figures killed in a July road accident and six supporters killed in August.

Why it mattersNgwenya was left shaken and traumatised after both crashes, and Sibanda said he wished him a speedy recovery.
