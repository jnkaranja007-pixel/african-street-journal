Munetsi scores, Wolves lose 4-2 to West Ham

Zimbabwean midfielder Marshall Munetsi scored his second league goal of the season but could not prevent Wolves from falling to a defeat against West Ham United.

Zimbabwean international Marshall Munetsi found the net for Wolverhampton Wanderers on Tuesday night, but his goal was not enough as the team lost 4-2 to West Ham United at the London Stadium. Munetsi scored in the 44th minute, bringing the score to 2-1 at halftime and offering his team a glimmer of hope after an early deficit. Despite his contribution, Wolves conceded two more goals in the second half, ultimately sealing their defeat.

The defeat saw Wolves' second goal come from a West Ham own goal, but it did not alter the final outcome. This latest strike marks Munetsi's second league goal of the current season. He has been a key player for Wolves since rejoining them in July after a loan spell at Paris FC, and the club retained him for their 2026/27 campaign with ambitions of returning to the top flight.

Munetsi's consistent form is a positive sign for the Zimbabwe national team, the Warriors, ahead of upcoming Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers. New coach Ramon Catala, who recently took charge, is expected to closely watch the performances of European-based players like Munetsi as he prepares the squad. Catala has emphasized the importance of these players due to their higher level of competition.

Why it mattersZimbabwean international Marshall Munetsi's performance is relevant to the national team's preparation for upcoming Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers.

Police Seek Help Identifying Seven Murder Victims in Harare

Seven unidentified bodies, including a boy aged 12-13, are held in Harare and Chitungwiza mortuaries following separate incidents between June 2025 and May 2026.

Zimbabwe Republic Police are appealing for public assistance to identify seven murder victims whose bodies are currently at mortuaries in Harare and Chitungwiza. The victims, found in separate incidents between June 2025 and May 2026, include a 12 to 13-year-old boy found in a pumpkin field near the Mukuvisi River. Other victims include a woman aged 24-27 found at Pioneer Cemetery, a man aged 25-35 found on Baines Avenue, and another man aged approximately 30 found in Damafalls Phase 1, Ruwa.

Additional unidentified bodies include a man approximately 25 years old found in Graniteside Industrial Area, another man around 30 years old found in Muguta, Epworth, and a seventh man found in a makeshift shack in Brookdale, Bindura. Police spokesperson Commissioner Paul Nyathi stated that investigations are ongoing and urged individuals with missing relatives to check if the descriptions match any of the deceased.

The bodies are being held at Sally Mugabe Hospital Mortuary, Parirenyatwa Group of Hospitals Mortuary, and Chitungwiza Hospital Mortuary. The public can provide information by contacting the National Complaints Desk at 0242 703 631 or via WhatsApp at 0712 800 197 to help establish the identities of these individuals.

Why it mattersThe unidentified murder victims and the ongoing police investigation directly impact the safety and security concerns of residents in Harare and surrounding areas.

Credsure Loses Bid to Halt US$1.25 Million Cannabis Financing Lawsuit

The High Court ruled that Harare Receivables Exchange presented enough evidence for a trial concerning a failed medicinal cannabis export venture.

Credit Insurance Zimbabwe Limited (Credsure) will face a US$1.25 million lawsuit after the High Court dismissed its application to halt proceedings. The case, brought by Harare Receivables Exchange, stems from a medicinal cannabis export venture involving Nhimbe Fresh Exports. Harare Receivables advanced funding against expected proceeds from a cannabis supply deal, with the transaction insured by Credsure. The financier alleges it released funds based on assurances from Credsure that no circumstances would lead to a claim rejection.

The dispute escalated when the cannabis buyer failed to pay, and Credsure subsequently denied the claim, citing Nhimbe Fresh's failure to meet policy conditions, including a cross-company guarantee. Harare Receivables had initially sued both Credsure and Nhimbe Fresh for US$1.875 million. Nhimbe Fresh defaulted and a judgment was entered against it for US$1.875 million plus monthly interest.

Harare Receivables continues to pursue Credsure, arguing the insurer's assurances induced the release of US$1.25 million. Justice Joseph Chilimbe noted that Credsure had raised concerns about policy non-compliance with Nhimbe Fresh on November 4, 2022, but three days later provided assurances to Harare Receivables that no adverse circumstances were known. The judge stated Credsure must explain this apparent contradiction during the trial.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the lawsuit involves a significant financial dispute over insurance claims related to a failed export venture, impacting confidence in trade finance arrangements.
