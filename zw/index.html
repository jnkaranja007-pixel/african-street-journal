Reserve Bank reports Zimbabwe Gold inflation lowest since 1980

Zimbabwe's annual inflation rate, measured in Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG), dropped to 2,9% in August, marking the lowest single-digit outturn in 46 years.

The Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ) announced on Tuesday that the annual Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG) inflation rate fell to 2,9% in August, a 0,3 percentage-point decline from 3,2% in July. This figure represents the lowest single-digit inflation recorded since 1980, which Governor John Mushayavanhu called a "milestone achievement" and evidence of effective policies.

Mushayavanhu stated that the low ZiG inflation demonstrates the success of prudent policies in delivering price, currency, and exchange rate stability. The central bank has focused on stabilizing inflation since April 2024, when Mushayavanhu took office. The US dollar inflation rate stood at 3,1% in August, indicating that prices of domestic goods and services have increased at a similar pace in both ZiG and US dollar terms since August 2025.

The bank noted that the muted impact of a global oil price shock on Zimbabwe's inflation, which saw ZiG inflation increase from 4,1% in January 2026 to 4,8% in April, benefited from well-anchored inflation expectations. This contrasts with other sub-Saharan African countries, which recorded average increases of between two to four percentage points in domestic inflation due to the Middle East conflict.

Why it mattersBusinesses and consumers in Zimbabwe are directly affected by the low inflation rate, which supports greater predictability in business planning and investment, and makes pricing and accepting payments in either foreign currency or ZiG more indifferent.

Mnangagwa's daughter-in-law claims US$9.3 million assets were gifts

Kelsea Tafirenyika, daughter-in-law of President Emmerson Mnangagwa, told a court that luxury properties and vehicles in a US$9.3 million money-laundering case were gifts from.

Kelsea Tafirenyika will remain in custody until Wednesday after her bail hearing was postponed. She informed the court that the assets, including luxury properties and vehicles, were gifts from her husband, Collins, who is President Emmerson Mnangagwa's son. Her lawyers stated that her husband's extensive business empire allowed him to afford these assets.

Investigating officer Detective Assistant Inspector Gift Machipisa countered this claim, telling the court that police have evidence suggesting otherwise. This evidence includes a Victoria Falls property where the owner allegedly confirmed receiving cash directly from Tafirenyika. The State alleges Tafirenyika acquired assets worth US$9,353,750 in Zimbabwe using proceeds from alleged dealings in pethidine hydrochloride, morphine sulphate, and dagga.

Machipisa also stated that police secured title deeds, Deeds Registry searches, and vehicle registration records for nine vehicles, along with an alleged forged identity document. He added that about ten witnesses are expected to testify, and police are still gathering evidence. The State also claims Tafirenyika owns 13 properties in Dubai and South Africa, which Machipisa cited as a flight risk.

Why it mattersThis event affects citizens by highlighting allegations of high-level financial misconduct and potential drug-related activities, which can erode public trust in institutions and the rule of law.

Zimbabwe sees capital outflows surge 91% to US$866 million

Zimbabwe's capital outflows nearly doubled in the first half of 2026, reaching US$866 million as companies increased external debt repayments, according to the Reserve Bank of.

Capital outflows from Zimbabwe surged by 91% to US$866 million in the first half of 2026, up from US$453,6 million during the same period last year, the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ) reported in its mid-term monetary policy review. This increase contributed to a sharp rise in total foreign payments, which reached US$7,3 billion through banks during the six months to June.

Outward capital remittances accounted for 12% of these foreign payments, compared to 9% a year earlier. The RBZ also noted a 162,1% increase in disinvestments to US165,5 million and a 74,7% rise in foreign investment outflows to US79,6 million. External loan repayments also grew by 80% to US$621 million over the period.

While external loan repayments were a major factor, the significant increases in disinvestments and foreign investment outflows suggest that companies are moving more capital offshore instead of reinvesting earnings locally. This trend occurs as the country seeks to attract foreign direct investment and encourage businesses to retain capital within Zimbabwe.

Why it mattersZimbabwean businesses and the economy are affected by the significant outflow of capital, which reduces funds available for local investment and growth.

Mnangagwa faces court challenge over Senate appointments

Two applicants have dragged President Emmerson Mnangagwa to the Constitutional Court, alleging he packed the Senate with politicians instead of independent professionals.

President Emmerson Mnangagwa is being challenged in the Constitutional Court over his appointment of 10 senators. Thamsanqa Ncube and Ibhetshu LikaZulu filed an urgent application, arguing that the nominees are predominantly Zanu-PF politicians, which they claim defeats the purpose of Constitution Amendment No. 3.

The applicants assert that Section 120(1)(e) of the amended Constitution requires the President to appoint senators based on their “professional skills and other competencies” after consultation with the National Assembly. They contend that the current nominees, including Jabulani Sibanda and Kudakwashe Tagwirei, do not meet this criterion.

Ncube and Ibhetshu LikaZulu are seeking an order to block the swearing-in of the nominees and to declare their appointments null and void. They also challenge the consultation process, claiming there was no meaningful debate or opportunity for input when the nominees were announced in Parliament.

Why it mattersThis legal challenge could alter the composition of the Senate, directly affecting the legislative process and the balance of power for all citizens.

Harare High Court awards Cranborne house to diplomat's ex-wife

A Harare High Court judge ruled that Marie Therese Kadurira should receive the Cranborne property after her 30-year marriage to Nathan Amos Kadurira ended, citing injustice in.

The Harare High Court has awarded a Cranborne house to Marie Therese Kadurira, the estranged wife of a diplomat, following the dissolution of their marriage of over three decades. Justice Philipa Phillips granted the divorce and ordered that House No. 6 Biddulph Road, Cranborne, become Marie Therese Kadurira's sole and exclusive property, despite her husband's claim that he purchased it before their marriage.

The court found that Marie Therese Kadurira had resided in the house with the couple's children, contributed to its upkeep and renovations, and made indirect contributions to the family throughout the lengthy marriage. Justice Phillips emphasized that financial contribution was not the only factor, noting the importance of indirect contributions and the marriage's duration.

Justice Phillips rejected Nathan Amos Kadurira's argument that pre-marital property could not be distributed under Zimbabwe's Matrimonial Causes Act, stating such property could be distributed unless it fell into specific excluded categories. The judge also noted that Nathan, a pensioner, had other living options at a Rusape complex where he operated businesses.

Why it mattersThis ruling directly affects Marie Therese Kadurira by securing her housing and financial stability after a long marriage, while Nathan Amos Kadurira loses a property he claimed as his own.
