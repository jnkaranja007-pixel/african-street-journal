EU Ambassador Katrin Hagemann Dies Suddenly at Harare Home

The European Union envoy, who began her assignment in December 2025, reportedly became ill and was found unresponsive by staff at her residence.

European Union Ambassador to Zimbabwe Katrin Hagemann has died. She reportedly became suddenly ill at her home in Harare on Thursday morning and was found unresponsive by staff. The EU delegation in Harare confirmed her death and stated that no further comment would be made at this time out of respect for her family's privacy. A book of condolences will be open to the public from Monday, September 7, to Thursday, September 10, 2026, at the EU Delegation.

Hagemann presented her credentials to President Emmerson Mnangagwa on December 11, 2025, marking the formal start of her tenure. During her brief assignment, she was a prominent voice on Zimbabwe’s governance, human rights, and economic reform agenda. She had previously served as head of the Central Africa division in the European External Action Service and held diplomatic posts in Kenya and Botswana.

The ambassador was actively engaged in strengthening EU-Zimbabwe relations, including trade and investment. In February, she raised concerns over shrinking civic space and political repression. She also played a key role in Zimbabwe’s Structured Dialogue on Arrears Clearance and Debt Restructuring, co-chairing the Governance Track. In July, she publicly addressed economic challenges, noting that corruption undermined investor confidence.

Why it mattersThe death of the EU Ambassador affects diplomatic relations and ongoing discussions on economic reform and investment in Zimbabwe.

OK Zimbabwe Restocks Shelves After Banks Unlock Credit

The retailer is reopening previously closed branches and resuming deliveries from major manufacturers following financial guarantees from local banks.

OK Zimbabwe has begun a comprehensive restocking program after securing financial guarantees from two local banks, which has restored credit access and supplier confidence. Major manufacturers like Dairibord, ZimGold, Olivine, Nestlé, and National Foods have resumed deliveries to OK Zimbabwe stores. The retailer is also reopening branches that were closed during a cash-flow crisis to increase customer traffic and re-establish its market presence.

These recovery efforts follow an agreement with trade creditors to defer repayments on outstanding debts, providing the company with necessary liquidity and operational flexibility. Corporate Rescue Practitioner Bulisa Mbano confirmed that these debt deferrals have created the space needed for the retailer to resume normal operations. Management is optimistic that these measures will stabilize the business, though the turnaround is still in its initial stages.

OK Zimbabwe entered corporate rescue in February 2026 due to a severe financial crisis marked by declining revenues and over US$30 million in supplier debts. The company struggled with pricing goods in local currency while sourcing inventory in US dollars, which eroded profit margins. Increased competition from informal vendors with lower overheads also contributed to customer loss. Investigations revealed distress asset sales and costly procurement decisions, including duplicate vehicle purchases exceeding US$560,000.

Why it mattersOK Zimbabwe's restocking program is affected by its ability to secure credit and resume normal trading after a financial crisis.

Ex-FIFA boss urges Zimbabwe to make sports facilities bankable

Fatma Samoura, former FIFA secretary-general, says Zimbabwe needs investable sports infrastructure projects, not just government funding.

Former FIFA secretary-general Fatma Samoura has urged Zimbabwe to develop commercially viable sports facilities to attract private and institutional investment. Samoura, now an Advisory Board member of the African Collaborations Group (ACG), stated that the country's infrastructure challenges stem from a lack of bankable projects rather than insufficient capital. For years, Zimbabwe has struggled to maintain stadiums meeting international standards, with several facilities previously failing CAF accreditation.

Samoura proposed that the solution lies in a new business model for sports infrastructure, moving beyond traditional government-funded refurbishments. She highlighted the ACG's Victory District model, which integrates sport and entertainment developments to generate year-round revenue. This approach aims to create self-sustaining projects that do not rely on public subsidies, fostering investor confidence through transparent regulatory frameworks and government willingness to partner with credible developers.

Implementing such integrated developments, which could include stadiums, hotels, retail outlets, and residential apartments, could transform cities like Harare. Samoura believes this would create jobs, boost tourism, support athlete development, and provide a consistent calendar of events. She also sees potential in linking sports infrastructure with Zimbabwe's tourism sector, particularly around Victoria Falls.

Why it mattersZimbabwe needs to develop bankable sports infrastructure projects to attract investment and create economic opportunities.

World Bank cautions Zimbabwe on ZiG currency transition

The international lender warned that forcing a shift to the ZiG as the sole currency could trigger capital flight and widen parallel market premiums.

The World Bank has advised Zimbabwe against a rapid transition to making the ZiG its sole currency, citing risks of capital flight and the reversal of stabilization gains. The country aims to phase out the use of dollars by 2030. The bank’s report highlights that forcing such a shift before the local currency gains credibility can destabilize the economy, referencing Zimbabwe's own experience with the Zimbabwean dollar's re-introduction in 2019.

Zimbabwe replaced its struggling dollar with the ZiG in April 2024, following multiple devaluations that fueled inflation. The World Bank emphasized that the pace and sequence of any currency transition are critical. The institution also noted that Zimbabwe has an opportunity to regain access to international debt markets and improve external financing by engaging with multilateral creditors on arrears clearance.

France and the UK have agreed to co-chair a body assisting Zimbabwe in restructuring its significant debt. The World Bank anticipates a 5% economic expansion for Zimbabwe this year, aligning with the government's forecast, but expects a slowdown in 2027 due to the projected strength of the El Niño cycle, which threatens drought in southern Africa.

Why it mattersInvestors and the government are affected by the World Bank's warning against prematurely making the ZiG the sole currency, as it could lead to capital flight and economic instability.

Proplastics profit jumps 50% on increased sales volumes

The plastics manufacturer reported a 50% rise in profit before tax, driven by a 23% increase in sales volumes despite global supply chain challenges.

Proplastics Limited saw its profit before tax surge by 50% to US$0,9 million in the first half of the year, as turnover climbed 22% to US$11,7 million on the back of a 23% rise in sales volumes. The company attributed its strong financial performance to disciplined execution and resilient demand across all market segments, alongside improved overhead control and a significant 56% reduction in finance costs due to decreased borrowings.

Despite global geopolitical tensions and supply chain disruptions, particularly affecting PVC resin supplies and raw material price volatility, Proplastics maintained its performance by activating alternative sourcing strategies. The company earned 97% of its revenue in US dollars, with exports contributing US$0,6 million, a 141% increase year-on-year. Cash generation from operations also rose 29% to US$1,6 million.

Looking ahead, Proplastics anticipates strengthening demand in the second half, its peak trading period. The company expects potential increases in product demand stemming from government and stakeholder investments in water-security projects due to an anticipated El Niño-induced drought, alongside accelerated infrastructure development across various sectors.

Why it mattersProplastics' profit increase affects investors through improved financial returns and potential for future growth, while farmers may see increased demand for products related to water security projects.
