Afreximbank, APPO launch Africa Energy Bank

The new bank aims to close the continent's energy financing gap, with its first meeting held in Abuja.

The African Export-Import Bank (Afreximbank) and the African Petroleum Producers’ Organisation (APPO) have officially launched the Africa Energy Bank (AEB). This initiative seeks to address the growing energy financing shortfall across the continent. The announcement followed the AEB's inaugural general meeting in Abuja, Nigeria, on Saturday.

Afreximbank stated on Saturday that the establishment of the Africa Energy Bank demonstrates African governments' commitment to securing their own energy future. The bank's creation is a direct response to the continent's energy financing challenges.

The AEB's first general meeting took place in Abuja on Saturday, marking a formal step in its operationalization. The bank's objective is to provide financing for energy projects within Africa.

Why it mattersAfrican governments are determined to secure their own energy future through the establishment of the Africa Energy Bank.

Potraz shifts to enabling digital innovation

Zimbabwe's telecommunications regulator will now support new digital businesses and artificial intelligence.

Zimbabwe's telecommunications regulator is changing its focus from controlling market entry to actively supporting digital businesses. The Postal and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority of Zimbabwe (Potraz) aims to attract investment, foster entrepreneurship, and speed up innovation in the face of rapid technological advancements.

Potraz director-general Gift Machengete stated that the authority is adopting a more flexible and technology-neutral regulatory approach. This shift is intended to bolster the growth of the digital economy, which includes emerging artificial intelligence (AI) businesses. Machengete noted that Potraz's mandate has expanded alongside the digital economy.

This new approach seeks to create an environment conducive to digital innovation and investment within Zimbabwe.

Why it mattersZimbabwean businesses in the digital and artificial intelligence sectors will benefit from a more supportive regulatory environment.

Invictus Energy faces funding squeeze for Musuma drilling

The Australian-listed firm has A$7,97 million in cash but quarterly outgoings of A$2,85 million.

Australian firm Invictus Energy faces a funding challenge for its Musuma-1 drilling in northern Zimbabwe. As of September 30, the company had A$7,97 million (US$5,6 million) in cash reserves.

Quarterly expenditures stood at A$2,85 million (US$2,0 million), meaning current cash could cover less than three quarters of planned spending. Invictus also reported no available loan or credit facilities at the end of the reporting period.

This situation raises questions about how the oil and gas explorer will finance its ongoing drilling programme. The company is preparing to drill its Musuma-1 exploration well.

Why it mattersInvictus Energy, an oil and gas explorer operating in Zimbabwe, faces financial challenges that could impact its drilling operations.

Bulawayo projects to create over 400 jobs

Three industrial projects, including a new shopping mall, aim to boost the city's modernisation drive.

The government announced that three major industrial projects in Bulawayo, including the new ZITF Fairgrounds Shopping Mall, will create over 400 jobs. These developments are expected to drive the city's modernisation efforts.

Information minister Zhemu Soda stated after a tour on Friday that these projects demonstrate Bulawayo's progress towards achieving upper-middle-income status. The minister's comments followed an inspection of three selected sites within the city.

The projects signal a push for economic development in Bulawayo. The government aims to uplift the city's economic standing through these new ventures.

Why it mattersOver 400 jobs are expected to be created by new industrial projects in Bulawayo, directly impacting local employment.
