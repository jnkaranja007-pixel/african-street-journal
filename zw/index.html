Zimbabwe mining ministry admits abandoning core duties

The ministry will return to abandoned mandates in 2027 as the sector aims for US$15 billion in export earnings.

Zimbabwe's mining ministry has admitted failing to perform core duties, revealing weaknesses in a sector expected to generate between US$13 billion and US$15 billion in mineral export earnings.

Permanent Secretary Thomas Utete Wushe stated the ministry will resume its abandoned mandates in 2027, indicating an institutional reset amid pressure to boost export earnings and industrial development.

The government seeks to accelerate investment and beneficiation to translate mineral wealth into economic growth and employment.

Why it mattersZimbabwe's mining sector faces implementation weaknesses due to the ministry's admitted failures, impacting its ability to generate export earnings and drive economic growth.

Zimbabwe health ministry warns of heatstroke risk

The Ministry of Health and Child Care advises vigilance as forecasts predict hotter-than-normal temperatures for the 2026/2027 season.

Zimbabweans are urged by the Ministry of Health and Child Care to protect themselves from extreme heat, as prolonged exposure to high temperatures can lead to dehydration, heat exhaustion, and potentially fatal heatstroke.

This public health advisory comes with forecasts of hotter-than-normal conditions for the 2026/2027 season, which are linked to El Niño conditions.

The ministry stated that these warmer temperatures and below-normal rainfall could increase heat exposure and water stress, putting vulnerable populations at greater risk.

Why it mattersZimbabweans face increased risks of dehydration, heat exhaustion, and heatstroke due to anticipated extreme heat.

Business · 2026-11-05

Zimbabwe gazettes Cabora Bassa petroleum agreement

The government has formalised the framework for developing the nation's petroleum resources.

Zimbabwe has advanced its Cabora Bassa oil and gas project by gazetting the Petroleum Production Sharing Agreement (PPSA). The government published the agreement in the Government Gazette last Friday under General Notice 2101 of 2026.

This formalisation follows the agreement's signing in May between the Government, Australia-based Invictus Energy, and its 80%-owned subsidiary, Geo Associates (Private) Limited. The gazetting provides public and legal recognition of the fiscal, commercial, and regulatory arrangements for the project.

The PPSA establishes the framework for developing Zimbabwe’s emerging petroleum resources.

Why it mattersThe gazetting of the Petroleum Production Sharing Agreement formalises the framework for developing Zimbabwe's emerging petroleum resources.

Education · 2026-11-05

Zimbabwe government cuts exam fee subsidies

From 2028, only BEAM beneficiaries will receive government assistance for Zimsec examination fees.

The Zimbabwean government will phase out universal subsidies for Zimbabwe School Examinations Council (Zimsec) fees, shifting costs to parents. Cabinet approved a revised framework that will maintain support for pupils in rural primary and secondary schools, satellite schools, and those under the Basic Education Assistance Module (BEAM) in 2027.

Starting in 2028, only BEAM beneficiaries will receive government assistance for examination fees. This change comes as the government cites mounting fiscal pressures and a need to prioritise vulnerable students. The move will leave other families to shoulder a greater share of national examination costs.

The government's decision to scale back subsidies means families will face increased education expenses. This policy shift impacts the financial planning for parents and guardians responsible for examination fees for their children.

Why it mattersParents and guardians in Zimbabwe will face higher education costs due to reduced government subsidies for school examinations.
