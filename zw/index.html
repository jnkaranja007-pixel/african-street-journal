Invictus Energy faces funding squeeze for Musuma drilling

The Australian-listed firm has A$7.97 million cash but quarterly outgoings of A$2.85 million.

Australian-listed Invictus Energy faces a funding challenge preparing to drill its Musuma-1 exploration well in northern Zimbabwe. The oil and gas explorer had A$7.97 million (US$5.6 million) in cash and cash equivalents as at September 30, against quarterly cash outgoings of A$2.85 million (US$2.0 million).

Invictus disclosed in its September 2026 quarterly activities and cash flow report that it had no available loan facilities, credit standby arrangements, or other financing facilities at the end of the reporting period. This raises questions about how the company will fund further exploration as its drilling programme gathers momentum.

The company's cash reserves could cover less than three quarters of expenditure at current levels.

Why it mattersInvictus Energy's limited cash reserves and lack of loan facilities may affect its planned oil and gas exploration in Zimbabwe.

Potraz shifts to enabling digital innovation

Zimbabwe's telecommunications regulator will now support AI businesses and entrepreneurship.

Zimbabwe's telecommunications regulator is changing its focus from controlling market entry to enabling digital businesses. The Postal and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority of Zimbabwe (Potraz) aims to attract investment, promote entrepreneurship, and accelerate innovation. Potraz director-general Gift Machengete stated the authority is adopting a flexible, technology-neutral approach to support the digital economy, including emerging artificial intelligence (AI) businesses.

Machengete announced this shift at the Zimbabwe-India-Africa Tech & Entrepreneurship Summit 2026. He explained that Potraz's mandate has expanded beyond traditional telecommunications regulation. The authority will now also focus on expanding connectivity, protecting data, and supporting responsible AI adoption.

This regulatory shift is intended to foster growth in the digital sector. The new approach seeks to create an environment conducive to technological advancement and new business ventures within Zimbabwe.

Why it mattersZimbabwean businesses involved in digital innovation and AI will benefit from a more supportive regulatory environment.

Bulawayo projects to create over 400 jobs

Three industrial projects, including a new shopping mall, aim to boost the city's modernisation drive.

The government announced that three major industrial projects in Bulawayo, including the new ZITF Fairgrounds Shopping Mall, will create over 400 jobs. These developments are expected to drive the city's modernisation efforts.

Information minister Zhemu Soda stated after a tour on Friday that these projects demonstrate Bulawayo's progress towards achieving upper-middle-income status. The minister's comments followed an inspection of three selected sites within the city.

The projects signal a push for economic development in Bulawayo. The government aims to uplift the city's economic standing through these new ventures.

Why it mattersOver 400 jobs are expected to be created by new industrial projects in Bulawayo, directly impacting local employment.
