Zimbabwe ranks 122nd in global rule of law index

The country's score improved by less than 1% to 0.40 on the 2026 World Justice Project index.

Zimbabwe was ranked 122nd out of 143 countries in the 2026 World Justice Project Rule of Law Index, released on Wednesday. The country achieved a score of 0.40, representing a less than 1% improvement from the previous year.

The index noted persistent weaknesses in government accountability, fundamental rights, and regulatory enforcement in Zimbabwe. Despite the marginal score increase, the nation remains poorly positioned globally in terms of the rule of law.

The World Justice Project report covers 143 countries worldwide.

Why it mattersZimbabweans are affected by the country's low ranking in the global rule of law index, which indicates weaknesses in government accountability and fundamental rights.

Bard Santner Microfinance bank unveils 13-member board

The new board will provide corporate governance oversight and protect depositors' funds.

Bard Santner Microfinance Bank has unveiled a 13-member board of directors in Zimbabwe. The board includes experienced banking executives, financial regulators, auditors, and corporate governance specialists.

The board's primary aim is to provide robust corporate governance oversight. It will also work to secure depositors' funds, which have been lost due to mismanagement and economic instability.

Zimbabwe has experienced hundreds of millions of dollars lost due to corporate governance failures and economic meltdowns. This new board seeks to prevent further losses for depositors.

Why it mattersDepositors in Zimbabwe have lost hundreds of millions of dollars due to mismanagement and economic instability.

Zimbabwe advances bid for IATF company headquarters

Afreximbank expressed satisfaction with Zimbabwe's preparations to host the Intra-African Trade Fair Company headquarters.

Zimbabwe is progressing towards hosting the permanent headquarters of the Intra-African Trade Fair Company (IATFCO). Afreximbank director Gainmore Zanamwe said the bank is satisfied with the government's preparations.

Zanamwe spoke to journalists on Tuesday evening after a meeting with Foreign Affairs and International Trade minister Amon Murwira at the Munhumutapa Offices. The bank’s satisfaction signals progress in Zimbabwe’s bid to host the IATFCO.

The Intra-African Trade Fair Company will manage the fair's operations.

Why it mattersZimbabwe is set to host the permanent headquarters of the Intra-African Trade Fair Company, positioning the country as a hub for continental trade.
