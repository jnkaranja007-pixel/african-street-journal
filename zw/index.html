RIDA Official Arrested, Charged in Lake Kariba Ferry Disaster

Ignatius Chiome, the Kariba Rural Infrastructure Development Agency (RIDA) District Coordinator, faces culpable homicide charges following the capsizing of the Mbuya Nehanda ferry.

Ignatius Chiome, 49, the Kariba Rural Infrastructure Development Agency (RIDA) District Coordinator, has been arrested and charged with culpable homicide in connection with the Lake Kariba ferry disaster. The arrest comes two weeks after the RIDA-operated Mbuya Nehanda ferry capsized on August eleven, resulting in the deaths of ninety-seven people. Chiome appeared in court and was remanded in custody, according to Zimbabwe Republic Police spokesperson Commissioner Paul Nyathi.

The ferry, which was traveling from Kariba towards Chalala, had an authorized capacity of ninety passengers but was reportedly carrying significantly more. Authorities later acknowledged the vessel was severely overloaded when it encountered rough conditions on the lake. This incident marks Zimbabwe's worst recorded maritime disaster, with seventy-seven people rescued from the tragedy.

The Mbuya Nehanda ferry served as a vital transport link for remote communities around Chalala, enabling residents to move people, fish, and other goods. Investigations into the disaster are ongoing, and Chiome has not yet entered a plea to the culpable homicide charge.

Why it mattersResidents of remote communities who relied on the ferry for transport, fish, and other goods are directly affected by the disruption of this crucial service and the loss of life.

Zimbabwe proposes US$1 million quarterly payment for SAA debt

Zimbabwe has suggested paying US$1 million every three months to settle a debt of over US$50 million owed to South African Airways (SAA), accumulated since 2020.

Zimbabwe's government has proposed a payment plan of US$1 million per quarter to address its outstanding debt to South African Airways (SAA), which totals over US$50 million. This debt stems from SAA ticket sales for flights between Johannesburg, Harare, and Victoria Falls, and has been accruing since 2020. The proposal, made in 2024, was rejected by SAA officials who considered the repayment timeline of over 16 years to be too long.

The South African Department of International Relations was scheduled to appear before Parliament on Tuesday to explain its efforts to recover these funds. SAA is owed a total of R1.4 billion by various countries, with Zimbabwe's debt forming the bulk of this amount. Former SAA chief financial officer Lindsay Olitzski stated that no funds had been received under the proposed plan.

Discussions regarding the debt have occurred between President Cyril Ramaphosa of South Africa and his Zimbabwean counterpart, Emmerson Mnangagwa, according to Newzroom Afrika. Despite these high-level talks and the long-standing nature of the debt, a business and finance writer, Ntokozo Khumalo, noted that six or so years later, nothing has been paid.

Why it mattersZimbabwean citizens and businesses face potential disruptions to air travel and economic relations with South Africa due to the unresolved debt, impacting cross-border trade and movement.

Mutapa awards US$41 million York-Sandawana road contract

Mutapa Energy Resources has appointed three contractors to upgrade and tar a 52-kilometre road linking York and Sandawana in Mberengwa, as the lithium miner develops.

Mutapa Energy Resources (MER) has awarded a US$41 million contract to three contractors for upgrading and tarring a 52-kilometre road between York and Sandawana in Mberengwa. Fossil, Masimba, and Shumba Mhazi Group (SM Projects) received different sections of the road and have been given mobilisation payments, according to MER chief executive officer Innocent Rukweza. Masimba will construct the Mutsime Bridge and work on a 17-kilometre section.

The road upgrade is part of MER's infrastructure development around its operations, including a planned US$300 million lithium concentrator plant at Sandawana, targeted for commissioning by November 2027. The project also involves relocating 104 families, with MER building seven demonstration houses before rolling out construction of the remaining homes. Each household will receive a four-roomed house, kitchen, and ablution facilities at an estimated cost of US$28 000.

MER aims to finalise all 104 houses by the end of September, with relocation of affected families to follow. More than 52 graves within the proposed plant site are expected to be exhumed around September 1, pending burial orders. The company has generated over US$80 million from ore deliveries to the Gwanda lithium plant this year and targets revenue of between US$100 million and US$140 million.

Why it mattersThe road construction and relocation of families directly affect communities in Mberengwa by changing their transport access and housing arrangements.

Blue Ribbon, 23 other millers halt Bulawayo operations over maize shortage

Twenty-four milling companies in Bulawayo have ceased operations due to a lack of locally produced maize, impacting consistent maize meal production in the southern region.

Blue Ribbon Industries and 23 other medium and small-scale millers have shut down their grain milling operations in Bulawayo, according to a letter sent to government ministries by Major (Rtd) David Moyo, chairperson of the Grain Millers Association Southern Region. The closures are attributed to a significant decline in locally produced maize available for commercial milling, affecting Matebeleland North, Matebeleland South, and Bulawayo.

The shortage has prevented millers from maintaining consistent production levels and building adequate maize reserves. Moyo's circular to the Ministry of Industry, the Ministry of Agriculture, and Bulawayo’s Minister of State stated that local maize supplies have declined, with some maize also infested by insects, increasing operational costs for millers.

Moyo also cited challenges with Statutory Instrument (SI) 87 of 2025, which set a US$40/MT price for local maize and requires upfront payment for imported maize, making it inaccessible on 30 to 60-day payment terms. He expressed concern that the Ministry of Agriculture is not prioritizing the southern region, a situation he described as a replay of the previous year.

Why it mattersConsumers in Bulawayo and the wider southern region of Zimbabwe are directly affected by reduced availability of maize meal due to the shutdown of milling operations.

Sinomine secures additional lithium export quota from Zimbabwe

Sinomine Resource Group has obtained an export quota for an additional 300 000 metric tonnes of lithium concentrate from Zimbabwe, as announced by the company.

Sinomine Resource Group has secured an additional export quota for 300 000 metric tonnes of lithium concentrate from Zimbabwe, according to the company. This follows an initial quota of 200 000 tonnes granted in April, with the new quota approved in July. Zimbabwe, Africa’s top producer of battery metal lithium, introduced export quotas in April after temporarily halting concentrate shipments in February due to alleged malpractice and leakages.

The Chinese company, which operates the Bikita lithium mine, stated in a half-year report seen by Reuters that the supply of lithium concentrate from Bikita has returned to normal after the February to April stoppages. This supply is sufficient to meet the raw material requirements of its smelting operations in China. Zimbabwe has been pushing mining firms to process more of the battery metal locally to maximize economic benefits and plans to ban the export of lithium concentrates from January 2027.

Sinomine operates two plants at Bikita with a combined capacity to produce 600 000 tonnes of spodumene concentrate and petalite concentrate. A recent technical upgrade is expected to boost Bikita’s yearly spodumene concentrate production capacity to 400 000 tonnes. Sinomine is also building a 100 000 tonne per year lithium sulphate plant at Bikita, anticipated to be completed in mid-2027.

Why it mattersThe additional export quota directly impacts Sinomine's operations and its ability to supply lithium concentrate to its Chinese smelting operations, affecting its production and revenue.
