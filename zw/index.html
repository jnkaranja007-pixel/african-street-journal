Miners press Reserve Bank for more access to foreign currency

The mining industry wants greater access to foreign currency, while the central bank is determined to keep its existing retention system in place.

Mining companies in Zimbabwe are renewing their dispute with the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe over how much foreign currency they can keep from their export earnings. The industry is pressing for greater access to foreign currency, according to the Zimbabwe Independent.

The central bank is holding to a system under which exporters keep a set share of their foreign currency earnings and hand the rest over to the Reserve Bank. The Independent reports that the mining sector's demand for wider access is at the centre of the disagreement, and that the bank is determined to keep the current arrangement.

The report sets out the tension between the two sides but does not say when the dispute is expected to be resolved or what either side plans to do next.

Why it mattersMining exporters who earn in foreign currency must decide how much they can hold onto, and the outcome of this dispute will determine the share they keep.

Zimbabwe police launch nationwide festive security blitz

The police force has stepped up road safety operations, stop-and-search patrols and crime awareness campaigns ahead of the festive season.

The Zimbabwe Republic Police has increased patrols, security blitzes and road safety enforcement across the country as preparations begin for the festive season, police spokesperson Commissioner Paul Nyathi announced on Monday, 16 November 2026.

According to Commissioner Nyathi, officers are paying particular attention to pupil safety as schools close and Zimbabwe School Examinations Council assessments wind up, warning parents that children must not travel at night on closing day.

The force will impound unroadworthy vehicles and target speeding, drunk driving, mobile phone use and dangerous overtaking, while citizens have been urged to avoid keeping large sums of cash at home to curb robberies.

Why it mattersMotorists face police impoundment for unroadworthy vehicles, and travellers are warned not to cross flooded roads during the rain season.

Zimbabwe miners forecast US$14,6bn revenue in 2027

Mineral earnings are expected to climb from an estimated US$13,6 billion this year, helped by more output and stronger commodity prices.

Zimbabwe's mining sector expects mineral revenue to reach US$14,6 billion in 2027, up from an estimated US$13,6 billion this year, NewsDay Zimbabwe reported. Gains are expected from higher production and favourable prices on international commodity markets.

Costs could eat into that growth, the report said. Electricity tariffs have risen high, production costs are climbing, taxes weigh on firms, and foreign currency is scarce, all of which put investment plans at risk. Mining executives nonetheless remain upbeat about next year.

Executives are looking for better economic conditions in 2027, along with greater mineral output, fuller use of production capacity and global commodity prices that should hold reasonably steady.

Why it mattersMining companies that draw on Zimbabwe's power grid and foreign currency now face higher costs, and their 2027 investment plans depend on those costs easing.

Bad weather blamed for Chivayo helicopter crash

A preliminary report into the crash that killed five people, including businessman Wicknell Chivayo, rules out an in-flight explosion.

Investigators say the helicopter carrying Wicknell Chivayo and four others flew into deteriorating weather before crashing on 30 September 2026 at Kudenga Farm, about 22 kilometres south-west of Marondera in Mashonaland East Province. The Air Accidents and Incidents Investigation Department of the Ministry of Transport and Infrastructural Development classified the accident as an unintended flight in Instrument Meteorological Conditions and a controlled flight into terrain.

The Bell 430, registration Z-PED and operated by Sky Helicopters Company (Pvt) Ltd, was destroyed on impact and in the fire that followed. All five occupants died, among them Chivayo's wife Lucy Chivayo, security officer Senelisiwe Magagula, and pilots Calvin Soda and Courage Mutavira. Officials found the aircraft intact until impact, with no sign of a break-up, structural fault or blast.

The Zimbabwe Meteorological Services Department had warned of thunderstorms and low cloud over the area that day. Investigators are still interviewing witnesses and the crew of the accompanying helicopter, and the wreckage has been moved to a secure facility. A final report will set out the probable cause and recommend safety measures.

Why it mattersThe final report will set out the probable cause and any safety measures for private helicopter operators flying in the region.

Security · 2026-11-17

Former Chiredzi councillor remanded over murder

Lovemore Muchono faces allegations of raping and killing a four-year-old pupil.

Lovemore Muchono, a former Chiredzi Rural District Council Ward 19 councillor who worked as a school transporter, was held in custody at the Chiredzi Magistrates Court last Wednesday. The court set 25 November as his next date, pending indictment at the High Court over the alleged rape and murder of four-year-old early childhood development learner Steadfast Makondo.

The State alleges Muchono collected the child from her home on 27 January without taking her to school, and drove her to an undisclosed location where she was sexually assaulted and killed. According to court filings, he then joined a search party for the missing learner before dumping her body in a bushy area, where it was found the following day. Medical examinations at Chiredzi Hospital confirmed sexual assault, and a post-mortem at United Bulawayo Hospitals attributed the death to suffocation.

The prosecution further states that on 2 February Muchono wrote a letter claiming the child had suffocated in his vehicle and saying he intended to take his own life. He remains in custody until the High Court proceedings.

Why it mattersParents who relied on Muchono to take their children to school now face a murder case involving a child in his care, with the High Court to decide whether he is indicted.
