Delta Corporation lifts half-year operating income 13% to US$112.2 million

Zimbabwe's largest beverage maker says domestic lager, sorghum beer and spirits volumes drove the gain, while a US$97 million tax claim hangs over the group.

Delta Corporation, Zimbabwe's largest beverage company, has posted operating income of US$112.2 million for the half year, up 13% from US$99.6 million a year earlier. Group revenue rose 18% to US$607.5 million over the six months to September 30.

Volume growth came from domestic lager, sorghum beer and spirits, with lager up 13% to 1.75 million hectolitres and Chibuku Super sales rising 27%. The company credited relative exchange rate stability, mining activity, diaspora remittances and liquidity from the agricultural marketing season, and said 97% of its revenue now comes from domestic foreign currency sales. The board declared an interim dividend of US$2.40 per share, a 20% increase.

Delta is contesting a US$97 million tax assessment from the Zimbabwe Revenue Authority covering 2019 to 2024, which the company says would have a material impact if upheld. It named economic policy stability and a review of the sugar tax as its priorities for the year.

Business · 2026-11-14

Mine director loses job reinstatement bid

High Court dismisses former Botha Gold Mine MD's application to reclaim his position.

Themba Hlongwani, former managing director of Botha Gold Mine, has failed in his attempt to get his job back at the Bindura-based company.

Justice Samuel Deme issued a 15-page judgment on November 11, striking Hlongwani and former board chairman Augustine Manyau's urgent application from the court roll with costs due to weaknesses in their case.

The two had challenged resolutions from an October 20 extraordinary general meeting that approved their dismissal and appointed Munyaradzi Nzarayapenga as interim board chairman.

Why it mattersThemba Hlongwani must accept his dismissal from Botha Gold Mine after the High Court rejected his application on November 11.

UK officials brief Zimbabwe on UN Security Council work

Two days of talks in Harare cover how the 15-member council makes its decisions before Zimbabwe takes its seat.

UK government officials are in Harare for a two-day dialogue with Zimbabwean officials on the operations, procedures and decision-making processes of the United Nations Security Council.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Trade said the talks are meant to familiarise Zimbabwe's officials with the council's work. Zimbabwe will take up its seat on January 1, 2027, after being elected to the body in June this year.

Zimbabwe will serve as a non-permanent member for the 2027 and 2028 period.

Why it mattersZimbabwe's diplomats must learn council procedures in time to sit on the 15-member body from January 1, 2027.
