Zimbabwe to roll out irrigation pumps on farms against El-Nino

The government will supply pumps to every farm with irrigation facilities, a move Agriculture Minister Auxious Masuka tied to the President's rural tractor programme.

Zimbabwe's government plans to roll out irrigation pumps on all farms with irrigation facilities across the country. The aim is to boost food production ahead of the predicted El-Nino weather phenomenon.

Agriculture, Mechanisation, Water Resources minister Auxious Masuka announced the plan in response to questions in Parliament on Wednesday. He said the programme was initiated by President Emmerson Mnangagwa and will target farms with water and irrigation infrastructure.

Masuka said the pump rollout follows the tractor programme the President started in rural areas.

Why it mattersFarms that already have irrigation infrastructure are the ones set to receive pumps, so their output is expected to hold up if El-Nino dry weather arrives.

Dolphin plans fixed wireless internet to reach areas beyond fibre in Zimbabwe

The telecoms group wants to sell broadband to households and businesses that fibre networks do not serve, a move that could add a new revenue stream for the company.

Dolphin Technologies Group is moving to add fixed wireless internet services in Zimbabwe, aimed at households and businesses in areas that fibre networks do not reach. The plan would take the company beyond its mobile virtual network operator business.

The group intends to use fixed wireless access (FWA) technology to extend internet service into locations where fibre and other technologies are unavailable. The rollout targets customers who have struggled to get reliable broadband in areas with limited infrastructure.

The expansion could open a new revenue stream for Dolphin and a larger share of the country's growing demand for internet connectivity.

Politics · 2026-11-13

Zimbabwe, UK deepen UN Security Council cooperation

UK officials are in Harare for a two-day dialogue to prepare Zimbabwe for its 2027-2028 UN Security Council tenure.

Zimbabwe and the United Kingdom are increasing diplomatic cooperation as Harare prepares for its upcoming term on the UN Security Council. Zimbabwe is set to begin its two-year tenure as a non-permanent member on January 1, 2027.

Officials from the UK government are currently in Harare for a two-day dialogue. The meetings aim to familiarize Zimbabwean officials with the operations, procedures, and decision-making processes of the 15-member Security Council, according to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Trade.

Harare was elected to the Security Council in June this year. This engagement is part of the diplomatic preparations for the country's role on the international body.

Why it mattersZimbabwean officials are being trained on UN Security Council operations ahead of their 2027-2028 tenure.
