ZIMRA sets November tax deadlines

Taxpayers must submit returns by November 5 and payments by November 10.

The Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (ZIMRA) has announced deadlines for tax returns and payments for October 2026. All taxpayers must submit their returns by November 5, 2026, according to Statutory Instrument 81 of 2025.

Payments for various tax types, including Pay As You Earn (PAYE), Withholding Taxes, Mining Royalties, and Plastic Carrier Bag Tax, are due by November 10, 2026. This notice applies to returns filed for the month ending October 31, 2026.

Other taxes with the same deadlines include Value Chain Withholding Tax, Presumptive Tax Return, Gaming Operators’ Tax, Fast Foods Tax, Remittance of Levy on Specified Minerals, Return for the Remittance of Tobacco Levy, and Presumptive Tax on Rental Income.

Why it mattersZimbabwean taxpayers must meet specific deadlines for submitting tax returns and making payments to avoid penalties.

Zimbabwe shares HIV funding model with Tanzania

The AIDS Levy is highlighted as a key mechanism for mobilising local resources for HIV programmes.

Zimbabwe shared its domestic financing model for HIV and AIDS response with a visiting Tanzanian delegation in Harare on Monday. The AIDS Levy, known as the National AIDS Trust Fund, was highlighted as a key mechanism for mobilising local resources to sustain HIV treatment, prevention, and care programmes.

Health and Child Care minister Douglas Mombeshora stated that the AIDS Levy has enabled Zimbabwe to take greater responsibility for financing its national HIV priorities amidst changing global health financing arrangements. He told the Tanzanian delegation that sustainable HIV programmes require national ownership, predictable domestic financing, strong institutions, and effective partnerships.

The exchange visit focused on sharing best practices in HIV response financing. Zimbabwe's model aims to ensure continued support for national HIV priorities through local resource mobilisation.

Why it mattersZimbabwean citizens and health programs are directly affected by the domestic financing model for HIV and AIDS response.

Victoria Falls entertainment spot ordered to lower music volume

A High Court ruling restricts Tshisa Nyama's music hours following a complaint from a local hospitality operator.

Victoria Falls' Tshisa Nyama entertainment spot must now play music at reduced volumes after a High Court ruling. The order follows a successful court application by Mosskatte Investments (Pvt) Ltd, a local hospitality operator. This decision raises questions about potential future noise restrictions for other entertainment venues in the area.

Justice Nokuthula Moyo of the Bulawayo High Court issued the order last week. Tshisa Nyama is now prohibited from playing loud music after 8pm on weekdays and 9pm on weekends. The ruling was based on complaints of excessive noise pollution from the venue located on Livingstone Way.

The court's decision could lead to similar noise control measures being imposed on other bars and nightclubs. This ruling directly impacts the operating hours and atmosphere of entertainment establishments in Victoria Falls.

Zimbabwe hosts Southern Africa Insurance Indaba

The four-day event in Victoria Falls will focus on futureproofing African insurance.

Zimbabwe will host the annual Southern Africa Insurance Indaba in Victoria Falls from November 11 to 14, 2026. The Insurance Institute of Zimbabwe (IIZ) is organizing the event at Elephant Hills Resort under the theme “Futureproofing African Insurance”.

The conference is scheduled to run for four days, bringing together insurance professionals from across the region. The IIZ is the host body for this gathering.

Separately, Southern African Development Community (SADC) health ministers will meet in the same week, from November 9 to 13, 2026, to discuss emerging health threats.

Why it mattersInsurance professionals and businesses in Zimbabwe will gather to discuss the future of the industry.
