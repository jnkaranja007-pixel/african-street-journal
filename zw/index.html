Police seek help identifying 11 bodies at Harare hospital mortuary

Police say the unclaimed bodies, recovered from road and mine accident sites and public places, are overwhelming the mortuary's capacity.

Police in Harare have appealed to the public to help identify 11 bodies held at the Parirenyatwa Group of Hospitals mortuary. Some were recovered from road and mine accident sites, while others were picked up from public places.

In a statement, police said the bodies were stretching the mortuary's carrying capacity. The appeal asks anyone with information about a missing relative or friend to come forward so the dead can be identified.

Until the bodies are identified, families cannot claim them for burial.

Why it mattersUntil families come forward, the bodies cannot be released, and the mortuary has no room to keep them.

Security · 2026-11-15

Clergyman's cyber case sent back to police

Prosecutors found no cyber report to support allegations against Batsirai Java.

Clergyman Batsirai Java, arrested on Wednesday for allegedly violating the Cyber and Data Act, has had his case sent back to police for further investigation. The state had accused Java of communicating false information to undermine public confidence in law enforcement agencies.

The prosecution referred the docket back to the police station after noting the absence of a cyber report to substantiate the case. Java was released from police custody following this decision by prosecutors, who cited insufficient supporting evidence.

The case involves allegations of spreading false information. The Cyber and Data Act in Zimbabwe governs online communication and data protection.

Why it mattersClergyman Batsirai Java was released from police custody after prosecutors found insufficient evidence to support charges under the Cyber and Data Act.

Sport · 2026-11-15

Lawyer takes Scottland FC match-fixing complaint to Parliament

Lonias Rozvi Majoni says ZIFA and the PSL have not acknowledged his formal complaints, despite a seven-day ultimatum he set them.

Harare lawyer Lonias Rozvi Majoni has petitioned Parliament over alleged integrity concerns in Zimbabwean football involving Scottland FC. He says the Zimbabwe Football Association (ZIFA) and the Premier Soccer League (PSL) have not acknowledged the formal complaints he lodged about the club's alleged misconduct.

Majoni submitted his complaint to ZIFA and the PSL on November 2, 2026. Neither body has formally acknowledged receipt, even after the seven-day ultimatum he gave them expired. He is now asking Parliament for legislative and oversight intervention on matters he says fall within the State's jurisdiction, while maintaining that football's internal disciplinary processes must stay independent.

The petition places the football governing bodies' handling of the complaint before lawmakers, with Majoni arguing the State should act where he believes the sport's own processes have stalled.
