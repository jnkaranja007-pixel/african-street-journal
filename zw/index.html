Caledonia nearly secures US$485 million for Bilboes gold project

The mining company expects cash reserves, domestic bank loans and gold-price hedging to cover the construction bill, according to UK investment bank Cavendish.

Caledonia Mining Corporation has virtually secured the US$485 million capital expenditure needed to develop its flagship Bilboes Gold Project. Cavendish, a UK investment bank, said cash reserves, domestic bank financing and gold-price hedging arrangements are expected to cover the construction bill.

The project is described as one that could transform the company, and the funding position strengthens the investment case for it. Cavendish's assessment is the basis for the report's view that the financing is close to complete.

The report does not give a construction start date or a timetable for production at the mine.

Why it mattersInvestors in the project now see a US$485 million construction bill covered mainly by cash, local bank loans and gold hedging, not new share issues.

Rautenbach renews bid for Springs Farm, threatening army's US$200m project

A US$200 million property development involving the Zimbabwe National Army and war veterans is in doubt after a long-running ownership fight over the Goromonzi farm.

A US$200 million property development involving the Zimbabwe National Army (ZNA) and war veterans faces an uncertain future. Controversial businessman Conrad Muller 'Billy' Rautenbach has renewed efforts to take control of Springs Farm in Goromonzi, the land at the centre of the dispute.

The land battle threatens to derail the ambitious project, which has been held up by a protracted legal dispute over who owns the farm. The Standard Zimbabwe reported the renewed push, and Bulawayo24 said the project's future is in question.

If the dispute is not settled, the development that the army and war veterans are counting on could stall indefinitely.

Why it mattersThe army and war veterans have a stake in the US$200 million development, and its future now depends on how the Springs Farm ownership case is resolved.

Harare police seek help identifying 11 unclaimed bodies

Parirenyatwa Hospital mortuary is at capacity due to bodies recovered from accident sites and public places.

Police in Harare are appealing for public assistance to identify 11 bodies currently held at the Parirenyatwa Group of Hospitals mortuary.

The unclaimed bodies, some recovered from road and mine accident sites and others from public places, are stretching the mortuary's carrying capacity, according to a police statement.

Authorities are urging anyone with information that could help identify the deceased to come forward to alleviate the strain on the hospital's facilities.

Why it mattersThe Parirenyatwa Hospital mortuary is at capacity, affecting its ability to handle new cases.

Lawyer Lonias Majoni asks Parliament to probe Zimbabwe football governance

Majoni's complaint to the football association and the Premier Soccer League went unanswered after a seven-day deadline lapsed.

On November 2, 2026, Harare lawyer Lonias Rozvi Majoni lodged a complaint with the Zimbabwe Football Association (ZIFA) and the Premier Soccer League (PSL), and has now taken the matter to Parliament. He wants lawmakers to investigate alleged governance and integrity problems in Zimbabwean football.

Majoni had given both bodies seven days to respond. Neither has yet formally confirmed that it received his petition, and that deadline has now passed without a reply.

The petition seeks an inquiry into alleged match-fixing and other integrity concerns within the sport.

Why it mattersIf Parliament takes up the petition, ZIFA and the PSL will have to explain their handling of the allegations in public.

Politics · 2026-11-16

Preacher Java faces charges over Tagwirei claims

Batsirai Java, 43, was arrested for allegedly publishing false statements about Kudakwashe Tagwirei's influence.

Harare preacher Batsirai Java, 43, appeared in court on Friday facing a charge of publishing false statements prejudicial to the State. Java handed himself to police on Thursday after allegedly recording a telephone conversation with Everson Mushava, who is still at large. The State claims the two published the recording on social media platforms between May 25 and May 28, 2026.

The State alleges that Java and Mushava falsely claimed that ZANU PF Politburo member Kudakwashe Tagwirei is now Zimbabwe's kingmaker and controls all government business. They reportedly claimed the President consults Tagwirei on ministerial appointments and that all ministers and senior security officials report to him. The State contends Java published these statements knowing they were false, intending to undermine public confidence in state institutions.

The case was referred back to the police during vetting at the Harare Magistrates Court due to a lack of a cyber report. Java has since been released from custody, with prosecutors ordering further investigation into the matter. The Herald and Bulawayo24 also reported on the case.

Why it mattersPreacher Batsirai Java faces charges for allegedly claiming Kudakwashe Tagwirei controls government business and ministerial appointments.
