Harare Woman Accused of Counterfeit Sugar Packaging Granted Bail

A 34-year-old Harare woman faces fraud charges for allegedly repackaging underweight sugar into fake Hulett packets.

A Harare woman, Spiwe Mutandwa, 34, appeared in court on Monday facing fraud charges and contravening the Trade Marks Act. She was granted US$200 bail by Harare magistrate Tapiwa Kuhudzai. The Zimbabwe Anti-Corruption Commission (ZACC) alleges that Mutandwa was repackaging sugar into counterfeit 2kg packets bearing the Tongaat Hulett brand without authorization. The repackaged sugar was reportedly underweight.

ZACC officers reportedly acted on intelligence received on August 29 and conducted surveillance before raiding a Southlea Park residence. A Toyota Dyna truck, registration number AGO 1885, was found at the premises. Investigators allegedly recovered a consignment of sugar in fake Hulett-branded 2kg bags, which were described as replicas of the company's packaging. The state claims the packaging was used in an alleged repackaging operation.

During the raid, investigators also seized four rolls of empty Hulett 2kg packaging, a blower machine, and other equipment. A total of 181 cases of Hulett-branded 2kg sugar were seized. Tongaat Hulett (Pvt) Ltd is listed as the complainant in the case. Mutandwa is scheduled to return to court at a later date as investigations are ongoing.

Why it mattersThe accused allegedly repackaged sugar into counterfeit branded packets, potentially impacting consumer trust and the market for legitimate sugar producers.

Student Accused of Hacking Bank, Stealing US$1.1 Million

A computer science student allegedly compromised bank systems during an internship, initiating fraudulent transactions that cost the institution over one million dollars.

A final-year Midlands State University Computer Science student faces charges of hacking into CABS bank systems and facilitating fraudulent transactions totaling US$1.1 million. Sabelo Malunga, 24, is accused of compromising the bank's IT infrastructure between November and February, during and after his internship. The prosecution alleges he downloaded remote-access software without authorization and concealed it to avoid detection.

The state claims Malunga continued to access CABS systems remotely after his internship ended, using malware to bypass internal controls and initiate fraudulent ZIPIT and VISA transactions. The breach was discovered on March 27 when VISA flagged suspicious international ATM activity. CABS moved to block accounts, but the bank had already incurred losses of US$210,500.

Further investigations on April 13 uncovered multiple malware infections on the bank's servers, identifying 1,911 fraudulent ZIPIT transactions valued at US$925,679, with funds channeled to various mobile money and bank accounts. CABS engaged a South African firm for forensic investigation, which reportedly traced the intrusion to Malunga. The total alleged loss is US$1,136,179, with no recovery to date. Malunga is in custody awaiting a bail hearing.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the alleged cybercrime resulted in a significant financial loss for the bank, potentially impacting its stability and security.

US Court Orders Lion King Composer to Pay Zimbabwean Comedian Legal Fees

A federal court ruled Lebohang Morake must pay Learnmore Jonasi $39,560 in attorneys’ fees after abandoning defamation claims over a joke.

A United States federal court has ordered South African composer Lebohang “Lebo M” Morake to pay Zimbabwean comedian Learnmore Jonasi US$39,560 in legal fees. The ruling follows Morake’s withdrawal of a multimillion-dollar lawsuit against Jonasi, who had joked about the translation of The Lion King’s opening chant. Jonasi, also known as Learnmore Mwanyenyeka, was declared the prevailing party under California’s anti-SLAPP law.

The legal dispute began after Jonasi made a humorous interpretation of the Zulu chant “Nants’ ingonyama bagithi Baba” during a podcast, offering a translation different from its traditional association with the film. Morake, involved in The Lion King’s music production, sued for defamation and misrepresentation, arguing the comedian trivialized a significant cultural composition. The court calculated Jonasi’s fees at US$39,560 but denied a request for an increased award.

Jonasi’s legal defense was significantly subsidized, with his lawyers discounting their fees to support his First Amendment free-speech rights. The court’s decision focused on Jonasi qualifying as the prevailing party under the anti-SLAPP statute, not on the linguistic accuracy of the chant’s translation. Jonasi reportedly paid approximately US$5,350 out of pocket for his representation.

Why it mattersZimbabwean comedian Learnmore Jonasi is directly affected by the court order requiring him to pay US$39,560 in legal fees to South African composer Lebohang Morake.

Afreximbank Appoints New Regional Leaders, Names Harare-Based Director

The African Export-Import Bank has appointed new senior executives to oversee regional operations, including a director for Southern Africa based in Harare.

Afreximbank has appointed Peter Adeshola Olowononi as director for Southern Africa, based in Harare. He will manage the bank’s operations, client coverage, and strategic engagement in the region. Olowononi previously served as director for client relations in Anglophone West Africa, bringing experience in transaction origination and business development.

These appointments are part of a broader leadership reshuffle aimed at expanding intra-African trade, industrialisation, and regional integration. The bank stated the changes will strengthen regional operations, improve client engagement, and enhance transaction execution across Africa and the Caribbean. Other appointments include Eric Intong Monchu as group managing director in Cairo and Roy Reid as chief operating officer of the Caribbean Office.

The bank noted that most appointees have internal experience, possessing institutional knowledge and established client relationships. Afreximbank president George Elombi stated these appointments reflect the institution's talent depth and will support the bank’s development mandate. The appointments come as Afreximbank continues to increase its role in financing trade and economic development across Africa and the Caribbean.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the bank strengthens its leadership to expand intra-African trade and economic development across key regional markets.

Zimbabwean Businesses Face Data Protection Compliance Deadline

All entities handling personal information must comply with the Cyber and Data Protection Act by September 1, 2026.

Zimbabwean companies and public agencies must ensure compliance with the Cyber and Data Protection Act and its associated regulations by September 1, 2026. This directive applies to any entity that collects or handles personal information, including details on identity, finances, health, or employment. The law mandates that these organizations align their practices with legal requirements to protect individual privacy.

According to Vengai Madzima, Senior Partner at Madzima Chidyausiku Museta Legal Practitioners, the right to privacy and data protection is a constitutional right. He emphasized that compliance requirements become more manageable when this fundamental premise is understood. Madzima stated that government agencies, businesses, universities, and financial institutions are all subject to these regulations.

The Cyber and Data Protection {Licensing of Data Controllers and Appointment of Data Protection Officers} Regulations outline specific compliance measures. Entities that fail to meet these standards before the September deadline risk legal repercussions. The Postal and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority of Zimbabwe (POTRAZ) is also involved in overseeing aspects of this regulatory framework.

Why it mattersBusinesses and government agencies in Zimbabwe are affected by the data protection laws, which require them to comply with new regulations for handling personal information.
