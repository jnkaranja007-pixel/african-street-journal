World Bank removes Zimbabwe from Fragile States list

The country's removal from the World Bank's Fragile and Conflict-Affected Economies classification signals recognition of economic reforms.

Zimbabwe has been removed from the World Bank's Fragile and Conflict-Affected Economies classification, effective July 1, 2026. Finance Minister Mthuli Ncube stated this delisting validates the ongoing economic turnaround, governance, and institutional reforms under the government's Vision 2030 roadmap. The World Bank updated its framework in July 2026, splitting the old category into two metrics: the Public FCV List and the Institutional Fragility List. Zimbabwe's omission from both removes a significant sovereign risk label.

Ncube welcomed the move as international recognition of policy consistency and macroeconomic stability, adding that Treasury remains committed to boosting institutional resilience and inclusive growth. He expressed a goal to translate this renewed global confidence into tangible investments, jobs, and improved local livelihoods. Global investment bank Citigroup also praised the country's macroeconomic stabilization, noting that investors holding outdated perceptions risk missing opportunities in the ongoing recovery.

Citigroup reported that Zimbabwe is breaking decisively with its history of hyperinflation and currency instability. The bank highlighted that investors clinging to outdated perceptions of economic turbulence risk missing out on significant opportunities created by the ongoing recovery. Ncube projected the economy to register a conservative 5 percent growth this year, following an 8 percent expansion in 2025.

Why it mattersInvestors may see reduced sovereign risk and opportunities for investment due to the country's delisting from fragile economies and macroeconomic stabilization.

Stanbic Bank Profit Rises 26% on Interest Income Growth

The bank's profit after tax reached ZiG858.4 million in the first half of 2026, driven by increased net interest income despite lower fee income.

Stanbic Bank Zimbabwe reported a 26% increase in profit after tax for the first half of 2026, reaching ZiG858.4 million from ZiG682.2 million in the same period last year. This growth was primarily fueled by a 33% rise in net interest income to ZiG1.1 billion, supported by a 16% expansion in net loans and advances to ZiG15.3 billion. The bank also saw its financial investments grow by 22% to ZiG1.1 billion.

The bank's performance was partly impacted by a 12% decline in non-funded income, which fell to ZiG1.3 billion. This decrease is attributed to the implementation of a regulatory directive on bank charges and transaction fees from April 1, 2026, and lower tobacco selling prices. However, total operating expenses were reduced by 13.6% to ZiG1.1 billion due to cost optimization and improved efficiencies. Customer deposits increased by 35% to ZiG28.2 billion.

Stanbic Bank maintained strong capital adequacy, with qualifying core capital at ZiG4.5 billion, equivalent to US$168.7 million, well above the regulatory minimum of US$30 million. The bank cited continued macroeconomic stability in Zimbabwe, characterized by low inflation and a stable exchange rate, as a supportive operating environment. Stanbic also continued its Corporate Social Investment initiatives in health, education, and the environment.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by Stanbic Bank's increased profitability and capital adequacy, which indicate a stable and growing financial institution.

Twenty-seven killed in head-on highway collision

A commuter omnibus carrying church congregants collided with a haulage truck near Makumimavi Primary School on Friday night.

Twenty-seven people died and seven were injured in a fatal road traffic accident on the Harare-Masvingo highway. The collision occurred at the 130-km peg near Makumimavi Primary School on Friday night, August 28, 2026. A Nissan Caravan NV350 kombi, carrying members of the African Apostolic Church (Mwazha) from Mvuma to Dema for a conference, was involved in a head-on collision with a Volvo haulage truck.

Zimbabwe Republic Police spokesperson Commissioner Paul Nyathi confirmed the incident, stating that the truck, which was foreign registered, was allegedly attempting to overtake another vehicle. The deceased's bodies were transported to Chivhu Hospital mortuary, awaiting identification by their next of kin. The number of passengers in the haulage truck was not specified.

The accident raises concerns about road safety, particularly the absence of a central barrier to prevent head-on collisions on this stretch of highway. The circumstances surrounding the crash, including the alleged overtaking maneuver by the truck, are under investigation by the police.

Why it mattersTwenty-seven people died and seven were injured in a head-on collision between a commuter omnibus and a haulage truck.

Zimbabwe Installs Five New Transmitters to Boost Broadcasting Reach

The government plans to install five new transmitters by year-end, aiming to increase national radio coverage to 85 percent and television to 42 percent.

Zimbabwe is set to install five new transmitters before the end of the year to expand broadcasting services nationwide. This initiative is projected to raise national radio coverage to 85 percent and television coverage from the current 38 percent to 42 percent. Information Minister Soda Zhemu announced these plans after touring the Zimbabwe Agricultural Show, emphasizing that the Broadcasting Authority of Zimbabwe, Transmedia Corporation, and Zimbabwe Broadcasting Corporation are collaborating to enhance infrastructure.

The Broadcasting Authority of Zimbabwe is acquiring modern equipment to monitor signal quality and coverage, ensuring citizens can access information and participate in national development. Minister Zhemu highlighted the recent launch of ZBC’s Over-the-Top (OTT) platform, Z+, which allows on-demand news and programs, as well as streaming of radio and television channels via mobile devices.

Transmedia Corporation has secured the necessary equipment for the five new transmitters, with installation scheduled for completion by year-end. Minister Zhemu stated that wider coverage is crucial, especially for farmers who need timely information for decision-making, particularly in light of the impending El Niño. Improved broadcasting infrastructure aims to keep citizens connected, educated, and informed to make sound decisions.

Why it mattersFarmers will be able to receive timely information for decision-making, especially concerning the impending El Niño.

Jetour Launches SUVs in Zimbabwe

Chinese automaker Jetour has introduced its Dashing, X70 Plus, T1, and T2 sport utility vehicles to the Zimbabwean market.

Chinese automotive brand Jetour has officially launched a range of sport utility vehicles (SUVs) in Harare, expanding its presence in Zimbabwe. The launch introduced the Jetour Dashing, a compact SUV; the family-oriented X70 Plus; and the rugged T1 and T2 models designed for off-road performance. Michael Hove, dealer principal of Jetour Zimbabwe, stated that the vehicles combine luxury, modern technology, and rugged performance suitable for local driving conditions.

To build consumer confidence, Jetour is offering a 10-year or one-million-kilometer engine warranty, aiming to set a new benchmark for automotive assurance in the country. Hove added that local customers will have access to a robust parts supply chain and certified technicians. Early adopter Greg Sebborn shared positive experiences, noting the vehicle's performance across various challenging road conditions over eight months of ownership.

Guest Viola Mkwamba expressed initial skepticism about new Chinese vehicles but was impressed by the Dashing's style and value. She commented that the Jetour vehicles are modern, stylish, and luxurious while remaining suitable for local road conditions, aligning with her view that a car should reflect personality and lifestyle.

Why it mattersZimbabwean motorists are affected by the introduction of new vehicle models offering luxury, technology, and off-road capabilities with extended warranties.
