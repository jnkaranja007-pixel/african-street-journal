Zimbabwe Banks Offer Car Loans as Safe Lending Option

Despite a stable financial sector, high borrowing costs prevent many working Zimbabweans from financing new vehicles.

Zimbabwean banks are increasingly offering vehicle asset finance, viewing it as a secure lending avenue. A financed vehicle provides lenders with tangible collateral and a clear repayment case, making it attractive in a cautious credit market. Banks are under pressure to increase lending, and vehicle finance has emerged as a safer option for expanding credit portfolios, moving beyond transactional income towards more productive lending that supports economic activity.

The banking sector's financial health appears robust, with total loans and advances reaching ZiG55.93 billion by December 31, 2024, and foreign-currency loans dominating at 88.17%. The capital adequacy ratio stood at 29.69% in 2025, well above the 12% regulatory minimum, and the non-performing loans ratio was 3.47%, below the 5% benchmark. These figures indicate a stable and liquid sector, though one that remains cautious in its lending practices.

However, this caution contrasts sharply with the accessibility of credit in neighboring South Africa, where individuals can secure substantial loans for cars and homes. In Zimbabwe, many employed citizens face prohibitively high borrowing costs, making new vehicle financing economically unviable. Consequently, the rational choice for many is to purchase used vehicles with cash, highlighting a weakness in the credit market where finance exists but on terms that often negate its purpose for ordinary citizens.

Why it mattersWorking Zimbabweans are affected by punitive borrowing costs that make financing new vehicles impractical, forcing them to opt for cash purchases of used cars.

Council Bulldozers Demolish Homes Amidst Fears of Operation Murambatsvina

Local authorities are razing structures in informal settlements, leaving thousands of families with uncertain futures and sparking fears of a repeat of past demolitions.

Local authorities have intensified demolitions of structures in informal settlements, leading to ruined lives and shattered dreams for thousands of families. Recent tours by the Local Government minister and Harare City Council officials have sent tremors through residents, raising fears of a looming Operation Murambatsvina. Structures in St Martins, near Sunningdale, were razed down about two weeks ago, following similar demolitions in Ridgeview, Belvedere in 2024, and Stoneridge this year.

Council officials argue that the targeted properties lack municipal approval or legal title deeds, or are situated on prohibited sites such as wetlands, road reserves, and land designated for public facilities. Residents are often victims of land barons and politicians who sell them land using fraudulent paperwork, only for municipal officials to later demolish the costly structures, sometimes after officials had watched them being built. These land barons and politicians often collude with corrupt municipal officials, creating a false sense of legitimacy for residents.

The demolitions are reportedly expected to extend to Budiriro, Kuwadzana, Glen View, Mabvuku, and Greendale. Authorities are urged to move away from a reactive approach and implement routine inspections to verify the legality of construction activities before residents invest their life savings in structures that could be demolished. This would prevent families from being thrown into desperation and facing inclement weather.

Why it mattersThousands of families are affected as their homes are demolished, leaving them with uncertain futures and ruined lives.

Afreximbank Appoints New Regional Leaders, Names Harare Director

The African Export-Import Bank has named Peter Adeshola Olowononi as director for Southern Africa, based in Harare, as part of a wider leadership reshuffle.

Afreximbank has appointed Peter Adeshola Olowononi as director, regional operations, Southern Africa, with his base in Harare. Olowononi will lead the bank’s operations, client coverage, and strategic engagement in the region. His prior role was director, client relations, Anglophone West Africa, and he brings experience in client coverage and transaction origination to this new position.

This appointment is part of a broader leadership restructuring aimed at strengthening Afreximbank’s presence in key regional markets to foster intra-African trade, industrialization, and integration. The bank stated that these changes are intended to enhance regional operations, improve client engagement, and expedite transaction execution across Africa and the Caribbean. Other appointments include Eric Intong Monchu as group managing director in Cairo and Roy Reid as chief operating officer for the Caribbean office in Barbados.

The bank noted that many of the newly appointed executives have advanced from within Afreximbank, bringing valuable institutional knowledge and established client relationships. These appointments align with the bank's ongoing expansion in financing trade and economic development across Africa and the Caribbean, reflecting the internal talent pool and supporting the bank's development mandate, according to Afreximbank president George Elombi.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the bank strengthens its leadership to expand intra-African trade, industrialization, and regional integration, potentially impacting investment opportunities and financial services.

Harare to Host 19th Comesa Business Forum

Zimbabwean businesses will connect with regional buyers and investors at the forum, held alongside the Comesa Heads of State Summit.

Harare will host the 19th Comesa Business Forum and Multi-Sectoral International Exhibition from October 19 to 21, 2026. The event, convened by the Comesa Business Council, ZimTrade, and the Zimbabwe National Chamber of Commerce, aims to boost intra-regional trade and investment. It will bring together businesses, financial institutions, development partners, and government representatives from across the Comesa region, which encompasses over 640 million people and a US$1 trillion GDP.

The forum, running under the theme “One Market, One Future: Advancing Inclusive Industrialisation, Investment and Regional Integration in Comesa,” will focus on strengthening regional value chains, expanding productive capacity, and promoting value addition. Structured business-to-business and business-to-government engagements are planned to foster partnerships and transactions. A private sector communiqué with recommendations for the Comesa Summit is expected.

Discussions will cover seamless trade systems, reducing non-tariff barriers, and strengthening trade corridors. Access to finance, reducing investment risks, and leveraging digital technologies will also be addressed. A Multi-Sectoral International Exhibition will run concurrently, featuring country pavilions and networking platforms, with priority sectors including manufacturing, agriculture, and financial services.

Why it mattersZimbabwean businesses and exporters will gain opportunities to engage with a regional market of over 640 million people and explore partnerships and transactions.
