Zimbabwe to build first utility-scale wind farm

The US$300 million hybrid project will combine wind, solar, and battery storage in Mashonaland Central province.

Zimbabwe will soon have its first utility-scale wind farm as part of a US$300 million project combining wind, solar power, and battery storage. Developers announced the project on Tuesday, though a power purchase agreement with utility Zesa is yet to be finalised.

The 228-megawatt Guruve-Mazowe (Chikonyora) project is located in Mashonaland Central province. Modaso Energy, a subsidiary of MISA Group, is developing the project with local renewable energy developer Tatanga Energy. This is the first project in sub-Saharan Africa outside South Africa to pair wind, solar, and storage on one site with a national utility as a key buyer.

The project is being developed by Modaso Energy and Tatanga Energy.

Why it mattersZimbabweans will benefit from increased energy supply from the new hybrid renewable energy project.

ZIMRA sets November tax deadlines

Taxpayers must submit returns by November 5 and payments by November 10.

The Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (ZIMRA) has announced deadlines for tax returns and payments for November 2026. All taxpayers must submit their returns for the month ending October 31, 2026, by November 5, 2026. Payments for these returns are due by November 10, 2026, according to Statutory Instrument 81 of 2025.

This notice applies to various tax types including Pay As You Earn (PAYE), Withholding Taxes, Mining Royalties, and Value Chain Withholding Tax. Other taxes with the same deadlines include Presumptive Tax Return, Gaming Operators’ Tax, Fast Foods Tax, and Plastic Carrier Bag Tax.

The authority also specified deadlines for the Remittance of Levy on Specified Minerals, Return for the Remittance of Tobacco Levy, and Presumptive Tax on Rental Income, all due by November 5 for returns and November 10 for payments.

Why it mattersBusinesses and individuals who owe taxes to ZIMRA must meet specific deadlines in November 2026 to avoid penalties.

Zimbabwe institute seeks Kenyan training partnership

The Institute of Social Development Practices aims to boost community development training through collaboration with Tharaka University.

Zimbabwe's Institute of Social Development Practices (ISDP) is seeking a partnership with Kenya's Tharaka University to enhance community development training. ISDP founder Petronella Muungani attended the annual human resource students’ international conference and expo 2026 in Kenya last week, where discussions for cooperation began.

Muungani stated that the visit opened avenues for collaboration between Zimbabwe and Kenya in areas including community development, youth empowerment, entrepreneurship, and poverty alleviation. This initiative aims to strengthen the role of social sciences in Africa's development agenda.

The ISDP's goal is to give social sciences and community development a stronger place on the continent's development agenda.

Why it mattersZimbabweans involved in community development, youth empowerment, and entrepreneurship will benefit from enhanced training programs resulting from this partnership.

Artisanal miners destroy Chengxi Mine property

Miners set fire to five excavators and assaulted police officers at Wanderer Mine Site Number 4.

Artisanal miners went on the rampage at Wanderer Mine Site Number 4 this Tuesday, destroying property belonging to Chengxi Chengetai Investments. The miners allegedly set fire to five excavators and beat up uniformed police officers.

The violence erupted after security personnel ordered the artisanal miners to leave the mine premises. Property worth thousands of United States dollars was reportedly destroyed during the incident.

Police were called to the scene to quell the disturbance. The full extent of the damage and the number of arrests are not yet known.

Why it mattersArtisanal miners destroyed property worth thousands of United States dollars at a gold mine.
