Stanbic Bank Profit Jumps 26% on Interest Income Growth

The bank reported a profit after tax of ZiG858.4 million for the first half of 2026, driven by increased lending and cost controls.

Stanbic Bank Zimbabwe announced a 26% increase in profit after tax for the half-year ending June 30, 2026, reaching ZiG858.4 million from ZiG682.2 million in the prior period. This performance was primarily fueled by a 33% surge in net interest income, which rose to ZiG1.1 billion, supported by a 16% expansion in net loans and advances to ZiG15.3 billion. The bank also saw a 22% growth in financial investments, reaching ZiG1.1 billion.

The bank's financial health was further bolstered by a 13.6% reduction in operating expenses, falling to ZiG1.1 billion due to cost optimization and efficiency gains. Customer deposits grew significantly by 35% to ZiG28.2 billion, reflecting strengthened customer relationships. However, non-funded income declined by 12% to ZiG1.3 billion, attributed to regulatory changes on bank charges implemented in April 2026 and lower tobacco selling prices.

Stanbic Bank maintained a strong capital position, with qualifying core capital at ZiG4.5 billion, equivalent to US$168.7 million, well above the regulatory minimum of US$30 million. The bank's chairman stated that management continues to monitor the economic environment and implement measures to preserve capital. The operating environment in Zimbabwe during the first half of 2026 was characterized by macroeconomic stability, low inflation, and a stable exchange rate.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the bank's strong financial results, which indicate profitability and stability in the Zimbabwean market.

Zimbabwe Inflation Falls to Lowest Level in 46 Years

The annual Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG) inflation rate dropped to 2.9% in August, marking the lowest single-digit inflation since 1980.

Zimbabwe's annual inflation rate in Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG) terms has fallen to 2.9% in August, the lowest single-digit figure recorded since 1980, according to the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ). This represents a 0.3 percentage-point decrease from the 3.2% inflation recorded in July. The central bank stated this is a "milestone achievement" and a testament to the effectiveness of current economic policies in maintaining price, currency, and exchange rate stability.

The RBZ noted that annual ZiG inflation had previously risen from 4.1% in January to 4.8% in April, influenced by global oil price shocks stemming from Middle East conflict. However, Zimbabwe experienced a muted impact compared to other sub-Saharan African nations, which saw average inflation increases of two to four percentage points. This resilience was attributed to well-anchored inflation expectations.

Since April 2024, when Governor John Mushayavanhu took charge, the Reserve Bank has prioritized inflation stabilization. While some businesses have criticized the tight monetary policy for potentially stifling industry, authorities maintain these measures are crucial for reversing hyperinflation's damage and restoring macroeconomic stability. The central bank indicated that prices for domestic goods and services have risen at a similar pace in both ZiG and US dollars since August 2025, suggesting businesses should be indifferent to accepting either currency.

Why it mattersBusinesses and investors are affected by the low and stable inflation environment, which should support greater predictability and certainty in business planning and investment for the wider use of ZiG.

Sinomine secures additional 300 000 tonnes lithium export quota from Zimbabwe

The Chinese firm received a second export allocation in July, following an earlier one in April, as Zimbabwe pushes for local processing of the battery metal.

Sinomine Resource Group has obtained an additional export quota of three hundred thousand metric tonnes of lithium concentrate from Zimbabwe, the company announced. This follows an initial quota of two hundred thousand tonnes granted in April. Zimbabwe, Africa's leading lithium producer, implemented export quotas in April after a February halt on concentrate shipments due to alleged malpractices.

The country is urging mining companies to increase local processing of lithium to maximize economic benefits, with a ban on concentrate exports set for January 2027. Sinomine, operator of the Bikita lithium mine, stated in its half-year report that the additional quota was secured in July. The company noted that lithium concentrate supply from Bikita has normalized after the earlier stoppages.

Sinomine operates two plants at Bikita with a combined capacity to produce six hundred thousand tonnes of spodumene concentrate and petalite concentrate annually. A recent upgrade will increase spodumene concentrate production capacity to four hundred thousand tonnes per year. The company is also constructing a one hundred thousand tonne per year lithium sulphate plant at Bikita, expected to be finished by mid-2027.

Why it mattersSinomine Resource Group is affected by Zimbabwe's export quotas and local processing requirements for lithium concentrate.
