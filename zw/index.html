Proplastics Profit Jumps 50% on Higher Sales Volumes

The plastics manufacturer reported a 22% increase in turnover to US$11.7 million, driven by a 23% rise in sales volumes.

Proplastics Limited saw its profit before tax climb 50% to US$0.9 million in the first half of the year, despite global supply chain disruptions and rising input costs. Turnover increased by 22% to US$11.7 million, fueled by a 23% surge in sales volumes. The company attributed this performance to disciplined execution and resilient demand across all market segments, noting improved overhead control and a significant 56% reduction in finance costs due to lower borrowings.

The company navigated global geopolitical tensions and supply chain disruptions, particularly those affecting PVC resin supplies and raw material price volatility, by activating alternative sourcing strategies. Proplastics earned 97% of its revenue in US dollars, with the remaining 3% in Zimbabwe Gold. Exports contributed US$0.6 million, a 141% increase from the previous year, representing 5% of total revenue. Cash generation from operations rose 29% to US$1.6 million.

Looking ahead, Proplastics anticipates strengthened demand in the second half, its peak trading period. The company expects potential increases in product demand due to government investments in water-security projects for irrigation and consumption, potentially spurred by an El Niño-induced drought. Accelerated infrastructure development across various sectors is also projected to boost demand for its products.

Why it mattersProplastics Limited is affected by global supply chain disruptions and rising input costs, which it mitigated through alternative sourcing and procurement strategies to maintain margins and supply continuity.

EU Ambassador Katrin Hagemann reportedly dies in Harare

Diplomatic sources report the death of the European Union's envoy to Zimbabwe, who began her mission in December 2025.

European Union Ambassador to Zimbabwe Katrin Hagemann has reportedly died at the EU residence in Harare. Diplomatic sources confirmed her passing, which occurred on Thursday. The cause of death has not yet been determined, and the EU has not issued an official statement regarding the ambassador's death.

Hagemann was appointed EU ambassador to Zimbabwe in 2025, succeeding Jobst von Kirchmann. She formally began her diplomatic mission on December 11, 2025, after presenting her Letters of Credence to President Emmerson Mnangagwa. Prior to her assignment in Zimbabwe, Hagemann held significant roles within the European External Action Service, including head of the Central Africa division and deputy head of the EU Delegation in Kenya.

The late ambassador was actively involved in fostering stronger EU-Zimbabwe relations across trade, investment, governance, and development cooperation. She outlined her priorities for the EU's partnership with Zimbabwe during her first Meet the Press engagement in Harare in February of this year. Her previous diplomatic experience also includes serving as head of political affairs in Botswana.

Why it mattersThe death of the EU Ambassador to Zimbabwe affects diplomatic relations and cooperation efforts between the European Union and the country.

Zimbabwe Revenue Authority Targets 22% Tax-to-GDP Ratio by 2030

Finance Minister Mthuli Ncube has set a new performance benchmark for Zimra, aiming to bolster fiscal resilience and domestic resource mobilization.

Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (Zimra) has been tasked by Finance Minister Mthuli Ncube to increase the country's tax-to-GDP ratio to 22% by 2030. This directive is part of intensified efforts to broaden the tax base, reduce revenue leakages, and strengthen domestic resource mobilization. Ncube stated that this target will serve as a key performance indicator for Zimra as Zimbabwe seeks to enhance fiscal resilience and lessen its reliance on external financing.

Speaking at Zimra’s Silver Jubilee celebrations in Harare, Ncube emphasized that the 22% tax-to-GDP ratio aligns with national development objectives and regional convergence ambitions. He noted that Zimbabwe's economy, now valued at over US$66 billion, should translate into a more sustainable revenue base. The minister highlighted the growing importance of domestic resource mobilization due to constrained access to affordable international financing and declining development assistance, framing it as a pillar of economic sovereignty.

To achieve this goal, Zimra is expected to broaden the tax base, improve taxpayer compliance, and simplify tax processes. The authority must also combat sophisticated economic crimes like smuggling and illicit financial flows through coordinated, intelligence-led, and technology-enabled responses. Modernizing systems to accommodate the country's increasing digitalization and interconnectedness is also a priority for effective border management and legitimate commerce facilitation.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the government aims to increase the tax-to-GDP ratio, which could impact corporate tax policies and revenue collection efficiency.

Nigeria to Host Trade Fair Aiming for Fifty Billion Dollars in Deals

Lagos will host the Intra-African Trade Fair in 2027 with ambitious targets for exhibitors, visitors, and investment.

Nigeria is set to host the Intra-African Trade Fair (IATF) in Lagos from November five to eleven, 2027. The event aims to generate more than fifty billion US dollars in trade and investment deals, attracting over one hundred thousand visitors and two thousand five hundred exhibitors. This edition, themed “Global Africa, Smart Trade: From Market Access to Market Power,” signals a strategic shift to position Africa as a dominant economic force.

The IATF has become a significant platform for accelerating intra-African trade, which grew by five point four percent in 2024 to reach two hundred and six point six billion US dollars. The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) is expected to further boost these figures. Previous editions have facilitated partnerships and strengthened regional value chains, with cumulative trade and investment deals exceeding one hundred and sixty-seven billion US dollars across the first four events.

The fair serves as a crucial marketplace for connecting businesses, unlocking opportunities, and translating ambitions into tangible commercial outcomes. It addresses challenges such as limited market knowledge, unclear regulations, and restricted access to trade finance that hinder cross-border business growth across the continent. The IATF aims to foster economic integration and industrialization, particularly in processing natural resources.

Why it mattersInvestors can benefit from the IATF's focus on facilitating trade, investment, and market access across the continent.
