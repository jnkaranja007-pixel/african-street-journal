Angola’s central bank reports women hold 41.3% of mid-level management posts

At a gender-equality workshop in Luanda, a separate assessment found women’s share of leadership positions fell to 9% in larger organisations.

Sources: Folha 8 and Mercado · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Angola’s central bank reported that women held 41.3% of its mid-level management posts at a gender-equality workshop in Luanda on Monday, 23 November. The central bank and the United Nations Global Compact in Angola organised the meeting to discuss women’s participation in financial-sector decision-making.

Eliana Pereira dos Santos, the Global Compact’s executive director in Angola, presented an assessment of organisations in its local network. It found women’s representation in leadership fell from about 51% in smaller organisations to 9% in larger ones. She called for institutional policies with targets, indicators and transparent career-progression criteria.

PwC Angola presented a diagnosis of gender equality in the financial sector, while Banco de Fomento Angola shared practices. The meeting ended with a discussion involving regulators and financial-institution representatives.

Why it mattersWomen seeking promotion in larger financial institutions face a leadership share of 9%, compared with about 51% in smaller organisations, according to the assessment.
