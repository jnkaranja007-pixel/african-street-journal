Angola president barred from third term

President João Lourenço cannot seek re-election in 2027 due to constitutional term limits.

Source: Folha 8 · 2026-11-11 · 1 min read

Angola faces a political transition as President João Lourenço is constitutionally barred from seeking a third term in 2027. This limitation, stemming from articles 113 and 110 of the Constitution, shapes the country's internal succession dynamics within the ruling MPLA party. The MPLA is navigating its most delicate leadership change since José Eduardo dos Santos left power.

The succession process within the MPLA is being kept secret to prevent premature internal disputes, according to Celso Filipe in Jornal de Negócios. Another report suggests João Lourenço has already chosen his successor, awaiting the opportune moment for announcement. However, concerns exist about potential factional crises or a dual leadership model where Lourenço retains party control while another figure leads the presidency, risking governmental paralysis.

Independent media highlight Angola's persistent internal socio-economic disparities, including a declining purchasing power and inflation, as major governmental obstacles. Editorials in O País call for institutional reforms and an end to oil dependency, arguing that 51 years of independence demand effective electoral democratization. This dual perspective reflects the tension between Angola's growing geoeconomic role and its internal challenges.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens face uncertainty regarding the country's political future and economic stability as the presidential term limit approaches.
