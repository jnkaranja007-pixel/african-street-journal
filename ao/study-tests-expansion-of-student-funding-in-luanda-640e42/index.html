Study tests expansion of student funding in Luanda

The research examines whether higher-education institutions can manage funds to ease access and help students from low-income households stay enrolled.

Source: Folha 8 · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

A study of student financing in Luanda examines whether expanding student funds can make higher education more accessible and help students remain enrolled. It focuses on institutions in the Angolan province during the 2023–2027 post-pandemic period.

The research says students mostly come from households earning less than 40,000 kwanzas a month, while institutions lack internal financial support mechanisms. Tuition, food, transport and academic materials add to the burden and contribute to students leaving their courses, the study says.

The study tests whether institutions have the administrative capacity to manage funds transparently and sustainably, and whether expansion can reduce socioeconomic inequalities in access and retention. Its findings describe families’ financial fragility and insufficient public and private funding.

Why it mattersStudents from low-income households face tuition, food, transport and study-material costs, while the research tests whether institutions can manage expanded funds.
