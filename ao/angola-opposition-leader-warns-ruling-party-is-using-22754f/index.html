Angola opposition leader warns ruling party is using elections to hold power

Adalberto Costa Júnior, leader of the Union for the Total Independence of Angola (UNITA), said the government was using elections to stay in power through control.

Source: Folha 8 · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Adalberto Costa Júnior, leader of the Union for the Total Independence of Angola (UNITA), the country's largest opposition party, told a debate in Benguela that Angola was being governed for the purpose of keeping power. He spoke at a session on political change ahead of the 2027 elections.

He said elections in Angola stopped the country each time they were held and that the government was using them to remain in office, citing control of the constitutional courts and national electoral commissions. He warned that leaders who feared losing power treated the risk as a threat to their lives, which he said created instability for everyone.

He urged wider participation in the vote, saying it was needed to avoid following the example of other African countries such as Tanzania and Uganda, but said the indicators were not good.

Why it mattersAngolans voting in 2027 face a contest that the main opposition party says is shaped by control of the courts and electoral commissions that validate results.
