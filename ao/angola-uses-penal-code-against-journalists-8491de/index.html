Angola uses penal code against journalists

The constitution guarantees free expression, but courts prosecute reporters for defamation.

Source: Folha 8 · 2026-10-30 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Angolan authorities are systematically using the Penal Code to prosecute journalists, despite the Constitution guaranteeing freedom of expression and the press. This practice violates international commitments made by the state, according to Folha 8.

Journalists in Angola who report on corruption, nepotism, or abuses of power often face criminal charges such as insult, slander, or defamation. These accusations are used by political figures and groups to silence critical reporting and investigative journalism.

This approach criminalizes speech and inverts the basic logic of democracy, where public scrutiny is a duty of the press and a right of citizens. The persistent use of the courts under the guise of protecting 'honor' neutralizes investigative journalism and critical voices.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens are affected as the government uses the courts to silence critical reporting and investigative journalism, undermining freedom of expression.
