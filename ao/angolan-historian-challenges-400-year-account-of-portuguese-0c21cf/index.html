Angolan historian challenges 400-year account of Portuguese rule

José Bento Duarte says Portuguese presence across Angola did not amount to effective colonial control over most people and regions for centuries.

Source: Noticias Lusofonas · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

José Bento Duarte, an Angolan economist and researcher in history, challenged the account of 400 years of Portuguese colonisation at a history meeting in Lisbon on Thursday night. He argued that Portuguese presence in Angola did not mean effective control over most of its population and regions.

Duarte pointed to the 1915 war against King Mandume in Cunene, saying some areas remained contested 60 years before Angola’s 1975 independence. He was a professor at the University of Luanda before and after independence. Historian Carlos Mariano Manuel, who joined remotely from Luanda, said Portuguese rule expanded gradually through diplomacy as well as military force.

Duarte also argued that Pedro João Baptista and Anastácio Francisco, whom he said made the first overland crossing between Angola and Mozambique before David Livingstone’s journey, remain under-recognised in place names in Lisbon and Angola.

Why it mattersAngolan readers and historians are presented with a challenge to the account of Portuguese rule, including Duarte’s claim that control remained contested in Cunene in 1915.
