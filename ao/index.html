Cabinda activist challenges UNITA leader

An open letter urges UNITA's president to consult cabindans directly, both at home and in europe.

An open letter addressed to the president of UNITA challenges the opposition party's approach to Cabinda. The author, an activist, states that for over two decades, Cabinda has been a site for intellectual and political incorporation, with opposition parties using local elites in their electoral strategies. The letter criticizes UNITA for presenting future projects for Cabinda without prior internal popular consultation, citing the president's recent economic vision for the region as an example.

The activist argues that UNITA's president should not rely on a few deputies or supporters to decide what is good for Cabinda, asserting that the Cabindan people and their intellectual elite have reached political maturity. The letter also points out the omission of the Cabindan diaspora, stating that the president never allocates time to meet or dialogue with them during European visits, despite their significant role in international political reflection.

The letter proposes a direct dialogue with Cabindans, both within the territory and among the diaspora in Europe, as a challenge to the UNITA president's reputation as a courageous politician. The author emphasizes that Cabindans do not need tutors or decisions made without their input, whether from the MPLA or UNITA, and that Cabinda's voice resonates beyond its borders.

Why it mattersCabindans, including their diaspora, are directly affected by political strategies and proposed future projects that are developed without their consultation.

Angola's MPLA party mourns King Ekuikui IV

The political bureau of the ruling party praised the traditional leader for his resistance against colonialism and devotion to national unity.

The MPLA party announced the death of King Ekuikui IV, also known as the soba of sobas, who was a militant member of the ruling party and a former Member of Parliament. The party's political bureau issued a statement recognizing his legacy.

In April 2008, MPLA's deputy campaign coordinator João Lourenço presented King Ekuikui IV with a Land Rover vehicle in Huambo province. The King, accompanied by one of his wives, thanked José Eduardo dos Santos and expressed satisfaction with road improvements and better living conditions in Bailundo.

The King had previously declared he would make an important statement to Angolans during the legislative elections in September 2008. He later publicly supported the MPLA, which the party's statement noted as an example of resistance against Portuguese colonialism and dedication to peace and national development.

Why it mattersThe death of King Ekuikui IV is significant because he was a member of the ruling MPLA party and a former parliamentarian, and his legacy of supporting the party is being publicly acknowledged.

Angola promotes trade at China expo

Angolan officials attend a major Chinese trade platform to attract foreign investment and find new business opportunities.

Angola is participating in a key international trade platform in China to showcase its economic potential and attract foreign investment. The event aims to identify new business and cooperation opportunities for Angolan companies. Minister Rui Miguêns de Oliveira's presence lends institutional weight to Angola's engagement, reinforcing economic ties with China.

The trade fair serves as a space for market convergence and strategic partnerships, allowing participants to present products and services. Angola seeks to increase its involvement in international trade dynamics, promote economic diversification, and strengthen the presence of its national products and services abroad.

Separately, Angolan citizens and Macau residents will soon be able to enter both territories without a visa, following an agreement signed on September 4, 2026. Macau's Secretary for Administration and Justice, Wong Sio Chak, stated this agreement will significantly ease travel for tourism, academic exchange, and trade.

Why it mattersAngola's participation in the Chinese trade platform aims to boost its economy by attracting foreign investment and expanding trade.

Mbalundu king criticised for party affiliation

A traditional leader's historical legitimacy is being undermined by involvement in partisan politics, a community member stated.

The traditional Kingdom of Mbalundu, a centuries-old institution in Angola, is facing criticism over its current king's public affiliation with the ruling party.

A community member, whose grandfather was a deacon and respected figure, stated that the king's role transcends political parties and should represent all citizens, regardless of their political or religious beliefs.

The kingdom's historical continuity as a symbol of identity and culture predates Angola's political parties, which emerged in the late 1950s and early 1960s.

Why it mattersThe actions of the King of Mbalundu are being questioned for potentially alienating citizens of different political affiliations within his kingdom.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Cabinda elites urged to drop armed struggle

A call for Cabinda's leaders to abandon the FLEC's legacy for international law as a new front.

Cabinda's elites are urged to abandon the militaristic legacy of the FLEC and pursue a non-partisan, peaceful, citizen-led alternative. This call comes as the continued armed struggle and internal divisions within the FLEC factions have weakened the cause, leaving the people unable to be heard with dignity internationally.

The FLEC, once a symbol of historical resistance in Cabinda, is now divided into multiple factions. Insisting on clandestine action or sporadic confrontations has inadvertently served Luanda's power games, allowing justification for a large military presence and blocking access to Western and African diplomatic circles wary of border changes through force.

With attention focused on Angola's upcoming elections and their customary promises to Cabinda's elites, the only remaining recourse is international law. Cabindans must adopt new political language to engage international bodies like the African Union and the United Nations, which negotiate with representative, peaceful actors armed with law, not insurgents.

Why it mattersCabinda's population faces diminished dignity and international representation due to the ongoing armed struggle and its consequences.
