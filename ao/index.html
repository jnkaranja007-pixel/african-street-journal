Angola has 370 mil people living with HIV, with fewer than half treated

ANASO president António Coelho said 60% of Angolans living with HIV are aged 15 to 24, and that government struggles to get medicines to those who need them, with stigma.

Angola has 370 mil people living with HIV, and only half receive antiretroviral therapy, ANASO president António Coelho said at the national HIV/AIDS forum. He said 60% of those living with HIV are aged 15 to 24.

Coelho said the government faces serious difficulties making medicines available to people who need them. He also criticised the national commission on HIV/AIDS, saying it operates timidly, and said stigma and discrimination deter many people from seeking treatment.

The report said fewer than 50% of people living with HIV benefit from antiretroviral treatment. Coelho warned that the situation could become alarming without action.

Angola seeks 10% stake in De Beers through consortium bid

The Global Diamond Consortium is positioned to buy Anglo American’s 85% holding, but Angola has not disclosed its planned investment or financing.

Angola plans to acquire a 10% stake in De Beers through the Global Diamond Consortium, which is positioned to buy Anglo American’s 85% holding in the diamond producer. The Ministry of Mineral Resources, Petroleum and Gas confirmed the plan to Bloomberg.

The consortium, led by former De Beers chief executive Gareth Penny, met Minister Diamantino Azevedo in Luanda on Thursday to present its purchase proposal. The ministry has not said how much Angola plans to invest or how it would finance the deal.

The consortium also includes Namibia, Gulf investors and some of De Beers’ largest customers. If the deal is completed, Angola and Namibia would join Botswana as African shareholders; Botswana’s President Duma Boko announced the country would double its stake to 30%. Anglo American has been trying to sell De Beers since 2024.

Business · 2026-11-18

PWN Luanda brings women leaders together for 28 November event

The fifth annual meeting at Luanda’s public administration school will discuss women’s leadership and barriers to senior roles.

Professional Women’s Network (PWN) Luanda will hold its fifth annual event on 28 November at the National School of Administration and Public Policy auditorium in Luanda. Professionals from several sectors are due to discuss women’s leadership and their role in changing organisations and society.

The programme includes experience-sharing sessions, debates and networking focused on women’s career obstacles and access to leadership positions. The organisation says it wants to bring professionals from different sectors together and encourage equal opportunities through collaborative learning and stronger support networks.

PWN Luanda, a non-profit formed in 2022, is part of an international network present in about 30 cities. At a Luanda conference in February, Banco de Fomento Angola executive director Francisca Costa said 24% of women had bank accounts and 59% had no formal contact with the financial sector.

Why it mattersThe event will address barriers to women’s access to leadership, while figures presented in Luanda show 59% have no formal contact with the financial sector.

Culture · 2026-11-18

Angola dance company turns Cokwe sand drawings into SONA 137

The contemporary work at Luanda’s Teatro Elinga brings geometric designs, traditional sound and movement together on stage.

The Contemporary Dance Company of Angola presented SONA 137, a contemporary dance work, at Teatro Elinga in Luanda about a month before Vanda Narciso wrote about it. The performance draws on sona, geometric sand drawings practised by the Cokwe people, especially in eastern Angola.

Narciso describes the choreography by Ana Clara Guerra Marques and Andy Rodríguez as exploring lines, paths and repeated movements. The original music by David López Sáez combines traditional references, including the kisanji, with contemporary elements. In sona tradition, drawings can carry stories, proverbs and lessons passed between generations.

Founded in 1991, the company is celebrating 35 years in 2026. SONA 137 brings that ancestral tradition into a contemporary performance, with dancers using their bodies to evoke the drawings’ lines and the human relationships they can represent.

Why it mattersAngolan audiences encounter Cokwe stories and teachings through a contemporary stage work by a company celebrating 35 years in 2026.
