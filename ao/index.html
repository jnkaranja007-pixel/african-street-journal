Angola rural teen pregnancy rate hits 42.5%

UN committee urges Angola to fund national strategy for adolescent sexual education and reproductive health services.

Teenage pregnancy in Angola's rural areas has reached 42.5%, according to a recent United Nations committee report on women's rights. The committee expressed concern over the high incidence, which affects 27.1% of women and girls aged 15 to 19 nationwide.

The UN committee urged Angola to adopt a national strategy with adequate resources to prevent adolescent pregnancy. Recommendations include offering age-appropriate sexual education and access to confidential, adolescent-friendly sexual and reproductive health services, along with voluntary contraception.

The report also calls for stricter measures against sexual violence and child marriage in Angola. It highlighted significant barriers women face in accessing economic resources and recommended expanding programs to promote female entrepreneurship and ease access to low-interest loans.

Why it mattersAdolescent girls in rural Angola are disproportionately affected by pregnancy, with 42.5% of them becoming pregnant.

Ebola kills 5,103 in DR Congo

The Democratic Republic of Congo's health ministry reports 10,467 total cases and 980 patients currently hospitalized.

The Democratic Republic of Congo's National Institute of Public Health (INSP) confirmed 5,103 deaths and 10,467 infections from Ebola as of March 11, 2026. In the last 24 hours, 45 deaths and 53 new cases were recorded, maintaining a mortality rate of 48.8%.

Currently, 980 patients are hospitalized or in isolation across eight affected provinces, with Ituri province being the epicenter, accounting for 70.8% of all infections. A total of 2,824 individuals have recovered and been discharged.

This outbreak is the deadliest in DR Congo's history, surpassing the 2018-2020 epidemic. It also projects to exceed the 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak which saw 28,616 cases and 11,325 deaths. Cases linked to the DR Congo situation have also appeared in Kenya and Nigeria.

Why it mattersAngola's neighbor, the Democratic Republic of Congo, is experiencing its deadliest Ebola outbreak, with over 5,100 deaths and a significant number of ongoing hospitalizations.

Angola's sovereign fund takes stake in credit bureau

The Fundo Soberano de Angola is now a shareholder in Bureau, a private credit information company.

Angola's sovereign wealth fund, the Fundo Soberano de Angola (FSDEA), has become a shareholder in Bureau, a private credit information company. The partnership, formalised through a subscription agreement signed on June 4, aims to promote and support Angola's economic development by assisting businesses, institutions, and citizens.

Bureau, established under Presidential Decree nº 275/20, operates as the country's first and only private credit information centre, holding a license from the Data Protection Agency to manage a national database. The company aims to reduce information asymmetry, minimise default rates, and foster financial education, potentially leading to lower interest rates and easier access to credit for Angolans.

With FSDEA as a reference shareholder, Bureau will enhance its corporate governance, including the planned integration of a director appointed by the fund onto its board. Bureau collects and analyses financial and consumption behaviour data to support more rigorous credit decisions, also enabling telecom operators to design tailored products and insurers to better understand client profiles.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens and businesses will benefit from improved access to credit and potentially lower interest rates due to the sovereign fund's investment in a credit information bureau.

IFC pledges more investment in Angola

The International Finance Corporation aims to boost private sector financing in agriculture and finance.

The International Finance Corporation (IFC) plans to increase its investment in Angola, focusing on the financial sector, small and medium enterprises, and agriculture. Makhtar Diop, the IFC's director-general, announced this on Tuesday in Luanda during the Africa Financial Summit 2026. This move signifies a significant expansion of the IFC's presence in the Angolan market.

Diop highlighted the Lobito Corridor's potential to support businesses beyond mineral transport, including agricultural products and other goods. The IFC seeks to assist companies that can use the corridor to develop value chains and stimulate economic activity along the logistics route. The institution is also in contact with entities in the banking, agricultural, and livestock sectors for potential financing agreements.

The IFC's strategy includes evaluating new investment possibilities through meetings with agricultural sector operators, as directed by the minister responsible for Economic Coordination. The goal is to enhance private sector participation in Angola's economic development by promoting national production, company creation, and economic diversification.

Why it mattersAngolan businesses in the financial, agricultural, and livestock sectors will be evaluated for new financing agreements and investment opportunities.

Business · 2026-11-04

Angola hosts African finance summit in Luanda

Minister José de Lima Massano opened the AFIS summit, highlighting Africa's potential and financial system needs.

Angola hosted the sixth edition of the Africa Investment Forum (AFIS) in Luanda, bringing together over a thousand financial leaders. Minister of State for Economic Coordination, José de Lima Massano, opened the two-day summit themed 'Making Capital Count: Unlocking Growth Through African Finance'. The event, organized by Jeune Afrique Media Group and the International Finance Corporation, aims to transform financial mobilization into economic growth.

During the opening, Minister Massano acknowledged global uncertainties like geopolitical shifts and financial volatility. He stated that Africa possesses significant advantages, including a young population, strategic natural resources, and the developing African Continental Free Trade Area. However, he stressed that economic transformation hinges on attracting investment, converting savings into productive assets, and supporting entrepreneurship.

Massano also pointed out that credit for agriculture, manufacturing, and small businesses in Africa remains limited and insufficient. He advocated for deeper regional integration to strengthen the continent's financial future. The minister noted that while assertive economic policies are needed, the quality and efficiency of African financial systems are equally important.

Why it mattersAngolan Minister José de Lima Massano is addressing African financial leaders at a summit in Luanda to discuss economic growth and investment.
