Angola becomes Ibero-American Community associate observer

The Community of Portuguese Language Countries congratulated Angola on achieving this new status at the XXX Ibero-American Summit.

Angola has officially gained associate observer status with the Ibero-American Community. This was formalized during the XXX Ibero-American Summit held in Madrid between November 3 and 5.

The Community of Portuguese Language Countries (CPLP) congratulated Angola, stating the status recognizes its multilateral role and opens cooperation opportunities with Ibero-American nations. Portugal's Foreign Minister Paulo Rangel said Angola's accession brings Africa, Latin America, and Europe closer.

Portugal actively supported Angola's diplomatic effort. The CPLP, marking 30 years, includes Angola, Brazil, Cabo Verde, Guinea-Bissau, Equatorial Guinea, Mozambique, Portugal, São Tomé and Príncipe, and Timor-Leste.

Why it mattersAngola's new status as an associate observer opens new avenues for cooperation and strengthens its international presence.

Angolan court clarifies insult laws

The Folha 8 newspaper explains the legal distinction between slander, libel, and insult.

Angolan legal experts are clarifying the definitions of slander, libel, and insult, often confused in legal cases. The Folha 8 newspaper detailed these distinctions on April 11, 2026, to help understand criminal imputability.

The newspaper highlighted that insult, defined in Article 213 of the Penal Code, targets an individual's intimacy. Examples provided include a former partner spreading rumors about someone's physical attributes on social media or a classmate mocking another's appearance.

These examples illustrate how insults affect personal dignity, even when based on suspicion. The Folha 8 report aimed to provide clarity on these specific legal concepts for public understanding.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens are affected by the clarification of insult laws, which impacts personal reputation and legal recourse.

Health · 2026-11-05

Ebola kills 5,103 in DRCongo

The Democratic Republic of Congo's National Institute of Public Health reported 10,467 total cases.

The Democratic Republic of Congo's National Institute of Public Health (INSP) reported 5,103 deaths and 10,467 infections as of March 11, 2026. In the last 24 hours, 45 deaths and 53 new cases were documented. The current mortality rate stands at 48.8% for the neighboring nation.

Currently, 980 patients are hospitalized or in isolation, while 2,824 individuals have recovered and been discharged. The contact tracing rate is 86.6%. Eight provinces have been affected, with Ituri province remaining the epicenter, accounting for 70.8% of all infections across 67 of 178 health zones.

This epidemic is the deadliest in DRCongo's history, surpassing the 2018-2020 outbreak. Projections suggest it could exceed the 2014-2016 West Africa epidemic, which saw 28,616 cases and 11,325 deaths in Guinea, Liberia, and Sierra Leone. Cases linked to the DRCongo situation have also been confirmed in Kenya and Nigeria.

Why it mattersAngola shares a border with the DRCongo, and the spread of Ebola poses a direct health risk to its citizens.

Business · 2026-11-05

Angola's sovereign fund buys stake in credit bureau

The Fundo Soberano de Angola will add a board member to Bureau, a private credit information company.

The Fundo Soberano de Angola (FSDEA) has become a shareholder in Bureau, a private company established to promote Angola's economic development. Bureau aims to reduce information asymmetry, minimize default rates, and promote financial education to improve access to credit for Angolans.

Bureau, legally established by Presidential Decree nº 275/20 and licensed by the Data Protection Agency, collects, processes, and analyzes financial and consumption behavior data. This information assists companies in making more rigorous credit decisions, potentially leading to lower interest rates and increased competition in the credit market.

The partnership, formalized through a subscription agreement on June 4, signifies a new phase of institutional consolidation and growth for Bureau. The inclusion of an FSDEA-designated administrator on Bureau's Board of Directors will strengthen its corporate governance.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens and businesses will benefit from improved access to credit and potentially lower interest rates due to the Fundo Soberano de Angola's investment in Bureau.
