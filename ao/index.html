Angola and Macau Sign Mutual Visa Exemption Agreement

The agreement will allow residents of both Macau and Angola to enter each other's territories for up to 30 days without a visa.

Macau and Angola have signed a mutual visa exemption agreement, a move expected to significantly ease travel and boost exchanges between the two regions. Wong Sio Chak, Macau's Secretary for Administration and Justice, stated that the accord will greatly simplify life for residents, particularly in areas of tourism, academic collaboration, and trade. He also expressed a desire to see more Angolan visitors and investors experience Macau's vitality and opportunities.

Eduardo Velasco Galiano, Angola's Consul in Macau, highlighted that the new agreement resolves bureaucratic barriers that previously hindered business dealings. He emphasized that the accord definitively removes uncertainty from commercial relations, paving the way for increased prosperity and economic interaction. Angola becomes the fourth Portuguese-speaking country, after Portugal, Brazil, and Cape Verde, to benefit from Macau's visa waiver program.

Under the terms of the agreement, Macau residents holding a Special Administrative Region passport can enter Angola visa-free for a maximum of 30 days. Similarly, Angolan passport holders will receive the same 30-day visa exemption for Macau. The exact date of the agreement's entry into force will be announced later, according to an official statement from Macau's Identification Services.

Why it mattersBusiness travelers and tourists from Angola and Macau will benefit from easier entry and longer stays, facilitating trade and cultural exchanges.

Angola Opens Dams to Combat Southern Drought

Two new reservoirs in Cunene province aim to supply water for people, agriculture, and livestock, addressing decades of drought.

President João Lourenço is set to inaugurate the Calucuve and Ndúe dams in Cuvelai municipality on Friday. These projects are part of the Program to Combat the Effects of Drought in Southern Angola (PCESSA) and are intended to provide water for consumption, agricultural irrigation, and livestock needs.

The Ndúe dam will serve an estimated fifty-five thousand inhabitants and sixty thousand head of cattle, with a storage capacity of one hundred seventy million cubic meters of water and a seventy-five-kilometer aqueduct. The Calucuve dam will benefit eighty thousand inhabitants and nearly two hundred thousand head of cattle, holding one hundred forty-one million cubic meters of water.

These inaugurations follow the operationalization of the Cafu canal and come nearly five years after construction began. Recurrent droughts, exacerbated by El Niño, have severely impacted southern Angola, particularly the Cunene, Huíla, and Namibe provinces, forcing many to seek water in neighboring Namibia.

Why it mattersThe new dams will provide a local water source for farmers and livestock, reducing reliance on external resources and potentially improving agricultural output.

Angola Guarantees Imports for Next Six Months

The National Bank of Angola reports a current account surplus exceeding two billion dollars, a 40.1% increase from the previous quarter.

Angola's import of goods and services is secured for the next six months, according to the National Bank of Angola's External Statistics Information Note. The report highlights a current account surplus of over two billion dollars, equivalent to 4.4% of the Gross Domestic Product. This figure represents a significant 40.1% expansion compared to the preceding quarter.

The positive performance of the current account is primarily attributed to a 31.7% increase in the surplus of the goods account. This growth occurred despite a rise in the deficit for services and primary income, which saw increases of 8.7% and 67.7% respectively. The capital and financial account, however, recorded a deficit of two point five billion dollars during the same period.

This capital and financial account deficit, which widened from one point two billion dollars in the prior quarter, was mainly due to medium and long-term capital movements, specifically public external debt. The surplus from foreign direct investment and other capital, including commercial credits and deposits with non-residents, also influenced this outcome. The international investment position worsened by one hundred seventy-four point five million dollars due to increased liabilities with non-residents.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the widening deficit in the capital and financial account, which impacts the country's overall financial position.

UNITA Returns to Uíge for Preparations for 2027 Elections

The opposition party plans to hold a Permanent Committee meeting to prepare for the upcoming general elections.

The National Union for the Total Independence of Angola (UNITA) returned to Uíge on Friday, where it will convene its Standing Committee in preparation for the 2027 general elections. Party leader Adalberto Costa Júnior stated the return was to "close an incomplete journey," referencing an August 9 incident where police dispersed party members. He appealed for a peaceful reception and departure, contrasting it with the previous events.

Costa Júnior recalled that UNITA rejected police escort upon leaving Uíge on August 9, deeming it insincere after the previous day's police intervention. He noted their welcome upon arrival this time and expressed hope for a similar departure, with a planned return to Luanda on Saturday. The party's return to Uíge was deemed necessary to reaffirm the democratic rule of law.

The UNITA leader emphasized that the state belongs to all Angolans and is a plural entity. The party's visit aims to reaffirm democratic principles and prepare its strategy for the upcoming electoral cycle. The events of August 8, which involved the dispersal of UNITA youth members and a postponed tribute to founder Jonas Savimbi, remain a point of contention.

Why it mattersUNITA's return to Uíge for electoral preparations directly impacts political stability and the upcoming 2027 elections.
