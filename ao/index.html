Folha 8 accuses Angola's ruling MPLA of 51 years of corruption and racism

The Luanda weekly says the party's leadership under President João Lourenço has left most Angolans with little while a small elite holds large fortunes abroad.

Folha 8, a Luanda weekly, says Angola has been governed by the same party, the MPLA, for 51 years and that the country remains among the most corrupt in the world. The paper says it has high infant mortality, deep social inequality and poor democratic practice, and it blames the party's leadership under President João Lourenço for all of it.

The paper says the party's elite, led by Lourenço, holds millions of dollars abroad while millions of Angolans have little or nothing. It recalls that General Francisco Furtado, then minister of state and head of the president's military household, warned that anyone who criticised the MPLA would be hit in the face. The paper contrasts Adalberto da Costa Júnior, who it says defends the power of ideas, with Lourenço, whom it accuses of relying on the force of power.

The paper also cites Lourenço's 2017 campaign speech in Bié, in which he blamed UNITA for destroying industrial capacity during the war and said this made it harder to create jobs for young people. Folha 8 says the MPLA's approach fuels hatred, discord and racism, and that reconciliation requires inclusion rather than exclusion.

Why it mattersThe article ties the party's leadership to a claimed flow of public wealth abroad and to limits on criticism, which bears on how readers judge Angolan governance.

Angola marks 51 years of independence in Australia

The Angolan Embassy in Canberra hosted a reception attended by local officials and the Angolan diaspora.

The Embassy of Angola in Australia organised an official reception in Canberra on November 11, 2026, to celebrate the 51st anniversary of Angola's National Independence. Ambassador António Luvualu de Carvalho hosted the event, which included local authorities, foreign diplomats, business people, academics, civil society representatives, and members of the Angolan community residing in Australia.

The celebration began with the national anthems, followed by a toast from the Australian government's representative, Ambassador Jonathan Muir, Director General of State Protocol, in honour of Angolan President João Manuel Gonçalves Lourenço and the Angolan people. Ambassador Luvualu de Carvalho responded with a toast to King Charles III and the Commonwealth of Australia.

Ambassador Luvualu de Carvalho highlighted the strong bilateral relations between Angola and Australia, noting that 2026 also marked 50 years since diplomatic relations were established on April 28, 1976. He also pointed to Angola's economic growth, with the country now being Africa's sixth-largest economy and third-largest in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens living in Australia gathered to commemorate their nation's independence anniversary.
