MPLA committee nullifies Higino Carneiro's leadership bid

The ruling party's Central Committee declared the candidacy void due to alleged document falsification.

The MPLA's Central Committee declared Higino Carneiro's candidacy for party president null and void on Wednesday. The decision followed accusations that Carneiro, a former governor and retired general, falsified documents.

Jurist Rui Verde stated that Carneiro could appeal to the Constitutional Court to potentially overturn the decision and order a repeat of the process. The MPLA's Central Committee also decided to initiate disciplinary proceedings against Carneiro for violating ethical and moral duties.

The party will communicate the facts to the Attorney General's Office due to strong indications of document forgery by Francisco Higino Lopes Carneiro. President João Lourenço issued superior orders for these actions.

Why it mattersAngola's ruling party leadership bid was nullified due to alleged document falsification.

Angola's economy declined sharply since 1974

In 1974, Angola was a global leader in sugar, coffee, and fishmeal production, but now faces widespread poverty.

In 1974, Angola was a world leader in several key industries, ranking as the second-largest producer of sugar, third in coffee, and fourth in cotton. The nation was also Africa's top exporter of beef and second in sisal and fishmeal. At that time, Angola possessed Africa's best silo network and four major railway lines, alongside significant industrial capacity including shipbuilding, breweries, and tire manufacturing.

50 years later, Angola faces severe challenges, with 68% of its population affected by poverty. The infant mortality rate is among the world's highest, at 250 deaths per 1,000 children. Access to clean water is limited to 38% of Angolans, and basic sanitation reaches only 44% of the population.

Healthcare services are inadequate, with only a quarter of Angolans having access, and many facilities suffer from poor infrastructure, staff shortages, and a lack of medicine. Chronic malnutrition affects 45% of Angolan children, and 25% die before the age of five, a stark contrast to the nation's past economic strength.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens are affected by widespread poverty, high infant mortality, and lack of access to basic services like water, sanitation, and healthcare.

Japan eyes Angola cooperation in logistics and finance

Japanese businesses want long-term partnerships in Angola, focusing on technology transfer and job creation.

Angolan Minister of Planning Victor Hugo Guilherme met a Japanese business delegation in Luanda to discuss cooperation in maritime transport, logistics, and financial services. The talks aimed to boost national production, improve goods movement, and strengthen Angola's regional market links.

The Japanese delegation, led by Ambassador Hiroaki Sano and JETRO executive director Sato Joji, expressed interest in long-term partnerships. Priorities include technology transfer, job creation, national workforce training, and developing value chains. They plan to visit Huambo and Lobito Corridor areas to assess opportunities in productive sectors.

Guilherme highlighted food security and human capital development as national priorities, advocating for agricultural technology and integrated economic corridors like Lobito and the South Corridor. Angola seeks investment to increase domestic production and reduce oil revenue dependence.

Why it mattersJapanese companies are seeking partnerships in Angola to transfer technology and create jobs.

Angola president promises end to hunger by 2027

João Lourenço claims MPLA will reveal a formula for Angolans to live without eating.

Angolan President João Lourenço, leading the MPLA party, has pledged that by 2027, the party will unveil a method enabling Angolans to live without consuming food. This promise follows the case of Indian yoga master Prahlad Jani, who reportedly survived for 14 days without food or water under observation by 30 doctors from India's Ministry of Defence.

The MPLA's claim is based on Jani's documented ability to abstain from eating and drinking, a feat previously explored in a 2006 Discovery Channel documentary. Jani himself stated after his observation, "I am strong and healthy because that is how God wants me to be." The packet notes that previous attempts by Angolans to follow such methods have resulted in death.

The concept of Angolans living without food was also a thesis of former leader José Eduardo dos Santos, who allegedly used misery and unemployment to force people down this path. Despite the claims, results in Angola have not been encouraging, with individuals attempting to live without sustenance reportedly dying before achieving the goal.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens are promised a way to live without eating by the ruling MPLA party in 2027.
