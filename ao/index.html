UNITA Leader Says Angola's Constitution Fuels Instability

Adalberto Costa Júnior claims the nation's founding document is undemocratic and risks future unrest.

Angola's constitution is "atypical" and "not democratic," risking instability, according to Adalberto Costa Júnior, leader of the UNITA opposition party. He criticized the current constitutional framework for suppressing direct presidential elections, stating that the ruling MPLA fears allowing citizens to vote directly for the president. Costa Júnior argued that the president is elected indirectly through the National Assembly, raising questions about the independence of sovereign bodies.

Costa Júnior explained that the constitution creates instability because a single ballot elects both the president and National Assembly deputies. This, he contends, limits the application and respect for law. The current constitution states the presidential candidate heading the list of the most voted political party or coalition in general elections becomes president. Angola is expected to hold general elections in 2027.

The UNITA president highlighted that the current president, João Lourenço, is in the final year of his second term and constitutionally barred from a third. Costa Júnior insisted on the need for the country to adopt a "pact for stability," suggesting that Angola would only be a true rule of law if it were different from its current state. He also stated his party opposes any constitutional review aimed at extending presidential terms.

Why it mattersThe UNITA leader's statements suggest potential political instability due to the undemocratic nature of the constitution, which could affect governance and future elections.

FNLA Central Committee Votes to Oust President Nimi a Simbi

An extraordinary meeting of the FNLA's Central Committee has voted to remove President Nimi a Simbi, citing internal conflicts and alleged statutory violations.

An extraordinary meeting of the FNLA's Central Committee, convened by over half its members, has voted to remove President Nimi a Simbi. The meeting, which reported a 52% quorum, also resolved to hold an extraordinary congress on October 30-31, 2026, to address internal divisions within the political party. The committee also decided to denounce the alleged illegal movement of provincial and municipal commissioners from the National Electoral Commission (CNE) affiliated with the FNLA.

Nationalist Ngola Kabango supported the call for new leadership, stating that the current moment is decisive for ending indiscipline from President Nimi a Simbi, whose mandate officially ended on September 21, 2026. The Central Committee recommended that the preparatory commission for the VI Ordinary Congress continue its work within the framework of the newly convened extraordinary congress.

However, the FNLA leadership has dismissed the decision, contesting the alleged removal of Nimi a Simbi from the party presidency. The promoters of the extraordinary meeting stated that it was called to respond to internal conflicts and the existence of two factions within the political formation.

Why it mattersThe FNLA's internal political struggle and leadership change directly affect party members and those involved in its electoral processes.
