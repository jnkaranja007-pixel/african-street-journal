Angola seeks 10% stake in De Beers

The government announced the move as part of an international consortium aiming to buy 85% of the diamond company.

Angola's government announced its intention to acquire a 10 percent stake in De Beers as part of an international consortium seeking to purchase 85 percent of the company. The Ministry of Mineral Resources, Petroleum and Gas stated that this operation, if finalized, would integrate Angola into De Beers' shareholder structure. The consortium includes Botswana, Namibia, and the Sultanate of Oman, with the deal awaiting regulatory approvals.

The Angolan government views this acquisition as a historic step for the national diamond sector, aiming to strengthen the country's position in the global diamond industry. This participation is expected to grant Angola a seat among De Beers' owners, increasing its influence on strategic decisions and enhancing value creation along the diamond production chain. The nation also intends to use this stake to boost domestic diamond cutting and polishing, energize the Angola Diamond Exchange, and foster skills development.

Angola is currently the world's third-largest diamond producer, following Russia and Botswana. In 2025, the country produced 15 comma two million carats, valued at approximately one comma eight billion dollars. The government believes this move will not only reinforce Angola's international market presence but also stimulate local industry growth.

TAAG adds seventh Airbus A220-300 to its fleet in Luanda

The Angolan national airline took delivery of the aircraft, registered D2-TAQ, which arrived from Canada under a leasing agreement.

TAAG Linhas Aéreas de Angola received its seventh Airbus A220-300 at Dr. António Agostinho Neto International Airport in Luanda. The aircraft, registered D2-TAQ, arrived from Canada under a leasing contract and is part of the airline's 2024-2029 Strategic Plan to modernise its fleet.

The A220-300 seats 137 passengers, 12 in business class and 125 in economy. The airline's chairman, Clóvis Lara Rosa, said each new aircraft helps TAAG manage its resources better and keep services running more consistently. The jet is used mainly on regional African routes, including Abidjan in Ivory Coast and Nairobi in Kenya.

The plane is also planned for a future route to Praia in Cape Verde, and it serves Lagos in Nigeria, Cape Town and Johannesburg. Before commercial operations begin, the aircraft must complete the administrative and regulatory procedures required by Angolan authorities.

Why it mattersPassengers on TAAG's regional and southern African routes should see more reliable flight availability once the aircraft enters service, after the Angolan authorities clear it.

Luanda hosts Angola's largest tourism fair

BITUR 2026 aims to attract investment and promote Angola's natural and cultural attractions to global markets.

Luanda will host the International Tourism Fair of Angola (BITUR 2026) from Friday, November 20, to Sunday, November 22, at the Tendas do Grupo Arena. The event, organized by the Institute for Tourism Promotion (INFOTUR) under the Ministry of Tourism (MINTUR), will focus on internationalizing Angola's tourism sector under the theme “Angola Like Never Before”.

BITUR 2026 is designed to foster direct dialogue between the public and private sectors to attract national and foreign investment for projects in hospitality, transport, and services. The fair also serves as a platform to showcase Angola's natural, cultural, and landscape riches to regional and international markets, seeking strategic alliances with blocs like the Southern African Development Community (SADC) and the Community of Portuguese Language Countries (CPLP).

A key part of the event is the International Tourism Forum (FIT 2026), themed “Connecting Angola to the World,” which will feature discussions on regional integration, visa facilitation, hospitality, gastronomy, territorial marketing, digital innovation, and new financing models. Individual tickets cost 2,500.00 Kz, with free entry for children under 12.
