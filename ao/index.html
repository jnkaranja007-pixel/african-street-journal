South Africa repatriates anti-apartheid fighters' remains from Angola

Angola's civil war was the site of the highest number of ANC combatant deaths during apartheid, according to Pretoria.

South Africa has initiated a repatriation operation for the remains of anti-apartheid activists who died in exile in Angola. Investigative teams have identified 89 graves and approximately 400 potential excavation sites within the country. Pretoria stated that Angola, during its civil war, was where the largest number of ANC fighters perished under the apartheid regime.

The operation seeks to understand the reasons behind the fighters' presence in Angola, their connections with the MPLA during its conflict against UNITA, and the purpose of the training camps. This effort aims to shed light on a lesser-known chapter of history involving the African National Congress (ANC) and its struggle against apartheid.

The repatriation is a significant undertaking, bringing closure to families and acknowledging the sacrifices made by these individuals. The identification of numerous burial sites underscores the scale of the ANC's presence and the challenges faced by its members during their exile and fight for liberation.

Why it mattersSouth Africa is repatriating the remains of anti-apartheid fighters who died in exile in Angola, identifying 89 graves and 400 potential excavation sites.

Angola pushes for proactive conflict resolution mechanisms

The Angolan diplomat urged the African Union to move beyond reacting to crises and implement anticipatory solutions for lasting peace.

Angola is hosting a Union Executive Council meeting in its capital, Luanda, to prepare documents for an upcoming summit focused on strengthening conflict prevention and resolution mechanisms across the continent. Minister Téte António stated that Africa must develop anticipatory capabilities rather than merely responding to crises after they erupt, emphasizing that regional and continental cooperation remains the most secure path to enduring peace.

The meeting's opening speeches highlighted Angola's commitment to this cause, with the chairman of the African Union Executive Council, Édouard Bizimana, commending President João Lourenço's efforts. Lourenço, who leads the MPLA party and was appointed the African Union's champion for peace and reconciliation, is spearheading the summit's agenda, aiming to translate political declarations into practical, effective solutions.

Despite significant institutional progress within the African Union, including bodies like the Peace and Security Council and the Continental Early Warning System, Bizimana acknowledged that Africa still faces substantial challenges. These include persistent conflicts, political crises, terrorism, transnational organized crime, illicit trafficking, inter-state tensions, humanitarian crises, and forced displacement.

Why it mattersAngola's push for proactive conflict resolution directly impacts the stability and safety of African nations and their populations.

Angola Hosts African Union Summit on Conflict Prevention

The 21st Extraordinary Conference of the African Union convenes in Luanda this weekend to focus on conflict prevention and turning political commitments into actionable goals.

Luanda is hosting the 21st Extraordinary Conference of the African Union this weekend. The meeting is expected to bolster Angola's diplomatic standing. Discussions will center on strengthening conflict prevention mechanisms across the continent.

A key objective of the conference is to transform existing political commitments into concrete objectives. This includes establishing clear responsibilities, securing necessary funding, and setting defined timelines for implementation.

The summit aims to move beyond declarations by ensuring that political will translates into tangible progress on peace and security initiatives throughout Africa. Angola seeks to play a leading role in these diplomatic efforts.

Why it mattersThe conference affects African Union member states by aiming to create concrete plans and funding for conflict prevention initiatives.

Cabo Verde President Demands African Union Reform

José Maria Neves calls for greater efficiency and effectiveness in implementing decisions and reforms within the organization.

Cabo Verde's head of state, José Maria Neves, has called for a profound reform of the African Union (AU), emphasizing the need for increased efficiency and effectiveness in its operations. Speaking on the sidelines of the 21st Extraordinary Session of the Conference of Heads of State and Government in Angola, Neves highlighted the AU's persistent difficulty in implementing its decisions.

Neves, who was the first Portuguese-speaking African head of state to arrive in Luanda for the summit focused on conflict prevention, stressed that reforms must aim for a more efficient organization and better results. He proposed establishing a multilevel governance system across the continent, redistributing tasks among the AU, sub-regional bodies, and individual states, acknowledging that such changes involve complex structural and habitual adjustments.

The President argued that to effectively prevent and resolve conflicts, and overcome constitutional breaches, the AU must significantly improve its decision implementation capacity and embrace necessary reforms. He stated that the AU needs to become a relevant political actor, primarily through its influence, to achieve its objectives on the continent.

Why it mattersAfrican Union member states, including Cabo Verde, are affected by the organization's ability to implement decisions and enact reforms for conflict prevention and resolution.
