Agriculture · 2026-11-07

Angola's farm sector grows to 23% of GDP

The agricultural sector's contribution to Angola's GDP has nearly doubled in 10 years, now standing at 23%.

Angola's economy is undergoing a structural transformation that is reducing its reliance on external vulnerabilities, according to Minister of State for Economic Coordination José de Lima Massano.

The agricultural sector's weight in the national GDP has almost doubled over the past decade, reaching approximately 23%. The food segment now constitutes 58% of the manufacturing industry, with industrial production increasing by 66% in September compared to the same period last year, based on INE data.

Massano stated that this structural shift supports a path toward sustained disinflation, as food and non-alcoholic beverages account for 56% of Angola's consumer price index. He advocated for continued public incentives to attract investment in agriculture, manufacturing, logistics, and national production.

Why it mattersAngolan farmers and the agricultural sector are directly impacted by the growth in GDP contribution and the call for increased investment.

Politics · 2026-11-07

MPLA committee endorses Lourenço's re-candidacy

The party's central committee approved the report on candidacies for the IX Congress.

The MPLA's highest body between congresses met in Luanda to approve the report for the upcoming IX Congress. Two pre-candidates were excluded, leaving only the current President, João Lourenço, as the sole candidate for party leader. The meeting, presided over by Lourenço himself, also approved the report from the preparatory commission regarding candidacies.

The Central Committee expressed its recognition and support for Lourenço's leadership, highlighting his efforts in consolidating party unity and addressing political and organizational challenges. The final communiqué encouraged the leader to continue with determination to overcome future challenges and congratulated him on the approval of his candidacy.

The committee's deliberations are considered important for the regular progress of the IX Ordinary Congress preparation. The final document also emphasized strengthening unity, cohesion, and political responsibility among members for the congress and the new political cycle.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens who are members of the MPLA party will have João Lourenço as their only choice for party president.

Angolan residents decry bridge's poor state

Residents of Boa Esperança 2 in Kicolo are concerned about a deteriorating bridge and waste accumulation.

Residents of Boa Esperança 2 in Kicolo, Angola, are expressing deep concern over the deteriorating state of a local bridge, which poses daily risks, particularly for students. Barreto Pascoal, a resident, called for the communal administrator of Kicolo to engage with the community to address the infrastructure's problems.

Pascoal stated that the bridge's condition reflects broader issues in the community and that administrators must be close to the people to understand their realities. He added that the accumulation of solid waste and poor access roads worsen the difficulties faced by inhabitants.

Other residents, including Mingo Castro, emphasized the urgent need for authorities to act before a serious incident occurs, noting that people are forced to use the bridge daily despite its dangers. Cristiano Bolevo highlighted that infrastructure and sanitation issues directly impact families' quality of life.

Why it mattersresidents of boa esperança 2 face daily risks due to a decaying bridge and poor sanitation.

Business · 2026-11-07

Angola central bank revises economic growth to 6.15%

Governor Manuel Tiago Dias announced the revised GDP forecast for 2026 at a conference in Luanda.

The National Bank of Angola (BNA) has revised its economic growth projection for 2026 upwards to 6.15%. Governor Manuel Tiago Dias announced this figure in Luanda during a conference marking the BNA's 50th anniversary.

Dias stated that consistent inflation deceleration has allowed for a prudent easing of monetary policy, while real interest rates remain positive. He noted that inflation fell from 27.5% in 2024 to 15.7% in December 2025, reaching single digits since July and standing at 8.43% in September.

The central bank projects inflation to be around 8% by the end of 2026. The BNA's Monetary Policy Committee also highlighted the need for sustained, productive growth that generates jobs.

Why it mattersAngolan businesses and consumers will be affected by the revised economic growth forecast and the central bank's inflation control measures.
