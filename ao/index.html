Angolan NGOs demand electoral probe

Three civil society groups asked prosecutors to investigate alleged failures in the voter registration process.

Angolan non-governmental organizations HANDEKA, UYELE, and MOSAICO have requested the Public Prosecutor's Office to open a judicial inquiry into the Ministry of Territorial Administration.

The groups cited the ministry's alleged failure to provide clarifications since June regarding the unofficial electoral registration process for next year's general elections. They also seek independent audits of the citizen database and financial accounts related to the 'proof of life' operation.

The NGOs argue that electoral registration transparency is a demand of a democratic state, not a political concession, and they are committed to ensuring the credibility of the electoral process through legal means.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens are directly affected by the transparency and integrity of the electoral registration process for upcoming general elections.

Kiluanji Kia Henda film premieres at Rotterdam festival

The Angolan artist's new film, "Karl Marx, Luanda," combines African science fiction with spaghetti westerns.

Angolan artist Kiluanji Kia Henda's new film, "Karl Marx, Luanda," will premiere at the 56th Rotterdam Film Festival from January 28 to February 7, 2027. The film imagines Angola renewing its commitment to communism and tells a love story with time travel. It blends African science fiction with spaghetti western elements, as described by the festival.

The movie is a co-production between Angola and Portugal, with Wonder Maria Filmes involved. This marks Henda's return to the Rotterdam festival, where he previously presented a performance in 2021. Henda, born in 1979, is a multidisciplinary artist who explores collective memory, public spaces, and society, often reflecting on post-colonialism.

Henda received Angola's National Prize for Art and Culture in 2012 and the Frieze Artist Award in 2017. His work has been exhibited internationally, including at the Centre George Pompidou in Paris, Tate Modern in London, and the Guggenheim in Bilbao. He is also creating a Memorial to Enslaved People for Lisbon.

Why it mattersAngolan culture is represented internationally through Kiluanji Kia Henda's new film at a major European festival.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Angola uses penal code against journalists

The constitution guarantees free expression, but courts prosecute reporters for defamation.

Angolan authorities are systematically using the penal code to prosecute journalists, despite the constitution guaranteeing freedom of expression and the press. This practice violates international commitments made by the state. The constitution's articles 40 and 44, which protect freedom of expression and the press, are being ignored.

Journalists in Angola often face criminal charges for defamation, slander, or insult when they report on corruption, nepotism, or abuse of power. These legal actions are frequently initiated by politicians and corrupt groups who seek to silence critical reporting. The justice system is described as being controlled by political parties.

The criminalization of speech in Angola inverts the basic logic of democracy, where public scrutiny is a duty of the press and a right of citizens. Instead, legal protections for 'honor' are used to silence investigative journalism and critical voices.

Why it mattersJournalists in Angola face prosecution under the penal code when reporting on corruption and abuse of power, undermining press freedom.
