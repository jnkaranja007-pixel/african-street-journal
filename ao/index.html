South Africa repatriates anti-apartheid fighters' remains from Angola

Angola's civil war was the site of the highest number of ANC combatant deaths during apartheid, according to Pretoria.

South Africa has initiated a repatriation operation for the remains of anti-apartheid activists who died in exile in Angola. Investigative teams have identified 89 graves and approximately 400 potential excavation sites within the country. Pretoria stated that Angola, during its civil war, was where the largest number of ANC fighters perished under the apartheid regime.

The operation seeks to understand the reasons behind the fighters' presence in Angola, their connections with the MPLA during its conflict against UNITA, and the purpose of the training camps. This effort aims to shed light on a lesser-known chapter of history involving the African National Congress (ANC) and its struggle against apartheid.

The repatriation is a significant undertaking, bringing closure to families and acknowledging the sacrifices made by these individuals. The identification of numerous burial sites underscores the scale of the ANC's presence and the challenges faced by its members during their exile and fight for liberation.

Why it mattersSouth Africa is repatriating the remains of anti-apartheid fighters who died in exile in Angola, identifying 89 graves and 400 potential excavation sites.

Angola Hosts African Union Conference on Conflict Prevention

The 21st Extraordinary Conference of the African Union convenes in Luanda this weekend to focus on conflict prevention and turning political commitments into actionable goals.

Luanda is hosting the 21st Extraordinary Conference of the African Union this weekend. The meeting aims to strengthen conflict prevention mechanisms across the continent and ensure that political agreements are translated into concrete objectives, responsibilities, and funding.

Angola expects to enhance its diplomatic standing through this conference. The discussions will center on practical steps to achieve peace and stability, moving beyond declarations to establish clear timelines and accountability for conflict resolution efforts.

The conference brings together leaders and delegates to address pressing security challenges. The focus on turning commitments into tangible outcomes signals a drive for more effective and results-oriented diplomacy within the African Union framework.

Why it mattersThe conference affects leaders and delegates by focusing on conflict prevention and turning political commitments into actionable goals with financing and deadlines.

Opposition Reports Thousands of Angolan Children Study Under Trees

UNITA claims the education system faces structural problems, including a deficit of schools and teachers, impacting millions of young Angolans.

The opposition party UNITA has highlighted a critical issue in Angola's education system, stating that thousands of children are forced to study outdoors. Raul Tati, the first prime minister of UNITA's shadow government, described this as a "harsh reality" and cited the Uíge province, where between forty thousand and forty-five thousand students learn under trees. This situation arises as the country prepares for the new academic year.

UNITA's assessment indicates a significant need for new school infrastructure, estimating that Angola requires two thousand to eight thousand additional schools to accommodate approximately four-point-five million children and youths aged five to eighteen who are not enrolled. The party also noted that out of seven thousand seven hundred public schools, twenty-five percent, totaling one thousand nine hundred and twenty-five, are in precarious conditions, with three hundred of these located in the capital, Luanda.

Beyond infrastructure, the opposition party pointed to a substantial teacher shortage. Angola's public school system employs two hundred and six thousand teachers, but UNITA asserts that sixty thousand to seventy-eight thousand more are needed to meet national educational demands. While the government has opened a public tender for eight thousand new teachers, UNITA's Raul Tati stated this figure is insufficient, proposing an annual recruitment of at least twelve thousand teachers over five years.

Why it mattersChildren and young people in Angola are affected by the lack of adequate school facilities and a shortage of teachers, preventing them from attending school or receiving education in suitable conditions.
