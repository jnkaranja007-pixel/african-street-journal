Angola registers over five million voters

The electoral registration process, which began on June 15, 2026, has seen more than five million eligible citizens update their details.

Over five million Angolans have updated their voter registration details since the process began on June 15, 2026. The national director of Official Electoral Registration, Pascoal Santana, announced the figures following a meeting with the Minister of Territorial Administration, Daniel Neto, and the National Electoral Commission (CNE). This update is part of preparations for Angola's sixth general elections, expected in 2027.

The registration drive is being conducted through 648 service points in municipal and communal administrations, supplemented by over 600 mobile brigades. Santana noted that these mobile units are crucial for reaching citizens in remote areas. However, the onset of the rainy season has created access challenges in some regions, prompting the government to deploy special transport like Unimog vehicles and helicopters to ensure continued registration.

The registration process for citizens living abroad is scheduled to commence on January 5, 2027, and conclude on March 31, 2027. Santana stated that the technical and material conditions for this international phase are being addressed. The electoral update aims to confirm citizens' presence and update their personal information, serving as proof of life for the electoral database.

Why it mattersOver five million eligible Angolan citizens are directly affected by the electoral registration update, which is a prerequisite for participating in the upcoming 2027 general elections.

Angolan bishops urge focus on citizens' needs

Catholic leaders call for transparent resource management and prioritizing family farming.

Angolan Catholic bishops meeting in Lunda Norte stated that citizens' hope remains stifled by social, political, cultural, and religious issues. The II Plenary Assembly of the Episcopal Conference of Angola and São Tomé (CEAST) began this weekend and continues until Thursday.

CEAST president José Manuel Imbamba said this environment harms emotional and psychological balance, leaving remote communities behind and vulnerable to manipulation. He stressed the urgent need to place people at the center of public policy to overcome social exclusion.

The bishops advocate for transparent resource management, prioritizing family agriculture and local production, valuing professionals like teachers and doctors, and combating corruption and waste. They also acknowledged the government's efforts in peacebuilding and national reconciliation, citing the recent inauguration of the Memorial to the Victims of Political Conflicts.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens are affected by social, political, cultural, and religious issues that stifle their hope and impact their emotional well-being.

Angola's MPLA party mourns former MP Ekuikui IV

The ruling party praised the traditional leader for his resistance against colonialism and devotion to national unity.

The MPLA party announced the death of Ekuikui IV, also known as the king of Bailundo, who served as a Member of Parliament for the party. The announcement came via a statement from the MPLA's political bureau.

In April 2008, then-deputy campaign coordinator João Lourenço presented Ekuikui IV with a Land Rover vehicle in Huambo province. The king thanked José Eduardo dos Santos and expressed satisfaction with road improvements and living conditions in Bailundo.

The MPLA statement recognized Ekuikui IV as an example of resistance against Portuguese colonialism, patriotism, and dedication to peace, national reconciliation, freedom, and development.

Why it mattersThe death of a former MPLA Member of Parliament and traditional leader affects those who respected his political and traditional roles.

Angolans track kwanza exchange rate daily

Financial markets and organizational management are key to building a sustainable economy, according to Folha 8.

The daily fluctuations of the kwanza exchange rate are now a common topic among Angolans, extending beyond economists to everyday citizens.

Financial markets and organizational management are presented as crucial components for developing a sustainable economy, with finances acting as the economy's circulatory system.

Resources, when correctly channeled, drive economic growth, while mismanagement leads to imbalances, inflation, unemployment, and reduced investment, impacting the cost of bread, medicine, and credit access.

Why it mattersAngolans are directly affected by the kwanza's exchange rate, which influences the price of essential goods and access to credit.

UNITA leader challenged over Cabinda policy

An open letter urges the opposition leader to consult Cabinda's people and diaspora.

An open letter addressed to the leader of UNITA challenges the party's approach to Cabinda. The author, writing from Folha 8, states that for over two decades, Cabinda has been a site for opposition electoral strategies. The letter criticizes UNITA for presenting projects for Cabinda without prior consultation with its people.

The author admires the UNITA leader's political courage and past positions on Cabinda, despite their debatable nature. The letter specifically references the leader's recent economic vision for Cabinda, presented without internal popular consultation. It suggests UNITA should gather popular contributions from Cabinda's municipalities for submission to the Angolan parliament.

The writer argues that UNITA should not rely on a few deputies or supporters to decide Cabinda's future. The letter also highlights the overlooked Cabindan diaspora in Europe, urging the UNITA leader to engage with them. The author challenges the leader to directly discuss and listen to Cabindans both at home and in Europe.

Why it mattersThe people of Cabinda and its diaspora are directly affected by political strategies and decisions made by Angolan opposition parties like UNITA.
