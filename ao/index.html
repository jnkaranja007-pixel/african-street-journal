UNITA returns to Uíge after police clashes

The opposition party will hold a political meeting in the city where its founder's anniversary event was disrupted by police.

The opposition UNITA party has scheduled its next political committee meeting in Uíge, the same city where police intervened during an event commemorating its founder, Jonas Savimbi, on August 8. At least 31 people were injured during that police action. UNITA has filed criminal complaints against the provincial governor and police commander, while authorities accuse the party of politicizing the incident.

The meeting, set for September 4, will focus on the country's political, social, and economic situation under the theme "With Unity and Inclusion, Achieve the Alternation of Power in 2027, UNITA — the Hope of Angolans." The party aims to assess its performance over June, July, and August and strategize for its goal of defeating the ruling MPLA in the upcoming elections.

UNITA also plans to review its recent parliamentary activities, including the rejection of several national-level proposals by the MPLA-dominated National Assembly. The party has criticized the consistent rejection of its initiatives, citing identical decision-making processes and signatories across multiple denied requests, including inquiries into a nationwide communication blackout and flooding in Benguela.

Why it mattersUNITA's decision to hold a meeting in Uíge highlights ongoing political tensions and the party's efforts to challenge the ruling MPLA.

Kwanza Stabilizes After 2023 Decline, Inflation Slows

Angola's currency has stabilized since late 2024, contributing to a significant drop in inflation rates by March 2026.

Following a sharp devaluation in 2023, Angola's Kwanza has shown stability from late 2024 through March 2026, according to IMF data. This currency stabilization, combined with restrictive monetary policies and structural factors, has led to a notable decrease in inflation. The annual inflation rate fell from 27.5% in December 2024 to 15.7% in December 2025, and further to 12.42% by March 2026.

Despite the slowing inflation, prices continue to rise, impacting household purchasing power. While a depreciated currency can enhance competitiveness and favor domestic production by making imports more expensive, it also increases the cost for businesses importing machinery and for merchants, ultimately raising final consumer prices. The evidence suggests that currency devaluation alone is insufficient for economic competitiveness; increased production and reduced reliance on imports are also crucial.

Angola's economy remains structurally vulnerable due to its heavy reliance on the oil sector for export revenue and foreign exchange. This dependence creates significant external vulnerabilities, with the national currency experiencing pressure when oil revenues decline and finding relief when they increase. The challenge lies in diversifying the economy beyond oil to achieve sustainable development and greater economic resilience.

Why it mattersBusinesses face increased costs for imported machinery and goods, while consumers experience rising prices despite slowing inflation.

Business · 2026-09-03

Angola Tax Authority Trains Staff on Financial Crimes

The Angolan Tax Authority (AGT) is enhancing its capacity to combat fiscal fraud, money laundering, and corruption through a specialized training program.

Angolan tax officials are undergoing training to better identify and address fiscal fraud, money laundering, and corruption. The initiative, organized by the Angolan Tax Authority (AGT) in collaboration with the Attorney General's Office, aims to equip tax technicians and other relevant personnel with enhanced knowledge on these interconnected financial crimes.

AGT administrator José Leiria stated that the training focuses on understanding fiscal fraud as a crime, defining AGT's role, coordinating with the Attorney General's Office, and guiding investigative bodies. He emphasized that fiscal fraud reduces state revenue, while corruption weakens the nation and damages its reputation. Attorney General Pedro Mendes de Carvalho highlighted the need for technical preparation and institutional cooperation to fight economic and financial crime.

Noémia Ido of the AGT noted that tax crimes are often linked to money laundering and specifically mentioned issues with the Work Income Tax (IRT), where some companies retain taxes but do not remit them to the state. Similar problems have been identified with Value Added Tax (VAT), involving improper collection and non-remittance by businesses.

Why it mattersThe training aims to improve the state's ability to collect taxes and combat financial crimes, which directly impacts government revenue and economic stability.
