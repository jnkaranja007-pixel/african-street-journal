Angola promotes economy in China

Angola aims to attract foreign investment and expand trade relations at a Chinese market platform.

Angola is participating in a major Chinese trade platform to showcase its economic potential and attract foreign investment. The official objective is to identify new business and cooperation opportunities, according to a government statement.

The Angolan delegation, led by Minister Rui Miguêns de Oliveira, aims to strengthen economic and commercial ties with China. This presence seeks to boost Angola's international trade participation and promote the diversification of its economy.

Separately, a visa waiver agreement signed on September 4, 2026, will allow Angolan citizens and Macau residents to enter each other's territories without visas, facilitating tourism and trade.

Cabinda elites urged to abandon armed struggle

A call for a non-partisan, peaceful, citizen-led alternative to the FLEC's legacy in Cabinda.

Cabinda's sensible elites must abandon the militaristic legacy of the FLEC and build a non-partisan, peaceful, citizen-led alternative. The Front for the Liberation of the Enclave of Cabinda (FLEC), now divided into factions, historically embodied resistance but its armed struggle and internal divisions have weakened the cause. This approach has inadvertently aided Luanda's justification for a large security presence, citing counter-terrorism and oil infrastructure protection.

Insisting on clandestine action or sporadic clashes has closed doors to Western and African chancelleries, which are traditionally hostile to border changes through force. The current impasse of the armed struggle has trapped Cabinda's cause, preventing its voice from being heard with dignity internationally. The focus on upcoming Angolan elections and their customary promises to Cabinda elites leaves international law as the only remaining recourse.

To be heard by the international community, from the African Union to the United Nations and the European Union, Cabindans must change their political approach. International bodies do not negotiate with isolated insurgents but with representative, peaceful actors armed with international law. The 1885 Treaty of Simulambuco and the Namibe Memorandum of Understanding provide a solid legal foundation for this struggle, which should now be pursued in international courts and before the African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights.

Why it mattersCabindans are affected by the legacy of armed struggle which has hindered their ability to be heard internationally and has been used by Luanda to justify security measures.

Angola hosts seminar on historical reparations

Mbanza Kongo will host the III International Seminar on Historical Reparations from November 9 to 14.

Angola will celebrate 51 years of independence on November 11, with Mbanza Kongo hosting the III International Seminar on Historical Reparations from November 9 to 14. The city was chosen because it was where the first ships of invaders landed, according to Tânia de Carvalho, president of the Angolan Association Osoko. The seminar aims to position Angola regarding its roadmap for historical reparations.

The event, organized with the International Committee for Reparations, seeks to gather specialists, academics, politicians, and social activists to conceive a document for the African Union and the United Nations. Carvalho stated that reparations are not just monetary and that Portugal must first acknowledge the crimes committed in Angola.

Carvalho said the United Nations classified the slave trade as the greatest crime against humanity. The association wants to initiate a debate on how this process would work in Angola, with experts developing a document for international bodies.

Why it mattersAngolans are affected by the ongoing debate on historical reparations and the potential for Portugal to acknowledge past crimes.

Angola agriculture sector grows to 23% of GDP

The agro-pastoral sector's contribution to the national economy has nearly doubled in 10 years, according to Minister José de Lima Massano.

Angola's economy is transforming, with the agro-pastoral sector nearly doubling its contribution to the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) over the past decade, now standing at approximately 23%. Minister of State for Economic Coordination José de Lima Massano announced this growth, noting that while oil exports still dominate foreign exchange, the agricultural sector's expansion is significant. The food segment alone constitutes 58% of the manufacturing industry, and industrial production rose 66% in September compared to the same period last year, according to the National Statistics Institute (INE).

Massano, speaking at the 50th anniversary of the National Bank of Angola (BNA), highlighted the importance of these figures, as food and non-alcoholic beverages account for 58% of Angola's Consumer Price Index. He stated that this structural economic shift supports the path towards sustained disinflation, or a slowdown in price increases. The minister advocated for continued public incentives to attract further investment in agriculture, manufacturing, logistics, and the promotion of national production.

The minister also reflected on Angola's history of high inflation, including hyperinflation, over the 50 years since independence. He credited legislative reforms and adjustments to monetary policy instruments for creating a new paradigm and mitigating inflationary pressures. Currently, Angola is experiencing price stability, and Massano stressed the need to safeguard these achievements against renewed inflationary pressures.

Why it mattersAngola's economic transformation, driven by agricultural growth, is helping to reduce inflation and stabilize prices for consumers.
