South Africa repatriates anti-apartheid fighters' remains from Angola

Angola's civil war hosted numerous anti-apartheid militants who died in exile, with South African teams identifying 89 graves and 400 potential excavation sites.

South Africa has initiated a repatriation operation for the remains of anti-apartheid militants who died while in exile in Angola. Investigative teams have identified 89 graves and approximately 400 sites for potential excavation within Angola. Pretoria stated that Angola, during its civil war, was where the largest number of ANC combatants died under the apartheid regime.

The presence of these militants in Angola raises questions about their connections to the MPLA, which was engaged in conflict with UNITA at the time. The purpose of these training camps and the specific roles played by the anti-apartheid fighters within Angola's complex political landscape are subjects of historical analysis.

This operation sheds light on a lesser-known chapter of the anti-apartheid struggle, highlighting the sacrifices made by individuals who sought refuge and engaged in military training abroad. The identification of burial sites underscores the significant presence and impact of these exiled fighters on Angolan soil during a turbulent period.

Why it mattersThe repatriation of anti-apartheid fighters' remains affects South African diaspora in Angola by acknowledging their historical presence and sacrifices in the country.

Angola Hosts AU Summit on Conflict Prevention

Luanda is the venue for the 21st Extraordinary African Union Conference this weekend, focusing on turning political commitments into actionable goals with clear timelines.

Angola's capital, Luanda, is hosting the 21st Extraordinary African Union Conference this weekend. The meeting aims to strengthen the prevention of conflicts across the continent. Angola seeks to enhance its diplomatic standing through this event.

The conference's agenda centers on transforming existing political commitments into concrete objectives. This includes establishing clear responsibilities, securing necessary funding, and setting specific deadlines for conflict prevention initiatives.

This gathering is expected to foster a more proactive approach to continental security. By focusing on measurable outcomes, the AU hopes to improve its effectiveness in addressing and mitigating conflicts before they escalate.

Why it mattersThe conference's focus on conflict prevention and turning political commitments into concrete goals affects investors by potentially creating a more stable business environment.

Angola Water Company Reduces Pumping Capacity Due to Drought

The Empresa Provincial de Águas e Saneamento de Benguela is implementing a dry season plan to manage lower water levels in the Catumbela River, impacting supply to four.

The Empresa Provincial de Águas e Saneamento de Benguela (EPASB-EP) announced it is enacting a dry season operational plan to optimize water distribution and mitigate the effects of reduced water availability. The company stated that the current dry period has led to a significant decrease in water levels in the Catumbela River, which is the primary source for the region's water supply system.

This reduction in water flow has directly impacted the pumping capacity at the Raw Water Pumping Station (EBAB), which has decreased from 4,600 cubic meters per hour to 3,000 cubic meters per hour. The EPASB-EP indicated that this diminished intake affects production levels and the regularity of water supply to the municipalities of Benguela, Lobito, Navegantes, and Baía Farta.

The company also noted that the reduced water volume affects the supply intended for water tanker operators. To address the situation, EPASB-EP will activate its dry season maneuver plan, though specific measures were not detailed. The company acknowledged the existing constraints and requested understanding from its customers regarding the situation.

Why it mattersThe reduction in water supply affects the municipalities of Benguela, Lobito, Navegantes, and Baía Farta, impacting residents and businesses that rely on the EPASB-EP system.

Portugal Extradites Angolan Banker Linked to Cybercrime Network

Angolan authorities received a former banker extradited from Portugal, who is sought in connection with a complex international cybercrime ring targeting financial institutions.

Angola's Criminal Investigation Service (SIC) announced Tuesday, August 25, the extradition of a suspect from Portugal. The individual, a former banker, had fled to Portugal and is now wanted by Angolan authorities for alleged involvement in an international criminal network. This network is under investigation for conducting cyberattacks against Angolan banking institutions.

The SIC described the investigation, initiated in January 2025, as highly complex and credited it with identifying a structured criminal organization. This group, reportedly comprising Angolan, Nigerian, Ivorian, Beninese, and Lebanese nationals, allegedly divided tasks to target banks in Angola and the wider region. Authorities stated that the investigation uncovered both completed and attempted attacks, with their intervention preventing some attempts and safeguarding significant funds.

The extradited suspect previously worked at a bank in Negage municipality, Uíge province. Investigators allege the individual provided access credentials and introduced a computer into the bank's internal network, facilitating remote access to the institution's systems. The attack, which occurred in January 2025, reportedly caused economic damages exceeding six billion kwanzas.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because a former banker extradited for cybercrimes allegedly caused over six billion kwanzas in damages to Angolan banks.
