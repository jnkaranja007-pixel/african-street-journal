Kiluanji Kia Henda film premieres at Rotterdam festival

The Angolan artist's new film, "Karl Marx, Luanda," combines African science fiction with spaghetti westerns.

Angolan artist Kiluanji Kia Henda's new film, "Karl Marx, Luanda," will premiere at the 56th Rotterdam Film Festival from January 28 to February 7, 2027. The film imagines Angola renewing its commitment to communism and tells a love story with time travel. It blends African science fiction with spaghetti western elements, as described by the festival.

The movie is a co-production between Angola and Portugal, with Wonder Maria Filmes involved. This marks Henda's return to the Rotterdam festival, where he previously presented a performance in 2021. Henda, born in 1979, is a multidisciplinary artist who explores collective memory, public spaces, and society, often reflecting on post-colonialism.

Henda received Angola's National Prize for Art and Culture in 2012 and the Frieze Artist Award in 2017. His work has been exhibited internationally, including at the Centre George Pompidou in Paris, Tate Modern in London, and the Guggenheim in Bilbao. He is also creating a Memorial to Enslaved People for Lisbon.

Why it mattersAngolan culture is represented internationally through Kiluanji Kia Henda's new film at a major European festival.

Angolan opposition forms civic monitoring platform

Four political parties and two civil society groups created a platform to ensure electoral integrity for 2027.

Four Angolan political parties, Bloco Democrático (BD), PDP-ANA, PNSA, and PPA, have joined forces with the Angolan Federation of Associations of People with Disabilities (FAPED) and the MUDEI movement to establish a Joint Civic Monitoring Platform.

This platform emerged from a National Conference on the Electoral Process (CNPE 2026) held in Luanda, which gathered over 300 representatives from civil society, youth, and human rights groups in late September to propose solutions for electoral integrity.

The group aims to create specific legislation defining electoral red lines and will demand strict adherence from the National Electoral Commission (CNE) and the Ministry of Territorial Administration (MAT) regarding vote counting, data integrity, and voter registration accessibility.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens will have a new platform to monitor the integrity of the 2027 general elections.

Angola's MPLA summons general Higino Carneiro for disciplinary hearing

The ruling party's Central Committee will investigate alleged document forgery and ethical breaches by the general.

Angolan general Higino Carneiro was summoned on Thursday, October 29, 2026, to appear before the MPLA's Central Committee Discipline and Audit Commission on Friday at 9:00 AM. This summons follows the committee's October 21 decision to nullify Carneiro's candidacy for party leadership and initiate disciplinary proceedings against him.

Carneiro had intended to challenge current MPLA president and President of the Republic, João Lourenço, at the party's IX Congress on December 9-10. His candidacy, submitted on June 25, was initially rejected due to alleged "grave irregularities," including falsified signatures and misuse of identification documents, according to MPLA official Job Capapinha.

The MPLA's Political Bureau upheld the rejection, forwarding the case to the Central Committee, which confirmed the nullification and decided to inform the Attorney General's Office due to strong indications of document forgery. The committee cited Carneiro's "voluntary and conscious violation of ethical and moral duties" as grounds for the disciplinary action.

Politics · 2026-10-30

Angola uses penal code against journalists

The constitution guarantees free expression, but courts prosecute reporters for defamation.

Angolan authorities are systematically using the Penal Code to prosecute journalists, despite the Constitution guaranteeing freedom of expression and the press. This practice violates international commitments made by the state, according to Folha 8.

Journalists in Angola who report on corruption, nepotism, or abuses of power often face criminal charges such as insult, slander, or defamation. These accusations are used by political figures and groups to silence critical reporting and investigative journalism.

This approach criminalizes speech and inverts the basic logic of democracy, where public scrutiny is a duty of the press and a right of citizens. The persistent use of the courts under the guise of protecting 'honor' neutralizes investigative journalism and critical voices.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens are affected as the government uses the courts to silence critical reporting and investigative journalism, undermining freedom of expression.
