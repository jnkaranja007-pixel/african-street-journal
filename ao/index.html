Angola Pushes for Proactive Conflict Resolution Mechanisms

Luanda hosts African Union meeting urging a shift from crisis reaction to anticipatory peacebuilding efforts.

Angola's foreign minister, Téte António, addressed the African Union Executive Council in Luanda, emphasizing the need for Africa to move beyond reacting to crises and instead develop proactive mechanisms for conflict prevention and resolution. The meeting, held in the Angolan capital, was dedicated to preparing documents for an upcoming summit focused on strengthening these preventative measures.

António stated that regional and continental cooperation remains the most secure path to lasting peace, advocating for the Luanda summit to transition from political declarations to practical, effective solutions. The African Union's Executive Council president, Édouard Bizimana, praised Angolan President João Lourenço's commitment to enhancing conflict resolution mechanisms across the continent. Lourenço, who leads the MPLA party, has been appointed the AU's champion for peace and reconciliation.

Despite significant institutional progress within the AU, including bodies like the Peace and Security Council and the Continental Early Warning System, Bizimana acknowledged persistent challenges. These include ongoing conflicts, political crises, terrorism, transnational organized crime, illicit trafficking, inter-state tensions, humanitarian crises, and forced displacement across Africa.

Why it mattersThe discussions in Luanda affect African nations by aiming to create more effective systems for preventing and resolving conflicts, potentially leading to greater stability.

Cabo Verde President Demands African Union Reform

José Maria Neves calls for greater efficiency and effectiveness in decision implementation by the African Union.

Cabo Verde's head of state, José Maria Neves, has called for a profound reform of the African Union, emphasizing the need for increased efficiency and effectiveness in implementing its decisions. Speaking on the sidelines of the 21st Extraordinary Session of the Conference of Heads of State and Government in Angola, Neves highlighted the organization's persistent difficulty in translating resolutions into tangible outcomes. He advocated for a multi-level governance system that redistributes responsibilities among the African Union, sub-regional bodies, and individual member states.

Neves, who was the first Portuguese-speaking African head of state to arrive in the Angolan capital for the summit focused on conflict prevention, acknowledged that implementing such reforms involves complex structural, normative, and habitual changes. He stressed that to effectively prevent and resolve conflicts, and overcome constitutional breaches, the Union must become a more relevant political actor capable of influencing continental affairs.

The summit's primary challenge is to operationalize the Early Warning and Early Action Mechanism, a concept long discussed within the organization. Neves's remarks underscore a broader concern about the Union's capacity to act decisively and implement its mandates, which he believes is crucial for maintaining stability and achieving its objectives across Africa.

Why it mattersThe President of Cabo Verde is affected by the African Union's difficulty in implementing decisions, which hinders conflict prevention and resolution efforts.
