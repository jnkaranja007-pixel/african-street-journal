Angola rural teen pregnancy rate hits 42.5%

UN committee urges Angola to fund national strategy for adolescent sexual education and reproductive health services.

Teenage pregnancy in Angola's rural areas has reached 42.5%, according to a recent United Nations committee report on women's rights. The committee expressed concern over the high incidence, which affects 27.1% of women and girls aged 15 to 19 nationwide.

The UN committee urged Angola to adopt a national strategy with adequate resources to prevent adolescent pregnancy. Recommendations include offering age-appropriate sexual education and access to confidential, adolescent-friendly sexual and reproductive health services, along with voluntary contraception.

The report also calls for stricter measures against sexual violence and child marriage in Angola. It highlighted significant barriers women face in accessing economic resources and recommended expanding programs to promote female entrepreneurship and ease access to low-interest loans.

Why it mattersAdolescent girls in rural Angola are disproportionately affected by pregnancy, with 42.5% of them becoming pregnant.

Politics · 2026-11-03

Jonas Savimbi's legacy highlighted by UNITA founder's foundation

The foundation emphasized Savimbi's commitment to Angolan identity and his role in challenging Portuguese colonial rule.

The Jonas Savimbi Foundation highlighted the founder's historical and political journey, emphasizing his commitment to Angolan identity and cultural origins. Isaías Samakuva, former UNITA leader, stated that understanding Savimbi's path means understanding the struggle against Portuguese colonial rule, as Savimbi believed freedom had to be won, not given.

Samakuva further noted that Savimbi's nationalism extended beyond military action, viewing war as a tool for peace, and pointed to Savimbi's diplomatic involvement in Angolan independence, citing the Alvor and Bicesse Accords as demonstrations of his negotiation skills. Manuel Savinhemba also spoke, highlighting Savimbi's role in valuing Angolans and including those historically marginalized.

The foundation stressed Savimbi's vision of independence as more than just a flag or government, but as a state built on social justice, inclusion, and economic autonomy for better living conditions for all citizens. Samakuva argued that true freedom requires addressing deep social and economic inequalities, ensuring national wealth benefits everyone, not just a few.

Why it mattersAngolans are reminded of the historical context of their independence struggle and the ideals of social justice and economic autonomy.

African Union and FAJ condemn attacks on journalists

The African Union and the Federation of African Journalists highlighted evolving threats and persistent impunity for crimes against media professionals.

The African Union's Commissioner for Political Affairs, Peace and Security, Bankole Adeoye, and the President of the Federation of African Journalists (FAJ), Omar Faruk Osman, issued a joint statement on the International Day to End Impunity for Crimes against Journalists. They expressed full solidarity with journalists and media professionals across Africa, honouring those killed, injured, detained, threatened, or forced into exile.

The officials stated that attacks on journalists are attacks on fundamental human rights, specifically the right to freedom of expression and access to information across Africa. They reaffirmed their commitment to a continent where journalists can work freely and safely, with crimes against them investigated and punished under the rule of law. The statement noted that women journalists face particular gender-based threats, harassment, and online violence.

This year's theme, "Threats evolve. Impunity persists," draws attention to how new technologies and evolving weaponry alter risks for journalists, especially in conflict zones, while perpetrators often escape accountability. The AU and FAJ urged member states to protect journalists' rights, investigate attacks, prosecute offenders, and provide effective reparations to victims.

Why it mattersJournalists across Africa, including those in Angola, face evolving threats and persistent impunity for crimes committed against them, impacting their safety and the public's access to information.
