Angolan opposition forms civic monitoring platform

Four political parties and two civil society groups created a joint platform to oversee the 2027 general elections.

Four Angolan political parties, Bloco Democrático (BD), PDP-ANA, PNSA, and PPA, have joined forces with disability and civil society groups FAPED and MUDEI to form a Joint Civic Monitoring Platform. The platform was established following a National Conference on the Electoral Process (CNPE 2026) held in Luanda, which gathered over 300 representatives from civil society and human rights organizations.

The CNPE 2026, promoted by the parties and their partners, aimed to gather proposals for the integrity of Angola's electoral process. The conference concluded with a commitment to make the CNPE a permanent mechanism for operational actions defending popular sovereignty. The new platform will work alongside a technical guidance manual for the 2027 general elections.

The platform's conclusions include a Charter of Recommendations for Electoral Transparency and a White Paper on the Electoral Process in Angola. Signatories demand the National Electoral Commission (CNE) strictly adhere to directives on vote counting, data integrity, and accessibility for people with disabilities, and require the Ministry of Territorial Administration (MAT) to ensure access to public service centers for voter registration.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens, particularly those with disabilities, will have their access to voting and the integrity of the 2027 general elections monitored by a new civic platform.

Angola uses penal code against journalists

The constitution guarantees free expression, but courts are used to silence critical reporting on corruption.

Angolan authorities are systematically using the Penal Code to prosecute journalists, despite the Constitution guaranteeing freedom of expression and the press. This practice violates the nation's international commitments on regional and global levels. The government frequently initiates criminal cases for insult, defamation, or slander against private and independent media professionals when they expose corruption, nepotism, or abuses of power.

Journalists in Angola face spurious accusations like insult, slander, or defamation when they report on public funds being misused by politicians and corrupt groups. These actions are often protected by a justice system described as a 'partidocracy'. In real democracies, public scrutiny is a duty of the press and a right of citizens, not a crime.

The persistent criminalization of speech in Angola inverts this fundamental logic under the guise of protecting 'honour'. This instrumentalizes courts to neutralize investigative journalism and silence critical voices. This practice directly ignores principles in articles 40 and 44 of the Angolan Constitution, which cover freedom of expression, information, and the press.

Why it mattersJournalists in Angola are facing criminal charges for reporting on corruption and abuse of power, undermining press freedom.

Angola's MPLA summons general Higino Carneiro for disciplinary hearing

The ruling party's Central Committee will investigate alleged document forgery and ethical breaches by the general.

Angolan general Higino Carneiro was summoned on Thursday, October 29, 2026, to appear before the MPLA's Central Committee Discipline and Audit Commission on Friday at 9:00 AM. This summons follows the committee's October 21 decision to nullify Carneiro's candidacy for party leadership and initiate disciplinary proceedings against him.

Carneiro had intended to challenge current MPLA president and President of the Republic, João Lourenço, at the party's IX Congress on December 9-10. His candidacy, submitted on June 25, was initially rejected due to alleged "grave irregularities," including falsified signatures and misuse of identification documents, according to MPLA official Job Capapinha.

The MPLA's Political Bureau upheld the rejection, forwarding the case to the Central Committee, which confirmed the nullification and decided to inform the Attorney General's Office due to strong indications of document forgery. The committee cited Carneiro's "voluntary and conscious violation of ethical and moral duties" as grounds for the disciplinary action.

Why it mattersGeneral Higino Carneiro faces disciplinary action and potential criminal charges for alleged document forgery within the ruling MPLA party.

Angola finds 31,000 ghost workers

Minister Teresa Rodrigues Dias announced that a new human resources system revealed thousands of inactive or deceased employees on the payroll.

Angola's public administration is paying for over 31,000 phantom employees, according to Minister Teresa Rodrigues Dias. The new integrated human resources management system, SINGERH, uncovered these discrepancies, which cost the state significant funds that could be saved with better control.

The minister stated that many listed employees have unknown whereabouts, lack identification, or are already retired or deceased. These cases, which raise suspicions of fictitious staff, are being handled with appropriate legal and administrative measures in coordination with the Ministry of Finance.

Organisations have been notified and given deadlines to justify these employee records. Those in regular situations need only verify their status with their respective human resources departments. SINGERH aims to integrate and modernise public administration's human resource management across the entire employment cycle.

Why it mattersAngolan taxpayers are losing money due to ghost workers on the government payroll.
