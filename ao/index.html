Angolan groups demand electoral transparency probe

Three civil society groups asked prosecutors to investigate the Ministry of Territorial Administration's handling of voter registration data.

Angolan civil society groups HANDEKA, UYELE, and MOSAICO have formally requested the Public Prosecutor's Office to launch a judicial inquiry into the Ministry of Territorial Administration (MAT). The organizations seek audits and accountability regarding the ministry's actions, particularly concerning the unofficial electoral registration process for next year's general elections.

The groups stated they have sought clarification from the MAT since June but received no response for over four months. They argue this institutional silence raises serious concerns about the ministry's legal duty to respond, the transparency of electoral administration, and citizens' right to scrutinize public administration acts. They specifically requested an investigation into the alleged failure to remove deceased individuals' records from the database of adult citizens.

The civil society organizations also called for an independent audit of the database and its computer file to assess integrity and legal compliance. They further demanded a financial audit of accounts and expenses related to the MAT's 'proof of life' operation. The groups emphasized that electoral registration transparency is a requirement of a democratic state, not a political concession, and reaffirmed their commitment to legality and citizens' political rights.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens' right to scrutinize public administration acts and ensure the credibility of the electoral process is directly affected by the lack of transparency in electoral registration.

Angola struggles to admit foreign workers for factories

Government invites investment but immigration hurdles delay project starts and increase costs.

Angola's government actively seeks foreign investment to build factories and diversify its oil-dependent economy. However, foreign companies face significant obstacles when trying to secure visas and work permits for essential technical staff. Some investors have already spent large sums on construction but cannot bring in the necessary specialists, potentially halting equipment installation and production trials.

This issue goes beyond simple visa delays, indicating a structural problem in coordinating government departments responsible for investment, immigration, and labor. Investors consider the ease of bringing in skilled professionals a key factor when deciding where to commit capital. Without clear, stable, and legal pathways for these workers, investment pledges risk remaining unfulfilled.

The process of bringing industrial projects online, especially in heavy industry, can take years and relies heavily on specialized engineers. Delays in obtaining work permits for these professionals can lead to idle equipment, construction setbacks, and increased financial burdens for companies. These permits are integral to the successful construction and operation of industrial projects.

Why it mattersForeign investors and their essential technical staff face difficulties entering Angola, potentially delaying or halting industrial projects and increasing operational costs.

Africa's general governance improves unevenly

Infrastructure, health, and education show gains, but security and rule of law decline.

Africa's general governance saw improvements between 2016 and 2025, despite global shocks, according to the IIAG 2026 report. Progress was uneven, driven by economic and human development gains in 44 countries, covering 68% of the continent's population. Infrastructure, in particular, advanced across 52 countries, representing 84% of the population, with mobile connectivity, energy, and internet access improving.

Education advanced in 34 countries (75% of the population) and health in 40 countries (87% of the population). However, security, rule of law, and participation declined in 34 and 31 countries respectively, affecting 78% and 60% of Africans. Despite this, security deterioration slowed from 2022, with 27 countries showing improvements.

The report also noted that 25 countries, home to nearly 47% of the population, achieved economic progress even as security worsened. Economic opportunities advanced more in countries with better security, seen in 20 nations representing 22% of the population.

Why it mattersAngola, like other African nations, experiences mixed governance progress, with gains in infrastructure and development offset by declines in security and rule of law.
