Angolan film "Kahitu" wins award in Brazil

The film, inspired by Uanhenga Xitu's work, addresses prejudice and social exclusion.

The Angolan film "Kahitu" received an honorable mention at the second International Film Festival of Paraty in Brazil. The movie, directed by Alberto Botelho, tells the story of a young man born with a physical disability facing prejudice and social exclusion.

This award marks the third international recognition for "Kahitu," which previously won Best Actor for Adriano Ferreira and an Honorable Mention at the 17th FESTin in Lisbon. The film explores themes of human dignity, inclusion, and Angolan socio-cultural realities.

The Paraty festival featured 55 films from seven countries, including Angola, Brazil, France, and Portugal, fostering cultural exchange. This latest award strengthens Angola's presence in international cinema circuits and highlights the quality of national productions.

Why it mattersThe film "Kahitu" has received international recognition, showcasing Angolan culture and social realities on a global stage.

Luanda faces prolonged fuel shortage

Motorists in Angola's capital are struggling to find gasoline, leading to long queues and traffic disruptions.

Motorists in Luanda, Angola, have been struggling for days to find gasoline at various filling stations. While diesel remains available, the lack of gasoline has forced drivers to search across the city for fuel, causing significant traffic congestion and long queues at the few stations that still have supply.

The ongoing shortage is causing growing concern among drivers, particularly those who rely on their vehicles for daily income. André Feliciano, a motorist, stated that the situation is compromising his professional activity, causing him to lose valuable work time while waiting in lines. Mauro Valente, another driver, called for authorities to explain the reasons for the scarcity and present solutions.

Drivers are desperate to understand the cause of the gasoline shortage and when the problem will be resolved. The situation is impacting daily livelihoods as people spend hours searching for fuel instead of working.

Why it mattersMotorists in Luanda are experiencing difficulties in obtaining gasoline, impacting their ability to work and earn a living.

Angola president barred from third term

President João Lourenço cannot seek re-election in 2027 due to constitutional term limits.

Angola faces a political transition as President João Lourenço is constitutionally barred from seeking a third term in 2027. This limitation, stemming from articles 113 and 110 of the Constitution, shapes the country's internal succession dynamics within the ruling MPLA party. The MPLA is navigating its most delicate leadership change since José Eduardo dos Santos left power.

The succession process within the MPLA is being kept secret to prevent premature internal disputes, according to Celso Filipe in Jornal de Negócios. Another report suggests João Lourenço has already chosen his successor, awaiting the opportune moment for announcement. However, concerns exist about potential factional crises or a dual leadership model where Lourenço retains party control while another figure leads the presidency, risking governmental paralysis.

Independent media highlight Angola's persistent internal socio-economic disparities, including a declining purchasing power and inflation, as major governmental obstacles. Editorials in O País call for institutional reforms and an end to oil dependency, arguing that 51 years of independence demand effective electoral democratization. This dual perspective reflects the tension between Angola's growing geoeconomic role and its internal challenges.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens face uncertainty regarding the country's political future and economic stability as the presidential term limit approaches.

Politics · 2026-11-11

Angolan Catholic bishops say hope is stifled by toxic influences

Bishops at CEAST's second plenary assembly in Lunda Norte call for people to be placed at the centre of public policy.

Catholic bishops of the Episcopal Conference of Angola and São Tomé (CEAST) are holding their II Plenary Assembly in Lunda Norte, in eastern Angola, running until Thursday. The conference's president, José Manuel Imbamba, an Angolan archbishop, said citizens' hope remains suffocated by social, political, cultural and religious toxins.

Imbamba said this environment seriously compromises people's emotional and psychological balance. The bishops said remote communities remain distant from the development table and are often subject to manipulation and exploitation. Imbamba said social exclusion remains an open wound and that people must be placed at the centre of all public policies.

Imbamba called for transparent management of resources, real priority for family agriculture and local production, and recognition of teachers and doctors, and urged a relentless fight against corruption, waste, favouritism and partisanship. He also praised the government's continued efforts on peace and national reconciliation, and welcomed the inauguration in Luanda on 14 October of the Memorial to the Victims of Political Conflicts, honouring those who died between 11 November 1975 and 4 April 2002.

Why it mattersThe call for family agriculture and local production priority bears directly on farmers, and the call to fight corruption and waste bears on public spending.
