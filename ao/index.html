Japan explores Angola cooperation strategies

Japanese business delegation and Angolan minister discuss maritime transport, logistics, and financial services to boost national production.

Angola and Japan are exploring enhanced cooperation in key economic sectors, following a meeting in Luanda between Angolan Minister of Planning Victor Hugo Guilherme and a Japanese business delegation led by Ambassador Hiroaki Sano. The discussions focused on maritime transport, logistics, and financial services, areas identified as having significant potential to stimulate national production, improve goods distribution, and strengthen Angola's connections to regional markets.

Minister Guilherme highlighted food security and human capital development as national priorities, advocating for the adoption of new agricultural technologies, training of skilled personnel, and integrated development of economic corridors, including the Lobito and Southern Corridors. Sato Joji, executive director of the Japan External Trade Organization (JETRO) in Nairobi, expressed Japanese companies' interest in forming long-term partnerships focused on technology transfer, job creation, national capacity building, and value chain development.

The Japanese delegation also showed interest in visiting projects and infrastructure, with planned trips to Huambo province and areas along the Lobito Corridor to assess opportunities in productive sectors. Cooperation with the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA) on national cadre training was also discussed, signaling Angola's effort to attract investment that can expand domestic output and reduce reliance on oil revenues.

Why it mattersAngolan businesses and workers may benefit from Japanese investment, technology transfer, and job creation opportunities.

Angolan youth urged to manage finances

A new analysis aims to guide young Angolans toward financial literacy, encouraging savings, investment, and entrepreneurship.

Young Angolans are being encouraged to develop strong financial management skills, encompassing earning, saving, and investing, to secure their future. The analysis emphasizes that understanding individual finances is directly linked to responsible behavior and active citizenship. It questions whether the current focus is solely on income generation or also on the ability to manage, protect, and grow acquired resources.

Financial education aims to shift the perception of economic success away from visible consumption towards the value of financial stability, savings, investment, and wealth accumulation. This involves a cultural shift where young people learn to prioritize and control expenses, avoid excessive debt, and plan effectively for the long term.

The core principles of financial literacy involve earning through work and opportunity creation, managing through planning and expense control, and multiplying through savings, investment, and productive activities. This approach connects financial decisions to personal behavior, influencing how individuals interpret economic success and build a stable future.

Why it mattersYoung Angolans are affected by learning how to manage their personal finances to build stability and wealth.

Angolan NGOs demand local government legislation

Four civil society groups urged the National Assembly to prioritize and approve pending legislation for the immediate implementation of local government.

A coalition of Angolan non-governmental organizations, including OMUNGA, UPANGE, Friends of Angola, and ALDA, has expressed concern over delays in establishing local governments. In an open letter to the National Assembly, they cited President João Lourenço's statements on the upcoming municipal elections and stressed the constitutional imperative to institutionalize local authorities. The groups are calling for the immediate inclusion of remaining local government legislation on the current legislative agenda for discussion and approval.

The NGOs advocate for broad public consultation, involving civil society, traditional authorities, and citizens across Angola's 21 provinces. They believe this inclusive process is crucial for shaping the model of local governance. President Lourenço had previously urged opposition parties to resolve parliamentary impasses hindering the creation of elected local councils, stating that consensus among parliamentary groups is essential for moving forward.

The organizations argue that the lack of agreement on key aspects of the local government legislative package is the primary cause of the delay, potentially leading to a setback in the decentralization of power. They are pushing for consensus-building between the ruling MPLA party and the main opposition UNITA to finalize the process and implement the long-awaited local government structures.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens are affected by the delay in establishing local governments, which hinders the decentralization of power and their participation in governance.

Angola president promises end to hunger by 2027

President João Lourenço claims the ruling MPLA party will reveal a method for Angolans to live without eating by the next general election.

Angolan President João Lourenço has stated that the ruling MPLA party will present a definitive solution in 2027 to enable Angolans to live without food. This claim follows a case in India where a yogi, Prahlad Jani, reportedly survived for 14 days without ingesting or expelling anything, under observation by a team of doctors chosen by the Indian Ministry of Defense. The MPLA government suggests this method could be taught to Angolans to combat persistent hunger.

The evidence for Jani's abilities was previously explored in a 2006 Discovery Channel documentary, where scientists observed him for 10 days without concluding he was a fraud. Former President José Eduardo dos Santos had also previously theorized about Angolans living without eating, reportedly compelling citizens through hardship to pursue this path. Jani himself stated his health was perfect after the fast, attributing it to divine will.

Despite these claims, attempts by Angolans to follow Jani's method have reportedly resulted in death before achieving the desired outcome. The MPLA's promise for 2027 suggests a belief that the party can deliver a guaranteed truth, akin to how the Indian government investigated Jani's claims. The effectiveness of this proposed solution remains uncertain, with past voluntary attempts proving fatal.

Why it mattersAngolan citizens are affected by the promise of a solution to hunger, a problem that has historically plagued the population.

Supreme Court unblocks politician's accounts

Mozambique's top court lifted a two-year freeze on Venâncio Mondlane's bank accounts, exposing flaws in the public prosecutor's case.

Mozambique's Supreme Court has ordered the unblocking of Venâncio Mondlane's bank accounts, which had been frozen for nearly two years. The court found that the freeze violated Mondlane's constitutional right to property, highlighting technical weaknesses in the Public Ministry's prosecution. This decision has raised questions about the integrity of the Mozambican justice system and its potential impact on regional and international perceptions.

The Public Ministry had frozen Mondlane's accounts based on allegations of conspiracy against state secrets and terrorism, using the Law on Extended Asset Loss and Asset Recovery. However, the Supreme Court's ruling indicated a failure by the prosecution to demonstrate a causal link between Mondlane's funds and the alleged criminal activities. This ruling serves as a significant setback for the prosecution and exposes investigative fragilities.

Mondlane intends to sue the Mozambican state from Lisbon for damages, a move that could create a sensitive diplomatic front and potentially affect foreign funding for the country. International law experts suggest this ruling could embolden regional human rights tribunals to question Mozambique's judicial credibility, particularly concerning the suppression of press freedom during Mondlane's trial.

Why it mattersVenâncio Mondlane is affected as his bank accounts were frozen for two years, impacting his financial activities.
