Angola says antiretroviral supplies are arriving despite funding cuts

The national AIDS institute says a public tender is bringing in medicines, while community programmes have been hit hardest by reduced external support.

Source: Noticias Lusofonas · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Angola has antiretroviral medicines in stock despite reduced external funding, Maria Furtado, director of the National Institute for the Fight Against AIDS, said at a workshop in Angola. She said the institute was monitoring supplies more closely to prevent treatment interruptions, and companies were delivering medicines after a public tender.

The institute received additional credit at the start of 2026, Furtado said. In 2025, 188,062 people received antiretroviral treatment, while about 377,000 people in Angola were living with HIV; the country recorded 21,000 new infections and 14,000 deaths associated with opportunistic diseases. External funding cuts have particularly affected non-governmental organisations’ community work.

Angola funds more than 86% of HIV tests, antiretrovirals and other supplies, Furtado said, identifying the Global Fund as one of the main external donors, with investment concentrated in three provinces. Mother-to-child transmission fell from 26% in 2019 to 13%. World AIDS Day is observed on 1 December.
