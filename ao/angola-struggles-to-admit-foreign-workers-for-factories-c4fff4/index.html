Angola struggles to admit foreign workers for factories

Government invites investment but immigration hurdles delay project starts and increase costs.

Source: Folha 8 · 2026-11-01 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Angola's government actively seeks foreign investment to build factories and diversify its oil-dependent economy. However, foreign companies face significant obstacles when trying to secure visas and work permits for essential technical staff. Some investors have already spent large sums on construction but cannot bring in the necessary specialists, potentially halting equipment installation and production trials.

This issue goes beyond simple visa delays, indicating a structural problem in coordinating government departments responsible for investment, immigration, and labor. Investors consider the ease of bringing in skilled professionals a key factor when deciding where to commit capital. Without clear, stable, and legal pathways for these workers, investment pledges risk remaining unfulfilled.

The process of bringing industrial projects online, especially in heavy industry, can take years and relies heavily on specialized engineers. Delays in obtaining work permits for these professionals can lead to idle equipment, construction setbacks, and increased financial burdens for companies. These permits are integral to the successful construction and operation of industrial projects.

Why it mattersForeign investors and their essential technical staff face difficulties entering Angola, potentially delaying or halting industrial projects and increasing operational costs.
