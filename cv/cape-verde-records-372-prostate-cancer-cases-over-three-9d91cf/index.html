Cape Verde records 372 prostate cancer cases over three years

Carla Barbosa said diagnoses rose most between 2024 and 2025, but better detection and cancer registration may explain part of the increase.

Source: Expresso das Ilhas · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Cape Verde recorded 372 prostate cancer cases over three years, Carla Barbosa of the national programme for cancer prevention and control said at a men’s health workshop. Diagnoses rose most between 2024 and 2025, she said, but the increase does not necessarily mean the disease became more common.

The cancer registry shows the most diagnoses among men aged 50 to 79. Barbosa said 50 people died from prostate cancer in 2022, making it the cancer that contributed most to cancer deaths in the country. She said late detection contributes to mortality, while early diagnosis is associated with better treatment and survival prospects.

Barbosa cited limited access to diagnostic tests across the islands, shortages of specialists and the absence of multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging and PET scanning in the public system. She also said Cape Verde lacks radiotherapy and called for stronger imaging and pathology services, decentralised care and closer links between health centres and hospitals.

Why it mattersMen across the islands face unequal access to diagnostic tests, while the public system lacks radiotherapy and two advanced imaging services.
