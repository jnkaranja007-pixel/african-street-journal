Cape Verde minister warns of child sexual violence

The minister also highlighted concerns over the upcoming November 15 presidential elections.

Source: Expresso das Ilhas · 2026-11-04 · 1 min read

Cape Verde's minister has warned that dating relationships between children and adults are becoming normalized, potentially leading to sexual violence. She called for stronger prevention, reporting, and victim protection measures, emphasizing the need to reinforce existing laws and combat silence from families and communities.

The minister's alert comes as Cape Verde prepares for presidential elections on November 15. Citizens interviewed by Expresso das Ilhas desire a president who listens, stays close to communities, defends national interests, and can engage in dialogue with various political groups. They also seek candidates with preparation, responsibility, and maturity beyond constitutional requirements.

The publication also features an interview with Yara Miranda, president of the Superior Institute of Legal and Social Sciences (ISCJS), on its 20th anniversary. Miranda discusses the institute's journey, current challenges like a dispute over its building, and future goals, including a doctorate in law. The report also notes the ongoing debate surrounding the 2027 State Budget, with the Prime Minister and the president of the National Association of Municipalities failing to attend budget committee hearings.

Why it mattersCape Verdean children are at risk of sexual violence due to normalized dating relationships with adults, and citizens are concerned about the profile of candidates for the upcoming presidential elections.
