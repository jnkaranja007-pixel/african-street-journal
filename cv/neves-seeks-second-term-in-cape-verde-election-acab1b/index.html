Neves seeks second term in Cape Verde election

Incumbent President José Maria Neves faces six challengers in an election testing the nation's democratic stability.

Source: Africanews · 2026-11-14 · 1 min read

Cape Verdeans vote on Sunday for their president, with incumbent José Maria Neves expected to win a second term. Six candidates are competing, including former justice minister Joana Rosa Amado, who aims to be the country's first female president. The election is seen as a measure of democratic stability in the Portuguese-speaking island nation.

The election occurs as Cape Verde's national football team made a historic debut at the World Cup, reaching the knockout stages. Tourism, which contributes about a quarter of the economy, is also experiencing a boom. However, the country faces challenges such as rising living costs, youth unemployment, and social inequality.

Cape Verde's economy is significantly supported by its diaspora, whose population abroad is estimated to be nearly three times the number of residents. Voters will decide if the nation can maintain its democratic reputation while addressing economic and social pressures.
