Cape Verdeans vote for sixth president

Six candidates are vying for the presidency in Sunday's election, presenting diverse visions for the nation's future.

Cape Verdeans went to the polls on Sunday, November 15, to elect their sixth President since independence in 1975. Six candidates, including Antero Tavares, Casimiro de Pina, José Maria Neves, Gilson Alves, Joana Rosa, and Loisito Tavares, are competing for the position at the Palácio do Platô.

Antero Tavares, a 58-year-old judge from Praia, is running on a platform of social inclusion and sustainable development, aiming for a presidency closer to citizens and focused on reducing regional disparities. Casimiro de Pina, a 52-year-old lawyer from Fogo, emphasizes legality and transparency in his campaign.

The candidates present different approaches to the role of head of state, with their campaigns ranging from institutional continuity to political renewal. The National Elections Commission (CNE) has officially admitted all six candidacies.

Why it mattersVoters are choosing their sixth president, a decision that will shape the nation's institutional direction and political renewal.

Cape Verde state faces original sin of dependency

A political analysis argues the nation's state was built to manage scarcity, not create wealth.

A political analysis from A Nacao argues that Cape Verde's state, built to administer scarcity and protect a vulnerable society, is showing signs of exhaustion. The state was designed to distribute resources and foster dependency, not to organize wealth production or promote economic autonomy.

The roots of this issue predate independence in 1975, stemming from historical integration into subordinate Atlantic circuits, including slave trading and fragile agriculture. While independence brought sovereignty and democracy brought freedoms, the nation remains heavily reliant on imports, tourism, remittances, and external flows, lacking a diversified industrial base.

The analysis calls for an intelligent industrial policy focused on agro-food processing, fish valorization, naval repair, and exportable services, rather than heavy industrialization. It states that geographical location alone is not a resource and that African integration requires quality production and competitive prices, not just proximity.

Why it mattersCape Verde's state structure, designed for dependency, hinders wealth creation and national autonomy.

Dino D’Santiago wins Latin Grammy for Brazil-Africa music project

The Cape Verdean musician accepted the award for Best Brazilian Popular Music/Afro-Portuguese Music Album with Brazilian artists Criolo and Amaro Freitas.

Musician Dino D’Santiago won a Latin Grammy on Thursday, November 12, for Best Brazilian Popular Music/Afro-Portuguese Music Album. He shared the award for his project with Brazilian artists Criolo and Amaro Freitas, accepting the prize in Las Vegas, USA. The collaboration aims to connect cultures and sounds across the Atlantic.

During his acceptance speech, D’Santiago highlighted his role in bringing music from Lusophone countries like Cape Verde, Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé and Príncipe, and Portugal to a global audience. The project originated from the 2024 song “Esperança,” a collaboration with Criolo, Amaro Freitas, and producer Nave, which also earned him a Grammy nomination that year. The album features 12 tracks blending genres such as rap, Brazilian popular music, jazz, morna, and funaná.

The trio's collaboration has been showcased internationally, including performances at festivals like Primavera Sound Porto, MEO Kalorama, and Rock in Rio during the summer of 2026. Further performances are scheduled for 2027, including a slot at Lollapalooza in Brazil in March. This Grammy win further cements D’Santiago's international recognition and the ability of Cape Verdean and Lusophone music to engage diverse audiences and regions.

Why it mattersDino D’Santiago, a Cape Verdean musician, won a Latin Grammy for an album that blends music from Lusophone countries and Brazil, showcasing international cultural exchange.
