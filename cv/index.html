Government Prepares Drought Mitigation Program

A new integrated plan will address animal watering and food deficits while supporting vulnerable families.

The Cape Verdean government is developing a comprehensive program to mitigate the effects of climate change, specifically targeting drought scenarios. Minister Eveline Ramos stated that the initiative will not only provide for animal watering and food needs but also offer income support to vulnerable households. This proactive approach is necessary given the nation's high vulnerability to climate fluctuations, which can lead to extreme weather events like floods or prolonged dry spells. The program aims to create resilience against these varied climatic conditions.

Beyond climate change impacts, the agricultural sector faces a structural challenge with the emigration of young people, leading to a shortage of labor. This exodus is expected to reduce the total cultivated area. The ministry is working on strategies to re-engage youth in the primary sector, recognizing the critical role they play in ensuring future agricultural productivity and food security.

The minister also reflected on the National Program for Agricultural Investment and Food Security (PNIASAN) from 2015-2025, deeming its first phase successful in poverty reduction, strengthening food security, and fostering agricultural development. Separately, the Ministry of Agriculture announced a public tender for the sale of donated rice from the Japanese government.

Why it mattersFarmers will be directly affected by the new drought mitigation program and the efforts to address labor shortages in the agricultural sector.

Brazilian Dancers Bring Contemporary Performances to Praia

Two Brazilian artists will present contemporary dance solos at the Centro Cultural Português as part of the KORDA artistic exchange program.

Praia will host two contemporary dance performances on Friday, September fourth, at the Centro Cultural Português. The event, part of the KORDA artistic exchange initiative, aims to foster artist circulation, creative process sharing, and dialogue between different cultural contexts. The free performances, starting at 18:30, will feature Brazilian choreographer and dancer Karla Mendes with her solo "Território de Travessia" and dancer and performer Ricardo Aparecido with "Re-Traços."

The participation of these two Brazilian artists is a result of the Funarte Conexões Internacionais Program, establishing an artistic exchange partnership between Brazil and Cape Verde. Mendes's work explores the experience of being a woman through lunar archetypes, focusing on body as a territory for creation and belonging. Aparecido's performance integrates movement, voice, sound, and light in a constantly transforming environment, building its narrative through chance and interaction.

Karla Mendes, with over two decades of experience, researches Afro-diasporic dances and the connection between body, ancestry, and spirituality. Ricardo Aparecido is a contemporary dancer, performer, and creator who blends his practice with Contact Improvisation, Capoeira Angola, and performance art. Both artists bring unique perspectives and extensive backgrounds to this cultural exchange.

Why it mattersThe event brings contemporary Brazilian dance to Cape Verde, fostering cultural exchange and showcasing artistic talent from both nations.

Small Traders Use Raffles for Extra Income

Raffle games are increasingly common in the municipal market, providing a vital income source for some vendors.

Small traders at the Praia Municipal Market are turning to raffle games to supplement their earnings. This practice has become a significant part of daily commerce, operating on a foundation of trust between sellers and customers.

The raffle system has expanded beyond physical stalls, reaching customers through social media platforms and connecting with buyers on other islands and within the diaspora. For some vendors, this activity represents their sole means of livelihood.

While many rely on raffles for additional income, the practice highlights a creative adaptation by small businesses facing economic pressures. The games are a testament to the ingenuity found within local markets.

Why it mattersSmall traders and vendors are affected by using raffle games as a primary or supplementary source of income.

Cape Verde Government to Remove Children from Prison System

The justice minister announced plans to transform the Orlando Pantera center into a socio-educational facility focused on youth rehabilitation.

The Cape Verdean government intends to separate the Orlando Pantera Center from the prison system and establish a dedicated socio-educational directorate. This move aims to focus on the education and reintegration of children and young people, according to the Minister of Justice. The minister stated a clear desire to avoid incarceration for individuals aged 12 to 16, advocating for the center to become a place of "education and re-education" rather than a holding facility.

The first step involves removing the Orlando Pantera Center from the current prison administration, followed by the creation of a distinct direction for the socio-educational system. The minister also expressed his intention to involve specialized professionals to foster a significantly different environment for the young residents. The Orlando Pantera Socio-Educational Center, established in December 2005, is a public institution tasked with implementing socio-educational protective measures for minors in conflict with the law.

The minister articulated that the Orlando Pantera Socio-Educational Center has no place within the prison system's structure, as it does not align with its objectives. He emphasized the need for a center that actively educates and rehabilitates children and youth, rather than simply detaining them and preparing them for further involvement with the penal system. The goal is to create a supportive and corrective environment, distinct from punitive measures.

Why it mattersChildren aged 12 to 16 who are in conflict with the law will be removed from the prison system and placed in a dedicated socio-educational facility.

Interclube da Praia Wins National Volleyball Titles

The Praia club secured both the men's and women's senior national volleyball championships on Sunday, August 30.

Interclube da Praia claimed the national senior volleyball championships for both men and women on Sunday, August 30, at the Oeiras Sports Complex in Mindelo. The women's team successfully defended their title by defeating ABC 3-0, with set scores of 25-22, 25-17, and 25-23. This victory marks their second consecutive national championship.

In the men's final, Interclube also achieved a 3-0 victory against Clube Desportivo Fonte Francês, one of the host teams. The set scores for the men's match were 25-19, 25-16, and 25-20. The team's coach, Nelo Ramos, told the press that securing both titles had a special significance due to the challenges faced during the competition.

Ramos stated that his team did not receive the same treatment as others and highlighted the women's team's resilience after losing their opening match against ABC. He also emphasized the respect Interclube holds for São Vicente teams, noting that matches against them are typically difficult. The coach added that winning against a home team like Desportivo Fonte Francês was particularly noteworthy.

Why it mattersThe Interclube da Praia volleyball club and its coach Nelo Ramos are affected by winning national titles, facing challenges and receiving unequal treatment during the competition.
