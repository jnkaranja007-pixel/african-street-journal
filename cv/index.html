Spanish Ship Meteoro Returns to Mindelo to Bolster Maritime Cooperation

The Spanish Armed Forces vessel will engage with local press and reinforce security cooperation in the Gulf of Guinea.

The Spanish naval vessel, the Meteoro, has returned to Mindelo, São Vicente, to strengthen maritime cooperation with Cape Verde. This visit is part of Spain's ongoing efforts in maritime security and cooperation within the strategically important Gulf of Guinea. The ship, commanded by Alejandro Fraga Pardo de Guevara, previously visited Mindelo in September 2024.

The Meteoro is a modern vessel designed for asymmetric and conventional threats, currently undertaking presence, surveillance, and neutralization tasks. Its presence in Cape Verde signifies a reinforced partnership between Cape Verde, Spain, and the European Union, focusing on regional water security and surveillance. The ship is part of Spain's Maritime Action Ship project, BAM, aimed at modernizing its fleet with advanced technology.

During its stop, the Meteoro will host a press encounter on board, providing an opportunity to discuss the collaborative initiatives. This engagement underscores the commitment to enhancing maritime safety and operational capabilities in the region, benefiting from the vessel's advanced automation and technological features.

Why it mattersInvestors may benefit from strengthened maritime security and cooperation, which can foster a more stable environment for trade and economic activity in the region.

Air and Sea Traffic Surges While Bus Activity Declines

Cape Verde's airports and ports saw significant increases in movements and passengers during the second quarter of 2026, according to national statistics.

Airports in Cape Verde recorded 9,572 aircraft movements in the second quarter of 2026, a 14.5% increase from the same period in 2025. Passenger traffic through these airports rose by 4.6%, adding 35,516 travelers for a total of 799,709. Cargo volume surged by 59.9%, an increase of 168,222 kilograms, while mail volume grew by 15.4%, an additional 16,892 kilograms.

Sea ports also experienced robust growth, with a 5.1% increase in ship traffic. Passenger movements in ports climbed by 17.2%, reaching 384,715 passengers, an increase of 56,533. Merchandise handled at ports grew by 10.9% to 879,586 tons, with 67.4% of this associated with disembarkation. Container movements increased by 16.7%, adding 4,366 units.

In contrast, land transport saw a decline. Bus routes remained unchanged in length at 756 kilometers, but the total kilometers traveled by buses decreased by 28.4%, and passenger numbers dropped by 10.6% to 5,430,922. The number of available seats also fell by 24.9%, despite an increase in passengers transported per kilometer.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the significant growth in air and sea cargo and passenger volumes, indicating increased trade and tourism, while a decline in bus services may impact local logistics and commuter options.

Cape Verde Minister Downplays COVID-19 Severity, Cites Vaccination Success

Minister Lúcio Fernandes stated that COVID-19 is now comparable to the common flu, with no severe cases or deaths reported this year.

Minister Lúcio Fernandes has dismissed alarmist concerns regarding COVID-19, asserting that the virus now behaves like a "normal flu." He highlighted that 177 cumulative cases have been recorded since the start of the year, a figure lower than the previous year's comparable period. Fernandes emphasized that detected cases are not presenting severe complications and no deaths have been registered.

The minister advocated for viewing COVID-19 as another respiratory virus with seasonal peaks, similar to influenza. He attributed the successful control of the disease's lethality to the effective vaccination campaign, which has provided protection against severe forms of the virus for the majority of the population. Consequently, Fernandes assured that no restrictive measures are anticipated.

Despite the relaxed outlook, Fernandes issued clear recommendations for vulnerable groups, including the elderly, chronically ill, and immunocompromised. He advised these individuals to wear masks in crowded places and practice frequent hand washing to mitigate potential risks. The focus remains on continued protection for these at-risk populations.

Why it mattersThe minister's statement affects the general population by reframing COVID-19 as a manageable illness, while urging continued caution for vulnerable groups.
