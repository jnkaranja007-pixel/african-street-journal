Cabo Verde ranks third in African governance index

The nation scored 70.3 points in the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, maintaining its position.

Cabo Verde maintained its third-place ranking in the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance (IIAG), scoring 70.3 out of 100 points. The index, published by the Mo Ibrahim Foundation on Saturday, October 31, assesses political and social trajectories across 54 African nations.

The IIAG 2026 report noted that overall governance in Africa saw a slight improvement between 2016 and 2025, driven primarily by economic opportunities and human development gains. However, progress was uneven, with security and the rule of law deteriorating across the continent.

While infrastructure, including mobile connectivity and internet access, showed significant growth, health and education also recorded improvements. Conversely, participation, rights, and inclusion saw setbacks in many countries, impacting a majority of the African population.

Why it mattersCabo Verde's governance performance is directly assessed in the Ibrahim Index, affecting its standing among African nations.

Cape Verde informal workers shun social protection

Only 5.1% of estimated self-employed workers in Cape Verde were covered by social security in 2023.

In Cape Verde's informal economy, many self-employed workers like Zuleica Pereira in Praia's Sucupira market contribute to the National Institute of Social Security (INPS) to secure their future. However, others, like Maria de Fátima, a fish seller, do not register, viewing the monthly payments as an unaffordable expense given daily needs.

A 2024 INPS study identified insufficient income, inadequate payment methods, and uncertainty in informal markets as key barriers to registration. Marcelino Monteiro, INPS Planning and Studies Director, stated that 59.2% of respondents cited insufficient monthly income, while 52.2% did not prioritize INPS contributions due to available income.

The study also revealed that 54.3% of respondents lacked sufficient information about INPS benefits, and 45.7% cited job market uncertainty as a barrier. These factors, alongside financial capacity, influence the decision to contribute to social protection.

Why it mattersSelf-employed workers in Cape Verde face challenges in contributing to social security due to insufficient income and a lack of information.

Cape Verde president rallies supporters for re-election bid

Jose Maria Neves seeks a second term in the November 15 presidential election, promising salary increases and reforms.

Hundreds of supporters cheered President Jose Maria Neves at a rally in Sao Vicente as campaigning began for the November 15 presidential election. Neves, backed by the African Party for the Independence of Cape Verde (PAICV), is seeking a second term in office. Six candidates are competing for the presidency ahead of the official end of campaigning on November 13, according to the National Elections Commission.

At the rally, Neves stated, "It is essential that we begin working to increase salary levels in Cape Verde. No country grows or competes on the basis of low wages." He added that implementing reforms is necessary to improve wages and enhance household income. His main rival is expected to be Joana Rosa, supported by the Movement for Democracy (MpD).

Cape Verde, an archipelago of 520,000 inhabitants, has been viewed as a model of democracy in Africa since its independence from Portugal in 1975. The election will determine the country's leadership for the next term.

Why it mattersCape Verdean citizens will vote in a presidential election on November 15, with the incumbent president promising reforms to increase salaries.

Cape Verde fuel prices drop in November

Gasoline and diesel costs decrease, but industrial fuels and kerosene rise.

Fuel prices in Cape Verde will decrease in November. Gasoline will drop from 197.20 to 189.30 escudos per liter, a reduction of 7.90 escudos. Normal diesel will also become cheaper, falling from 190.30 to 185.80 escudos per liter.

The price of diesel for electricity production will decrease from 175.80 to 171.20 escudos per liter, a cut of 4.60 escudos. Marine diesel will fall from 153.30 to 149.40 escudos per liter. However, fuel for energy production and certain industrial activities will increase.

Fuel oil 380 will rise by 5.40 escudos per kilogram, from 94.80 to 100.20 escudos. Fuel oil 180 will increase by 4.30 escudos per kilogram, from 104.90 to 109.20 escudos. Kerosene will become more expensive, with its price per liter rising from 216.90 to 225.70 escudos.
