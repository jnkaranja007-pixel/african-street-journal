Cabo Verde ranks third in governance index

The archipelago scores below its 2016 levels despite strong performance in rights and participation.

Cabo Verde ranks third in the Ibrahim Index of African Governance, behind Seychelles and Mauritius, but ahead of Botswana and South Africa. The 2026 IIAG report, covering 2016-2025, notes that the archipelago's overall score has declined from a decade ago, with deterioration accelerating in the last five years.

The country leads in the Rights category and performs well in Rule of Law and Justice, and Participation, contrasting with a continental trend where nearly 80% of Africans live in countries with declining rights. Cabo Verde also scores well on Women's Equality, yet it is among states where Security and Rule of Law, and Economic Opportunity foundations have worsened over the past decade.

The biennial index, published by the Ibrahim Foundation, uses 96 governance indicators. The official presentation of the 2026 IIAG findings is scheduled for today in Cairo.

Why it mattersCabo Verde's governance performance has declined over the past decade, impacting its citizens.

Banco BAI Cabo Verde launches mobile bank branch

The first mobile banking agency in Cape Verde will bring services to different locations.

Banco BAI Cabo Verde (BAICV) launched the country's first mobile banking agency on Friday, October 30. The inaugural ceremony took place at the parking lot of the Bank of Cape Verde (BCV) in Praia.

BAICV stated the mobile agency demonstrates its commitment to financial inclusion and customer proximity, aiming to extend services to various localities for a more accessible banking experience.

The Governor of the Bank of Cape Verde, Óscar Santos, presided over the opening ceremony.

Why it mattersThe new mobile agency will extend banking services to different localities, enhancing financial inclusion and customer proximity.

Cape Verde's education system needs reform

The current system, rooted in the slave era, is insufficient for economic autonomy and national transformation.

Cape Verde's education system, which originated during the slave society, is now considered inadequate for the nation's future needs.

Minister of Education Arnaldo Brito and Dr. Miguel Cruz Sousa have inspired reflections suggesting the historical educational philosophy, while once emancipatory, no longer serves the country's pursuit of economic autonomy.

The current focus on certificates and administrative employment risks creating frustration as the economy does not transform as rapidly as expectations.

Why it mattersCape Verdeans are educated for a system that does not transform quickly enough, limiting their opportunities.

Praia mayor seeks special status for capital city

Fernando Pinto argues the capital needs differentiated treatment due to its national responsibilities.

Fernando Pinto, mayor of Praia, is calling for the capital city to receive "differentiated treatment" to reflect its status as the nation's capital. He stated that Praia should be seen and treated as the capital, not just as a municipality, and actively participate in national solutions.

Pinto believes investment should be spread across all municipalities to prevent over-concentration of people and services in Praia. He highlighted that Praia receives a large influx from rural exodus, African coastal migration, cruise ships, and hosts the presidency, government, major companies, universities, and embassies, necessitating balanced national investment.

The mayor also emphasized the urgent need to invest in inter-island connectivity, allowing citizens to travel and work safely between islands like Praia and Maio. This initiative coincides with World Cities Day and aims to foster dialogue on urban development challenges in Cape Verde.

Why it mattersThe mayor's proposals for balanced national investment and improved inter-island connectivity directly affect all Cape Verdean citizens.
