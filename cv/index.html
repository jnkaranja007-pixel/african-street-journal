Cape Verde Government Removes Children from Prison System

The justice minister announced plans to transform the Orlando Pantera center into a socio-educational facility focused on rehabilitation.

The Cape Verdean government intends to remove the Orlando Pantera Center from the prison system and establish a dedicated socio-educational directorate. Justice Minister Clóvis Silva stated his opposition to incarcerating children aged 12 to 16, advocating for the center to become a place for education and re-education rather than a holding facility. The initial step involves amending the organic law of the Ministry of Justice to separate the center from the Directorate-General of Prison Services.

Silva explained that the current setup, where children are kept behind iron doors, prepares them for further involvement in the prison system. He envisions a facility that actively educates and rehabilitates, creating a completely different environment with specialized professionals. The reform also includes plans for a new center designed as a welcoming, family-like space offering tailored training for young people facing academic failure or social exclusion.

The minister has already presented the project to international partners, including the European Union, United Nations agencies, and diplomatic missions, to secure support. The strategy also incorporates collaboration with individuals undergoing addiction recovery for awareness and community support actions, aiming for early preventive intervention before criminal offenses occur. The Orlando Pantera Center, established in December 2005, currently serves as a public institution for implementing socio-educational measures for minors in conflict with the law.

Why it mattersChildren aged 12 to 16 in conflict with the law will be moved from a prison environment to an educational and rehabilitative center.

Police detain woman, seize drugs and cash in Praia

Judicial Police arrested a 45-year-old suspect and confiscated cocaine, cannabis, and 6,530 escudos during a home search.

Praia, Cape Verde – Judicial Police (PJ) arrested a 45-year-old woman on suspicion of drug trafficking following a court-ordered search of her home. The operation, authorized by a public prosecutor and a criminal investigation judge, also resulted in the seizure of evidence relevant to the ongoing investigation.

During the search, authorities discovered 18 individual doses of cocaine and 15.728 grams of cannabis, prepared for sale on the illicit market. Additionally, police confiscated cutting agents used in drug preparation, drug paraphernalia, a mobile phone, and 6,530 escudos in cash, among other items deemed significant for the case.

The suspect has been placed in pre-trial detention as the investigation continues. The PJ stated the seized items were crucial for understanding the scope of the alleged drug distribution network operating within the Praia Judicial District.

Why it mattersA 45-year-old woman is in pre-trial detention for drug trafficking, affecting local communities through illicit trade.

Interclube da Praia Wins National Volleyball Titles

The Praia club secured both the men's and women's senior national volleyball championships in Mindelo.

Interclube da Praia claimed the senior national volleyball championships for both men and women on Sunday, August 30, at the Polidesportivo Oeiras in Mindelo. The women's team successfully defended their title by defeating ABC 3-0, with set scores of 25-22, 25-17, and 25-23, marking their second consecutive national championship. The men's team also achieved a 3-0 victory against Clube Desportivo Fonte Francês, with set scores of 25-19, 25-16, and 25-20.

Coach Nelo Ramos of Interclube described the dual titles as having a "special flavor" due to the challenges faced throughout the competition. He stated that his team "were not treated like other teams" and highlighted the women's team's resilience in reacting after losing their opening match against ABC. Ramos also emphasized the respect his team holds for São Vicente teams, noting that matches against them are typically difficult.

The men's final victory against Desportivo Fonte Francês, one of the host teams, was particularly noted by Ramos as deserving of special recognition. He added that the win against the home team makes the achievement even more significant. Both the men's and women's finals concluded with identical 3-0 set victories for Interclube da Praia.

Why it mattersThe Interclube da Praia volleyball club secured two national championships, impacting athletes and coaches involved in the sport.

New ENAPOR Chief Aims to Make Cape Verde a Regional Logistics Hub

The new chairman of the national port authority wants to transform the islands' strategic location into a competitive advantage for trade and employment.

Hamir Inocêncio, the new chairman of ENAPOR, stated that Cape Verde's privileged geographical position is insufficient on its own to generate economic benefits. He emphasized the need to strategically leverage this location to create a logistics platform, competitive advantage, investment, business, and jobs. Inocêncio aims to position the archipelago to progressively assume logistics and distribution network functions in West Africa, exploring complementarities with sub-regional ports.

Inocêncio highlighted that international logistics operators choose ports based on efficiency, cost, speed, security, and reliability. Therefore, Cape Verde must become useful, competitive, and necessary on trade routes. Key priorities include modernizing infrastructure, implementing digital transformation, reducing logistical costs, and increasing productivity. The new ENAPOR board, including executive administrators Aleida Sofia Delgado Lima Moreno and Carlos Alberto Alves Delgado, was appointed by the Minister of Maritime and Transport, João do Carmo Soares.

Minister Soares called for visionary and technically competent leadership to address the evolving challenges in ports and maritime transport. He stressed that port excellence in the current era increasingly depends on professional efficiency, modernized infrastructure and equipment, digitized processes, and the adoption of smartport models. Soares also advocated for continued investment in modernizing the national port system to enhance logistical efficiency, supply chain management, productivity, and innovation.

Why it mattersThe new leadership of ENAPOR seeks to transform Cape Verde into a regional logistics hub, which could attract international investment and create employment opportunities.

Hydrogeologist and Footballer Alberto Mota Gomes Dies at 91

Alberto Mota Gomes, a respected hydrogeologist and former national football selector, passed away in Praia at the age of 91.

Cabo Verde mourns the loss of Alberto Mota Gomes, who died at 91, leaving behind a legacy that spanned science, sport, and culture. Known affectionately as Engenheiro Betinho, he was a distinguished hydrogeologist, a former international footballer, and the first national football team selector. His passing on Sunday, August 23, in Praia, marks the end of an era for many generations who benefited from his expertise and character.

Mota Gomes was a recognized authority in hydrogeology, particularly in the exploration and extraction of underground water resources, where his knowledge was so profound that colleagues often remarked, "Mota Gomes never failed." Beyond his scientific contributions, he was instrumental in founding the Geociências magazine at the Instituto Superior de Educação, fostering scientific dissemination in Cabo Verde. His collaborative spirit and humble approach to sharing his vast knowledge were hallmarks of his professional life.

His influence extends beyond his technical achievements. Mota Gomes is remembered for his simplicity, affability, and ease of interaction, qualities that made him a respected figure. He leaves behind a lasting impact on studies of groundwater in Cabo Verde, the history of national football, and the countless individuals who had the privilege of working and interacting with him.

Why it mattersThe passing of Alberto Mota Gomes affects Cabo Verde by removing a key figure in hydrogeology and national football, impacting scientific knowledge and cultural memory.
