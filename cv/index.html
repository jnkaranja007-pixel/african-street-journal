Ilha do Sal hosts 3rd circus festival

The festival aims to bring circus art to the public through free shows in public spaces.

The island of Sal in Cape Verde is hosting the 3rd edition of the International Circus Festival of Cape Verde. The festival's objective is to present circus art to the entire population with free performances in public areas. This initiative promotes access to culture, artistic diversity, and exchange between artists from different nations.

A notable highlight of this year's festival is the participation of the Brazilian company Cia. Corpo na Contramão, traveling from Brazil to be part of the event. The organization stated that the festival's return offers a chance for cultural exchange between Brazil and Cape Verde, enhancing the event's artistic offerings and exposing Cape Verdean audiences to varied circus art forms.

The organization described the return of Sal & Circo as an important moment for culture on Ilha do Sal. They emphasized the role of street art in fostering inclusion, social interaction, and community connection.

Why it mattersResidents of Ilha do Sal will have access to free circus performances in public spaces, promoting cultural engagement.

Rain bands approach Cape Verde southern islands

The National Institute of Meteorology and Geophysics forecasts variable rain for Maio, Santiago, Fogo, and Brava.

Rain cloud bands are approaching Cape Verde, with the National Institute of Meteorology and Geophysics (INMG) predicting cloudy to overcast skies and possible rain for Maio, Santiago, Fogo, and Brava islands between today and Tuesday.

The INMG also forecasts light to moderate rain in Boa Vista on Tuesday afternoon, and drizzle in Santo Antão. Winds will be light to moderate from the north-northeast, and sea waves are expected to reach 2.5 meters, potentially affecting inter-island travel.

NGA AgroClimate Tech Ltd advised residents and agricultural communities to monitor weather changes as the rain bands approach the southern islands, potentially increasing cloudiness and causing localized showers.

Why it mattersResidents and agricultural communities on the southern islands face increased cloudiness and localized showers.

UN committee urges Cape Verde to protect minors from de facto unions

The UN CEDAW committee recommended changes to the Civil Code to prevent retroactive recognition of unions involving minors.

A United Nations committee has recommended that Cape Verde amend its Civil Code to stop the retroactive recognition of de facto unions where one partner was a minor. The Committee on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW) made this recommendation after Cape Verde presented its tenth periodic report on October 16 in Geneva.

The CEDAW committee expressed concern that the Civil Code's recognition of de facto unions after three years of cohabitation could legitimize unions that began when a party was underage. The committee also noted that many de facto unions lack legal recognition, leaving women vulnerable in cases of separation or widowhood, and called for formal recognition procedures across all islands, ensuring women's rights to property, alimony, and housing.

The committee also voiced concern over a sharp decline in women's representation in the national government, falling from 53.3% in 2010 to 16.7% in the 2026 executive. CEDAW recommended amending the Parity Law to ensure equitable representation in executive posts and urged the archipelago to reform its Gender-Based Violence Law to explicitly criminalize political violence and online harassment against women.

Why it mattersThe UN CEDAW committee's recommendations aim to protect minors and women from legal and economic vulnerability within de facto unions and to address the declining representation of women in Cape Verde's government.

Politics · 2026-11-03

Cape Verde president rallies supporters for election

Jose Maria Neves seeks a second term, promising salary increases and reforms in the November 15 vote.

Cape Verde President Jose Maria Neves began campaigning for a second term on Saturday with a rally in Sao Vicente, where hundreds of supporters cheered him on. Neves, backed by the PAICV party, is one of six candidates competing in the November 15 presidential election.

At the rally, Neves stated that increasing salary levels is essential for national growth and competitiveness. He emphasized the need for reforms to improve wages and household income, according to his remarks.

The official end of campaigning is November 13, according to the National Elections Commission. Joana Rosa, a former Justice Minister backed by the MpD, is expected to be Neves' main opponent.

Why it mattersPresidential candidate Jose Maria Neves is campaigning on a platform of increasing salaries and implementing reforms that could affect the income of Cape Verde's inhabitants.

Cabo Verde targets British tourists at World Travel Market

The archipelago aims to boost its presence in the UK market, a key source of visitors.

Cabo Verde is participating in the World Travel Market 2026 in London to promote the island nation as a tourist destination to international operators. The Institute of Tourism of Cabo Verde is leading the effort to strengthen the country's presence in the British market, which is currently its largest source of tourists.

The World Travel Market is a major global platform for the tourism sector, bringing together destinations, tour operators, airlines, hotels, and technology providers from over 180 countries. Cabo Verde's participation seeks to foster business relationships, create new opportunities, and increase international visibility.

This engagement is part of Cabo Verde's broader tourism promotion strategy, with a specific focus on attracting more visitors from the United Kingdom. The event provides a space for negotiation and contact between buyers and sellers in the tourism industry.

Why it mattersCabo Verde's tourism sector is seeking to attract more visitors from the United Kingdom, a key source of international tourists.
