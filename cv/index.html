Cape Verde identifies night heatwaves as health threat

A new study reveals nighttime heatwaves pose the greatest health risk in Cape Verde, impacting respiratory and chronic illnesses.

Nighttime heatwaves are the biggest health threat in Cape Verde, according to a new study by the National Institute of Public Health (INSP). Contrary to public perception, the research found that elevated temperatures after dark are more prevalent and harmful than daytime sun exposure.

Epidemiologist Giseila Garcia stated that excessive heat affects the population's health broadly, leading to increased respiratory diseases and worsening existing dermatological and chronic conditions. This study, prepared in 2024 and implemented from January 2025, is the first to establish specific parameters for identifying heatwaves within the country.

Authorities are developing an early warning system using thermal sensors on Brava island and will publish official heatwave data in the Boletim Oficial. The study, a partnership between INSP and the National Institute of Meteorology and Geophysics (INMG), analyzed six municipalities and was funded by Luxembourgish Cooperation.

Why it mattersCape Verdeans face increased risks of respiratory and chronic illnesses due to nighttime heatwaves.

Cape Verde minister warns of child sexual violence

The minister also highlighted concerns over the upcoming November 15 presidential elections.

Cape Verde's minister has warned that dating relationships between children and adults are becoming normalized, potentially leading to sexual violence. She called for stronger prevention, reporting, and victim protection measures, emphasizing the need to reinforce existing laws and combat silence from families and communities.

The minister's alert comes as Cape Verde prepares for presidential elections on November 15. Citizens interviewed by Expresso das Ilhas desire a president who listens, stays close to communities, defends national interests, and can engage in dialogue with various political groups. They also seek candidates with preparation, responsibility, and maturity beyond constitutional requirements.

The publication also features an interview with Yara Miranda, president of the Superior Institute of Legal and Social Sciences (ISCJS), on its 20th anniversary. Miranda discusses the institute's journey, current challenges like a dispute over its building, and future goals, including a doctorate in law. The report also notes the ongoing debate surrounding the 2027 State Budget, with the Prime Minister and the president of the National Association of Municipalities failing to attend budget committee hearings.

Why it mattersCape Verdean children are at risk of sexual violence due to normalized dating relationships with adults, and citizens are concerned about the profile of candidates for the upcoming presidential elections.

Africa CDC urges Saudi Arabia to lift Kenya travel bans

The African Union's health agency says Kenya's Ebola response is transparent and effective.

The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) on March 11 urged Saudi Arabia to lift travel restrictions imposed on Kenya due to Ebola. The AU agency stated that Kenya has demonstrated strong leadership and transparency in its response to the epidemic.

Africa CDC acknowledged Saudi Arabia's sovereign right to protect its citizens but stressed that public health measures should be guided by scientific evidence, not fear. Kenya has reported nine confirmed Ebola cases, including two deaths, all linked to a single imported case, with no new cases since October 19.

The agency called on other countries to avoid similar restrictions without public health justification and warned against disproportionately penalizing African nations that report outbreaks transparently and respond effectively.

Why it mattersAfrican nations face potential travel restrictions and penalties despite transparent outbreak reporting and effective responses.

Cape Verde president begins election campaign

Jose Maria Neves rallied hundreds of supporters in Sao Vicente, promising salary increases and reforms.

Cape Verde President Jose Maria Neves launched his campaign for a second term on Saturday in Sao Vicente, cheered by hundreds of supporters. The election is scheduled for November 15.

Neves, backed by the African Party for the Independence of Cape Verde (PAICV), told the rally that increasing salary levels is essential for national growth and competitiveness. He stated that reforms are necessary to improve wages and household income.

Six candidates are competing in the presidential race, with campaigning officially ending on November 13. Joana Rosa, former Justice Minister and candidate for the Movement for Democracy (MpD), is considered Neves' main rival.

Why it mattersPresidential candidate Jose Maria Neves is campaigning on a platform of economic reform and increased salaries for Cape Verdeans.
