Neves seeks second term in Cape Verde election

Incumbent President José Maria Neves faces six challengers in an election testing the nation's democratic stability.

Cape Verdeans vote on Sunday for their president, with incumbent José Maria Neves expected to win a second term. Six candidates are competing, including former justice minister Joana Rosa Amado, who aims to be the country's first female president. The election is seen as a measure of democratic stability in the Portuguese-speaking island nation.

The election occurs as Cape Verde's national football team made a historic debut at the World Cup, reaching the knockout stages. Tourism, which contributes about a quarter of the economy, is also experiencing a boom. However, the country faces challenges such as rising living costs, youth unemployment, and social inequality.

Cape Verde's economy is significantly supported by its diaspora, whose population abroad is estimated to be nearly three times the number of residents. Voters will decide if the nation can maintain its democratic reputation while addressing economic and social pressures.

Why it mattersVoters will choose their next president on Sunday, deciding if Cape Verde can maintain its democratic reputation while addressing economic and social pressures.

Cape Verde state reform needed to boost wealth creation

A political analysis argues the nation's state structure is designed for scarcity, not wealth production.

Cape Verde's state structure is showing signs of exhaustion, designed to manage scarcity and protect a vulnerable society rather than organize wealth production and economic autonomy. This original sin of state-building transformed a historical need for protection into a lasting culture of dependency, according to a political analysis.

The roots of this problem predate independence in 1975, stemming from the archipelago's integration into Atlantic circuits serving external interests, marked by slave trading, intermediation economies, and agricultural fragility. Emigration became a common response to a lack of economic horizons, with citizens' resistance lacking conditions for productive conversion.

While independence brought sovereignty and democracy consolidated freedoms, a productive transformation has not matched political gains. The nation remains heavily reliant on imports, tourism, remittances, and external flows, lacking a diversified industrial base. The analysis calls for intelligent industrial policy focused on agro-food processing, fisheries, naval repair, water and energy industries, and exportable technologies and services.

Why it mattersA political analysis argues the nation's state structure needs reform to shift from dependency to wealth creation, impacting all citizens' economic future.

Cape Verdean Dino d’Santiago wins Latin Grammy with Brazilian artists

The award went to the album "Criolo, Amaro & Dino", recorded with Brazilian rapper Criolo and pianist Amaro Freitas from Recife.

Cape Verdean musician Dino d’Santiago won a Latin Grammy on Friday, 12 November, in Las Vegas, United States. The prize was in the category of Best Brazilian Popular Music/Afro-Portuguese Brazilian Music Album for "Criolo, Amaro & Dino", which he recorded with Brazilian artists Criolo and Amaro Freitas.

Accepting the award, d’Santiago named Cape Verde, Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé and Príncipe and Portugal, and thanked the Portuguese language for making them one people. Freitas, born in Recife, said the work joins Black music and the African diaspora across the world, while Criolo dedicated the win to Indigenous peoples in Brazil.

The album has 12 tracks mixing rap, Brazilian popular music, jazz, morna and funaná. The three artists split studio sessions between Rio de Janeiro, Recife, São Paulo and Lisbon. The project began with the song "Esperança", released in 2024, which earned d’Santiago his first Latin Grammy nomination that year.

Why it mattersCape Verdean and Portuguese-speaking musicians now have an award-winning collaboration to point to abroad, and the trio has more shows scheduled in 2027, including Lollapalooza in Brazil in March.
