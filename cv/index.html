Cape Verde tourist prices rise 7.5% year-on-year

Hotels, cafes, and restaurants saw the largest price increases in the third quarter, according to the National Statistics Institute.

Tourist prices in Cape Verde increased by 7.5% in the third quarter compared to the same period last year, the National Statistics Institute (INE) announced. The Tourist Price Index (IPT) showed that prices for hotels, cafes, and restaurants rose by 7.5%, while car rentals became 6.4% more expensive. Entertainment and culture services saw no price variation.

The INE's findings also revealed a 5.2% quarterly price increase between July and September, a significant jump from the 0.6% rise in the second quarter. This rise is also higher than the 1.3% observed in the third quarter of 2025. Accommodation services experienced notable increases, with guesthouses up 11.4% and hotels up 10.1% year-on-year.

Boa Vista island recorded the highest year-on-year price variation at 8.6%, followed by São Vicente at 7.5% and Santo Antão at 7%. Sal saw a 6.3% increase, and Santiago had a 3% rise. Restaurants saw prices increase by 1.9% and cafes, bars, and similar establishments by 0.5% compared to the third quarter of 2025.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the rising costs of tourist services, which could impact future investment decisions in the sector.

Cape Verde government invests in Praia road upgrades

The government will rehabilitate and asphalt roads in the capital, Praia, with World Bank support.

The Cape Verdean government has announced a significant investment in the rehabilitation and asphalting of key roads in Praia, the capital city. The announcement was made by the Minister of Infrastructure, Housing and Territorial Planning, Carlos Tavares, during a visit to the municipality. The project includes repairs between Quebra Canela and Vila Nova, as well as along Avenida dos Combatentes da Liberdade da Pátria, near Electra. Drainage work is also planned to address flooding in low-lying areas.

Minister Tavares stated that the projects are being finalized in coordination with the Praia Municipal Chamber, with works expected to commence soon. He emphasized a focus on structural improvements rather than rushed interventions to ensure lasting enhancements to traffic flow. While awaiting the larger structural works, the government will address immediate road safety issues, such as potholes.

The initiative is supported by the World Bank and involves collaboration with the Praia Municipal Chamber to ensure quality and relevance to the population's needs. Additional projects to ease traffic congestion in Vila Nova are also funded by the World Bank. Minister Tavares highlighted the importance of government and municipal coordination for urban mobility challenges in the capital.

Why it mattersResidents of Praia will benefit from improved road conditions and reduced flooding.

Barbosa elected leader of opposition parliamentary group

Emanuel Barbosa will lead the Movement for Democracy's parliamentary group, aiming to foster internal cohesion and present a responsible opposition.

Emanuel Barbosa was elected on Monday, October 20, in Praia, as the new leader of the parliamentary group for the Movement for Democracy (MpD), the main opposition party. Celso Ribeiro, Sandra Galina, Liver Gomes, and Ivone Cardoso were appointed as vice-presidents. The new leadership aims to strengthen internal unity, leverage the experience of its deputies, and establish a responsible, dialoguing, and proactive opposition.

Barbosa stated that the group will offer qualified opposition, criticizing when necessary but also presenting concrete proposals. This leadership transition follows the national convention that elected Paulo Veiga as the MpD's national president. The elected leader emphasized the importance of dialogue, proximity, and listening to citizens, ensuring parliamentary actions reflect the concerns of Cape Verdeans.

The new leadership reaffirmed its commitment to constructive opposition, maintaining oversight of government actions and openness to dialogue in matters of national interest. The previous leader, Luís Carlos Silva, resigned following changes to party statutes that mandated a renewal of the parliamentary group's leadership within 15 days of the national convention.

Why it mattersCape Verdean citizens are affected as the new leadership of the main opposition party's parliamentary group aims to represent their concerns through constructive criticism and policy proposals.

Red Cross strengthens drought prevention in vulnerable communities

The Red Cross in Cape Verde is implementing a protocol to protect livelihoods and prepare communities for drought before crises occur.

The president of the Cape Verde Red Cross, Arlindo Carvalho, emphasized the need for proactive measures against drought to safeguard populations and minimize human and material losses. He stated that intervening before a crisis allows for alleviating suffering and preventing potential loss of life, rather than just reacting to save lives and reduce hardship after the fact.

The protocol includes measures for livelihood protection, community preparedness, and training response teams. Initially, actions will focus on Boa Vista, Porto Novo municipality in Santo Antão, and specific municipalities on Santiago island, identified by a study as areas significantly affected by drought. The International Federation is a partner in this initiative.

Carvalho explained that while the current phase targets specific areas, the possibility of extending the program to other islands remains open. The project is supported by a study that pinpoints the most visible drought-affected regions, guiding the direct intervention efforts in this first phase.

Why it mattersFarmers and rural communities are directly affected by drought prevention measures aimed at protecting their livelihoods and reducing losses.

Cape Verde reports 1,400 viral conjunctivitis cases

Sal island health authorities confirm a viral conjunctivitis outbreak with 1,400 cases, primarily affecting children and hotel workers.

Health authorities on Sal island, Cape Verde, reported approximately 1,400 cases of viral conjunctivitis on Monday, October 20. The outbreak saw a peak of 429 cases in one epidemiological week. The Secretary of State for Health, Artur Veiga, stated the situation is under control and urged the public to remain calm, emphasizing there is no cause for alarm or extreme measures.

A national technical team will be dispatched to bolster epidemiological investigation and support the Sal Regional Health Delegation and Hospital. The clinical presentation aligns with viral conjunctivitis, predominantly impacting children, young people, and hotel industry employees. Investigations on 644 cases revealed no hospital admissions.

While the specific causative agent is awaiting confirmation from samples sent to the Pasteur Institute in Dakar, epidemiological analysis indicates the average age of those affected is 16 years. Chã de Matias is identified as the most critical area, accounting for 45 percent of analyzed cases due to its population density.

Why it mattersThe conjunctivitis outbreak affects children, young people, and hotel workers on Sal island, potentially impacting their daily activities and health.
