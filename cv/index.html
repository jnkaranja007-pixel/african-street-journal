two trains collide in Spain

A collision between a Renfe AVE and an Iryo train near Toledo killed several people.

A collision between a Renfe AVE train and an Iryo train occurred near Urda in the province of Toledo, Spain, shortly before 9:00 AM local time.

The Renfe train reportedly struck the rear of the stopped Iryo train, causing two carriages of the Iryo train to derail and fall down an embankment. Emergency teams, health professionals, and officials from both train operators were dispatched to the scene.

Spanish newspaper El País reported that authorities fear the death toll may rise. Government sources cited by El País suggested a possible human error, with an operator at the Atocha control center potentially giving a mistaken order for the Renfe train to proceed despite an obstructed track. The exact circumstances of the accident are still under investigation. High-speed rail traffic between Madrid and Andalusia has been suspended. The accident occurred at 7:00 AM Cape Verde time.

Why it mattersHigh-speed rail traffic between Madrid and Andalusia has been suspended, affecting travel for Cape Verdeans.

Cabo Verde joins Russia-Africa summit

The nation participates in the third Russia-Africa Summit with a delegation led by Ambassador Domingos Mascarenhas.

Cabo Verde participated in the third Russia-Africa Summit with a delegation led by Ambassador Domingos Mascarenhas. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Communities stated the country's participation aligns with African Union co-organization of the event. The government views the summit as a chance to enhance multilateral dialogue and coordination between nations, consistent with its foreign policy of pragmatism and active neutrality.

As a Small Island Developing State and an African Union member, Cabo Verde's diplomacy focuses on defending national sovereignty, promoting peace and collective security, and sustainable development. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Communities considers the summit's non-binding Political Declaration and Action Plan as dialogue platforms between Russia and Africa. The government is open to exploring new cooperation programs with the Russian Federation.

Cooperation between the two countries has historical roots, including support from the former Soviet Union during Cabo Verde's independence and in human resource training. The government emphasized that participation adheres to traditional foreign policy principles, such as respecting the UN Charter, territorial integrity of sovereign states, and national interests.

Why it mattersCabo Verde's participation in the Russia-Africa Summit aims to reinforce multilateral dialogue and explore new cooperation programs with the Russian Federation.

Cape Verde hosts national workshop on local finances

Officials from 22 municipalities gathered in Praia to discuss ways to boost municipal revenue and financial autonomy.

The second National Workshop on Local Finances is taking place in Praia on October 27 and 28, organized by the Ministry of State Reform, Local Power and Decentralization. The event aims to strengthen municipal revenues across Cape Verde and includes representatives from all 22 municipalities, as well as national and international institutions.

Held at the Hotel Oásis Atlântico Praia Mar as part of the Joint Program for the Promotion of Local Development in Cape Verde, the workshop's opening session featured the Minister of State Reform, Local Power and Decentralization, José Casemiro de Pina. Also present were the Ambassador of Luxembourg to Cape Verde, Jean-Marie Frentz, and the Resident Coordinator of the United Nations System in Cape Verde, Patrícia Portela de Souza.

This second edition focuses on sharing experiences and enhancing municipal capacity for mobilizing own revenues. Key discussions include property valuation methods, preliminary results of a pilot phase, a simulator for calculating ITI and IPI taxes, and progress in municipal action plans to improve local income. The workshop also presents municipal best practices and analysis of local revenues in the 22 municipalities to foster financial autonomy and sustainable local development.

Why it mattersLocal government officials and representatives from all 22 municipalities in Cape Verde are affected by discussions on strengthening municipal revenues and financial autonomy.

Cape Verde government keeps gas prices steady

Government approved stable butane gas prices for November, while fuel prices may slightly decrease.

The price of butane gas will not increase in November, and available indicators suggest slight decreases for gasoline and diesel, according to the Government. The decision to maintain gas prices at October levels was approved on Tuesday, October 27, by the Council of Ministers.

This decision comes as the MpD party accuses the Executive of not sufficiently addressing the impact of rising fuel costs on the cost of living for Cape Verdean families. The government's announcement provides some relief amidst ongoing economic concerns.

The Assembly of the Republic will also debate the State of Justice this Thursday. Priorities for human resources, reducing case backlogs, and improving court responses were presented by PAICV, MpD, and UCID. UCID also highlighted rising sexual crimes and the need to review gender-based violence policies.

Why it mattersCape Verdean families will not face higher butane gas prices in November.

MpD calls for justice reform results

The ruling party wants courts to deliver faster, more accessible justice for citizens, families, and businesses.

The Movement for Democracy (MpD) parliamentary group leader, Emanuel Barbosa, stated that Cape Verde needs to see concrete results from its justice sector reforms. He called for improved court operations, more staff, and quicker case processing. Barbosa made these remarks during parliamentary sessions preparing for October's second plenary meeting, where the MpD discussed the country's political situation and upcoming debates on transparency, governance, and the state of justice.

Barbosa acknowledged progress in recent legislatures but highlighted ongoing issues with accessing justice and resolving cases. "We know that justice delayed leaves people waiting and weakens trust in the state," he said, emphasizing that reforms must lead to tangible improvements for citizens, families, and businesses. He stressed the need for resources like infrastructure, personnel, and training to implement these reforms effectively.

The MpD leader also addressed equal treatment under the law, stating, "There can be no first-class or second-class Cape Verdeans in matters of justice." He insisted that judicial institutions must operate independently and without pressure. Barbosa questioned the timing of revisiting the state of justice, a topic previously raised by the opposition PAICV four times. He linked current public concerns to rising fuel prices in October, which have increased the cost of living for families, transporters, fishermen, and small businesses.

Why it mattersThe MpD party is pushing for reforms in the justice system to improve outcomes for citizens, families, and businesses.
