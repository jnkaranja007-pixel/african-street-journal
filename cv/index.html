Cape Verde council questions economic forecasts

The CFP wants more detail on government predictions for 5.6% GDP growth and 2% inflation in 2027.

Cape Verde's Ministry of Finance projects the nation's GDP will grow 5.6% in 2027, with average annual inflation at 2%. The Financial Policy Council (CFP) presented a slightly more moderate outlook, anticipating 5.3% growth and 2.2% inflation. The government's growth forecast exceeds predictions from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) at 5% and the Bank of Cape Verde (BCV) at 4.9%.

The CFP stated that the government needs to better explain the factors supporting its scenario, particularly the evolution of tourism, investment, and domestic demand. While deeming the official projections plausible, the Council noted that achieving the predicted growth depends on international conditions and the economy's resilience to shocks. Cape Verde's reliance on imported fuel and food makes it vulnerable to global price swings.

Fuel prices rose significantly between July and October 2026, with gasoline up 21.7%, regular diesel up 36%, and diesel for electricity generation up 40.9%. These increases could raise transport, production, and energy costs, impacting inflation and household purchasing power. In an adverse scenario, economic growth could slow to 3% and inflation rise to 4.2%, potentially reducing tax revenue and increasing state financing needs.

Why it mattersCape Verde's economy faces risks from fluctuating international fuel prices and its dependence on imports, potentially affecting inflation and household purchasing power.

Cape Verde minister attends biodiversity conference

The minister will join global leaders to accelerate the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework implementation.

Cape Verde's Minister of Environment will represent the nation at a high-level ministerial segment of a biodiversity conference on October 27 and 28. The meeting aims to strengthen political commitment to biodiversity conservation and sustainable use. Discussions will focus on accelerating the implementation of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, with attention to 2030 targets, financial resource mobilization, and international cooperation to halt biodiversity loss.

The conference program includes country interventions, ministerial consultations, and high-level panels on biodiversity commitments. Cape Verde's delegation also includes the National Director of Environment, João Mascarenhas Monteiro, and the Permanent Representative and National Focal Point for the Convention on Biological Diversity, Liza Helena Alves Lima. Their participation extends to the 12th meeting of the Parties to the Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety and the 6th meeting of the Parties to the Nagoya Protocol.

The Cartagena Protocol ensures safe handling of modified organisms, while the Nagoya Protocol governs access to genetic resources and benefit-sharing. This biennial conference seeks to develop strategies and agreements to address the global biodiversity crisis. The minister's attendance signifies Cape Verde's engagement with international efforts to protect the environment.

Why it mattersCape Verde's participation in the biodiversity conference shows its commitment to international environmental agreements and sustainable resource management.

Cape Verde interim president flags court delays

Janira Hopffer Almada says slow justice erodes public trust and calls for more staff and better technology.

Interim President Janira Hopffer Almada stated that slow judicial processes remain a major concern for Cape Verde's justice system, despite past investments.

Almada called for strengthening human resources, improving judicial management, and valuing merit to boost court efficiency and public confidence. She highlighted a report indicating a need for more magistrates and judicial officers, better inspection services, and modernized technology.

The President also supported legislative reforms, including the Administrative Process Code set for January 4, 2027, to better protect citizens' rights and ensure judicial decisions are enforced. New mediation and arbitration regimes were also noted as ways to provide faster, less costly solutions.

Why it mattersInterim President Janira Hopffer Almada highlighted that slow judicial processes and the need for better court capacity affect citizens' trust in justice.
