Praia Firefighters Seek Modern Equipment Amidst Urban Growth

The Praia Fire Department, celebrating 48 years, faces challenges from the city's rapid expansion, requiring updated facilities and specialized gear for high-rise emergencies.

The Praia Fire Department is calling for improved resources to address the growing demands of the expanding urban landscape. Commander Carlos Teixeira stated that the city's accelerated growth and increasing population necessitate new conditions and more modern facilities to ensure adequate response to residents' needs. He highlighted that the current fire station is outdated and no longer fully meets the requirements of an expanding city, emphasizing the need for a modern infrastructure capable of keeping pace with urban development.

A significant challenge identified is the rise in multi-story buildings, which requires specialized equipment for firefighting at height. Teixeira noted the initiative by the Praia Municipal Assembly president to provide a ladder truck, in partnership with the government, as a crucial step towards enhancing intervention capabilities in tall buildings. The department also handles a substantial number of daily calls, with medical emergencies being a primary focus, averaging ten to fifteen ambulance service requests each day.

Teixeira stressed that effective response relies on both available resources and personnel preparedness, asserting that better material and infrastructural conditions lead to more efficient interventions. He praised the dedication of the firefighters, describing their work as invaluable for public protection and assistance. He expressed his commitment to securing improved conditions for the force to deliver increasingly effective services to the Praia population.

Why it mattersThe Praia Fire Department's need for better equipment and facilities affects the safety and response times for all residents, including those in newly constructed high-rise buildings.

Dutch Court Sentences Rwandan Man to Life for 1994 Genocide

A 66-year-old man, Eugene N., was convicted of genocide, pillaging, and destruction of homes, receiving a life sentence from a Dutch court.

A Dutch court has sentenced Eugene N., a 66-year-old Rwandan national, to life imprisonment for his role in the 1994 genocide in Rwanda. The Hague District Court found him guilty of genocide, pillaging, and destruction of homes. The judge stated that the offenses deeply shocked society and constituted a significant violation of legal order in Rwanda and globally, justifying the life sentence.

Prosecutors accused Eugene N. of personally throwing a grenade into a stadium in southern Rwanda where approximately three thousand people seeking refuge were massacred. The defense argued that Eugene N. was also a victim of violence during the period and denied the charges, citing that family members were also killed. The United Nations reports that over eight hundred thousand people, primarily from the Tutsi minority, were killed in Rwanda during the spring of 1994.

Eugene N. had been residing in the Netherlands since 1998 after acquiring Dutch nationality and was sought by Rwandan authorities who issued an international arrest warrant in 2014. Due to his Dutch citizenship, he cannot be extradited to Rwanda. Dutch investigators have been examining the case since 2020, interviewing numerous witnesses both in the Netherlands and in Rwanda, as European courts utilize universal jurisdiction to try severe crimes committed abroad.

Why it mattersRwandan diaspora members may be affected by the conviction of a national for genocide, impacting perceptions and potential legal scrutiny.

New Inspector General Vows to Combat Price Gouging and Hygiene Lapses

Elisângelo Monteiro, newly reappointed to lead IGAE, plans to enhance national inspections and digitalize operations to protect consumers and the economy.

Elisângelo Monteiro has taken office as Inspector General for the second time, pledging to strengthen national inspections and combat price speculation. Monteiro stated that infractions are often driven by bad intentions, particularly regarding price gouging, where consumers pay more than the actual value. He emphasized the need for a strong and consistent regulatory presence to curb illicit and unfair practices that harm the economy and public health.

Initial inspections under Monteiro's leadership have already identified issues with hygiene standards and food preservation, posing potential public health risks. These concerns will be a primary focus moving forward. The agency also aims to digitize its procedures, transforming IGAE into a more efficient and data-driven economic oversight body. Monteiro expressed confidence in becoming the community's most digital economic police force.

The IGAE currently employs between 15 to 17 inspectors and plans to increase this number to approximately 30. This expansion will require investment and will be implemented progressively throughout Monteiro's term. The Minister of Economy, António Batista, supports firm oversight to protect public health and consumers, acknowledging the challenging business environment and the need to restore state authority and predictability.

Why it mattersConsumers may face higher prices and potential health risks due to poor hygiene and food conservation practices, while businesses could be impacted by increased regulatory scrutiny.

Cape Verde Inspectors Close Three Food Shops for Hygiene Lapses

Authorities in Praia temporarily shut down three food vendors in Achada Santo António and Fazenda for failing to meet basic health and safety standards, seizing illegal alcohol.

The General Inspection of Economic Activities (IGAE) in Praia has temporarily suspended three food retail establishments in the Achada Santo António and Fazenda neighborhoods. The operation, conducted jointly with the National and Municipal Police, targeted approximately twenty businesses. The closures followed findings of severe hygiene deficiencies, including expired products, unlicensed alcohol, and pest infestations.

During the inspections, officials confiscated products past their expiration dates, alcoholic beverages lacking fiscal seals, and illegal "garrafinha" spirits. Other violations identified included the absence of posted prices and operating permits, inadequate ventilation, overcrowding, and improper food handling. Staff also lacked proper training and health certifications.

The IGAE urged all merchants to adhere to legal requirements to prevent health risks to consumers and avoid legal penalties. The operation aimed to enforce regulations concerning food safety, labeling, and general business practices within the city.

Why it mattersFood vendors in Praia were temporarily closed due to hygiene issues, affecting the availability of goods for consumers.

Praia Municipality Denies Lifeguard Equipment Shortages

The Praia Municipal Chamber's lifeguard coordinator stated that rescue boards and first aid kits are available, contradicting earlier reports from lifeguards.

Admilson Melo, coordinator of lifeguards for the Praia Municipal Chamber (CMP), asserted that the municipality possesses rescue boards for its beaches, refuting claims made by lifeguards. He stated that one board is at Kebra Canela beach and another is stored with the Praia Firefighters. Melo also confirmed the availability of three first aid kits, noting that the kit for São Francisco beach is not kept on-site due to a lack of storage.

Despite contesting the reported lack of equipment, Melo acknowledged the need to enhance resources and revealed that the CMP is preparing proposals for the 2027 budget. He indicated that the municipality is also planning to establish a four-person rescue and diving team, with training to take place in Portugal. Melo recognized that the current eleven lifeguards are insufficient for adequate coverage across all municipal beaches, with five new professionals already recruited and ten more planned.

The CMP aims to extend lifeguard service hours, which Melo suggested would be accompanied by a salary adjustment, as people continue to use the beaches until late evening. Current monthly salaries are approximately twenty-four thousand escudos, including a five thousand escudos food allowance. The municipality also expects coverage from the National Institute of Social Security and intends to implement a risk allowance for lifeguards, following recent distribution of new uniforms.

Why it mattersLifeguards are affected by the municipality's denial of equipment shortages and the acknowledgment of insufficient staffing, impacting their ability to ensure beach safety.
