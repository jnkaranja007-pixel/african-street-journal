Cape Verdeans Support Women's Autonomy in Marriage and Motherhood

A recent Afrobarometer study reveals strong public backing for women's rights to decide on marriage, childbirth, and education, even when pregnant.

A comprehensive study on sexual and reproductive health in Cape Verde indicates widespread public agreement on women's autonomy. The 10th Round of Afrobarometer, conducted in 2024-2025, found that 89% of respondents believe women should decide if and when they marry, with higher support in urban areas and among those without material deprivation. Similarly, 86% of participants affirmed women's right to decide when and how many children they want, with women expressing stronger support than men.

The survey also highlighted significant public endorsement for keeping girls in school regardless of pregnancy. Ninety-three percent of respondents agreed that pregnant girls or those with children should be allowed to continue their studies, a view consistently held across different educational backgrounds and socioeconomic statuses. Furthermore, 95% of participants believe schools should provide sex education to help young people make informed decisions, with minimal opposition.

Regarding reproductive health access, 90% of Cape Verdeans support access to contraceptives for all, irrespective of marital status, and 79% favor making them available to all sexually active individuals, regardless of age. Support for these measures generally increases with educational attainment and is slightly higher in urban areas and among women.

Why it mattersCape Verdeans are affected by these findings as they reflect societal attitudes towards women's rights and reproductive health decisions.

Cape Verde Child Malnutrition Rates Rise

Acute malnutrition and stunting in children under five have worsened in Cape Verde since 2015, according to national statistics.

Acute malnutrition and stunting among children under five have worsened in Cape Verde since 2015, according to data released by the National Statistics Institute (INE). The Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) statistical report shows acute malnutrition rose from 3% to 5.9% between 2015 and 2019, before falling to 4% in 2024. Within the subgroup of children aged two to five, there was a clear degradation of the indicator, increasing from 2.7% to 4.6% between 2015 and 2024, with peaks reaching 8.1% in 2022.

Stunting prevalence among children under five in Cape Verde stood at 12.6% in 2024, up from 11.8% in 2015. The report concludes that between 2009 and 2024, there has been no progress in reducing the rate of child stunting, despite international nutrition targets. These figures are part of the seventh statistical report on the UN's Sustainable Development Goals, managed by Cape Verde's National Statistics Institute.

The report, which presents an updated picture of Cape Verde's position on SDG implementation, also noted a decrease in the proportion of the population living below the international poverty line, from 4.6% in 2015 to 2.3% in 2022. The SDGs include 169 measures grouped into 17 major objectives across social, environmental, economic, and institutional areas, with a target for implementation by 2030.

Why it mattersChildren under five in Cape Verde are experiencing increased rates of acute malnutrition and stunting, impacting their immediate health and long-term development.

Cape Verde Economy Faces Downward Risks Despite Growth Forecast

The Public Finance Council finds the 2027 economic growth forecast plausible but warns of significant downward risks.

The Public Finance Council (CFP) has assessed the State Budget proposal for 2027, deeming the projected 5.6% economic growth plausible for Cape Verde. However, the council issued a cautionary note, highlighting that risks to this forecast are predominantly downward. This vulnerability is attributed to the nation's reliance on tourism, fluctuations in international commodity prices, and the broader global economic uncertainty.

In its report on the macroeconomic forecasts underpinning the 2027 budget, the CFP recommended that the government provide more robust substantiation for its underlying assumptions. Additionally, the council advised a more thorough analysis of the potential impacts of unforeseen shocks on public finances. The council's findings were presented in the context of the upcoming state budget, which outlines the nation's economic direction.

The CFP's analysis underscores the delicate balance Cape Verde must maintain to achieve its economic objectives. While the projected growth indicates potential progress, the identified risks necessitate careful monitoring and strategic planning to mitigate potential negative impacts on the national economy and public finances.

Why it mattersInvestors face downward economic risks due to tourism dependence and global uncertainty, impacting financial planning.

Driver Held After Schoolgirl Fatally Struck

A clandestine taxi driver faces pre-trial detention following the death of a seven-year-old student near Miraflores School.

A young driver involved in the fatal collision with Aylla Danielle, a third-year student at Miraflores Educational Center, has been placed in preventive detention, the National Police announced on October 19. The driver was operating an unlicensed taxi, known locally as a 'Clã,' when the incident occurred near the school.

The National Police urged all drivers to exercise extreme caution, particularly around educational institutions, emphasizing that a moment's distraction can have devastating consequences. Aylla Danielle, aged seven, was struck by a vehicle while crossing a pedestrian crossing as she left school on October 14.

Witnesses reported that the child was thrown several meters and sustained multiple injuries. She was admitted to Agostinho Neto University Hospital with severe polytrauma and later suffered cardiac arrest, according to the hospital's clinical director.

Why it mattersThe driver's detention and the police warning directly impact road safety for children and drivers.

Barbosa Elected MpD Parliamentary Group Leader

Emanuel Barbosa will lead the MpD parliamentary group with a focus on internal cohesion and constructive opposition.

Emanuel Barbosa has been elected as the new president of the MpD Parliamentary Group. He takes over from Luís Carlos Silva, who held the position since June. Barbosa, a deputy for the Europe and Rest of the World constituency, stated his intention to strengthen internal cohesion and value the experience of fellow deputies.

The new leadership's commitment includes fostering a responsible, dialogical, and proactive opposition. Barbosa emphasized the importance of dialogue, proximity, and listening to citizens, aiming to translate their concerns into concrete proposals for national development. He described the opposition's role as one that criticizes when necessary but also offers solutions.

The MpD Parliamentary Group's new leadership reaffirms its dedication to constructive opposition, maintaining a firm stance in overseeing government actions while remaining open to dialogue for the national interest. Barbosa's election follows the National Convention that appointed Paulo Veiga as the president of the MpD party.

Why it mattersEmanuel Barbosa's election as parliamentary group leader affects investors by signaling a commitment to constructive dialogue and oversight of government actions.
