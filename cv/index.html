CVsky adds ATR 72-600 to boost inter-island flights

The airline's operational fleet grows to three aircraft with the new plane, with plans for a fourth by January 2027.

Cape Verdean airline CVsky received a new ATR 72-600 aircraft on November 6 at Nelson Mandela International Airport in Praia. The new plane will increase the company's operational capacity and improve air travel connections between the archipelago's islands.

The ATR 72-600, serial number MSN 1829, is undergoing final certification with the Civil Aviation Authority (AAC) and is expected to be integrated into commercial operations within days. This addition brings CVsky's operational fleet to three aircraft, with a fourth planned for January 2027.

The airline stated the expansion aims to ensure a reserve aircraft for greater protection and continuity of national air links. The new model is suited for regional aviation and island markets, enhancing the regularity, reliability, and efficiency of domestic flights.

Why it mattersCape Verdean residents and businesses relying on inter-island travel will experience improved connectivity and reliability.

Cape Verde prosecutors warn against politicizing justice

The ASSIMP union expressed concern over the Interior Minister's public statements regarding the "Shark" operation.

The Association of Trade Union of Public Prosecutors (ASSIMP) in Cape Verde issued a statement on Thursday, November 5, expressing concern over public declarations made by the Minister of Internal Administration on November 4. The minister had stated that no case would be "shelved" going forward and questioned why arrests related to the "Shark" operation only occurred in 2026, despite phone interceptions from 2016 and 2017.

ASSIMP stated that public comments from government officials on ongoing legal cases carry significant institutional weight and can undermine public trust in the justice system. The association emphasized that criminal investigations and prosecutions are the exclusive domain of the Public Ministry, which operates autonomously and is bound only by law. They argue that political powers should not publicly scrutinize or direct the timing of judicial proceedings.

The union rejected any insinuation that legal processes are influenced by political convenience, asserting that the timing of investigative actions depends solely on technical and legal reasons. ASSIMP also called for responsible exercise of freedom of expression, particularly from government officials, to uphold the independence of the judiciary and public confidence in justice.

Why it mattersCape Verdean citizens may lose faith in their justice system if they perceive political interference in legal proceedings.

AdS dismantles 70 illegal water connections in Praia

The public company Águas de Santiago removed illicit water hookups in September and October, aiming to reduce a 60% water loss rate.

Águas de Santiago (AdS) announced it dismantled approximately 70 illegal water connections in Praia during September and October. These illicit connections contribute to the capital city's water loss rate, which stands at about 60%. The company stated these actions followed rigorous legal procedures and technical training for its team.

The public company carried out the dismantling operations as part of its inspection and enforcement efforts against irregularities in the water supply network. AdS highlighted that each dismantled illegal connection represents a step towards reducing waste and ensuring water is distributed fairly to those who need it. The company urged the public to collaborate and act responsibly to stop water theft and waste.

The challenge of water loss on Santiago island remains substantial, with the city of Praia experiencing a nearly 60% rate. AdS emphasized that tackling these illegal connections is important for improving water management. The company reinforced the need for public cooperation to combat water theft and waste effectively.

Why it mattersResidents of Praia are affected by water loss due to illegal connections, impacting fair distribution.

Cape Verde students use music to combat gender-based violence

A new competition in Praia encourages secondary school students to create songs about respect and equality.

The Fundação de Religiosos para a Saúde (FRS) launched a music competition in Praia on November 25, targeting secondary school students. This initiative, funded by Spanish Cooperation, uses music to raise awareness about gender-based violence (GBV) and promote respect. Students can use existing instrumentals, create their own with AI, or perform a cappella.

Victoria Seoane, FRS coordinator, stated that the competition aims to directly involve students in reflecting on GBV through their own compositions. She explained that music has always been a tool to connect with young people and raise awareness. The contest also seeks to encourage youth leadership and foster new masculinities, promoting equal relationships.

Prizes for the competition include a laptop, studio recording time, and a music video for the first-place winner, with a phone and tablet for the second and third places respectively. A special award will be given for the best lyrics. This project, developed with the Ministry of Education and Ministry of Family, is a pilot in Praia with potential future expansion.

Why it mattersSecondary school students in Praia are directly involved in creating music to address and prevent gender-based violence, with prizes and artistic support offered.

Cape Verde government appoints family and friends to jobs

The "Cabo Verde para todos" program has become a VIP club for those with the right connections, not universal opportunity.

The government of Cape Verde has transformed its "Cabo Verde para todos" initiative into an exclusive club, appointing relatives and close associates to key positions. This shift means that citizens without the correct surname, neighborhood ties, or party membership are excluded from these opportunities.

The program, initially presented as a promise of meritocracy, transparency, and opportunity for over half a million Cape Verdeans, has been criticized for favouring a select few. Appointed individuals include a second cousin needing a directorial role for their resume and a childhood neighbor whose main qualification was playing football together.

This exclusive approach to appointments affects fundamental state functions, prioritizing family and friendship over broader public interest. The appointments ensure continued harmony and financial support for Sunday lunches among the chosen circle.

Why it mattersCape Verdean citizens are denied opportunities for public service roles based on personal connections rather than merit.
