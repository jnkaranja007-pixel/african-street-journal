Cape Verde Authorities Suspend Three Praia Businesses for Hygiene Violations

Inspectors found health risks, expired products, and illegal alcohol during a sweep of retail establishments in Praia.

Three retail businesses in Praia have been suspended due to inadequate hygiene conditions and other violations, according to regional authorities. The operation, conducted by the Santiago Sul and Maio Regional Command, inspected approximately twenty retail establishments. Officials stated that the conditions found posed health risks to consumers. During the inspections, which concluded in the early evening, authorities also seized expired products, untaxed alcoholic beverages, and illegally produced spirits known as 'garrafinha'.

Further irregularities identified included the absence of posted prices and operating hours, lack of business permits, missing health certificates, improper food handling, and missing product labels. Some establishments were found to have pest infestations, poor ventilation, overcrowding, accumulated waste, and inadequately trained staff. The sale of prohibited items was also noted.

The General Inspectorate of Economic Activities (IGAE) urged business operators to comply with legal requirements. The agency warned that non-compliance could endanger consumer health and lead to legal penalties. The inspections aimed to ensure public safety and adherence to commercial regulations within the capital city.

Why it mattersConsumers in Praia are at risk of purchasing unsafe food and beverages due to poor hygiene and regulatory non-compliance in retail establishments.

Joint Mission Proposes Population Strategy Amid Demographic Shifts

A joint mission to São Vicente identified demographic transition, child protection, and elder care as key priorities for national support.

A joint mission to São Vicente has proposed a population strategy to address Cape Verde's changing demographics, including an aging population, declining birth rates, and migration. David Matern of the Joint Office stated that the country requires a comprehensive approach to these shifts. The United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) has offered support to develop this strategy.

The mission, conducted with the Minister of Family, Inclusion and Social Development, Adélsia Almeida, focused on several key areas. These included the demographic transition, safeguarding children, and strengthening the care system. Matern specifically highlighted the need for a new legal status for the elderly.

The joint mission's work continues on Santo Antão island on August 27 and 28. The initiative aims to provide a framework for managing the nation's evolving population structure and ensuring adequate social support systems are in place for all age groups.

Why it mattersThe proposed population strategy and focus on elder care and child protection directly impact the social welfare and future planning for Cape Verde's residents.

Cape Verde Elected to Vice-Presidency of African Public Health Emergencies Fund

Health Minister Lúcio Fernandes will represent the nation on the fund's monitoring committee, aiming to enhance transparency and effectiveness in regional health crisis response.

Cabo Verde has been elected to the vice-presidency of the African Public Health Emergencies Fund (APHEF) Monitoring Committee. Health Minister Lúcio Fernandes secured the position during a recent World Health Organization for Africa meeting in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. In this role, Cabo Verde will oversee the fund's implementation and operations, working alongside other member states and the WHO to improve governance and effectiveness.

This vice-presidency does not entail direct financial management of the fund or automatic funding for Cabo Verde. However, the Ministry of Health stated that the election offers a significant opportunity to amplify Cabo Verde's influence in African decision-making forums. It also promotes the sharing of national best practices and deepens regional cooperation in preparing for, preventing, and responding to health emergencies.

The African Public Health Emergencies Fund is a strategic tool designed to bolster African nations' capacity for rapid, predictable, and flexible financial mobilization during epidemics, outbreaks, and other health crises. Kenya's John Mbadi, Secretary of National Treasury and Economic Planning, will serve as the committee's president.

Why it mattersCape Verde's election to the APHEF vice-presidency allows it to influence regional health emergency response mechanisms and share national expertise.

MpD Declares Prime Minister's Delegation of Intelligence Powers Illegal

The opposition party argues that transferring control of the SIR to the Interior Minister violates laws and the institutional model designed for democratic oversight.

The Movement for Democracy (MpD) has declared its strong opposition to Prime Minister Francisco Carvalho's decision to delegate powers of the Republic's Information Service (SIR) to the Minister of Internal Administration, Carlos Miguel Sena Castro Teixeira. The party asserts this move is "ostensibly illegal and unconstitutional," fundamentally altering the established political-institutional framework for Cape Verde's intelligence service.

According to the MpD, legislation including Law No. 70/VI/2005 and Decree-Law No. 55/2009 clearly places the SIR under the direct and exclusive authority of the Prime Minister. The party emphasizes that this is not merely an administrative arrangement but a deliberate model to ensure democratic control over a sensitive service, noting the SIR lacks investigative powers like detaining suspects or directly initiating legal proceedings.

The opposition views the delegation as particularly problematic, creating a "dangerous proximity" between the intelligence service and entities responsible for internal security and criminal police. They argue this concentration of power under one minister risks the institutional balance crucial for democratic legality, especially given the SIR's historical context and the consensus built around its oversight.

Why it mattersThe opposition party's challenge to the delegation of powers over the Republic's Information Service could impact the checks and balances within the government's security apparatus.

US Suspends Visa Interviews Globally for Immigrant Applicants

The U.S. Department of State has temporarily halted all immigrant visa interview scheduling worldwide to implement new training for consular staff on candidate assessment.

The United States has suspended the scheduling of immigrant visa interviews globally as consular staff undergo mandatory training. This initiative aims to enhance the evaluation of visa applicants, particularly concerning their potential reliance on public benefits in the U.S. The State Department confirmed the training is underway at American embassies and consulates worldwide, leading to adjustments in visa service appointments.

This measure affects individuals seeking permanent residency, including relatives of U.S. citizens, those immigrating through family sponsorship, and foreign workers pursuing residency via employment. Applicants with existing appointments are being notified of cancellations or postponements and will need to await new scheduling. The suspension is a temporary shift in consular operations.

The U.S. government states the training is necessary to better assess applicants under new criteria. This action aligns with the administration's reinforced immigration policies, which focus on stricter controls and oversight of entry and stay processes in the United States. The Reuters news agency reported on the global impact of this directive.

Why it mattersCape Verdeans seeking to emigrate to the United States for permanent residency will experience delays in their visa application process due to the suspension of interviews.
