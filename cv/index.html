Family Recognizes Unidentified Man Found in São Vicente

The unidentified man, who died on August 15, was recognized by his family after police sought public assistance.

The body of a man found without identification in São Vicente has been recognized by his family. The individual had been rescued by the Municipal Firefighters of São Vicente and taken to Baptista de Sousa Hospital, where he remained until his death on August 15. Following the case, the Judicial Police (PJ) issued an alert requesting public collaboration to identify the body, as it had not been possible to determine his identity at the time.

According to the PJ, subsequent investigations and collected information led to the body being recognized by relatives. This Wednesday, September 2, the family completed the necessary procedures, allowing for the funeral arrangements to proceed. The PJ stated that the elements gathered during the investigation point to death by natural causes.

Judicial authorities expressed gratitude to the public for their cooperation throughout the process, particularly for the information provided. The public's contribution was significant in resolving the case, enabling the identification of the citizen and ensuring a dignified funeral. The PJ confirmed that the investigation's findings indicate the death was due to natural causes.

Why it mattersThe family of the deceased man is affected because they can now proceed with funeral arrangements after his identity was confirmed by authorities.

Emigrants Represent Cape Verde Abroad, Official Says

The nation's diaspora is now seen as a partner in development, prompting a reevaluation of diplomatic roles.

Cape Verdean emigrants are increasingly viewed not just through the lens of remittances and return, but as a powerful political force representing the nation abroad. This perspective suggests that each emigrant acts as a de facto ambassador, carrying the country's language, culture, music, and identity across borders. This phenomenon predates formal diplomatic structures, with early settlers establishing a presence in cities like Lisbon, Dakar, and Boston.

The strategic value of having citizens integrated into economically and institutionally strong societies is significant for a small island nation. This includes professionals such as doctors, entrepreneurs, and artists who contribute to their host countries while maintaining a connection to their homeland. Public policy now recognizes this diaspora as a key partner for development, actively seeking to map and mobilize their skills and investments.

This evolving understanding necessitates a reevaluation of traditional consular services, which focused on documentation and administrative support. The modern emigrant, including second and subsequent generations, faces new challenges related to integration, dual nationality, professional mobility, and transnational families. Diplomatic missions must adapt to these changing realities and the sensitive political landscapes of host countries regarding immigration and identity.

Why it mattersThe diaspora's integration into powerful societies offers strategic advantages, prompting a reevaluation of diplomatic roles and public policy.

Woman Held for High-Risk Drug Trafficking in Praia

Judicial police detained a 45-year-old woman in Achada de Santo António, Praia, and she was ordered into pre-trial detention.

A 45-year-old woman is in pre-trial detention after being arrested by the Judicial Police (PJ) in Praia's Achada de Santo António neighborhood. She is suspected of high-risk drug trafficking and of converting and concealing assets. The PJ stated the suspect underwent initial judicial questioning before the most severe measure, preventive detention, was applied.

The operation, conducted by the PJ's Central Section for the Investigation of Narcotrafficking and Organized Crime (SCITECO), resulted in the seizure of 18 individual doses of cocaine and 15.728 grams of cannabis, along with cutting agents and drug paraphernalia. Authorities also confiscated a mobile phone and 6,530$00 in cash, among other items deemed relevant to the investigation.

The search and seizure operation was carried out under a court order issued by the Public Prosecutor's Office and authorized by the Criminal Investigation Court of the Praia Judicial District. The PJ reported that the seized drugs were prepared for sale on the illicit market.

Why it mattersA woman is in pre-trial detention for suspected drug trafficking, impacting her freedom and potentially leading to asset forfeiture.

Santo Antão victims recognized, reparations paid

Forty-five years after a violent crackdown on farmers, the state has officially acknowledged victims of the 1981 regime actions.

The state has officially recognized victims of torture and mistreatment on Santo Antão dating back to 1981. A fifth list of beneficiaries for a pension established for these victims was published on January 27, 2026, confirming arbitrary arrests, beatings, and torture during the I Republic. This official acknowledgment follows years of attempts to downplay or conceal the events.

On August 31, 1981, farmers and residents of Ribeira Grande protested against imposed agrarian reforms and demanded the release of individuals detained the previous day. Military forces blocked their path in Boca de Figueiral, and according to testimonies, shots were fired, killing Adriano Santos and wounding others. Subsequent arrests, beatings, and trials of civilians in military courts followed.

While material reparations have been made, political responsibility remains. The PAICV, as the institutional successor to the ruling party of that era, is called upon to issue a clear apology to the victims, their families, and Santo Antão. The current Prime Minister's response to a public prosecutor's accusation is also being scrutinized for potential authoritarian echoes, serving as a historical reminder to recognize such reflexes before they become ingrained.

Why it mattersFarmers and their families are directly affected by the historical events and ongoing recognition of past state violence.
