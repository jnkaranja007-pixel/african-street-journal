Cape Verde agency finds non-ionizing radiation within limits

ARME measured 77 sites in Praia and found exposure levels below international health recommendations.

The Economy Multilateral Regulatory Agency (ARME) reported that non-ionizing radiation (NIR) levels in Praia are within recommended limits. A 2026 monitoring campaign across 77 locations in the capital found no danger to public health from radiofrequency stations.

ARME stated that all 77 measured points in Praia met exposure limits ranging from 28 V/m to 61 V/m, with most values significantly below these thresholds. The agency also monitored other islands, with results expected this year.

This initiative aligns with ARME's role in managing the radio spectrum, aiming to track population exposure from radiofrequency stations used for mobile, radio, and television services. The findings adhere to International Commission on Non-Ionizing Radiation Protection (ICNIRP) guidelines.

Why it mattersCape Verdean residents in Praia are protected from harmful exposure to non-ionizing radiation from radiofrequency stations.

Banco BAI Cabo Verde launches mobile bank branch

The first mobile banking unit in Cape Verde will bring services to different locations.

Banco BAI Cabo Verde (BAICV) launched the country's first mobile bank branch on Friday, October 30. The inaugural ceremony took place at the parking lot of the Bank of Cape Verde (BCV) in Praia.

The mobile branch aims to increase financial inclusion and customer proximity, according to BAICV. The bank stated the initiative will reinforce its presence by taking services to various localities, offering a more accessible banking experience.

The launch event was presided over by the Governor of the Bank of Cape Verde, Óscar Santos. BAICV stated the mobile branch is another step in its innovation and transformation.

Woman's death ends five-year homicide-free period in Praia

Celeste Cunha, 64, was allegedly killed on October 28, ending a five-year period without gender-based violence homicides in Cape Verde's capital.

Celeste Cunha, 64, was allegedly murdered on the night of Wednesday, October 28, in Praia's Castelão neighborhood. The incident, linked to gender-based violence (VBG), prompted condemnation from the Cape Verdean Institute for Gender Equality and Equity (ICIEG) and the Cape Verdean Association for the Fight Against Gender-Based Violence (ACLCVBG). This death marks the end of a five-year period without homicides associated with VBG in the capital city.

The ICIEG expressed sorrow over Cunha's death and reiterated calls for a zero-tolerance policy towards gender-based violence. The institution stated that the case highlights the ongoing need to strengthen prevention efforts and the response capacity of victim support entities. The ICIEG is coordinating with relevant authorities and, with the Cape Verdean Institute for Children and Adolescents (ICCA), is providing psychological support to secondary victims, including the victim's daughter and a minor granddaughter.

The ACLCVBG also condemned the event, warning about the severity of violence against women within family settings. The association called for a swift and thorough investigation, demanding that the alleged perpetrator be brought to justice. Both organizations are urging for increased societal awareness and improved services for victims and secondary victims.

Health · 2026-10-31

São Vicente opens cancer treatment centre

The new oncology unit at Hospital Baptista de Sousa aims to reduce patient travel to Santiago island.

São Vicente officially opened its oncology centre at Hospital Baptista de Sousa on Friday, October 30, 2026. This new facility aims to bring chemotherapy treatments closer to patients in the Barlavento region, reducing the need for inter-island travel for cancer care.

The centre, part of the ONCO-CV project, will initially treat approximately three breast cancer patients. In 2025, 64 cancer patients from Barlavento were evacuated to the Hospital Universitário Agostinho Neto on Santiago island for treatment.

The oncology unit is equipped with specialized equipment for preparing and administering chemotherapy. The project partnered with Fundação Calouste Gulbenkian and received technical collaboration from the Portuguese Institute of Oncology of Porto.

Why it mattersCancer patients in the Barlavento region of São Vicente will receive chemotherapy closer to home, reducing travel burdens.

Cape Verde's education system needs reform

The current system, rooted in the slave era, is insufficient for economic autonomy and national transformation.

Cape Verde's education system, which originated during the slave society, is now considered inadequate for the nation's future needs.

Minister of Education Arnaldo Brito and Dr. Miguel Cruz Sousa have inspired reflections suggesting the historical educational philosophy, while once emancipatory, no longer serves the country's pursuit of economic autonomy.

The current focus on certificates and administrative employment risks creating frustration as the economy does not transform as rapidly as expectations.

Why it mattersCape Verdeans are educated for a system that does not transform quickly enough, limiting their opportunities.
