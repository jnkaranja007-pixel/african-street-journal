Cape Verdeans Support Women's Autonomy in Marriage and Motherhood

A recent Afrobarometer study reveals strong public backing for women's rights to decide on marriage, childbirth, and education, even when pregnant.

A comprehensive study on sexual and reproductive health in Cape Verde indicates widespread public agreement on women's autonomy. The 10th Round of Afrobarometer, conducted in 2024-2025, found that 89% of respondents believe women should decide if and when they marry, with higher support in urban areas and among those without material deprivation. Similarly, 86% of participants affirmed women's right to decide when and how many children they want, with women expressing stronger support than men.

The survey also highlighted significant public endorsement for keeping girls in school regardless of pregnancy. Ninety-three percent of respondents agreed that pregnant girls or those with children should be allowed to continue their studies, a view consistently held across different educational backgrounds and socioeconomic statuses. Furthermore, 95% of participants believe schools should provide sex education to help young people make informed decisions, with minimal opposition.

Regarding reproductive health access, 90% of Cape Verdeans support access to contraceptives for all, irrespective of marital status, and 79% favor making them available to all sexually active individuals, regardless of age. Support for these measures generally increases with educational attainment and is slightly higher in urban areas and among women.

Why it mattersCape Verdeans are affected by these findings as they reflect societal attitudes towards women's rights and reproductive health decisions.
