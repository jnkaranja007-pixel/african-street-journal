Cape Verde confirms Neves re-election with 63.96% of valid votes

The election commission reported 68.33% abstention, the highest in a presidential election, and said its voter register relies on 2007 data.

Sources: A Nacao and Mindel Insite · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Cape Verde’s National Electoral Commission confirmed José Maria Neves’ re-election as president on Saturday, 21 November, after he won 82,621 votes, or 63.96% of valid votes, in the 15 November election. The commission said the final count gave him an absolute majority in the first round.

Joana Rosa placed second with 40,568 votes, or 31.40%. Commission president Maria do Rosário Gonçalves said abstention reached 68.33%, a record for a presidential election in the country. Of 419,532 registered voters, more than two-thirds did not vote.

Gonçalves said the voter register was based on information from 2007 and needed updating to reflect population movement. Abstention was 65% in Cape Verde and 81% among registered voters abroad; 14,266 of the 76,452 voters registered overseas cast ballots.

Why it mattersCape Verde’s election commission must review a voter register based on 2007 information as abstention reached 81% among registered voters abroad.
