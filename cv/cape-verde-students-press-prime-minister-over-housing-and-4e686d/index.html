Cape Verde students press prime minister over housing and crowded classes

Students from Uni-CV and UTA say displaced classmates lack accommodation and the Maritime Institute's small classrooms are often overcrowded.

Sources: Mindel Insite and A Nacao · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Students from the University of Cape Verde (Uni-CV) and the Atlantic Technical University (UTA) asked Prime Minister Francisco Carvalho for better housing and teaching conditions at a meeting in São Vicente on Thursday, 19 November. They raised a shortage of university residences, crowded classrooms and poor infrastructure at the ISECMAR maritime institute.

Students from other islands and São Tomé and Príncipe who attend ISECMAR struggle to find accommodation, they said. Telmo, a scholarship student from São Tomé and Príncipe, told the meeting he had lived in different places during his three years in Cape Verde and sometimes relied on others to stay in São Vicente. A university residence due to open on 1 October, according to the Student Support Fund, FICASE, was still not accepting students.

A third-year student said small classrooms are often overcrowded, forcing some students to move elsewhere to follow lessons. João Cardoso, who studies theology and history and geography, also raised student anxiety and asked what education changes the government plans over the next five years.

Why it mattersStudents from other islands and São Tomé and Príncipe who depend on scholarships still have no place in the residence that was due to open on 1 October.
