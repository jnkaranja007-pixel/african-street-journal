CHRAGG calls for dialogue over Lissu conviction

The human rights commission says it found the proceedings compliant with procedure but stresses ongoing rights protections.

Sources: Daily News Tanzania and The Citizen · 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

Following the conviction and sentencing of CHADEMA national chairman Tundu Lissu, the Commission for Human Rights and Good Governance (CHRAGG) has urged respect for judicial independence and dialogue. In a position statement issued in Dodoma on November 13, 2026, it said disagreements over the case should be pursued through lawful and democratic channels. Lissu was acquitted of treason by the High Court in Dar es Salaam on November 6, but convicted of incitement and jailed for four years.

The commission said its review focused on rights and governance rather than the merits of the judgment, and it has no power to review the court's findings. It found Lissu's arrest on April 9, 2025, his court presentation, committal and trial followed the applicable procedure. An inquiry on June 27, 2025, reported that he had freedom of worship, privacy, contact with his lawyers and time for exercise in custody.

CHRAGG cautioned that procedure alone does not establish that every human rights obligation was met, pointing to legal representation, time to prepare a defence and the right to examine witnesses. It also said peaceful advocacy for constitutional and electoral reform must be distinguished from criminal conduct, and that authorities must keep protecting personal liberty against arbitrary detention.

Why it mattersLissu's supporters and rights advocates now have an official government body on record urging that disputes over the verdict go through the courts and political process.
