Tanzania plans 100 standard neonatal units by 2028

The expansion would raise the national total of newborn care units from 438 to 538, Deputy Health Minister Florence Samizi said.

Source: Daily News Tanzania · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Tanzania's deputy health minister, Dr Florence Samizi, said the government intends to open 100 standard neonatal intensive care units nationwide by 2028, meant to follow World Health Organisation standards. She spoke in Dar es Salaam at World Prematurity Day events hosted by the Doris Mollel Foundation with the Tanzania Preterm Family Network.

Samizi said the government is building 36 such units now: 35 of its own, 15 at regional hospitals and 20 at district hospitals and health centres, plus one at Katoro backed by the Doris Mollel Foundation. In 2018 the country had only 14 neonatal intensive care units, against more than 438 newborn care units today.

The target is to cut newborn deaths from 24 to 12 per 1,000 live births by 2030. Samizi said the new facilities will let mothers and premature babies stay together, offer counselling and privacy, and allow fathers to take part in care when mothers cannot.

Why it mattersMothers and premature babies in districts without specialised care would gain nearby units, and the ministry must deliver 36 under construction first.
