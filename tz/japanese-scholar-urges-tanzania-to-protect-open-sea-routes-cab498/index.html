Japanese scholar urges Tanzania to protect open sea routes and shared rules

Masayuki Tadokoro linked secure maritime trade to prosperity and urged cooperation that protects smaller countries as well as restraining powerful ones.

Source: The Chanzo · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Japanese international relations scholar Masayuki Tadokoro urged Tanzania and Japan to cooperate on protecting open sea routes and international rules during a public lecture in Dar es Salaam on November 24. He argued that economic prosperity depends on rules that constrain powerful countries and protect smaller states.

Tadokoro, a professor at the International University of Japan and professor emeritus at Keio University, connected Japan’s post-war development to secure maritime trade and Tanzania’s position on the Indian Ocean. His presentation said the concern was not another country’s rise, but the erosion of rules protecting all countries’ rights and security.

Japan announced an updated Free and Open Indo-Pacific policy on May 2, seeking cooperation with partner countries on resilient supply chains, digital infrastructure, economic growth and security. The lecture also addressed Japan’s post-war constitution and its restrictions on military power.

Why it mattersTanzania and Japan now face a call to cooperate on sea routes and rules that protect the rights and security of all countries.
