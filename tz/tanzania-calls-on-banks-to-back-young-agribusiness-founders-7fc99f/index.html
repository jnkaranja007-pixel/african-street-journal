Tanzania calls on banks to back young agribusiness founders

40 young entrepreneurs from Dodoma and Singida shared 40m/- in grants under the second phase of the Generation Food Accelerator.

Source: Daily News Tanzania · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Tanzania's government wants banks, local councils and private firms to do more for young people running farm businesses. Deputy Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Agriculture, Eng Athumani Kilundumya, made the appeal at the close of the programme's second phase in Dodoma, where he said the sector can create jobs, raise incomes and speed up economic growth.

40 entrepreneurs from Dodoma and Singida received the grants, worth 40m/- in total, under the Generation Food Accelerator (GFA), run through the Vijana Kilimo Biashara project. Kilundumya said young founders need capital, skills, technology, business advice and market access, and that opportunities stretch across seed production, machinery hire, input supply, produce collection, storage and processing, not just planting.

The GFA is run by Rikolto with the World Food Programme (WFP) and funded by the Mastercard Foundation, and it targets people aged 18 to 35 working in sorghum, sunflower, vegetable and fruit supply chains. Of 731 applications, 100 entrepreneurs were picked, and women make up 53 per cent of them. WFP's Isaac Mathe said 38 businesses have registered, licensed and tax-compliant, and 43 jobs have been created.

Why it mattersThe 40 grant recipients and 38 newly formalised businesses now face the task of turning small grants into lasting enterprises, with banks urged to make loans easier to obtain.
