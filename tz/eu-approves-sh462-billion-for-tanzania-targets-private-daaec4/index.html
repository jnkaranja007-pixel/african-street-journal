EU Approves Sh462 Billion for Tanzania, Targets Private Sector

The European Union has allocated Sh462.8 billion for Tanzania, with funds directed towards private sector initiatives rather than direct government budget support.

Source: The Chanzo · 2026-10-21 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X

The European Union has approved a Sh462.8 billion package for Tanzania, a decision that will channel support through partners and modalities benefiting the private sector. This funding, approved on July 24, 2026, allocates €129 million for mineral and agricultural value chains, €20 million for digital economy and connectivity, and €7 million for a cooperation facility. The EU Delegation confirmed that the funds align with the Global Gateway strategy and DIRA 2050, emphasizing mutual benefit for Tanzanian and EU interests.

This approach marks a shift from previous direct budget support to the government, which has been discontinued due to a reported deterioration in human rights, democracy, and the rule of law following the October 2025 election. The EU stated that future cooperation depth will depend on Tanzania's progress in restoring respect for human rights and the rule of law, and that support will avoid government systems.

The EU's strategy prioritizes mobilizing private investment in sectors like digital, energy, and transport, creating opportunities for European companies. This model was previously used in 2024 when the EU blended a €15 million grant with European Investment Bank credit lines to support small and medium-sized businesses, aiming to reduce lending risks for local banks.

Why it mattersTanzanian businesses and agricultural value chains will benefit from new EU funding channeled through private sector initiatives.
