Tanzania signs €175 million French loans for water, food and dairy

The agreements allocate €90 million to Lake Victoria water and sanitation, €55 million to food infrastructure and €30 million to smallholder dairy.

Sources: Farmers Review Africa and The Chanzo · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Tanzania signed three concessional loan agreements worth €175 million with the French Development Agency in Paris on November 24, to fund water and sanitation, urban food infrastructure and dairy projects. The agreements were signed by Finance Minister Khamis Mussa Omar and the agency’s chief executive, Christophe Lecourtier.

The largest loan, €90 million, will fund the second phase of the Lake Victoria Water and Sanitation Project for communities around the lake. A €55 million loan will support food-trade and distribution infrastructure in Mwanza and Tanga. The remaining €30 million will finance a programme for smallholder dairy farmers across Tanzania, co-financed with the International Fund for Agricultural Development, to improve milk production and climate adaptation.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and East African Cooperation said the agreements bring Tanzania’s total development cooperation with the agency to €1.3 billion. The water project and urban food programme are also expected to receive European Union grants administered by the agency.

Why it mattersSmallholder dairy farmers across Tanzania are included in a €30 million programme to improve milk production and adaptation to climate change.
