Tanzania signs €175 million in French loans for water, food and dairy

The three agreements include €90 million for Lake Victoria water and sanitation, plus financing for urban food infrastructure and smallholder dairy.

Sources: The Chanzo, The Citizen and Farmers Review Africa · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Tanzania signed three concessional loan agreements worth €175 million, about TSh513 billion, with the French Development Agency in Paris on November 24, 2026. The financing covers water and sanitation, urban food infrastructure and climate-smart agriculture.

The largest loan, €90 million, will fund the second phase of the Lake Victoria Water and Sanitation Project, which is intended to improve clean-water and sanitation access for communities around the lake. A €55 million loan will support food-trade and distribution infrastructure in Mwanza and Tanga. The remaining €30 million, co-financed with the International Fund for Agricultural Development, is for a programme to improve smallholder dairy production and climate adaptation, according to a Ministry of Foreign Affairs and East African Co-operation statement.

The government says European Union grants administered through the agency will complement the water and urban food financing.

Why it mattersSmallholder dairy farmers are covered by a €30 million programme to improve milk production and their capacity to adapt to climate change.
