Tanzania maintains Ebola alert despite no local cases

Heightened screening and public-health precautions remain in place following nine cases in neighbouring Kenya.

Source: The Chanzo · 2026-10-23 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Tanzania's Ministry of Health confirmed no Ebola cases have been detected within the country, yet it is maintaining heightened entry screening and public-health precautions. This alert, introduced last week, remains necessary due to the ongoing outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and recent imported cases in Kenya, where the total has risen to nine.

Chief Medical Officer Prof Bruno Fokas Sunguya stated on October 21 that "So far, there is no Ebola disease in Tanzania." The alert is a response to Kenya's situation, where nine cases are linked to an imported infection, with one confirmed death as of October 20, according to the US Centres for Disease Control and Prevention. Tanzania's measures include mandatory online health-surveillance forms and screening at all entry points, with high-risk travellers facing 21 days of monitoring.

The Ministry is reinforcing screening at airports, ports, and land borders, alongside community surveillance and treatment site readiness. This effort builds on a September guideline for managing filovirus cases, finalised with the WHO. Symptoms of the Bundibugyo virus disease can include fever, tiredness, muscle pain, headache, sore throat, vomiting, and diarrhoea, appearing two to 21 days after infection.
