Dar es Salaam launches cleanliness campaign

The regional commissioner urged residents to maintain cleanliness at home and combat littering to improve the environment and public health.

Source: Habari Leo · 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

Dar es Salaam's Regional Commissioner, Albert Chalamila, launched a cleanliness campaign on November 14, 2026, in Kijitonyama, urging residents to start cleaning their homes and become ambassadors against littering. He stated that Dar es Salaam faces environmental pollution from improper waste disposal, blocked drains, and dirty markets and beaches, which damage the city's image and endanger public health. The region produces approximately 4,600 tons of waste daily, making resident participation crucial for a cleaner environment.

Chalamila emphasized that the campaign aims to ensure Dar es Salaam remains clean, especially in preparation for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) and upcoming international conferences. He stressed that cleanliness should be a daily habit, not just for major events, as the campaign seeks to build a lasting culture of hygiene. The region has also secured an investor from Ghana for the Pugu dumpsite to process waste, and all 564 local government areas are committed to participating.

The campaign will involve street cleaners, and motorcycles will be used to monitor progress and report on street cleanliness across Dar es Salaam. Mathew Andrew, Chairman of Dar es Salaam Sanitation Contractors, highlighted the importance of stakeholder cooperation and requested government support for local contractors, including official recognition for waste collection vehicles within the transport system to facilitate their operations without hindrance.

Why it mattersResidents of Dar es Salaam must actively participate in a new cleanliness campaign to improve environmental conditions and public health, with 4,600 tons of waste generated daily.
