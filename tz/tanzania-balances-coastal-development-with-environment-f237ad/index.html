Tanzania balances coastal development with environment

The Mwalimu Julius Nyerere Bridge connects Kigamboni and Dar es Salaam, supporting economic growth.

Source: Daily News Tanzania · 2026-11-11 · 1 min read

Tanzania is investing in coastal infrastructure like the Kigamboni Bridge in Dar es Salaam to link economic development with environmental protection as it pursues its climate and blue economy goals before COP31 in Türkiye.

The 680-metre Mwalimu Julius Nyerere Bridge, opened on April 19, 2016, by the late President John Magufuli, connects Kurasini and Kigamboni across Kurasini Creek, replacing a ferry service. The National Social Security Fund contributed 60 percent of project financing, with the government providing the remaining 40 percent.

The bridge has six vehicle lanes and two pedestrian and cycling lanes, improving movement for people and goods. The government, through the Office of the Vice-President, is advancing policies to protect marine ecosystems and build resilience to climate risks ahead of international climate action discussions.

Why it mattersThe government is balancing infrastructure development with environmental protection to support sustainable use of marine resources and protect coastal communities.
