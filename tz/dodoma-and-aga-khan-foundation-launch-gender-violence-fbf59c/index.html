Dodoma and Aga Khan Foundation launch gender-violence campaign with income programme

The Tuinuke Pamoja project pairs community education on gender-based violence with household income activities across seven districts, delivered through 105 local groups.

Source: Habari Leo · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Dodoma's regional government has joined the Aga Khan Foundation (AKF) in a campaign against gender-based violence that pairs education with economic empowerment. The work runs under the Tuinuke Pamoja project, which the Irish embassy in Tanzania funds and AKF carries out across seven districts.

Onorata Rwegasira, a community development officer with the regional secretariat, said the partners are encouraging couples to work together on income-generating activities, arguing that a better household economy can ease tension at home. Project officer Agnes Nkuba said AKF works through 105 groups across Dodoma's councils, using a publicity vehicle, open discussions, religious and traditional leaders and community groups to reach residents.

The campaign was launched on 25 November. Asha Nyoni, coordinator of the 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence at the Ministry of Community Development, Gender, Women and Special Groups, said the government wants to work with development partners, including AKF, to end all forms of gender-based violence.

Why it mattersCouples and women in seven Dodoma districts are being recruited into income-generating work, with AKF's 105 local groups carrying the campaign to households.
