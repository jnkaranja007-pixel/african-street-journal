Tundu Lissu convicted of incitement, sentenced to four years

Tanzanian opposition leader Tundu Lissu was found not guilty of treason but convicted of incitement on November 6, 2026.

The High Court in Dar es Salaam on Friday, November 6, 2026, convicted Tundu Lissu, national chairperson of CHADEMA, of incitement and sentenced him to four years in prison. A three-judge panel ruled that his April 2025 election remarks did not prove the intention to intimidate the government required for treason, but instead encouraged non-peaceful obstruction of the election. This verdict removes the possibility of a death sentence but does not secure Lissu's immediate release.

Lissu challenges the incitement conviction and has requested bail pending his appeal, partly to attend his elder brother Alute’s burial. The court proceedings concluded without an announced decision on bail. The case has been ongoing since Lissu's arrest after a CHADEMA rally in Mbinga, involving a lengthy committal process at Kisutu and a High Court trial that faced disputes over anonymous witnesses, courtroom access, and police evidence.

The trial's basis lies in Lissu's April 3, 2025, speech at CHADEMA's Mikocheni offices, where he discussed the party's 'No Reforms, No Election' campaign. Prosecutors argued his statements about stopping the election and mobilising rebellion indicated an intent to intimidate the executive, while Lissu maintained they were part of a political campaign for electoral reform. He was arrested on April 9, 2025, in Mbinga and charged with treason on April 10, 2025, at the Kisutu Resident Magistrate’s Court.

Why it mattersTanzanian opposition leader Tundu Lissu faces a four-year prison sentence after being convicted of incitement, impacting his political activities and freedom.

Tanzania aims for 70% domestic medicine production by 2030

Government has provided conflicting targets for local drug manufacturing, creating uncertainty about the goal.

Tanzania aims to produce 70 per cent of its medicine needs by 2030, a significant increase from the current 20 per cent. Health Minister Mohamed Mchengerwa announced this ambition at the Tanzania Pharmaceutical Technologists Association conference in Arusha. Achieving this goal would reduce the nation's reliance on imported medicines, which strains foreign exchange reserves and creates vulnerability during global health crises.

However, the government has presented inconsistent targets for domestic medicine production. In January 2026, Minister Mchengerwa stated a goal of 65 per cent, while the Medical Stores Department later published an 80 per cent target. On October 13, 2026, the Ministry of Health informed Parliament of a target exceeding 60 per cent, adding further confusion.

Questions remain about how the percentage target is measured, whether it includes medical devices and laboratory products, and the specific growth rate required to meet the goal. The current domestic production meets about 20 per cent of national demand, according to the Ministry of Health.

Why it mattersTanzanians' access to essential medicines and medical supplies is at stake due to unclear government targets for domestic production.

Tanzanian law allows adopted children to inherit

The Law of the Child Act clarifies that adopted children have legal inheritance rights from adoptive parents.

Tanzanian law now confirms that adopted children can inherit property from their adoptive parents. The Law of the Child Act, Cap. 13 [R.E. 2023], establishes adoption as a legal process, distinct from informal family arrangements. Section 64 of the Act details the legal consequences of adoption orders, transferring parental rights and responsibilities.

Under Section 65(1) of the Act, if an adoptive parent dies without a will, their property devolves as if the adopted child were their natural child. An adoption order legally establishes a child's right to inherit from adoptive parents, without needing to prove a biological link. Section 66 also addresses adopted children's inclusion in wills.

This legal framework ensures that children raised and cared for by adoptive parents have inheritance rights. The adoption order is the key legal document establishing this relationship and its consequences for property devolution.

Why it mattersAdopted children in Tanzania are legally entitled to inherit from their adoptive parents, ensuring they are not disinherited.

Business · 2026-11-09

Tanzania hosts African ethics conference

Experts from Africa, Europe, and the US met in Dar es Salaam to discuss responsible business practices.

Tanzania is aiming to become a regional centre for ethical leadership and responsible business, as experts gathered for the Absa BEN-Africa Conference 2026 in Dar es Salaam. The two-day event, held at the University of Dar es Salaam Business School, brought together scholars, policymakers, and business leaders from Africa, Europe, and the United States.

Justice (Emeritus) Sivangilwa Mwangesi, Commissioner of the President’s Office – Ethics Secretariat, stated that ethical leadership should be seen as a foundation for development, not a barrier to economic progress. Discussions covered corporate responsibility, sustainable growth, and the impact of technology, with a focus on African philosophies like Ubuntu for strengthening business practices.

Absa Bank Tanzania Managing Director Obedi Laiser noted the conference showcased Africa's capacity to influence global ethics discussions, reaffirming the bank's support for integrity and accountability. University of Dar es Salaam Business School Dean Prof Omari Khalifa Mbura highlighted universities' role in preparing future leaders for ethical challenges.

Why it mattersThe conference in Dar es Salaam brought together international experts to discuss ethical leadership and responsible business practices, potentially influencing business growth across Africa.

Sport · 2026-11-09

Tanzania builds sports facilities for global events

The nation is investing in diverse venues beyond football to bid for major competitions like the All-Africa Games.

Tanzania is accelerating investment in sports infrastructure to become a hub for international sporting events, according to Neema Msitha, Executive Secretary of the National Sports Council. The country aims to bid for major competitions, including multi-sport events, leveraging its peace, hospitality, and cultural attractions. Previously, a lack of facilities hindered hosting bids, but the government is prioritizing modern sports complexes to meet international standards.

Msitha stated that completing these projects will open new opportunities for local athletes and shift the focus to stakeholders utilizing the enhanced facilities. She cited Morocco, Egypt, South Africa, and Algeria as examples of nations successful in hosting due to their established sports infrastructure. The government is establishing the Sports Infrastructure Management Agency (SIMA) to oversee these developments.

The infrastructure program includes multipurpose venues, athletics tracks, basketball and netball courts, tennis facilities, swimming pools, and indoor arenas. The Benjamin Mkapa Stadium in Dar es Salaam already has a World Athletics-certified track. New facilities are planned for Kawe, Arusha, and Dodoma, with recreation centres also slated for Dar es Salaam and Dodoma.

Why it mattersTanzania's investment in diverse sports facilities aims to attract international competitions and create more opportunities for local athletes.
