CCM expels former Vice President Nchimbi for party rule violations

Tanzania's ruling Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM) party expelled Emmanuel Nchimbi a day after his resignation, citing conduct that violated party rules and regulations.

The Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM) party announced the expulsion of former Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi on August 26, 2026. This decision came a day after Nchimbi publicly resigned from his position. Party General Secretary Asha Rose Migiro stated that Nchimbi's expulsion was due to conduct that violated the party's rules concerning discipline and ethics, a matter discussed and resolved by both the Central Committee and the National Executive Committee.

Migiro informed the press at approximately 10:30 pm that the National Executive Committee unanimously passed a resolution to remove Nchimbi from membership. The expulsion is effective immediately, meaning Nchimbi is no longer a member of the Chama Cha Mapinduzi. This action follows Nchimbi's resignation, which was announced through a dormant personal account, a day before parliament was scheduled to resume its session.

Nchimbi's resignation and subsequent expulsion occur amidst speculation of a falling-out with President Samia Suluhu Hassan, though neither side has confirmed this. His advocacy for a new constitution and his stance on reform and justice are seen by some as contributing factors to the rift. Nchimbi was considered a reformer within the ruling party, having previously championed greater tolerance towards political opponents and activists.

Why it mattersThis event directly impacts Tanzanian citizens by altering the political landscape and potentially influencing future governance and policy directions, particularly regarding constitutional reform and political tolerance.

Prosecution cross-examines CHADEMA chairperson Lissu in treason trial

State attorneys questioned Tundu Lissu on his April 3, 2025 speech and its online publication after he concluded his defense in the High Court.

The prosecution began cross-examining CHADEMA chairperson Tundu Lissu on Wednesday in Dar es Salaam, focusing on remarks he made in an April 3, 2025 speech and its subsequent online publication. State Attorney Nassoro Katuga pressed Lissu on whether he intended his speech to reach the public and if he controlled its streaming by Jambo TV. Lissu confirmed he wanted the speech to reach citizens, stating it contained "very important information," but denied controlling the publication, attributing that decision to a Jambo TV employee.

The questioning followed a two-day pause in proceedings, with Lissu having delivered his final defense submissions earlier on Wednesday. Lissu, a lawyer and CHADEMA chair, acknowledged his legal training and lack of immunity. He confirmed making the words attributed to him, including references to "kukinukisha," "mobilising rebellion," and "stopping the election," but argued these phrases were taken out of context from a longer speech.

The exchange became pointed when Lissu stated he had no obligation to provide documentary proof for awards and international bodies he cited in his defense, unless challenged by the prosecution. Katuga also challenged Lissu's position that citizens can lawfully oppose election laws and government decisions through public mobilization, a significant part of the cross-examination.

Why it mattersThis trial affects citizens by examining the limits of free speech and protest, potentially influencing how individuals can express dissent against government decisions and election laws.

Tanzania hosts ADDI delegation at National Museum

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and East African Cooperation arranged a special visit for the African Diaspora Development Institute (ADDI) delegation to the National Museum.

A delegation from the African Diaspora Development Institute (ADDI) visited the National Museum of Tanzania in Dar es Salaam on August twenty-sixth, two thousand twenty-six. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and East African Cooperation organized the visit to allow the guests to learn about Tanzania's history, culture, and heritage, aiming to strengthen cultural diplomacy.

Ambassador Arikana Chihombori-Quao from Zimbabwe, founder and president of ADDI, led the delegation. She urged Africans to unite, cooperate, and maintain peace, emphasizing the importance of unity in protecting and developing Africa's heritage.

Dr. Aloyce Mwambwiga, Chief Curator of the National Museum and House of Culture, encouraged both local citizens and international visitors to explore the National Museum to learn more about Tanzania's history, culture, and heritage. This visit is part of efforts to promote cultural diplomacy and showcase Tanzania through its historical and cultural legacy to both domestic and international guests.

Why it mattersThe visit directly affects the ADDI delegation by providing them with an opportunity to learn about Tanzania's cultural heritage and strengthens cultural diplomacy efforts for Tanzania.

Tanzanian Energy Ministry Intervenes in Ntorya Gas Dispute, Secures Production Pledge

Tanzanian energy officials met with gas developers APT and Ndovu Resources following a dispute notice, aiming to streamline operations and meet the December 2026 production.

The Ministry of Energy's Permanent Secretary, Dr. James Mataragio, convened a meeting on August 26, 2026, with key stakeholders in the Ntorya Gas Development, including ARA Petroleum Tanzania Limited (APT), Ndovu Resources Limited, the Tanzania Petroleum Development Corporation (TPDC), and the Petroleum Upstream Regulatory Authority (PURA). This intervention followed a notice of dispute issued by Ndovu Resources on August 21, 2026, alleging breaches by APT of its obligations under the Farmout Agreement and Joint Operating Agreement.

Ndovu Resources' dispute notice specifically cited APT's failure to implement the approved 2026 Work Programme and Budget (2026 WP&B), which included drilling the CH-1 well. Instead, APT reportedly pursued alternative programs that delayed the drilling and lacked the same firm commitment. These proposed changes were not approved by the Joint Venture, TPDC, or PURA, leading to material delays and minimal work during 2026.

Following the ministry's meeting, APT Chief Executive Officer Dr. Salman Al Shidi pledged to ensure the company fulfills its responsibilities and completes the necessary steps to commence gas production by the revised target of December 2026. The Ntorya gas field, located in the Ruvuma Basin, holds an estimated 1.6 trillion cubic feet of natural gas, with APT owning 75 percent and Ndovu 25 percent of the project.

Why it mattersThis dispute and the ministry's intervention directly affect the timely development of a significant natural gas resource, impacting energy supply and potential revenue for the country and its citizens.

Tanzania proposes sugar law changes to boost industry control and investment

The government introduced a bill to parliament today that seeks to amend the Sugar Industry Act, Chapter 251, to improve product quality and increase investment.

The Tanzanian government has submitted the Agricultural Laws (Amendments) Act, 2026, to parliament, proposing changes to four laws, including the Sugar Industry Act. Minister of Agriculture Daniel Chongolo presented the bill, stating that the amendments aim to expand the scope of controlled sugar products and enhance their quality.

Key proposed changes include requiring registration for sugar factories based on the intended use of the produced sugar. Additionally, the bill seeks to allow refined and raw sugar imports by existing raw sugar processing plants for industrial use, aiming to increase control and promote investment in the sector.

Further amendments separate the implementation agreement from the import license for sugar, reinforcing the agreement as a tool for regulating producers and raw sugar imports for industrial use. The bill also expands the scope of individuals allowed to repackage domestically produced sugar in quantities not exceeding 100 grams, including those not registered as producers, to strengthen control over repackaging activities and protect sugar quality and standards.

Why it mattersThese proposed changes directly affect sugar producers and importers by introducing new registration requirements and import regulations, potentially altering their operational procedures and market access.
