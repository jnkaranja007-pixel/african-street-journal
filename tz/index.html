Tanzania Strengthens Anti-Money Laundering Systems

The nation has made significant progress in combating financial crimes, with ongoing legislative amendments to further bolster its financial sector.

Tanzania has successfully strengthened its systems to combat money laundering and terrorism financing, according to the Minister of Finance, Ambassador Khamis Mussa Omar. Speaking at the 26th Ministerial Council meeting of the Eastern and Southern Africa Anti-Money Laundering Group (ESAAMLG) in Kigali, Rwanda, Ambassador Omar highlighted the country's achievements in its mutual evaluation. He stated that while significant strides have been made, the government is actively addressing identified gaps to enhance the financial sector and overall economic development.

The country is working on 15 areas requiring further attention, including amendments to 12 laws applicable to both mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar. Four laws have already been passed by Parliament for mainland Tanzania, and Zanzibar is set to table a bill to amend three laws. These advancements are the result of collaboration among various institutions, including the Ministry of Finance, banking regulators, and the Ministries of Justice and Home Affairs.

The ESAAMLG meeting convened government leaders and senior officials from across the Eastern and Southern Africa region to coordinate efforts against financial crimes. Tanzania's delegation included the Minister of Constitution and Legal Affairs, Dr. Juma Homera, and the Deputy Governor of the Bank of Tanzania, Ms. Sauda Msemo, who also chairs the National Committee of Experts on Anti-Money Laundering. The participation underscores the nation's commitment to regional cooperation in financial security.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as strengthened anti-money laundering efforts signal a more stable and regulated financial environment, potentially attracting foreign investment and improving trade.

Canadian Biotech Tanzania Brings Rapid Diagnostics Closer to Patients

CheckFor UTI, a new rapid diagnostic product, aims to provide preliminary health information to Tanzanian patients before they seek medical treatment.

Canadian Biotech Limited-Tanzania is introducing rapid diagnostic technology, branded as CheckFor, to bring preliminary health screening closer to patients in Tanzania. The innovation focuses on simplicity, allowing individuals to obtain initial information about potential health issues, such as urinary tract infections, using a test strip and a color chart. This approach aims to reduce the tendency for patients to immediately purchase medication without a proper diagnosis.

The CheckFor UTI test specifically screens for indicators like leukocytes and nitrites in urine samples, providing results within minutes. While the technology is designed for ease of use without sophisticated laboratory equipment, Dr. Bartazary John, Director of Business Development at Canadian Biotech Tanzania, emphasizes that it serves as a screening tool and should not replace professional medical diagnosis. Residents at the 27th MedExpo Tanzania 2026 showed varied awareness of this new health innovation.

This innovation seeks to integrate a testing step into the patient's decision-making process, encouraging them to seek appropriate medical advice rather than self-medicating based on symptoms. Anna Beatus, a Dar es Salaam resident, noted that the technology could help individuals avoid unnecessary medicine purchases by guiding them toward further diagnosis if symptoms persist, thereby promoting a more informed approach to healthcare.

Why it mattersTanzanian patients experiencing symptoms can use rapid diagnostic tests to get preliminary information before deciding on treatment, potentially avoiding unnecessary medication.

Tanzania, DRC Accelerate Railway and Fibre-Optic Projects

The two nations will speed up a Standard Gauge Railway link and a fibre-optic cable across Lake Tanganyika to boost regional connectivity and trade.

Tanzania and the Democratic Republic of Congo have agreed to fast-track joint infrastructure projects, including a Standard Gauge Railway (SGR) link and a national fibre-optic cable across Lake Tanganyika. The commitment was reaffirmed by Tanzania's Minister for Foreign Affairs and East African Cooperation, Dr Mahmoud Thabit Kombo, during a meeting with DRC’s Ambassador to Tanzania Jean Pierre Massala in Dar es Salaam.

The SGR connection, planned to pass through Burundi, aims to strengthen regional connectivity and trade between Tanzania and the DRC. Concurrently, a fibre-optic cable will be laid from Kigoma in Tanzania to Kalemie in the DRC's Tanganyika Province via Lake Tanganyika, intending to improve digital communication between the two countries. Both nations also agreed to accelerate the establishment of a Joint Economic Commission.

Minister Kombo stated Tanzania's commitment to deepening cooperation with the DRC in various sectors, including transport, mining, oil and gas, and defence. He commended Ambassador Massala for his contributions to bilateral relations, particularly in development, trade, and investment. Ambassador Massala thanked the Tanzanian government for its cooperation during his tenure and emphasized the importance of sustained strategic development cooperation.

Why it mattersCitizens of Tanzania and the DRC will benefit from improved regional connectivity, trade, and digital communication through these accelerated infrastructure projects.

Dodoma Grape Farmers Increase Harvest Sevenfold With Bank Support

Matumbulu farmers boosted annual production from 70 to 480 tonnes on 170 acres with financing and technical aid from TADB.

Grape farmers in Dodoma's Matumbulu area have seen their annual harvest surge from 70 tonnes to 480 tonnes on the same 170 acres. This significant increase is attributed to financial support, essential inputs, and technical guidance provided by the Tanzania Agricultural Development Bank (TADB). The expansion has also generated employment, with over 300 women and at least 70 young people now involved in farm activities like harvesting and weeding.

The farmers' group chairman, Alex Yared, stated that production began rising substantially after receiving TADB's financial backing and training on improved farming methods. He noted that prior to the support, the group harvested approximately 70 tonnes from 170 acres, a figure that has now reached 480 tonnes. Individual farmers have also experienced growth, with one reporting a tenfold increase in personal harvest from three tonnes to 30 tonnes annually on a 10-acre plot.

Looking ahead, the Matumbulu farmers plan to install drip irrigation systems to further boost production beyond 600 tonnes from the 170 acres. They also intend to establish a small processing facility to add value to their grapes, moving beyond sales of fresh produce. TADB Central Zone Manager Yodas Mwanakatwe highlighted the bank's role in providing production capital and training in modern practices, emphasizing capacity building as key to improving farmer productivity and investment management.

Tanzania Integrates Digital Systems to Improve Healthcare Delivery

The nation is enhancing its health sector through electronic medical records and data-driven decision-making, aiming for broader access to quality care.

Tanzania is actively transforming its healthcare system by adopting digital technologies, moving away from paper-based records towards electronic medical records and digital health platforms. This shift is crucial for expanding access to quality healthcare amidst growing populations, limited resources, and a shortage of health professionals. The Ministry of Health is implementing initiatives like the District Health Information Software 2 (DHIS2) for collecting, analyzing, and reporting health data, enabling better monitoring and decision-making by health managers.

The country is also prioritizing the collection of individual-level electronic health information, a move away from relying solely on aggregated data. Government documents show that Electronic Medical Record (EMR) systems have been integrated into tertiary hospitals, improving the digital management of patient information and healthcare services. This technological advancement extends beyond individual facilities, aiming to create a more connected and efficient national health information network.

These digital advancements are supported by global efforts, with the World Health Organisation (WHO) emphasizing the importance of digital infrastructure and data utilization in strengthening health systems. By investing in digital infrastructure and integrating health data, Tanzania aims to promote evidence-based decision-making and enhance its capacity to monitor diseases and respond to emerging health challenges.

Why it mattersTanzanians are affected by the digital transformation of healthcare through improved access to quality services, better disease monitoring, and more efficient health information management.
