Tanzania adopts new tools in malaria fight

Improved nets, medicines, and vaccines offer hope but elimination remains distant.

Tanzania is entering a new phase in its fight against malaria, incorporating improved mosquito nets, preventive medicines, and vaccines to reduce infections and deaths. These new tools offer fresh hope, but the country faces the difficult reality that scientific progress alone will not automatically lead to elimination.

Malaria caused an estimated 610,000 deaths globally in 2024, with most victims being African children under five. While global efforts have prevented billions of cases, mosquitoes are increasingly resistant to insecticides, and resistance to preventive medicines also threatens progress. New-generation nets combining insecticides and seasonal malaria chemoprevention are being prioritized.

Vaccination is also adding protection, with two WHO-prequalified vaccines being introduced across Africa. By April 2026, 25 African countries had introduced malaria vaccination, which reduces malaria incidence by about half in the year following immunization. Sustaining gains and moving towards elimination will require combining interventions, strengthening surveillance, and increasing local financing and management.

Why it mattersTanzania is adopting new malaria interventions, including dual-ingredient nets and vaccines, to protect its population, particularly young children.

Tanzania miners seek loan security for licences

Small-scale miners in Tanzania are struggling to access finance as banks do not recognise mining licences as loan security, hindering business growth.

Small-scale miners in Tanzania are urging the government to work with banks to accept mining licences as security for loans. Banks currently fail to recognise these licences when assessing applications, leaving miners unable to secure financing for equipment and operations. Francis Mkasiwa, Head of Research and Policy, presented these recommendations at the 2026 Extractive Forum in Dodoma.

Mkasiwa stated that lending mechanisms should reflect the operating conditions of small-scale miners, enabling them to acquire equipment and improve production. Stakeholders also called for increased investment in financial literacy programmes to help miners manage their income and savings. The forum highlighted that many women in mining still lack capital to develop their allocated areas and meet licensing costs.

The forum also addressed concerns over compensation for communities affected by extractive projects, with calls for timely payments and effective livelihood restoration measures. Miraji Malinda reported that women and persons with disabilities face barriers to decision-making and economic opportunities within the mining sector, advocating for gender equality and disability inclusion.

Why it mattersSmall-scale miners in Tanzania face difficulties acquiring loans because their mining licences are not accepted as security by banks, impacting their ability to expand operations.

Dar es Salaam launches cleanliness campaign

The regional commissioner urged residents to maintain cleanliness at home and combat littering to improve the environment and public health.

Dar es Salaam's Regional Commissioner, Albert Chalamila, launched a cleanliness campaign on November 14, 2026, in Kijitonyama, urging residents to start cleaning their homes and become ambassadors against littering. He stated that Dar es Salaam faces environmental pollution from improper waste disposal, blocked drains, and dirty markets and beaches, which damage the city's image and endanger public health. The region produces approximately 4,600 tons of waste daily, making resident participation crucial for a cleaner environment.

Chalamila emphasized that the campaign aims to ensure Dar es Salaam remains clean, especially in preparation for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) and upcoming international conferences. He stressed that cleanliness should be a daily habit, not just for major events, as the campaign seeks to build a lasting culture of hygiene. The region has also secured an investor from Ghana for the Pugu dumpsite to process waste, and all 564 local government areas are committed to participating.

The campaign will involve street cleaners, and motorcycles will be used to monitor progress and report on street cleanliness across Dar es Salaam. Mathew Andrew, Chairman of Dar es Salaam Sanitation Contractors, highlighted the importance of stakeholder cooperation and requested government support for local contractors, including official recognition for waste collection vehicles within the transport system to facilitate their operations without hindrance.

Why it mattersResidents of Dar es Salaam must actively participate in a new cleanliness campaign to improve environmental conditions and public health, with 4,600 tons of waste generated daily.
