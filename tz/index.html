Tanzania scientists explore traditional medicine strategies

Government convenes practitioners, researchers, and investors to develop a national framework for local medicines.

Tanzanian scientists, health professionals, and traditional medicine practitioners met in Dar es Salaam on November 9, 2026, to discuss advancing local remedies. The Ministry of Education, Science and Technology organized the conference to explore how science and research can strengthen traditional medicine.

Deputy Minister Wanu Hafidh Ameir stated that the meeting aims to create a national framework for identifying, researching, and commercializing local medicines and medical devices. She highlighted challenges such as inadequate knowledge documentation and insufficient research on safety and efficacy, hindering the exploitation of Tanzania's abundant plant resources.

The government seeks to transform traditional medicine research findings into innovations and products that improve healthcare, create jobs, and reduce reliance on imported goods. The conference theme focuses on harnessing innovation, research, and indigenous knowledge for developing traditional medicine and health products.

Tanzania aims for 70% domestic medicine production by 2030

Government has provided conflicting targets for local drug manufacturing, creating uncertainty about the goal.

Tanzania aims to produce 70 per cent of its medicine needs by 2030, a significant increase from the current 20 per cent. Health Minister Mohamed Mchengerwa announced this ambition at the Tanzania Pharmaceutical Technologists Association conference in Arusha. Achieving this goal would reduce the nation's reliance on imported medicines, which strains foreign exchange reserves and creates vulnerability during global health crises.

However, the government has presented inconsistent targets for domestic medicine production. In January 2026, Minister Mchengerwa stated a goal of 65 per cent, while the Medical Stores Department later published an 80 per cent target. On October 13, 2026, the Ministry of Health informed Parliament of a target exceeding 60 per cent, adding further confusion.

Questions remain about how the percentage target is measured, whether it includes medical devices and laboratory products, and the specific growth rate required to meet the goal. The current domestic production meets about 20 per cent of national demand, according to the Ministry of Health.

Why it mattersTanzanians' access to essential medicines and medical supplies is at stake due to unclear government targets for domestic production.

Tanzania horticultural exports reach 569m US dollars

The value of Tanzania's horticultural exports nearly doubled over four years, reaching 569m US dollars in 2024.

Tanzania's horticultural exports have nearly doubled in value over four years, reaching 569m US dollars in 2024, as the government works to access 14 new markets.

Vice President Deogratius Ndejembi stated that export value rose from 290.7m US dollars in 2020, with the sector growing nine to 12 per cent annually. He directed Tanzania Trade Development Authority to identify products and quality requirements for new markets.

Horticultural production increased by 15.9 per cent to 9.78 million tonnes in 2024/2025 from 8.44 million tonnes in 2022/2023. Avocado output reached 203,473 tonnes in 2025/2026, with exports valued at 255.4bn/-.

Why it mattersTanzanian farmers are directly affected by efforts to build their capacity to meet international standards and increase participation in avocado farming.

Politics · 2026-11-10

CHADEMA says police tortured detained members

The opposition party claims 10 leaders and members are held without bail or lawyers, alleging mistreatment.

CHADEMA has accused police in Tanzania of torturing 10 detained leaders and members, denying them bail and access to lawyers. The party stated that arrests began as early as October 10, with one member, Saragana Bwire, arrested inside the High Court on November 6. Communications director Brenda Rupia described the detentions as a "covert operation targeting CHADEMA leaders and members."

According to a statement issued on Sunday, November 8, detainees have reportedly been subjected to torture and forced confessions. CHADEMA named several detained officials, including Fadhili Benedict Mbeya, Fredy Masele, Grace Shio, Edson Malyo Myinga, Rodrick Mwazembe, Ham Mbeyale, Neema David Mhanuzi, Issa Kwisa Lupembe, and Gwamaka Mbughi. The party also alleged that women were searched by male officers and denied hygiene items.

CHADEMA is demanding police bail for the detainees under section 134 of the relevant Act, which requires police bond or bail for incomplete investigations, excluding serious offences. Police had not publicly responded to the specific allegations by Monday evening. The party insists that suspects should not face torture or ill-treatment in custody.

Why it mattersCHADEMA members and leaders are directly affected by alleged torture, denial of legal access, and prolonged detention by police.

Education · 2026-11-10

Tanzanian College professor urges parents to teach internet laws

Professor Robert Mashenene advised parents to guide children on social media use and Artificial Intelligence for academic benefit.

Professor Robert Mashenene, the Deputy Vice-Chancellor of the College of Business Education (CBE) in Dar es Salaam, urged parents and guardians to educate secondary school students on social media usage laws at home. Mashenene stated that while giving modern phones to children is good, parents must inform them about the 'dos and don'ts' of social media. He made these remarks during the 26th form four graduation ceremony at Green Acres Schools in Dar es Salaam last weekend.

Mashenene also advised parents to involve children in household chores like cooking and assisting with family businesses. Jonathan Kasabila, the headteacher of Green Acres Secondary School, echoed this, stating parents should control and limit students' phone usage at home. Kasabila recommended that parents ensure their children have designated phone usage times and provide them with guidance on what to do and for how long.

The advice comes as schools grapple with how students use technology. Mashenene specifically mentioned guiding children on using Artificial Intelligence (AI) for their studies. The call highlights a growing concern among educators about responsible digital citizenship among young Tanzanians.
