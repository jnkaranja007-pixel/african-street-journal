Tanzania fish exports rise 11.3% to US$181.6M

The East African nation's seafood earnings grew due to investments in processing, preservation, and logistics, boosting international competitiveness.

Tanzania's fish exports reached US$181.6 million in the year ending August 2026, an 11.3% increase from US$163.1 million the previous year. This growth, attributed to improvements in processing, preservation, and logistics, highlights the impact of investments in seafood processing, cold storage, and export infrastructure. The Bank of Tanzania reported the US$18.5 million rise, underscoring efforts to strengthen the country's position in global markets.

Lake Victoria contributes an estimated 80% to 85% of Tanzania's annual fish exports, which range between 57,000 and 60,000 tonnes. Nile perch fillets are a primary export, supplying markets in Europe, the Americas, and Asia. Sardines from Lakes Victoria and Tanganyika, along with fish maws sent to China and Hong Kong, also contribute to export earnings. The United States, the European Union, and other African nations are also key export destinations.

Initiatives like the Fish4ACP project are enhancing processing capabilities and reducing post-harvest losses. Improved smoking kilns and energy-efficient stoves minimize smoke exposure and contamination, while solar-powered freezers and new ice plants in Kigoma and Uvinza, with a capacity of 15 tonnes daily, are cutting production costs by 50%. Training in marketing, financial literacy, and fisheries regulation further supports the sector's development.

Why it mattersFarmers and fishers are affected by the increased export earnings and investments in processing and infrastructure that improve their livelihoods.

Tanzania launches digital library system

Education Minister Adolf Mkenda urged the Tanzania Library Services Board to prioritise books over new buildings.

Tanzania has launched its Integrated National Digital Library System, known as iNDLS, a platform designed to consolidate member registration, ISBN/ISSN applications, cataloguing, and lending management. Education Minister Adolf Mkenda officially inaugurated the system, developed by the library board in collaboration with the e-Government Authority, at the National Library in Dar es Salaam. The digital service aims to broaden access to publications and library information via web browsers and a mobile application, though copyright restrictions may limit full online availability of some titles.

During the launch, Mkenda advised the Tanzania Library Services Board to shift funding from constructing new library facilities towards acquiring and distributing books. He stressed the importance of repairing existing infrastructure while prioritising the purchase of local titles to support writers and publishers and expand reading access. The minister also encouraged local authorities and organisations to establish small public libraries, with the board providing the necessary books.

The initiative addresses long-standing challenges in book access in Tanzania, where, according to a 2025 UNESCO report, there was one bookshop for every 116,000 people and one public library for every 189,000 people. The ministry also plans to explore centralised subscriptions for online academic resources to reduce costs for universities and ensure transparent administration of government support for publishing, focusing on publishers with proven track records.

Why it mattersStudents and researchers will gain wider access to publications and academic resources through the new digital library system and potential centralised subscriptions.

Tanzania joins SADC summit on regional governance

The nation participated in a virtual meeting to bolster solidarity, integration, and security cooperation among member states.

Tanzania has joined Southern African Development Community (SADC) member states in efforts to strengthen regional governance, solidarity, and integration. The nation participated in an Extraordinary Summit of Heads of State and Government held virtually on October 21, 2026. Ambassador Dr. Samwel William Shelukindo, Permanent Secretary of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and East African Cooperation, represented Tanzania at the meeting.

Leaders at the summit discussed key issues related to the governance and institutional operations of the regional bloc. The meeting was convened to approve the appointment of the SADC Deputy Executive Secretary for Regional Integration and to elect the incoming Chairperson of the SADC Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation.

These measures are part of SADC’s broader efforts to enhance governance structures and cooperation among member states, particularly concerning peace and security. The initiative aims to foster a conducive environment for deeper regional integration and sustainable development across Southern Africa.

Why it mattersTanzania is affected by decisions made regarding regional governance, solidarity, and integration within the SADC bloc.

Energy Ministry presents budget report to parliamentary committee

The Deputy Minister for Energy Salome Makamba submitted the first quarter budget implementation report for the 2026/27 financial year to the Parliamentary Standing Committee.

The Ministry of Energy presented its budget implementation report for the first quarter of the 2026/27 financial year to the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Energy and Minerals in Dodoma. Deputy Minister Salome Makamba led the presentation, which covered progress in electricity and renewable energy subsectors, including research, production, and utilization of renewable energy sources.

The report detailed the implementation status of key projects such as the Second Phase of the Electricity Supply Project for Hamlets (HEP IIA and HEP IIB), the Strong Grid Project, and clean cooking energy initiatives. Budget implementation reports for the Tanzania Electric Supply Company Limited (TANESCO) and the Rural Energy Agency (REA) were also presented.

The meeting, chaired by Subira Mgalu, was attended by members of the parliamentary committee, Permanent Secretary Engineer Felshesmi Mramba, and the Managing Directors of TANESCO and REA, Lazaro Twange and Engineer Hassan Saidy, respectively. The session reviewed activities and budget execution in the electricity and renewable energy sectors during the initial quarter.

Why it mattersParliamentary committee members are affected as they review the ministry's budget execution and project progress in the energy sector.

Tanzania bank and tech firm launch farming loans for youth

Azania Bank and Dronlytics offer financing for young farmers to adopt solar irrigation technology and expand operations.

Dronlytics Technologies Tanzania Limited and Azania Bank Plc have partnered to provide a dedicated agricultural loan facility for small-scale and youth farmers in Morogoro. This initiative aims to enable experienced young farmers, participating in the Dronlytics Block Farm Program, to transition to commercial-scale farming. The program supports the government's Building a Better Tomorrow: Youth Initiative for Agribusiness by offering a clear progression path for youth who have gained practical farming experience, with the first cohort expected in early 2027.

The collaboration combines Azania Bank's expertise in agricultural finance with Dronlytics' modern farming solutions, notably a USA-patented solar irrigation system designed to increase crop yields. This technology allows for up to three harvests annually, independent of seasonal rainfall, by precisely delivering water and inputs to crops. A University of Arkansas study showed similar systems reduced water use by 67% and fertilizer use by 30%, while also decreasing methane emissions from fields.

Farmers will own their irrigation systems, financed through Azania Bank and supported by Dronlytics' warranty. The program also includes drone scouting and a technology platform that integrates field and satellite data for precision agriculture. This integrated approach, from seed to market, with centralized services and mechanization, allows farmers to manage larger areas and concentrate on business development.

Why it mattersYoung Tanzanian farmers are affected by gaining access to financing and advanced irrigation technology, enabling them to increase harvests and farm at a commercial scale.
