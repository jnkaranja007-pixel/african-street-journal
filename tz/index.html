Hundreds of Tanzanian Students Depart for International Universities

Global Education Link facilitated the departure of hundreds of Tanzanian students to pursue higher education in fields like computer engineering and health sciences.

Hundreds of Tanzanian students departed the country today for international universities to pursue higher education through Global Education Link (GEL). The students left from Julius Nyerere International Airport in Dar es Salaam, with many choosing programs in science, technology, computer engineering, cyber security, artificial intelligence, civil engineering, health, law, and business.

Dar es Salaam Branch Manager Micky Musa, speaking for GEL's CEO, stated that this initiative aims to connect Tanzanian youth with global education and equip them with competitive skills. GEL is also preparing groups to travel to China, Malaysia, the United States, and the United Kingdom, encouraging aspiring students to visit their offices for assistance with university selection, admissions, visas, and travel.

The students cited the rapid pace of technological transformation and the growing importance of digital skills as reasons for choosing fields like computer science engineering and artificial intelligence. This ongoing trend of Tanzanian students seeking international education reflects a rising demand for globally recognized qualifications and specialized skills that can contribute to Tanzania's socio-economic development and global competitiveness.

Why it mattersHundreds of Tanzanian students are affected by gaining access to international higher education opportunities and globally competitive skills through GEL's programs.

President Hassan Appoints Ndejembi New Vice President

President Samia Suluhu Hassan swore in Deogratius Ndejembi as Tanzania's new Vice President, emphasizing service and leadership.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan presided over the swearing-in ceremony of Deogratius Ndejembi as Tanzania's new Vice President in Dodoma. She urged him to view the position as a responsibility to serve all Tanzanians, rather than a personal privilege. The President cited Ndejembi's qualifications, leadership experience, discipline, professionalism, and youthful energy as key factors in his appointment.

The decision to appoint Ndejembi was supported by the Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM) Central Committee and National Executive Committee, who endorsed his qualities. President Hassan noted that the party's choice to elevate a young leader was part of a strategy to nurture the next generation of national leaders. She expressed confidence that Ndejembi's prior experience in public and private sectors would aid him in his new duties.

President Hassan highlighted the government's commitment to fulfilling the CCM election manifesto and its development agenda towards 2030, including a 25-year vision for economic and social progress. She stressed that achieving these goals requires unity, cooperation, stability, and good leadership, urging Ndejembi to work closely with other leaders and prioritize national interests.

Why it mattersThe appointment of a new Vice President affects the country's leadership and its ability to implement national development plans and fulfill election promises.

Morogoro Festival Draws Crowds, Boosts Local Economy

Thousands attended the National Culture Festival's "Nyama Choma Night" in Morogoro, stimulating economic activity through trade and creativity.

Morogoro residents turned out in large numbers for the "Nyama Choma Night" event, a highlight of the ongoing Fourth National Culture Festival. The event, held on August 28-29, 2026, provided entertainment and served as a significant opportunity to boost local economic activities.

The festival aims to foster unity and solidarity while stimulating economic engagement for citizens. It also seeks to promote Tanzania both domestically and internationally by showcasing its rich cultural heritage, arts, and tourism potential.

Participants from various parts of Tanzania gathered for the festivities, contributing to a vibrant atmosphere. The event facilitated economic growth through commerce, cultural exchange, and innovation, bringing together diverse communities.

Why it mattersThe "Nyama Choma Night" event and the broader National Culture Festival directly stimulate economic activity for citizens through trade, cultural exchange, and innovation.

Tanzania and DR Congo Agree to Repatriate Refugees

The two nations and UNHCR will begin a voluntary repatriation process for Congolese refugees in Tanzania.

Tanzania and the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), in conjunction with the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR), have agreed to initiate the process of returning Congolese refugees residing in Tanzania who are willing to go back to their home country. Deputy Minister of Home Affairs, Ayoub Mohammed Mahmoud, announced this decision at the Tripartite Commission Meeting in Lubumbashi. He stated that Tanzania will ensure the repatriation is voluntary, safe, and dignified, and will not pressure refugees to return.

For those not yet ready to return, Tanzania will continue to provide asylum and services at the Nyarugusu Refugee Camp in Kigoma, utilizing available resources. The voluntary repatriation process will not impede Tanzania's ability to accept new asylum seekers fleeing insecurity in parts of the DRC. The Deputy Minister urged DRC leaders to visit Nyarugusu Camp to engage directly with their citizens and address their needs, fostering a sense of belonging.

All parties have committed to creating an enabling environment for refugees who wish to return to reunite with their families and contribute to their nation's development. The DRC leaders' visits are intended to build relationships and hope, not to persuade refugees to return. This agreement marks a significant step in addressing the refugee situation and fostering regional cooperation.

Why it mattersCongolese refugees in Tanzania are directly affected by this agreement, which outlines a voluntary process for their return home.
