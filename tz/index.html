Tanzania invites Algerian firms to explore investment

Ambassador Mobhare Matinyi seeks partnerships in agriculture, energy, pharmaceuticals, construction, and manufacturing.

Tanzania is seeking to translate its friendship with Algeria into stronger commercial partnerships, inviting Algerian companies to explore investment opportunities across agriculture, energy, pharmaceuticals, construction, and manufacturing. Tanzania’s Ambassador to Algeria, Mobhare Matinyi, made the appeal during the Embassy’s monthly press briefing in Algiers on Monday. The initiative aims to inform the public about emerging opportunities for trade, investment, and technical collaboration.

Ambassador Matinyi highlighted potential collaboration in agriculture and agro-processing, noting Algeria's advances in food processing and export readiness. He engaged with Algerian companies involved in food processing, meat, and chicken production, encouraging investment in Tanzania. Opportunities were also presented in livestock technologies, animal health, feed production, fertilizers, irrigation, and food packaging.

The Embassy's engagements followed participation in the "Made in Algeria" Agri-Food Trade Fair on October 17, where Tanzania identified companies whose capabilities could support national priorities. Both countries occupy strategic positions, with Algeria linking North Africa and the Sahel, and Tanzania connecting East, Central, and Southern African markets representing over 500 million people.

Tanzania youth leader charged with counselling terrorism

CHADEMA official Coletha Clavery Ntidyicha faces charges of advising the public to commit terrorist acts.

Coletha Clavery Ntidyicha, 31, chairperson of CHADEMA’s youth wing BAVICHA in Kigoma Region, was charged with counselling terrorist acts at the Kisutu Resident Magistrate’s Court in Dar es Salaam. The charge sheet, dated November 3, 2026, alleges she advised the public to participate in acts causing serious property damage to destabilise the country’s structures between August 1 and October 23, 2026.

CHADEMA stated on November 4 that Ntidyicha was brought to court without her lawyers present, alleging denial of legal access. The charge sheet, under the Prevention of Terrorism Act, does not specify the words used, the communication method, or the property targeted. Ntidyicha was arrested on October 14 in Temeke, and her detention became the subject of a High Court application.

CHADEMA’s communications director, Brenda Rupia, said on November 4 that the party supports the rule of law but believes terrorism charges are used to silence opponents. A High Court application concerning Ntidyicha's whereabouts had previously overcome a government objection regarding a defective affidavit on October 28, allowing an additional affidavit to be considered.

Tanzania hosts African seed sector talks

Dar es Salaam will convene African Union states and agricultural stakeholders from November 10-13 to improve seed access and food security.

Tanzania will host a four-day meeting in Dar es Salaam from November 10-13 to discuss transforming Africa’s seed sector. The gathering will bring together African Union member states and agricultural stakeholders to improve access to quality planting materials and advance food security across the continent.

The event comprises the seventh Steering Group Meeting and third General Assembly of the African Seed and Biotechnology Partnership Platform (ASB PP), convened by the African Union Development Agency-NEPAD (AUDA-NEPAD) and the African Union Commission (AUC). Representatives from regional economic communities, research institutions, farmers’ organizations, private-sector actors, development partners, and civil society will attend.

Discussions will focus on strengthening the partnership platform's effectiveness, reviewing the implementation of the African Seed and Biotechnology Programme (ASBP), and identifying priority interventions. Key priorities include implementing the ASBP 10-year action plan, advancing the Farmer-Managed Seed Systems Framework, and harmonising regional seed policies.

Why it mattersTanzania will host a continental meeting aimed at improving seed systems and food security for African nations.

CAF delegation praises Tanzania's 2027 AFCON readiness

Inspectors noted progress on stadiums and infrastructure, with playing surfaces showing visible growth.

A Confederation of African Football (CAF) delegation, led by General Secretary Samson Adamu, expressed satisfaction with Tanzania’s progress in preparing to host the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. The inspectors visited infrastructure in Dar es Salaam, Zanzibar, and Arusha, acknowledging advancements toward meeting tournament requirements.

Leodegar Tenga, chairman of the AFCON Local Organising Committee, reported that nearly all commitments to CAF are met, with ongoing work on stadium enhancements, particularly the playing surfaces. Grass has been planted and is expected to grow visibly within two to three weeks, addressing a key area of focus.

Improvements to access roads near stadiums are also in progress, with the Tanzania National Roads Agency (TANROADS) involved. Hospitality facilities for dignitaries are being built, alongside considerations for ordinary fans, visiting families, and football tourists to ensure a positive experience and promote Tanzania's tourism.

Why it mattersTanzania is preparing to host the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations, impacting infrastructure development and the experience for players and fans.

Business · 2026-11-06

CRDB Bank enhances digital services

Customers can now self-register for internet banking and unlock accounts without visiting a branch.

CRDB Bank has upgraded its internet banking services in Dar es Salaam, introducing systems for faster, easier, and more secure transactions. Customers can now register for the service themselves, eliminating the need to visit a bank branch.

Bonaventure Paul, CRDB Bank's Director of Customer Service, stated that these improvements aim to remove challenges for internet banking users and boost efficiency. The service now operates 24 hours a day, seven days a week, allowing customers to access banking services anytime.

A key enhancement is the self-registration system for customers with a CRDB account linked to an ATM card. Additionally, customers can now unlock their internet banking accounts if they enter the wrong password multiple times, without needing to visit a branch for verification.

Why it mattersCRDB Bank customers in Tanzania can now access and manage their internet banking services more conveniently and securely.
