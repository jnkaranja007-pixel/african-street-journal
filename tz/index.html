Tanzania Post to judge regional managers on results

Regional managers and headquarters leaders will be assessed on business growth, resource use and service quality, with action for those who fall short.

Shirika la Posta Tanzania has set business results, efficient use of resources and service quality as the main measures of its leaders' performance. Managers of regional offices and headquarters staff will be assessed on the growth they achieve, and those who fail to carry out their duties will face accountability measures after performance reviews.

Postamasta Mkuu Macrice Mbodo said no leader will be outside these assessments and that leadership posts come with a duty to show measurable results. He made the remarks on November 12, 2026, in Dar es Salaam, while opening a two-day training for regional managers on results-based management and productivity. Assessments will take each area's conditions into account, with successful managers recognised and weaker areas analysed.

Mbodo cited Morogoro region, where a manager has set a target of shipping 5,000 parcels after strong demand for parcel delivery exposed a shortage of vehicles. He said Tanzania ranks 66th globally and fourth in Africa in the Universal Postal Union assessment, behind Egypt, Morocco and Mauritius, and that the organisation aims to become Africa's leading postal service.

Why it mattersRegional postal managers who miss their targets will face accountability measures, and Morogoro's 5,000-parcel goal depends on resources management has promised to provide.

TanTrade boosts local production for export

Tanzania Trade Development Authority aims to increase value addition and market access for domestic products.

The Tanzania Trade Development Authority (TanTrade) is working to translate industrial and SME policies into business opportunities by strengthening local production, adding value to domestic products, and expanding market access. TanTrade Director General Ephraim Mafuru stated yesterday that the authority prioritizes connecting production with markets to ensure industrial growth creates sustainable businesses and jobs. He cited cashew nuts as an example, emphasizing the need for domestic processing to retain economic benefits and employment within Tanzania.

Mafuru explained that TanTrade will assist businesses in turning increased domestic production into market opportunities through market research, trade information services, and capacity-building programs. The authority is also promoting the "Made in Tanzania" initiative to strengthen product identity and consumer confidence. These efforts align with the 2026–2031 Programme for Country Partnership, launched recently by Deputy Minister for Industry and Trade Dennis Londo.

Minister for Industry and Trade Judith Kapinga said the policies aim to enhance industrial capacity, increase value addition, and open wider market opportunities for local products. Industrial production is currently valued at approximately 6.2tri/- annually, with about 400,000 tonnes of manufactured products exported. The success of these initiatives depends on collaboration between government, the private sector, and entrepreneurs.

Why it mattersThe government's focus on strengthening local industries and adding value to domestic products aims to create more jobs and increase export earnings for Tanzania.

Samia demands fair carbon returns at COP31

Tanzania's president urged leaders in Antalya, Türkiye, to turn climate promises into investments for development.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan called for fair carbon-market returns and accessible climate financing at the COP31 conference in Antalya, Türkiye, on November 11. She stated that climate shocks cost Tanzania approximately two to three per cent of its gross domestic product annually. The president highlighted carbon market opportunities in transport, waste management, renewable energy, and forests, which cover 48.1 million hectares.

Hassan advocated for transparent carbon market mechanisms under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement to ensure fair pricing and benefit sharing. She stressed that developing countries need increased grants and concessional financing for adaptation, alongside technology transfer. Tanzania’s National Adaptation Plan for 2025–2035 estimates US$37 billion is needed for 36 programmes.

The president also called for affordable financing, private investment, and technology transfer to expand electricity access, particularly in rural areas, defining energy justice as equitable access to affordable, reliable, and sustainable energy. She linked climate-resilient development to Tanzania’s 25-year development vision, including equipping youth with skills for green industries.

Why it mattersTanzania faces annual economic losses of two to three percent of its GDP due to climate shocks, and the National Adaptation Plan requires US$37 billion.

Zanzibar spice farms create jobs for 73 youths

Spice tourism on the islands offers economic opportunities beyond traditional farming.

Zanzibar's spice farms are transforming agricultural heritage into employment for young people. Hassan Hamad, owner of Zito Spice Tour in Dole, Unguja, employs over 73 young individuals in roles ranging from cultivation to guiding visitors. His farm, inherited from his parents, now attracts international tourists interested in how spices like cloves and cinnamon are grown.

Spice tourism, which gained prominence around 1995, allows visitors to experience spice origins firsthand, moving beyond simple purchases. Kassim Malik Kassimu, a manager at Zito Spice Tour, notes that tourists are fascinated by seeing the plants and learning about cultivation and traditional uses. This familiarity gives local employees an advantage in serving visitors.

The farm offers employment and income to young people, helping them sustain their livelihoods while promoting the islands' historical identity. Beyond cloves, farms cultivate spices such as black pepper, ginger, cardamom, turmeric, and vanilla, diversifying the tourist experience and creating more opportunities.

Why it mattersYoung people in Zanzibar are finding employment and income through spice tourism on local farms.

Simba Queens and JKT win Women's Premier League matches

Simba Queens beat Mashujaa 2-0 while JKT edged Yanga Princess 1-0, and Bunda Queens' coach has called for better officiating.

Simba Queens beat Mashujaa 2-0 at Major General Isamuhyo Stadium in Dar es Salaam, with Vivian Corazone scoring in the 42nd minute and Zainah Nandede heading in a late second goal. At KMC Complex in Dar es Salaam, JKT beat Yanga Princess 1-0, Asha Mwalala scoring in the 36th minute. Kilimanjaro Wonders and Fountain Gate Princess drew 0-0 at Ushirika Stadium in Moshi.

Bunda Queens head coach Alley Ibrahim called on the Tanzania Football Federation and the Tanzania Premier League Board to address officiating problems in the Women's Premier League after his side drew 2-2 with Alliance Girls in Mwanza on Tuesday. He warned that poor refereeing could discourage sponsors from investing in the women's game.

Ibrahim asked football officials to visit stadiums regularly, as they do in the men's league, and said some people involved in women's football operate without proper structure. Alliance Girls head coach Emmanuel Kibona said defensive errors, particularly by his goalkeeper, cost his side in the first half.

Why it mattersSponsors and investors in the Women's Premier League are affected, as Ibrahim says poor officiating makes their financial contributions feel wasted.
