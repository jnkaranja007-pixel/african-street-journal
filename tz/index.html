Tanzania warns of El Niño rains

The Tanzania Meteorological Authority forecasts normal to above-normal rainfall from October to January.

Tanzanians should prepare for El Niño-influenced rains expected from October 2026 to January 2027, according to the Tanzania Meteorological Authority (TMA). The TMA forecasts normal to above-normal rainfall, with increased rain likely in November and continuing into January. This warning urges sensible preparation rather than panic, as El Niño does not guarantee disaster for every district.

The TMA advises citizens to heed location-specific updates and local authorities’ advice, avoiding rumours and alarming social media messages. People in flood-prone areas, river valleys, and low-lying settlements are urged to follow official instructions to move to safer ground before rising waters impede evacuation. Families should plan evacuation routes, secure essential documents and medicines, and assist vulnerable individuals.

Authorities must enforce environmental rules, particularly against sand extraction from riverbeds, which can weaken banks and worsen flooding. Communities are encouraged to clear drains without dumping rubbish into waterways, while councils should inspect bridges and communicate warnings promptly. Farmers are advised to follow local forecasts and extension advice to protect stored produce and prepare for potential waterlogging.

Why it mattersTanzanians face potential flooding and need to prepare for heavy rains and their consequences.

Tanzania Sisal Board partners for women's employment

The Tanzania Sisal Board and Mwanamke Initiative Foundation will collaborate to train women and youth in sisal product manufacturing.

The Tanzania Sisal Board (TSB) is planning to partner with the Mwanamke Initiative Foundation to create jobs for women, youth, and people with disabilities by expanding sisal value addition. This initiative aims to teach practical skills for making sisal-based items for local and tourist markets, moving beyond traditional rope uses. TSB Director Saddy Kambona stated the partnership would boost employment, entrepreneurship, and women's economic independence.

Ms Sabra Mohamed, Executive Director of Mwanamke Initiative Foundation, visited TSB in Tanga to discuss investment and skills development. She requested technical assistance from TSB trainers to teach sisal processing and product-making at their Bungi centre in Zanzibar, set to launch early next year. TSB will provide sisal fibres to training institutions and incubation centres, including those in Zanzibar, to support the production of marketable goods.

The collaboration could also explore using sisal waste for gas and charcoal production, contributing to cleaner cooking energy. Abdulatif Famau, Sisal Incubation Centre supervisor, noted that cooperation could improve product quality and market access by facilitating skills exchange between mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar.

Why it mattersWomen and young people in Tanzania will gain new employment and entrepreneurship opportunities through sisal product manufacturing.

Tanzania urges neighbours to act on Lake Tanganyika pollution

Permanent Secretary Dr Richard Muyungi called for concrete action beyond policy to save the lake's biodiversity and 13 million people.

Tanzania has called on neighbouring countries to take concrete action against pollution, illegal fishing, and climate change threatening Lake Tanganyika. Permanent Secretary Dr Richard Muyungi urged Tanzania, Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Zambia to strengthen cooperation and improve fisheries management during the 15th Ordinary Meeting of the Lake Tanganyika Authority (LTA) Management Committee in Dar es Salaam.

Dr Muyungi highlighted declining water quality and unsustainable practices as major challenges affecting the lake’s ecosystem and the livelihoods of over 13 million people. He stressed that effective conservation requires practical decisions and alignment with the region’s Strategic Action Programme, also calling for greater private-sector participation to mobilise resources.

The LTA Management Committee meeting aims to assess conservation progress and identify measures to address emerging challenges like unsustainable fishing and habitat degradation. Recommendations from this meeting will inform the Conference of Ministers scheduled for October 29 and 30, seeking to protect the lake's biodiversity and economic potential.

Why it mattersOver 13 million people in Tanzania and neighbouring countries depend on Lake Tanganyika for their livelihoods, which are threatened by pollution and climate change.

Tanzania avocado exports surpass 2030 target

Tanzania exported 52,000 tonnes of avocados in the 2025/2026 season, exceeding its 2030 goal.

Tanzania exported 52,000 tonnes of avocados in the 2025/2026 season, surpassing its 2030 target of 40,000 tonnes.

This achievement represents a 237 per cent increase from the 15,432.77 tonnes exported in the 2020/2021 season, earning 97.2 million US dollars compared to 44.2 million US dollars previously. Agriculture Minister Daniel Chongolo stated that this success was driven by collaboration between the government and private sector in strengthening production, markets, and infrastructure.

The Third National Avocado Stakeholders’ Forum in Dodoma heard that production grew by 36.24 per cent to 203,473 tonnes over the same period. The Cereal and Other Produce Regulatory Authority plans to operationalise crop collection and storage centres and formalise trade in the next season.

Why it mattersTanzanian farmers earned more foreign exchange and saw increased production of avocados.

Tanzania parliament committee satisfied with TBS building

The parliamentary committee on industry, trade, agriculture, and livestock visited the TBS Njedengwa facility in Dodoma and expressed satisfaction with the quality.

The Parliamentary Standing Committee for Industry, Trade, Agriculture, and Livestock has approved the quality of the Tanzania Bureau of Standards (TBS) building in Njedengwa, Dodoma. Committee Chairman Deodatus Mwanyika stated that the investment will increase efficiency in testing services and bring services closer to the people. The committee visited the building, inspected laboratories, and reviewed equipment for product testing and quality assurance.

Mwanyika explained the visit was part of the Parliament's oversight role on the government, specifically the Ministry of Industry and Trade, to monitor project implementation and budget execution. The committee assessed public fund usage, construction quality, purchased equipment, ongoing activities, and the presence of qualified professionals. They expressed satisfaction with the construction standards and laboratory equipment.

TBS Director General Dr. Ashura Katunzi thanked President Samia Suluhu Hassan and Minister Judith Kapinga for enabling the investment, which strengthens TBS's capacity to serve citizens more effectively. She emphasized that the new Dodoma facility and laboratories are crucial for bringing professional services closer to customers, reducing service access challenges, and improving product quality testing efficiency. TBS will continue to enhance services to support industrial and trade growth, ensuring products meet required standards for consumer health and safety.

Why it mattersThe parliamentary committee's approval of the TBS Njedengwa facility investment will improve product testing efficiency and safety for Tanzanian consumers.
