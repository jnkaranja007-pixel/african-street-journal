CCM Expels Ex-Vice President Nchimbi After Resignation

Tanzania's ruling party, CCM, announced the expulsion of former Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi for violating party rules, a day after his resignation.

Tanzania's ruling party, Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM), has expelled former Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi following his resignation. Party General Secretary Asha Rose Migiro stated on August 26, 2026, that Nchimbi's expulsion was due to conduct violating party rules and regulations, a decision unanimously passed by the party's National Executive Committee. The expulsion follows Nchimbi's resignation, announced via a personal account, just before parliament's scheduled session.

The party's Central Committee and National Executive Committee deliberated on Nchimbi's actions, concluding that he had violated the party's constitution, rules, and regulations. Migiro confirmed that Nchimbi is no longer a member of CCM, effective immediately. This move provides context for the timing of Nchimbi's resignation, which occurred a day before parliament could potentially initiate impeachment proceedings against him.

Speculation has surrounded a falling-out between Nchimbi and President Samia, with disagreements over political direction and the 2030 presidential succession cited as potential causes. Nchimbi, known for advocating reform, pushing back against political violence, and calling for a new constitution, was seen by some as a figure aiming to bridge divides within the party amidst increasing security apparatus involvement in politics.

Why it mattersFormer Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi has been expelled from Tanzania's ruling CCM party for violating its rules, impacting his political standing and potentially his future role.

Lissu Cross-Examined on Speech Publication in Treason Trial

The prosecution pressed CHADEMA chairperson Tundu Lissu on remarks made in an April 3, 2025 speech and whether he controlled its online publication, which he denied.

The prosecution began cross-examining CHADEMA chairperson Tundu Lissu on Wednesday in the High Court treason trial, focusing on his April 3, 2025 speech and its online publication. Lissu acknowledged delivering the speech to CHADEMA aspirants and wanting it to reach the public, but he rejected the claim that he controlled the Jambo TV recording's release. He stated a station employee, identified as PW17, made the decision to stream and publish the footage.

The questioning followed a two-day pause in proceedings, including a public holiday for Maulid. Lissu had previously presented his defence, detailing his political activism and arguments for election reform. State Attorney Nassoro Katuga led the prosecution's questioning, establishing Lissu's legal background and CHADEMA chairmanship. The exchange became pointed when Lissu confirmed he had not produced documentary proof for awards he mentioned, stating he had no obligation unless the prosecution challenged his account.

Lissu did not deny making the words attributed to him, including references to "kukinukisha," mobilising rebellion, and stopping the election. His defence maintained that these phrases were taken out of context from a longer speech. The prosecution tested Lissu's stance that citizens can lawfully oppose election laws and government decisions through public mobilisation, a key point in his defence.

Why it mattersTundu Lissu's defence in a treason trial, focusing on his right to protest and the context of his speech, directly affects his personal liberty and political standing.

African Diaspora Institute Explores Tanzania's Heritage

A delegation from the African Diaspora Institute visited the National Museum of Tanzania in Dar es Salaam to learn about the country's history and culture.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and East African Cooperation organized a special visit for a delegation from the African Diaspora Institute (ADDI) to the National Museum of Tanzania in Dar es Salaam on August 26, 2026. The visit aimed to provide guests with an opportunity to learn about Tanzania's history, culture, and heritage, while also strengthening cultural diplomacy.

The delegation was led by ADDI Founder and President, Ambassador Arikana Chihombori-Quao from Zimbabwe, who urged Africans to be united and maintain peace, emphasizing the importance of unity in preserving and developing Africa's heritage. The ADDI's visit is part of ongoing efforts to promote cultural diplomacy and showcase Tanzania through its history and heritage to visitors from within and outside the country.

Dr. Aloyce Mwambwiga, a Lead Curator at the National Museum and House of Culture, encouraged citizens and foreign visitors to visit the National Museum to learn more about Tanzania's history, culture, and heritage. The museum serves as a key site for understanding the nation's past and cultural identity.

Why it mattersThe visit by the African Diaspora Institute to the National Museum of Tanzania offers a platform for cultural exchange and learning about Tanzanian heritage for diaspora members.

Tanzania Energy Ministry Pushes Gas Production Deadline Amid Dispute

Officials met with APT and Ndovu Resources to streamline operations for a December 2026 production target, following a dispute over development plans.

Tanzanian energy officials met with ARA Petroleum Tanzania (APT) and Ndovu Resources on August 26, 2026, to address delays in the Ntorya Gas Development project. The Ministry of Energy aims to streamline operations to meet a revised production deadline of December 2026. This meeting follows a notice of dispute filed by Ndovu Resources on August 21, 2026, alleging APT breached obligations under their agreements by proposing changes to the 2026 Work Programme and Budget.

Ndovu Resources claims APT has repeatedly sought to alter the approved plan, which includes drilling the CH-1 well, and has not fulfilled its commitment to the original work program. The notice states that APT's actions have led to significant delays, limited expenditure, and minimal progress on the project in 2026. The dispute resolution period is 90 days, after which Ndovu may refer the matter to the London Court of International Arbitration.

The Ntorya gas field, located in southern Tanzania's Mtwara region, is estimated to hold 1.6 trillion cubic feet of natural gas. APT operates the project with a 75 percent stake, while Ndovu holds 25 percent. The Tanzania Petroleum Development Corporation is in discussions to acquire a 15 percent share. The production deadline was initially set for September 2026 but has been extended to December 2026.

Why it mattersInvestors face potential delays and arbitration in the Ntorya gas project due to a dispute between the operating companies over development plans.
