Kenyan court orders police to produce missing Tanzanian activist

Justice Alexander Muasya Muteti ordered police to present Twaha Mwaipaya in court on November 2, 2026, or explain his detention.

Kenya’s High Court ordered police on October 30, 2026, to produce Tanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya before the Milimani High Court on November 2, 2026. The court also demanded any detention warrant and an explanation for his custody. Justice Alexander Muasya Muteti issued the directive following an urgent application by the human-rights organisation Vocal Africa, which is suing on Mwaipaya's behalf. Mwaipaya was reportedly taken in the Kamulu area on October 25.

The court order directs the Inspector General of Police, the Directorate of Criminal Investigations, and the Officer Commanding Ruai Police Station to present Mwaipaya "live or dead." They must show cause why he should not be released. Justice Muteti summoned Inspector General Douglas Kanja Kirocho and DCI Director Amin Mohamed Ibrahim to explain the alleged arrest and detention. Responses from the respondents are due by 9 a.m. on November 2, before the 11 a.m. mention.

This habeas corpus direction does not determine whether police took or are holding Mwaipaya, or if the reported abduction occurred. Vocal Africa's application also sought preservation of CCTV, ANPR, toll, mobile-phone, border, and vehicle-tracking records, alongside an independent investigation. However, Friday’s court order focuses solely on Mwaipaya's production and the upcoming hearing.

Why it mattersTanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya is missing after a reported abduction in Kenya, and Kenyan authorities have been ordered by the High Court to account for his whereabouts.

Tanzania opens government city in Dodoma

The new administrative capital in Mtumba now houses ministries and includes electric vehicle charging stations.

Tanzania officially opened new government office buildings and road infrastructure in its new capital city in Mtumba, Dodoma on October 30, 2026. The move aims to strengthen public administration and consolidate government operations.

Minister of State Kitila Mkumbo stated the project marks a milestone in establishing Dodoma as the administrative capital, building on efforts from previous administrations. The Government City incorporates smart-city features, including electric vehicle charging and natural gas refuelling stations, to promote cleaner energy use.

Tanzania has invested Sh700 billion from domestic resources in the construction of the Government City. The relocation process to Dodoma began in December 2017 and was completed in October 2019.

Why it mattersTanzanians will see government operations consolidated in the new capital city of Dodoma.

Tanzania, Angola sign four deals to boost trade

Leaders aim to turn 50 years of solidarity into economic ties with agreements on air services and technology.

Tanzanian President Samia Suluhu Hassan and Angolan President João Manuel Lourenço signed four cooperation agreements in Dar es Salaam on Thursday, October 29. The deals aim to convert 50 years of solidarity into practical economic links, with agreements on the transfer of sentenced persons, air services, and memoranda on information and communications technology and meteorology.

President Lourenço stated that economic cooperation had seen little progress in 50 years and that the visit sought to correct this. This was the first Angolan presidential state visit to Tanzania since 1979. President Hassan announced Tanzania would reopen its embassy in Angola and appoint an ambassador, noting that annual two-way trade averaged US$2 million to US$3.5 million.

The leaders agreed to organize a trade and investment forum to identify business opportunities. A new air-services agreement provides a framework for flights, aiming for a direct Dar es Salaam–Luanda service to support commerce and tourism. Discussions also included a long-term rail and maritime connection between Angola's Lobito port and Tanzania's Dar es Salaam port.

Why it mattersThe agreements aim to boost economic cooperation and trade between Tanzania and Angola, creating new opportunities for businesses and travelers.

Tanzania permanent secretary urges reduced sugar, salt, and fat intake

Dr Grace Magembe advised citizens to eat natural foods and exercise regularly to prevent non-communicable diseases.

Permanent Secretary of the Ministry of Health, Dr Grace Magembe, urged Tanzanians to reduce their consumption of sugar, salt, and fat today in Dodoma. She spoke during National Nutrition Day 2026, which focused on strengthening health through natural eating and reducing unhealthy ingredients. Dr Magembe stated that Tanzania has abundant natural foods like grains, roots, and fruits that are vital for health.

Dr Magembe identified poor food choices, not a lack of food, as the main health challenge in Tanzania. She encouraged prioritizing natural, nutritious options over processed foods high in sugar, fat, and salt. The permanent secretary also emphasized the importance of regular physical activity to prevent weight gain and non-communicable diseases, warning against sedentary lifestyles.

Parents and guardians were urged to set good examples for children by choosing healthy foods and lifestyles, as children learn eating habits from adults. Dr Magembe specifically advised reducing sweets, soda, and ice cream to protect children's health from an early age. She highlighted that improving nutrition requires behavioral changes and collaboration among families, health professionals, and stakeholders.

Why it mattersTanzanian citizens are directly affected by health advice to reduce consumption of sugar, salt, and fat to prevent non-communicable diseases.

Tanzania marks one year since deadly election violence

Thousands killed in protests suppressed by security forces, with victims still traumatised and critics facing charges.

One year after Tanzania's October 29 election, many citizens remain traumatised by violence that erupted when security forces suppressed protests. President Samia Suluhu Hassan won the election with 98 percent of the vote after most opposition parties were excluded. A government commission reported 518 deaths, while rights groups and the opposition claim thousands were killed.

Twaha Bomeza, 61, lost four front teeth and suffered an eye injury from shrapnel during a shootout in Dar es Salaam, requiring surgery he cannot afford. The Catholic Church held masses to honour victims, and the main opposition party, Chadema, declared a seven-day mourning period, stating Tanzania has become an authoritarian state. The streets of Dar es Salaam were heavily policed.

Opposition leader Tundu Lissu faces a treason trial verdict on November 6, having begun a hunger strike for lawyer access. Another opposition official, John Pambalu, was recently charged with treason, and dissident Twaha Mwaipaya was abducted in Kenya and is missing. Analysts describe Tanzania as tense with rising arbitrary arrests.

Why it mattersCitizens are traumatised by election violence, face arbitrary arrests, and live in fear of abduction and treason charges.
