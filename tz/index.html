Tanzania Digital Health Push Adds AI, Hospital Tech to Procurement

The nation's healthcare sector is embracing digital transformation, with electronic records and artificial intelligence shaping service delivery and creating new market.

Tanzania's healthcare sector is entering a new phase of digital transformation, integrating electronic medical records, interoperable health information systems, and artificial intelligence. This shift is creating new opportunities for technology providers, distributors, and investors as the country aims to strengthen data use and digital tools across its health system. The Ministry of Health is advancing digital health through initiatives focused on interoperability and the digitalization of primary healthcare, supporting systems that improve data access for clinical and management decisions.

Electronic medical record coverage has reached 100% in national and referral hospitals, with significant adoption expanding across district facilities. Healthcare providers are increasingly seeking technologies to improve diagnosis, patient management, and operational efficiency, with AI applications for disease surveillance and diagnostics being explored. The Afya-Tek digital system, launched in December 2024, aims to strengthen community-level healthcare services.

The World Health Expo (WHX) 2026 in Nairobi will convene healthcare technology providers, hospital decision-makers, and investors as Tanzanian organizations evaluate technologies based on practical outcomes. Interoperability remains a critical consideration, with programs emphasizing secure data exchange across health facilities to enhance patient history access, hospital management, and clinical support.

Why it mattersInvestors and technology companies are presented with new opportunities as Tanzania's healthcare sector adopts digital tools, electronic medical records, and artificial intelligence for improved service delivery and operational efficiency.

Tanzania Regulator Slashes Fuel Prices for September

The Energy and Water Utilities Regulatory Authority announced a significant drop in petrol and diesel costs, effective September 2.

The Energy and Water Utilities Regulatory Authority (EWURA) has reduced the prices of petroleum products for September 2026. Petrol prices have fallen by 102/- per litre, and diesel by 101/- per litre. These new prices, effective from September 2, 2026, apply to fuel dealers operating under Tanzania’s regulated indicative pricing system.

EWURA stated it is closely monitoring global oil market developments, which are influenced by factors such as the security situation in the Middle East. Under the Petroleum Act of 2015, fuel prices in Tanzania are determined by a market-based system, but retail dealers must not exceed EWURA's set indicative prices.

The regulatory body has also called on the public to assist in monitoring compliance by reporting any fuel stations found selling petroleum products above the prescribed indicative prices. This move aims to ensure fair pricing for consumers across the country.

Why it mattersConsumers and businesses in Tanzania will benefit from lower transportation costs due to the reduction in fuel prices.

Arusha Police Detain Activist; Accounts of Detention Differ

Human rights activist Godlisten Malisa was detained at the Namanga border post on August 31, with his account of events contradicting the police's official statement.

Arusha police confirmed on Tuesday the detention of activist Godlisten Elisante Malisa at the Namanga border post on August 31. Police stated Malisa and another unidentified individual were arrested on "criminal allegations under investigation" and later released on bail. This official account follows hours of uncertainty after Malisa's lawyer and a friend reported him missing from the border crossing.

Malisa, however, stated he was not bailed out and did not post bail, asserting he was not investigated for any criminal charges. He reported spending the night in a holding cell at the Diplomacy and Tourism Police Station in Arusha after being taken from the border by five individuals identifying as police officers. He claimed they initially acted courteously and he was not mistreated.

The activist recounted arriving at Namanga after his flight from the United States was rerouted due to industrial action in Kenya. He stated Tanzanian officials could not read his passport, leading to a delay. His lawyer reported he was removed by individuals not in uniform. Malisa's belongings were returned when he left the station on September 1.

Why it mattersHuman rights activist Godlisten Malisa was detained and his account of the events conflicts with the police's official statement regarding criminal allegations and bail.

MP Questions Government Plan to Elevate Driving Profession

A Member of Parliament has asked for the government's strategy to ensure the driving profession gains dignity and respect equal to other careers.

Dodoma: Rashid Shangazi, the Member of Parliament for Mlalo, questioned in parliament the government's strategy to ensure the driving profession achieves dignity, status, and respect comparable to other careers. He sought to understand the measures in place to elevate the profession.

Responding to the inquiry, Regina Qwaray, the Deputy Minister in the President's Office for Public Service Management and Good Governance, stated that the government has taken various steps to ensure the driving profession is accorded the same dignity and respect as other careers. She affirmed the government's commitment to this goal.

The Deputy Minister detailed that the government is improving the career development structure for drivers, enhancing professional training and educational standards, and strengthening incentives and welfare for the driving cadre. These actions aim to meet and manage the demands of the working environment for drivers.

Why it mattersDrivers are affected by government plans to improve their professional training, welfare, and career development structures.
