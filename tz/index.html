CHRAGG calls for dialogue after Lissu conviction

The Commission for Human Rights and Good Governance says disputes over the ruling belong in lawful channels, not the street.

Four years in prison: that is the sentence the High Court in Dar es Salaam handed Tundu Lissu, chairman of the opposition party CHADEMA, after it acquitted him of treason on November 6 but convicted him of incitement. The Commission for Human Rights and Good Governance (CHRAGG) has now urged respect for judicial independence and constructive dialogue over the case.

In a position statement issued in Dodoma on November 13, the commission said disagreements over the judgment should be pursued through the constitution and through lawful, democratic channels. It said it had no power to review or overturn the court's findings. Its records showed Lissu's arrest on April 9, 2025, his appearance before the Kisutu Resident Magistrates' Court the next day, and his committal and trial all followed the criminal procedure framework.

CHRAGG cautioned that following procedure is not the same as meeting every human rights duty, and pointed to access to a lawyer and time to prepare a defence. An inquiry it opened on June 27, 2025 found Lissu had freedom of worship, privacy, contact with his legal team and exercise while in custody. The commission added that peaceful calls for constitutional and electoral reform must be kept separate from criminal conduct.

Why it mattersLissu, who has been sentenced to four years, and his party now face a dispute over the ruling that the commission says must be settled through the courts and the constitution.

Tanzania reports clean-cooking gains

President Samia Suluhu Hassan said Tanzania expects clean-cooking access to reach 35 per cent by the end of 2026.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan reported an expansion in clean-cooking access, stating Tanzania expects it to reach 35 per cent by the end of 2026. Leaders at COP31 urged that international pledges be measured by improvements in households’ lives. Discussions reviewed progress since the 2024 Paris clean-cooking summit, which mobilised US$2.2 billion in commitments, with approximately US$740 million deployed.

President Samia said access in Tanzania increased from 16 per cent in 2022 to 28.6 per cent in March 2024, attributing progress to rural electrification, reduced electricity tariffs, and subsidies for liquefied petroleum gas equipment. By March 2026, the government had distributed over 346,000 subsidised LPG cylinders and 49,000 induction stoves. The Cook Fund programme enabled over 413,000 households, nearly two million people, to gain access to clean-cooking solutions.

The Cook Fund programme supported hundreds of local businesses and 45 public institutions, including schools serving more than 62,000 students. Interventions contributed to an estimated reduction of 3.8 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent emissions. President Samia pledged to strengthen the national clean-cooking strategy through appropriate technologies, better distribution networks, and greater private investment, particularly in rural areas.

Tanzania's Works ministry orders better data for construction projects

The ministry wants its agencies to improve how they collect and analyse data to track public spending and project results.

The Ministry of Works has told directors and monitoring and evaluation managers in its supervised institutions to improve data collection and analysis. The call was made by Veila Shoo, director of the ministry's Monitoring and Evaluation Unit, when she closed a first-quarter meeting in Dodoma.

Shoo said accurate data lets the government make informed decisions, monitor public funds and assess outcomes in the construction sector. "We cannot achieve Vision 2050 without accurate data," she said, adding that every shilling spent on construction must be monitored. The meeting brought together the Tanzania National Roads Agency (TANROADS), the Tanzania Buildings Agency (TBA), the Engineers Registration Board (ERB) and the Contractors Registration Board (CRB), among others.

Participants discussed the sector's role in the National Development Vision 2050, the management of national plans and the state of monitoring and evaluation. They were also urged to prioritise strategic infrastructure projects, including roads and modern buildings, in line with long-term development goals.

Why it mattersAgencies that build roads and buildings must now show how their spending is tracked, with the data they report feeding national planning.

Agriculture · 2026-11-14

Africa experts push digital tech against fake seeds

African agricultural experts met in Dar es Salaam to discuss using digital technology and biotechnology to improve seed quality and food security.

African agricultural experts are advocating for increased use of digital technology to combat counterfeit seeds, which threaten farmers' productivity and food security. This proposal emerged from the Seventh Steering Group Meeting of the African Seed and Biotechnology Partnership Platform, organised by AUDA-NEPAD and held in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, on November 12, 2026.

Dr James Onsando, an adviser on seed sector issues, stated that digital authentication systems could help farmers identify genuine seeds from fake ones, often sold in imitative packaging. He explained that concealed verification codes on seed packages would allow farmers to confirm authenticity, protecting them from financial losses due to poor germination and harvests from counterfeit seeds.

Delegates also explored biotechnology's role in developing higher-yielding seed varieties resilient to climate change, pests, and diseases. However, expanding access requires investment in research, supportive policies, and regulatory frameworks. These discussions align with the Comprehensive Africa Agriculture Development Programme's 2026–2035 agenda, which aims to significantly increase agricultural production and investment across the continent.

Why it mattersFarmers in Tanzania and across Africa face financial losses from counterfeit seeds, and digital authentication systems are being proposed to help them identify genuine products.
