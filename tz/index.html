Tanzania and Angola sign four cooperation deals

Leaders aim to transform 50 years of solidarity into practical economic ties with new agreements.

Tanzanian President Samia Suluhu Hassan and Angolan President João Manuel Lourenço signed four cooperation agreements in Dar es Salaam on Thursday, October 29. The deals aim to convert 50 years of solidarity into tangible economic links, addressing the slow progress in trade since formal relations began.

Agreements covered the transfer of sentenced persons and air services, with new memoranda on information and communications technology and meteorology. President Lourenço stated that African nations need stronger intra-continental ties to achieve development goals, noting that economic and trade relations between Tanzania and Angola remain very small.

Tanzania will reopen its embassy in Angola and appoint an ambassador to boost bilateral relations. The leaders agreed to organize a trade and investment forum to identify business opportunities, following President Samia's 2025 visit to Angola which also sought to reset economic cooperation.

Why it mattersTanzania and Angola signed four cooperation instruments to boost economic ties and trade between the two nations.

Tanzania joins 47 African nations in Moscow summit

Prime Minister Mwigulu Nchemba represented Tanzania at the Third Summit of Heads of State and Government discussing economic cooperation and security.

Tanzania joined 47 other African nations in Moscow, Russia, for the Third Summit of Heads of State and Government. Prime Minister Dr Mwigulu Nchemba attended the opening ceremony, which Russian President Vladimir Putin officially opened on Wednesday. The summit focused on economic cooperation, security, science, technology, and social issues, with 22 heads of state present.

African leaders, including President Nchemba representing Tanzania, pressed Russia for investments that create jobs and process local resources. Burundi President Évariste Ndayishimiye, the African Union chairperson, stated that Africa sought to strengthen partner ties and wanted partnerships that turn resources into domestic production and higher-value exports. President Putin highlighted Russia's digital payment infrastructure as a tool to expand cross-border services.

The summit, themed 'Platform for Independent Development: Russia’s Advanced Solutions in Key Economic and Social Sectors', showcased Russia's capabilities in industry, agriculture, energy, transport, finance, digital technologies, healthcare, and education. Russia-Africa trade reportedly exceeded US$27 billion in 2025, and Russia supplied over 15 million tonnes of grain to the continent last year.

Why it mattersTanzania's Prime Minister attended a summit focused on economic cooperation and security with Russia, impacting the nation's international relations and potential for investment.

Tanzania marks one year since deadly election unrest

Official figures state 518 people died and over 2,000 were injured in the October 29, 2025 events.

One year after the October 29, 2025 election day violence, stakeholders gathered on October 29, 2026, to assess the nation's progress. Official figures report at least 518 people died and over 2,000 were injured during the events.

Human rights advocate Dr. Helen Kijo-Bisimba, civil society executive Lutgard Kokulinda Kagaruki, lawyer Jebra Kambole, and others participated in the Kivulini Talks session. The discussion, moderated by Khalifa Said, focused on what is known and unknown about the unrest, and the steps taken since.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan established two commissions following the events. While official figures cite around 500 deaths, unofficial reports suggest thousands were killed and injured, with significant property destruction.

Why it mattersTanzanians are affected by the violence that resulted in hundreds of deaths and injuries following the October 29, 2025 election.

Tanzania plans to cut state funding for public firms

A 25-year roadmap aims to make public corporations financially self-reliant, reducing dependence on government subsidies.

Tanzania is launching a 25-year plan to make its public institutions financially independent. The Office of the Treasury Registrar (OTR) will coordinate reforms to decrease reliance on government subsidies for public and statutory corporations (PSCs). Commercial entities are expected to fund their own operations, while those providing essential services will receive appropriate support.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan has urged public institutions to improve performance and reduce their need for routine government support, particularly those receiving subsidies without delivering results. Chief Secretary Ambassador Dr Moses Kusiluka stressed the need for better revenue collection and expenditure control. An analysis of 227 PSCs shows that government subventions are the primary financing source for most, with Treasury support increasing by an average of about 15 per cent annually over three financial years.

Nearly 82 per cent of these institutions depend on government financing, with fewer than one in five operating on a self-sustaining basis. The reform framework distinguishes between recurrent and development funding, acknowledging that investments in infrastructure like water treatment plants or transport may not generate immediate revenue. The goal is to encourage stronger management and cost efficiency.

Why it mattersPublic institutions in Tanzania will need to improve their financial management and revenue generation to reduce dependence on government funding.

Tanzania accused of using laws to silence critics

Families of those killed in election violence demand justice and medical aid, while rights groups cite abuse of counterterrorism laws.

Families of at least 500 people killed in election-related violence a year ago are demanding justice and help with medical bills. Neema Munuo, whose 18-year-old daughter Maria Chami was shot dead on election day, wants the government to acknowledge her suffering. Meshack Sane, who was shot in the leg, told the BBC that families can no longer afford hospital expenses.

On the anniversary of President Samia Suluhu Hassan's election with 98% of the vote, Amnesty International accused her government of weaponising counterterrorism laws to silence critics and instill fear. The opposition dismissed the election result as a mockery of democracy, as two main challengers were excluded.

Tundu Lissu, the 58-year-old leader of the main opposition Chadema party, was put on trial for treason before the poll, preventing him from standing. A verdict in his case is due next week. Lissu had been demanding electoral reforms and recently went on a hunger strike, alleging denial of access to lawyers, which prison authorities denied.

Why it mattersFamilies of victims of election violence and opposition figures are seeking justice and reforms in Tanzania.
