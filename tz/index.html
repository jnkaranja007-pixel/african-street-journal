CHRAGG calls for dialogue over Lissu conviction

The human rights commission says it found the proceedings compliant with procedure but stresses ongoing rights protections.

Following the conviction and sentencing of CHADEMA national chairman Tundu Lissu, the Commission for Human Rights and Good Governance (CHRAGG) has urged respect for judicial independence and dialogue. In a position statement issued in Dodoma on November 13, 2026, it said disagreements over the case should be pursued through lawful and democratic channels. Lissu was acquitted of treason by the High Court in Dar es Salaam on November 6, but convicted of incitement and jailed for four years.

The commission said its review focused on rights and governance rather than the merits of the judgment, and it has no power to review the court's findings. It found Lissu's arrest on April 9, 2025, his court presentation, committal and trial followed the applicable procedure. An inquiry on June 27, 2025, reported that he had freedom of worship, privacy, contact with his lawyers and time for exercise in custody.

CHRAGG cautioned that procedure alone does not establish that every human rights obligation was met, pointing to legal representation, time to prepare a defence and the right to examine witnesses. It also said peaceful advocacy for constitutional and electoral reform must be distinguished from criminal conduct, and that authorities must keep protecting personal liberty against arbitrary detention.

Why it mattersLissu's supporters and rights advocates now have an official government body on record urging that disputes over the verdict go through the courts and political process.

Tanzania banks partner on carbon project finance

CRDB Bank and NCMC sign deal to boost green investment and economic growth.

Tanzania is seeking domestic financing for carbon projects to boost green investment and economic growth. Minister of State Hamad Yussuf Masauni urged CRDB Bank and the National Carbon Monitoring Centre (NCMC) to collaborate on financing, developing, and promoting these projects. This call came on November 13, 2026, during the signing of a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between NCMC and CRDB Bank in Antalya, Türkiye, on the sidelines of the UN Climate Change Conference (COP31).

Minister Masauni stated that climate change requires innovative financing and strategic partnerships, identifying carbon trading as a key area for economic transformation in the National Development Vision 2050. He emphasized that carbon trading should deliver tangible economic, social, and environmental benefits, including improved livelihoods and job creation. Masauni also called on CRDB Bank to develop financial products like loans and guarantees to help carbon project owners access capital.

NCMC Executive Director Kathryn Kigaraba believes the partnership will allow carbon projects to access local financing, strengthening the country's capacity for carbon trading. CRDB Chief Executive Officer Abdulmajid Nsekela added that the agreement will attract capital and facilitate projects with economic and social benefits. Permanent Secretary Richard Muyungi noted the partnership supports Development Vision 2050 by using carbon trading to attract investment and stimulate productive activities.

Why it mattersCRDB Bank and NCMC will collaborate on financing carbon projects, aiming to attract investment and stimulate productive activities according to the National Development Vision 2050.

Tanzania calls on banks to back young agribusiness founders

40 young entrepreneurs from Dodoma and Singida shared 40m/- in grants under the second phase of the Generation Food Accelerator.

Tanzania's government wants banks, local councils and private firms to do more for young people running farm businesses. Deputy Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Agriculture, Eng Athumani Kilundumya, made the appeal at the close of the programme's second phase in Dodoma, where he said the sector can create jobs, raise incomes and speed up economic growth.

40 entrepreneurs from Dodoma and Singida received the grants, worth 40m/- in total, under the Generation Food Accelerator (GFA), run through the Vijana Kilimo Biashara project. Kilundumya said young founders need capital, skills, technology, business advice and market access, and that opportunities stretch across seed production, machinery hire, input supply, produce collection, storage and processing, not just planting.

The GFA is run by Rikolto with the World Food Programme (WFP) and funded by the Mastercard Foundation, and it targets people aged 18 to 35 working in sorghum, sunflower, vegetable and fruit supply chains. Of 731 applications, 100 entrepreneurs were picked, and women make up 53 per cent of them. WFP's Isaac Mathe said 38 businesses have registered, licensed and tax-compliant, and 43 jobs have been created.

Why it mattersThe 40 grant recipients and 38 newly formalised businesses now face the task of turning small grants into lasting enterprises, with banks urged to make loans easier to obtain.
