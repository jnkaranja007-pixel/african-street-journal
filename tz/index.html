El Niño rains threaten Tanzania's vulnerable populations

Children, pregnant women, the elderly, and disabled face heightened risks as heavy rains are forecast until January.

Heavy rains linked to the El Niño weather phenomenon are expected to cause flooding in Tanzania, posing significant risks to vulnerable groups including children, pregnant women, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. The Tanzania Meteorological Authority (TMA) forecasts normal to above-normal rainfall until January next year, warning of potential hazards like severe flooding.

Beyond property damage, flooding can endanger lives by limiting escape routes and access to essential services. For children, disrupted education and health threats from contaminated water are major concerns. The United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) warns that this El Niño could be severe, affecting over 347 million children globally, with an estimated 41 million in Eastern and Southern Africa facing heavy rainfall.

UNICEF Executive Director Catherine Russell stated that El Niño could lead to displacement, malnutrition, water shortages, and disease outbreaks, impacting children's lives, health, and education. Climate-related disasters have displaced over 20,000 children daily between 2016 and 2023, and disrupted education for 171 million pupils worldwide in 2025, prompting UNICEF to call for early action and investment in resilient systems.

Why it mattersVulnerable populations in Tanzania, including children, pregnant women, the elderly, and persons with disabilities, face increased risks of life-threatening situations, health issues, and disrupted education due to anticipated El Niño-driven floods.

Lissu files appeal against incitement conviction and four-year sentence

CHADEMA chairperson Tundu Lissu challenges the High Court ruling that replaced his treason charge with incitement, after prosecutors failed to prove treason.

CHADEMA national chairperson Tundu Lissu has filed a notice of appeal against his incitement conviction and four-year prison sentence handed down by the High Court on November 6. The notice, dated November 7, bears a Court of Appeal registry stamp showing it was filed on November 9. It states that Lissu intends to attend the appeal hearing, though no hearing date has been set.

The High Court replaced a treason charge with incitement on its own initiative, a finding Lissu disputes. The charge stems from an April 3, 2025 address in Dar es Salaam during CHADEMA's No Reforms, No Election campaign, where Lissu says his remarks sought constitutional and electoral reforms. The three-judge panel, led by Judge Dunstan Ndunguru, found prosecutors had not proven the intent required for treason but convicted him under section 390 of the Penal Code.

The court acknowledged Lissu was a first offender with diabetes who had spent 577 days on remand, but chose imprisonment over a fine, citing statements it said encouraged election disruption. Lissu requested bail pending appeal, including to attend his elder brother Alute Mughwai Lissu's funeral, and no bail was announced. The notice does not overturn the conviction or suspend the sentence.

Why it mattersLissu remains imprisoned under the four-year sentence while his appeal is pending, and no bail has been granted.

Bus and lorry crash kills 21 in Iringa

Police blame a bus driver who pulled onto the main road from a smaller road without caution, killing 21 people, including two boys.

21 people died and five were hurt when a Fuso passenger bus hit a Scania lorry at Kiponzelo junction in Iringa Region on November 9, 2026. The bus was on its way from Wasa to Iringa town, while the lorry was heading from Iringa towards Congo. The dead included 10 men, nine women and two boys aged three to five.

Iringa Regional Police Commander Alan Bukumbi said the driver pulled out from a smaller road onto the main road without taking care, and that the lorry was already on the main road when the crash happened. The bus driver, named only as Amos, was among the dead. Injured people are being treated at Iringa Regional Referral Hospital and two other local health facilities, and the bodies are held at the referral hospital.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan sent condolences on November 9 to Iringa Regional Commissioner Kheri James and the bereaved families. Police urged drivers and other road users to obey traffic laws and safety rules. The crash came a month after two Morogoro collisions that killed 32 people in October.

Why it mattersThe families of the 21 people killed and the five injured are the people directly affected, and the victims include two young boys.

Tanzania balances coastal development with environment

The Mwalimu Julius Nyerere Bridge connects Kigamboni and Dar es Salaam, supporting economic growth.

Tanzania is investing in coastal infrastructure like the Kigamboni Bridge in Dar es Salaam to link economic development with environmental protection as it pursues its climate and blue economy goals before COP31 in Türkiye.

The 680-metre Mwalimu Julius Nyerere Bridge, opened on April 19, 2016, by the late President John Magufuli, connects Kurasini and Kigamboni across Kurasini Creek, replacing a ferry service. The National Social Security Fund contributed 60 percent of project financing, with the government providing the remaining 40 percent.

The bridge has six vehicle lanes and two pedestrian and cycling lanes, improving movement for people and goods. The government, through the Office of the Vice-President, is advancing policies to protect marine ecosystems and build resilience to climate risks ahead of international climate action discussions.

Why it mattersThe government is balancing infrastructure development with environmental protection to support sustainable use of marine resources and protect coastal communities.

Tanzania to host 2027 East Africa Jua Kali trade fair

The nation officially accepted the responsibility for organizing the 27th East African Community Small and Medium Traders Exhibition.

Tanzania will host the 27th East African Community (EAC) Small and Medium Traders Exhibition, known as Jua Kali, in 2027. This event offers Tanzania a significant opportunity to showcase its entrepreneurs, boost trade and investment, and strengthen economic cooperation among EAC member states. Alana Nchimbi, Acting Director of Employment and Skills Development from the Prime Minister's Office, confirmed the government's readiness to ensure successful preparations and coordination.

Nchimbi expressed Tanzania's pleasure and honor in accepting this role, recognizing the exhibition's importance for the SME sector. She thanked the EAC Secretariat and member states for their trust. The government will collaborate closely with the EAC Secretariat, member governments, the private sector, and other stakeholders to ensure high standards for the event, aiming for it to be historic and economically beneficial.

The Jua Kali exhibition serves as a vital platform connecting SMEs from EAC countries to display products, share experiences, find markets, and build business networks. Tanzania's hosting is expected to increase opportunities for local entrepreneurs to promote their innovations and services within the regional market, stimulating intra-EAC trade growth.

Why it mattersTanzanian entrepreneurs will gain regional exposure and market access at the 2027 Jua Kali exhibition.
