Tundu Lissu jailed for four years for incitement

The High Court acquitted the CHADEMA chairman of treason but found him guilty of a lesser charge.

The High Court in Dar es Salaam sentenced CHADEMA national chairperson Tundu Lissu to four years in prison on November 6, 2026, for incitement. A three-judge panel, led by Judge Dunstan Ndunguru, found that the prosecution failed to prove the treason charge but that Lissu’s statements advocating for the disruption of the 2025 election constituted incitement.

Lissu, who conducted his own defence, insisted on his innocence and accused the judges of political persecution, stating he was not charged with incitement. The court maintained that trial evidence focused on incitement, giving Lissu notice and an opportunity to defend himself against the lesser offence. The courtroom was full with relatives, journalists, and dozens of advocates.

The prosecution had alleged Lissu intended to mobilise the public to obstruct the election to intimidate the executive, based on statements made at an April 3, 2025 meeting in Dar es Salaam during CHADEMA’s No Reforms, No Election campaign. The treason charge carried a potential death sentence, while the incitement conviction did not secure his immediate release.

Why it mattersTanzanian opposition leader Tundu Lissu was sentenced to four years in prison for incitement, avoiding a treason charge.

Tanzania trains health workers against diabetes, hypertension

A three-year programme will equip 25,500 students and 1,600 professionals with skills to manage chronic diseases.

Tanzania is launching a three-year programme to train 25,500 health students and 1,600 practising health professionals to combat rising diabetes and hypertension. Amref Health Africa Tanzania and the Novo Nordisk Foundation are partnering to support 113 health training institutions and establish two Centres of Excellence. This initiative aims to improve the detection, prevention, and management of cardiometabolic diseases.

The programme addresses identified gaps in how nurses and clinical officers are trained, as these professionals play a central role in primary healthcare delivery. A previous assessment found that cardiometabolic disease subjects were taught separately with limited practical learning. Hypertension affects more than 25 per cent of Tanzanian adults, and diabetes prevalence is between three and five per cent.

The initiative will collaborate with the government and universities to align educational improvements with national health priorities. Denmark’s Ambassador to Tanzania, Jesper Kammersgaard, highlighted the partnership as an example of international collaboration strengthening the health sector. Professor Flemming Konradsen of the Novo Nordisk Foundation stated that investing in health professional education is essential for reducing healthcare inequalities.

Why it mattersTanzanian adults are directly affected by rising rates of hypertension and diabetes, which require better management by trained health professionals.

Tanzania Agricultural Development Bank pays 8.4bn/- dividend

Parliamentary committee commends TADB for improved efficiency and increased dividend payment to government.

The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Public Investments (PIC) has commended the Tanzania Agricultural Development Bank (TADB) for increasing its dividend payment to the Government from 500m/- to 8.4bn/-. PIC Chairperson Ojambi Didas Masaburi announced the commendation in Dar es Salaam, citing improved efficiency in managing public capital.

Masaburi stated that TADB has safeguarded the government's 60bn/- capital investment and strengthened its capacity to contribute to state revenue. The bank has also opened eight zonal offices in Kagera, Ruvuma, and Kigoma regions to bring financial services closer to farmers, enabling smallholder farmers to secure loans.

TADB Board Chairman Ismail Andulile confirmed the committee directed the bank to ensure loans reach rural smallholder farmers. TADB will continue collaborating with commercial banks to improve agricultural lending efficiency across Tanzania.

Why it mattersTanzanian farmers will have improved access to loans for expanding agricultural activities, potentially increasing production and their economic well-being.

Business · 2026-11-08

Tanzania changes PPP law to attract investment

New legislation allows public-private partnerships to resolve disputes through international bodies.

Tanzania has amended its Public-Private Partnership (PPP) law to encourage investor participation in development projects. The changes also establish a framework for resolving investment disputes through international arbitration. PPP Centre Executive Director David Kafulila announced on social media that these amendments are part of efforts to strengthen the country's investment climate.

Kafulila, appointed PPP Commissioner in 2023, oversaw the legal revisions under the Ministry of Finance. Key changes include permitting international dispute resolution forums, such as the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID), to hear investor cases. This aligns with President Samia Suluhu Hassan's directives for policy and institutional reforms aimed at improving the investment environment.

The move also supports Finance Minister Balozi Omary Khamis's call for comprehensive economic reforms to meet the goals of the National Development Vision 2050. These legal adjustments are intended to create a more predictable and secure environment for both local and foreign investors engaging in the nation's development initiatives.

Why it mattersInvestors in Tanzanian development projects will now have access to international bodies for dispute resolution.

Culture · 2026-11-08

Zeinah Mwinyipembe challenged colonial-era gender norms

A 1926 Mambo Leo column by Zeinah Mwinyipembe critiqued dowry and marital inequality.

In 1926, Zeinah (Regina) Mwinyipembe wrote to the Mambo Leo magazine's advice column, comparing dowry practices among Tanganyika's Black community to chattel slavery. She questioned the societal acceptance of men having multiple wives while a woman having multiple husbands was not. Mwinyipembe's article, titled "Umama wa Ustaarabu" (to be the mother of civilisation), directly addressed a male contributor, accusing him of writing solely due to his literacy rather than informed opinion.

The responses to Mwinyipembe's article were deemed inappropriate for publication by the editors, who described them as 'dini tupu' or 'purely religious,' indicating strong male backlash. Mwinyipembe's critique extended to the unequal distribution of household wealth and freedom within marriage, noting that wives were treated as slaves and that husbands could arbitrarily leave and return. She also highlighted the severity of physical abuse women endured, comparing it to the treatment of donkeys.

Mwinyipembe's observations about women handling domestic responsibilities, including those previously assigned to slaves, and bearing the burden of emotional labour, remain relevant in present-day Tanzania. Her nuanced responses, such as likening a man who used demeaning phrases to 'leaves' that wither quickly, demonstrated a sophisticated critique of patriarchal attitudes. The editors' decision not to publish reader responses underscores the controversial nature of her arguments at the time.

Why it mattersZeinah Mwinyipembe's 1926 writings highlight enduring issues of gender inequality, domestic violence, and the unequal division of labour that continue to affect women in Tanzania.
