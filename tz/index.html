Dr Magembe joins Tanzanian delegation at WHO Africa session

Tanzania's Chief Medical Officer, Dr Grace Magembe, has arrived in Addis Ababa to participate in the 76th Session of the WHO Regional Committee for Africa.

Dr Grace Magembe, Tanzania’s Chief Medical Officer and Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Health, joined the Tanzanian delegation in Addis Ababa for the 76th Session of the World Health Organization (WHO) Regional Committee for Africa. The meeting, which runs from August 25 to 28, 2026, focuses on strengthening health systems and sharing experiences among African nations. Dr Magembe was welcomed by Tanzania’s Ambassador to Ethiopia, Innocent Shiyo, and other officials.

The Tanzanian delegation, led by Deputy Minister for Health, Dr Florence Samizi, will engage in discussions aimed at improving health service delivery across the continent. Dr Magembe is also scheduled to attend various side meetings and activities centered on health and international cooperation, facilitating the exchange of strategies.

Tanzania's participation underscores its role in advancing Africa’s health agenda, particularly in maternal, child, and reproductive health. This commitment aligns with President Dr Samia Suluhu Hassan's position as the African Union (AU) Champion for Maternal and Child Health, reflecting the nation's dedication to promoting the well-being of women and children in Africa.

Why it mattersThis event affects Tanzanian citizens by influencing the strategies and experiences shared to improve health service delivery within the country, especially for women and children.

Tanzania Vice President Nchimbi resigns from office

Emmanuel Nchimbi announced his resignation from politics and public service today, effective September four, 2026, making him the first vice-president to quit since independence.

Tanzanian Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi tendered his resignation today, August 25, 2026, through his Instagram account, which the Chief Secretary confirmed President Samia Suluhu Hassan has accepted. Nchimbi stated he is retiring from politics and public service, with his resignation from the Vice Presidency effective September four, 2026.

The resignation follows reports of a rift between Nchimbi and President Hassan, which escalated after Nchimbi's July 24, 2026, speech urging the public to “fight for the new constitution.” He was also summoned before the party’s ethics committee on July 29, 2026, following a proposal by a Cabinet minister.

Nchimbi's announcement on an account he last used in 2023 surprised many, as he had not used it for official communications before. Political analyst William Maduhu noted that if Nchimbi were expelled from his political party, his position as Vice President would automatically become vacant, as a member of a political party is a qualification for the office.

Why it mattersThe resignation of the Vice President affects all Tanzanian citizens by creating a vacancy in a high-ranking political office and signaling potential shifts in the country's political landscape.

Business · 08/26/2026

Tanzania advances Lake Victoria investments to boost economy, regional integration

The Permanent Secretary for East African affairs, Charles Ngeleja Kadonya, highlighted the Lake Victoria Basin's strategic role in economic growth and EAC integration.

Tanzania is actively pursuing investments in the Lake Victoria Basin to enhance its economy and strengthen regional integration within the East African Community (EAC). Charles Ngeleja Kadonya, Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and East African Cooperation, emphasized the lake's potential for trade, investment, transportation, and resource utilization during a tour in Mwanza.

Kadonya stressed that these projects must directly benefit citizens by creating employment, increasing incomes, and reducing poverty. He also underlined the importance of enabling Tanzanians to participate fully in these investments to become part of the value chain and benefit from the lake's resources.

During his visit, Kadonya inspected the MV Mwanza – Hapa Kazi Tu vessel, rescue boats, and a rehabilitated research vessel used for fish stock and water quality studies. Mwanza Regional Commissioner Said Mtanda noted ongoing government investments in strategic projects, including Mwanza Airport upgrades and port infrastructure, to strengthen the region's economy.

Why it mattersCitizens in the Lake Zone and across Tanzania are affected by potential job creation, income increases, and poverty reduction through these economic development initiatives.

Tanzania seeks global investment to restore degraded land

Tanzania has called on the international community to increase investment, technology, and financial resources to combat desertification and land degradation.

Tanzania urged the international community to boost investment and financial resources for land restoration and to counter desertification, according to Hamad Masauni, Minister of State in the Vice President's Office (Union and Environment). The minister emphasized the need to protect grazing areas, forests, and water sources during the 17th Conference of the Parties to the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD COP17), which opened in Ulaanbaatar.

Masauni stated that the conference, themed “Restore Land, Restore Hope,” focuses on increasing investment in land restoration, building resilience against drought, and sustainably managing grazing lands. He noted that the declaration of two thousand twenty-six as the International Year of Rangelands and Pastoralists strengthens Tanzania's agenda for conserving grazing areas and improving pastoralist communities' well-being.

According to Thimoth Mande, Tanzania's National Coordinator for the Convention, international cooperation is crucial for the country to address land degradation, biodiversity loss, and climate change comprehensively. Tanzania's participation in the conference allows it to showcase ongoing efforts and seek further investment and technology, potentially leveraging resources from organizations like the Global Environment Facility (GEF) and the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD).

Why it mattersThis event affects Tanzanian farmers and pastoralists by seeking resources to restore land, which directly impacts their livelihoods and production capacity.

Education · 08/26/2026

Dk Kiruswa funds Lailupa classroom completion with Sh milioni 6

Member of Parliament Dk Steven Kiruswa contributed Sh milioni 6 from his personal funds to complete a preschool classroom in Lailupa, Longido district.

The community of Lailupa in Meirugoi village, Longido district, received Sh milioni 6 from MP Dk Steven Kiruswa to finalize construction of a preschool classroom. This contribution will cover the remaining costs for one door, six windows, sixty corrugated iron sheets, twenty desks, timber, and plastering work for the Sh milioni 12 project.

Local residents, including mothers, youth, and traditional leaders, had initiated the classroom construction after children were observed studying under a tree, exposed to rain or sun. The Lailupa Hamlet Chairman, Ndung’anak Mepukor, stated that families contributed money, sold livestock, and participated in digging foundations and carrying bricks to build the classroom.

Dk Kiruswa, who is also the Deputy Minister of Minerals, made the pledge during a visit to inspect development projects in the ward. He commended the community's efforts and promised to ensure the preschool is registered and staffed by a qualified teacher by January next year, replacing the current volunteer teacher without formal qualifications.

Why it mattersChildren in Lailupa will no longer have to study under a tree or walk more than ten kilometers to school, gaining access to a safe learning environment closer to home.
