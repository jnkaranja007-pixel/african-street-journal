Tanzania Ruling Party Expels Vice President After Resignation

Emmanuel Nchimbi's departure from his vice presidential post is followed by expulsion from the ruling CCM party, deepening political rifts.

Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi resigned on August 25, 2026, a move followed swiftly by his expulsion from Tanzania's ruling CCM party for disciplinary and ethical violations. This sequence of events, occurring after Nchimbi's resignation letter appeared online and was accepted by President Samia, suggests a fallout within the party leadership. The party's decision contrasts with Nchimbi's stated intention to retire from public service, highlighting internal schisms.

Nchimbi's resignation and subsequent expulsion echo historical political departures within Tanzania, notably Aboud Jumbe's exit four decades prior, who relinquished multiple high-profile positions. Jumbe's departure was linked to his support for a three-government Union structure during the 'Crisis of the Union' period. The recent events also follow the suspension of the investigative newspaper Mwanahalisi for reporting Nchimbi's resistance to pressure to resign due to disagreements with the President.

The vice-presidency in Tanzania is often seen as a largely ceremonial role, though constitutionally empowered to assist with Union matters and act in the President's absence. However, the position holds potential for succession, as demonstrated in 2021. Nchimbi's situation, marked by his expulsion from the CCM party, further tests Tanzania's reputation for peace and stability amidst these internal political developments.

Why it mattersThe expulsion of Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi from the ruling party affects political stability and the internal dynamics of Tanzania's governing elite.

Tanzania Health Summit to Convene 2,000 Delegates

The 13th Tanzania Health Summit will focus on improving health systems and building health sovereignty with over 70 exhibitions and 40 forums.

Over 2,000 delegates are expected at the 13th Tanzania Health Summit (THS) in Dar es Salaam from October 5 to 7, focusing on improving health systems under the theme “Building Health Sovereignty and Strengthening Health Systems in a Changing Global Order.” The event will bring together government leaders, healthcare professionals, policymakers, entrepreneurs, and young change-makers from more than 25 countries.

The summit will feature over 70 exhibitions and 40 institutional forums, alongside the THS Health Workers Awards, Youth Capacity Building programs, and CHIA Innovation Awards. THS President Dr. Omary Chillo emphasized the event's importance in empowering nations to break foreign dependency, control medical supplies, and build self-sufficient healthcare systems resilient to shocks.

The THS is recognized as a leading Public Private Health Platform, having convened over 10,000 stakeholders from more than 230 institutions in its 12-year history. It has contributed to reforms like the Universal Health Insurance Act and Integrated Community Health Workers Programs, showcasing over one thousand research studies. Organizations like JICA and Ifakara Health Institute will present projects at this year's summit.

Why it mattersThe Tanzania Health Summit aims to improve health systems and achieve health sovereignty, affecting healthcare professionals, policymakers, and entrepreneurs by fostering collaboration and innovation.

Attorney General Affirms Ndejembi's Vice Presidency

The Attorney General explained the constitutional procedures followed in appointing Deogratius Ndejembi as Vice-President after a resignation.

Attorney General Hamza Johari stated in the National Assembly that Deogratius Ndejembi's appointment as Vice-President followed Tanzania's 1977 Constitution. The assembly had endorsed Ndejembi following Ambassador Emmanuel Nchimbi’s resignation. Johari explained that President Samia Suluhu Hassan nominated Ndejembi, with the nomination submitted to the National Assembly. The process adhered to constitutional and legal requirements for filling a vacant Vice-Presidential office.

Johari clarified that the effective date of a resignation is when the letter is received or a specified future date. He cited Article 50(4) of the Constitution, which mandates the President to appoint a successor within 14 days of a vacancy caused by removal, death, or resignation. This appointment must then be confirmed by the National Assembly through a majority vote. The Attorney General emphasized that constitutional provisions should be interpreted harmoniously, not in isolation.

Addressing legal interpretations, Johari referred to Article 47(4), which outlines qualifications for holding the Vice-Presidential office, including being a Tanzanian citizen, at least 40 years old, and a member of a political party. He stressed that these qualifications apply even to appointed successors, not just those who 'contest' for the office. Interpreting Article 50(4) separately from Article 47 would lead to unreasonable outcomes, he warned.

Why it mattersThe appointment of a new Vice-President affects the political leadership and governance structure of Tanzania.

President Samia Thanks Egyptian Envoy for Stronger Tanzania-Egypt Ties

President Samia Suluhu Hassan met with outgoing Egyptian Ambassador Sherif Ismail to acknowledge his contributions to bilateral cooperation.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan met with Egypt's Ambassador to Tanzania, Sherif Ismail, during his farewell visit to State House in Dar es Salaam. The President thanked Ambassador Ismail for his four-year tenure, during which he contributed to strengthening the long-standing and friendly relations between Tanzania and Egypt. She noted that cooperation has expanded across multiple sectors including trade, investment, education, health, agriculture, energy, tourism, and transport.

Discussions highlighted the importance of increasing trade and investment to maximize opportunities and benefit citizens of both nations. The leaders also addressed improving infrastructure and transport services to facilitate trade and create access to other African markets. In education, President Samia expressed gratitude for Egyptian scholarships and capacity-building programs offered to Tanzanians. Discussions also covered strengthening health sector cooperation through professional training, medical equipment access, and pharmaceutical production.

Both President Samia and Ambassador Ismail emphasized the importance of sustaining cooperation in these and other areas of mutual interest. This continued collaboration is seen as crucial for strengthening overall diplomatic and economic relations between Tanzania and Egypt. The meeting concluded with an acknowledgment of the progress made during the ambassador's term and a commitment to future engagement.

Why it mattersTanzanian and Egyptian citizens are affected by expanded cooperation in trade, investment, education, health, and infrastructure, aiming for greater economic benefits and opportunities.

Tanzania, DR Congo Agree to Repatriate Refugees

The two nations and UNHCR will begin a voluntary repatriation process for Congolese refugees in Tanzania.

Tanzania and the Democratic Republic of Congo, in conjunction with the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR), have agreed to start a voluntary repatriation process for Congolese refugees residing in Tanzania. Deputy Minister of Home Affairs, Ayoub Mohammed Mahmoud, announced this decision at a Tripartite Commission Meeting in Lubumbashi. He stated that Tanzania will ensure the return is voluntary, safe, and dignified, without any coercion.

For those not yet ready to return, Tanzania will continue to provide asylum and services at the Nyarugusu Refugee Camp in Kigoma, utilizing available resources. The voluntary repatriation process will not impede Tanzania's ability to accept new asylum seekers fleeing insecurity in parts of the DRC. The Deputy Minister also urged DRC leaders to visit Nyarugusu Camp to engage directly with their citizens and address their needs.

The agreement aims to create an enabling environment for refugees willing to return to reunite with their families and contribute to their nation's development. The DRC leaders' visits are intended to build connection and hope, not to persuade refugees to return. Both nations committed to facilitating the return of those who wish to go back to their home country.

Why it mattersCongolese refugees in Tanzania are affected by the agreement to begin a voluntary repatriation process, impacting their future living situations.
