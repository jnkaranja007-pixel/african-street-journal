Safaricom reports 1.25 billion cyberattack attempts

Kenya's leading telecom operator detected millions of daily intrusion attempts in its 2026 financial year.

Safaricom, a leading telecommunications operator in Kenya, detected and monitored approximately 1.25 billion intrusion attempts during its 2026 financial year. The company also investigated over 130,000 security events. These figures, released in Nairobi on October 27, 2026, highlight the significant pressure on digital service providers.

The report translates to an average of 3.4 million intrusion attempts daily, or nearly 40 every second. While not all attempts result in successful cyberattacks, the numbers indicate the scale of automated software and vulnerability searches facing essential digital services. Safaricom's 2026 Sustainable Business Report provides this data.

These statistics serve as a warning for Tanzania, where digital services are increasingly vital for daily life and economic activity. The report suggests that cybersecurity threats are continuous challenges requiring investment and preparedness for Tanzania's growing reliance on mobile money, online platforms, and digital payments.

Why it mattersTanzania's economy is becoming increasingly dependent on digital services, making it vulnerable to the scale of cyber threats highlighted by Safaricom's report.

Samia inaugurates Sh700 billion government city in Dodoma

Tanzania's new administrative hub, Mtumba Government City, officially opened with modern offices and infrastructure.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan officially opened the Mtumba Government City in Dodoma on October 30, inaugurating new offices, roads, and infrastructure. Angolan President João Lourenço attended the ceremony for the new administrative hub, designed to consolidate ministries and public institutions. The government invested approximately Sh700 billion in the project, which includes 59.1 kilometres of paved roads and 1,635 streetlights.

President Samia directed government officials to enhance their ICT systems, ensuring citizens do not need to travel to Dodoma for every service. Construction of the modern facilities began in 2021 to meet current and future administrative needs. The project aims to reduce paperwork and enable electronic document exchange between institutions.

The move to Dodoma as the capital began in 1973, with operations formally relocating in 2016 under President John Magufuli. The government states the relocation aims to bring public services closer to citizens and stimulate growth in central regions, creating demand for transport and housing.

Why it mattersTanzanian citizens will benefit from improved access to government services and potentially reduced travel needs due to the new administrative hub.

Tanzanian banks partner with startups

Innovators gain access to financial systems and capital, boosting the country's growing tech sector.

Banks in Tanzania are collaborating with technology startups to help innovators develop business solutions and access markets. These partnerships allow startups to integrate their systems with banking services, creating products for financial management and transaction tracking. Gwakisa Bill Mwaipopo, CEO of XB Limited, stated that collaboration with NMB bank provided his company access to Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) for developing business support technology.

Prosper Absalom, founder of Daraja Technology, also highlighted the importance of financial institution support, like that from NMB, in bringing startup technologies to market. He noted that regulatory hurdles and access to banking systems are significant challenges, making partnerships with established institutions valuable. Absalom urged the government to encourage more financial institutions to collaborate with innovators.

This collaboration occurs as Tanzania's startup ecosystem expands, with the number of startups growing from 247 in 2020 to over 1,300 in 2026. Tanzania Startup Association Chairman Paul Makanza stated these companies support approximately 20,000 direct and 124,000 indirect jobs, emphasizing the need for capital and markets alongside innovation for business growth.

Why it mattersTanzanian innovators and startups are gaining access to financial systems and capital, enabling them to develop and scale their businesses.

Alute Lissu, lawyer and brother of Tundu Lissu, dies

The constitutional lawyer died early Sunday after a long illness, his family announced.

Alute Lissu, a constitutional and administrative lawyer, died early Sunday after a long illness, his family confirmed. He had been admitted to Sani Hospital and his condition worsened before his death. His family expects the burial to take place in Singida, but will announce further arrangements after attending the treason case judgment of his brother, Tundu Lissu, on Friday.

Vincent Lissu Mughwai, Alute’s younger brother, described the deceased as a father figure and role model, particularly to Tundu, stating that Alute’s example inspired Tundu to become a lawyer. Alute Lissu, who leaves five children, had his wife pass away many years ago. He was known within the family as Lute.

Alute Lissu, who had spent over four decades in courtrooms, worked for the State before private practice, and saw public legal education as central to citizenship. His legal work focused on constitutional access and state accountability. He graduated from the University of Dar es Salaam’s Faculty of Law in 1982.

Why it mattersAlute Lissu's death affects his five children and his brother Tundu Lissu, a politician whose treason case judgment the family plans to attend.

Politics · 2026-11-02

Kadogosa orders construction equipment returned

Tanzania's Minister of Works ordered contractors to keep equipment on project sites.

Tanzania's Minister of Works, Masanja Kadogosa, ordered the return of construction equipment to project sites in Shinyanga region. This directive, issued in October 2026, aims to ensure projects proceed quickly and finish on time. Minister Kadogosa stated that contractors moving equipment between projects to meet initial payment requirements, known as 'Mobilization', causes delays and unnecessary costs for the government.

The order is being implemented on the 7.3-kilometer Iringa Bypass road construction project. As of October 29, 2026, the contractor had returned 41 of the 65 required machines. The Tanzania Roads Agency (TANROADS) met with the contractor on October 30, 2026, to emphasize the need to resume work immediately after returning the equipment.

This action ensures necessary machinery remains at project locations, speeding up work and preventing delays. The goal is to complete projects according to contract timelines. The minister's directive addresses concerns about contractors relocating equipment to satisfy initial payment terms, which can disrupt project schedules and lead to extra transport expenses.

Why it mattersContractors moving equipment between projects in Tanzania face orders to return machines to project sites to prevent delays and extra costs.
