CHADEMA youth leader charged with counselling terrorism

Coletha Clavery Ntidyicha, 31, faces charges of advising the public to commit terrorist acts to destabilise Tanzania.

Coletha Clavery Ntidyicha, 31, chairperson of CHADEMA’s youth wing BAVICHA in Kigoma Region, was charged with counselling terrorist acts at the Kisutu Resident Magistrate’s Court in Dar es Salaam on November 3, 2026. The charge sheet alleges she advised the public to participate in acts involving serious property damage to destabilise the country’s political, constitutional, economic, and social structures between August 1 and October 23, 2026.

CHADEMA stated on November 4 that Ntidyicha was brought to court without her lawyers present, alleging denial of legal access. The prosecution follows a legal challenge to her detention, which began with her arrest on October 14. The charge sheet, under the Prevention of Terrorism Act, lists her occupation as salon work and does not specify the words used or the nature of the communication.

CHADEMA’s communications director, Brenda Rupia, said on November 4 that the party supports the rule of law but believes terrorism charges are used to silence opponents. A High Court application had sought Ntidyicha’s production or release, with a decision on whether the substantive application could proceed made on October 28.

Tanzania trains health workers against diabetes, hypertension

A three-year programme will equip 25,500 students and 1,600 professionals with skills to manage chronic diseases.

Tanzania is launching a three-year programme to train 25,500 health students and 1,600 practising health professionals to combat rising diabetes and hypertension. Amref Health Africa Tanzania and the Novo Nordisk Foundation are partnering to support 113 health training institutions and establish two Centres of Excellence. This initiative aims to improve the detection, prevention, and management of cardiometabolic diseases.

The programme addresses identified gaps in how nurses and clinical officers are trained, as these professionals play a central role in primary healthcare delivery. A previous assessment found that cardiometabolic disease subjects were taught separately with limited practical learning. Hypertension affects more than 25 per cent of Tanzanian adults, and diabetes prevalence is between three and five per cent.

The initiative will collaborate with the government and universities to align educational improvements with national health priorities. Denmark’s Ambassador to Tanzania, Jesper Kammersgaard, highlighted the partnership as an example of international collaboration strengthening the health sector. Professor Flemming Konradsen of the Novo Nordisk Foundation stated that investing in health professional education is essential for reducing healthcare inequalities.

Why it mattersTanzanian adults are directly affected by rising rates of hypertension and diabetes, which require better management by trained health professionals.

Tundu Lissu jailed for four years for incitement

The High Court acquitted the CHADEMA chairman of treason but found his remarks on the 2025 election constituted incitement.

The High Court in Dar es Salaam on November 6, 2026, sentenced CHADEMA national chairperson Tundu Lissu to four years in prison for incitement. A three-judge panel found that the prosecution had failed to prove the treason charge against him.

Judge Dunstan Ndunguru's panel ruled that Lissu's remarks calling for the obstruction of the 2025 election amounted to incitement, a lesser offence than treason. Lissu, who conducted his own defence, insisted on his innocence and accused the judges of political persecution, requesting bail pending appeal.

The original charge concerned remarks made at an April 3, 2025 meeting in Dar es Salaam during CHADEMA’s No Reforms, No Election campaign. The prosecution had called 17 witnesses, alleging Lissu intended to mobilise the public to obstruct the election to intimidate the executive.

Why it mattersCHADEMA national chairperson Tundu Lissu was sentenced to four years in prison for incitement, a political conviction that removes the threat of a death sentence for treason.

Tanzania targets 80 percent clean cooking energy by 2034

The government is distributing subsidised LPG cylinders and cookstoves to rural areas.

The Tanzanian government is intensifying its clean energy campaign to ensure 80 percent of citizens use clean cooking energy by 2034. Deputy Minister for Energy Salome stated that the Rural Energy Agency (REA) is distributing subsidised six-kilogramme LPG cylinders and improved cookstoves in villages and semi-urban areas. This initiative aims to increase access to clean cooking energy, especially in rural communities where firewood and charcoal remain prevalent.

Public education campaigns are underway through meetings and seminars to highlight the benefits of cleaner cooking solutions. These efforts are part of a national agenda to reduce the burden of firewood collection on women, lower dependence on firewood and charcoal, improve public health, and minimise environmental damage. The government is committed to reaching rural communities with these subsidised gas cylinders and cookstoves.

The National Clean Cooking Energy Strategy targets measurable implementation results to ensure rural households benefit. The Deputy Minister reiterated the goal of 80 percent adoption by 2034, emphasising the importance of this transition for reducing health impacts and environmental degradation.

Why it mattersTanzanian citizens, particularly women in rural areas, will benefit from reduced firewood collection and improved health due to the clean energy initiative.
