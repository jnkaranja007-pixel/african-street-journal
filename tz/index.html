Tanzania charges CHADEMA official with treason

John Pambalu faces death penalty for allegedly inciting protests to obstruct the 2025 election.

John Justine Pambalu, CHADEMA’s director for organisation, training and elections, was charged with treason at the Kisutu Resident Magistrate’s Court in Dar es Salaam on October 21. Prosecutors allege Pambalu, between September 1 and October 29, 2025, intended to persuade the public to obstruct the October 2025 General Election. He allegedly manifested this intention through "endless protests" across the country to intimidate the Executive.

Gaston Shundo Garubindi, CHADEMA’s director of legal and human rights, stated Pambalu was arrested in Arusha and transferred to Dar es Salaam. This charge is legally similar to the treason trial of CHADEMA national chairperson Tundu Lissu, whose judgment is due on November 6, 2026. Lissu’s case stems from remarks made in April 2025, while Pambalu’s charge sheet covers a later period.

Pambalu’s prosecution follows a turbulent period for CHADEMA, including court restrictions and arrests. In April, the Court of Appeal quashed an injunction that had frozen the party’s political activity. Following the July 7 Saba Saba demonstrations, 23 people were charged with terrorism in Mwanza, and CHADEMA reported 51 members arrested and charged across several zones.

Why it mattersCHADEMA officials are facing treason charges, which carry a potential death sentence, impacting their political activities.

Tanzania maintains Ebola alert despite no local cases

Heightened screening and public-health precautions remain in place following nine cases in neighbouring Kenya.

Tanzania's Ministry of Health confirmed no Ebola cases have been detected within the country, yet it is maintaining heightened entry screening and public-health precautions. This alert, introduced last week, remains necessary due to the ongoing outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and recent imported cases in Kenya, where the total has risen to nine.

Chief Medical Officer Prof Bruno Fokas Sunguya stated on October 21 that "So far, there is no Ebola disease in Tanzania." The alert is a response to Kenya's situation, where nine cases are linked to an imported infection, with one confirmed death as of October 20, according to the US Centres for Disease Control and Prevention. Tanzania's measures include mandatory online health-surveillance forms and screening at all entry points, with high-risk travellers facing 21 days of monitoring.

The Ministry is reinforcing screening at airports, ports, and land borders, alongside community surveillance and treatment site readiness. This effort builds on a September guideline for managing filovirus cases, finalised with the WHO. Symptoms of the Bundibugyo virus disease can include fever, tiredness, muscle pain, headache, sore throat, vomiting, and diarrhoea, appearing two to 21 days after infection.

Why it mattersTanzania's Ministry of Health is maintaining heightened entry screening and public-health precautions due to the Ebola outbreak in neighbouring Kenya.

President Samia to attend 70th anniversary of Tanzania CSOs

The three-day event in Dar es Salaam from October 28 to 30 will celebrate civil society's contribution to national development.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan will attend the 70th anniversary of civil society organisation (CSO) registration and regulation in Tanzania. The event, scheduled for October 28 to 30, 2026, at Mlimani City in Dar es Salaam, will gather CSO leaders to discuss their sector's role in national development and social welfare. The Ministry of Home Affairs is managing online registration for participating organisations via its website or the RSMIS portal.

Registrar of Societies Emmanuel Kihampa stated that President Samia's presence will add prominence to the commemoration, focusing on civil society's support for social services and community participation. The anniversary theme is “Civil Society Organisations: The Foundation of Unity, Solidarity and National Development.” This event occurs as the government expands digital systems for CSO registration and oversight.

The Ministry of Home Affairs completed its electronic Registration and Management Information System, which is now operational, according to Home Affairs Minister Patrobas Katambi. Tanzania had 12,966 registered CSOs, with 126 registered between July 2025 and April 2026, comprising 12 religious and 114 non-religious organisations. The electronic platform allows organisations to access registration services remotely.

Why it mattersCivil society organisations and their leaders in Tanzania are directly affected by the commemoration and discussions on their role in national development.

Tanzania sees 1,605 relationship-related killings

Network of Human Rights Defenders reports 458 killings linked to relationship disputes in first half of 2026.

The Tanzania Network of Human Rights Defenders (THRDC) reported that 1,605 people were killed in relationship-related disputes between 2024 and June 2026. In the first six months of 2026, police recorded 941 killings, with 458 of these linked to relationship conflicts. This follows 926 killings in the same period of 2025 and 2,193 killings in 2024, of which 422 were related to relationship issues.

Women and children are among those most affected by these incidents, according to a statement released by THRDC on October 22, 2026. The network cited jealousy, marital and family disputes, financial disagreements, and child-rearing challenges as common causes. Specific cases mentioned include the deaths of Leah Mazengo in Bahi and Margeth Hamis Richard in Musoma, as well as two children in Morogoro killed by their father before he died by suicide.

THRDC attributed the rise in killings to weak anti-violence systems, lack of education on safe relationships, mental health challenges, and a shortage of social welfare experts. The network urged the government to strengthen reporting systems for threats, increase social welfare officers, and called on parliament to enact specific laws against gender-based and domestic violence. THRDC also announced a campaign titled “Love Is Not War; DO NOT KILL”.

Why it matters1,605 people have been killed in relationship-related disputes in Tanzania between 2024 and June 2026.
