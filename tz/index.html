Kenyan court orders police to produce missing Tanzanian activist

Justice Alexander Muasya Muteti ordered police to present Twaha Mwaipaya in court on November 2, 2026, with a detention warrant.

Kenyan police have been ordered by the High Court in Nairobi to produce missing Tanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya before the Milimani High Court on November 2, 2026. Justice Alexander Muasya Muteti issued the order on October 30, requiring authorities to present Mwaipaya with any detention warrant and explain why he should not be released. The directive follows an urgent application by the human-rights organisation Vocal Africa, which is suing on behalf of Mwaipaya after his reported abduction in Kamulu on October 25.

The court directed the Inspector General of Police, the Directorate of Criminal Investigations, and the Officer Commanding Ruai Police Station to produce Mwaipaya "live or dead." The order also requires Inspector General Douglas Kanja Kirocho and DCI Director Amin Mohamed Ibrahim to explain the alleged arrest and detention. Vocal Africa's application also sought preservation of CCTV, tracking records, and an independent investigation into the incident.

This habeas corpus direction aims to protect individuals from arbitrary loss of liberty, as guaranteed by Kenya's Constitution. The court's immediate focus is on Mwaipaya's production and the respondents' replies before the November 2 hearing. Mwaipaya's whereabouts and condition remain unconfirmed as of October 30, with no public reports indicating his release or appearance in court.

Why it mattersTanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya is missing after a reported abduction in Kenya, and Kenyan authorities have been ordered to account for his whereabouts.

Tanzania mourns historian John Iliffe

Professor John Iliffe, who died on August 20, 2026, significantly shaped Tanzanian history studies.

Professor John Iliffe, a historian renowned for his work on Africa, particularly Tanzania, passed away on August 20, 2026. Iliffe joined the University College, Dar es Salaam's history department in 1965, shortly after completing his PhD on German colonialism in Tanganyika. He co-directed a project collecting oral memories of the Maji Maji rebellion across southern Tanzania.

Iliffe's prolific writing covered diverse subjects, including Africa's poor, the HIV/AIDS epidemic, capitalists, and ruling elites. His book 'Africans: The History of a Continent' is a widely used introductory text, with its fourth edition completed just before his death. He was among foreign historians brought to the new department by its first head, Terence Ranger.

The Tanzania National Archives in Dar es Salaam noted Iliffe's frequent use of its documents, with his signature appearing on research sheets from the 1960s and 70s. President Julius Nyerere had called for historians to use oral traditions, a call Iliffe joined by collecting local historical knowledge.

Why it mattersThe work and legacy of Professor John Iliffe, who significantly contributed to Tanzanian historical studies and taught at the University College, Dar es Salaam, are being remembered.

Tanzania opens new government city in Dodoma

President Samia Suluhu Hassan inaugurated the Mtumba Government City, a project costing Sh700 billion.

Tanzania's capital city, Dodoma, has officially opened its new Government City in Mtumba, inaugurated by President Samia Suluhu Hassan on October 30, 2026. This move fulfills a vision initiated in 1973 to establish Dodoma as the nation's administrative and development hub. The new city signifies modern governance and national direction, aiming to align with 21st-century needs and foster investment, trade, and international cooperation.

The Mtumba Government City project, which began its second phase of construction in 2021, involved an investment of approximately Sh700 billion. The development includes about 59 kilometers of roads and modern communication systems, consolidating key government institutions into a central administrative center. This move is intended to enhance public institution efficiency and streamline government operations within a single hub.

Dr. Norga Komba, a member of the Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM) Political Committee in China, views the opening as a tangible outcome of long-term vision and a developmental legacy for current and future Tanzanians. The relocation of government activities to Dodoma, strengthened in 2016 and largely completed by 2019, represents a new chapter in Tanzania's governance history.

Why it mattersThe new Government City in Dodoma centralizes government ministries and institutions, impacting administrative efficiency for all Tanzanians.

Tanzania election victims await justice one year on

Thousands may have died in protests after the October 29 election, with many victims still traumatised and seeking redress.

One year after Tanzania's October 29 election, victims of the ensuing violence are still awaiting justice and struggling with trauma. President Samia Suluhu Hassan won the election with 98 percent of the vote after most opposition parties were excluded, leading to protests that security forces suppressed. Rights groups claim thousands died, while a government commission reported 518 deaths.

Twaha Bomeza, a 61-year-old from Dar es Salaam, lost an eye and four teeth during a shootout on election day, leaving him with an internal eye wound, shortness of breath, and constant headaches. He requires surgery for his injuries but cannot afford it. The Catholic Church held masses to honour victims, and the main opposition party, Chadema, observed a seven-day mourning period.

Government critics continue to face fear of abduction, arbitrary arrest, and charges of treason or terrorism. Bomeza expressed his pain, stating, "My blood was spilled here, but a year has passed, and there is no more blood here."

Why it mattersVictims of election violence, like Twaha Bomeza, are still suffering from injuries and lack of medical care a year after the events.

Tanzania and Russia explore new trade and investment opportunities

Engineer Mashauri Jofrey Sabwoya highlights potential for economic and technological cooperation following recent high-level visits.

Tanzania and Russia are entering a new phase of relations, with opportunities in trade, investment, education, science, technology, energy, agriculture, and industry identified as key areas for cooperation. These potential benefits stem from recent diplomatic engagements, including President Samia Suluhu Hassan's visit to Russia in June 2026 and Prime Minister Dr. Mwigulu Nchemba's participation in the Third Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow in October 2026.

Engineer Mashauri Jofrey Sabwoya, who serves as the Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM) Diaspora Secretary in Russia and Chairman of the Tanzania Students' Federation there, believes these high-level meetings open doors for enhanced collaboration. He emphasizes the need to translate these diplomatic efforts into tangible benefits for Tanzanian citizens, moving beyond mere agreements and discussions.

Sabwoya noted that the historical relationship between Tanzania and Russia, which began with support for liberation movements, now has the potential to evolve into a partnership yielding direct advantages for the populace. He specifically pointed to the opportunities in sectors such as mining, industry, and technology as areas where Russia's experience and knowledge could accelerate Tanzania's development.

Why it mattersTanzanians, including students and business people, can benefit from expanded economic and technological cooperation between Tanzania and Russia.
