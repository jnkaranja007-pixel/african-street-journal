CHADEMA memorial marks one year since election crisis deaths

The opposition party held a ceremony in Dar es Salaam to honor over 500 people killed during the 2025 election crisis.

Tanzania's opposition party, CHADEMA, held a national memorial event on November 3, 2026, in Dar es Salaam to remember those who died during the 2025 election crisis. Official figures state that over 500 people were killed and thousands more injured during the unrest.

The event, held at the party's office, included prayers, poetry, and the lighting of candles. Party communications personnel Brenda Rupia read a message stating, “The lives of our loved ones were of great value.” Names of confirmed deceased scrolled across a screen during the ceremony.

CHADEMA Vice Chairperson John Heche addressed the difficult historical moment, vowing to maintain the party's stance against abductions and killings. He also addressed social media claims that the party leadership might be blamed for the crisis.

Why it mattersThe memorial event honors Tanzanians killed and injured during the 2025 election crisis, directly affecting their families.

Tanzania targets 50 per cent formal jobs by 2050

Government initiatives aim to boost women's participation in innovation and procurement, with 23.34bn/- awarded to women's groups.

Tanzania aims for at least 50 per cent of its citizens to have formal employment by 2050, Deputy Minister Engineer Maryprisca Mahundi told the National Assembly. This is part of efforts to expand economic opportunities and promote inclusive growth.

As of June 2026, women made up 43 per cent of public sector employees, and the government is establishing incubation centres to promote women-led businesses, targeting a 30 per cent increase in women-owned start-ups by 2040. Public institutions awarded 944 procurement opportunities worth 23.34bn/- to 394 women's groups between the 2023/24 and 2025/26 financial years.

Digital platforms like iSOKO, which registered 24,269 women by June 2026, are used to expand market opportunities. 10 per cent of local government authorities' own-source revenue is allocated as interest-free loans to special groups, with women accounting for four per cent of beneficiaries.

Why it mattersWomen in Tanzania are directly affected by government initiatives aimed at increasing their participation in formal employment, entrepreneurship, and access to financial resources.

Foreign MPs ask Tanzania to guarantee fair trial for Tundu Lissu

Lawmakers from six countries and the EU urged President Hassan to rule out the death penalty ahead of Friday's verdict.

Members of Parliament from six countries and the European Union have appealed to Tanzanian President Samia Suluhu Hassan to ensure a fair trial for CHADEMA chairperson Tundu Lissu and to exclude the death penalty. The High Court is scheduled to deliver its treason judgement on Friday, November 6, 2026. This appeal was published by British MP Alicia Kearns on November 3, who stated she wrote the letter with 23 other parliamentarians.

The lawmakers' letter, dated November 2, seeks assurances for Lissu and the protection of peaceful political opposition. They argue that political disagreement should not be criminalized and that capital punishment against opponents risks turning the justice system into a tool of repression. Dar es Salaam Special Zone Police Commander Jumanne Muliro warned on November 3 of alleged plans for disorder on judgement day, as police monitor reports of people mobilizing to attend court.

Lissu’s family is preparing to attend the court session before traveling to Arusha for the funeral of his elder brother, Alute Mughwai Lissu. The appeal references a February finding by the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention that Lissu’s detention was arbitrary and calls for his release. The Commonwealth Ministerial Action Group also recommended in July that his detention be resolved within 30 days, a deadline that has passed.

Why it mattersTundu Lissu faces a treason verdict on Friday, November 6, 2026, with foreign MPs seeking guarantees for his fair trial and exclusion of the death penalty.

Tanzania deputy minister clarifies mine royalty collection

Mine licence holders cannot collect statutory charges from small-scale miners, says Deputy Minister Steven Kiruswa.

Deputy Minister for Minerals, Steven Kiruswa, stated in the National Assembly that mine and pit owners lack the authority to impose or collect royalties from small-scale miners.

Kiruswa explained that royalties, ranging from one to seven per cent based on mineral type, are collected through official government systems, including a 0.3 per cent local government levy. This clarification followed concerns raised by Kahama Urban MP Benjamin Ngaywa about licence holders demanding royalties from small-scale miners.

The government has cancelled licences of holders who failed to develop their areas and is conducting surveys, including aerial surveys in Lake Zone and Masutu areas, to identify new mineral deposits for small-scale miners. The Geological Survey of Tanzania aims to increase identified mineral potential from 16 per cent to 50 per cent by 2030.

Tanzania teams reach football finals

Taifa Stars advanced to the AFCON knockout stage and Serengeti Boys reached the U17 final.

Tanzania's national football teams have achieved significant international success in the past year. The Taifa Stars reached the Round of 16 at the Africa Cup of Nations in Morocco for the first time, securing a 1-1 draw against Tunisia on December 30, 2025, to advance. The Serengeti Boys, the national Under-17 team, reached the final of their continental championship, qualifying for the FIFA Under-17 World Cup in Qatar.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan announced a 500m/- reward for the Taifa Stars following their AFCON progression. The team's journey included a 1-0 loss to hosts Morocco in the Round of 16 on January 4, 2026. The Serengeti Boys' performance in the U17 Africa Cup of Nations demonstrated growing confidence and resilience in youth football.

These achievements, all occurring since President Hassan began her new term on November 3 last year, signal a strengthening of Tanzania's sporting profile. The national women's teams also saw success, with the Tanzanite Queens qualifying for the FIFA Under-20 Women’s World Cup and the Twiga Stars winning the Women’s Africa Cup of Nations.

Why it mattersTanzanian football fans and players will be inspired by the national teams' historic achievements in continental and global tournaments.
