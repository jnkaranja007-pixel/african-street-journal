Ndejembi Sworn In As Tanzania's New Vice President

Deogratius Ndejembi, 39, officially assumes the nation's second-highest office following unanimous parliamentary approval.

Deogratius Ndejembi has been sworn in as Tanzania's new Vice President at Chamwino State House in Dodoma, following unanimous approval from the National Assembly. The 39-year-old former Energy Minister replaces Ambassador Emmanuel Nchimbi and brings youthful energy to the role. President Samia Suluhu Hassan reminded Ndejembi that the office is a responsibility to Tanzanians, urging him to complement efforts to build a more productive, competitive, and inclusive economy.

Ndejembi's appointment comes at a critical time for Tanzania's development agenda, with a focus on accelerating economic growth and improving living standards. The President emphasized the need for stronger implementation of development policies, closer institutional coordination, and consistent follow-up on projects. Ndejembi is expected to foster an environment conducive to private sector investment, innovation, and job creation, aiming to transform national ambitions into measurable outcomes.

The transition follows the resignation and subsequent expulsion of former Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi from the ruling party. CCM officials cited Nchimbi's failure to appear before the Ethics Committee and his public statements, including a call for a new constitution, as reasons for his expulsion. Ndejembi's success will be measured by his contribution to economic transformation, public services, job creation, and improved living standards.

Why it mattersThe new Vice President's focus on economic transformation, private sector investment, and job creation directly impacts citizens and businesses.

Tanzania Prepares for Influx of Delegates at Major International Events

Arusha Regional Administrative Secretary Dr. Toba Nguvila urged local businesses and agricultural stakeholders to enhance services for upcoming international gatherings.

Arusha Regional Administrative Secretary Dr. Toba Nguvila has called on local business and agricultural stakeholders to prepare for a surge in demand driven by major international events. With thousands of delegates expected for the 153rd Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU) Assembly, HortiLogistica Africa 2026, and the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON), Dr. Nguvila emphasized the need to raise service standards in housing, transport, food, financial services, and healthcare. He urged agricultural players to build reliable supply networks for local hotels and restaurants, stressing clear coordination on capacity, packaging, pricing, and food safety.

HortiLogistica Africa 2026, anticipated to draw 15,000 attendees in November, is seen as a critical test for these preparations. Dr. Nguvila also advised tourism stakeholders to develop attractive tour packages to popular destinations like Ngorongoro, Serengeti, and Tarangire, ensuring clear pricing and itineraries. Institutions were directed to define roles, assign coordinators, and ensure readiness of essential services such as electricity, water, health, transport, and emergency response.

Businesses were encouraged to maintain quality services, fair pricing, cleanliness, and security, while residents were reminded to act as professional ambassadors. Sadio Events noted Tanzania's suitability for hosting high-profile events due to its peace, infrastructure, and conference facilities. The Tanzania Horticulture Association (TAHA) CEO highlighted that these events will connect local producers with investors and markets, expecting over 500 businesses to participate.

Why it mattersLocal businesses and agricultural stakeholders in Arusha are affected by the need to improve services and supply chains to meet the demands of thousands of delegates attending upcoming international events.

Forum Warns New Constitution Unlikely by 2030

Civil society leaders propose amending the current constitution to bridge the gap to the 2030 elections, citing risks of crisis.

Leading Tanzanian opinion leaders have proposed amending the current constitution to create a transitional document for the 2030 elections. They expressed concern that a new constitution is unlikely by the 2030 deadline, warning that holding elections under the current framework could trigger a crisis worse than the one following the October 2025 election. These views emerged at the Tanzania Democracy Forum on August 29, 2026, organized by civil society groups including the Tanganyika Law Society and Twaweza.

Former Prime Minister Judge Joseph Warioba cautioned that current commission processes are delaying tactics and that the country risks becoming refugees if it fails to address unresolved issues. He suggested focusing on key areas like the electoral system, power distribution, the Union, and ethics for immediate amendment, similar to the electoral law, while awaiting a new constitution. Warioba argued for a transitional approach, enacting laws based on agreed-upon constitutional areas.

Other stakeholders, including Jenerali Ulimwengu and Professor Issa Shivji, echoed concerns that rushing a new constitution amid unresolved political issues could lead to a worse outcome. Ulimwengu suggested respecting and working within the 1977 Constitution to prepare for the 2030 elections, emphasizing the need for national consensus in drafting any new constitution. The forum highlighted the unresolved issues from the October 2025 crisis as a major impediment.

Why it mattersThe country's political leaders and civil society organizations are concerned about the potential for a constitutional crisis and instability if the current framework is used for the 2030 elections.

Tanzania Medical Expo Draws Global Health Firms

The 27th Medexpo Tanzania 2026 will convene over 20 countries' medical and pharmaceutical companies to foster business in East Africa.

Dar es Salaam is positioning itself as a regional health center with the upcoming 27th Medexpo Tanzania 2026. The three-day event, scheduled for September 2–4 at the Diamond Jubilee Expo Centre, aims to connect medical and pharmaceutical firms from over 20 nations, seeking to expand opportunities within the East African market. Organizers anticipate manufacturers, distributors, investors, and healthcare providers will attend to forge business partnerships and explore the latest medical technologies.

The exhibition will feature a wide array of products, including surgical instruments, hospital furniture, laboratory equipment, diagnostic imaging systems, dental solutions, physiotherapy equipment, and pharmaceutical innovations. Organizers stated the event serves as a leading regional platform for the medical, healthcare, and pharmaceutical industries, offering three days dedicated to business, learning, and networking. Health Minister Mohamed Mchengerwa is expected to inaugurate the ceremony, joined by ambassadors, government officials, and industry leaders.

Institutions such as TanTrade, the Medical Stores Department, the Pharmacy Council, the Tanzania Medicines and Medical Devices Authority, the Tanzania Investment Centre, and Aga Khan Hospital are supporting the expo. This backing highlights the event's growing significance in promoting quality healthcare and generating new commercial prospects in the medical sector. The program also includes product launches, live demonstrations, and networking sessions tailored for hospital procurement teams, pharmacists, clinicians, distributors, and healthcare investors.

Why it mattersMedical and pharmaceutical firms from over 20 countries will attend to seek business partnerships and expand opportunities in the East African market.

Tanzania Slashes Land Survey Fees to 130,000 Shillings

New government guidelines reduce the cost of surveying a single plot from up to 400,000 shillings.

The Tanzanian government has reduced the cost of surveying developed land plots to 130,000 shillings, according to Deputy Minister of Lands, Housing and Human Settlements Development, Kaspar Mmuya. This new fee structure aligns with the 2021 Land Regularization Guidelines. Previously, citizens paid between 250,000 and 400,000 shillings for plot planning and surveying.

Mmuya announced the reduction while responding to a question from Dickson Lutevele, the Member of Parliament for Mafinga Mjini. Lutevele had inquired about the government's plans to introduce affordable land surveying services for residents in the Isalavanu, Bumilaying, Rungemba, and Saohili wards. The new fee applies to the planning and surveying of a single plot.

For standard surveying not involving regularization, costs are determined by the 2016 Plot Price Estimation Guidelines. These guidelines consider factors such as the area's distance, the size of the plot being surveyed, proximity to benchmark points, required equipment, and the cost of boundary beacons. The move aims to make land ownership more accessible.

Why it mattersCitizens in specific wards will benefit from reduced land surveying costs, making property ownership more affordable.
