Tanzania finds 223 billion cubic feet of natural gas

Energy Minister Juma Homera announced the discovery at the Kasa-1X ST2 well in Mtwara's Mnazi Bay area.

Tanzania's Energy Minister Juma Homera announced on October 27 the discovery of an estimated 223 billion cubic feet (Bcf) of natural gas at the Kasa-1X ST2 well in Mtwara's Mnazi Bay area. This find follows a three-well campaign by the Tanzania Petroleum Development Corporation (TPDC) and French operator Maurel & Prom (M&P). The government stated the discovery will add to domestic gas supply.

The 223 Bcf figure announced by Homera is an early estimate and not yet a certified recoverable reserve. M&P's own technical update on October 22 estimated gas volumes at approximately 200 Bcf gross, or 120 Bcf net to its 60 per cent interest, without classifying it as a reserve. The two production wells, MB-5 and MS-2, are already on stream, increasing Mnazi Bay's output to about 130 million cubic feet a day.

Gas from the Mnazi Bay field, one of Tanzania's two producing gas fields, is used for power generation, industry, transport, and households. The three-well programme was expected to add 45 to 55 million cubic feet a day to the field's average output of 110 million cubic feet a day. First production from the Kasa exploration well is envisaged as early as November.

Why it mattersTanzania has discovered a significant natural gas find that will bolster domestic supply for power, industry, transport, and households.

Tanzania targets 7.3tri/- for climate projects at COP31

Experts met in Dar es Salaam to prepare proposals for the UN climate conference in Türkiye.

Tanzania aims to secure 7.3tri/- for environmental and climate change projects at the upcoming 31st Conference of the Parties (COP31) in Antalya, Türkiye. Experts met in Dar es Salaam on October 28, 2026, to develop proposals and a common national position for the conference, scheduled from November 9 to 20. The financing is required for implementing the National Development Vision 2050, which identifies 7.3tri/- for its Environmental Management Transformation Programme.

Director of Environment Kemilembe Mutasa urged stakeholders to prioritise the interests of over 60 million citizens and speak with one voice during negotiations. At COP30 in Brazil, Tanzania chaired the African Group of Negotiators, coordinating Africa’s position on climate financing and leading to the establishment of a climate negotiations training centre for young people. Zanzibar's Ministry of Blue Economy and Fisheries plans to advocate for increased financing for resilience projects, including sea walls and public education.

The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) is collaborating with the Vice-President’s Office on the global Restoration Initiative. This project aims to restore 5.2 million hectares of degraded land by 2030 in the Great Ruaha and Lake Rukwa basins, covering Iringa, Mbeya, and Rukwa regions. Zanzibar's Dr Makame Omar Makame stated the Isles are vulnerable to climate change impacts on agriculture and fisheries.

Why it mattersTanzania needs 7.3tri/- for environmental and climate change projects to support its National Development Vision 2050.

East African legal bodies press Kenya, Tanzania over activist's disappearance

Twaha Mwaipaya was reportedly abducted in Kenya on October 25, with authorities offering conflicting accounts.

Tanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya was reportedly abducted in Kenya on October 25, drawing intervention from East African legal bodies. Amnesty International Kenya stated that three unidentified men intercepted Mwaipaya's taxi in Kamulu, Machakos County, forcing the driver out and taking Mwaipaya in one of three vehicles. CHADEMA, Mwaipaya's party, has called for his safety and accountability for those responsible.

The Kenyan Government Spokesperson's office stated on October 27 that it had received no formal report of the abduction, while the Tanzania High Commission in Nairobi also reported no such claim. This contrasts with a Reuters report of a police document listing Mwaipaya as missing on October 26. The Uganda Law Society described the incident as part of wider regional cross-border repression, urging Tanzania to seek extradition through judicial safeguards if it has a case.

No Tanzanian authority has publicly stated it is seeking Mwaipaya's return or accused him of an offence. The Pan-African Solidarity Network held a press conference in Nairobi on October 26 regarding the case. Kenya's constitution protects against arbitrary deprivation of liberty and guarantees a fair trial.

Why it mattersTanzanian activist Twaha Mwaipaya is missing in Kenya, with his political party and regional legal bodies demanding answers from both governments.

Tanzanian businesses explore Russian trade and investment

Mkuyu Africa and other private sector players met Russian investors in Moscow to discuss cooperation in energy, agriculture, and mining.

Tanzanian private sector representatives, including Mkuyu Africa, met with Russian investors in Moscow yesterday to explore trade and investment opportunities. The discussions, held on the sidelines of the Russia-Africa Summit, aimed to strengthen commercial ties and focused on potential cooperation in energy, agriculture, mining, construction, and infrastructure.

The Tanzanian delegation, which included members of the Tanzania Private Sector Federation (TPSF), showcased the country’s investment potential. Mkuyu Africa Director Juneid Othman stated the forum provided an opportunity to establish business relationships and exchange experiences with potential partners for business expansion.

Kenyan participant Peter Omong noted the importance of such platforms for entrepreneurs seeking to expand operations and find new opportunities. He connected with a trader dealing in heavy fuel oil and fertilisers, viewing the engagement as a step towards broadening his business network.

Why it mattersTanzanian businesses are seeking new trade and investment opportunities with Russian investors.

Tanzania's Tundu Lissu begins hunger strike

Jailed opposition leader protests denied access to lawyers ahead of treason verdict.

Tanzania's jailed opposition leader Tundu Lissu started a hunger strike on Monday to protest his inability to meet his lawyers.

Lissu, the Chadema party leader, began refusing food after his legal team was barred from entering Ukonga prison, where he is detained on treason charges. His lawyers had accused prison officers of eavesdropping on their meetings.

The High Court had ruled earlier this month that Lissu must be allowed private meetings with his lawyers. Lissu faces the death penalty if convicted, with a verdict expected on November 6. He was detained last April and accused of inciting a boycott of the 2025 elections.

Why it mattersOpposition leader Tundu Lissu is on hunger strike in jail while facing treason charges and a potential death penalty.
