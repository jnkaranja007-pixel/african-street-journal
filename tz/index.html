Graduates Gain Practical Skills Through VETA Programs

Seven hundred ninety-four university graduates are enrolling in vocational training to enhance their job prospects and meet labor market demands.

A total of 794 university graduates have enrolled in vocational training programs offered by the Vocational Education and Training Authority (VETA) to acquire additional practical skills and improve their competitiveness in the labor market. VETA Director General Anthony Kasore stated that the growing number of graduates seeking vocational training reflects the increasing importance of technical skills for employment and adapting to changing labor market demands. Some enrolled individuals are already employed in public and private institutions, seeking to enhance their professional capabilities.

Mr. Kasore urged other graduates and Tanzanians needing additional skills to utilize VETA's open enrollment. The authority is actively linking these graduates with 16 industries to provide practical experience and build competence in their fields. This initiative aims to strengthen graduates' capacity and improve their employment prospects. VETA has also provided skills training to 4,465 entrepreneurs, including food vendors and barbers, to improve business operations and productivity.

The vocational training programs are designed to equip participants with better working methods, enhance service quality, and increase overall productivity. By offering practical skills and industry connections, VETA aims to bridge the gap between academic qualifications and the demands of the contemporary job market. The authority continues to encourage broader participation from individuals seeking to upskill or reskill.

Why it mattersUniversity graduates are affected by gaining practical skills and industry connections to improve their competitiveness in the labor market.

Tanzania arrests 28 migrants from Burundi and DR Congo

Immigration officials apprehended the individuals during a crackdown in Kigoma Region for entering the country without valid permits.

Tanzanian immigration officers arrested 28 individuals from Burundi and the Democratic Republic of Congo in Kigoma Region on the night of September 3, 2026. The arrests occurred during a patrol and crackdown operation aimed at enforcing immigration laws. Some migrants were found engaged in agricultural work, while others were apprehended at bus stations.

Kigoma Regional Deputy Immigration Officer, ACI Jeremiah Mwakibinga, stated that the detained individuals will face charges in court for entering Tanzania without proper documentation. These actions are part of ongoing efforts to control the entry and residence of foreigners and to strengthen national security.

The Kigoma Special Zone Immigration Office has indicated that patrols and operations will continue across the region. The goal is to ensure compliance with immigration laws and maintain national security. The apprehended migrants were found in various locations within the region.

Tanzanian Businesses Leverage Social Media for E-Commerce Sales

Tanzanians are increasingly buying goods through social media platforms like Instagram, using mobile money for transactions and boda boda riders for delivery.

Tanzania's e-commerce landscape differs significantly from Western models, with social media platforms like Instagram serving as primary marketplaces. Consumers discover products, communicate with sellers via direct messages or WhatsApp, and place orders without traditional online storefronts or card payments. Transactions are typically completed using mobile money services such as M-Pesa or Airtel Money, with proof of payment sent to the seller.

Delivery is facilitated by local boda boda riders who coordinate directly with customers, often completing deliveries within hours of the initial product discovery. This informal yet efficient system bypasses established courier networks and formal logistics, allowing for rapid fulfillment of orders within urban areas. The process highlights a unique adaptation of e-commerce to local infrastructure and consumer habits.

A cross-border dimension also exists, with Tanzanians and Kenyans purchasing goods from each other's countries. Products are often sent via bus services, with parcels collected by recipients on the other side. This inter-country trade relies on bus companies acting as informal parcel networks, enabling goods to move between cities like Mombasa and Dar es Salaam, often arriving by the next day.

Why it mattersTanzanian consumers are affected by this e-commerce model through convenient access to a wide range of goods and rapid delivery, while sellers benefit from low overheads and direct customer engagement.

ATCL Resumes Dar-Moscow Flights After Risk Assessment

Tanzania's national airline will restart its route to Russia on September 7, 2026, following a standard operational risk evaluation.

Kampuni ya Ndege Tanzania (ATCL) announced the resumption of its flights between Dar es Salaam and Moscow, effective Monday, September 7, 2026. The decision follows the completion of a routine risk assessment concerning the operating environment for this route. ATCL stated that this evaluation was conducted in consultation with relevant authorities as part of the company's ongoing safety monitoring procedures across its network.

The airline confirmed that flight services are reinstated when the assessment indicates that conditions permit continued operations. The specific flights affected are TC422 and TC423. Passengers who had previously postponed their travel due to the service suspension will be permitted to rebook their journeys without incurring additional charges.

ATCL's announcement provides a clear timeline for the return of the Dar es Salaam to Moscow route. The company's commitment to regular risk assessments underscores its approach to ensuring the safety and viability of its international flight operations. Passengers are advised to contact the airline for rebooking arrangements.

Why it mattersInvestors may see renewed opportunities for trade and travel facilitation as ATCL reopens its Moscow route.

Zanzibar Women Turn Cultural Traditions Into Tourism Livelihoods

A group of forty women in Bububu are earning income by sharing their knowledge of traditional cuisine, clothing, and daily life with tourists.

In Zanzibar, women are increasingly leveraging the islands' rich cultural heritage to build sustainable livelihoods through tourism. Beyond the famed beaches, visitors are seeking authentic experiences, including traditional cuisine, clothing, and daily customs. This cultural tourism provides significant opportunities, especially as women constitute over fifty percent of Zanzibar's population, which numbered 1,889,773 residents in 2022.

The 'Mamas of Zanzibar' group, based in Bububu, exemplifies this trend. Founded six years ago by Maskat Shineni, the collective of forty women preserves and promotes Zanzibari culture, offering activities like henna painting, traditional food preparation, and insights into local life. This initiative has become a vital source of income, enabling women to support their families and improve their circumstances.

While the 'Mamas of Zanzibar' offer a collective livelihood and a space for mutual support, the journey has not been without challenges. Maskat Shineni started the group during a personal transition, finding an opportunity in the shared knowledge of her neighbours. However, the group faces limitations in resources and capacity, preventing them from assisting all members with their difficulties, and some early participants have departed due to slow progress.

Why it mattersWomen in Zanzibar are directly benefiting from tourism by earning income through cultural activities, which helps them sustain their families.
