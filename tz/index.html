Tanzania and Angola pledge to boost regional security and health

Presidents Samia Suluhu Hassan and João Manuel Gonçalves Lourenço met in Dar es Salaam to discuss peace efforts and maternal health initiatives.

Tanzania and Angola have pledged to strengthen cooperation on regional security and improving maternal and child health. President Samia Suluhu Hassan announced this on October 29 in Dar es Salaam after bilateral talks with Angolan President João Manuel Gonçalves Lourenço. The leaders discussed ongoing instability in Southern Africa, the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, and the Great Lakes region.

President Hassan stated that both nations are concerned about regional conflicts and are exploring ways to support peace restoration efforts. She also urged President Lourenço to support her African Union initiatives aimed at advancing maternal and child health, highlighting Tanzania's goal of zero preventable deaths during childbirth and for newborns. The cooperation aims to share experiences and strengthen health interventions.

Separately, Home Affairs Minister Patrobas Katambi represented President Hassan at a three-day commemoration of 70 years of civil society registration. Registrar of Societies Emmanuel Kihampa stated that 12,966 societies are registered on the electronic system, with 126 new registrations and 350 inspections conducted between July 2025 and April 2026.

Why it mattersTanzanian and Angolan leaders agreed to cooperate on regional security and maternal health, aiming to restore peace and reduce preventable deaths for mothers and children.

Tanzania announces 223 bcf gas find in Mtwara

Energy Minister Juma Homera announced the discovery at the Kasa-1X ST2 well, adding to domestic supply.

Tanzania has announced the discovery of an estimated 223 billion cubic feet (Bcf) of natural gas at the Kasa-1X ST2 well in Mtwara's Mnazi Bay area. Energy Minister Juma Homera made the announcement on October 27, following a three-well campaign by the Tanzania Petroleum Development Corporation and French operator Maurel & Prom (M&P). The government stated the find will add to domestic gas supply.

The 223 Bcf figure is an early estimate from the Kasa-1X ST2 well and should not yet be treated as a certified recoverable reserve, according to Homera. M&P, which operates the block with a 60 per cent interest, estimated gas volumes at approximately 200 Bcf gross in an October 22 technical update. The ministry had previously signalled the discovery on October 18, stating that drilling of two development and one exploration well had been completed.

The Kasa exploration well is envisaged to begin production as early as November, with an expected gross potential of 50 million cubic feet a day. This find is significant as Mnazi Bay is one of Tanzania's two producing gas fields, alongside Songo Songo. Gas from Mnazi Bay is used for power generation, industry, transport, and households.

Why it mattersThe discovery of natural gas will strengthen gas availability for power generation, industry, transport, and households in Tanzania.

Tanzania launches food choice framework

The Tanzania Women Lawyers Association supports the new Tanzania Nutrient Profile Model 2026.

Tanzania launched a new framework today in Dodoma to promote healthier food choices, supported by the Tanzania Women Lawyers Association (TAWLA). TAWLA Executive Director Tike Mwambipile stated the association helped develop the Tanzania Nutrient Profile Model (TzNPM) 2026, which classifies food based on sugar, salt, and fat content.

Mwambipile said TAWLA considers nutrition a vital public health issue and has partnered with the Ministry of Health to strengthen laws and policies. She highlighted that good nutrition is essential for national development and particularly for women's health due to their family roles.

The framework addresses Tanzania's nutrition challenges, including stunting and rising rates of overweight and obesity. The Ministry of Health's Permanent Secretary, Dkt. Grace Magembe, stressed the need for household changes, reduced salt, sugar, and fat consumption, and increased physical activity.

Why it mattersThe new framework and the Tanzania Nutrient Profile Model 2026 aim to help consumers make informed decisions about food purchases.

Tanzania election victims await justice one year later

Protests following the October 29 election were suppressed by security forces, leaving many traumatised.

One year after Tanzania's October 29 election, victims of the violence still await justice. President Samia Suluhu Hassan won with 98 percent of the vote after most opposition parties were excluded, leading to protests that security forces suppressed. Rights groups and the opposition claim thousands died, while a government commission reported 518 deaths.

Twaha Bomeza, 61, lost an eye and four teeth to shrapnel during a shootout on election day in Dar es Salaam. He requires surgery for an internal eye wound, suffers shortness of breath, and constant headaches. Bomeza stated his blood was spilled but no action has followed a year later.

The Catholic Church held masses on Thursday to honour the victims, and the main opposition party, Chadema, announced a seven-day mourning period. Government critics continue to face fear of abduction, arbitrary arrest, or charges of treason and terrorism.

Why it mattersVictims of election violence, like Twaha Bomeza, are still suffering physical injuries and awaiting medical care a year later.

Tanzania grants TPDC four oil and gas exploration licences

State-owned corporation takes over Songosongo operations as it seeks to boost domestic resource development.

Tanzania has awarded the Tanzania Petroleum Development Corporation (TPDC) four independent licences to explore for oil and natural gas in the Mnazi Bay North, West Songo Songo, Eyasi-Wembere, and Lindi-Mtwara blocks. The licences, signed on October 28, 2026, in Dar es Salaam, mark the first time TPDC has received such permits in its own name, signalling a greater state role in the nation's petroleum industry.

Vice-President Deogratius Ndejembi stated the licences enable TPDC to fulfil its responsibilities under the Petroleum Act of 2015, which mandates a central role for the corporation in the sector. Permanent Secretary James Mataragio added that this move will accelerate exploration by allowing TPDC to seek financially and technologically capable investors.

The government is also preparing TPDC to take over operations at the Songosongo gas field, where the development licence previously held by PanAfrican Energy Tanzania has expired. This initiative aims to maximise Tanzania's estimated 57 trillion cubic feet of natural gas reserves, building on the over 301 billion cubic feet produced between March 2021 and March 2025.

Why it mattersTanzania's state-owned TPDC will now have direct control over oil and gas exploration in four blocks and operations at the Songosongo gas field, aiming to increase the nation's energy production and revenue.
