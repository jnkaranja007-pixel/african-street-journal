Tanzania's WAPO Radio suspends all broadcasts indefinitely

Management cites operational and technical reasons for the immediate halt without giving a return date.

WAPO Radio in Tanzania has halted all broadcasts until further notice, taking effect immediately. Sylvester Gamanywa, the general overseer of the station, described the sudden suspension as an operational and technical matter. In a notice directed to listeners, customers and stakeholders, station management did not provide a specific reason for the interruption or name any authority responsible for ordering it.

Speaking to DW Kiswahili on November 16, 2026, Gamanywa declined to elaborate on the exact cause while internal discussions continued, stating that giving details would be inappropriate at that stage. The station is owned by WAPO Mission International in Tanzania. Management apologised for the disruption while thanking supporters for their patience and trust, and appealed for prayers from the public before addressing the technical issues.

Independent Tanzanian broadcasters face severe financial pressures, including more than 18 statutory levies, high electricity costs and demands for unpaid airtime from government agencies. Amos Ngosha, chairperson of the National Independent Broadcasters Association, stated in an August 6 report that several station owners had voluntarily shut down because they could no longer afford to operate.

Why it mattersListeners, customers and stakeholders face an indefinite loss of broadcasts from WAPO Radio without any scheduled date for services to resume.

Zanzibar Second Vice-President orders swift justice for abuse cases

Hemed Suleiman Abdulla urged security authorities in Pemba to speed up investigations into sexual abuse, drug trafficking and theft.

Zanzibar Second Vice-President Hemed Suleiman Abdulla directed security and judicial authorities in Pemba to accelerate investigations and prosecutions for sexual abuse, drug trafficking and theft. Speaking during a tour in Micheweni District on Saturday, November 14, 2026, he warned that delays undermine community protection and harm farmers and livestock keepers affected by crop and property theft.

Mr Hemed also instructed the Zanzibar Economic Empowerment Agency to remove bureaucratic hurdles for citizens accessing a 47bn/- government fund for entrepreneurs, young people and women. During inspections of development projects, he announced that a fish landing site being built by SCENIC Company in Msuka Baharini village will be completed by February 2027.

Micheweni District Commissioner Khatib Juma Mjaja presented a three-month performance report detailing ongoing local measures to address social problems. Mr Hemed further urged council officials in Micheweni District to strengthen revenue collection and instructed the North Pemba Regional Security and Defence Committee to improve public safety.

Why it mattersFarmers and livestock keepers in Micheweni District face direct livelihood impacts from crop and property theft, while citizens await access to a 47bn/- empowerment fund.

Tanzania plans 100 standard neonatal units by 2028

The expansion would raise the national total of newborn care units from 438 to 538, Deputy Health Minister Florence Samizi said.

Tanzania's deputy health minister, Dr Florence Samizi, said the government intends to open 100 standard neonatal intensive care units nationwide by 2028, meant to follow World Health Organisation standards. She spoke in Dar es Salaam at World Prematurity Day events hosted by the Doris Mollel Foundation with the Tanzania Preterm Family Network.

Samizi said the government is building 36 such units now: 35 of its own, 15 at regional hospitals and 20 at district hospitals and health centres, plus one at Katoro backed by the Doris Mollel Foundation. In 2018 the country had only 14 neonatal intensive care units, against more than 438 newborn care units today.

The target is to cut newborn deaths from 24 to 12 per 1,000 live births by 2030. Samizi said the new facilities will let mothers and premature babies stay together, offer counselling and privacy, and allow fathers to take part in care when mothers cannot.

Why it mattersMothers and premature babies in districts without specialised care would gain nearby units, and the ministry must deliver 36 under construction first.

Politics · 2026-11-17

Samia consults party leaders on reconciliation commission

Tanzania's main opposition party CHADEMA stayed away from talks on a body to coordinate constitutional reform.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan met political party leaders on November 16 at the Dar es Salaam State House to discuss forming a Reconciliation and Mediation Commission to prepare for constitutional amendments. Tanzania's main opposition party, CHADEMA, did not attend the meeting, which brought together members of the Council of Political Parties.

Council members proposed that the planned body should represent both sides of the Union and ensure gender equality, according to a presidency statement signed by Director of Presidential Communications Bakari Machumu. In an X post, Samia said consultations will continue with political stakeholders, civil institutions and the private sector.

The commission remains a proposed body, with no announced membership or working arrangements. CHADEMA deputy chairperson John Heche said in July that the party's Central Committee required the release of national chairperson Tundu Lissu and a mediator before talks could begin.

Why it mattersThe commission's membership and working arrangements are still unannounced, so the parties and groups it would involve do not yet know their role in the constitutional process.

Culture · 2026-11-17

Habari Leo publishes lessons on Swahili proverb meaning

New educational columns in Tanzanian newspapers examine the literal and figurative meanings of the traditional Swahili saying.

Habari Leo and Daily News Tanzania published educational features explaining the traditional Swahili proverb Usilolijua ni sawa na usiku wa giza. The publications stated that the literal meaning compares an unknown thing to a dark night. The outlets noted that the figurative meaning reflects how a lack of knowledge can prevent people from seeing the truth or making sound judgments.

According to the newspapers, the saying teaches three lessons which include seeking knowledge, avoiding premature judgments, and remaining open to learning. The reports added that not knowing something does not automatically mean a person is wrong.

The publications concluded that the enduring wisdom of the proverb encourages readers to understand situations before judging them, rejecting opportunities, or making decisions.

Why it mattersReaders across Tanzania can access the published language lessons in Habari Leo and Daily News Tanzania to study Swahili proverbs.
