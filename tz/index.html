Tanzania switches on 2,115-megawatt hydropower plant, boosting national grid capacity

President Samia Suluhu Hassan officially commissioned the Julius Nyerere Hydropower Project on Saturday, adding significant generation capacity to the national electricity grid.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan officially switched on the Julius Nyerere Hydropower Project (JNHPP) on Saturday, August twenty-second, 2026, in Pwani. The 2,115-megawatt plant on the Rufiji River is now a central part of Tanzania’s national electricity grid and supports the country’s industrialisation efforts. The ceremony was attended by Egypt’s Prime Minister, Dr Mustafa Kamal Madbouly, and former African Development Bank president Dr Akinwumi Adesina.

The project, costing roughly Sh7.452 trillion, equivalent to about US$2.9 billion, was financed entirely through domestic government resources, with no foreign loans. TANESCO’s director-general, Lazaro Twange, confirmed that more than Sh7.11 trillion has already been paid to the contractor from the national budget. An Egyptian consortium, Arab Contractors and Elsewedy Electric, carried out the engineering, procurement, and construction, while Chinese firms PowerChina and Dongfang Electric Corporation supplied the machinery.

Construction ran from June 2019 to March 2025, with the plant beginning to feed power into the grid in February 2024. By May thirty-first, 2026, JNHPP was supplying about 44.9 per cent of all electricity to the national grid over the preceding year. This addition increased Tanzania’s total installed generation capacity to 4,646MW against a peak national demand of 2,271MW, creating a surplus of 2,375MW that the government aims to export.

Why it mattersThe commissioning of this hydropower plant directly impacts Tanzanian citizens by providing a more stable and abundant electricity supply, which is expected to reduce power cuts by 85 per cent.

Tanzanian Chief Medical Officer joins WHO Africa health session in Addis Ababa

Dr. Grace Magembe, Tanzania's Chief Medical Officer, arrived in Addis Ababa to participate in the 76th Session of the WHO Regional Committee for Africa, focusing on strengthening.

Dr. Grace Magembe, Tanzania’s Chief Medical Officer and Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Health, has joined the Tanzanian delegation in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, for the 76th Session of the World Health Organization (WHO) Regional Committee for Africa. The meeting, which runs from August 25 to 28, 2026, aims to strengthen health systems and facilitate the exchange of experiences among African countries.

Dr. Magembe was received by Tanzania’s Ambassador to Ethiopia, Innocent Shiyo, and other Tanzanian officials. She is expected to participate in various side meetings and activities centered on health and international cooperation, with the goal of improving health service delivery to citizens.

Tanzania's participation underscores its role in advancing Africa’s health agenda, particularly in maternal, child, and reproductive health. This aligns with President Dr. Samia Suluhu Hassan's appointment as the African Union (AU) Champion for Maternal and Child Health, reflecting the nation's commitment to women's and children's well-being across the continent.

Why it mattersTanzanian citizens, especially women and children, are affected by the discussions and strategies developed at this session, which aim to improve health service delivery and outcomes.

Tanzania Vice President Nchimbi resigns, retires from politics

Emmanuel Nchimbi announced his resignation from the Vice Presidency and public service today, effective September four, 2026, marking the first such departure since independence.

Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi tendered his resignation today, August 25, 2026, through his Instagram account, stating his decision to retire from politics and public service. President Samia Suluhu Hassan has accepted the resignation, which will become effective on September four, 2026. Nchimbi informed the President in a letter that he was fulfilling a promise to step aside if she desired a different Vice President.

The resignation follows reports of a rift between Nchimbi and President Samia, with tensions escalating after Nchimbi's July 24, 2026, speech urging the public to "fight for the new constitution." He was also reportedly summoned before the ruling party's ethics committee during a National Executive Committee meeting in Zanzibar on July 29, 2026.

Political analyst William Maduhu noted that the Vice President's position could become vacant if the sponsoring political party expels the office holder from membership. Nchimbi's announcement on his dormant Instagram account surprised many, as he had not used it for official communications or major milestones since 2023.

Why it mattersThe resignation of the Vice President creates political uncertainty and could lead to shifts in government policy and stability, directly affecting citizens.

Tanzania boosts Lake Victoria investments for economic growth

The Permanent Secretary for East African Affairs, Charles Ngeleja Kadonya, highlighted the Lake Victoria Basin's strategic role in national economic development and regional.

Tanzania is advancing investments in the Lake Victoria Basin to strengthen its economy and East African Community (EAC) integration. Charles Ngeleja Kadonya, Permanent Secretary for East African Affairs, emphasized that the lake offers significant opportunities in trade, investment, transportation, and resource utilization, which can drive economic development in the Lake Zone and the country.

Kadonya made these remarks in Mwanza during a tour to assess various projects related to the Lake Victoria Basin, stressing that these initiatives should directly benefit citizens. He noted that regional projects are crucial for creating employment, increasing incomes, reducing poverty, and improving livelihoods. He also advocated for full Tanzanian participation in these investments to ensure they benefit from the value chain.

During his tour, Kadonya inspected the MV Mwanza – Hapa Kazi Tu vessel, rescue boats, and a rehabilitated research vessel used for fish stock and water quality studies. Mwanza Regional Commissioner Said Mtanda added that the government continues to invest in strategic projects, including upgrading Mwanza Airport and improving port and water transport infrastructure.

Why it mattersCitizens in the Lake Victoria Basin are affected by new employment opportunities and improved livelihoods stemming from increased trade, investment, and transportation projects.

Tanzania seeks global investment to restore degraded land

Tanzania has called on the international community to increase investment, technology, and financial resources to combat desertification and land degradation.

Tanzania's Minister of State, Vice President's Office (Union and Environment), Hamad Masauni, made the appeal at the 17th Conference of Parties to the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD COP17) in Ulaanbaatar. The conference, themed “Restore Land, Restore Hope,” emphasizes increasing investment in land restoration, building resilience to drought, and sustainable management of grazing areas.

Masauni highlighted that land degradation in Tanzania stems from deforestation, agricultural expansion, uncontrolled burning, soil erosion, unsustainable land use, and urban sprawl. He noted that Tanzania's participation in the conference offers opportunities to gain experience, technology, partnerships, and financial resources to improve sustainable management of grazing areas, restore degraded land, conserve forests and water sources, protect biodiversity, and enhance farmers' and pastoralists' resilience to environmental challenges.

Thimoth Mande, Tanzania's National Coordinator for the Convention, stated that international cooperation is crucial for Tanzania to address land degradation, biodiversity loss, and climate change comprehensively. He added that Tanzania's involvement aims to attract more resources from environmental funds like the Global Environment Facility (GEF) and the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD), as well as private sector investment, to expand land restoration projects.

Why it mattersThis story is important because it details Tanzania's efforts to secure international funding and technology to address widespread land degradation, which directly impacts the livelihoods of farmers and pastoralists, and affects the country's natural resources.
