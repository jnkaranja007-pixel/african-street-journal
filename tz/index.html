Education · 2026-11-13

Tanzania plans solar power and clean cooking for 1.4 million poor households

The Light to Learn initiative, presented at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Türkiye, is aimed especially at girls whose homework is squeezed out by household chores.

Tanzania wants to bring solar energy and cleaner cooking technologies to approximately 1.4 million vulnerable households through a proposed initiative called Light to Learn. Mr John Mapunda, a senior community development officer at the Ministry of Community Development, Gender, Women and Special Groups, presented the plan at the Tanzania Pavilion on November 10, 2026.

Mapunda used the example of a 12-year-old girl supported by the Tanzania Social Action Fund (TASAF). She fetches water, collects firewood, cooks and cares for younger siblings and sick relatives, often finishing after dark, when she may have to study by kerosene lamp while her brother has finished his homework in daylight. The initiative's message is that access to light is a right to education.

The proposal uses existing social protection structures to reach the poorest families and treats household electrification, clean cooking and gender equality as one intervention rather than separate concerns.

Why it mattersPoor households with no electricity rely on kerosene lamps, and the plan would replace them with solar power and cleaner cooking for about 1.4 million households.

Climate · 2026-11-13

Samia calls for fair carbon returns as COP31 faces finance gap

Tanzania's president told leaders in Antalya, Türkiye, on November 11 that climate promises must be turned into investments that protect communities.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan asked for fair carbon-market returns and easier access to climate finance at the COP31 conference in Antalya, Türkiye, on November 11. She said prolonged droughts, unpredictable rainfall and rising seas cost Tanzania an estimated two to three per cent of its gross domestic product each year.

Carbon-market opportunities, she said, exist in transport, waste management, renewable energy and forests, which cover about 48.1 million hectares. She called for a transparent carbon market mechanism under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement that would secure fair pricing and shared benefits for communities, and asked that countries' differing development needs and capabilities be taken into account when climate expectations are set.

Tanzania's National Adaptation Plan for 2025–2035, submitted in November 2025, estimates US$37 billion is needed for 36 programmes, including agricultural water security and climate-resilient infrastructure.

Why it mattersClimate shocks cost Tanzania an estimated two to three per cent of its gross domestic product annually, and the country estimates US$37 billion is needed for adaptation programmes.

Agriculture · 2026-11-13

Africa experts push digital tech against fake seeds

African agricultural experts met in Dar es Salaam to discuss using digital technology and biotechnology to improve seed quality and food security.

African agricultural experts are advocating for increased use of digital technology to combat counterfeit seeds, which threaten farmers' productivity and food security. This proposal emerged from the Seventh Steering Group Meeting of the African Seed and Biotechnology Partnership Platform, organised by AUDA-NEPAD and held in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, on November 12, 2026.

Dr James Onsando, an adviser on seed sector issues, stated that digital authentication systems could help farmers identify genuine seeds from fake ones, often sold in imitative packaging. He explained that concealed verification codes on seed packages would allow farmers to confirm authenticity, protecting them from financial losses due to poor germination and harvests from counterfeit seeds.

Delegates also explored biotechnology's role in developing higher-yielding seed varieties resilient to climate change, pests, and diseases. However, expanding access requires investment in research, supportive policies, and regulatory frameworks. These discussions align with the Comprehensive Africa Agriculture Development Programme's 2026–2035 agenda, which aims to significantly increase agricultural production and investment across the continent.

Why it mattersFarmers in Tanzania and across Africa face financial losses from counterfeit seeds, and digital authentication systems are being proposed to help them identify genuine products.

Health · 2026-11-13

Tanzania, Nordic nations discuss AI in health

Tanzania and Nordic countries will collaborate with Muhimbili University to improve health systems using AI technology.

Tanzania and Nordic countries, including Sweden, Finland, Norway, and Denmark, have discussed using Artificial Intelligence (AI) and innovative technologies to enhance health systems. The collaboration will involve Muhimbili University of Health and Allied Sciences in improving these systems. The government emphasized the importance of testing the safety, quality, and effectiveness of these technologies before public use.

Dr. Vivian Wonanji, Head of Health Sector Improvement, stated that the government uses pilot studies to gather evidence on technology quality, effectiveness, and user acceptance, considering sustainability from the outset. She added that all key stakeholders, including central and local governments, development partners, experts, higher learning institutions, research bodies, and citizens, are involved from the beginning to ensure informed decision-making on adopting innovations.

The discussion focused on the Quadruple Helix Model, integrating government, academia, private sector, and society to develop technological solutions. Participants explored combining academic knowledge, investment, policies, and citizen needs to address real health challenges. AI applications include analyzing patient data, aiding disease diagnosis, improving hospital management, and supporting healthcare professionals' decisions. The government plans to fund beneficial technologies through public budgets and health insurance to reduce reliance on donor funding and direct patient payments.

Why it mattersThe government plans to integrate beneficial health technologies into public budgets and the health insurance system, reducing patient costs.

Culture · 2026-11-13

Tatunane band pioneered global Tanzanian music

The 1980s band fused American and Tanzanian sounds, winning international awards but gaining little local traction.

Tatunane, a Tanzanian band formed in the late 1980s, pioneered the fusion of American rock, funk, and Tanzanian folk music. The group, which included lead singer Abdalla Omar “Dulla” Mnanga and penny whistler Omari Naliene, drew members from the Bagamoyo College of Arts. They were the first Tanzanian band to achieve global acclaim, blending Swahili poetry with tribal dances.

The band gained international recognition in 1991 with their debut album AfroBrazz, winning the prestigious Prix Découvertes award from Radio France. Despite their global success, band member Seif Rengwe noted that the local audience did not embrace their music. Tatunane ceased to exist by the mid-1990s, partly due to the death of penny whistler Omari Naliene.

Tatunane's contributions are often overlooked as contemporary Bongo Flava music celebrates its 30th anniversary. The band's innovative Bingo Fusion style, which captivated international audiences, contrasts with Bongo Flava's current widespread domestic and global appeal. Tatunane's legacy as pioneers of American-influenced music fusion in Tanzania remains largely unacknowledged.

Why it mattersThe legacy of Tatunane, Tanzania's first band to win international music awards, is being re-evaluated as modern Bongo Flava music gains global recognition.
