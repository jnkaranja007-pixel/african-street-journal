Tanzania opposition party mourns 500 killed in 2025 election crisis

CHADEMA held a memorial event in Dar es Salaam for victims of the violence that followed the October 29, 2025, elections.

Tanzania's opposition party, CHADEMA, held a national memorial event on November 3, 2026, in Dar es Salaam to remember over 500 people killed during the 2025 election crisis. The party announced a seven-day mourning period, flying flags at half-mast. The ceremony included prayers, poetry, candle lighting, and laying flowers.

Party communications personnel Brenda Rupia read a message stating, “The lives of our loved ones were of great value.” The message honored those lost and affirmed the party's commitment to truth, dignity, and justice. Vice Chairperson John Heche addressed the difficult historical period and claims of leadership blame for the crisis.

A Commission of Inquiry, chaired by retired Chief Justice Mohamed Chande Othman, investigated the violence and presented findings on April 23, 2026. The commission reported 518 deaths and 2,390 injuries, with evidence suggesting planned and coordinated execution by organised groups motivated by grievances or financial promises.

Why it mattersTanzanian citizens, particularly families who lost loved ones or suffered injuries during the 2025 election crisis, are directly affected by the ongoing remembrance and the findings of the Commission of Inquiry.

Tanzania seeks AfDB funding for new power line

The government discussed financing for a 400kV transmission line from Kisongo to Segera with the African Development Bank.

The Tanzanian government, through the Ministry of Energy and TANESCO, met with the African Development Bank (AfDB) in Dodoma to discuss financing for energy projects. Key among these is a proposed 400-kilovolt (400kV) power transmission line stretching from Kisongo through Same to Segera.

Commissioner for Electricity and Renewable Energy, Engineer Innocent Luoga, chaired the talks, emphasizing the need to expand transmission infrastructure to meet rising energy demand. The project aims to strengthen the national power system, increase transmission capacity, and ensure reliable electricity for homes and businesses, particularly in Arusha and Tanga regions.

Discussions also covered expanding the Lemugur power substation in Arusha and building new substations in Same and Segera. The meeting explored financing arrangements and implementation preparations for these initiatives.

Why it mattersTanzania is seeking funding for a new power line to improve electricity supply for households and economic activities.

MPs ask Tanzania to guarantee Lissu fair trial

Lawmakers from six countries and the EU urged President Hassan to rule out the death penalty for Tundu Lissu.

23 parliamentarians from six countries and the European Union have asked Tanzanian President Samia Suluhu Hassan to guarantee a fair trial for CHADEMA chairperson Tundu Lissu and rule out the death penalty. The appeal, published by British MP Alicia Kearns on November 3, comes ahead of the High Court's treason verdict scheduled for Friday, November 6, 2026.

The lawmakers' letter, dated November 2, seeks assurances for Lissu and protection for peaceful political opposition, stating that political disagreement should not be criminalised. Dar es Salaam police, however, warned on November 3 of alleged plans for disorder on judgement day, as they monitor reports of mobilisation for court attendance. Lissu’s family is preparing to attend court before travelling to Arusha for his elder brother's funeral.

The appeal references a February finding by the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention that Lissu’s detention was arbitrary and calls for his release. It also cites a July recommendation from the Commonwealth Ministerial Action Group for his detention to be resolved within 30 days, a deadline that has passed. President Hassan, on November 2, instructed the human-rights commission to respond to foreign allegations while investigating domestic conditions.

Why it mattersTanzanian opposition leader Tundu Lissu faces a treason verdict on November 6, with international lawmakers seeking assurances of a fair trial and no death penalty.

Tanzania strengthens food security efforts

Officials and stakeholders emphasize resilient food systems beyond just increasing crop production.

Tanzania is enhancing its approach to sustainable food security by focusing on improved seed varieties, agricultural innovation, and stronger extension services. The government is collaborating with the private sector, research institutions, development partners, and farmers to achieve this goal. Stakeholders agree that food security requires more than just increasing crop yields; it necessitates developing resilient food systems capable of facing climate change, pests, and diseases.

Dr. Wilhelm Mafuru, CEO of the National Agricultural Extension Services Agency (NAESA), stated that food security now means ensuring food systems are resilient and serve all communities, not just increasing production. He highlighted that agriculture is vital to Tanzania's economy and livelihoods, with millions depending on it. Dr. Mafuru noted that climate variability, rising input costs, and market disruptions threaten productivity and farmer incomes, underscoring the need for a systemic approach to food security.

Building these resilient food systems involves coordinated efforts such as providing quality seeds, timely extension services, reliable markets, and adequate storage. NAESA, under the Ministry of Agriculture, aims to improve extension services. Bayer Life Science (T) Ltd has promoted drought-tolerant seeds, helping farmers adapt to changing climates. Bayer Communications Business Partner for Crop Science in Africa, Ms. Lisa Bohlmann, noted that Tanzanian smallholder farmers need better access to quality inputs, knowledge, and modern technologies.

Why it mattersTanzanian farmers face challenges from climate variability, pests, and market disruptions that threaten their productivity and incomes.

Tanzania opens applications for boxing commission leadership

The National Sports Council is accepting applications until November 17th for eight leadership positions within the TPBRC.

The National Sports Council of Tanzania (NSC) has opened applications for leadership roles at the Tanzania Professional Boxing Regulatory Commission (TPBRC). Interested candidates have until November 17th to submit nomination forms for positions including president, vice-president, six zonal representatives, and one women’s representative. Elections are scheduled for November 24th in Dar es Salaam, following candidate interviews on November 20th.

Application fees range from 1m/- for presidential and vice-presidential hopefuls to 200,000/- for zonal and women’s representative candidates. Eligibility criteria for the top two posts require a bachelor’s degree and five years of leadership experience, with former boxers needing three years post-retirement. Zonal representatives need primary education and three years of sports leadership experience in Tanzania.

The zonal positions cover Northern, Southern Highlands, Western, Lake, and Central zones, with specific regions listed for each. All candidates must be Tanzanian citizens aged 18-75 and have no criminal convictions, particularly those involving prohibited substances or illegal sporting methods. Application forms are available at NSC offices and on the council’s website.

Why it mattersTanzanian citizens seeking to lead professional boxing in the country must meet specific educational, experience, and financial requirements by November 17th.
