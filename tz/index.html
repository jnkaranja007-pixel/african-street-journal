Tanzania minister meets King Charles III at CHOGM

Ambassador Mahmoud Thabit Kombo discussed investment and economic growth with leaders in Antigua.

Tanzania's Minister for Foreign Affairs, Ambassador Mahmoud Thabit Kombo, attended the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM) in Antigua and Barbuda. The meeting aimed to boost investment, economic resilience, and job creation across member states, with discussions covering infrastructure, renewable energy, agriculture, and climate change effects. Ambassador Kombo represented President Samia Suluhu Hassan at the event.

On the sidelines of the CHOGM, Ambassador Kombo held discussions with King Charles III of the United Kingdom, British Prime Minister Andy Burnham, and Antigua and Barbuda Prime Minister Gaston Browne. Tanzania used the platform to strengthen international relations and advance economic diplomacy, seeking to attract investment and promote economic growth. The country also highlighted the importance of private sector involvement in its National Development Vision 2050.

The meeting brought together leaders of Commonwealth member states and company chief executives to explore opportunities for promoting investment and strengthening economic resilience. Participants emphasized practical cooperation between governments and investors, alongside greater private sector participation in member states' development.

Why it mattersTanzania's participation in the CHOGM aimed to attract investment and promote economic growth, benefiting the country's development goals.

Tanzania eyes global energy investment at ADIPEC

The nation aims to attract capital for its 57.54 trillion cubic feet of natural gas and renewable energy projects.

Tanzania is seeking global investment for its energy sector, with Energy Minister Dr Juma Homera attending the 2026 Abu Dhabi International Petroleum Exhibition and Conference (ADIPEC) in the UAE. Dr Homera stated that the country has developed strategies to attract both domestic and foreign capital, highlighting energy security as a global priority. Tanzania possesses approximately 57.54 trillion cubic feet of natural gas resources suitable for Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) and Mini-LNG projects.

Further investment opportunities include the 2,115-megawatt Julius Nyerere Hydropower Project, the 150MW Kishapu Solar Power Project, wind energy in Singida Region, and geothermal resources in Mbeya and Songwe regions. Dr Homera noted Tanzania's potential to become a regional energy trading hub due to its strategic location. He stressed the need for predictable policies, strong legal frameworks, and reduced bureaucracy to boost competitiveness.

The government is implementing the National Energy Compact to mobilize approximately 4.039bn US dollars in private sector investment, focusing on renewables, transmission infrastructure, and Public-Private Partnerships. These efforts aim to transform energy resources into viable projects, attract international investors, and strengthen Tanzania's global energy market position.

Why it mattersTanzania is actively seeking foreign capital for its energy sector development, including natural gas and renewable projects.

Tanzania plans cold rooms to cut crop waste

The government is exploring simple technology to preserve perishable produce like bananas and vegetables.

The Tanzanian government is considering building cold rooms in markets nationwide to reduce post-harvest losses, Agriculture Minister Daniel Chongolo announced on Saturday, October 31, 2026. The initiative, part of the Sh700 million (US$264,835) Mamsera project, aims to use cost-effective technology to preserve perishable goods such as bananas, fruits, and vegetables. This will allow farmers more time to sell their produce instead of losing it to spoilage.

Chongolo stated that the facilities would help store bananas showing signs of spoilage safely, preventing the waste of invested time and resources. Jenifa Audiface, a local trader and farmer, noted that the current lack of cold storage forces farmers to sell harvests at low prices to avoid spoilage. Deputy Permanent Secretary Prof Peter Msofe confirmed the Mamsera Market construction is 70% complete and expected to improve market access for banana and avocado farmers.

The Minister also directed construction to begin at the Holili International Grain Market by April 2027, with a design including space for a fruit and vegetable market. Rombo MP Prof Adolf Mkenda said this would provide access to markets like Kenya, enabling farmers to earn dollars from exports, such as Tanzanian bananas sold in Dubai.

Why it mattersFarmers will benefit from reduced crop spoilage and improved market access.

Alute Lissu, constitutional lawyer, dies after long illness

The brother of opposition figure Tundu Lissu was admitted to Sani Hospital before his death on Sunday.

Alute Lissu, a constitutional and administrative lawyer, died early Sunday after a long illness, his family announced. He had been admitted to Sani Hospital in Arusha where his condition worsened. His family expects the burial to take place in Singida, but will announce further arrangements later.

Vincent Lissu Mughwai, Alute’s younger brother, described the deceased as a father figure and role model, particularly to Tundu Lissu, the CHADEMA Chairperson. Relatives were travelling to Ukonga Prison to inform Tundu of his brother's death. Alute Lissu leaves five children; his wife died many years ago.

The death occurred shortly after a profile based on Alute’s final interview with The Chanzo, conducted on October 8. The interview, which lasted over three hours, began at his Arusha home and covered his four decades in legal practice. Alute Lissu graduated from the University of Dar es Salaam’s Faculty of Law in 1982.

Why it mattersThe death of Alute Lissu, a prominent lawyer and brother to opposition leader Tundu Lissu, affects his immediate family and the legal community.

Tanzania mobilizes funds for 83bn/- Babati market

The new market in Manyara Region will accommodate 810 traders, replacing an overcrowded 1977 facility.

The Tanzanian government has begun preparing to raise funds for a new modern market in Babati Town Council, Manyara Region. Deputy Minister Festo Dugange stated in Parliament on November 3, 2026, that the market will house 810 traders and cost an estimated 83bn/-.

The current market, built in 1977, is severely overcrowded, serving 210 traders instead of its intended 91. A 54,437.5-square-metre plot at Maisaka has been allocated for the new construction. Local government authorities are directed to allocate five percent of uncommitted revenue towards market construction.

The Babati Town Council must submit a project proposal to TAMISEMI for assessment and budget preparation to enable phased construction. Deputy Minister Dugange also offered to inspect the existing market with Babati Town MP Emmanuel Kambai.

Why it matters810 traders in Babati Town Council will have a new, modern facility to conduct their business, replacing an overcrowded and outdated market.
