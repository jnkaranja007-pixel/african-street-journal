EU Approves Sh462 Billion for Tanzania, Targets Private Sector

The European Union has allocated Sh462.8 billion for Tanzania, with funds directed towards private sector initiatives rather than direct government budget support.

The European Union has approved a Sh462.8 billion package for Tanzania, a decision that will channel support through partners and modalities benefiting the private sector. This funding, approved on July 24, 2026, allocates €129 million for mineral and agricultural value chains, €20 million for digital economy and connectivity, and €7 million for a cooperation facility. The EU Delegation confirmed that the funds align with the Global Gateway strategy and DIRA 2050, emphasizing mutual benefit for Tanzanian and EU interests.

This approach marks a shift from previous direct budget support to the government, which has been discontinued due to a reported deterioration in human rights, democracy, and the rule of law following the October 2025 election. The EU stated that future cooperation depth will depend on Tanzania's progress in restoring respect for human rights and the rule of law, and that support will avoid government systems.

The EU's strategy prioritizes mobilizing private investment in sectors like digital, energy, and transport, creating opportunities for European companies. This model was previously used in 2024 when the EU blended a €15 million grant with European Investment Bank credit lines to support small and medium-sized businesses, aiming to reduce lending risks for local banks.

Why it mattersTanzanian businesses and agricultural value chains will benefit from new EU funding channeled through private sector initiatives.

Tanzania Vice President Challenges Banks on Startup Success Metrics

Vice President Ndejembi urged financial institutions and support organizations to prioritize durable businesses and job creation over mere idea testing.

Vice President Deogratius Ndejembi has called on banks, investors, and startup support organizations in Tanzania to shift their focus from the quantity of tested ideas to the quality of businesses that emerge. Speaking at the opening of Tanzania Startup Week 2026 in Dar es Salaam, Ndejembi emphasized that success should be measured by the creation of durable companies, job opportunities, and customer reach, rather than just the number of innovations put through incubation or testing phases.

The five-day event, themed "Building Tanzania’s Startup Economy," brings together various stakeholders to discuss critical aspects of the startup ecosystem, including capital, market access, policy, and job creation. Ndejembi's remarks followed an announcement by NMB Bank, the event's main sponsor, detailing its Sh2 billion allocation for tech startups and the testing of over 300 innovations since 2021. However, the bank reported integrating only six startups into its systems, with a goal to reach 30 by 2030.

Ndejembi acknowledged NMB's initiative but pressed for concrete evidence of disbursed financing and actual business expansion post-support. He identified risk, stemming from businesses lacking conventional collateral, as a significant challenge, rather than a lack of capital. A Ministry of Communication and Information Technology policy document highlights a weak formal finance ecosystem, collateral barriers, and limited investment readiness as obstacles to digital innovation.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the Vice President is questioning the effectiveness of capital deployment and highlighting risks that hinder the growth of startups into sustainable businesses.

Tanzania Envoy Presents Credentials, Seeks Mining Investment

Tanzania's High Commissioner to Australia, Macocha Moshe Tembele, presented his credentials and discussed strengthening bilateral ties, particularly in the mining sector.

Tanzania's High Commissioner to Australia, Macocha Moshe Tembele, formally presented his Letters of Credence to Governor-General Sam Mostyn on October 20, 2026, in Canberra. During their discussion, Governor-General Mostyn acknowledged Tanzania's historical contributions and expressed satisfaction with the strong bilateral relationship, highlighting cooperation in the mining sector.

Governor-General Mostyn encouraged increased engagement with Australia's mining industry, noting significant interest from Australian companies in Tanzania's investment climate. She also welcomed the rise in Australian tourism to Tanzania and called for enhanced cooperation to facilitate travel for visitors. Ambassador Tembele thanked the Governor-General and congratulated Australia on the election of Milton Dick as President of the Inter-Parliamentary Union.

Ambassador Tembele commended Australian investments in Tanzania, especially in mining, and stated his intention to visit Perth to meet with interested companies. He also requested Australia to expand scholarship and capacity-building programs for Tanzanians, underscoring a desire for deeper collaboration across various sectors.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by discussions aimed at strengthening cooperation in the mining sector and highlighting favorable investment opportunities in Tanzania.

WorldFish Initiative Targets 25,000 Jobs in Tanzania

A new WorldFish project aims to create thousands of jobs in Tanzania's fisheries and aquaculture sectors by 2028.

An international research organization, WorldFish, has launched an initiative targeting the creation of 25,000 jobs for women and young people in Tanzania by June 2028. The project, running from July 2026 to June 2028, focuses on fisheries, aquaculture, feed innovation, and sustainable aquatic food systems. It aims to expand economic opportunities across the fish value chain by improving productivity, reducing post-harvest losses, and supporting small businesses.

The initiative seeks to address challenges faced by women and young people who dominate fish processing, trading, and distribution but remain in informal, lower-income activities. WorldFish plans to improve access to technical skills, financing, equipment, technology, and markets. This will enable participants to establish viable enterprises and secure more sustainable incomes, contributing to the broader Women and Youth Economic Empowerment in Fisheries Program.

WorldFish will promote affordable fish feed production through lower-cost formulations and alternative ingredients, aiming to reduce farmer expenses and create business opportunities in feed manufacturing. The project also includes strengthening hatcheries, improving fish seed systems, promoting climate-smart aquaculture, and training farmers and service providers. Interventions will also target fish processing and preservation with improved technologies and cold-chain systems to reduce spoilage.

Why it mattersWomen and young people in Tanzania will gain access to skills, financing, and markets to establish viable enterprises and secure more sustainable incomes.

Tanzania Seeks Investors for Nala Techno Valley Project

The nation is leveraging an AI conference in South Korea to attract investment for a new technology hub in Dodoma.

Tanzania is actively seeking investors and development partners for its Nala Techno Valley project during an international Artificial Intelligence conference in Seoul, Republic of Korea. Deputy Minister for Communication and Information Technology Dr. Switbert Mkama is leading the Tanzanian delegation at the event, which focuses on how AI and digital technologies can drive economic growth and improve public services. The proposed techno valley, planned for Nala in Dodoma Region, has been allocated approximately 400 acres by the government to serve as a hub for AI, robotics, data processing, and digital skills training.

Dr. Mkama highlighted Tanzania's commitment to developing technology that addresses citizen needs and local contexts during a discussion on AI in public services. He emphasized the country's ongoing efforts to collaborate with international entities and technology firms to secure investment, expertise, and technological solutions essential for its digital transformation. The conference provides a platform for Tanzania to showcase its vision for technological advancement and innovation.

The Nala Techno Valley project aims to establish a significant technology and innovation center, fostering startup development and providing specialized digital skills training. By participating in this global AI forum, Tanzania aims to forge partnerships that will accelerate the realization of this ambitious project and contribute to the nation's broader digital agenda.

Why it mattersInvestors are sought to fund the development of a technology and innovation hub in Tanzania.
