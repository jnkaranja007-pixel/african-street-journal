Nchimbi Resigns as Vice President, Expelled from Ruling Party

Emmanuel Nchimbi's resignation as Vice President and subsequent expulsion from the CCM party follows reports of disagreements with President Samia and mirrors a past high-profile.

Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi resigned on August 25, 2026, and was subsequently expelled from the ruling Chama Cha Mapinduzi (CCM) party for disciplinary and ethical violations. Attorney General Hamza Johari confirmed Nchimbi ceased to be Vice President after losing his CCM membership. The party's Women's Wing (UWT) congratulated Deogratius Ndejembi on his appointment as the new Vice President, stating the decision reflects the party's confidence in his integrity and commitment.

Nchimbi's departure follows a period of reported internal conflict within the CCM, with his resignation letter citing a desire to retire from public service. However, the party's swift expulsion, which occurred the day after his resignation, suggests a more contentious exit. This situation is reminiscent of Aboud Jumbe's departure from multiple high-profile positions four decades ago, which historian Issa Shivji described as a "trial" related to political disagreements over the Union structure.

The events surrounding Nchimbi's resignation have exposed internal divisions within the CCM. Just days before his resignation, the investigative newspaper Mwanahalisi was suspended for reporting that Nchimbi was resisting pressure to resign due to disagreements with President Samia. The UWT chairperson, Mary Chatanda, emphasized the importance of party discipline, stating that no member is above the CCM and all must adhere to its constitution and procedures.

Why it mattersThe resignation and expulsion of Vice President Emmanuel Nchimbi and the subsequent appointment of a new Vice President directly impacts the political leadership and stability within Tanzania's ruling party.

Samia Urges Egypt to Deepen Trade and Investment Ties

President Samia Suluhu Hassan met with the outgoing Egyptian Ambassador, Sherif Ismail, to discuss strengthening economic cooperation and infrastructure development.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan has called for increased trade and investment with Egypt, following a farewell meeting with Ambassador Sherif Ismail. The President highlighted the importance of deepening economic ties to maximize opportunities and benefit both Tanzanian and Egyptian citizens. Discussions also centered on improving infrastructure and logistics to facilitate trade and expand access to other African markets.

During Ambassador Ismail's four-year tenure, Tanzania and Egypt have expanded cooperation across multiple sectors, including agriculture, energy, health, education, tourism, and transport. President Samia expressed gratitude for Egypt's continued provision of scholarships and capacity-building programs in education, as well as discussions on strengthening health sector cooperation through professional training and medical equipment access.

The meeting underscored the mutual interest in sustaining and expanding cooperation in various areas to bolster diplomatic and economic relations. Both leaders emphasized the need for continued collaboration to achieve greater economic benefits and enhance regional trade connectivity through improved infrastructure and logistics services.

Why it mattersTanzanian farmers and businesses could benefit from increased trade and investment opportunities with Egypt, as well as improved logistics for market access.

Tanzania Health Summit Gathers 2,000 Delegates for System Reforms

The 13th Tanzania Health Summit will convene over 2,000 participants from more than 25 countries to discuss health sovereignty and system strengthening.

Over two thousand delegates are expected to attend the 13th Tanzania Health Summit (THS) in Dar es Salaam from October 5 to 7. The event, themed “Building Health Sovereignty and Strengthening Health Systems in a Changing Global Order,” aims to foster deliberations on improving health systems. Participants will include government leaders, healthcare professionals, policymakers, entrepreneurs, and young change-makers from over 25 countries.

The summit will feature more than 70 exhibitions and 40 institutional forums, alongside the THS Health Workers Awards recognizing outstanding individuals. Programs like Youth Capacity Building (YOCAB) and CHIA Innovation Awards will also contribute to strengthening healthcare systems. THS President Dr. Omary Chillo emphasized the event's role in empowering nations to reduce foreign dependency and control medical supplies, reinforcing local workforces against global shocks.

Dr. Chillo highlighted the summit's success over 12 years in convening over 10,000 stakeholders and shaping reforms such as the Universal Health Insurance Act and Integrated Community Health Workers Programs. Organizations like the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA), World Bicycle Relief & Buffalo Bicycles, and Ifakara Health Institute (IHI) will present projects and invite new partnerships.

Why it mattersThe summit aims to strengthen health systems and achieve health sovereignty, which could impact the availability and control of medical supplies and services for Tanzanians.

Lissu Declares No Faith in Judges for Election Disputes

Tundu Lissu told the High Court he has "absolutely no faith" in courts for electoral matters, citing democratic alternatives for reform during his treason trial cross-examination.

Tundu Lissu stated in the High Court on Thursday that he lacks faith in the judicial system for election disputes, preferring what he termed democratic alternatives for reform. This declaration came as state attorneys continued to cross-examine him in his ongoing treason trial. Lissu defended his campaign slogan, "No Reforms, No Election," and expressions like "kukinukisha" and "mobilising rebellion," asserting they are democratic methods to oppose an electoral process he deems unfair. He drew parallels to Mwalimu Nyerere's views on legitimate rebellion against oppressive systems.

The exchange occurred on the second day of the prosecution's cross-examination, with Lissu representing himself. He had previously challenged the reliability of state witnesses and argued the prosecution failed to prove intent or publication of a disputed video. Lissu acknowledged delivering a speech to CHADEMA aspirants in April 2025, with journalists present, and later requesting their departure for a private session, maintaining that essential information had already been shared publicly.

Prosecutors pressed Lissu on the distinction between public and private portions of the meeting and the absence of the video from court evidence. Lissu confirmed journalists remained for parts he wanted published, while private discussions occurred separately. He maintained that the alleged words in the video, central to the charge, were both heard and seen, questioning the prosecution's ability to proceed without the recording itself.

Why it mattersFarmers and investors may be impacted by the political instability and potential for unrest arising from disputes over electoral processes and the rule of law.

Tanzania and DR Congo Agree to Repatriate Congolese Refugees

Tanzania, DR Congo, and UNHCR will begin a voluntary repatriation process for Congolese refugees in Tanzania who wish to return home.

Tanzania and the Democratic Republic of Congo, in conjunction with the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR), have agreed to initiate the voluntary repatriation of Congolese refugees currently residing in Tanzania. Deputy Minister of Home Affairs, Ayoub Mohammed Mahmoud, announced this decision during a Tripartite Commission Meeting in Lubumbashi. The process will prioritize the safety and dignity of refugees choosing to return, with Tanzania assuring it will not pressure or unduly influence their decision.

For those refugees not yet ready to return to the DRC, Tanzania will continue to provide shelter and services within its available resources, particularly at the Nyarugusu Refugee Camp in Kigoma region. The Deputy Minister emphasized that the commencement of voluntary repatriation will not impede Tanzania's ongoing commitment to accepting new asylum seekers fleeing insecurity in parts of the DRC.

The agreement also includes a call for DRC leaders to visit the Nyarugusu Camp to engage directly with their citizens, understand their needs, and foster a sense of belonging. These visits are intended to build relationships and hope, not to persuade refugees to return. Both nations have committed to creating an enabling environment for refugees who opt to go back to reunite with families and contribute to their nation's development.

Why it mattersCongolese refugees in Tanzania face a decision about returning home, impacting their current living situation and future prospects.
