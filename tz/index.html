Samia consults party leaders on commission for constitutional changes

CHADEMA stayed away from the Dar es Salaam meeting, where council members proposed equal representation for both sides of the Union and women.

President Samia Suluhu Hassan met political party leaders at the Dar es Salaam State House on November 16 to discuss forming a Reconciliation and Mediation Commission that would coordinate preparations for constitutional amendments. Tanzania’s main opposition party, CHADEMA, did not attend.

The presidency said members of the Council of Political Parties recommended that the proposed commission represent both sides of the Union and ensure gender equality. The commission has not been established, and its membership and working arrangements have not been announced.

Samia said consultations would continue with political stakeholders, civil institutions and the private sector. In July, CHADEMA deputy chairperson John Heche said the party’s Central Committee required national chairperson Tundu Lissu’s release and a mediator before talks could begin.

Why it mattersThe proposed commission would coordinate preparations for constitutional amendments, while CHADEMA has said Lissu’s release and a mediator are conditions for talks.

TOL Gases lists shares after raising 10.57bn/- in rights issue

The 129 per cent subscription will help fund a carbon dioxide plant and infrastructure expected to cost 15.7bn/-.

TOL Gases PLC began trading newly issued shares on the Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange after a rights issue raised 10.57bn/-, exceeding its 8.17bn/- target. The proceeds are earmarked mainly for a new carbon dioxide plant and supporting infrastructure.

Company chairman Leonard Kitoka said the issue reflected shareholders’ confidence in TOL Gases’ management and growth prospects. Managing director Daniel Warungu said completing the plant by the second quarter of 2027 was the company’s immediate priority; the project is expected to cost 15.7bn/-.

The company expects the plant to increase production capacity and improve supply reliability. The listing brings TOL Gases’ total issued ordinary shares to approximately 69 million, which investors can trade on the secondary market, according to Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange chief business development officer Emmanuel Nyalali.

Why it mattersTOL Gases shareholders can trade the newly listed shares, while the company is due to commission its 15.7bn/- plant in the second quarter of 2027.

Tanzania opens Lindi boys’ school under SEQUIP

The school is one of seven regional boys’ secondary schools, easing the need for families in Lindi and Mtwara to send sons far away for education.

Tanzania’s Secondary Education Quality Improvement Project has established seven regional boys’ secondary schools, including Lindi Boys’ Secondary School in the country’s south. The school began classes in July 2026 with 23 Form Five students studying science combinations.

Headmaster Fransico Zebedayo said the school gives families in Lindi and Mtwara a local boarding option, after some had sent children to distant regions for secondary education. He said parents had previously faced the cost of sending their children elsewhere, while such schools had been built exclusively for girls.

The students include 11 taking Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics, eight studying Chemistry, Biology and Geography, and four pursuing Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Form Five student Raheem Kondo said boarding allows students to spend more time studying and have teachers available for closer supervision.

Why it mattersFamilies in Lindi and Mtwara now have a regional boys’ boarding school, reducing the need to pay to send children to distant regions.

Business · 2026-11-18

NBC launches campaign with TSh 4 million grand prizes for digital payments

The three-month programme runs through February 16, 2027, and includes rewards for customers and 150 merchant cashiers.

National Bank of Commerce (NBC) launched the second edition of its Tabasamu Tukupe Mashavu campaign in Dar es Salaam, with the programme running from November 17, 2026, to February 16, 2027. The bank is promoting card, mobile and other digital payments during the festive season.

NBC said nine grand-prize winners will each receive TSh 4 million: five will be chosen through card transactions and four through Lipa Kiganjani payments. The campaign also offers weekly and monthly draws, and 150 cashiers using NBC point-of-sale terminals can earn rewards tied to growth in transaction value, Head of Transactions Mangire Kibanda said.

Customers can enter through NBC cards, its Kiganjani mobile service, Lipa Kiganjani, NBC agents and ATMs. The Gaming Board of Tanzania will supervise all prize draws, while customers can open accounts through the Kiganjani app after registration and verification.

Why it mattersThe programme offers nine winners TSh 4 million each and rewards 150 merchant cashiers according to growth in transactions processed through NBC terminals.

Karimjee Foundation and Glasgow mark 10 years of Tanzania conservation scholarships

The programme has supported 25 Tanzanians, and every graduate is now working in conservation in Tanzania, the foundation’s chair said.

The Karimjee Foundation and the University of Glasgow marked 10 years of their conservation scholarship programme at a gathering in Dar es Salaam held over the weekend. The programme funds Tanzanians to take a master’s degree in African Ecosystem Conservation Management at Glasgow.

Foundation chair Yusuf Karimjee said students spend one year studying in Glasgow and a second year on practical training in Tanzania, including a long research project. The foundation covers tuition and living allowances and offers up to two scholarships each year. Karimjee said 25 Tanzanians, past and present, have taken part, and every graduate now works in conservation in Tanzania.

Karimjee said 11 participants have gone on to PhD studies, while the group has published 16 scientific papers and made more than 20 conference presentations. Graduates teach conservation scientists and contribute research used in wildlife management and community safety, he said. He urged alumni, partners and university staff to discuss the programme’s next 10 years.

Why it mattersThe foundation pays tuition and living allowances for up to two students each year, while graduates work in conservation in Tanzania.
