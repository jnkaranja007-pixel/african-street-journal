Tanzania aims for top 10 global food producer status

Prime Minister Nchemba announced the goal at the Borlaug Dialogue in Des Moines, USA, citing Vision 2050.

Prime Minister Mwigulu Nchemba stated Tanzania's ambition to join the world's top 10 food-producing nations by 2050. This goal is part of Tanzania’s Development Vision 2050, aiming for leadership in African food production. Nchemba announced this during the Norman Borlaug International Dialogue in Des Moines, United States.

Tanzania has achieved a 130 per cent food self-sufficiency level through its agricultural initiatives. Agricultural exports increased from US$2.1bn in 2021/2022 to US$3.7bn in 2024/2025. Agriculture contributes 24.6 per cent to Tanzania’s GDP and employs 65 per cent of the workforce.

Nchemba urged increased investment in farmers, science, youth, women, and resilient food systems to support Africa's agricultural potential. He stressed the need for action and innovation to build food systems for present and future generations, under the theme 'Before Hunger Defeats Us'.

Why it mattersTanzania aims to become a top global food producer, impacting farmers and the national economy.

Tanzania expands health insurance to all income groups

The government launched the second phase of its Universal Health Insurance scheme, aiming to cover all citizens regardless of their ability to pay.

Tanzania's Health Minister Mohamed Mchengerwa launched the second registration phase of the Universal Health Insurance scheme in Mtwara on October 23, 2026. This expansion opens enrolment to all income groups, including those who can finance their own membership, moving beyond the initial focus on the poorest households. The initiative aims to ensure that a person's ability to pay does not prevent them from seeking necessary medical treatment.

Health Permanent Secretary Dr Grace Magembe stated that the scheme is now open to everyone, encompassing both those who can afford their own cover and those classified by the government as unable to pay. Mtwara was chosen for the national launch due to its leading performance in the first phase's enrolment. The Universal Health Insurance Act, 2023, makes insurance compulsory and mandates employer registration for workers in formal and informal employment.

The first phase, which began on January 26, 2026, targeted 276,004 households identified as unable to afford insurance, with the government allocating Sh48.8 billion. While 997,015 beneficiaries were registered, only 495,034 had collected membership cards, highlighting the need for the second phase to ensure usable cover. The Ministry of Health's 2026/27 budget statement indicates phase two will also register an additional 589,772 households unable to pay.

Why it mattersAll Tanzanian citizens will now have access to health insurance, regardless of their income level, to prevent financial hardship from hindering medical treatment.

Health · 2026-10-24

Muhas trains 27 health workers in dialysis

The five-week hemodialysis training at Muhas aimed to improve kidney patient care across Tanzania.

Muhimbili University of Health and Allied Sciences (MUHAS) in Dar es Salaam has completed its fifth hemodialysis training for 27 health professionals. The participants, including doctors, clinical officers, and nurses, underwent the short course which began on September 14, 2026, and concluded on October 23, 2026. This training is part of MUHAS's efforts to enhance the skills of healthcare providers in treating patients with kidney failure.

Meshack Shimwela, Registrar of Private Health Facilities, stated that the training is an important step in improving healthcare services and patient efficiency, noting that Tanzania now has 77 hemodialysis centers, with 29 government-owned. Professor Emmy Meta, MUHAS Director of Continuing Education, reported that 105 health professionals have been trained across various regions since the program's inception, with this latest cohort including two doctors, 17 clinical officers, and eight nurses from Dar es Salaam, Arusha, Morogoro, and Kagera.

The training involved classroom theory, practical skills in MUHAS's lab, and five weeks of clinical practice. Practical sessions took place at Muhimbili National Hospital and five Africa Healthcare Network (AHN) dialysis centers in Dar es Salaam. These centers include Shree Hindu Mandal Hospital, Cardinal Rugambwa Hospital, CCBRT, E.M. Hospital, and Shree Hindu Mandal Super Specialized Polyclinic. Professor Meta emphasized the importance of follow-up to assess the practical application of learned skills.

Why it mattersTanzanians with kidney disease will receive improved care from newly trained healthcare professionals.

Tanzania minister orders review of Mbeya transport routes

Bajaji drivers and vendors resume work after a four-day protest over market and route changes in Mbeya.

Bajaji drivers and vendors in Mbeya, Tanzania, returned to work on October 23 after a four-day protest that disrupted transport and trade. The stoppage ended following a directive from Prof Riziki Shemdoe, the Minister of State in the Prime Minister’s Office, ordering a 14-day review of bajaji and daladala routes and access to a planned market. The review will involve drivers, traders, the Mbeya City Council, LATRA, and the Regional Commissioner’s Office.

The dispute began on October 12 when Mbeya Regional Commissioner Jumanne Sagini ordered the City Council to allocate formal spaces for traders and bajaji operators within seven days. The council planned to direct bajaji outside the TANZAM highway and relocate 1,715 traders to Old Airport, Kabwe, and Mwanjelwa. Enforcement on October 19 led to drivers being forced to clear areas and vendors having goods seized, prompting the joint protest.

Erica Mlembe, a vendor at Kabwe, stated on October 19 that her goods were seized and she had no place to work, impacting her ability to pay school fees. The conflict also affected daladala services, with bajaji drivers attacking them with stones from October 19, leading to increased transport fees and people walking on October 20. The 14-day review is the latest documented outcome as of October 23.

Why it mattersBajaji drivers and vendors in Mbeya are directly affected by the dispute over transport routes and market access, impacting their livelihoods and ability to earn income.

Politics · 2026-10-24

Tanzanian opposition figure charged with treason

John Pambalu faces potential death penalty for alleged election obstruction and protests.

John Pambalu, a senior official with Tanzania's Chadema party, was charged with treason on Wednesday, a crime carrying a potential death penalty. The charges stem from alleged actions intended to obstruct the October 2025 General Election and intimidate the Republic of Tanzania's executive through protests.

Chadema's deputy chairperson John Heche confirmed Pambalu's treason charges, stating he was informed of the situation at a public meeting in Arusha. Heche also anticipates similar charges against himself but vowed not to flee the country, asserting Chadema's commitment to continue their fight.

Pambalu is the second Chadema official to face treason charges, following party leader Tundu Lissu, whose trial is nearing its conclusion. The Chadema party was excluded from the October 2025 vote, which President Samia Suluhu Hassan officially won with 98 percent of the vote.

Why it mattersJohn Pambalu and Tundu Lissu, leaders of the Chadema party, face treason charges and a potential death penalty.
