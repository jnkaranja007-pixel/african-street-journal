Tanzania Integrates Digital Systems to Improve Healthcare Delivery

The nation is enhancing its health sector through electronic medical records and data-driven decision-making, aiming for broader access to quality care.

Tanzania is actively transforming its healthcare system by adopting digital technologies, moving away from paper-based records towards electronic medical records and digital health platforms. This shift is crucial for expanding access to quality healthcare amidst growing populations, limited resources, and a shortage of health professionals. The Ministry of Health is implementing initiatives like the District Health Information Software 2 (DHIS2) for collecting, analyzing, and reporting health data, enabling better monitoring and decision-making by health managers.

The country is also prioritizing the collection of individual-level electronic health information, a move away from relying solely on aggregated data. Government documents show that Electronic Medical Record (EMR) systems have been integrated into tertiary hospitals, improving the digital management of patient information and healthcare services. This technological advancement extends beyond individual facilities, aiming to create a more connected and efficient national health information network.

These digital advancements are supported by global efforts, with the World Health Organisation (WHO) emphasizing the importance of digital infrastructure and data utilization in strengthening health systems. By investing in digital infrastructure and integrating health data, Tanzania aims to promote evidence-based decision-making and enhance its capacity to monitor diseases and respond to emerging health challenges.

Why it mattersTanzanians are affected by the digital transformation of healthcare through improved access to quality services, better disease monitoring, and more efficient health information management.
