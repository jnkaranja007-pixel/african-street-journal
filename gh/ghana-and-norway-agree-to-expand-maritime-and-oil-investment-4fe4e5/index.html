Ghana and Norway agree to expand maritime and oil investment

Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa said the governments will also support digital-economy startups and accelerate work on a tax treaty.

Sources: MyJoyOnline and 3News · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Ghana and Norway agreed to expand cooperation in maritime activities and oil and gas investment, and to support startups, particularly in the digital economy. Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa announced the agreement after talks with Norway’s foreign minister, Espen Barth Eide, during a visit to Norway on Wednesday.

Ablakwa said the two governments also agreed to accelerate work on a Double Taxation Agreement, which he expects to encourage investment between them. The discussions covered the blue economy, maritime cooperation and investment, according to his Facebook post.

Ablakwa is due to co-chair the Nordic-Africa Business Conference, part of this year’s Nordic-Africa Foreign Ministers Meeting. Norway also pledged to work with Ghana during President John Dramani Mahama’s African Union chairmanship on issues including Africa’s debt crisis and reform of the global financial architecture.

Why it mattersStartups and businesses in maritime and oil and gas sectors are included in the cooperation, while both governments must accelerate work on the tax agreement.
