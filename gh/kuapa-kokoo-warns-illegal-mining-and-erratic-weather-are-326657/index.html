Kuapa Kokoo warns illegal mining and erratic weather are cutting cocoa output

Managing Director Emmanuel Kwabena Arthur says pods are scarce at the harvest peak, while polluted rivers leave farmers without clean water.

Sources: MyJoyOnline and 3News · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Ghana’s cocoa production is unusually low at the peak of the season, Kuapa Kokoo Managing Director Emmanuel Kwabena Arthur said at the farmers’ 32nd annual delegates conference. He said the pods on trees were not encouraging as the season approached the end of November.

Arthur said illegal miners, known locally as galamsey, have taken over abandoned cocoa farms and polluted rivers. Farmers lack clean water to irrigate crops and mix pesticides and insecticides, he said. Changing rainfall patterns have also disrupted flowering and pod formation and made planting and farm-maintenance schedules harder to predict.

Ghana Cocoa Board has projected lower production this year than last year, Arthur said, but he warned output could fall further below expectations. He urged the government to strengthen enforcement against illegal mining and chainsaw operations in forest reserves and cocoa-growing communities.

Why it mattersCocoa farmers must manage crops with polluted water and disrupted rainfall, while the season’s output is already below expectations.
