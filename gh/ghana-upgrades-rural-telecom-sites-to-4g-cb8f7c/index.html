Ghana Upgrades Rural Telecom Sites to 4G

Government aims to bridge the digital divide by enhancing connectivity in underserved areas with faster mobile technology.

Ghanaian authorities plan to upgrade all Rural Telephony Project (RTP) sites nationwide to at least 4G technology. Communications Minister Samuel Nartey George stated this initiative is crucial for closing the country's digital connectivity gap and improving access to modern telecommunications services in remote communities. He noted that current 2G and 3G networks are insufficient for contemporary digital demands, especially as global standards move towards 5G and 6G.

The upgrade is part of a comprehensive review of the RTP, which will be presented to the Cabinet. Minister George emphasized that telecommunications infrastructure is now considered a vital component of national development, on par with investments in roads, schools, and hospitals. He made these remarks after inspecting several RTP and other telecommunications facilities in the Eastern Region, including sites at Dumpong, Nkumkrom, Nsakye, and Obosono, as well as an ongoing cell site construction at the Koforidua lorry terminal.

This year, approximately GH¢30 million has been allocated to rural telephony projects through the GIFEC budget. The government is also actively working to expand network infrastructure and enhance connectivity in peri-urban areas and other underserved communities. The minister indicated that the move to 4G is a minimum requirement given the global technological progression. This upgrade is intended to ensure that all citizens can benefit from advancing digital services.

Why it mattersFarmers in rural areas will gain better access to digital services and information, potentially improving agricultural practices and market access.
