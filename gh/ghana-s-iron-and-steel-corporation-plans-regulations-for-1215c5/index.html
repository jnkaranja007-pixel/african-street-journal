Ghana's iron and steel corporation plans regulations for Parliament within three months

The proposed legislative instrument would cover mining, steel processing plants and the scrap metal trade, and the corporation says consultations are in their final stages.

Sources: MyJoyOnline and 3News · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

The Ghana Integrated Iron and Steel Development Corporation (GIISDEC) is targeting the submission of a proposed legislative instrument to Parliament within the next two to three months to regulate the country's iron and steel industry. The rules are expected to set guidelines for the upstream, midstream and downstream segments, including the scrap metal sector.

Chief Executive William Okofo Dartey disclosed the plan at a stakeholders' consultation forum in Accra on Thursday, November 26, 2026. He said the consultation is meant to operationalise the Ghana Integrated Iron and Steel Development Corporation Act, 2019 (Act 988), and to gather contributions from industry players and government agencies before the instrument is finalised. Mr Dartey said the midstream framework would guide the establishment and operation of steel processing plants, promoting local value addition, as Ghana currently imports significant quantities of raw materials, including steel slabs.

Mr Dartey said the corporation had held stakeholder engagements over the past year with traditional authorities, district assemblies and communities in areas with iron ore deposits, including the Shieni deposit in the Tatale-Zabzugu District. He said the corporation was in the final stages of consultations.

Why it mattersSteel companies and scrap metal traders must prepare for rules covering their operations, with the instrument due before Parliament within two to three months.
