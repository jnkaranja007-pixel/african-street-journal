Jospong subsidiary and Turkish firm begin Ghana farm-product trials

The research will test whether products made from discarded shrimp and crab shells protect crops, improve soil and offer farmers cheaper inputs.

Source: MyJoyOnline · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Under a memorandum of understanding signed at COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye, the African Agribusiness Consortium (AAC), a subsidiary of Ghana’s Jospong Group of Companies, and ChitoGlobal Biotechnology will test farm products made from discarded shrimp and crab shells on Ghanaian farms. The products are based on chitosan, a substance found in crustacean shells, and the first step is joint research and field trials.

Those trials will examine whether the products protect seeds and plants, help crops withstand dry spells, strengthen natural defences against fungal disease, and affect soil quality and yields. Jospong’s Chief Investment and Innovation Officer, Noah Gyimah, said the company will first secure approval from Ghana’s environmental and standards authorities before using the products on its farms. AAC will also assess commercial viability, potential buyers and whether Ghana has enough raw material for local production.

If the trials prove the products suitable, AAC intends to make them available to smallholder farmers. The consortium also plans to test their use on its own commercial rice, maize, vegetable and soybean crops.

Why it mattersGhanaian smallholder farmers are the intended market, but they will receive the products only if the trials establish their suitability.
