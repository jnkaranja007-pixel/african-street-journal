Ghana’s US$10 billion programme needs payment checks from the start

The 2027 Budget covers eight priorities and calls for spending tied to verified results, including evidence before contractors are paid.

Source: MyJoyOnline · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Ghana’s 2027 Budget sets a US$10 billion programme for 2027 to 2030 across eight priorities, from commercial agriculture to energy, pharmaceuticals and technology. It promises value-for-money assessments for every project and transparent reporting of spending commitments.

The government says support will be time-bound and linked to verified results. That requires agencies to define what evidence authorises payments: the budget analysis points to checking advance guarantees, inspecting completed facilities and tying later instalments to agreed milestones. An Auditor-General’s report found that the District Assemblies Common Fund administrator paid GH¢10.7 million in advances on 13 contracts; work had not started on any when audited, and the guarantees had expired.

The programme document projects about 20,000 direct and indirect jobs under its pharmaceutical and biomanufacturing pillar. It proposes separating temporary from ongoing direct jobs, checking available payroll and social security records, and explaining estimates for indirect employment.

Why it mattersAgencies overseeing the eight priorities must set evidence standards for payments and report how the projected 20,000 pharmaceutical and biomanufacturing jobs are counted.
