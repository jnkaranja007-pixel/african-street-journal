NPA, UMaT Explore Partnership to Boost Petroleum Sector

The National Petroleum Authority and the University of Mines and Technology are discussing collaboration to enhance innovation and training in Ghana's downstream petroleum.

The National Petroleum Authority (NPA) is in discussions with the University of Mines and Technology (UMaT) to forge a partnership aimed at strengthening innovation, technical training, and professional development within Ghana's downstream petroleum sector. NPA Chief Executive Officer Godwin Kudzo Tameklo stated that collaboration with academic institutions is crucial for advancing the industry through research and novel solutions to sector challenges. The NPA is considering offering internships to UMaT students in its technical departments to provide practical industry exposure.

Further discussions between the NPA and UMaT include potential special training and secondment opportunities for UMaT teaching staff. The university might also be integrated into training programs for the proposed NPA Centre of Excellence, and there is a possibility of retooling a laboratory at UMaT as part of this initiative. Professor Richard Amorin, Dean of UMaT's School of Petroleum Studies, expressed gratitude for the NPA's openness to collaboration, noting that the university will relay the discussions to its Vice Chancellor to formalize the partnership.

Both institutions are optimistic that this engagement will effectively bridge the gap between academic research and practical industry application, thereby fostering skills development and innovation in Ghana's downstream petroleum industry. The NPA reiterated its commitment to building partnerships with academic bodies as part of its regulatory mandate for the sector.

Why it mattersThe partnership aims to improve skills and innovation in the downstream petroleum sector, which could affect the livelihoods of those employed in it.
