Ghana's statistics service reports 1.0% annual capital growth per worker

Its report finds employment rose to 13.3 million in 2025, while unemployment reached 12.8% and earnings remained below pre-pandemic levels.

Source: MyJoyOnline · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

The Ghana Statistical Service (GSS) said capital per worker grew by 1.0% a year since 2020, according to its 2026 Productivity, Employment and Growth Report. Productivity growth also slowed, and the report found that more people were working without a corresponding improvement in job quality.

Employment increased from 11.1 million in 2021 to 13.3 million in 2025, but unemployment was 12.8%, rising to 32.4% among people aged 15 to 24 in the labour force. In 2025, the average worker produced GH₵15,197 in value added and earned GH₵9,388; the GSS said average earnings remained below their pre-pandemic level.

Agriculture employed the most people but had the lowest output per worker, at GH₵8,859 in 2025. The GSS recommended better productivity measurement, investment in technology and skills, job matching for young people and links between productivity gains and earnings.

Why it mattersWorkers earned GH₵9,388 on average in 2025, compared with GH₵15,197 in value added per worker, while average earnings remained below their pre-pandemic level.
