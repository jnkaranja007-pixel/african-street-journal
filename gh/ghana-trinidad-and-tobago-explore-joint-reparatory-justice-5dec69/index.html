Ghana, Trinidad and Tobago explore joint reparatory justice mechanism

Ministers discussed a dedicated platform, potentially co-chaired by AU and CARICOM, for engagement with the UK.

Source: MyJoyOnline · 2026-11-05 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Ghana and Trinidad and Tobago are exploring a joint mechanism to advance reparatory justice and engage with the United Kingdom. The proposal emerged from a bilateral meeting between Ghana's Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa and his Trinidad and Tobago counterpart Sean S.M. Sobers during the 2026 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda.

The ministers agreed that existing platforms like the Commonwealth Foreign Affairs Ministers Meeting are too broad for focused reparatory justice discussions. They stressed the need for a dedicated platform to coordinate engagement with the UK and other international partners, emphasizing that reparatory justice should encompass acknowledgement, reconciliation, and non-repetition, not just financial compensation.

Trinidad and Tobago proposed development-focused interventions such as scholarships, healthcare infrastructure support, and educational improvements as part of the reparatory process. Climate cooperation was also identified as a potential area. This initiative follows Ghana's diplomatic success in March 2026 when the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution A/RES/80/250, recognizing the enslavement of Africans as a grave crime against humanity.
