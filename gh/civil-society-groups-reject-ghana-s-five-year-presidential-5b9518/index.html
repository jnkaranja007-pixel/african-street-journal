Civil society groups reject Ghana's five-year presidential term proposal

More than 100 groups also want citizens to elect local leaders without presidential shortlisting in the proposed metropolitan, municipal and district chief executive system.

Sources: MyJoyOnline and 3News · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

More than 100 civil society organisations in Ghana rejected a proposal to extend presidential and parliamentary terms from four years to five, and called for changes to plans for electing Metropolitan, Municipal and District Chief Executives (MMDCEs), the heads of local government. They said citizens should choose local-government leaders without presidential interference.

The groups made their positions during a two-day engagement with the Constitutional Reform Implementation Committee, which is seeking agreement on proposed amendments to Ghana's 1992 Constitution. Committee Executive Secretary Oliver Barker-Vormawor said the organisations agreed with about 89% of the government's proposals, while outstanding issues fell from 41 to 10.

Koku Anyidoho, an adviser to the Christian Council of Ghana's General Secretary, said 18 position papers were presented. Barker-Vormawor said the unresolved proposals, including term length and whether MPs should be ministers, would go to Cabinet for possible reconsideration.

Why it mattersCabinet must decide whether to revisit 10 unresolved proposals, including the presidential and parliamentary term length and MMDCE election rules.
