Ghana takes in 42.5 to 62 tonnes of mercury a year through illicit routes

Gold miners in four regions handle the toxic metal daily, and stakeholders say it is reaching water supplies and the food chain.

Source: MyJoyOnline · 2026-11-16 · 1 min read

Stakeholders have disclosed that between 42.5 and 62 tonnes of mercury enter Ghana each year through illicit channels, for use in artisanal and small-scale gold mining. Dr Sylvester Akpah, chief executive of Smart Innovation Ghana Limited, made the claim at a symposium in Accra organised by the United States Embassy under its Ambassador's Water Experts Programme.

Dr Akpah said more than 2.5 million people depend directly or indirectly on small-scale mining, and the mercury reaches them through unidentified routes. Mining communities in the Western, Ashanti, Eastern and Central regions are the worst hit, with several water bodies already contaminated. He named Tarkwa and nearby communities as under severe pressure, warning that the pollution could cut access to safe drinking water.

Professor Daniel Nukpezah of the University of Ghana's Institute for Environment and Sanitation Studies said mercury does not break down in the environment and can turn into methylmercury in water, where it builds up in fish. Dr Akpah called for tougher enforcement and wider use of gold-catching technology, which he said remained limited despite investment in importing the equipment.
