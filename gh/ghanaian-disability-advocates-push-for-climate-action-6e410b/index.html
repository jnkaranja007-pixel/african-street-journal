Ghanaian disability advocates push for climate action inclusion

Persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected by climate change but excluded from negotiations.

Source: MyJoyOnline · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Advocates from the Ghana Federation of Disability Organizations are demanding inclusion at the United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Türkiye. They argue that persons with disabilities are disproportionately vulnerable to climate change impacts, such as floods that destroyed assistive devices like wheelchairs in Accra last June. "Climate change affects all of us, but affects some more than others, and those some are persons with disabilities," stated Peter Anoma-Kodie, Executive Director of the federation.

Anoma-Kodie highlighted that physical limitations make escape during extreme weather events difficult, and slow-onset impacts like heatwaves pose severe health risks, particularly to persons with albinism. A critical barrier identified is the lack of accessible early warning systems and disaster communication, with information often not provided in formats like sign language or live captions. This exclusion turns natural hazards into fatal disasters for disabled populations.

The group seeks a seat at the negotiation table to ensure their specific needs are addressed in climate action plans. They point out that disaster management protocols often overlook accessibility requirements, leaving disabled individuals without timely information or accessible transport during emergencies. The movement, backed by the African Group of Negotiators (AGN), aims to change this narrative at COP31.

Why it mattersPersons with disabilities in Ghana face increased vulnerability to climate events like floods and heatwaves, with a lack of accessible warnings exacerbating risks.
