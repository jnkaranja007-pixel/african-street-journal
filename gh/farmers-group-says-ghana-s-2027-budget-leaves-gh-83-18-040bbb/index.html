Farmers' group says Ghana's 2027 Budget leaves GH¢83.18 billion food-programme gap

The Peasant Farmers Association of Ghana says agriculture receives 3.64% of government spending, while the Feed Ghana Programme needs GH¢85.6 billion a year.

Source: MyJoyOnline · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

The Peasant Farmers Association of Ghana (PFAG), a farmers' lobby group, says Ghana's 2027 Budget allocates 3.64% of total government expenditure to agriculture-related agencies and programmes, below the 10% commitment under the Comprehensive Africa Agriculture Development Programme (CAADP). In a release on Thursday, November 26, PFAG also said the Ministry of Food and Agriculture's allocation falls 5.1%, from GH¢3.038 billion in 2026 to GH¢2.882 billion in 2027.

PFAG said the Feed Ghana Programme requires an estimated GH¢85.6 billion annually, but the programme and complementary interventions receive about GH¢2.43 billion, leaving a financing gap of about GH¢83.18 billion. The group said the budget allocates GH¢200 million to the National Food Buffer Stock Company (NAFCO), about 26% of the GH¢770 million requested to buy excess grain, while thousands of farmers have unsold produce and falling farm-gate prices.

The budget also lists a US$3 billion commercial agriculture investment programme for 2027–2030 and targets for additional rice and oil-palm land. PFAG called for a funding pathway to the 10% benchmark and an emergency fund for droughts, floods, pest outbreaks and market disruptions.

Why it mattersFarmers with unsold grain face a buffer-stock purchase allocation of GH¢200 million, about 26% of the GH¢770 million requested.
