Ghana Maritime Authority readies oil-spill vessel

The "MV Esther Ocloo" is now operationally ready for deployment after a ten-day training program to protect offshore oil and gas assets.

The Ghana Maritime Authority's (GMA) oil-spill response vessel, "MV Esther Ocloo," has completed a ten-day work-up program, making it ready for deployment. The training, conducted by the Ghana Navy and GMA at the Sekondi Naval Base, aimed to ensure the vessel's operational readiness since its acquisition in 2023. The vessel is now crewed with Ghana Navy personnel and GMA officers for enhanced efficiency.

Director-General of the GMA, Dr. Ali Kamal-Deen, stated that the "MV Esther Ocloo" will now actively protect Ghana's maritime environment and offshore oil and gas installations. Previously docked, the vessel is now available for deployment to fulfill the GMA's regulatory mandates and responsibilities. The training signifies a shift from the vessel sitting idle to actively contributing to maritime security.

Commodore Stephen Billins Nyam, Flag Officer Commanding the Western Naval Command, highlighted the training's significance in assessing the vessel's capabilities and personnel preparedness. This collaboration between the Ghana Navy and GMA enhances maritime security and coordination at sea. The 40-meter "MV Esther Ocloo" is a strategic asset for oil-spill detection, recovery, surveillance, and environmental protection.

Why it mattersThe "MV Esther Ocloo" is now ready to protect Ghana's offshore oil and gas installations, ensuring the safety and sustainability of the maritime sector.
