Galamsey threatens Ghana gas pipelines

Illegal miners are digging near Ghana Gas infrastructure, risking energy supply disruptions.

Source: Ghanaian Times · 2026-10-26 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Illegal mining, known as galamsey, is now posing a direct threat to Ghana's energy infrastructure, with reports of miners digging beneath gas pipelines operated by Ghana National Gas Company Limited (Ghana Gas). This activity is concentrated in the Western and Ashanti regions, according to Ghana Gas General Manager Maxwell Kwame Kelly. The potential damage to these pipelines could interrupt gas supplies crucial for electricity generation and industrial activity.

Mr. Kelly warned that damage to the pipelines could take months to repair, impacting electricity generation nationwide and imposing significant costs on the economy. The Ghanaian Times stressed that protecting gas pipelines must become a priority in the fight against galamsey, alongside environmental concerns. Authorities, including the Minerals Commission and security agencies, are urged to strengthen surveillance and take decisive action against those involved.

Ghana Gas is engaging with traditional authorities and community leaders to raise awareness about the dangers of digging near restricted areas and to establish reporting mechanisms for suspicious activities. Principal Manager for Health, Safety and Environment, Mr. Jaasu Yahaya, highlighted the importance of community engagement and emergency preparedness in safeguarding lives and infrastructure.

Why it mattersGalamsey activities near gas pipelines threaten Ghana's energy security, potentially disrupting electricity supply and industrial operations.
