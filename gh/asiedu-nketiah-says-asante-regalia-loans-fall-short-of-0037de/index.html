Asiedu Nketiah says Asante regalia loans fall short of restitution

The NDC chairman says some royal objects displayed at the Manhyia Palace Museum remain on loan from British museums rather than transferred to community ownership.

Sources: MyJoyOnline and 3News · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Johnson Asiedu Nketiah, chairman of Ghana’s National Democratic Congress (NDC), said the Asante people deserve more than temporary access to royal regalia taken during colonial military campaigns. Speaking at the Movement for Freedom of Nations Assembly in Moscow on November 16, he said objects returned for display at the Manhyia Palace Museum in Kumasi were subject to different arrangements: some had been restituted, while others remained on loan from British museums.

Asiedu Nketiah said a loan does not transfer ownership, and argued that restored rights and accepted obligations should guide assessments of redress. He said claims over identifiable objects could be investigated, while reparations for the transatlantic slave trade raised harder questions about responsibility, applicable law and who should benefit. He called for evidence-based claims and responses that can be implemented.

He also said affected communities should help design reparative agreements and scrutinise proposed remedies. He urged the assembly to support existing African Union and Caribbean reparations mechanisms rather than create parallel institutions.

Why it mattersAsante communities seeking redress must distinguish objects they can display from those whose ownership has not been transferred from British museums.
