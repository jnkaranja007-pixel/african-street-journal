Ghana and US renew cooperation to curb mercury pollution from illegal mining

The partnership will share scientific expertise and technology to improve monitoring and promote mercury-free gold extraction in Ghana.

Source: Ghanaian Times · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Ghana and the United States renewed cooperation to tackle mercury pollution from illegal mining at a two-day symposium in Accra, focusing on scientific collaboration, technology transfer and sustainable gold extraction.

US Embassy Chargé d’Affaires Rolf Oslon said contamination threatens freshwater sources including the Pra and Ankobra rivers. He cited mercury levels in the Birim River that exceeded a community water treatment plant’s capacity, affecting its operations.

The symposium was organised through the US Government’s Ambassador’s Water Experts Programme, a joint initiative of the US Departments of the Interior and State. Oslon said the United States supports Ghana’s efforts against illegal mining, including activity financed and undertaken by foreign actors.

Why it mattersThe community water treatment plant affected by mercury in the Birim River has faced contamination beyond its treatment capacity.
