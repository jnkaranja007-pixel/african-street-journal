Bank of Ghana puts Justice Annie Ruth Jiagge on new GH¢10 note

The judge appears on the green note in the Heritage Series, which enters circulation from December 1; existing notes remain legal tender.

Source: 3News · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Ghana's central bank has placed the face of Justice Annie Ruth Jiagge on its new green GH¢10 note, part of the Heritage Series launched at its head office, Bank Square, in Accra, on Thursday, November 12. The upgraded notes enter circulation from December 1, while the existing series remains legal tender.

Governor Dr Johnson Pandit Asiama told the launch the designs pair national heroes with historic sites, monuments and Adinkra symbols, so that Ghanaians meet their history in everyday transactions. Jiagge appears under the theme Justice and Equality, and the reverse shows the Supreme Court building with the Martyrs of the Rule of Law monument in front of it, beside Epa, a handcuff-shaped Adinkra symbol of law and justice.

Jiagge read law in London, at the London School of Economics and Lincoln's Inn, and returned to the Gold Coast in 1950 as a qualified lawyer. She practised as a barrister before becoming a magistrate, and by 1961 President Kwame Nkrumah had appointed her a High Court judge, one of the very first women in the Commonwealth to hold such a post.

Why it mattersCash users must accept the upgraded GH¢10 note alongside the existing series after it enters circulation on December 1.
