Ghana brokers $270m poultry project to cut chicken imports

A $270 million investment agreement aims to revitalize Ghana’s poultry industry, boost domestic production, and reduce reliance on imported chicken.

Ghana's 24-Hour Economy and Accelerated Export Development Secretariat has secured a US$270 million investment to overhaul the nation's poultry sector. The agreement, brokered with entities including the Tony Blair Institute, Agrium Capital, Petra Pension Trust, and Axis Pension Trust, aims to strengthen local production and food security by developing an integrated poultry value chain. This initiative covers feed production, hatcheries, commercial farming, processing, and distribution networks.

The deal is expected to significantly reduce Ghana's annual poultry import bill, which currently stands at approximately US$400 million for about 270,000 tonnes of chicken. Presidential Adviser Goosie Tanoh stated the agreement is a landmark intervention to build a competitive domestic industry capable of meeting substantial local demand. The investment aligns with the government's 24-Hour Economy agenda, promoting round-the-clock economic activity and job creation, particularly for youth and women in agriculture.

Agrium Capital CEO Rod Bassett indicated the project will be implemented through a proprietary development process over 10 to 12 months, establishing a fully integrated poultry production system. The project will roll out in three phases, designed for close integration with rural communities to foster sustainable economic opportunities and local participation in the industry.

Why it mattersGhanaian farmers and agribusinesses will benefit from expanded domestic production and a strengthened value chain, while investors gain access to a significant revitalized sector.

Ghana President Endorses Luanda Action Plan for African Peace

President John Mahama welcomed the Luanda Action Plan, a strategic framework prioritizing early warning systems and preventative diplomacy for African security.

President John Mahama has officially endorsed the Luanda Action Plan, a strategic framework adopted by the African Union on August 30. This plan emphasizes proactive measures, focusing on early warning systems and preventative diplomacy over reactive responses to conflicts. Addressing the AU Extraordinary Summit in Luanda, Angola, Mahama stressed Ghana's commitment to sustainable, African-owned financing for peace and security initiatives, stating that early warnings must lead to timely and predictable actions.

Mahama called for an African financing architecture that is predictable for planning, flexible for response, and sustainable to reduce reliance on external resources. He linked these insights to Ghana's own 'Accra Reset' initiative, suggesting its applicability to peace and safety alongside healthcare. The President urged the AU to ensure commitments are backed by clear responsibilities, measurable indicators, and credible financing, moving beyond prolonged discussions to concrete action.

Ghana also champions the UN General Assembly's March 25 resolution declaring the enslavement of Africans a grave crime against humanity, achieved through a coalition including the African Union and the African Diaspora. Following this, Ghana convened the 'Next Steps Conference' in Accra, which decided to include indentured labor as a historical injustice and established advisory councils for reparatory justice, cultural heritage restitution, and legal matters.

Why it mattersGhana's President is advocating for African-owned and financed peace and security mechanisms, which could impact the continent's stability and resource allocation.

Ghana Overlooks Medium Businesses in MSME Support

A policy gap exists in Ghana where medium enterprises, crucial for industrial growth, receive insufficient attention alongside micro and small businesses.

Ghana's national economic conversation often highlights micro-entrepreneurs and small business owners but overlooks medium enterprises, creating a critical policy blind spot. While micro-businesses operate with one to five employees and small businesses with six to twenty-nine, medium enterprises employ thirty-one to one hundred people. These medium-sized firms, though numerically fewer than micro-businesses, are vital for formal job creation and industrial advancement.

The Ghana Enterprises Agency classifies businesses by employee count and annual turnover, with micro firms earning under GH¢300,000, small firms between GH¢300,000 and GH¢6 million, and medium firms between GH¢6 million and GH¢18 million. Despite comprising a small fraction of Ghana's approximately 1.9 million businesses, small and medium enterprises are significant drivers of the formal economy, contributing disproportionately to job creation and value addition.

A substantial US$4.8 billion annual financing gap identified by the Bank of Ghana hinders the growth of these small and medium enterprises, with over half lacking access to formal credit. This lack of capital contributes to a high MSME failure rate, with only 20% of startups succeeding. Addressing the distinct needs of both small and medium enterprises is essential for building a resilient industrial base and fostering inclusive growth in Ghana.

Why it mattersSmall and medium enterprises in Ghana are affected by a significant financing gap, limiting their ability to scale and contribute to formal job creation and industrial growth.

Iceland rejects EU membership talks by narrow margin

A referendum saw 52.8% of voters turn against resuming accession negotiations with the European Union.

Icelanders have voted against restarting talks on joining the European Union, with 52.8% of voters rejecting the proposal in a referendum. The public broadcaster RUV reported that 225,031 votes were cast, with 47.2% supporting the resumption of talks. The government had promised a referendum by 2027 but brought it forward due to international tensions, though the debate focused on the nation's fishing industry.

The outcome means full EU membership, which would have brought Iceland into the customs union and potentially the eurozone, is off the table for the foreseeable future. Prime Minister Kristrún Frostadóttir stated the issue would not be revisited during her government's term. The referendum campaign was described as technical, with the "No" side appealing to national identity and a desire for formal independence.

Fishing rights, which account for nearly 40% of Iceland's exports, were a central concern for many voters. Fears that EU membership would lead to a loss of control over lucrative fishing grounds under the bloc's policies influenced the decision. The vote revealed a geographical divide, with the capital, Reykjavík, favoring talks while rural areas opposed them.

Why it mattersIcelandic voters rejected EU membership talks, impacting potential trade agreements and economic integration.

Widow Sues Airline After Husband's Death During Turbulence

A woman is seeking damages from an airline after her husband died and she suffered a broken back during severe turbulence over Myanmar.

Linda Kitchen, 74, has initiated legal proceedings at the High Court against Singapore Airlines following an incident in May 2024. Her husband, Geoff, 73, sustained fatal injuries when their flight from London Heathrow experienced a sudden drop of approximately 6,000 feet. The flight was en route to Singapore, Indonesia, and Australia when the turbulence occurred over Myanmar, leading to an emergency diversion to Bangkok, Thailand.

Kitchen described the turbulence as so severe that the aircraft felt like it was dropping, causing personal items to fall and her husband to fall on top of her. Oxygen masks were deployed during the event. Geoff Kitchen is understood to have suffered a heart attack at high altitude. Linda Kitchen sustained a broken back and spent 10 days in a Thai hospital before being airlifted back to the UK, where she required significant home assistance.

Lawyers for Linda Kitchen stated that the incident vividly highlights the severity of what happened and that the family has ongoing questions about the event and its preventability. The lawsuit aims to secure damages for specialist support for her back and to provide answers to Geoff's loved ones. The BBC has contacted the airline for comment.

Why it mattersInvestors may be affected by potential legal liabilities and reputational damage to the airline following this lawsuit.
