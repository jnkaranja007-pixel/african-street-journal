Galamsey threatens Ghana gas pipelines

Illegal miners are digging near Ghana Gas infrastructure, risking energy supply disruptions.

Illegal mining, known as galamsey, is now posing a direct threat to Ghana's energy infrastructure, with reports of miners digging beneath gas pipelines operated by Ghana National Gas Company Limited (Ghana Gas). This activity is concentrated in the Western and Ashanti regions, according to Ghana Gas General Manager Maxwell Kwame Kelly. The potential damage to these pipelines could interrupt gas supplies crucial for electricity generation and industrial activity.

Mr. Kelly warned that damage to the pipelines could take months to repair, impacting electricity generation nationwide and imposing significant costs on the economy. The Ghanaian Times stressed that protecting gas pipelines must become a priority in the fight against galamsey, alongside environmental concerns. Authorities, including the Minerals Commission and security agencies, are urged to strengthen surveillance and take decisive action against those involved.

Ghana Gas is engaging with traditional authorities and community leaders to raise awareness about the dangers of digging near restricted areas and to establish reporting mechanisms for suspicious activities. Principal Manager for Health, Safety and Environment, Mr. Jaasu Yahaya, highlighted the importance of community engagement and emergency preparedness in safeguarding lives and infrastructure.

Why it mattersGalamsey activities near gas pipelines threaten Ghana's energy security, potentially disrupting electricity supply and industrial operations.

Ghanaian education fund supports 61 students with GH¢138,536

The Upper West Regional Chief Imam Educational Endowment Fund disbursed funds for the 2026/27 academic year, increasing beneficiaries by 38.64%.

The Upper West Regional Chief Imam Educational Endowment Fund has disbursed GH¢138,536 to support 61 students for the 2026/27 academic year. This represents an increase of 38.64% from the previous year, when 44 students received aid. The fund, established in October 2022, aims to assist academically promising but financially disadvantaged students pursuing tertiary education.

Fund Secretary Dr Saeed Moomin announced the disbursement in Wa, noting the numerous requests for educational assistance reflect families' financial difficulties. Beneficiaries are selected through academic assessments, financial need interviews, and home verification. For some, like Ibrahim Salim, a Political Science student, the aid has made the difference between continuing his education and putting it on hold.

Students like Amatus Ahmed and Ayisha Yahaya expressed gratitude for the lifeline provided, especially for those from rural communities. Stakeholders urged beneficiaries to use the funds wisely and contribute back to the fund later. Former Wa Municipal Chief Executive Tahiru Issahaku Moomin called for unity against poverty, while Headmaster Adams Iddrisu Thirdman cautioned against misuse of the funds.

Why it matters61 tertiary students in Ghana received financial assistance for their education, enabling them to continue their studies.

Kojo Kwarteng becomes a lawyer

Emmanuel Kojo Kwarteng added law to a career in journalism, marketing, and banking.

Emmanuel Kojo Kwarteng qualified as a lawyer on Friday, October 9, 2026. This legal achievement adds to his extensive career which already includes journalism, marketing, corporate communications, public relations, banking, authorship, alternative dispute resolution (ADR), and community service.

Kwarteng studied at the Ghana Institute of Journalism and the University of Ghana Business School, earning a BSc in Administration and an MBA in Marketing. He later obtained an LLB and LLM from Mountcrest University and the University of Essex in the UK, and also studied at the Kellogg School of Management in the USA.

He previously worked as Head of Corporate Affairs at GCB Bank Plc, where he managed media relations and reputation during significant financial events like the 2017 integration of UT/Capital Bank and the 2023 Domestic Debt Exchange Programme (DDEP).

Julius Nainoo elected NDC Berekum West chairman

Delegates elected Julius Nainoo with 334 votes, unseating incumbent James Ntim who received 302 votes.

Julius Nainoo has been elected the new National Democratic Congress (NDC) chairman for the Berekum West Constituency in the Bono Region. He secured 334 valid votes, defeating the incumbent chairman, James Ntim, who received 302 votes. The election was supervised by the Electoral Commission at Koraso.

Other elected officials include Simeon Sakyi as Vice Chairman with 336 votes, and Regina Ferka as Women Organiser with 43 votes. Several other positions, including Communications Officer and Youth Organiser, were filled by acclamation. Newly elected chairman Nainoo pledged to work towards making the NDC more appealing in the constituency.

The newly elected executives aim to work for the NDC to retain the Berekum West parliamentary seat and political power in the 2028 elections. Madam Esther Mmra secured 39 votes for Deputy Women Organiser, and Sampson Adjei polled 445 votes for the Zongo Caucus position.

Why it mattersDelegates of the National Democratic Congress in Berekum West Constituency elected new party leadership.

Ghana seeks BRICS funding for energy sector

The nation needs US$4.4 billion for energy investments between 2025 and 2030, with the New Development Bank as a potential source.

Ghana is seeking to broaden its development and energy financing options by pursuing membership in BRICS. The country aims to translate diplomatic ties into capital, technology, investment, and markets, with the New Development Bank (NDB) identified as a key institution for long-term finance. Ghana's energy sector faces a significant funding gap across generation, transmission, distribution, and infrastructure, with limited government capacity to fund large projects directly.

The nation requires approximately US$4.4 billion for energy sector investments from 2025 to 2030, with an estimated US$2.6 billion expected from private sources, according to the IMF. The NDB could serve as an additional financing channel, complementing existing partners like the World Bank and African Development Bank. Access to NDB funding depends on Ghana's ability to present bankable projects with credible sponsors and clear risk allocation.

BRICS economies offer expertise in oil and gas, power generation, renewables, and electric mobility, presenting an opportunity for Ghana to gain technology and knowledge beyond just capital. The NDB approved about US$3.17 billion for 19 projects in 2025, demonstrating the availability of substantial capital. Ghana intends to develop a pipeline of projects meeting international investment standards to attract and deploy this capital effectively.

Why it mattersGhana's energy sector requires substantial investment, and joining BRICS could provide access to new financing sources like the New Development Bank.
