Nickie Akosa launches BaseNord facility to deepen Ghana-Sweden trade

The new co-working hub in Accra aims to support startups and boost commercial ties following record bilateral trade levels between the two nations.

Trade relations between Ghana and Sweden recorded significant growth in 2025, with bilateral imports and exports reaching their highest levels in three years. Data from Trading Economics show that Ghana’s exports to Sweden were valued at US$3.07 million, while Swedish exports to Ghana reached US$86.3 million during the year.

To support growing commercial ties, Nickie Akosa, Sweden’s Honorary Consul to Ghana, has established BaseNord in the East Legon neighbourhood of Accra. The purpose-built facility provides dedicated co-working spaces, private offices, meeting rooms and virtual office solutions for startups and investors.

Ms Akosa stated that the hub addresses the difficulties early-stage businesses face in finding professional working environments. The initiative also aims to assist Ghanaian businesses seeking partners in Scandinavia and Swedish investors establishing operations in Ghana, particularly in renewable energy, food and agriculture.

Why it mattersStartups and investors looking for workspace and commercial partners in Ghana and Sweden are directly affected by the launch of the new facility.

NPA and UMaT Explore Partnership to Strengthen Downstream Petroleum Sector

The National Petroleum Authority and the University of Mines and Technology are discussing a partnership to boost training and innovation in the industry.

The National Petroleum Authority is exploring a partnership with the University of Mines and Technology to strengthen technical training, innovation, and professional development in the downstream petroleum industry. NPA Chief Executive Officer Godwin Kudzo Tameklo disclosed the initiative during a courtesy call by a university delegation led by Professor Richard Amorin on Tuesday, October 20, 2026.

Discussions between the two institutions covered potential student internships within technical departments of the NPA and special training or secondment opportunities for teaching staff. The collaboration may also include incorporating the university into training programmes at a proposed NPA Centre of Excellence alongside the possible retooling of a laboratory at UMaT.

Both parties expressed optimism that the engagement will bridge the gap between academic research and industry practice. The NPA stated that building partnerships with academic institutions aligns with its mandate to regulate, oversee, and monitor the downstream petroleum sector.

Why it mattersUniversity students and teaching staff are directly affected through upcoming internship placements, training programmes, and potential staff secondment opportunities.

Ghana Immigration Service Orders November Deadline For Unearned Salary Refunds

Two immigration officers face a November 7 deadline to return unearned salaries totaling up to GH¢57,006 following parliamentary committee scrutiny.

Two officers within the Ghana Immigration Service who collected up to GH¢57,006 in unearned wages must return the funds by November 7, 2026. The Minister for the Interior, Muntaka Mohammed-Mubarak, identified the beneficiaries as Abida Zakaria and Abora Angelina during an appearance before Parliament's Public Accounts Committee. Officials who authorised these payments face financial liability if the money remains unrecovered after the deadline.

The committee also reviewed GH¢37,907 in excess salary arrears paid to 7 officers, with approximately GH¢12,900 still outstanding from an initial GH¢24,900 recovery. Lawmakers discussed an additional recovery of GH¢206,085 regarding an inspector who resigned following four years of paid study leave without fulfilling service obligations.

To prevent future payroll discrepancies, the interior minister announced plans to deploy new human resource software for better staff transfers and salary validation. He also proposed self-accounting arrangements for security services to improve financial oversight and simplify payroll audits.

Why it mattersTwo immigration officers and seven others are affected by strict refund demands and oversight changes regarding unearned salaries and excess arrears.

Dr Valentine Mensah warns delayed funding undermines Ghana judiciary

Inadequate financial resources and delayed funding are weakening effective justice delivery across the courts in Ghana.

Delays in funding and inadequate resources are undermining effective justice delivery in Ghana, according to Dr Valentine Mensah, a D&D fellow in public financial management and corporate governance. Speaking at a JoyNews and An Amalgam of Professionals event on Tuesday, October 20, he stated that the judiciary needs dependable financial support, proper staffing, and essential facilities.

Data cited shows Parliament approved GH¢945 million for the judiciary in 2024, but only GH¢700 million was released, with compensation absorbing 79 per cent. In 2025, audited expenditure exceeded GH¢1.6 billion, with compensation taking 87 per cent. Furthermore, JUSAG reported more than 130 courts operating without magistrates.

Dr Mensah called for sustainable funding arrangements and improved staffing plans. He noted that the judicial complex has remained unfinished for more than a decade due to incomplete planning and funding delays.

Why it mattersCitizens and court users are affected by delayed justice resulting from severe underfunding, severe staff shortages, and unfinished judicial infrastructure across Ghana.

Teacher Alleges Widespread School Sexual Misconduct in Ghana

Stephen Desu claims that almost every school nationwide has one or two educators involved in predatory behavior.

A teacher at Ekumpoano Catholic Basic School, Stephen Desu, has alleged that sexual misconduct by educators is widespread across schools in Ghana. Speaking on Joy News on Tuesday, October 20, he claimed that almost every school has one or two staff members engaged in such behavior, warning that the issue spans primary, junior high, and senior high levels.

The remarks follow an investigation into allegations of sexual abuse involving female students at Obuasi Senior High Technical School. Following reports by investigative correspondent Manasseh Azure Awuni, the Ghana Education Service interdicted headmaster Zakaria Abdulai, and police arrested him. The allegations against Mr Abdulai have not yet been established in court.

Mr Desu blamed the Ghana Education Service for failing to deal decisively with misconduct, arguing that a lack of punishment allows such actions to fester. Education authorities will need to establish the extent of the problem through formal investigations, as the widespread claims remain unsubstantiated.

Why it mattersStudents and school administrators across Ghana are affected by institutional responses to allegations of sexual misconduct.
