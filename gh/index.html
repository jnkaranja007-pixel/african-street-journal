Ghana's National Investment Bank returns to profit

The bank's capital adequacy ratio rose to 23% after a government cash injection and bond offering.

Ghana's National Investment Bank (NIB) has reported a profit after tax of GH¢343.9 million for the 2025 financial year, a significant turnaround from GH¢2.7 million in 2024. This recovery follows a GH¢450 million cash injection, GH¢1.5 billion in re-marketable bonds, and the transfer of GH¢500 million in government-held Nestlé Ghana shares. The interventions pushed NIB's capital adequacy ratio to approximately 23% by May 2025, from a negative level as low as -53.13% at the end of 2024.

The Finance Ministry stated that these measures protected GH¢6.4 billion in depositor funds and preserved over 900 jobs, contrasting with the widespread job losses and institutional collapses during the 2017-2019 banking sector clean-up. During that period, 420 financial institutions failed, costing over 10,000 jobs and requiring GH¢25 billion in government spending.

NIB's financial performance in 2025 showed total assets more than doubling to GH¢12.22 billion from GH¢5.84 million in 2024. Customer deposits increased to GH¢10.20 billion from GH¢6.47 billion, while loans and advances grew to GH¢730 million from GH¢670 million. Total equity climbed to GH¢1.55 billion.

Ghana targets AI facility within 24 months

The government aims to complete the first phase of a national computing facility to boost local artificial intelligence development.

Ghana plans to complete the initial phase of a national computing facility within two years, providing local researchers, universities, and businesses with essential computing power for artificial intelligence (AI) development. The Minister for Communication, Digital Technology and Innovations, Samuel Nartey George, stated that procurement processes are underway with the Public Procurement Authority for the necessary data centre infrastructure and equipment. This facility is intended to reduce the current reliance on external computing services purchased by Ghanaian entities.

The planned facility will offer computing resources crucial for AI research and commercial applications, which often demand significant processing power for model training, application execution, and large-scale data analysis. Minister George described the project as a foundational step towards positioning Ghana to actively participate in the evolving AI economy. He cautioned that this facility represents an initial milestone rather than a complete solution to the nation's AI infrastructure needs.

The government's 24-month target outlines the timeline for the first phase of the computing facility. Further investment and consistent policy implementation will be necessary to achieve Ghana's broader AI ambitions. The success of the facility will depend on its capacity, affordability, reliability, and accessibility to its intended users, including researchers, universities, and businesses working with AI technologies.

Why it mattersGhanaian researchers, universities, and businesses will benefit from local access to computing power for AI development, reducing reliance on foreign services.

Analyst urges Ghana to boost farm extension services

Dr Joseph Opoku Gakpo says stronger support for agricultural extension personnel is essential to improving food security and farmer innovation.

Agricultural analyst Dr Joseph Opoku Gakpo has called on the Ghanaian government to prioritize agricultural extension services, stating that enhanced support for these personnel is crucial for helping farmers adopt new technologies and ultimately improving national food security. He emphasized that investments in agricultural research will yield limited benefits if farmers do not receive clear communication about innovations and cannot implement them effectively.

Dr Gakpo highlighted that effective extension services act as a vital link between research institutions and farming practices. He noted that without adequate investment in extension personnel and their supporting systems, innovations developed in universities and research centers may not reach the farmers who need them. This gap limits the potential benefits of agricultural research investments, hindering the adoption of practices like regenerative farming or improved post-harvest techniques.

The analyst stressed that extension workers require not only technical knowledge in areas such as crop and livestock production, soil health, and pest management but also strong communication and interpersonal skills. Building trust with farmers is paramount for the successful introduction of new technologies, as demonstrated by research co-authored by Dr Gakpo, which identified trust as a key factor in technology acceptance among Ghanaian farmers.

Why it mattersGhanaian farmers are affected because they may not receive the necessary information and support to adopt new farming innovations, which could improve their yields and food production.

IMF mission reviews Ghana's 2027 budget plans

Discussions will focus on fiscal performance, revenue, inflation, and debt as Ghana seeks to consolidate economic gains.

An International Monetary Fund (IMF) mission began formal engagements with the Ghanaian government today to review the country's fiscal performance, revenue mobilization, and preparations for the 2027 Budget. Sources familiar with the visit indicate the discussions will cover economic performance for the first nine months of the year, necessary policies to maintain recent gains, and Ghana's debt position. Expenditure management for the remainder of the current year and spending plans for 2027, with a focus on infrastructure investment, are also on the agenda.

The mission will also examine Ghana's inflation-targeting framework, monetary policy, and foreign exchange arrangements. Potential assessments of the Bank of Ghana's financial standing, international reserves, and measures to protect the central bank's independence are expected. Initial discussions involve officials from the Ministry of Finance and the Bank of Ghana, with other economic stakeholders anticipated to join the two-week visit.

This mission is part of Ghana's engagement under the Policy Coordination Instrument (PCI), a non-financing framework designed to consolidate economic gains and sustain policy reforms. The PCI, which does not provide direct IMF funding, allows the government to demonstrate progress on policy commitments and bolster confidence among investors and development partners. Finance Minister Dr. Cassiel Ato Forson has described the framework as crucial for strengthening economic resilience and supporting broad-based growth.

Why it mattersInvestors and government officials are affected by the IMF review which will shape economic policies and investor confidence.
