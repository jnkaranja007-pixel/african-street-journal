Corporate Ghana Trades Boardrooms for Sports Stadium

Thirty-four companies participated in the JoySports Invitational Tournament 2026, fostering networking and fitness.

Corporate Ghana swapped suits for sports gear on Saturday, August 29, transforming the University of Ghana Sports Stadium into a hub for competition and networking. The JoySports Invitational Tournament 2026 saw executives and employees from 34 companies and institutions engage in a day of sport and camaraderie, beginning with an early morning health walk. Ayawaso West Wuogon MP John Dumelo was a special guest.

Following an aerobics session, participants competed in various disciplines including football, swimming, table tennis, chess, Scrabble, ludo, and novelty events like sack races. The football matches saw intense rivalry, with titles and job descriptions seemingly set aside as colleagues became teammates. The CEO Penalty Shootout was a highlight, with male and female executives taking turns to score.

The event aimed to promote fitness and provide a platform for corporate professionals to engage in recreational activities outside their usual work environments. The mix of competitive sports and lighthearted novelty events ensured a day filled with energy, laughter, and opportunities for informal networking among Ghana's business community.

Why it mattersCorporate professionals and their companies are affected by this event as it provides a platform for fitness, networking, and team building outside of the office.

Bank of Ghana Launches Committee to Investigate Seized Foreign Currency

The Bank of Ghana has established a committee to investigate undeclared foreign currency moving through the country's borders, aiming to strengthen financial system monitoring.

The Bank of Ghana (BoG) has inaugurated a Preliminary Investigation Committee on Seized Foreign Currency to address the flow of undeclared foreign currency through Ghana’s airports and border points. Governor Dr Johnson Pandit Asiama stated the initiative aims to close a significant gap in the financial system by enhancing monitoring and investigation of seized currency.

Dr Asiama highlighted that substantial amounts of foreign currency are reportedly moving through the country's borders without proper declaration. He explained that these undeclared flows pose risks to Ghana’s financial sector integrity, creating opportunities for money laundering, tax evasion, and other illicit financial activities. Such movements also weaken financial intelligence and divert funds from the formal market.

The Bank of Ghana is collaborating with several agencies, including the Ghana Revenue Authority, Ghana Airports Company Limited, National Security, the Economic and Organised Crime Office (EOCO), the Financial Intelligence Centre, and the Attorney General’s Office. This multi-agency effort seeks to safeguard Ghana’s borders, markets, and financial resources.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because undeclared currency flows create opportunities for money laundering and tax evasion, weakening the financial system's integrity.

Influencer Denies $32,000 Visa Fraud Charges

Social media personality Ibrahim Kailani Kpa pleaded not guilty to defrauding seven individuals of $32,000 for non-existent visas.

Accra Circuit Court granted bail to social media influencer Ibrahim Kailani Kpa, known as ‘Powder Guy’, who faces charges of defrauding seven people of $32,000. Kpa, also a tiler, allegedly promised visas to Nicaragua, Canada, and the United States, as well as World Cup travel packages, between 2023 and 2026. He was admitted to GH¢400,000 bail with three sureties, one of whom must be a family member, and ordered to report to the case investigator.

The prosecution stated that Kpa advertised his services on TikTok and Facebook, collecting between $2,000 and $9,000 from each complainant for visa processing, which he claimed would take two weeks to a month. When the visas were not secured, Kpa allegedly became unreachable and blocked the complainants. The complainants reported the matter to the police, leading to his arrest in East Legon, Accra.

During police investigations, Kpa reportedly admitted receiving the $32,000 and claimed he disbursed it to alleged accomplices, Jalil, Chapman, and Danlad, who have since gone into hiding. Kpa was unable to lead investigators to these individuals. The court has ordered Kpa to report to the investigator as directed while investigations continue.

Why it mattersSeven individuals are affected by the alleged visa fraud, losing a total of $32,000 in payments for travel documents that were never secured.

Ghana Commission Lists 100 Unrecognized Universities

The Ghana Tertiary Education Commission has released a list of 100 universities not recognized due to quality assurance concerns.

The Ghana Tertiary Education Commission (GTEC) has published a list of 100 universities that are not recognized as of August 28, 2026. This action was taken due to concerns about the quality assurance standards of these institutions. GTEC has advised the public to be cautious and conduct thorough checks regarding any certificates obtained from these listed universities.

The commission's public notice, dated August 28, 2026, includes institutions from various countries such as the USA, India, Mexico, and Ghana. Among the listed universities are Universidad Azteca, Breyer State Theology University, Osiri University, and Faith University Seminary. Several Ghanaian institutions, including Debest College of Science, Arts and Business, and Volta University College, also appear on the list.

This move by GTEC aims to protect students and the public from fraudulent educational qualifications. The commission's directive emphasizes the importance of verifying the accreditation status of tertiary institutions before enrolling or accepting credentials. The list serves as a warning against institutions operating without meeting the required educational standards.

Why it mattersStudents and individuals seeking higher education or professional certification are affected by this list, as it highlights institutions whose qualifications may not be recognized.

Bank of Ghana Identifies $4.8 Billion SME Credit Gap

The central bank attributes the significant financing shortfall to a lack of credit assessment tools rather than insufficient capital.

Ghana's financial sector faces a substantial $4.8 billion credit gap for micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs), according to the Bank of Ghana. Deputy Governor Matilda Asante-Asiedu stated that while payment infrastructure is robust, the development of credit assessment capabilities has lagged. This gap is identified as a primary obstacle to business growth, particularly for enterprises unable to provide conventional collateral.

The Bank of Ghana proposes that access to fair credit should be the new benchmark for financial inclusion, shifting focus from account ownership and transaction volume to actual lending to businesses. This approach emphasizes leveraging existing transaction data, such as cash flow and merchant activity, to build credit records. Regulators now view the failure to convert this data into credit assessments as a supervisory concern, not just a commercial choice.

The National Insurance Commission estimates that approximately 95% of SMEs are constrained by collateral requirements, preventing viable businesses with strong cash flows and contracts from accessing necessary funding. The push for open banking reform is now directed towards facilitating lending to these businesses, with success measured by credit extended rather than the number of APIs published.

Why it mattersThe $4.8 billion credit gap affects Ghanaian MSMEs by limiting their ability to secure loans needed for expansion and operations, due to a lack of effective credit assessment methods.
