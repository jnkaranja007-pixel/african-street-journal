DBG Funds Maize, Rice, Cassava, Poultry for Food Security

The Development Bank Ghana is directing significant financing towards key agricultural value chains to bolster food security and economic growth.

The Development Bank Ghana (DBG) is channeling a substantial portion of its capital into critical agricultural sectors, including maize, rice, cassava, sorghum, and poultry, to enhance national food security and foster economic transformation. This strategic allocation is based on the bank's analysis of sectors with high potential for job creation and sustainable growth, identified as 'growth pole areas' that require deliberate intervention due to market failures and financing constraints.

According to DBG Chief Executive Officer Prof Randolph Nsor-Ambala, these investments go beyond individual business funding, aiming to transform entire value chains. He stated that job creation, leading to upward social mobility and economic empowerment, is a primary driver. Additionally, improved food security is expected to reduce economic pressures such as inflation and exchange rate instability caused by high import levels.

Since its inception, DBG has disbursed over ¢2.5 billion, with more than half directed towards agribusiness, agriculture, and manufacturing. Prof Nsor-Ambala emphasized that the focus on specific value chains like maize, rice, cassava, sorghum, and poultry is a result of analytics demonstrating their significant contribution to achieving the bank's stated objectives of job creation and economic stability.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly impacted as the Development Bank Ghana is investing in key agricultural value chains like maize, rice, cassava, and poultry.

Ghana government targets horticulture for export growth

New policies aim to boost production, processing, and market access for fruits and vegetables to increase national earnings.

The Ghanaian government is prioritizing the horticulture sector to drive export growth, with a focus on enhancing production, processing, certification, and market access. Minister of Trade, Agribusiness and Industry, Elizabeth Ofosu-Adjare, stated that the goal is to ensure increased output of fruits and vegetables translates into greater economic value for the nation. This initiative aims to capitalize on the US$5 billion in non-traditional exports recorded in 2025.

The government's newly approved agribusiness policy and the 'Feed the Industry' program are designed to tackle challenges such as raw material supply, financing, value addition, and market access. However, officials emphasize that simply increasing production is insufficient; Ghanaian horticultural products must meet international standards for food safety, traceability, and quality to compete effectively. Efforts are underway to streamline certification, expand testing services, and build capacity among farmers and exporters.

Davies Narh Korboe, President of the Federation of Associations of Ghanaian Exporters, called for building on current achievements to reach US$10 billion in exports. He stressed the importance of producing what is consumed, processing what is produced, and exporting what the world demands. Korboe also highlighted that farmers, while at the start of the value chain, should not remain at the bottom of wealth creation, addressing concerns about inadequate irrigation, financing, and post-harvest losses.

Why it mattersGhanaian farmers and exporters are affected by government efforts to improve production, processing, and market access for horticultural products to boost national export earnings.

EU Film Festival Returns to Accra Promoting Peace and Inclusion

The European Union Film Festival Ghana 2026 has launched in Accra, aiming to foster peace, tolerance, and cultural exchange through cinema.

The European Union Film Festival (EUFF) Ghana 2026 has officially launched in Accra, marking its return after a five-year hiatus. The festival, themed "Peace, Tolerance, and Inclusion," will run from October 5 to 11, 2026. It aims to utilize cinema as a platform for promoting understanding and cultural dialogue between European Union member states and Ghana.

Organized in collaboration with EU member-state embassies and local partners, the festival will feature a diverse program of film screenings, filmmaker engagements, and industry activities. Ms. Esiawonam Adisenu, Director of EUFF, stated that the event seeks to connect Ghanaian and European filmmakers and audiences, encouraging participation from film enthusiasts, students, and creative professionals.

Ambassador Rune Skinnebach of the European Union to Ghana highlighted that the festival's core pillars are dialogue, talent development, and cultural exchange. This initiative is part of the EU's broader commitment to strengthening cultural diplomacy with Ghana and fostering understanding between societies through shared cinematic experiences.

Why it mattersThe EU Film Festival in Accra provides a platform for cultural exchange and dialogue between European and Ghanaian filmmakers and audiences.

Tanker drivers end strike, demand road repairs within one month

Petroleum tanker drivers have suspended a two-day strike, demanding the government fix critical roads within one month or face renewed industrial action.

The Ghana National Petroleum Tanker Drivers Union has suspended its sit-down strike after two days, following interventions from the Ghana Chamber of Bulk Oil Distributors and the Chamber of Oil Marketing Companies. The drivers will resume normal operations on Friday, September 4, after weeks of escalating pressure over what they described as dangerous and deteriorating road conditions. The Union had issued a two-week ultimatum before commencing the strike on Wednesday, September 2.

The strike affected tanker operations at key loading points, including the Tema industrial area, as drivers protested the condition of roads used to transport petroleum products. Specific routes cited include the road from the Valco Roundabout in Tema to Kpone Junction, and roads within the Takoradi and Buipe depot enclaves, described as critical national economic corridors. The Union claims these roads generate approximately GH¢300 million weekly in tax revenue.

Despite calling off the strike, the tanker drivers' demands remain unchanged. They expect government to take immediate and concrete steps to fast-track road rehabilitation, giving a one-month window to demonstrate "visible and measurable progress." Failure to meet this expectation could trigger a return to industrial action, including a prolonged sit-down strike, leaving government under pressure to act.

Why it mattersPetroleum tanker drivers are affected by the deplorable road conditions, which pose safety risks and disrupt their ability to transport fuel.
