Ghana to build 21,000-tonne grain storage facility

The Ministry of Food and Agriculture signed an MoU with DMT Collateral Management for the facility in Wa, Upper West Region.

Ghana will soon have a 21,000-metric-tonne grain storage facility in the Upper West Region following a Memorandum of Understanding signed between the Ministry of Food and Agriculture and DMT Collateral Management. The project, scheduled for completion by August 2027, aims to reduce post-harvest losses and bolster national food security by constructing silos in Wa.

The facility will serve as a food security hub and an emergency reserve, described as a "hospital bin" for food supplies during crises, according to DMT Collateral Managing Director Paul Minlah. The project involves a €4.8 million export credit facility and an additional US$5 million investment, with DMT Collateral managing technical development and financing. Local capacity will be built through training for National Service personnel and Ghanaian technicians.

Minister of Food and Agriculture Eric Opoku stated the facility addresses inadequate storage infrastructure despite increased rice and maize production, which strains the National Food Buffer Stock Company's capacity. This Wa project is the first phase of a national expansion, with 15 to 20 similar facilities potentially planned across the country. Omanbapa AgriTech Limited also presented publicity materials for the upcoming 42nd National Farmers’ Day celebration.

Why it mattersGhanaian farmers will benefit from reduced post-harvest losses and improved storage for their rice and maize harvests.

Ghana chamber seeks industry-wide financing collaboration

The Ghana Chamber of Clean Energy is pushing for joint efforts to overcome funding hurdles in the electric two-wheeler sector.

The Ghana Chamber of Clean Energy (GCCE) is calling for increased collaboration between government, financial institutions, and industry players to address financing challenges in Ghana's electric two-wheeler sector. Ms. Emmanuella Biney, GCCE Programmes and Outreach Manager, stated that solving these constraints requires more than just capital, but also suitable financing structures, stronger institutional ties, and supportive policies to boost investor confidence.

Ms. Biney made these remarks at a stakeholder workshop in Accra on Tuesday, organized by the GCCE and the William Davidson Institute at the University of Michigan. The event gathered manufacturers, assemblers, retailers, and financial institutions to discuss funding barriers and explore policy solutions. Ms. Biney emphasized that no single entity can solve these issues, necessitating coordinated action.

The workshop identified seven preliminary financing gaps, including the limited implementation of import duty waivers for electric two-wheeler companies and difficulties in securing follow-on investment after grant funding. Other issues highlighted were limited access to affordable local-currency debt and an information gap between companies and capital providers. Findings will inform a joint GCCE and WDI report to guide policy and financing measures for electric mobility businesses.

Why it mattersGhana's emerging electric two-wheeler industry faces financing challenges that hinder its growth and investor confidence.

Mahama defends speed cameras after child deaths

President Mahama says Ghana cannot delay stricter traffic enforcement as children continue to die on roads.

President John Dramani Mahama defended the TraffiTech-GH speed enforcement system, stating that the deaths and injuries of schoolchildren hit by speeding vehicles made stricter traffic regulation necessary. He said Ghana could not afford to delay measures to control speeding while children were losing their lives or suffering permanent disabilities on their way from school. The president stressed that protecting vulnerable road users, especially schoolchildren, must be a priority in the nation's efforts to reduce road crashes.

Speaking at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda, President Mahama argued that protecting lives justified using technology for traffic enforcement. He rejected public concerns about penalty costs and road conditions, stating that road maintenance and traffic law enforcement were not mutually exclusive. He cited the United Kingdom, where speed cameras operate despite imperfect roads, to support his view that enforcement should not wait for all infrastructure issues to be resolved.

Road safety statistics show Ghana recorded 2,949 road crash deaths in 2025, an 18.2 per cent increase from 2,494 in 2024. The crashes also caused 16,714 injuries, and pedestrian knockdowns rose by seven per cent to 2,561 cases. President Mahama stated that controlling excessive speed is an immediate road safety intervention, particularly in areas with many pedestrians and children.

Why it mattersGhanaian schoolchildren are dying or suffering permanent injuries from speeding vehicles on their way home from school.

Politics · 2026-11-05

Ghana, Trinidad and Tobago explore joint reparatory justice mechanism

Ministers discussed a dedicated platform, potentially co-chaired by AU and CARICOM, for engagement with the UK.

Ghana and Trinidad and Tobago are exploring a joint mechanism to advance reparatory justice and engage with the United Kingdom. The proposal emerged from a bilateral meeting between Ghana's Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa and his Trinidad and Tobago counterpart Sean S.M. Sobers during the 2026 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda.

The ministers agreed that existing platforms like the Commonwealth Foreign Affairs Ministers Meeting are too broad for focused reparatory justice discussions. They stressed the need for a dedicated platform to coordinate engagement with the UK and other international partners, emphasizing that reparatory justice should encompass acknowledgement, reconciliation, and non-repetition, not just financial compensation.

Trinidad and Tobago proposed development-focused interventions such as scholarships, healthcare infrastructure support, and educational improvements as part of the reparatory process. Climate cooperation was also identified as a potential area. This initiative follows Ghana's diplomatic success in March 2026 when the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution A/RES/80/250, recognizing the enslavement of Africans as a grave crime against humanity.

Why it mattersGhana's Minister of Foreign Affairs and his Trinidad and Tobago counterpart are discussing a new mechanism for reparatory justice, potentially involving the African Union and CARICOM, to engage with the United Kingdom.
