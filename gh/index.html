MTN Ghana mobile money transactions grow 44.4%

The company plans to invest US$1.1 billion over three years to expand network infrastructure and digital services.

MTN Ghana reported a 44.4 per cent year-on-year increase in the value of Mobile Money transactions for the first half of 2026. The company plans to invest US$1.1 billion over three years to expand its network infrastructure and digital services across Ghana.

The company disclosed this at a Regional Media and Stakeholder Engagement in the Volta Region. General Manager for Southern Ghana, David Gyimah Boadi, stated that approximately US$380 million is earmarked for investment in 2026 to improve network capacity and service quality. MTN plans to deploy 500 new network sites in 2026, with the Volta Region set to benefit.

Service revenue reached GH¢15 billion, a 32.3 per cent increase, while earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation rose by 47.1 per cent to GH¢9.3 billion. Profit after tax increased by 46.8 per cent to GH¢5.1 billion. Mobile Money transaction volumes and revenue each grew by 23.3 per cent, with active users up 3.1 per cent.

Why it mattersMTN Ghana's expansion plans and financial performance will impact businesses and consumers through improved digital services and network infrastructure.

Ghana vows justice for teen shot in New York

Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa contacted the victim's father to offer government support.

Ghana's government has pledged to seek justice for 15-year-old Javits Frimpong, a Ghanaian national fatally shot in the Bronx, New York, on Thursday, October 29. Unidentified assailants reportedly shot Javits at approximately 9:20 a.m. while he was en route to school.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs issued a statement on Friday, October 30, condemning the killing and assuring the family of the government's commitment to holding perpetrators accountable. Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa spoke directly with the victim's father, Thomas Frimpong, expressing condolences and promising government support.

Ghana's Permanent Mission in New York has engaged with US authorities to investigate the shooting and provide legal and consular aid to the family. The Ministry reaffirmed its dedication to safeguarding the safety and dignity of Ghanaians abroad.

Why it mattersGhanaian citizens living abroad are directly affected by violence and the government's commitment to their safety.

Four arrested for leasing land to illegal miners

Unit committee members allegedly took money from galamsey operators who degraded land near Abesua bridge.

Four people, including two Unit Committee members, were arrested in Ghana's Ashanti Region for their role in illegal mining that caused the Abesua bridge to collapse. The bridge, the sole access for several communities, fell after galamsey operators excavated land around the River Kukubu, leading to its overflow. The arrests followed public outcry after a JoyNews report exposed the damage.

District Chief Executive Ibrahim Issahak confirmed that preliminary investigations link local leaders to the illegal miners, stating the Unit Committee members allegedly collected money and leased land for mining near the bridge and river buffer zone. The four suspects are in police custody and will appear in court on November 3, 2026, facing charges for illegal mining and damaging public infrastructure.

The bridge collapse has halted economic activities, preventing farmers from reaching markets and disrupting healthcare access. Schoolchildren struggle to attend classes as the swollen river makes crossing impossible. Emergency repair work is underway to make the road motorable while permanent reconstruction plans are considered.

Why it mattersResidents of Abesua and surrounding communities are cut off from markets and essential services due to the collapsed bridge.

Minister calls for better MahamaCares patient outreach

Northern Regional Minister Ali Adolf John Mburidiba stressed the need for public education on the Ghana Medical Trust Fund in Tamale.

Northern Regional Minister Ali Adolf John Mburidiba called for intensified public education and stronger stakeholder collaboration to ensure eligible patients in the region benefit from the Ghana Medical Trust Fund, known as MahamaCares. He spoke yesterday at a forum in Tamale organised by the Fund, which included representatives from health institutions, Metropolitan, Municipal and District Assemblies, traditional authorities, civil society organisations, and healthcare professionals.

Mr Mburidiba described the forum as an opportunity to strengthen collective efforts to improve healthcare access and protect vulnerable households from the financial burden of serious illnesses. He stated MahamaCares is part of President John Dramani Mahama's healthcare agenda, aiming for an integrated system combining disease prevention, early detection, routine treatment, health insurance, and access to specialised medical services.

The minister highlighted that the Ghana Medical Trust Fund addresses gaps in healthcare financing, making specialised treatment more accessible, equitable, and financially sustainable, particularly for residents of rural and hard-to-reach communities. Tamale Teaching Hospital and other designated private hospitals will serve residents, but Mr Mburidiba stressed the need for adequate information on the Fund's coverage, eligibility requirements, and application procedures for the initiative to succeed.

Why it mattersEligible patients in Ghana's Northern Region may not understand or benefit from the Ghana Medical Trust Fund, facing financial burdens from serious illnesses without adequate information.

Ghana Water honours GIHOC Distilleries and KANE-EM Limited

The water utility recognised two major commercial clients for their prompt bill payments at an event in Accra.

Ghana Water Limited's Accra West Region recognised GIHOC Distilleries and KANE-EM Limited yesterday for consistently paying their water bills on time. The event, held in Accra as part of Customer Service Month, aimed to strengthen relationships between the utility and its commercial clients.

Ing. Francis Lamptey, Regional Chief Manager, stated that timely payments enable Ghana Water to meet operational expenses, including electricity bills, treatment chemicals, repairs, and staff salaries. GIHOC Distilleries CEO, Mr Jones Borteye Applerh, highlighted water's essential role in beverage production and affirmed the company's commitment to their partnership.

The Accra West Region serves approximately 67,000 customers across five districts. As tokens of appreciation, Ghana Water presented GIHOC Distilleries and KANE-EM Limited with G-Water products and souvenirs. The utility plans similar appreciation visits for other commercial customers to foster stronger business ties.

Why it mattersPrompt bill payments by commercial customers like GIHOC Distilleries and KANE-EM Limited help Ghana Water Limited cover operational expenses and sustain its services to 67,000 customers.
