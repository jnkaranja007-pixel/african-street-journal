Health · 2026-11-08

Minority demands answers over Ghana health digital platform shutdown

Parliamentary caucus questions US$64,510.95 cloud hosting bill leading to suspension of critical health systems.

Ghana's Minority Caucus in Parliament is demanding an explanation from the government and the Minister of Health over the suspension of critical Ghana Health Service (GHS) digital platforms. Technology company Akamai suspended the services on October 29, 2026, due to an unpaid cloud hosting bill of US$64,510.95, forcing health facilities to revert to paper-based reporting.

The affected systems include the District Health Information Management System (DHIMS2), Planning and Budget Management Information System (PBMIS), eTracker, HIV and Tuberculosis trackers, and the National Malaria Data Repository. Dr Nana Ayew Afriyie, Ranking Member of Parliament’s Health Committee, stated that the disruption occurred less than a year after the government launched the Ghana Health Information Management System (GHIMS) in October 2025.

The Minority Caucus questions the government's investment in GHIMS and the lack of financial and contractual arrangements to prevent such disruptions, especially with the Ministry of Health's budget increasing to GH¢22.8 billion in 2026. They are calling for the immediate restoration of services, disclosure of GHIMS expenditure, an investigation into the suspension, and an audit of digital health infrastructure and funding.

Why it mattersHealth reporting across Ghana has been disrupted, forcing facilities to use paper systems due to an unpaid US$64,510.95 cloud hosting bill.

Kofi Wayo, Ghanaian politician and businessman, dies at 71

The outspoken figure, known for founding the United Renaissance Party, passed away on November 7, 2026.

Ghanaian politician and businessman Charles Eric Kofi Wayo, known as Kofi Wayo, died at age 71. He passed away in the early hours of Saturday, November 7, 2026, at Nyaho Clinic in Accra.

Wayo, who lived in East Legon, was recognised for his blunt commentary on governance and corruption. He founded the United Renaissance Party (URP) and contested the Ayawaso East parliamentary seat in 2000.

He also advocated for agricultural development and supported farmers by distributing tools. The family has not yet announced funeral arrangements.

Why it mattersThe passing of Kofi Wayo removes a distinctive voice from Ghana's public discourse.

Sport · 2026-11-08

Ghanaian elected West Africa tennis president

Isaac Aboagye Duah will also serve as Vice President of Tennis Africa after an unopposed election in Angola.

Isaac Aboagye Duah, President of the Ghana Tennis Federation, was elected President of the West Africa Zone and Vice President of Tennis Africa on November 7, 2026. He was elected unopposed at the Tennis Africa Annual General Meeting in Luanda, Angola. The West Africa Zone includes 15 countries.

Mr Duah, who has led the Ghana Tennis Federation since 2017, aims to strengthen cooperation in developing the sport across the sub-region. His leadership has focused on inclusive governance and creating opportunities for tennis clubs and foundations. Ghana has seen success in junior competitions and represented the country in the Davis Cup and Billie Jean King Cup under his tenure.

He stated his vision is for an African tennis community united in purpose, strong in governance, and increasingly competitive globally. His new responsibilities will promote stronger cooperation among national federations, expand junior development, and encourage investment in tennis infrastructure and competitions.

Business · 2026-11-08

Accra Brewery launches Wild Roots herbal drink

Wild Roots, a new ready-to-drink alcoholic beverage, is now available in Ghana.

Accra Brewery PLC has introduced Wild Roots, a herbal ready-to-drink alcoholic beverage in Ghana. This new stimulant-free offering was developed in line with regulatory requirements and launched at the company’s premises in Graphic, Accra. The beverage includes selected herbal and fruit flavours, such as wormwood, gentian, and bitter orange, providing a balanced bittersweet taste.

Country Marketing Lead Vanessa Kavi stated that Wild Roots was developed before regulatory changes on stimulants in alcoholic drinks, showing the company’s commitment to innovation and compliance. She explained that the brand draws inspiration from the efforts of hardworking Ghanaians and encourages consumers to acknowledge their achievements. The product was developed without stimulants like caffeine, kola nuts, and guarana, adhering to the Food and Drugs Authority’s directive.

The launch is part of Accra Brewery PLC’s strategy to diversify its portfolio and respond to changing consumer preferences and regulations in Ghana’s alcoholic drinks market. The company recently appealed to the government to delay new excise duty rates on locally produced beer, which are set to increase from 32.5% to 40%.

Why it mattersAccra Brewery PLC has introduced a new product in Ghana, expanding its beverage portfolio.

Culture · 2026-11-08

Asempa FM's Ekosiisen wins best radio programme award

Host Philip Osei Bonsu also named 2026 GJA Journalist of the Year at the 30th awards ceremony.

Asempa FM's current affairs programme, Ekosiisen, received the Best Radio Programme of the Year (Ghanaian Language) award at the 30th Ghana Journalists Association (GJA) Media Awards. The programme was recognised for its Twi-language broadcasting and its contribution to public discourse on national issues. The awards ceremony took place in Accra on Saturday, November 7, 2026.

The same night, Ekosiisen's host, Philip Osei Bonsu, known as O.B., was honoured as the 2026 GJA Journalist of the Year. These dual honours celebrated Asempa FM, a subsidiary of the Multimedia Group Limited. O.B. credited his team's commitment and professionalism for the programme's success.

Ekosiisen covers political developments, public affairs, and national issues in the Twi language. The 30th GJA Media Awards were held under the theme “Celebrating Excellence, Advancing Media Freedom and Combating Hate Speech in the Digital Age”.

Why it mattersGhanaian radio listeners are affected by the recognition of a programme that discusses national issues in their language.
