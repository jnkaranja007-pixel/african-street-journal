Range Rover launches first electric model after year-long delay

The new electric vehicle, built in Solihull, represents a significant shift in the company's electrification strategy despite recent financial challenges.

Range Rover has unveiled its first fully electric model, the Range Rover Electric, following a year-long delay. The vehicle will be manufactured in Solihull, marking a substantial change in the company's electrification program. This initiative is supported by extensive upgrades to its West Midlands facilities.

Parent company JLR has trained ten thousand five hundred workers for electric production, proceeding with modernization despite financial pressures and planned job cuts. New battery and electric drive unit production lines are supporting the launch, with around nine thousand employees in Solihull and an additional one thousand five hundred across the region receiving electrification training.

JLR's Electric Propulsion Manufacturing Centre in Wolverhampton is now producing battery packs and electric drive units alongside internal combustion engines. This launch follows a turbulent year for the carmaker, which experienced falling profits and restructuring after a cyber attack last year.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the company's shift to electric vehicles and its ongoing modernization efforts amidst financial pressures.

Ghana Reference Rate Drops to 10.18% in September

The benchmark rate used by commercial banks to price loans saw a marginal decline, potentially easing borrowing costs for some customers.

The Ghana Reference Rate (GRR), a key benchmark for commercial bank lending, decreased to 10.18% in September 2026 from 10.61% in August. This 0.43 percentage-point reduction, calculated by JOYBUSINESS using industry data, was primarily driven by lower Treasury bill and interbank rates. The Bank of Ghana’s Monetary Policy Rate remained stable during this period.

This marginal drop in the GRR, which uses Treasury bill, interbank, and the Monetary Policy Rate in its calculation, could offer some relief to borrowers with variable-rate loans. While those with fixed rates will not see immediate changes, the development may encourage new borrowers as banks compete with lower advertised rates, with some customers reportedly accessing credit between 11% and 12.5%.

Introduced in 2017 by the Bank of Ghana and the Ghana Association of Banks, the GRR aims to provide a transparent benchmark for lending rates. The rate has shown fluctuations throughout 2026, moving from a high of 11.71% in March to lows around 10.02% in June, before its recent decline from August's 10.61% to September's 10.18%.

Why it mattersBorrowers with variable-rate loans may see their lending costs decrease due to the lower benchmark rate.

Shop owner fined for skipping sanitation day

A Hohoe Magistrate Court sentenced a shop owner to a GHS 240 fine or one month imprisonment for failing to participate in a mandatory clean-up exercise.

Esther Ofori, a provision shop owner in Hohoe, has been fined GHS 240 by the Hohoe Magistrate Court for refusing to participate in a communal clean-up exercise. The court also imposed a one-month jail sentence if the fine is not paid. Ofori pleaded guilty to the charge, which contravenes section 21 of the Hohoe Municipal Assembly bye-laws.

The clean-up exercise, organized by the Hohoe Municipal Assembly, is held on the first Thursday of every month between 0600 and 1000 hours as part of National Sanitation Day. During these hours, all businesses, except those providing emergency health or humanitarian services, are prohibited from operating. Business owners are required to use their own resources to clean their premises and surrounding areas, including desilting drains.

On July 2, 2026, at approximately 0922 hours, a Municipal Sanitation Taskforce observed Ofori operating her shop and selling to customers in the Atabu area. Her refusal to join the cleaning efforts led to her being served with a court summons. Environmental Health Analyst Daniel Dotse Eklo prosecuted the case before relieving judge Lambert Y. Keriba.

Why it mattersShop owners and other business operators are directly affected by mandatory clean-up days that restrict operating hours and require participation.

Davis convicted in Tupac Shakur murder case

Duane “Keffe D” Davis was found guilty of murder in Las Vegas, ending a 30-year-old case and raising questions about the unsolved murder of his rival, the Notorious B.I.G.

Duane “Keffe D” Davis has been convicted of the 1996 murder of rapper Tupac Shakur in Las Vegas. The verdict follows a decades-long investigation into the drive-by shooting that killed Shakur. Prosecutors faced an uphill battle, with no eyewitnesses or murder weapon presented at trial, relying instead on Davis's own admissions in media appearances and a police interview.

The conviction of Davis, who admitted his nephew used a firearm he provided to shoot Shakur, brings closure to one of hip-hop's most enduring mysteries. This case's resolution has drawn parallels to the still-unsolved murder of Christopher Wallace, known as the Notorious B.I.G., who was killed in a similar drive-by shooting months after Shakur. Davis was present at a party the night Wallace was killed, and his connection to both cases has long been a subject of speculation.

The successful prosecution of Davis, despite the lack of traditional evidence, highlights the impact of his own words in cracking the cold case. Former prosecutor Neama Rahmani described the decision to charge Davis as a gutsy move, given the case's complexity and the passage of time. The connection between the two iconic rappers' deaths, both central figures in the 1990s East Coast-West Coast hip-hop rivalry, continues to fascinate observers.

Why it mattersThe conviction of Duane Davis for the murder of Tupac Shakur may inspire renewed efforts to solve the parallel, unsolved murder of the Notorious B.I.G., affecting fans and those interested in the cultural impact of these artists.

Accused Threatener Granted GH¢100K Bail

A marketer accused of threatening Economic and Organised Crime Office staff has been granted bail, with his passport ordered deposited.

Kweku Osei Korankye Asiedu, a marketer, was granted GH¢100,000 bail with two sureties by an Accra Circuit Court. Asiedu faces charges including threat of death, offensive conduct, and publication of false information. He pleaded not guilty to all charges. The court also rescinded a previously issued bench warrant for his arrest and ordered him to report to the case investigator monthly.

The prosecution, led by Chief Inspector Wisdom Alorwu, stated that Asiedu was invited for questioning following a video circulated on social media in which he allegedly threatened EOCO staff. The video, recorded on May 29, 2026, reportedly contained statements like, “We know the officers’ houses, we know where they stay and will kill you.” Another video on July 13, 2026, allegedly falsely claimed an order from the Presidency to deny food to an individual in EOCO custody.

These alleged actions, according to the prosecution, were intended to provoke a breach of the peace and undermine EOCO officials' duties. The incidents reportedly instilled fear among EOCO personnel, with the driver of the Executive Director allegedly chased by unidentified men on motorbikes. Asiedu was arrested and allegedly admitted to the offences when cautioned.

Why it mattersThe accused marketer is facing charges for allegedly threatening EOCO staff and publishing false information, which has led to his arrest and subsequent bail.
