Cedi’s year-to-date fall against dollar reaches 10.6 per cent

Bank of Ghana data puts the dollar at GH¢11.69 on the interbank market in November, while Fitch Solutions forecasts further weakness in 2027.

The Ghana cedi’s year-to-date depreciation against the US dollar reached 10.6 per cent in November, according to the Bank of Ghana’s latest Summary of Economic and Financial Data. The dollar traded at GH¢11.69 on the interbank market that month.

Fitch Solutions forecasts that the cedi will average GH¢12.40 to the dollar in 2027. Associate Director Mike Kruiniger said lower gold prices and the government’s foreign-exchange reserve accumulation drive could limit dollar flows into the domestic market.

Kruiniger said tighter dollar supply could make it harder for the cedi to withstand demand and bring back price pressures next year. The report says a weaker cedi would raise the cost of imported goods and production inputs, adding pressure to businesses and inflation.

Why it mattersManufacturers and traders relying on imported raw materials, machinery and finished products face higher costs as the cedi weakens, with Fitch forecasting an average of GH¢12.40 to the dollar in 2027.

Ghana banks face pressure to make digital services work reliably

The Bank of Ghana reported rising mobile-money transaction values and volumes in the first quarter of 2025 compared with a year earlier.

Ghanaian banks are handling more payments, transfers and account services through digital channels, while customers and businesses increasingly depend on them for everyday transactions. The shift is raising expectations for reliable, secure services that work across payment platforms.

The Bank of Ghana’s 2025 Payment Systems Oversight Annual Report said new technologies, changing customer behaviour and cooperation across the financial sector helped make payment channels more efficient, secure and accessible. Its FinTech Sector Report for the first quarter of 2025 recorded higher mobile-money transaction values and volumes than in the corresponding period of 2024.

Banks are being urged to judge digital services by completed customer journeys, not just how many people start an online process. The measures cited include transaction success rates, service availability, complaint volumes, resolution times and continued customer use; branches and service centres remain important for complex issues and disputed transactions.

Why it mattersCustomers and businesses relying on digital banking need transactions to complete; banks are advised to track success rates, complaints and resolution times.

Ghana and US renew cooperation to curb mercury pollution from illegal mining

The partnership will share scientific expertise and technology to improve monitoring and promote mercury-free gold extraction in Ghana.

Ghana and the United States renewed cooperation to tackle mercury pollution from illegal mining at a two-day symposium in Accra, focusing on scientific collaboration, technology transfer and sustainable gold extraction.

US Embassy Chargé d’Affaires Rolf Oslon said contamination threatens freshwater sources including the Pra and Ankobra rivers. He cited mercury levels in the Birim River that exceeded a community water treatment plant’s capacity, affecting its operations.

The symposium was organised through the US Government’s Ambassador’s Water Experts Programme, a joint initiative of the US Departments of the Interior and State. Oslon said the United States supports Ghana’s efforts against illegal mining, including activity financed and undertaken by foreign actors.

Why it mattersThe community water treatment plant affected by mercury in the Birim River has faced contamination beyond its treatment capacity.

Ghana launches 2026-2028 migration strategy to coordinate agencies

Interior Minister Muntaka Mohammed Mubarak says the plan will strengthen migrant protection, border management and action against trafficking and smuggling.

Ghana has launched the International Organisation for Migration (IOM) Strategy for 2026-2028, a plan to coordinate state agencies and strengthen collaboration with society on migration governance. Interior Minister Muntaka Mohammed Mubarak launched the revised strategy with IOM, the Ghana Immigration Service and other state institutions.

Mubarak said the plan would improve systems to identify, refer and protect migrants, support voluntary return and help migrants reintegrate, particularly vulnerable people. He said stronger national referral mechanisms would help ensure vulnerable migrants receive support. The strategy also calls for better migration data, contingency planning and risk monitoring to respond to insecurity, climate vulnerability, displacement and public health emergencies.

IOM Ghana led development of the plan. IOM Chief of Mission Fatou Diallo Ndiaye said the organisation would support Ghana’s efforts and promote policy coordination at national, regional and international levels. By 2028, the strategy is intended to deliver more coordinated border management, stronger action against trafficking and smuggling, improved reintegration support and more reliable migration data.

Why it mattersThe strategy sets out stronger referral and reintegration support for vulnerable migrants, with progress expected by 2028.

Culture · 2026-11-18

Ghana Tourism Society sets three-day youth festival at Legon

The November 19-21 event will combine tourism education, a fair and cultural performances as the society marks its 20th anniversary.

The Tourism Society of Ghana and the University of Ghana will host the 2026 Youth in Tourism Festival from Thursday, November 19, to Saturday, November 21, at the university’s VIP Car Park in Accra. The organisers say the festival is intended to encourage young people to explore domestic tourism and preserve Ghanaian culture.

The programme includes a Tourism Summit, a campus tour featuring the Visit Ghana double-decker bus, a tourism fair and exhibition, Bajoo Night and a health walk. The summit is due to bring students, academics and tourism professionals together to discuss entrepreneurship, cultural preservation, destination marketing and careers in the sector.

The festival coincides with the Tourism Society of Ghana’s 20th anniversary. Hotels, travel agencies, tour operators, airlines and other businesses are among the organisations the society has asked to support the event.

Why it mattersHotels, travel agencies, tour operators and other tourism businesses are being asked to support the festival running from November 19 to 21.
