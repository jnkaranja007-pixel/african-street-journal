Ghana economy ranks eighth in Africa

President Mahama announced Ghana's Gross Domestic Product reached $114 billion in 2025, making it the second-largest economy in West Africa.

President John Dramani Mahama announced Ghana is now Africa’s eighth-largest economy, with a Gross Domestic Product of approximately $114 billion in 2025. Speaking at the Ghana Investment Summit 2026 in Accra, Mahama stated the country is the second-largest economy in West Africa. The International Monetary Fund projects Ghana’s GDP to reach $128.64 billion by the end of 2026, maintaining its current economic standing.

Mahama reported Ghana’s economy grew by six per cent in 2025 and is projected to grow by 5.7 per cent in 2026. Inflation has fallen from 23.5% in January 2025 to 5.5 per cent in October 2026, contributing to a more stable business environment. Gross International Reserves stood at $12.05 billion in September 2026, covering 4.5 months of imports.

The nation’s currency, the Cedi, has shown greater stability, appreciating by 40.7 per cent against the US dollar in 2025 and remaining seven per cent stronger in September 2026 compared to the previous year. S&P Global affirmed Ghana’s B-/B rating with a stable outlook, while Fitch upgraded the country’s rating to B with a positive outlook.

Ghana exports to China hit $290 million

China's zero-tariff policy boosted Ghanaian shipments to $290 million in June, with more market access expected.

Ghanaian exports to China reached $290 million in June 2026, a surge attributed to China's zero-tariff policy. This initiative aims to expand market access for local exporters into the Chinese market.

Chinese Ambassador Cong Song announced the figures at a forum in Accra, noting that bilateral trade reached $14.12 billion in 2025. Ghana's exports to China that year were $2.67 billion, a 33.9 per cent increase. The policy removes tariffs on African goods without reciprocal market opening or volume limits.

The zero-tariff policy is expected to reduce costs for African products, improve their competitiveness, and attract investment. The Minister for Foreign Affairs stated the government seeks partnerships that establish local production and create jobs, urging Ghanaian exporters to meet Chinese market requirements.

Why it mattersGhanaian exporters can now access China's market of 1.4 billion people with reduced costs and no reciprocal demands, following the implementation of a zero-tariff policy.

Ghana's new cedi notes spark debate over portraits

The Bank of Ghana's new currency series has ignited public discussion about replacing human faces with national symbols.

The Bank of Ghana (BoG) launched its new Ghana Cedi Heritage Series on Thursday, November 12, 2026, aiming for stronger and safer banknotes. However, public reaction quickly shifted from monetary policy to debates over the chosen figures, the replacement of the "Big Six," and the identity of the portrait on the GH¢50 note, reportedly Theodosia Okoh, Ghana's flag designer.

This recurring debate highlights the challenges of featuring individuals on currency in a diverse society, potentially leading to division and costly redesigns. The BoG and government are advised to consider neutral, unifying imagery like architecture, landscapes, wildlife, festivals, and traditional art to avoid partisan disputes and reputational risks associated with visual errors on currency.

Globally, central banks are moving away from human faces on banknotes to promote social cohesion and timeless appeal. Examples include the Eurozone's use of generic architectural styles, South Africa's "Big Five" wildlife, and Costa Rica's celebration of biodiversity, demonstrating how currency can foster national pride without favouring specific groups or historical figures.

Why it mattersThe Bank of Ghana's decision to feature specific individuals on new banknotes has led to public debate and calls for withdrawal and reprint, impacting taxpayer-funded currency production.

Ghana re-elected to ITU Council and wins Radio Regulations Board seat

Ghana's National Communications Authority director-general Edmund Yirenkyi Fianko polled 173 of 177 votes for a Radio Regulations Board seat.

Ghana was re-elected to the ITU Council for the 2027 to 2030 term at the Plenipotentiary Conference in Doha, Qatar, alongside 12 other African countries. The Council oversees the International Telecommunication Union's programmes, budget and finances between its four-yearly conferences.

Rev. Ing. Edmund Yirenkyi Fianko, Director-General of the National Communications Authority, was re-elected to the 12-member Radio Regulations Board, which approves the rules for applying the international Radio Regulations and hears disputes. Members sit as independent experts, not national delegates. Fianko received 173 of a possible 177 votes, the highest among all Radio Regulations Board candidates across the five ITU regions, according to Communication Minister Samuel Nartey George.

Fianko was first elected to the Board in 2022 with 126 votes. Ghana has held a seat on the ITU Council without a break since 2002, and Fianko is only the second Ghanaian to serve on the Board, after the late Major (Rtd) John Ray K. Tandoh, elected in 1998. The Minister announced both results on Monday, 16 November 2026.

Why it mattersFianko's Board seat shapes decisions on spectrum and satellite orbits, which set connectivity costs and reach across Africa.

Sport · 2026-11-17

Ghana's Black Challenge thrash Iraq 6-0 to reach Amputee World Cup last 16

The team finished second in Group D behind Angola, and will now face the USA on Wednesday, November 18, for a place in the quarter-finals.

Ghana's Black Challenge beat Iraq 6-0 in their final group game of the WAFF Amputee Football World Cup in Mexico, securing a place in the Round of 16. Needing a convincing win after a 2-0 defeat to Angola and a 1-1 draw with Colombia, the team took control early, with Mohammed Mubarik opening the scoring with a long-range strike.

Hamza Mohammed doubled the lead before the break, and Ghana kept up the pressure after half-time to complete the emphatic win. Coach Richard Obeng's side finished second in Group D, behind Angola, who beat Colombia 2-0 in the other group fixture.

Ghana will play the United States in San Juan de los Lagos on Wednesday, November 18, for a place in the last eight as they continue their quest for a first Amputee Football World Cup title.

Why it mattersA win over the USA on Wednesday would put Ghana into the quarter-finals, the furthest the team has reached at this tournament.
