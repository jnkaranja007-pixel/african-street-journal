Telecel Ghana Foundation helps over 500 patients go home

The Telecel Ghana Foundation's Homecoming initiative paid medical bills for insolvent patients at 16 government hospitals.

The Telecel Ghana Foundation launched its 2026 Homecoming initiative to help over 500 insolvent patients leave 16 government hospitals nationwide. The program began at Shai Osudoku District Hospital in Dodowa, where the Foundation paid the outstanding medical bills for 29 patients who were ready to go home. Telecel Ghana also provided National Health Insurance Scheme registration and renewal for 120 individuals.

Rita Agyeiwaa Rockson, who leads the Foundation, Sustainability and External Communications at Telecel Ghana, stated that the initiative aims to prevent financial hardship from keeping recovered patients from their families. Since 2011, Homecoming has assisted financially vulnerable patients and helped hospitals recover bills, freeing up beds. The Foundation also donated maternal-health equipment to Shai Osudoku District Hospital.

Dr Alexander Adjei, Medical Director of Shai Osudoku District Hospital, called the intervention a vital example of how partnerships can improve healthcare. Florence, a beneficiary, shared her relief after two weeks of uncertainty, wanting to return home to rest and care for herself and her baby.

Cenpower marks decade of Ghana power plant

The 350 MW Kpone Independent Power Plant has supplied electricity to one million households since 2019.

Cenpower Generation Ltd celebrated a decade since the sod-cutting ceremony for its 350 MW Kpone Independent Power Plant (KIPP). The event, themed “Powering Ghana’s Future Through Reliable and Sustainable Power Generation,” brought together government officials, shareholders, and energy sector stakeholders.

The company, incorporated in 2003 by four Ghanaian entrepreneurs, began developing KIPP to meet Ghana’s electricity demand. The project achieved financial close in December 2014, with sod-cutting on 29 January 2015, and commenced commercial operations on 10 June 2019.

Since beginning operations, KIPP has supplied enough electricity for about one million homes. Vice President Professor Jane Naana Opoku-Agyemang described Cenpower as an example of Ghanaian enterprise and private sector participation in national infrastructure development.

Why it mattersGhanaian entrepreneurs and private investors are contributing to the nation's energy infrastructure and supply.

UN advocate calls for financial health council in Ghana

Queen Máxima urges coordinated efforts between government and private sector to boost citizen financial well-being.

Queen Máxima of the Netherlands, the UN Secretary-General’s Special Advocate for Financial Health, has called for Ghana to establish a Financial Health Council. This council would coordinate government and private sector actions aimed at improving the financial well-being of Ghanaians. She spoke in Accra at the joint inauguration of the Working Committee on the National Financial Inclusion and Development Strategy, which will develop a strategy for 2027 to 2031.

Queen Máxima stated that financial inclusion, while important, does not automatically lead to financial security or better livelihoods. She outlined four key components of financial health: managing daily finances, withstanding financial shocks, achieving long-term goals, and maintaining confidence in one's financial situation. Access to services like bank accounts, insurance, and pensions are part of this broader picture.

The committee is tasked with developing the strategy document for implementation between 2027 and 2031. Queen Máxima's call emphasizes that financial inclusion should serve as a means of achieving overall financial health and socio-economic development.

Ghana to track road crash data monthly

President Mahama will compare new TraffiTech system results with last year's figures to measure impact on deaths.

President John Dramani Mahama announced on Thursday, November 5, that Ghana will review road crash data monthly to assess the effectiveness of the new TraffiTech system. The government will compare accident figures recorded since the system began on November 1, 2026, with data from the same period last year. This review aims to measure the system's impact on reducing crashes and fatalities.

Speaking at Jubilee House, President Mahama urged motorists to adhere to speed limits, especially the 50-kilometre-per-hour limit in urban areas, as automated traffic enforcement increases. He expressed confidence that TraffiTech, which replaces manual police checks with automated speed detection, will significantly reduce road deaths. Last year, 2,151 people died in road crashes in Ghana, including 474 pedestrians, many of whom were schoolchildren.

The President stated that the speeding detection component is the only part of TraffiTech currently operational. He assured that a month-to-month comparison with last year's data would reveal surprising reductions in accidents and lives saved.

Why it mattersGhanaian motorists and their families are directly affected by the new automated traffic enforcement system aimed at reducing road fatalities.

Business · 2026-11-06

ECG Accra West meets major power consumers

Electricity Company of Ghana engaged businesses and industries to gather feedback on services and operations.

The Electricity Company of Ghana (ECG) Accra West Region management met with representatives from businesses and industries, known as Special Important Customers (SICs). This meeting concluded the company's October Customer Service Month, themed ‘The Extra Mile’, which focused on improving customer experiences. The SICs contribute about 40 per cent of the region's total revenue.

Discussions during the engagement addressed ECG's billing and payment systems, reasons for power outages and planned solutions, and the company's digital platforms for communication. The Accra West Regional Management team also visited over 70 SICs at their locations to acknowledge their partnership and contributions to ECG's growth. General Manager Mrs Sariel Adobea Etwire thanked customers for their consistent patronage and cooperation.

Mrs Etwire stated that staff are committed to providing prompt, responsive, and customer-focused services, emphasizing that going the extra mile is a commitment to delivering the best possible service. Some customers acknowledged ECG's efforts to improve service delivery and maintain dialogue with the business community.

Why it mattersBusinesses and industries in the Accra West Region are directly affected by ECG's service delivery, billing, and power supply reliability.
