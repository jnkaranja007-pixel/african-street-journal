Ghanaian teenager killed in Toronto shooting

Toronto police are investigating the homicide of 18-year-old Prince Abdul Malik, identified by authorities as Malik Abass.

An 18-year-old Ghanaian, Prince Abdul Malik, was shot and killed on Saturday, November 7, in Toronto, Canada. Toronto police responded to reports of a shooting in the Rathburn Road and The East Mall neighbourhood around 4:27 pm. Officers found the teenager with gunshot wounds and he was pronounced dead at the hospital.

Authorities identified the deceased as Malik Abass, an 18-year-old from Mississauga, who was known to family and friends as Prince Abdul Malik. His family confirmed his Ghanaian background. The suspect or suspects had fled the scene before police arrived, and investigators have not disclosed a motive or made any arrests. This incident brings Toronto’s homicide toll for 2026 to 33.

Investigations into the teenager’s death are ongoing.

Bawumia urges parties to back long-term national plans

The NPP flagbearer proposed multi-decade masterplans for coastal erosion, flood control, and transport after Japan talks.

Former Vice President Mahamudu Bawumia has called on Ghana’s political parties to commit to long-term national development plans that transcend changes in government. He suggested that adopting national masterplans for key sectors would ensure continuity and prevent the scrapping of projects by successive administrations.

Bawumia made this proposal following engagements in Japan with officials from the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA), Nippon Koei, CTI Engineering, and Oriental Consultants Global. Discussions focused on coastal erosion, flood control, disaster risk management, transport systems, and urban planning.

He reflected on Japan’s planning approach, where consensus is built around 30-year objectives, and successive governments implement them. Bawumia believes Ghana could benefit from a similar system, particularly for critical areas like coastal erosion, flood management, and public transport, where political leadership changes often interrupt ongoing projects.

Why it mattersGhanaian political parties are urged to agree on binding, multi-decade masterplans for national development projects.

Ghana government allocates GH¢10m to media fund

President Mahama announced the 2027 budget allocation to boost journalists' welfare, training, and investigative work.

President John Dramani Mahama has committed GH¢10 million from the 2027 national budget as initial funding for the revived Media Development Fund. This fund is intended to enhance journalists' welfare, professional growth, investigative journalism, and safety training.

The president made this announcement during the 30th Ghana Journalists Association (GJA) Media Awards ceremony held in Accra on Saturday. Philip Osei Bonsu from the Multimedia Group was honoured as the GJA/P.A.V. Ansah Journalist of the Year.

The government plans to collaborate with corporate bodies, including the Bank of Ghana, to secure additional contributions for the fund. The awards ceremony celebrated media professionals under the theme “Celebrating Excellence, Advancing Media Freedom and Combating Hate Speech in the Digital Age”.

Why it mattersJournalists in Ghana will benefit from a new fund supporting their welfare, professional development, and safety.

Hearts of Oak beat Heart of Lions 3-0

Ransford Mensah and John Antwi scored for Hearts of Oak in their Ghana Premier League victory.

Accra giants Hearts of Oak secured a decisive 3-0 win against Heart of Lions in a Ghana Premier League Matchweek 10 fixture at the University of Ghana Stadium in Legon on Sunday.

Ransford Mensah opened the scoring in the second minute, with John Antwi adding a brace in the 25th and 48th minutes to seal the victory. Abdul Karim was named the NASCO Man of the Match for his performance.

The win moves Hearts of Oak to 20 points after 10 matches, strengthening their pursuit of league leaders, and provides a boost for coach Nebojsa Kapor's team before their next match against FC Samartex 1996.

Ghanaian researcher targets digital security gaps

Johnnecia Agyemang's framework aims to improve cybersecurity policy compliance and management for US institutions.

Ghanaian researcher Johnnecia Serwaa Agyemang is developing a cybersecurity governance framework to strengthen policy compliance and organisational resilience in the United States. Her work, affiliated with the University of Illinois Springfield, addresses how institutions can better implement and improve cybersecurity policies amidst rising digital threats. The research examines the link between leadership, employee accountability, and effective security management.

Agyemang's research, titled 'A Cybersecurity Governance Framework for Strengthening Policy Compliance and Organizational Cybersecurity Management,' focuses on organisational weaknesses that persist despite technological investments. She emphasizes that effective cybersecurity requires leadership commitment, employee training, and clear responsibilities, not just technology. This approach aims to protect sensitive information and foster a culture of shared security responsibility.

The United States relies heavily on digital infrastructure for essential services, a dependence that is also growing in Ghana. Agyemang's framework is designed to help organisations connect cybersecurity leadership with practical security implementation. Her work seeks to contribute to stronger digital security practices and reduce vulnerabilities caused by inconsistent policy application.

Why it mattersGhanaian institutions may benefit from improved cybersecurity governance and resilience as a result of this research focused on US institutions.
