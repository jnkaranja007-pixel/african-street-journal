Ghana Study Finds Oranges, Sardines Reduce Air Pollution Symptoms

A new Ghanaian study suggests that a diet rich in oranges, peanuts, sunflower oil, eggs, and sardines can help street vendors in Accra mitigate health issues caused by prolonged.

Researchers from the University of Cape Coast found that street vendors who consumed specific antioxidant-rich foods reported fewer symptoms associated with air pollution. The study, led by Professor Kofi Amegah, followed 307 vendors in Accra for 12 months, with one group receiving a weekly supply of these foods while a control group maintained their usual diets.

The findings indicate significant reductions in health complaints among the intervention group. They were 55 percent less likely to develop a persistent cough, 60 percent less likely to experience difficulty breathing, and 81 percent less likely to suffer dizziness. The most notable difference was an 92 percent reduction in irregular heartbeat, and a 66 percent decrease in hospital admissions for the studied symptoms.

The study focused on vendors working along Accra's busiest roads, including Atomic Junction, Madina, and Kwame Nkrumah Circle, where air quality monitors recorded average PM2.5 levels of about 40 micrograms per cubic meter, exceeding the World Health Organization's recommended 15 micrograms per cubic meter. Professor Amegah noted that the biological basis for these results is that antioxidants help neutralize pollutants.

Why it mattersStreet vendors and other outdoor workers in Accra who are exposed to high levels of air pollution may find a practical way to reduce their health risks by incorporating specific foods into their diet.

DirectAid distributes GH¢2 million in flood relief to 1,500 families

Humanitarian organization DirectAid began distributing food and household items to residents of Alajo, Accra, as part of a GH¢2 million relief program for June 2026 flood victims.

DirectAid started distributing relief items to flood-affected residents in Alajo, Accra, on Tuesday, August 25. This initiative is part of a larger GH¢2 million program designed to support approximately 1,500 families across five communities impacted by the June 2026 floods. Beneficiaries in Alajo received packages containing rice, cooking oil, spaghetti, beans, tomatoes, mattresses, bedsheets, blankets, towels, and cleaning products.

Sami Henedak Ahmed, Country Director of DirectAid, stated that the intervention aims to support families whose homes and livelihoods were affected by the June floods. The relief items were provided through the support of DirectAid’s donors in Kuwait, with assistance from government officials coordinating the exercise. The organization also plans to extend support to clinics and hospitals by donating medical supplies.

The distribution will continue in Alogboshie on Wednesday, Kasoa on Thursday, and Nana Krom on Saturday, concluding next week with a final distribution in Abeka. The June 2026 floods resulted in the loss of many lives and destroyed property valued at millions, affecting nearly 92,000 people and killing 39, according to Muntaka.

Why it mattersResidents in Alajo and four other communities affected by the June 2026 floods are receiving essential food and household items, directly addressing their immediate needs for recovery.

US Judge Rejects Ghislaine Maxwell's Conviction Appeal

A U.S. judge dismissed Ghislaine Maxwell's attempt to overturn her conviction and 20-year prison sentence for assisting Jeffrey Epstein in sex abuse.

U.S. District Judge Paul Engelmayer in Manhattan rejected Ghislaine Maxwell’s claims as meritless, stating that most were frivolous or based on arguments previously lost. Maxwell, who represented herself, sought a writ of habeas corpus to declare her punishment unlawful, arguing that new evidence from the Epstein Files Transparency Act was relevant.

Judge Engelmayer’s 67-page decision, made public on Tuesday, stated that any relevant new evidence incriminates Maxwell or reinforces the correctness of prior legal rulings. He also called her claim that prosecutors illegally withheld evidence "conclusory, cynical, and wrong." Prosecutors had argued Maxwell’s latest claims were baseless, filed too late, or failed to show her trial was unfair.

Maxwell had also asserted that lawyers for Epstein’s accusers acted as “De Facto Prosecutors” and that prosecutors did not conduct their own investigations. Jurors convicted Maxwell on five charges for recruiting and grooming underage girls for Epstein between 1994 and 2004. She is currently housed at a minimum-security federal prison camp in Bryan, Texas.

Why it mattersThis ruling directly affects Ghislaine Maxwell, confirming her 20-year prison sentence and denying her last broad effort to overturn her conviction.

Gold Fields flags Ghana license risk as half-year profit surges

Uncertainty over the renewal of Gold Fields’ mining leases in Ghana is weighing on the company’s valuation, despite an 81% jump in half-year profit.

Gold Fields reported an 81% increase in half-year profit, driven by higher gold prices and output, but its CEO Mike Fraser stated that the uncertainty surrounding the renewal of its Tarkwa mine leases in Ghana is affecting investor valuation. The leases are set to expire in April 2027, and the company has not yet received a formal response to its renewal application submitted in November 2025.

Fraser indicated that the market has largely discounted the Tarkwa asset in Gold Fields’ portfolio, causing its shares to trade at a discount compared to peers. He emphasized that an early resolution would benefit all parties involved, noting that there are "a lot of influences" in the decision-making process without further elaboration.

The company is considering all available options, including exercising its legal rights under the leases, which Fraser described as a last resort to protect shareholder value. Ghana’s Minerals Commission CEO, Isaac Andrews Tandoh, denied in May that the government was delaying renewals, stating that Gold Fields must present its development plans for review.

Why it mattersThe uncertainty surrounding mining lease renewals directly impacts Gold Fields' operations and valuation, affecting its shareholders and potentially the Ghanaian mining sector's regulatory environment.

Ghana Maritime Authority commissions 4 boreholes, improving student attendance in Wa

The Ghana Maritime Authority and Humanity First Ghana have installed four solar-powered boreholes in Wa, ending water scarcity and reducing school disruptions for students.

The Ghana Maritime Authority (GMA), in partnership with Humanity First Ghana, has commissioned four new solar-powered mechanized boreholes in the Wa Municipality. These facilities, located at T.I. Ahmadiyya Senior High School, Chansa, Biihee, and Boli, are part of a larger seven-borehole Corporate Social Responsibility initiative aimed at addressing severe water distress and improving public sanitation across the Upper West Region.

Naval Captain (Rtd) Dr. Kamal-Deen Ali, Director General of the GMA, stated that the intervention aligns with national development priorities to provide clean, potable water to underserved communities. He emphasized that bringing these facilities directly to schools and communities prevents pupils and students from leaving premises to search for water, thereby protecting instructional hours and enhancing their studies.

The new boreholes have brought immediate relief to beneficiary schools. At T.I. Ahmadiyya Senior High School, Headmaster Issahaque Yahaya noted that reliable water helps manage sanitation for 1,433 students. Students reported a decrease in typhoid cases and improved hygiene routines, while at Boli M/A Primary B, Headteacher Osman Issah confirmed that pupils no longer leave school during instructional periods to fetch water.

Why it mattersStudents in the Wa Municipality now have consistent access to clean water at their schools, preventing academic disruptions caused by water scarcity and improving their health and attendance.
