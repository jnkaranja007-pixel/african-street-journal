Ghana businesses face 120% collateral barrier for loans

The Association of Ghana Industries says high loan security demands prevent firms accessing credit despite falling interest rates.

Businesses in Ghana are struggling to get bank loans because lenders demand collateral worth up to 120% of the borrowed amount. Eric Defor, Chairman of the Economic Affairs Committee of the Association of Ghana Industries (AGI), stated this on Wednesday, November 11. He noted that despite improved liquidity and lower interest rates in the financial sector, these high collateral requirements remain a significant hurdle for companies needing credit to grow.

Mr Defor explained that while banks have more funds available, partly due to the repayment of obligations from the Domestic Debt Exchange Programme, accessing these funds is difficult. He highlighted that some banks are now asking for security valued at nearly 120% of the loan amount. He acknowledged banks' caution following Ghana's financial crisis but argued that these lending conditions hinder businesses from benefiting from the improving economy.

The AGI official urged the government to address these constraints as it prepares the 2027 Budget. He also called for more transparency on where banks are directing their funds, expressing concern that money might be flowing into government securities instead of business lending. Mr Defor stressed the need for reliable data to understand the flow of funds, especially considering the Bank of Ghana's liquidity management operations.

Why it mattersGhanaian businesses are unable to secure loans for expansion due to excessive collateral demands from banks.

Ghana urged to renegotiate low carbon credit prices

Minority Chief Whip Frank Annoh-Dompreh says the proposed US$5 to US$25 per tonne range undersells Ghana's environmental resources.

Minority Chief Whip Frank Annoh-Dompreh has urged the government to renegotiate the terms of Ghana's proposed carbon credit agreement. Speaking in Parliament on Wednesday, the Nsawam-Adoagyiri MP questioned a pricing range of US$5 to US$25 per tonne, which he set against a global average of about US$40 per tonne. He asked the sector minister and negotiating authorities to review the financial terms before Ghana commits to the arrangement.

Referring to page eight, paragraph 5.6 of the committee's report, he said the pricing gap warranted closer scrutiny. He acknowledged Ghana's carbon market agreements with Switzerland, Sweden and Singapore, and the proposed arrangement with South Korea, saying such partnerships must stay consistent with Ghana's Nationally Determined Contributions. He also called for methane recovery from landfill sites to cover more facilities, citing harmful leachate at the Sumia landfill in his constituency.

He pressed the government to bring its carbon credit agreement with British Petroleum (BP) before Parliament, saying negotiations had concluded and the Attorney-General had reviewed the terms. He urged the minister to submit it before the House rose, if time allowed. Despite his concerns, he encouraged MPs to support the committee's report and the agreement, while maintaining that fair prices and local environmental problems must remain priorities.

Why it mattersCommunities near landfill sites such as Sumia, where leachate is destroying plant and animal life, would be affected by any expansion of methane recovery.

Ghana's Nana Dr. Antwi-Boasiako Amoah presses COP31 on climate finance

As chair of the African Group of Negotiators, Ghana's envoy says developed countries have no excuse to renege on their climate finance commitments.

Ghana is pressing for wider access to international climate finance at the UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Türkiye. Nana Dr. Antwi-Boasiako Amoah, a Ghanaian who chairs the African Group of Negotiators, is leading that push for vulnerable economies to carry out their climate action plans.

At a press conference on Africa's position, Amoah said developed countries had no excuse to renege on their climate finance commitments under the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change. He said developing countries had done nothing wrong in their historical emissions. Ghana's Financial Stability Review warns that flood-related losses could reach about US$200 million a year by 2050.

The 2027 Budget will show how much Ghana can invest in flood protection, agricultural resilience and disaster preparedness. If developed countries cut bilateral climate assistance, the government may have to borrow more domestically or commercially to fund that work.

Why it mattersFarmers, public borrowers and households face the cost of flood, drought and irregular rainfall damage, which the packet links to government spending and food supplies.

Ghana urges bankable green projects at COP31

Minister Seidu Issifu called for concrete outcomes from China-Africa climate commitments at the Antalya conference.

Ghana has urged for stronger efforts to transform China-Africa green development commitments into bankable projects with mobilised financing. Minister of State for Climate Change and Sustainability, Seidu Issifu, made the call on Wednesday at the Ghana Pavilion during the 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Turkey.

Mr Issifu highlighted China’s pledge under the FOCAC Beijing Action Plan to support 30 clean energy and green development projects across Africa. He stressed that success depends on translating these pledges into viable projects capable of attracting investment and delivering measurable benefits, noting Ghana's selection to receive financial support from the Global South.

The Jospong Group's General Manager for Green Transition, Dr Glen Gyimah, stated that Ghana would host the secretariat for participating countries receiving this support. This funding aims to facilitate electric technology, green industrialisation, climate-smart agriculture, and water security initiatives, with Ghana seeking to convert its climate vulnerability into a bankable pipeline budget.

Why it mattersGhanaian institutions and the private sector can now access funds to develop bankable green budgets for climate resilience projects.

Teenager charged with murder over Ghanaian boy's death in New York

A 17-year-old suspect faces murder charges for the fatal shooting of 15-year-old Javits Frimpong in the Bronx.

A 17-year-old has been charged with murder in the fatal shooting of 15-year-old Ghanaian Javits Frimpong in the Bronx, New York. The New York Police Department arrested the suspect on Wednesday morning and charged him with murder. Javits was shot on October 29, 2026, and later pronounced dead at St. Barnabas Hospital.

Javits' father, Thomas Frimpong, who brought his son from Ghana for education, expressed relief at the arrest. Javits' mother, Doris Gyamfi, travelled from Ghana seeking justice for her only child. US civil rights lawyer Ben Crump stated Javits, who dreamed of becoming a doctor, had moved to the US about a year before his death.

Hundreds attended a rally in the Bronx on Saturday under the banner “Justice for Javits”. Ghana's Ministry of Foreign Affairs is engaging with US authorities and the family, with the government covering travel costs for Javits' mother and contributing to funeral expenses. Ghana’s UN Mission is also working with Ben Crump to seek justice for Ghanaians killed in the US.

Why it mattersThe killing of Ghanaian teenager Javits Frimpong in New York has prompted government engagement and financial support for his family seeking justice.
