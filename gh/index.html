Ghanaian judicial unions suspend strike

JUSAG and SSAJUG call off industrial action after government engagement on salary reviews.

The Judicial Service Staff Association of Ghana (JUSAG) and the Senior Staff Association of the Judicial Service of Ghana (SSAJUG) have suspended their indefinite strike. The unions announced the suspension, effective November 10, 2026, following engagements with the government over salary reviews and related allowances.

The decision came after the unions received official correspondence from the Office of the President addressing their concerns. A meeting involving the Chief Justice, Justice Gabriel Scott Pwamang, and Judicial Secretary Ahmed Musah confirmed a revised government approval that largely addressed the issues that triggered the strike.

The Minority Caucus in Parliament had appealed to President John Dramani Mahama to intervene, citing the disruption to justice administration and hardship for the public. The strike suspension is expected to allow normal activities to resume while final commitments are processed.

Why it mattersJudicial service staff in Ghana have suspended their strike after negotiations with the government over pay and allowances.

UN declares climate change a global health pandemic

Global temperatures hit 1.47 °C above pre-industrial levels, straining health systems and causing millions of deaths.

Global climate leaders and United Nations officials declared on November 9, 2026, that climate change has become a global health crisis of pandemic proportions. Rising temperatures, ocean heating, and drought are straining health systems and causing millions of premature deaths worldwide, according to a World Meteorological Organisation (WMO) update.

The WMO confirmed global temperatures are 1.47 °C above pre-industrial baselines from January to September 2026, making the past 12 years the warmest on record. El Niño conditions are expected to push sea-surface temperatures nearly four times the standard threshold between November 2026 and January 2027. UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell warned that climate destruction directly destabilises human physiology and national health infrastructures.

Extreme heat waves in 2026 caused an estimated 50,000 excess deaths in Europe, with dangerous heat spikes also recorded in India, Pakistan, and North America. African negotiators at COP31 in Antalya, including Ghana, are pushing for accessible adaptation funding and other finance and trade priorities.

Why it mattersAfrican negotiators, including those from Ghana, are pressing for climate finance and adaptation funding at COP31 in Antalya.

Two officers interdicted over illegal Service Passport scheme

Ministry of Foreign Affairs refers Mahama Yahaya Amantana and Alhassan Sumani to police for investigation into alleged bribery.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs has interdicted two officers, Acting Director of Passports Mahama Yahaya Amantana and junior officer Alhassan Sumani, over an alleged illegal scheme to issue Ghanaian Service Passports to unqualified people for money. The Ministry stated on November 8, 2026, that it had sent both officers to the Ghana Police Service for investigation and possible prosecution. This action follows two petitions received by the Ministry, with the first submitted on October 8, 2026.

Preliminary findings by a committee established on October 13, 2026, confirmed that 10 individuals who did not qualify for Service Passports received the documents between January 2023 and August 2026 after allegedly paying a syndicate. The first petitioner claimed to have paid US$29,000 in 2023, while the second alleged a US$5,000 payment in 2026. Alhassan Sumani, identified as a key figure and alleged mastermind, reportedly operated as a middleman before joining the Foreign Service on November 29, 2024.

The Ministry reaffirmed its commitment to improving passport administration, highlighting reforms such as chip-embedded passports and 24-hour operations. The passport application fee was reduced from GH¢500 to GH¢350. The Vice Chairperson of Parliament’s Foreign Affairs Committee, Cletus Seidu Dapalah, claimed the scheme began in 2023 under the previous New Patriotic Party administration.

Ghana artists tap pop culture for climate action stories

Creative sector leaders and activists met at the Climate x Culture Summit 2026 to discuss integrating climate messages into art.

Ghanaian artists and activists met at the Climate x Culture Summit 2026 to discuss integrating climate change narratives into popular culture. Participants agreed that climate action needs to be embedded within cultural expressions rather than existing separately. Many Ghanaians currently only learn about climate change when its impacts are felt, failing to connect their daily habits to environmental consequences.

Speakers at the summit noted that effective climate communication is lacking, with most people unaware of how actions like dumping waste contribute to flooding. Musician and climate activist Okyeame Kwame's potential reach through a climate song was highlighted as an example of how music can connect with more people than climate reports. Past works by artists like Stonebwoy and M.anifest have successfully brought issues like illegal mining into public discourse.

Africa's creative sector, which generates over two million jobs annually, was recognized for its economic significance and its potential to influence climate policy. The summit emphasized that effective climate stories should be local, human-centered, specific in linking actions to consequences, and offer clear next steps for engagement. Creatives should be involved early in policy discussions, and artists can also document indigenous environmental knowledge.

Why it mattersGhanaians will be better informed about climate change and how their actions contribute to its impacts through relatable cultural narratives.

Ghana minister urges Chinese factories for jobs

Ms Gomashie asked Chinese investors to build factories in Ghana to boost the economy and create jobs.

Ghana's Minister of Tourism, Culture and Creative Arts, Ms Abla Dzifa Gomashie, has invited Chinese investors to establish factories in Ghana. She spoke at the China’s Governance and World Development Global Dialogue series in Accra on Friday, urging a partnership that moves beyond trade and infrastructure. Ms Gomashie stated that Ghana possesses the necessary human resources and skilled workforce to support industrial development and create sustainable employment, particularly for the youth.

The minister encouraged Chinese businesses to partner with local stakeholders in developing Ghana’s tourism sector, promising an enabling environment. She highlighted China’s support for rehabilitating the National Theatre of Ghana, calling it an important investment in the creative arts industry. This project, she said, will improve facilities, promote cultural exchanges, and boost tourism.

Chinese Ambassador to Ghana, Mr Cong Song, expressed China's readiness to support Ghana’s industrialisation through increased investment and value-added production. He said China is willing to share development experience and encourage Chinese enterprises to invest in Ghana. Both officials acknowledged concerns about Chinese nationals' activities and stressed compliance with local laws.

Why it mattersGhanaian youth stand to gain sustainable employment opportunities through the establishment of Chinese factories.
