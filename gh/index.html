Agriculture · 2026-11-15

Ghana to invest $3 billion in commercial agriculture

The government plans to boost jobs and food security through a major investment in agriculture, including the Pwalugu Multipurpose Dam.

Ghanaian Minister of Finance, Dr. Cassiel Ato Forson, announced a US$3 billion investment in commercial agriculture under the New Economy Programme. This initiative aims to drive job creation, enhance food security, and foster industrial growth. A significant component is the US$1 billion Pwalugu Multipurpose Dam, designed to irrigate 25,000 hectares, support aquaculture, enable year-round vegetable production, and generate 70 MW of hydropower.

The strategy includes developing eight integrated agricultural industries and establishing Rice Development Hubs in key regions, bringing an additional 100,000 hectares under rice cultivation. These hubs will feature irrigation, mechanisation, milling, and logistics to boost yields and create approximately 125,000 jobs. Public procurement laws will be amended to prioritise Ghana-grown rice and staple foods for public institutions.

The government also aims for self-sufficiency in sugar production by 2030 through local sugarcane cultivation and commercial mills, supported by import permits. The poultry sector will see development across its value chain, from breeder farms to processing and distribution, with efforts to reduce feed costs by developing commercial maize and soya bean production. An estimated annual fish supply gap of 500,000 metric tonnes will be addressed by boosting aquaculture and restoring fisheries, with a US$500 million World Bank financing arrangement secured for consideration.

Why it mattersThe government will invest US$3 billion in commercial agriculture, aiming to create over 250,000 jobs and achieve self-sufficiency in key food items by 2032.

Education · 2026-11-15

Catholic Bishops demand school abuse investigations

Ghana's Catholic Bishops' Conference is heartbroken by allegations of sexual abuse of minors in schools and calls for immediate investigations.

Minors in Ghanaian schools have been allegedly abused by adults placed in charge of their care, and the Ghana Catholic Bishops’ Conference wants authorities to look into it without delay. The bishops, who met for their annual plenary assembly in Jamasi in the Ashanti Region on Saturday, November 14, issued a communiqué calling the reports heartbreaking.

The bishops named the Ministry of Education, the Ghana Education Service, school authorities and the Police as the bodies that should investigate dispassionately and let the law take its course. In the same communiqué they cited violence, disrespect for authority, examination malpractice and vandalism as signs of declining discipline, including in some Catholic mission schools.

The Conference also urged schools to value moral education and character formation as much as academic results, and to step up prevention against drug trafficking and substance abuse. It called for stronger cooperation between government and religious missions, based on mutual respect, clearly defined responsibilities and fidelity to each institution's founding ethos.

Why it mattersParents of pupils in mission schools now face an open question about how safe their children are, and the Police and education agencies are the bodies that must answer it.

Security · 2026-11-15

Ghanaian man stabbed to death in London

Metropolitan Police have launched a murder investigation into the death of 21-year-old Thomas Oduro.

A 21-year-old Ghanaian man, Thomas Oduro, was stabbed to death on Friday, November 13, in north London. Emergency services were called to Lordship Lane in Haringey around 6 pm after reports of an attack. Paramedics attended the scene but could not save Oduro, who was pronounced dead.

The Metropolitan Police identified the victim as Thomas Oduro from Tottenham and have informed his next of kin. Detective Chief Inspector Phil Clarke is leading the murder investigation and appealed for witnesses who were near Lordship Recreation Ground at the time of the incident to come forward. Increased police presence is expected in the area.

The killing of Oduro follows a series of recent deaths of young Ghanaians abroad, including incidents in the United States and Canada. Investigations into Oduro's death are ongoing, and police are asking anyone with information to contact them via 101 or Crimestoppers.

Why it mattersThe death of Thomas Oduro in London is being investigated by police, with his family receiving support.

Climate · 2026-11-15

Ghanaian disability advocates push for climate action inclusion

Persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected by climate change but excluded from negotiations.

Advocates from the Ghana Federation of Disability Organizations are demanding inclusion at the United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Türkiye. They argue that persons with disabilities are disproportionately vulnerable to climate change impacts, such as floods that destroyed assistive devices like wheelchairs in Accra last June. "Climate change affects all of us, but affects some more than others, and those some are persons with disabilities," stated Peter Anoma-Kodie, Executive Director of the federation.

Anoma-Kodie highlighted that physical limitations make escape during extreme weather events difficult, and slow-onset impacts like heatwaves pose severe health risks, particularly to persons with albinism. A critical barrier identified is the lack of accessible early warning systems and disaster communication, with information often not provided in formats like sign language or live captions. This exclusion turns natural hazards into fatal disasters for disabled populations.

The group seeks a seat at the negotiation table to ensure their specific needs are addressed in climate action plans. They point out that disaster management protocols often overlook accessibility requirements, leaving disabled individuals without timely information or accessible transport during emergencies. The movement, backed by the African Group of Negotiators (AGN), aims to change this narrative at COP31.

Why it mattersPersons with disabilities in Ghana face increased vulnerability to climate events like floods and heatwaves, with a lack of accessible warnings exacerbating risks.

Politics · 2026-11-15

Odai elected NDC Greater Accra Deputy Youth Organiser

Daniel Niibi Afotey Odai won the post by a single vote over his closest rival in Saturday's regional executive elections.

Daniel Niibi Afotey Odai has been elected Deputy Regional Youth Organiser of the National Democratic Congress (NDC) for the Greater Accra Region, after polling 45 votes on Saturday, November 14, 2026. He narrowly beat Atsu Eric Afeme, who received 44, while Jones Klutse won 32 and Rashid Isah eight.

Campaigning under the slogan "Youth in Politics: From Spectators to Decision Makers", Odai pledged a functional Greater Accra Youth Secretariat, a database of young party members across the region's 34 constituencies, and a 24-hour youth hotline. A youth desk officer in every constituency and a welfare scheme for young members are also on his list.

The post is part of the NDC's internal reorganisation ahead of the 2028 general elections. Odai previously spent eight years as Deputy Youth Organiser for the Krowor Constituency and is the District Director of the Youth Employment Agency (YEA) in Krowor.
