Ghana applies to join BRICS as partner

Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa announced the decision, citing expanded economic options and diversified partnerships.

Ghana's government under President John Dramani Mahama has applied to join BRICS as a partner, according to Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa. The move aims to expand Ghana's economic options and diversify international partnerships, Ablakwa stated on October 28, 2026. He revealed that the Cabinet decided Ghana will join the bloc, which includes major emerging economies like Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa.

Ablakwa believes partnering with BRICS will offer Ghana economic options, diversify its base, and foster cooperation within the Global South framework. The bloc brings together 11 major emerging markets and developing countries, serving as a platform for consultation on global and regional issues. However, concerns exist that Ghana could become a market for powerful BRICS economies and see its natural resources exploited without a clear industrial strategy.

Ghana's decision affirms its policy of non-alignment, a strategy previously used by President Kwame Nkrumah to secure support for the Akosombo Dam from the United States and the Ghana Nuclear Reactor Project from the Soviet Union. The Minority in Parliament has threatened a vote of censure against Minister Ablakwa over alleged financial irregularities concerning the evacuation of Ghanaians from South Africa.

Why it mattersGhana's application to join BRICS may offer new economic avenues but also risks exploitation of its natural resources.

FC AshantiGold sack coach Zito and two assistants

Ghana Premier League club dismisses technical team over poor results and internal disunity.

Ghana Premier League side FC AshantiGold 04 terminated the contracts of Head Coach Abdul Karim Kabutey Zito, Assistant Coach Ernest Okyere, and Goalkeepers’ Trainer Robert Dabo on October 27, 2026. The Obuasi-based club announced the decision, which took immediate effect, following a consultative meeting of its Interim Management Committee on Monday, October 26.

FC AshantiGold CEO Edward Kwasi Akuoko attributed the dismissals to inconsistent results and disunity within the technical team. The club has won three of their eight matches this season, placing them 11th in the league with 11 points, and Akuoko cited poor away performances and reports of discord among staff as reasons for the action.

Karim Zito had joined AshantiGold in February 2026, guiding them to promotion to the Ghana Premier League. A caretaker coach will now manage the team until a new substantive head coach and technical director are appointed.

Why it mattersGhana Premier League club FC AshantiGold 04 has sacked its head coach, assistant coach, and goalkeepers' trainer.

Politics · 2026-10-28

five inmates transferred after alleged poisoning plot

Aide says Bernard Antwi-Boasiako was warned of a plot by inmates at Nsawam Prison.

Five inmates were transferred from Nsawam Prison after an alleged plot to poison politician Bernard Antwi-Boasiako, known as Chairman Wontumi. A senior aide, Dr Palgrave Boakye-Danquah, said the plot was discovered when someone overheard inmates discussing plans to poison the former NPP Ashanti Regional Chairman. Mr Wontumi informed his aide about the threat after a court appearance on Tuesday, October 27.

Dr Boakye-Danquah visited the prison over the weekend and confirmed the incident with the officer in charge, who assured him investigations were underway. A committee has been set up to probe the alleged plot, and the five inmates have been moved to a different cell. The aide stated that Mr Wontumi remained calm and unafraid despite the alleged threat to his safety.

The disclosure comes as Mr Wontumi marks 100 days in prison. Dr Boakye-Danquah planned to visit the Nsawam Prison again on Wednesday morning to check on Mr Wontumi's welfare. The aide expressed confidence that the alleged plot would not succeed.

Why it mattersBernard Antwi-Boasiako, a politician, was the target of an alleged poisoning plot by inmates.

Education · 2026-10-28

dilapidated classroom forces wuling pupils to study under trees

a 1990 mud-built block at wuling m/a basic school has developed deep cracks, forcing lessons outdoors.

Pupils at Wuling M/A Basic School in Ghana's Upper West Region are studying under trees because their three-unit classroom block is dilapidated. The mud-built structure, constructed in 1990, has deep cracks and is considered unsafe by school authorities. This forces lower primary classes and kindergarten pupils, who have never had a dedicated facility, to learn outdoors.

Head Teacher Mr Alfred Mwin-ire Ileku stated that rainfall and heat frequently interrupt lessons, sometimes leading to cancellations. He noted that appeals to the Jirapa Municipal Education Directorate have not yet resulted in action. The school has 254 pupils and only 10 teachers, with just two handling the entire Junior High School department.

The Assembly Member for the Tuggo Electoral Area, Mr Paul Bamaaka, has petitioned the Jirapa Municipal Assembly multiple times regarding the school's infrastructure. He is awaiting a response and has appealed for support from corporate organisations and individuals to build a suitable classroom block.

Why it mattersChildren in the Jirapa Municipality are forced to learn outdoors due to a collapsing classroom, exposing them to harsh weather and distractions.

Agriculture · 2026-10-28

Savanna Agri Chains doubles revenue, plans regional expansion

Ghanaian shea butter exporter Savanna Agri Chains aims to increase its sourcing from 22 to 40 districts and expand into Burkina Faso.

Ghanaian company Savanna Agri Chains, which processes and exports shea butter and baobab, has doubled its revenue between 2023 and 2025. Founded in 2016, the company works with over 1,000 women harvesters across 22 districts in Northern Ghana and holds EcoCert and Regenified certifications for its organic and land-restoring practices.

The company now seeks investment to expand its operations, aiming to increase its procurement coverage to 40 districts in Ghana and establish a presence in Burkina Faso's Central South Region. This expansion aims to grow the number of collectors and aggregators from 2,800 to 7,000, positively impacting their household livelihoods.

Savanna Agri Chains was recognized for its environmental impact through land restoration methods like composting and efficient water use. NFIL's support will help formalize the company's operations and prepare it for fundraising to centralize operations and upgrade processing equipment, capturing more local value.

Why it mattersGhanaian farmers and collectors will be directly affected by the expansion of Savanna Agri Chains' sourcing operations.
