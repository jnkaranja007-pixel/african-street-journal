Fitch Solutions forecasts Ghana's 2026 growth at nearly 6%

The firm expects Ghana's economy to slow to 4.7% growth in 2027 due to rising inflation.

Fitch Solutions has maintained its forecast for Ghana's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) to grow by nearly 6.0% in 2026. The UK-based firm noted that Ghana's economy has remained insulated from global events, supported by high gold prices and low domestic inflation.

The Ghanaian economy's oil trade deficit was only 0.3% of GDP in the second quarter of 2026, thanks to offshore fields. Fitch Solutions anticipates a current account surplus of 5.3% of GDP in 2027, which will support Ghana's reserves and limit currency depreciation.

However, Fitch Solutions expects growth to slow to 4.7% in 2027 as inflation rises to 11.3% from 4.7% in 2026. This is due to unfavorable base effects, increased money supply, and higher global food prices, which will reduce purchasing power and private consumption.

Zenith Bank Ghana marks 21 years with health walk

The bank organized a seven-kilometre walk to promote healthy living and strengthen relationships with stakeholders.

Zenith Bank Ghana Limited held a seven-kilometre health walk on Saturday to celebrate 21 years of banking in Ghana. The event, themed “21 Years of Excellence, A Future of Possibilities,” aimed to encourage healthy living and strengthen relationships with the people it serves.

Managing Director and CEO Mr Henry Chinedu Onwuzurigbo led the walk, which started at Zenith Heights and passed through several Accra landmarks before returning. Also present were the Chairman of the Board, Mr Charles Boakye Nimako, and Zenith Bank Sierra Leone MD/CEO Mr Ugochukwu Irechukwu, along with staff, partners, and customers.

Mr Onwuzurigbo stated the walk promoted healthy lifestyles and unity, highlighting the bank's commitment to SMEs through tailored products and digital solutions like the Zenith Virtual Card and GhanaPay. He also pledged a donation to the Ghana Wheelchair Basketball Federation.

Why it mattersZenith Bank Ghana celebrated its 21st anniversary with a health walk involving staff, partners, and customers.

Sakumono murder suspect arrested with pistol in Ashanti Region

Police retrieved a Smith & Wesson pistol and seven rounds of ammunition from the 72-year-old suspect.

Police in the Ashanti Region arrested a 72-year-old man, Samuel Christopher Osei Anto, on Sunday, November 1, 2026, at Tikrom near Ejisu in connection with the deaths of Irene Atubiga and her uncle, George Atubiga. The victims were killed at their Sakumono residence in the Greater Accra Region on Friday, October 30, 2026.

During the arrest at his hideout, police recovered a Smith & Wesson pistol and seven live ammunition rounds from the suspect. The Ashanti Regional Police Command stated that preliminary investigations suggest the murders stemmed from a GH¢500,000 debt dispute, where the suspect allegedly failed to repay money borrowed from George Atubiga.

The suspect is currently in police custody assisting with investigations, and the recovered firearm and ammunition have been secured as exhibits. The victims were identified as Irene, 47, and George, 57.

Why it mattersA 72-year-old man is in custody facing charges related to a fatal shooting following a financial dispute.

Ghana Airport Company Limited marks 20 years

The company pledged to strengthen airport infrastructure and improve passenger experience.

The Ghana Airport Company Limited (GACL) launched its 20th anniversary celebrations last week in Accra, pledging to enhance airport infrastructure and operational efficiency. The company, incorporated in 2006, aims to focus on stronger infrastructure, smarter technology, and improved passenger service in its next development phase. Managing Director Mrs Yvonne Nana Afriyie Opare stated the anniversary is an opportunity to reflect on the company’s journey and renew its commitment to Ghana’s aviation industry.

GACL has overseen significant infrastructure projects including Terminal 3 at Accra International Airport and upgrades to Tamale and Prempeh I International Airports. The company is also repurposing Terminal 2 at AIA for international operations. Mrs Opare highlighted these developments as reflecting Ghana’s ambition to become a competitive aviation hub in West Africa, despite economic uncertainties and the COVID-19 pandemic.

The company's mandate includes planning, developing, managing, and maintaining airports and airstrips across Ghana. Mrs Opare commended staff for their steadfastness and professionalism, describing them as the heartbeat of the organisation. The anniversary theme was "A Legacy of Resilience, Developing Further, Serving Better."

Why it mattersGhana's aviation infrastructure and competitiveness as a regional hub are being developed by the Ghana Airport Company Limited.
