Ghana needs 759 million cedis for health supply chain

The government plans to use domestic resources to fund a four-year master plan amid withdrawal of foreign aid.

Ghana requires over 759 million cedis to address critical gaps in medicine and medical supplies for its health facilities over the next four years. The government is seeking domestic funding for the 2025-2029 health sector supply chain master plan as some foreign aid to the health sector is being withdrawn. This plan aims to ensure health facilities, especially those serving women and children, have essential commodities.

The new master plan is a revision of the 2021-2025 plan, which achieved 35% of its targets due to COVID-19. The revised plan includes 47 strategic interventions across two technical areas to ensure availability, timely access, and affordability of quality health commodities. Dr. Edward Bright Agyekum, Director for Procurement and Supply Chain at the Ministry of Health, stated that local resource mobilization is key, with strategies in place to achieve priorities despite potential donor withdrawal.

The master plan seeks to build an integrated distribution system for medical stores and develop a well-coordinated financial system for the public health sector by 2029. It also includes a mapping exercise to track money flow and expenditures. Daniel Oswald Owusu Afranie, Technical Director with the Global Health Supply Chain, emphasized the need for the country to own the implementation for universal health coverage by 2030.

Why it mattersGhanaian citizens will have improved access to essential medicines and medical supplies in health facilities.

News · 2026-10-24

family demands justice for boy killed at festival

A six-year-old boy was buried on Friday, October 23, 2026, after being shot on October 3, 2026.

The Akwamu Royal Family of Nchiraa in the Bono Region has appealed to President John Dramani Mahama and the Inspector-General of Police to investigate the fatal shooting of six-year-old Agyei Agyapong Gethro during the Fofie Yam Festival. The boy was buried on Friday, October 23, 2026, following the incident on October 3, 2026.

Opanin Paul Kwabena Baffoe, acting head of the royal family, called for an impartial investigation into the shooting and urged authorities to clarify the circumstances and identify those involved. The family expressed concern over recurring gunfire during the festival and asked for accountability from security agencies.

Assembly Member George Nketia also appealed for calm and urged the youth against carrying guns to public gatherings. He stated that those found involved would be arrested, echoing the royal family's call for the police to pursue the matter to its logical conclusion.

Sarah Adwoa Safo seeks NPP Dome-Kwabenya seat again

Former MP Sarah Adwoa Safo announced her decision to contest the NPP parliamentary primaries after consultations with party leaders.

Former Dome-Kwabenya Member of Parliament Sarah Adwoa Safo has declared her intention to run in the New Patriotic Party (NPP) parliamentary primaries for the constituency. This reverses her earlier stated plans to seek opportunities elsewhere. Her decision, announced on Thursday, October 22, 2026, follows discussions with NPP leadership and executives.

Adwoa Safo, who served as the MP for Dome-Kwabenya for 12 years, stated her commitment to the constituency's development. She aims to build on past achievements and address ongoing challenges. The former MP highlighted the NPP's improved vote count in the constituency from 63,373 in 2012 to 75,041 in 2020 under her representation.

She is now calling for engagement with party members, stakeholders, and constituents as she seeks to represent Dome-Kwabenya again in Parliament. Adwoa Safo previously announced she would not contest the seat.

Business · 2026-10-24

Absa Place earns IFC EDGE Advanced green building certification

The new Accra headquarters of Absa Bank Ghana Ltd achieved the certification for energy and water efficiency.

Absa Bank Ghana Ltd’s new headquarters, Absa Place, has earned EDGE Advanced certification from the International Finance Corporation (IFC). The building, located at 64 Independence Avenue, Ridge, Accra, was recognised for its high standards in energy and water efficiency. The IFC’s EDGE Advanced system assesses improvements in energy use, water consumption, and the embodied carbon of construction materials.

The certification affirms the bank’s commitment to developing infrastructure that delivers long-term operational efficiency, according to Absa Bank Ghana Ltd Managing Director Dr Edward Nartey Botchway. IFC’s Principal Country Officer for Ghana, Deo Onyango, stated that the achievement demonstrates how sustainability can contribute to competitiveness and long-term value creation.

Absa Place features a 570-kilowatt-peak solar installation designed to meet at least 80 per cent of the bank’s on-site electricity needs. The building also uses a water-cooled chiller system and a glass façade that blocks over 70 per cent of external heat. In 2025, the bank operated 54 solar-powered ATMs generating approximately one million kilowatt-hours of clean energy annually.

Why it mattersAbsa Bank Ghana Ltd's new headquarters in Accra has been certified for its resource efficiency.
