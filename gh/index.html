Allround Baby Pants Launches Pull-Up Diapers in Ghana

The company introduces Ghana's first pull-up diaper, aiming to simplify baby care for mothers and enhance infant comfort.

Allround Baby Pants has launched its pull-up diapers in Ghana, a product the company claims is the first of its kind in the country. The new diaper is designed to offer mothers a more convenient and hassle-free experience when changing babies, while also providing enhanced comfort for infants. Head of Marketing Abigail Ankomah stated that the product was developed to address a gap in the local market for such convenient baby care solutions.

The company highlighted the diaper's 360-degree fit, which stretches with the baby's movement, ensuring comfort and ease of use. Alongside the product launch, Allround initiated the Authoritative Baby Care Lecture and the Allround Scientific Parenting Academy. These educational programs aim to equip new and expectant mothers with practical childcare knowledge, with plans to expand across more cities and hospitals over the next year.

Medical Director of Ridge Hospital, Dr. Leslie Issah Adam, welcomed the initiative, noting that the parenting lectures support child health efforts by providing information on topics like exclusive breastfeeding, rash prevention, and reducing respiratory illnesses. A mother who has used the product, Konadu-Yiadom Prempeh, praised its absorbency and secure fit, noting that it prevents leakage and stays in place even with an active baby.

Why it mattersMothers in Ghana are affected by the introduction of a new diaper designed for easier use and greater comfort for their babies.

Professor Urges Mahama to Intensify Fight Against Illegal Mining

A finance professor called for stronger presidential leadership against galamsey following the deaths of seven miners in a pit collapse.

Professor of Finance at the University of Ghana Business School, Prof. Godfred Bokpin, has urged President John Dramani Mahama to take more decisive action against illegal mining, known as galamsey. This call comes after a mining pit collapsed at Asaman Tamfoe in the Eastern Region on Saturday, August 29, 2026, killing seven miners. The incident, which occurred underground around 11:00 p.m., has intensified concerns over lives lost in illegal mining activities nationwide.

National Disaster Management Organisation (NADMO) figures show approximately 30 people have died in mining-related incidents in the Eastern Region since the start of 2026. Prof. Bokpin acknowledged President Mahama's successes in other areas but stressed that a leader must address all national challenges, not just convenient ones. He stated that the President's strong mandate from the 2024 election implies high expectations for his leadership across all sectors.

Prof. Bokpin warned that delays in confronting illegal mining will worsen the damage to lives and the environment, making the problem harder to solve over time. He believes President Mahama has the capacity to tackle the galamsey crisis and urged him to "go the extra mile" to prevent failure on this critical issue. The professor emphasized that leadership must be demonstrated during the most significant national challenges.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the continued environmental damage and potential instability caused by unregulated illegal mining operations.

NAIMOS Arrests 10 Illegal Miners, Seizes Excavators

Authorities confiscated mining equipment and firearms during an operation targeting illegal mining activities in the Savannah Region.

The National Anti-Illegal Mining Operations Secretariat (NAIMOS), supported by the Ghana Army and Police Service, apprehended ten individuals suspected of illegal mining in Kpali, Sawla-Tuna-Kalba District. The operation, which took place between August 25 and 27, also resulted in the seizure of two excavators, three excavator monitors, a Toyota Hilux, a fuel bowser, and other mining gear. Three firearms and 25 live cartridges were recovered from the site.

The joint security team acted following a reconnaissance mission on August 19 that confirmed reports of illicit mining. Upon arrival, officers observed active mining operations and significant environmental damage. The arrested suspects include three Chinese nationals and seven Ghanaians. To halt immediate resumption of activities, fifteen makeshift structures and a generator were destroyed.

NAIMOS stated that the arrested individuals and seized items were transferred to the Savannah Regional Police Command for further investigation and legal proceedings. The confiscated heavy equipment has been secured at designated locations. This action is part of ongoing efforts to combat illegal mining and protect the region's water bodies and environment from degradation.

Why it mattersTen suspected illegal miners, including three Chinese nationals, were arrested and mining equipment was seized, impacting the operations of those engaged in illicit resource extraction.

Minister Urges Reading Culture for National Transformation

Tourism Minister Abla Dzifa Gomashie called for a strong reading culture, emphasizing its role in national development and critical thinking.

Abla Dzifa Gomashie, Minister of Tourism, Culture and Creative Arts, urged Ghanaians to cultivate a robust reading culture, describing it as a vital tool for national advancement. Speaking at the 23rd Ghana International Book Fair in Accra, themed "Transforming Ghana’s Future Through Books," she highlighted reading as a crucial investment for the nation's youth.

The Minister explained that reading expands minds, enhances critical thinking, introduces new ideas, and fosters understanding of diverse experiences. She referenced South Korea's development, attributing its progress partly to a societal value placed on education, knowledge, and reading, underscoring that human capital and knowledge are central to sustainable development, not just infrastructure.

Edward Yaw Udzu, Chairman of the Ghana International Book Fair, noted the industry's evolution with digital technologies and urged stakeholders to embrace innovation while maintaining core values. He cited challenges such as high production costs, limited financing, piracy, and declining reading habits, calling for government support for publishing and libraries, and encouraging investment in Ghanaian content and storytelling.

Why it mattersThe citizenry is affected by the call to develop a reading culture, which the Minister stated is a powerful instrument for national transformation and a critical investment for the future.

OSP Faces Delivery Test After Years of Public Support

The Centre for Democratic Development says the Office of the Special Prosecutor must now prove its worth after years of public backing and institutional development.

The Office of the Special Prosecutor (OSP) is at a critical juncture, facing a significant test to deliver on its mandate following years of substantial public support, according to the Centre for Democratic Development (CDD-Ghana). Dr. Kojo Pumpuni Asante, Director of Programmes and Advocacy at CDD-Ghana, stated that the office has spent considerable time establishing its operational structures and recruiting staff, with 2023 and 2024 marking its true operational phase.

Dr. Pumpuni Asante emphasized that while the OSP deserves time to continue its work, there must be a clear expectation of tangible results. He noted that the public's close attention stems from their consistent support since the institution's inception, having defended it against various challenges. This public backing provides a strong foundation but also raises expectations for the OSP's performance.

Instead of a complete reset, Dr. Pumpuni Asante advocates for maintaining progress and strengthening the OSP, viewing it as the appropriate framework for addressing political corruption. He believes the ultimate goal should be to enhance its independence through constitutional protection, but for now, the Supreme Court's affirmation of its legality offers the necessary space to consolidate and continue its operations.

Why it mattersGhanaians are affected by the OSP's performance because they have supported the institution from its establishment and now expect it to deliver on its mandate to tackle political corruption.
