AI strategy risks GH¢500bn target, warns MyJoyOnline

Ghana's new National Artificial Intelligence Strategy, aiming to inject GHc500 billion into the economy, faces potential setbacks from AI-driven job displacement and foreign.

A recent analysis by MyJoyOnline highlights that Ghana's National Artificial Intelligence Strategy (2025–2035) may inadvertently undermine its own goal of adding GHc500 billion to the national economy. The report suggests that while AI is seen as an efficiency booster, the aggressive replacement of human workers with automated software could lead to an 'AI Layoff Trap,' reducing consumer spending.

The analysis points out that AI displacement differs from past industrial revolutions by directly targeting routine cognitive tasks, such as basic legal research, entry-level bookkeeping, and customer service. These roles are crucial entry points for thousands of tertiary graduates entering Ghana's job market annually, and their elimination creates 'Diamond-Shaped Organizations' with fewer junior positions.

MyJoyOnline identifies three critical blindspots in the strategy: a potential collapse in Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) income tax revenue as white-collar workers are replaced, invisible foreign exchange capital flight due to recurring payments for foreign-owned AI models in US Dollars, and the erosion of domestic purchasing power, which threatens consumer-facing businesses.

Why it mattersThis story is important because the displacement of white-collar workers by AI could reduce domestic purchasing power and government tax revenue, affecting many Ghanaians.

Ghana Maritime Authority partners local leaders to improve inland water safety

The Ghana Maritime Authority is collaborating with Upper West Assemblies and traditional leaders to enforce new safety measures on the Black Volta, aiming to prevent recurring.

The Ghana Maritime Authority (GMA) has initiated a partnership with Municipal and District Chief Executives, security heads, traditional authorities, and boat operators in the Upper West Region. This collaboration, part of the GMA’s Life Jackets for Safety and Livelihoods Initiative, seeks to build grassroots consensus and enforce a strict “No Life Jacket, No Boarding” policy to eliminate fatal capsize incidents on inland waterways.

Director-General Naval Captain (Rtd) Dr. Kamal-Deen Ali stated that enhancing inland water safety is a top priority, citing the tragic loss of seven students in a disaster at Dikpe last year. The GMA has established a Black Volta Coordination Office in Wa and trained 200 Inland Water Safety Guards, with a substantial number deployed to the Upper West Region to monitor riverbanks and ensure passenger compliance.

To address the leading causes of drowning, identified as non-compliance and lack of standard life jackets, the GMA is offering subsidized life jackets at GH₵250 each, or GH₵230 for bulk community purchases, down from over GH₵400. They also plan to expand the safety guard force from 200 to over 350 personnel and have removed 26,448 submerged tree stumps nationwide since 2019 to clear navigational paths.

Why it mattersRiverine commuters and boat operators in the Upper West Region are directly affected by new safety regulations, subsidized life jackets, and increased enforcement on the Black Volta.

World Bank confirms 56.4% of Ghanaians remain in poverty despite growth

New findings from the World Bank indicate that over half of Ghana's population lives in poverty, highlighting a disconnect between economic growth and household living conditions.

The World Bank's 10th Ghana Economic Update, released in August 2026, reports that 56.4% of Ghanaians are still in poverty. This figure comes despite strong economic growth in 2025, significant disinflation, and improved macroeconomic indicators. The Bank's Division Director for Ghana, Liberia, and Sierra Leone noted that growth is concentrated in sectors with limited job creation capacity, and spatial disparities are widening.

The Africa Policy Lens (APL) stated that these World Bank findings validate its April 2026 Ghana Wellbeing Tracker report. That report concluded Ghana was “stabilising but not yet thriving,” with its Ghana Wellbeing Index (GWI) at 58.5 out of 100, placing the country in the fragile-to-neutral range.

APL's Policy Brief No. 1 for August 2026 emphasized that both assessments, using different methodologies, agree that macroeconomic recovery has outpaced household economic recovery. This convergence reinforces the policy challenge of achieving “recovery without broad-based welfare gains,” according to APL, and highlights the need for stabilization to translate into jobs and improved living standards.

Why it mattersThis report directly affects Ghanaians by showing that over half of the population remains in poverty, indicating that economic improvements are not broadly reaching households.

GRA Commissioner-General funds mothers' hostel and police vehicle in Upper Denkyira East

Anthony Kwasi Sarpong, Commissioner-General of the Ghana Revenue Authority, supported two development initiatives in his home community, including a mothers' hostel and a new.

The Upper Denkyira East Constituency received a new mothers' hostel for Dunkwa-on-Offin Municipal Hospital and a pickup vehicle for the Upper Denkyira East Police Command. Commissioner-General of the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA), Anthony Kwasi Sarpong, through the Boaduwaa-Sarpong Foundation, partnered with the Emelia Ankomah Education, Health and Welfare Fund for these interventions.

The mothers' hostel will provide accommodation for caregivers of hospital patients, addressing challenges faced by those staying with relatives during admissions. The new pickup vehicle is intended to enhance the operational capacity of the Upper Denkyira East Police Command for emergency response, patrols, and crime prevention.

Mr. Sarpong, who is from the area, reaffirmed his commitment to initiatives that directly improve the lives of people in his home community. The projects were welcomed by some residents as tangible investments in healthcare, social welfare, and community security, aiming to improve quality of life and strengthen public services.

Why it mattersResidents of Upper Denkyira East will benefit from improved healthcare support for patient caregivers and enhanced police response capabilities.

IEA clarifies US$1.7bn gold program loss as accounting issue

The Institute of Economic Affairs (IEA) states that a significant portion of the reported US$1.7 billion loss in Ghana's gold purchase program is due to accounting and valuation.

The Institute of Economic Affairs (IEA) announced that the reported US$1.7 billion loss from the Bank of Ghana’s Domestic Gold Purchase Programme is largely an accounting issue, not an actual loss to the economy. Professor Alexander Bilson-Darku, Director of Research at the IEA, speaking at their assessment of the 2026 mid-year budget review in Accra on Wednesday, urged for proper disaggregation of the figure before drawing conclusions.

Professor Bilson-Darku explained that the first two components of the reported loss represent payments from the central bank to the Ghana Gold Board (GoldBod) for gold purchased and exported. He noted these payments constitute revenue for GoldBod, a state institution, making it a cost for the central bank but not a loss to the state as a whole.

The most significant part, accounting for almost 90 per cent of the US$1.7 billion, stems from the Bank of Ghana's valuation method. This involves converting dollar proceeds into cedis on the central bank's books using reference rates that can differ from prevailing foreign exchange rates, creating a book issue rather than a cash loss for Ghana.

Why it mattersThe clarification on the US$1.7 billion loss impacts the public's understanding of Ghana's financial health and the performance of state-owned enterprises.
