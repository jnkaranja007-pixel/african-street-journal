Ghana's urban population reaches 19.8 million

Nearly two million young people aged 15 to 35 are neither employed nor in education or training.

Nearly 19.8 million people, or 58.7 per cent of Ghana’s population, lived in urban areas in 2025, according to the Ghana Statistical Service (GSS). This growth highlights increasing demand for housing and essential services. The GSS data, from the Ghana in Numbers, 2025 publication, also shows persistent inequalities in living conditions.

Government Statistician Dr Alhassan Iddrisu stated that local figures, not national averages, should guide development decisions. The report estimates Ghana’s housing deficit at about 1.8 million units in 2021, a decrease from 2.8 million in 2010. The average household size has fallen to 3.6 people.

Despite improvements in access to electricity, drinking water, and sanitation, 30.8 per cent of the urban population lives in slums. Additionally, nearly two million young Ghanaians between 15 and 35 years old were neither employed nor in education or training as of the third quarter of 2025, with women making up over 60 per cent of this group.

Why it mattersNearly 19.8 million people in Ghana live in urban areas, facing persistent housing and sanitation gaps, while almost two million young people are not in employment, education, or training.

Ghana envoy expects US police to identify teen killer

Ambassador Victor Emmanuel Smith seeks motive for the fatal shooting of 15-year-old Javits Badu Nkansah Frimpong in New York.

Ghana’s Ambassador to the United States, Victor Emmanuel Smith, expects American investigators to identify those responsible for shooting 15-year-old Ghanaian teenager Javits Badu Nkansah Frimpong in New York. He called for a comprehensive investigation into the motive for the killing on Monday, November 2, and whether it was linked to school bullying allegations.

Ambassador Smith believes surveillance recordings could provide leads, despite attackers wearing masks. He expressed confidence that police would use evidence to track down suspects and determine if they were connected to a student accused of bullying Javits. He cautioned against assuming the alleged bully’s involvement without proof.

The investigation must establish if the killing was retaliation for bullying and punishment, Smith stated on Channel One TV. Residents of the Bronx neighbourhood where Javits was killed plan a vigil on Thursday, November 5, to remember the teenager and protest youth violence.

Why it mattersGhanaian teenager Javits Badu Nkansah Frimpong was shot and killed in New York, prompting the Ghanaian ambassador to seek justice and answers.

JUSAG strike grounds Cape Coast courts

Judicial Service Staff Association of Ghana members began an indefinite strike on Monday, November 2, over unapproved salary reviews.

The Cape Coast Court Complex was locked and silent on Monday, November 2, as the Judicial Service Staff Association of Ghana (JUSAG) began a nationwide strike. Red bands blocked the entrances, preventing access for staff, lawyers, and the public, halting all judicial activities in the Central Regional capital.

JUSAG declared the indefinite strike after the government failed to approve the Judicial Council’s recommendations for the 2026 salary review. The association demanded approval by the end of October and payment of revised salaries with arrears from January to October 2026.

The strike has affected court operations nationwide, leaving litigants like a 75-year-old man from Dunkwa-on-Offin stranded. Traders near the court complex also reported a loss of customers due to the closure.

Why it mattersCourt activities nationwide have halted, stranding litigants and affecting traders, due to a strike by judicial staff over salary reviews.

Illegal mining threatens Ghana's regional diplomacy

Minister Zanetor Agyeman-Rawlings warns shared rivers polluted by mining could spill toxic waters into Côte d'Ivoire.

Illegal mining in Ghana is straining diplomatic ties with neighbouring West African nations due to the pollution of shared river systems. Environment Minister Dr. Zanetor Agyeman-Rawlings stated that toxic waters from rivers like the Tano and Bia could flow into Côte d'Ivoire, escalating the issue beyond an environmental crisis to a threat to regional diplomacy and national security. She called for immediate, coordinated action from government, traditional leaders, security agencies, and communities to tackle the menace.

Dr. Agyeman-Rawlings warned that illicit proceeds from illegal mining were allegedly being used to finance violent extremism in the Sahel region, making Ghana complicit in activities that jeopardise sub-regional security. The destruction of forests and pollution of water bodies are also severely undermining Ghana's food security and exposing future generations to health hazards. She stressed that gold production should not come at the expense of agriculture, water security, and other vital economic sectors.

Scientific evidence, including research on the Odaw River, shows severe pollution from illicit mining, with the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) identifying hazardous levels of heavy metals like mercury and lead. As a medical doctor, Dr. Agyeman-Rawlings expressed concern over public health impacts, especially for children and pregnant women, noting that contaminated boreholes can serve as drinking water sources for schoolchildren miles away.

Why it mattersGhana's illegal mining activities are polluting shared rivers, directly impacting neighbouring countries and potentially funding extremism, threatening regional stability.

Ghana urged not to revoke mining lease over debt

Dr Steve Manteaw advises negotiation for US$8.38 million owed by Northern Ashanti Mines.

Dr Steve Manteaw, co-chair of the Ghana Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative (GHEITI), has advised the government against revoking Northern Ashanti Mines’ lease due to outstanding statutory payments.

He suggested negotiating a payment plan instead, arguing that keeping the mine operational allows it to generate revenue to settle its debts. Dr Manteaw stated that indebtedness alone is not sufficient grounds for license revocation, unlike serious technical breaches.

The Minerals Commission claims Northern Ashanti Mines owes over US$8.38 million. Dr Manteaw expressed concern that the handling of this dispute might unfairly target Ghanaian-owned companies and undermine the government's policy of increasing local participation in the mining sector.

Why it mattersRevoking mining leases and shutting down local mining businesses over financial difficulties could undermine the government’s policy of placing Ghanaians at the centre of the country’s mineral industry.
