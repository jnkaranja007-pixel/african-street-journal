Manso Keniago chief demands NAIMOS evidence after six die

The community claims the anti-illegal mining task force shot residents unprovoked, with some killed in their homes.

The Chief of Manso Keniago, Nana Obeng Mensah Ababio, has challenged the National Anti-Illegal Mining Operations Secretariat (NAIMOS) to provide proof of an alleged attack that led to a reprisal shooting, killing six people and injuring others, including a toddler. The community claims the NAIMOS operatives fired sporadically without provocation, a stark contrast to the task force's assertions. Residents, many dressed in black and red, expressed grief and anger following the increased death toll.

According to the community, the shooting began after traditional authorities tried to stop NAIMOS operatives from tracking a confiscated excavator on a new road. Chief Mensah Ababio stated that some victims were shot inside their homes or while attempting to flee, presenting rifle pellets as evidence to the police. He also challenged NAIMOS to release drone footage, asserting that the community did not mobilize to attack the task force, and that the engagement was solely between chiefs.

The community disputes claims that operatives came under attack, calling untrue the photographs of a bullet-riddled vehicle. Angry youth, who reportedly prevented the District Chief Executive, Benjamin Marfo, from reaching the scene, are demanding an independent investigation. The Manso Adubia NDC has called for NAIMOS to be withdrawn pending the investigation's outcome.

Why it mattersSix people died and several others were injured in a clash between residents and an anti-illegal mining task force, prompting calls for an independent investigation.

Dangote eyes local processing of over half of Ghana's cocoa by 2028

Finance Minister Cassiel Ato Forson says the Nigerian billionaire has approached him and President John Dramani Mahama about the plan, which would expand Ghana's cocoa value.

Finance Minister Dr Cassiel Ato Forson has said Nigerian billionaire Aliko Dangote wants Ghana to process more than 50 per cent of its cocoa locally by 2028. Forson said Dangote personally contacted him and President John Dramani Mahama about investing in local cocoa processing.

Speaking to TV3 after the presentation of the 2027 Budget Statement, Forson also said a major sub-regional businessperson wants to sign a contract to process 500,000 metric tonnes of cocoa in Ghana. He said talks on the terms of that agreement are ongoing and that the investor is ready to begin operations.

Forson said Dangote believes Ghana can process more than half of its cocoa locally by 2028, which could expand the country's cocoa value addition industry.

Why it mattersCocoa that is processed locally rather than exported raw would add value within Ghana, and the government is already negotiating terms with the investor.

Ghana opens US$137M floating dock at Takoradi Port

The shipyard will offer ship repairs to operators who now sail up to 5,500 kilometres for such work.

At the Port of Takoradi, Ghana has launched the Prime Meridian Floating Dock project, a US$137 million shipyard. Prime Meridian Docks Ghana Limited developed it under a 25-year concession from the Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority, according to the project's developers. Some maritime operators in the region currently travel up to 5,500 kilometres to get vessels repaired.

The project has reached financial close, and the African Development Bank is contributing to its funding. The facility will have a 13,500-tonne lifting capacity, a 200-metre jetty, a modern workshop, offices and heavy marine equipment. Once operational, it is expected to create up to 430 direct jobs.

The yard will serve most offshore supply vessels, tugs, barges and mid-sized cargo ships in the Gulf of Guinea, but not very large crude carriers or large container vessels. Winning a steady flow of contracts will depend on equipment availability, turnaround times, operating costs and attracting international customers. Nigeria and Côte d'Ivoire offer regional alternatives, so pricing will decide whether vessels divert to Takoradi.

Why it mattersShipping operators in the Gulf of Guinea who now travel up to 5,500 kilometres for vessel repairs will gain a closer option once the 430 jobs and 13,500-tonne dock are in service.

Bank of Ghana clarifies GH¢50 note portrait

The central bank stated the image of Theodosia Okoh was artistically adjusted for security, not her sister's photo.

The Bank of Ghana (BoG) has clarified that the portrait on the new GH¢50 Heritage Series banknote is of Theodosia Okoh, the designer of Ghana’s national flag, and not her sister, Dr Letitia Obeng. The central bank issued a statement on Friday, November 13, 2026, explaining that the image was developed from different photographs of Mrs Okoh but underwent artistic adjustments for currency production.

According to the BoG statement, changes made during the engraving process, such as altering Mrs Okoh’s head tie to a hairstyle common for the period, led to differences in her appearance that caused public concern. The bank acknowledged the close resemblance to Dr Obeng but confirmed the portrait was solely based on Mrs Okoh's images. The clarification aims to recognise Mrs Okoh’s contribution to Ghana’s national identity.

The Heritage Series banknotes are scheduled to enter circulation on December 1, 2026. The Bank of Ghana stated it appreciated the constructive engagement from the Okoh family and the public's interest in preserving national heritage. It pledged to ensure distinguished personalities on currency are represented with dignity and respect.

Why it mattersThe Bank of Ghana confirmed the portrait on the new GH¢50 note is Theodosia Okoh, which will enter circulation on December 1, 2026.

Politics · 2026-11-14

Sam Jonah urges Mahama to restore executed General Acheampong's rank

Business magnate Sir Sam Jonah says the rank was removed by decree before the former Head of State was shot in 1979 without a trial.

Ghanaian businessman Sir Sam Jonah has asked President John Mahama and the Ghana Armed Forces to give back the military rank of General Ignatius Kutu Acheampong, the former Head of State. Acheampong was stripped of his rank by decree and then shot by firing squad on June 16, 1979, without being convicted by any court.

Speaking at the second General Kutu Acheampong Public Lecture Series, Jonah said that restoring the rank would not endorse Acheampong's rule or erase the suffering of those who lived under it. He pointed out that other former military leaders executed in the same period still keep their titles in historical records, while Acheampong is called Mr Acheampong. He asked the President, the Armed Forces Council and Parliament to restore the rank of General, reinstate his name on the rolls of the Ghana Military Academy and return the honours tied to that rank to his family.

Jonah cited a 2019 acknowledgement by the Ghana Armed Forces that the 1979 executions were an aberration and said the decision to strip Acheampong of his rank should be looked at again. He also named Operation Feed Yourself, which promoted domestic food production, and policies on Ghanaian participation in mining as initiatives Ghanaians should recognise.

Why it mattersIf the rank is restored, the family would receive the honours that go with it and Acheampong's name would return to the Ghana Military Academy rolls.
