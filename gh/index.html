Minority demands answers over Ghana health data shutdown

Parliamentarians want the Health Minister to explain why digital health platforms were suspended over a $64,510.95 hosting bill.

Ghana's Minority Caucus in Parliament is demanding an explanation from the government and the Minister of Health, Kwabena Mintah Akandoh, regarding the shutdown of critical Ghana Health Service digital platforms. Technology company Akamai suspended the platforms on October 29, 2026, due to an unpaid cloud hosting bill of US$64,510.95, forcing health facilities to revert to paper-based reporting.

The affected systems include the District Health Information Management System (DHIMS2), HIV and Tuberculosis trackers, and the National Malaria Data Repository. Dr Nana Ayew Afriyie, Ranking Member of Parliament’s Health Committee, stated that the disruption has impacted digital health reporting nationwide. The Minority Caucus has called for the immediate restoration of services, full disclosure of expenditure on the Ghana Health Information Management System (GHIMS), and an investigation into the suspension.

Minority Leader Alexander Afenyo-Markin is pushing for the Minister of Health to appear before Parliament to brief the House on the shutdown. Majority Leader James Agalga assured that steps would be taken to invite the minister to discuss the challenges and restoration measures. The Ghana Health Service has assured that no data has been lost, but the Minority insists that data preservation does not ensure uninterrupted access to essential health information.

Why it mattersThe shutdown of digital health platforms affects all health facilities in Ghana, forcing a return to paper records and disrupting health reporting.

US embassy offers expedited visa interviews for Ghanaians

Ghanaians with urgent travel needs can now apply for earlier visa appointments at the US Embassy in Accra.

The United States Embassy in Accra has announced a process for Ghanaians to request earlier appointments for nonimmigrant visa interviews in cases of unexpected emergencies. This provision is for individuals needing to travel urgently to the United States for reasons such as emergency medical care, a family member's funeral, or unforeseen business needs. The embassy cautioned that expedited slots are limited and not guaranteed.

To apply, Ghanaians must first complete the DS-160 form, pay the visa fee, and book the earliest available regular interview. After these steps, they can submit an emergency request through the appointment portal, detailing their circumstances and providing supporting documents like medical records. Approved requests will lead to an email notification with instructions to select a new interview date.

Applicants whose emergency requests are declined will also be notified by email but will keep their original interview appointments. The U.S. Department of State's general guidance confirms that expedited appointments are considered for urgent and unforeseen circumstances, requiring applicants to provide evidence of their need.

Why it mattersGhanaians needing to travel to the U.S. for urgent medical care, funerals, or business may be able to secure an earlier visa interview.

Ghana targets 50% domestic cocoa processing

Government aims to retain more value from cocoa exports after global prices fell from US$10,000 to US$4,000 per tonne.

Ghana aims to process at least 50% of its annual cocoa production domestically, according to Trade, Agribusiness and Industry Minister Elizabeth Ofosu-Adjare. This strategy seeks to increase agricultural value addition and reduce dependence on raw commodity exports. The move follows a sharp decline in global cocoa prices from approximately US$10,000 per tonne in early 2025 to about US$4,000 by early 2026.

Ofosu-Adjare stated that the price collapse exposed the vulnerability of economies exporting raw commodities. Ghana will utilize its installed cocoa grinding capacity, which exceeds 500,000 metric tonnes, to achieve its processing goals. Non-traditional exports, driven by processed cocoa products, reached a record US$5 billion in 2025, a 30.7% increase from the previous year.

The government plans to extend local processing to other commodities like cashew, fruits, and vegetables. European investors are encouraged to form joint ventures and establish processing facilities in Ghana. This initiative comes as Ghana's cocoa sector faces production challenges due to unfavourable weather, with COCOBOD expecting a 16% decline in the 2025/26 season.

Why it mattersGhanaian farmers and the wider agricultural sector will be affected by the government's push for increased domestic processing of cocoa and other commodities.

Culture · 2026-11-07

Mfantsipim students start gospel music movement

Noise of Joy formed in July 1970, blending popular music styles with Christian messages.

Contemporary gospel music began in Ghana in the early 1970s, with Mfantsipim School students forming groups like Noise of Joy. Guitarist Emmanuel Lartey formed Noise of Joy in July 1970 with fellow students and girls from Wesley Girls’ High School. A visit by Youth For Christ’s Teen Team in September 1970 affirmed the students' musical direction.

School tutors and staff encouraged the students to perform, embracing the new sound as Methodism was born in song. On January 15, 1972, Noise of Joy merged with the Evangels to form Joyful Way Singers, combining guitar music with piano styles. Professor Joe-Nat Clegg-Lamptey described this fusion as ‘like dynamite!’

Joyful Way toured nationally in 1972 and appeared on GBC radio, later launching the Explosion of Joy concert on Boxing Day 1974. This concert, which still draws about 4,000 patrons annually, may be Africa’s longest-running contemporary gospel concert. Joyful Way inspired later groups like Calvary Road Singers, founded around December 1976.

Why it mattersYoung Ghanaians in the 1970s, particularly students at Mfantsipim School, developed and popularized a new style of contemporary gospel music.

Business · 2026-11-07

UN advocate calls for financial health council in Ghana

Queen Máxima urged a coordinated approach to boost financial well-being and socio-economic development for Ghanaians.

Queen Máxima of the Netherlands, the UN Secretary-General’s Special Advocate for Financial Health, has called for Ghana to establish a Financial Health Council. This council would coordinate government and private sector actions to improve the financial well-being of Ghanaians. She spoke in Accra at the joint inauguration of the Working Committee on the National Financial Inclusion and Development Strategy.

The committee will develop a strategy for implementation between 2027 and 2031. Queen Máxima stated that access to financial services like bank accounts, insurance, and pensions does not automatically ensure financial security. She outlined four components of financial health: managing daily finances, withstanding shocks, achieving long-term goals, and maintaining financial confidence.

Queen Máxima emphasized that financial inclusion is a means of achieving financial health and broader socio-economic development. The strategy document is intended for implementation over the next five years, from 2027 to 2031.

Why it mattersGhanaians will be directly affected by a new council and strategy aimed at improving their financial well-being.
