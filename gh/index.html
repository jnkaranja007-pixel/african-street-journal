Ghanaian Hospital Fails Lead to Maternal Deaths

A grieving husband points to systemic failures and negligence after his wife died during a scheduled C-section.

Theresa Boakye Dua, 31, died at Ridge Hospital in Accra during a scheduled Caesarean section, leaving her husband devastated and angry. He believes negligence and unprofessional conduct within the healthcare system contributed to her death. Her husband has vowed to reveal the full truth, stating this tragedy is about preventing similar losses for other families and questioning why preventable maternal deaths persist in Ghana.

The Deputy Minister of Health, Dr. Grace Ayensu-Danquah, recently highlighted maternal mortality as Ghana's worst-performing health indicator. In 2025, the country recorded approximately 945 institutional maternal deaths. She emphasized that the Ministry of Health cannot address this crisis alone and called for a new approach to tackle the problem effectively.

Theresa's story has resonated on social media, prompting others to share their own difficult childbirth experiences, including delays in care and the loss of loved ones. These shared narratives collectively illustrate a maternal health crisis that extends beyond statistics, representing individual lives, families, and profound grief.

Why it mattersWomen in Ghana are dying during childbirth due to perceived failures and negligence in the healthcare system, even in cases of scheduled procedures.

Landlady Recalls Hijacker's Friendly Demeanor

A former property manager recounts her interactions with a man now accused of facilitating the 9/11 attacks.

Holly Ratchford, who managed the Parkwood Apartments in San Diego, California, in the late 1990s, has spoken about her past interactions with Omar al-Bayoumi. Al-Bayoumi, a Saudi national, is alleged to have provided support to Nawaf al-Hazmi and Khalid al-Mihdhar, two of the hijackers involved in the September 11, 2001 attacks. Ratchford described Bayoumi as friendly and sociable, recalling his participation in pool parties and his family's invitations for meals.

Ratchford stated that Bayoumi, who lived at the complex with his family from September 1999, introduced her to the two men who would later become hijackers. She expressed disbelief and ongoing struggle to reconcile the seemingly ordinary neighbor with his alleged role in the attacks. Her testimony is part of a $1 trillion lawsuit filed by victims' families against the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, though the kingdom and Bayoumi deny the allegations.

The federal judge in the 9/11 families' lawsuit found a year ago that evidence supported a reasonable inference of coordination between Bayoumi, a Saudi diplomat, and the Saudi government in assisting the hijackers. This ruling allowed the case to proceed, despite Saudi Arabia's appeal. Ratchford has provided a sealed declaration to the court but had not previously spoken publicly about her experiences.

Why it mattersFamilies of victims and survivors of the 9/11 attacks are affected by allegations that a former resident of a property managed by Holly Ratchford facilitated the hijackers' arrival in the US.

Ghana Tourism Creates Over 24,000 New Jobs in One Year

The tourism sector's expansion has led to significant job growth, with new programs aiming to train young professionals.

Ghana's tourism industry is increasingly becoming a key source of employment and skills development for young people. Between 2021 and 2022, tourism-related jobs grew by more than 24,000, reaching 276,985 positions. By 2023, direct employment in hotels, restaurants, travel agencies, and other tourism enterprises stood at approximately 204,591. When including indirect jobs in supporting industries like handicrafts, the total employment figure for 2023 was estimated at 429,641.

The sector's growth continued into 2024, with Ghana recording 1,288,804 international tourist arrivals and a rise in licensed tourism enterprises from 5,786 to 6,702. This expansion generated about US$4.8 billion in revenue, increasing demand for workers across various services. In 2025, international arrivals saw a further increase to 1,306,962, reinforcing the market for tourism businesses and creating more employment and entrepreneurial opportunities.

To further support youth employment, the Ghana Tourism Authority and Ghana Tourism Federation are launching initiatives like the Graduate Tourism Employment and Mobility (GTEM) Programme. This program will train and certify 200 graduates as professional tour guides, deploying them to various tourism establishments and providing mobility support to help them start their own businesses.

Why it mattersYoung people are directly affected through increased job opportunities and skills training within the growing tourism sector.

MP John Dumelo Joins Corporate Ghana at JoySports Tournament

The Member of Parliament for Ayawaso West Wuogon will participate in the annual event focused on sport, fitness, and networking.

Member of Parliament for Ayawaso West Wuogon, John Dumelo, will attend the JoySports Invitational Tournament 2026 on Saturday, August 29. The event brings together companies and institutions for a day dedicated to sports, health, and business connections. Dumelo is scheduled to join the early morning health walk, which commences at 6:00 am from the University of Ghana Stadium.

Following the health walk, participants will engage in an aerobic session before the official opening ceremony at 7:30 am. Sporting competitions will then begin at 8:00 am, featuring disciplines such as football, chess, table tennis, scrabble, and draughts. The tournament includes swimming, penalty shootouts for CEOs and ladies, and novelty events, concluding with the football final between 5:00 pm and 6:00 pm.

The JoySports Invitational Tournament is a significant corporate sporting event in Ghana, offering a platform for companies to foster fitness, teamwork, and networking. Winners and outstanding performers across various disciplines will receive prizes. This year's edition anticipates a day of competition and entertainment, with Dumelo joining corporate leaders and employees at the University of Ghana.

Why it mattersCorporate leaders and employees are affected by the event's focus on networking and fitness, with the participation of a Member of Parliament potentially highlighting the importance of these activities.

SankofaFresh Cuts Food Waste, Connects Farmers to Markets

Ghanaian technology platform SankofaFresh uses data and cold-chain monitoring to reduce post-harvest losses and improve market access for smallholder farmers.

Ghanaian food-systems technology platform SankofaFresh is working to reduce post-harvest losses and connect smallholder farmers to more reliable markets. The platform uses technology to improve how fresh produce is monitored, stored, and connected to buyers. Leehouse and Chemical Ventures, a food manufacturing company in Ghana, has adopted SankofaFresh, which has helped reduce losses and create more dependable market opportunities for farmers.

SankofaFresh was co-founded by Rhoda Osei-Nkwantabisa, Joshua Kojo Aduampong Mantey, George Arthur, and Rosemary Agbzo. Joshua Mantey leads the platform’s data and artificial intelligence systems for cold-chain monitoring and farmer-to-market matching. His PhD research at Northeastern University involves large-scale computational modeling and machine learning, an approach applied to SankofaFresh's technology to boost efficiency and cut waste across the agricultural value chain.

The platform's efforts to reduce post-harvest losses and strengthen market access for smallholder farmers earned it the Audience Choice Award at the World Bank Group Youth Summit Pitch Competition in 2026. SankofaFresh positions technology, data, and market connections as vital tools for ensuring that more food grown by farmers reaches consumers and businesses, contributing to food security and nutrition in Ghana and beyond.

Why it mattersSmallholder farmers are affected by reduced incomes due to post-harvest losses, and SankofaFresh aims to mitigate this by connecting them to better markets.
