Mahama unveils $10bn 'New Economy' plan for Ghana

The four-year programme from 2027 to 2030 aims to grow what Ghana produces and processes at home, and will feature in the 2027 Budget.

President John Dramani Mahama has announced a New Economy programme, a US$10 billion investment plan covering 2027 to 2030. It is meant to shift Ghana from restoring economic stability to expanding how much it produces, how much it processes locally and how many jobs its industries can create.

The programme is built around seven priority areas: macroeconomic stability, commercial agriculture and agribusiness, energy, critical minerals, textiles and garments, tourism and the creative economy, and pharmaceutical manufacturing. It is expected to feature prominently in the 2027 Budget, which Finance Minister Dr Cassiel Ato Forson is scheduled to present to Parliament on Thursday, November 12.

The government says the plan targets constraints such as expensive credit, unreliable or costly energy, inadequate processing facilities and heavy dependence on imported goods. Ghana completed the final review of its International Monetary Fund programme in July 2026, recorded six per cent growth in the second quarter and saw inflation fall to 5.2 per cent in September.

Why it mattersBusinesses, farmers and workers are the groups the government says the programme must reach, with agriculture, energy and textiles among the sectors targeted for investment.

Parliament restores Afenyo-Markin to ECOWAS delegation

A dispute over Ghana's representation to the regional parliament ends with the approval of a revised list of nominees.

Ghana's Parliament has approved a motion to reconstitute its delegation to the ECOWAS Parliament, returning Minority Leader Alexander Afenyo-Markin to the body. The decision came after Deputy Minority Leader Patricia Appiagyei asked to be taken off the proposed list of replacements, citing a lack of consultation and alleged irregularities in the selection process.

Parliament had first removed Mr Afenyo-Markin from the delegation, but he kept asserting his place in the regional legislature, where he serves as Third Deputy Speaker. His appearance at proceedings in Port Harcourt, Nigeria, reportedly led the ECOWAS Parliament to refuse to swear in the revised delegation, and Ghana threatened to withdraw from the body before the matter was settled. Speaker Alban Bagbin had referred him to the Privileges Committee after a petition by former Majority Leader Mahama Ayariga.

Under the new arrangement, George Kweku Ricketts-Hagan will lead the delegation, with Mr Afenyo-Markin as a member. The other members are Emmanuel Kwasi Bedzrah, Laadi Ayii Ayamba, Eric Afful, Dominic Napare, Kwame Anyimadu-Antwi and Bryan Acheampong, while Millicent Yeboah Amankwah will serve as observer. The reconstitution is expected to let the delegation resume its work in the ECOWAS Parliament.

Why it mattersGhana's seven delegation members and one observer are named in the reconstituted team, and the country's work in the ECOWAS Parliament depends on the settlement.

Ghana launches $18.8m rice project to aid 20,000 northern farmers

The African Development Bank grant funds seven districts across four northern regions, targeting more than 20,000 smallholder rice farmers.

Ghana's Minister of Food and Agriculture, Dr Eric Opoku, launched a $18.8 million rice programme on Monday. The Regional West African Resilient Rice Value Chain Project, known as REWARD, aims to raise rice output and reduce the country's dependence on imported rice.

The project is supported by a grant from the African Development Bank. It is expected to benefit more than 20,000 smallholder farmers across seven districts in four northern regions, including Mamprugu-Moagduri in the North East Region and Tamale Metropolitan, Mion and Savelugu in the Northern Region.

The programme also covers Nandom and Wa Municipalities in the Upper West Region and West Gonja Municipal in the Savannah Region. It targets weak domestic supply, low uptake of improved technologies, weak production systems and fragmented farming activities.

Why it mattersMore than 20,000 smallholder rice farmers in seven northern Ghana districts are expected to receive support aimed at lifting their production.

Ghana cannot celebrate austerity amid infrastructure deficit, says Bokpin

Economics Professor Godfred Alufar Bokpin says spending cuts will take a while to reach ordinary Ghanaians, ahead of the 2027 Budget presentation.

Economics Professor Godfred Alufar Bokpin said on Tuesday that Ghana cannot celebrate extensive austerity expenditure cuts while a huge infrastructure deficit holds back growth and burdens citizens. Speaking on Joy News' PM Express, he acknowledged the government's progress in stabilising the economy but warned that its heavy reliance on spending cuts would delay benefits for ordinary Ghanaians.

Bokpin argued that the 2026 Budget, like its predecessor, relied on spending restraint that reduced demand without addressing the economy's limited production capacity. He cited a commuter spending nine to 12 hours travelling to Kumasi, saying an inflation rate of 5.2% means nothing to that person. He credited the government with falling inflation, lower interest rates and a relatively resilient cedi.

Finance Minister Dr Cassiel Ato Forson presents the 2027 Budget on Thursday, November 12. The government plans to unveil a four-year New Economy programme, and the Minority in Parliament has questioned how it will be financed and asked the government to account for existing flagship initiatives, including the 24-Hour Economy.

Why it mattersGhanaians, including commuters facing long journeys, will wait for stabilisation gains to reach their daily lives, while the Minority says the new New Economy programme's funding is unclear.

600 pupils in Ashaiman taught about drugs and cybercrime

Officials from three agencies gave basic school pupils in Ashaiman, Greater Accra, warnings on substance abuse and online harm.

600 pupils from three basic schools in Ashaiman Municipality, Greater Accra Region, were sensitised on Saturday to drug abuse and cybercrime. The session, themed “Securing Our Future: Say No to Drug Abuse and Cyber-crimes,” was run by the Ashaiman Municipal office of the National Commission for Civic Education, working with the Narcotics Control Commission and the Cyber Security Authority.

The municipal director of the commission, Ms Salvata Mawulom Koku, said children are the future and need to hear about these dangers early. Ms Ayisha Ibrahim Tarl, deputy narcotics control officer, told pupils not to use stimulants, depressants or hallucinogens to feel better or escape problems, and set out the offences and penalties for drug crimes. Mr Martin Aboagye of the Cyber Security Authority listed risks such as cyberbullying, sexual extortion and cyber grooming, and warned that online footprints can resurface years later.

The municipal director of education, Ms Gloria Kwakuyi, urged the pupils to behave well and use the internet for its benefits.

Why it mattersPupils at three Ashaiman basic schools received the warnings directly, and the sessions give them guidance on drug trafficking and online risks.
