Ghana targets universal electricity access by 2030, connects 200 communities

Ghana aims to achieve universal electricity access by 2030, with its current access rate approaching 90 percent, according to Energy and Green Transition Minister John Abdulai.

Ghana has connected an additional 200 communities to the national grid, bringing the country's electricity access rate close to 90 percent, Energy and Green Transition Minister John Abdulai Jinapor announced. This expansion is part of a broader government effort to achieve universal electricity access by 2030, addressing the remaining gap in coverage.

Minister Jinapor, speaking at the Future of Energy Conference, emphasized that the target extends beyond mere connections to include the quality, reliability, and cost of electricity. He stated that a reliable and affordable power supply is crucial for supporting competitive industries and Ghana's industrialization agenda, which requires consistent power for businesses.

The government also plans to strengthen its electricity infrastructure to accommodate rising demand from new sources like electric vehicles. This will necessitate investments in generation, transmission, distribution, renewable energy, and storage systems to ensure a stable power supply for both households and businesses as the country approaches its 2030 goal.

Why it mattersGhanaian citizens and businesses are affected by the government's plan to expand electricity access and improve its reliability and affordability, which directly impacts their daily lives and economic activities.

Bank of Ghana Governor Calls for Stronger Virtual Asset Oversight

Dr. Johnson Asiama emphasized the need for coordinated regulatory action to safeguard financial stability and protect consumers as Ghana launches its Virtual Assets Coordinating.

Ghana has established a Virtual Assets Coordinating Committee to enhance oversight of the nation's virtual asset sector. The Bank of Ghana Governor, Dr. Johnson Asiama, stated that the rapid evolution of virtual assets and digital finance necessitates a proactive approach to regulation and supervision.

The committee will provide a statutory platform for coordinated regulatory and supervisory actions among relevant institutions, operating under the Virtual Assets Service Providers Act, 2025 (Act 1154). Dr. Asiama highlighted the importance of effective coordination, timely information sharing, and a shared commitment among regulatory bodies to maintain the integrity of Ghana's financial system.

This new body aims to facilitate the harmonized implementation of the Act and its subsidiary instruments, strengthen information sharing, and improve inter-agency cooperation. It will also support coordinated responses to emerging risks such as money laundering, terrorist financing, cybersecurity, and consumer protection, requiring collaboration with both domestic and foreign counterparts.

Why it mattersThis development affects individuals and businesses involved in virtual assets by introducing new regulatory oversight and measures against financial crimes.

Ghana Maritime Authority, Humanity First install 4 boreholes in Wa

The new solar-powered boreholes in Wa Municipality aim to end water scarcity, improve sanitation, and keep students in class by providing reliable water access to schools and.

The Ghana Maritime Authority (GMA) and Humanity First Ghana have commissioned four new solar-powered mechanized boreholes in Wa Municipality, Ghana. These facilities, located at T.I. Ahmadiyya Senior High School, Chansa, Biihee, and Boli, are part of a seven-borehole Corporate Social Responsibility initiative to address severe water distress and improve public sanitation across the Upper West Region.

Naval Captain (Rtd) Dr. Kamal-Deen Ali, Director General of the Ghana Maritime Authority, stated that the intervention directly supports national development goals by delivering clean, potable water to underserved communities. He emphasized that reliable water access is crucial for education and health, preventing students from leaving school premises to search for water and disrupting their studies.

The new boreholes bring immediate relief to schools that previously faced chronic academic disruptions due to water scarcity. At T.I. Ahmadiyya Senior High School, Headmaster Issahaque Yahaya noted that the lack of water exacerbated sanitation issues for 1,433 students. Student leaders reported that the previous water scarcity led to frequent typhoid cases and forced students to miss morning hygiene routines, causing lateness and punishments.

Why it mattersStudents in Wa Municipality, particularly at T.I. Ahmadiyya Senior High School and Boli M/A Primary B, will no longer miss instructional hours or suffer health issues due to water scarcity, improving their educational outcomes.

DirectAid delivers relief items to Alajo flood victims

Humanitarian organization DirectAid distributed food and household essentials to residents of Alajo affected by the June 2026 floods, with further distributions planned for four.

DirectAid, a humanitarian organization, distributed food and household items to residents of Alajo on Tuesday, August 25, who were affected by the June 2026 floods. This distribution is part of a larger relief program valued at approximately GH¢2 million, aiming to support about 1,500 families across five flood-affected communities.

Beneficiaries in Alajo received packages containing rice, six liters of cooking oil, 10 packs of spaghetti, beans, tomatoes, and other food items. The organization also provided household essentials, including mattresses, bedsheets, blankets, towels, and cleaning products like soap and Parazone. Sami Henedak Ahmed, DirectAid's Country Director, stated the intervention supports families whose livelihoods and homes were impacted by the floods.

The relief items were funded by DirectAid’s donors in Kuwait, with assistance from government officials coordinating the effort. Distributions are scheduled to continue in Alogboshie on Wednesday, Kasoa on Thursday, Nana Krom on Saturday, and conclude next Tuesday in Abeka, with plans to extend support to clinics and hospitals with medical supplies.

Why it mattersResidents of Alajo and four other communities are directly affected by receiving essential food and household items after losing their homes and livelihoods in the June floods.

Oppong Nkrumah accuses GoldBod CEO of distracting from US$1.7 billion losses

Kojo Oppong Nkrumah, MP for Ofoase-Ayirebi, claims Sammy Gyamfi is using extortion allegations to divert attention from significant financial losses at the Bank of Ghana and.

Member of Parliament Kojo Oppong Nkrumah has accused Sammy Gyamfi, CEO of the Ghana Gold Board (GoldBod), of attempting to distract the public from over US$1.7 billion in losses incurred by the Bank of Ghana (BoG). The International Monetary Fund reported that the Domestic Gold Purchase Programme's operations in 2025 led to these staggering losses, equivalent to 1.5% of GDP.

Mr. Nkrumah stated on Nhyria FM’s “Kro Yi Mu Nsem” that Mr. Gyamfi's recent allegations of extortion against Minority Leader Alexander Afenyo-Markin are diversionary tactics. He urged Ghanaians to focus on demanding transparency and accountability regarding the management of the country’s gold resources and central bank finances, rather than being swayed by what he termed “unfounded allegations.”

The Minority Leader, Alexander Afenyo-Markin, has sued Sammy Gyamfi and others over the extortion claims, while also demanding intervention for the Gold Board's alleged failure to account for the US$1.7 billion, which he pegs at around GH¢22 billion. Mr. Nkrumah described Mr. Gyamfi’s actions as a personal attack against those seeking accountability.

Why it mattersThe public is affected by the alleged mismanagement of US$1.7 billion in public funds and the political dispute surrounding accountability for these losses.
