Anti-graft Groups Demand Parliament Disclose GH¢70,000 Payment Details

Transparency International Ghana and the Ghana Anti-Corruption Coalition seek full disclosure on an alleged GH¢70,000 payment linked to parliamentary vetting.

Transparency International Ghana (TI-Ghana) and the Ghana Anti-Corruption Coalition (GACC) are demanding full disclosure regarding an alleged GH¢70,000 payment connected to the vetting of nominees by Parliament’s Appointments Committee. The groups stated that while Deputy Minority Leader Patricia Appiagyei reportedly rejected the funds, the issue requires scrutiny to prevent erosion of public trust. They cited previous allegations of bribery and financial impropriety during nominee vetting between 2020 and 2026, noting that past handling of such claims has not inspired public confidence.

The anti-corruption organizations commended Appiagyei and any other MPs who returned similar funds, but emphasized that individual actions cannot substitute for institutional transparency. The controversy is further complicated by conflicting accounts over a memorandum attributed to Appiagyei, which the Minority Caucus has called fake. The Office of the Special Prosecutor has initiated preliminary investigations into the authenticity of the document, the source of the funds, and their intended purpose.

TI-Ghana and GACC want the Parliamentary Service to publish details of the GH¢70,000 allocation, including its source, purpose, authorizing body, and budgetary basis. They also seek clarification on whether the funds were public, private, caucus-related, or linked to nominees, stressing that accountability is crucial to maintaining public trust in Parliament.

Why it mattersThe public and investors are affected by the alleged GH¢70,000 payment as it raises concerns about transparency and potential corruption within Parliament, impacting public trust and financial oversight.

Train Hits Car, Killing 12-Year-Old Boy

A collision between a train and a car at a level crossing in County Mayo resulted in the death of a young passenger.

A 12-year-old boy died after a train struck a car at a level crossing near Ballyhaunis on Thursday evening. Gardaí and emergency services arrived at the scene in Kiltybo around twenty and forty-five local time following reports of the collision. The boy was pronounced dead at the scene, while the car's driver, a man in his late 40s, was taken to hospital with non-life-threatening injuries.

All passengers aboard the train were able to disembark safely. Forensic Collision Investigators are scheduled to conduct a technical examination of the site, and the road remains closed as a result of the incident. The Coroner has been informed, and the deceased boy has been transported to Mayo University Hospital for a post-mortem examination.

Gardaí are appealing to the public for any witnesses who may have seen the incident to come forward and provide information. The investigation into the circumstances of the collision is ongoing, with authorities seeking to piece together the events that led to the fatal crash.

Why it mattersA 12-year-old boy died and a man was injured when a train collided with a car.

MTN Ghana, University of Ghana Forge Digital Innovation Partnership

The collaboration aims to bridge academia and industry, positioning Ghana as a hub for global digital innovation through initiatives like an AI hackathon.

MTN Ghana and the University of Ghana have signed a Memorandum of Understanding to foster digital innovation, aiming to connect academic research with industry needs. MTN Ghana CEO Stephen Blewitt stated the partnership is a natural alliance designed to drive Ghana's digital growth and position it as an epicenter for global digital innovation. The collaboration will move beyond theoretical ideas to include mentorship, incubation, and real-world deployment of digital solutions.

University of Ghana Vice-Chancellor Prof. Nana Aba Appiah Amfo emphasized the critical role of the partnership in preparing students for a rapidly evolving job market, noting that education must align with technological advancements like AI and data analytics. The university is reviewing its curriculum to include compulsory modules in digital literacy, applied AI, and inclusion to enhance the student experience and readiness for future work.

The initiative includes the (Tɛkyerɛma Pa) AI Hackathon 2026, part of MTN's 30th-anniversary celebrations, which encourages Ghanaian youth to actively participate in building and shaping the AI revolution. MTN Ghana's Chief Digital Officer Ibrahim Misto stressed that the nation's young people should not just be consumers of AI but leaders in its development, aligning with MTN's focus on connectivity, fintech, and digital infrastructure.

Why it mattersThe partnership between MTN Ghana and the University of Ghana aims to drive digital transformation and prepare students for future job markets impacted by AI and digital platforms.

Patchbay Band Questions Live Music Recognition

The veteran Ghanaian live band argues its 15-year presence and contributions are overlooked in industry discussions.

Patchbay Band, active since 2011, is questioning the recognition afforded to live bands within Ghana's music industry. The group, which has performed alongside established artists and worked with figures like Akwaboah Snr, believes its longevity and impact on live music should not be disregarded. "Our brand shouldn’t be chasing for stuff, because we’re currently a long-lasting band," stated guitarist Dominic Quarchie, emphasizing the band's deliberate efforts to build its brand and its position within the industry.

Band manager Philip Acquah further questioned the existence of a structured music industry in Ghana that adequately represents the interests of live bands. He pointed out that organizations like TGMA are privately owned, leading him to question if a true industry framework exists. While Patchbay Band is registered with the Ministry of Tourism and the Musicians Union of Ghana (MUSIGA), Acquah noted that the scope of these institutions does not fully encompass the band's specific work.

Patchbay Band asserts that its extensive experience and sustained presence in the music scene warrant greater consideration in dialogues concerning Ghana's music industry and the live music sector. The band's leadership feels their contributions have not been adequately acknowledged, despite their consistent efforts to establish and maintain their brand over more than a decade.

Why it mattersLive bands like Patchbay are affected by a lack of industry structure and recognition, limiting their visibility and influence.

State Enterprises Profit GH¢19.8 Billion, Nine Boards Dissolved

Ghana's state-owned enterprises reported a significant profit turnaround from a loss of GH¢2.2 billion in 2024 to GH¢19.8 billion in 2025, prompting the dissolution of nine.

Ghana's state-owned enterprises (SOEs) have reported a dramatic financial turnaround, moving from a GH¢2.2 billion loss in 2024 to a GH¢19.8 billion net profit in 2025. Government attributes this shift to increased revenues, reduced finance costs, and improved foreign-exchange performance. However, questions remain about the distribution of these profits, with only a fraction reportedly received as dividends, and whether a few strong performers are masking ongoing losses in other SOEs.

Amidst these financial results, President Mahama has dissolved the governing boards of nine major state institutions, including BOST, VALCO, CBG, GNPC, Ghana Post, TDC, and the National Sports Authority. This action raises questions about whether it represents a governance reset aimed at enhancing performance and accountability or a typical cycle of political appointments and leadership changes. The timing of these board dissolutions, as SOEs appear to be improving, is also under scrutiny.

Separately, Chief Justice Paul Baffoe-Bonnie's comments praising improvements in some state entities, such as MIIF and SSNIT, have drawn criticism from the NPP, who accuse him of entering partisan politics. Meanwhile, the Office of the Special Prosecutor is investigating allegations of a GH¢70,000 payment related to parliamentary vetting, with Asokwa MP Patricia Appiagyei denying the claims. The investigation will examine the memorandum's authenticity, its source, and the circumstances of the alleged payment.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the reported financial performance of state-owned enterprises and the subsequent dissolution of their governing boards, which could signal changes in management and operational direction.
