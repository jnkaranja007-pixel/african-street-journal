Ghana aims for 100% local cocoa processing by 2030

Finance Minister Cassiel Ato Forson urged international buyers to add value in Ghana, as the government plans to process all cocoa domestically.

Ghana plans to process all its cocoa beans domestically by 2030, phasing out raw bean exports, according to Finance Minister Dr Cassiel Ato Forson. He urged international buyers to process beans in Ghana, stating that the country must move beyond exporting raw materials. The government aims to increase local processing to 50% by 2028 and 100% by 2030.

Dr Forson presented the 2027 Budget to Parliament on Thursday, explaining that Ghana's historical financing model encouraged shipping beans abroad, allowing other economies to benefit from processing. He cited a visit to Bangkok for the World Bank–IMF Annual Meetings, where he saw products like chocolate and cocoa cognac made from cocoa. The new Cocoa Board Act, which has gone to Parliament, mandates that the Cocoa Board must process 50% of its cocoa locally.

A Legislative Instrument will detail the timeline for reaching full local processing, with a target of 100% by 2030. Discussions are underway for a proposed arrangement involving 500,000 metric tonnes of cocoa for local value addition, with Dangote reportedly interested in using Ghana as a processing hub. Dr Forson also highlighted that Ghana spends US$3.5 billion annually on food imports, arguing that investing in commercial agriculture and local processing will better utilize the country's resources and retain value within the economy.

Why it mattersGhanaian traders will no longer export raw cocoa beans by 2030, with a target of 50% local processing by 2028.

Ghana army disputes claims it abandoned stampede victim, cites GH¢143,800 support

The military says it has spent about GH¢143,800 on Sandra's care, including GH¢85,000 in KATH bills and GH¢28,800 for her eight-month stay at the 37 Military Hospital in Accra.

The Ghana Armed Forces (GAF) has rejected suggestions that it abandoned Sandra Baafi Boateng, 25, who suffered a severe head injury in the military recruitment stampede at the Baba Yara Sports Stadium in Kumasi on November 12, 2025. The military says it has spent approximately GH¢113,800 on her medical care and given her family GH¢30,000 in cash.

The GAF says Sandra was treated at Komfo Anokye Teaching Hospital (KATH) until February 26, 2026, when a military medical team flew her by air to the 37 Military Hospital in Accra at her family's request. A medical assessment on March 8 found her condition had not changed significantly after the transfer. Her mother was presented with the GH¢30,000 and an air bed at a ceremony on October 28, 2026.

The military estimates KATH's bills at about GH¢85,000 and her eight-month stay at the 37 Military Hospital at GH¢28,800, at GH¢120 a day. After her discharge, her family asked for her to return to Kumasi, and the GAF says treatment will continue at the 4th Military Hospital there until she recovers.

Why it mattersSandra Baafi Boateng's family now depends on the military's promised treatment at the 4th Military Hospital in Kumasi for her continued recovery.

BoG defends new GH¢50 note portrait

Bank of Ghana board member Beatrice Annan says the image of flag designer Theodosia Okoh was adapted from authentic photos for banknote production.

Beatrice Annan, a Board Member of the Bank of Ghana (BoG), defended the portrait of Theodosia Salome Okoh on the new GH¢50 Heritage Series banknote on Thursday, November 12. Mrs Annan stated the image was developed from three authentic photographs of the late artist to meet currency production demands. This clarification follows public discussion, including a claim by Professor Eddie Obeng that the portrait resembled his mother, Dr Letitia Obeng, rather than Mrs Okoh.

Mrs Annan explained on Facebook that the portrait was an engraved artistic representation, not a direct photograph reproduction, designed for strict banknote-printing standards. She said the BoG used two photos of Mrs Okoh in her younger years and one from a later period to create a single image of her as a mature woman. Adjustments were made to hairstyle and outline for clear engraving and security features, while retaining a family resemblance.

The new GH¢50 note, dedicated to Mrs Okoh who designed Ghana’s national flag in 1957, was launched by President John Dramani Mahama on November 12, 2026. The redesigned notes, celebrating prominent Ghanaian figures, are expected to enter circulation on December 1, 2026. The Bank is engaging with the Okoh family to explain the portrait's creation and address concerns before the new series is widely used.

Why it mattersThe Bank of Ghana is engaging with the Okoh family to explain the portrait on the new GH¢50 note before its December 1, 2026 circulation.

Ghana Stock Exchange assesses blockchain to modernise capital market

The exchange is weighing digital assets and distributed ledger systems to improve trading, ownership records and settlement.

The Ghana Stock Exchange (GSE) is assessing blockchain and distributed ledger technologies to improve trading, securities ownership and settlement, its Managing Director, Ms Abena Amoah, said at the exchange's 36th anniversary celebration in Accra. The exchange said it would not adopt new technologies simply because they were fashionable and would ensure each innovation protected investors' assets.

Ms Amoah said the exchange had already embraced technology, pointing to the introduction of electronic trading in 2007, which let stockbrokers trade from different locations, and the digitisation of share ownership, which removed the need for physical share certificates. The exchange is particularly interested in technologies that give investors low-cost access to investment platforms, secure holding of securities and easy verification of ownership, and she stressed that investor confidence must not be compromised.

Frank Sekyere Tawiah, chief executive of Core Afrique Investment Limited, warned in the keynote address that Ghana risked losing control of its capital market to foreign digital investment platforms if it failed to adapt to blockchain and tokenised securities. He urged the GSE and other market institutions to explore a parallel digital securities market while keeping the existing trading system.

Ecobank JoyBusiness SME Fair opens at Accra Mall

The three-day exhibition, running November 13 to 15, lets Ghanaian firms sell directly to shoppers under one roof.

Ghanaian businesses and shoppers are gathering at GHUD Park, Accra Mall, for the Ecobank JoyBusiness SME Fair 2026, which opens today. The exhibition runs from November 13 to 15, 2026, and is powered by JoyPrime, the event's organiser.

Stalls will cover fashion, beauty, food, technology, home essentials and lifestyle, with locally made goods and special deals on offer. Exhibitors say the fair gives them a chance to raise their brand profile, win new customers and build lasting trade relationships. Business owners wanting a stand can call Diana on 055 023 8383 or Desmond on 024 454 6983.

Organisers describe the fair as a marketplace where firms promote their products while buyers compare a range of goods and services in a single location.

Why it mattersSmall firms that secure a stand can reach shoppers in person over the three days and try to convert that contact into sales.
