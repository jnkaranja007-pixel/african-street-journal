BaseNord Opens to Boost Ghana-Sweden Trade

A new facility in Accra aims to connect Ghanaian and Swedish entrepreneurs with investors and partners.

BaseNord, a new facility designed to foster business connections between Ghana and Sweden, has opened in Accra's East Legon neighborhood. The center offers co-working spaces, private offices, and virtual office solutions to support startups and established businesses. Sweden's Honorary Consul to Ghana, Nickie Akosa, established BaseNord in response to the challenges startups face in finding professional working environments and the growing interest from Swedish investors in Ghana.

The initiative aims to deepen commercial ties, which saw significant growth in 2025 with bilateral trade reaching its highest levels in three years. Ghana's exports to Sweden were valued at US$3.07 million, while Swedish exports to Ghana reached US$86.3 million. BaseNord will provide practical support, information, and assistance for Ghanaian businesses seeking partners in Scandinavia and for Swedish investors looking to establish operations in Ghana.

Ms. Akosa, drawing on her experience in the renewable energy sector and establishing foreign company operations in Ghana, envisions BaseNord as a hub for strengthening trade relations. The center intends to facilitate partnerships and strengthen bilateral trade, particularly in the renewable energy, food, and agriculture sectors, by addressing bureaucratic processes and local market navigation.

Why it mattersInvestors and entrepreneurs can access workspace and networking opportunities to facilitate business between Ghana and Sweden.

Ghana's Judiciary Faces Partisan Influence, Warns Ex-Unilever Executive

Public trust in courts has fallen significantly, with many Ghanaians believing judges are swayed by political leaders, according to Yaw Nsarkoh.

Ghana's judiciary is structurally vulnerable to partisan influence, a trend that has gradually eroded public trust over years, according to Yaw Nsarkoh, former Executive Vice President of Unilever Ghana and Nigeria. Speaking at a Tuesday event, Nsarkoh cited Afrobarometer data showing a drop in public trust from 61% in 2002 to 38% in 2022, with 57% of respondents believing judges are influenced by political leaders. He described these figures as empirical signals of weakening judicial independence, not mere accusations.

Nsarkoh pointed to aggressive appointment powers exercised by successive administrations since 2000, noting that over 70% of Supreme Court members between 2017 and 2024 were appointed by a single president. He also referenced reports from organizations like CDD-Ghana highlighting persistent delays and opaque criteria for judicial elevation, particularly during politically sensitive periods. These factors, he argued, reinforce public suspicion about the judiciary's impartiality.

To strengthen judicial independence, Nsarkoh proposed radical measures, including an institutional role for medically fit, living retired Supreme Court justices in proceedings concerning the executive's attempts to remove sitting judges. He stated that the cumulative effect of these trends is a public belief that Ghana's judiciary has become structurally vulnerable to partisan influence, shaping the political imagination of the Fourth Republic.

Why it mattersThe judiciary's perceived vulnerability to partisan influence affects all citizens by potentially compromising the fairness and impartiality of legal proceedings.

NPA, UMaT Explore Partnership to Boost Petroleum Sector

The National Petroleum Authority and the University of Mines and Technology are discussing collaboration to enhance innovation and training in Ghana's downstream petroleum.

The National Petroleum Authority (NPA) is in discussions with the University of Mines and Technology (UMaT) to forge a partnership aimed at strengthening innovation, technical training, and professional development within Ghana's downstream petroleum sector. NPA Chief Executive Officer Godwin Kudzo Tameklo stated that collaboration with academic institutions is crucial for advancing the industry through research and novel solutions to sector challenges. The NPA is considering offering internships to UMaT students in its technical departments to provide practical industry exposure.

Further discussions between the NPA and UMaT include potential special training and secondment opportunities for UMaT teaching staff. The university might also be integrated into training programs for the proposed NPA Centre of Excellence, and there is a possibility of retooling a laboratory at UMaT as part of this initiative. Professor Richard Amorin, Dean of UMaT's School of Petroleum Studies, expressed gratitude for the NPA's openness to collaboration, noting that the university will relay the discussions to its Vice Chancellor to formalize the partnership.

Both institutions are optimistic that this engagement will effectively bridge the gap between academic research and practical industry application, thereby fostering skills development and innovation in Ghana's downstream petroleum industry. The NPA reiterated its commitment to building partnerships with academic bodies as part of its regulatory mandate for the sector.

Why it mattersThe partnership aims to improve skills and innovation in the downstream petroleum sector, which could affect the livelihoods of those employed in it.

Teacher Alleges Widespread Sexual Misconduct in Ghanaian Schools

A teacher claims one or two educators in nearly every school engage in sexual misconduct, leaving students vulnerable.

A teacher at Ekumpoano Catholic Basic School, Stephen Desu, has alleged that sexual misconduct by teachers is pervasive across Ghana’s educational institutions. He stated that the issue has persisted for years, with one or two teachers in almost every school reportedly involved in such behavior. Desu warned that a failure to address this problem decisively could leave students at risk of abuse.

Desu elaborated that the problem is not limited to senior high schools but also exists at primary and junior high levels, though he noted it is more severe at the senior high level. He suggested that some teachers view postings to senior high schools as an opportunity to engage in predatory sexual behavior. These comments were made during a discussion on Joy News’ PM Express concerning allegations at Obuasi Senior High Technical School.

The teacher attributed the ongoing problem to the Ghana Education Service's (GES) perceived failure to act decisively against sexual misconduct. He argued that a lack of punishment for wrongdoing encourages repetition and imitation. Desu called for a more robust response from education authorities to protect students and handle complaints effectively, especially in light of recent scrutiny following the Obuasi SHTS allegations.

Why it mattersStudents are at risk of sexual abuse due to alleged widespread misconduct by teachers in schools across Ghana.

Google Cloud outage disrupts UK banks and services

A power event at a third-party facility in London caused widespread disruption to UK financial institutions and online platforms.

Google Cloud has resolved a significant disruption affecting its UK cloud services, which impacted numerous banks, businesses, and government services. The outage, originating from a power event at a third-party facility in Google's London data centre region, caused widespread errors and intermittent network failures. Services including Revolut, Lloyds Bank, and HSBC confirmed disruptions, with customers reporting inability to access funds and complete transactions.

The tech giant acknowledged the issue, stating that multiple services were affected by the power event in the 'europe-west2' cloud region. This incident led to over 1,500 reports of problems with Revolut alone. Other affected platforms included Royal Mail, DPD, and the HM Courts and Tribunals Service, which experienced issues with its video platform for remote hearings.

A Google Cloud spokesperson apologized for the inconvenience and confirmed that services have been restored. The company plans to publish an analysis of the incident on its service health dashboard. The event highlights the reliance of many online platforms on a few major cloud computing providers and the potential impact of infrastructure failures.

Why it mattersUK banks and businesses were affected by the outage, leading to difficulties in accessing funds and completing transactions.
