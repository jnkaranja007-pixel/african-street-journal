Ghana's Borrowing Costs Plummet as Inflation Falls Sharply

Average lending rates dropped by over 11 percentage points in one year, driven by eased inflation and central bank policy adjustments.

Ghana's borrowing costs have significantly decreased, with average lending rates falling by more than 11 percentage points within a year, according to a World Bank report. The dramatic easing of inflation, from 23.2% in February to 5.4% in December 2025, allowed the Bank of Ghana to implement a substantial monetary policy easing. Average lending rates fell from about 27% in June 2025 to 15.6% by June 2026, while the central bank's policy rate dropped from 28% in April 2025 to 14% by March 2026.

The World Bank described Ghana's disinflation in 2025 as remarkable, noting the lowest headline inflation since 1999. This improvement was attributed to tight monetary policy, a 28.9% appreciation of the cedi, and lower food prices, leading to more favorable financial conditions for households and businesses. However, the report cautioned that price stability remains vulnerable to external factors and supply-side pressures.

Despite the progress, inflation saw a rebound to 5.3% by June 2026, partly due to increased food and energy import costs, higher energy and fertilizer prices, and climate-related production disruptions. The conflict in the Middle East also contributed to rising fuel prices. The World Bank warned that recent inflation increases highlight the need to protect the gains made during the sharp disinflation period.

Why it mattersBusinesses and households are affected by the significant drop in borrowing costs, which has made financial conditions more favorable, although recent inflation pressures pose a risk.

Sydney Police Investigate Second Mistaken Identity Killing

Organised crime is suspected in two fatal shootings within 48 hours, with one victim possibly targeted due to association with residents.

Sydney police are investigating a second potential mistaken identity killing linked to organised crime in under two days. On Wednesday afternoon, a man in his 40s was fatally shot in a drive-by attack at a Merrylands West home. He died at the scene after arriving moments before the shooting. Police stated it is tragically possible the wrong person was shot, as the victim had no known links to organised crime but associated with residents of the address who are known to police.

The previous day, a university student in his 20s with no criminal ties was killed during a home invasion in Glen Alpine. Police described this incident as an "innocent life" taken by organised crime. New South Wales Police Commissioner Mal Lanyon called the student's death "completely unacceptable" and stated that no community should live in fear of such violence.

Following the Tuesday shooting, a social media post from a known organised crime figure suggested the wrong person was targeted. This follows other alleged mistaken identity incidents, including the kidnapping and murder of an 85-year-old man earlier this year and the fatal shooting of a plumber last year.

Why it mattersOrganised crime activity and mistaken identity killings directly impact community safety and create fear among residents.

Ghana Ports Authority Recognizes 410 Staff for Service

The Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority honored 410 employees for dedication and contributions between 2021 and 2025.

The Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority (GPHA) recognized 410 employees for their hard work and contributions from 2021 to 2025. The awards, part of the GPHA's 40th anniversary, celebrated staff whose performance advanced the authority's operations and development. Dr. Samuel Danaa, General Manager for Administration, urged awardees to use the recognition as motivation for future achievements, emphasizing it as a mark of excellence.

The selection process, conducted between January and March 2026, involved nominations from various departments and was based on criteria including commitment, initiative, punctuality, and length of service. Engineer Eric Afre, Chairman of the Awards Committee, stated that the committee collaborated with department heads to identify deserving employees. Captain James R. Quayson, Director of Ports, Takoradi, speaking on behalf of the Director-General, encouraged staff to prioritize customer service, teamwork, and integrity.

Awardees represented departments such as Marine Operations, Finance, IT, and Human Resource Management. Representatives from staff unions congratulated the recipients and called for increased focus on staff welfare and stronger management-union collaboration. They highlighted that a motivated workforce and improved working conditions are crucial for sustaining GPHA's growth and competitiveness, urging all employees to act as ambassadors for GPHA and Ghana's ports.

Why it mattersEmployees of the Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority are recognized for their service, impacting morale and potentially future performance within the organization.

Chief Justice Clarifies Court Operations During Legal Vacation

Urgent legal cases, including injunctions and body release applications, will continue to be heard during the judiciary's annual vacation period.

Chief Justice Paul Baffoe-Bonnie stated that the judiciary's legal vacation, running from August 1 to September 30, does not halt urgent court proceedings. Designated vacation courts and judges remain available to handle emergency matters, ensuring continuity for critical legal processes. The Chief Justice addressed concerns that the vacation period might cause undue delays, emphasizing that arrangements are in place for essential cases to be heard.

During a visit to the Minerals Income Investment Fund (MIIF) in Accra, Justice Baffoe-Bonnie explained that while judges take their annual leave, vacation judges will manage urgent applications. He noted that some judges continue to handle emergency matters and their own ongoing cases, proceeding on leave later. The Chief Justice also mentioned that the judiciary is revising its Rules of Court to address procedural technicalities contributing to case delays, with new rules expected by year-end.

Specialized courts have been established to expedite justice delivery for specific case categories. The Chief Justice commended MIIF for its performance and resource management, highlighting its role in mobilizing mineral royalties and supporting government budgets. He encouraged the MIIF management to continue their effective work in generating returns for the country.

Why it mattersThe judiciary's operational continuity during its legal vacation ensures that urgent business and investment-related legal matters can still be processed.

LA Clippers fined $30m by NBA for salary cap violations

The NBA imposed its largest-ever fine and suspended owner Steve Ballmer for 12 months over illegal dealings with Kawhi Leonard.

The LA Clippers organization has been fined $30m by the National Basketball Association for violating salary rules to funnel money to star player Kawhi Leonard. The league also announced the team will forfeit five first-round draft picks between 2029 and 2033. Owner Steve Ballmer received a 12-month suspension from all league and team activities for his involvement in Leonard’s sponsorship contracts.

NBA commissioner Adam Silver stated the Clippers engaged in "flagrant violations" and "knowingly seeking to help Mr Leonard obtain off-court income opportunities." The investigation found a pattern of misconduct and multiple significant rules violations by the Clippers, who are repeat offenders of salary-cap circumvention rules. Two-time NBA champion Leonard was fined $700,000. Team president Gillian Zucker was suspended for one year without pay for misleading investigators.

Leonard, 35, accepted full responsibility for his actions and expressed regret for the distraction. He stated he entered his contract in good faith and had no knowledge of any intent to circumvent the salary cap. The NBA will monitor the Clippers organization and personnel for five years. Leonard joined the Clippers in 2019 and played for the franchise for seven years before departing earlier this year.

Why it mattersThe LA Clippers organization and its owner face significant financial penalties and suspensions for violating salary rules, impacting their business operations and reputation.
