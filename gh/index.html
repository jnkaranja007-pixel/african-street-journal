Ghana takes in 42.5 to 62 tonnes of mercury a year through illicit routes

Gold miners in four regions handle the toxic metal daily, and stakeholders say it is reaching water supplies and the food chain.

Stakeholders have disclosed that between 42.5 and 62 tonnes of mercury enter Ghana each year through illicit channels, for use in artisanal and small-scale gold mining. Dr Sylvester Akpah, chief executive of Smart Innovation Ghana Limited, made the claim at a symposium in Accra organised by the United States Embassy under its Ambassador's Water Experts Programme.

Dr Akpah said more than 2.5 million people depend directly or indirectly on small-scale mining, and the mercury reaches them through unidentified routes. Mining communities in the Western, Ashanti, Eastern and Central regions are the worst hit, with several water bodies already contaminated. He named Tarkwa and nearby communities as under severe pressure, warning that the pollution could cut access to safe drinking water.

Professor Daniel Nukpezah of the University of Ghana's Institute for Environment and Sanitation Studies said mercury does not break down in the environment and can turn into methylmercury in water, where it builds up in fish. Dr Akpah called for tougher enforcement and wider use of gold-catching technology, which he said remained limited despite investment in importing the equipment.

Business · 2026-11-16

Ghana cybersecurity losses hit GHS209.7 million

Financial losses from cyber incidents between January and September 2026 far exceeded the total for 2025, raising concerns.

Ghana recorded over GHS209.7 million in financial losses from 4,938 cybersecurity incidents in the first nine months of 2026. This figure surpasses the GHS25 million lost from 4,604 incidents throughout the entirety of 2025. The escalating losses have prompted concerns regarding the security of digital transactions and public trust in online services.

Dr Divine Selase Agbeti, Director-General of the Cyber Security Authority (CSA), stated that the increased use of mobile money, digital banking, and AI has expanded opportunities but also amplified cyber threats. He emphasized that trust is vital for the digital economy, warning that declining confidence could slow data adoption and weaken the broader digital economy. Mr Duke Banson of the Ghana Cybersecurity Industry Forum highlighted the need for stronger collaboration among professionals to enhance national preparedness.

Dr Agbeti urged a move from reactive measures to proactive prevention, advocating for security integration from the design stage of digital systems. He also called for strengthened governance, making cybersecurity a senior-management priority and incorporating it into risk management. The Ghana Cybersecurity Industry Forum, established under the Cybersecurity Act, 2020 (Act 1038), aims to coordinate the national cybersecurity ecosystem and develop industry standards.

Why it mattersGhanaian businesses and individuals lost over GHS209.7 million to cyber incidents between January and September 2026, impacting digital transaction security.

Business · 2026-11-16

IMF says Ghana's reforms are bearing fruit with stronger growth

The IMF says the economy grew 6% last year and inflation has fallen to single digits, after a difficult period of policy changes.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) says Ghana is reaping the benefits of difficult policy reforms, with stronger-than-expected growth, single-digit inflation and an improved fiscal position. IMF Director of Communications Julie Kozack made the remarks at a news conference in Washington, DC, saying decisions taken under challenging circumstances are now supporting economic stability.

Kozack said the economy expanded by 6% last year and that the Fund projects 5.7% growth this year. She said Ghana's external position has strengthened and its debt-to-GDP ratio is around 50%. She added that the primary surplus is on track to meet year-end targets, and she praised the extensive social dialogue the government held with stakeholders to explain reforms and collect feedback.

Kozack said the IMF's engagement is moving beyond crisis management toward consolidating reforms and building longer-term resilience, including through Ghana's decision to pursue a Policy Coordination Instrument. She said the next priority is strengthening the economy's ability to withstand future shocks.

Why it mattersThe IMF's projected 5.7% growth and a primary surplus on track for year-end targets point to a stronger fiscal footing for the government's next phase of reforms.

Politics · 2026-11-16

NDC elects regional executives across Ghana

The governing party held elections supervised by the Electoral Commission to select new regional leaders for the next four years.

Delegates of the National Democratic Congress (NDC) on Saturday elected new regional executive officers across Ghana to lead the party for the next four years. The polls, overseen by the Electoral Commission (EC) with significant security, saw competitive races for various positions in multiple regions.

In the Greater Accra Region, Benjamin Danso defeated incumbent Emmanuel Nii Ashie Moore by over 500 votes to become the new regional chairman. Felicia Mekpoi Bortey secured her fourth term as Regional Women’s Organiser with 65 votes, while Mohammed Abdul Muqmin Juda was elected Regional Youth Organiser. The Ashanti Region re-elected its incumbent Chairman, Augustus Andrews, who received 814 votes against Isham Alhassan's 622.

The NDC's reorganisation at the grassroots level now concludes with these regional elections, preparing the party for national officer elections in December. The Bole-Bamboi MP, Yusif Sulemana, congratulated the new Savannah Region executives, urging unity for the 2028 general elections, a region described as the home of President John Dramani Mahama.

Why it mattersThe governing National Democratic Congress has completed its regional executive elections, setting the stage for national officer elections in December as the party prepares for the 2028 general elections.

Sport · 2026-11-16

Angola beat Ghana 2-0 in amputee World Cup opener

Ghana's Black Challenge now face Iraq in a must-win group game at the tournament in Mexico, their head coach has said.

Ghana's Black Challenge began their WAFF Amputee Football World Cup campaign with a 2-0 defeat to Angola on Saturday at the San Martin stadium in San Juan de los Lagos, Mexico. Angola took the lead before half-time and added a second goal after the break, while Ghana held their shape early on.

Ghana's head coach, Stephen Richard Obeng, had promised an attacking approach against Iraq before the Angola game, as his side looked to keep their hopes alive in the tournament, which runs from November 13 to 22, 2026.

The defeat leaves Ghana with a fight to progress, and the team will turn its focus to its next group game.

Why it mattersA second defeat would leave Ghana's Black Challenge with little chance of reaching the next stage of the tournament, which ends on November 22.
