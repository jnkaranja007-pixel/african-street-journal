Newsfile weighs University of Ghana hostel fees, detention directive and $60.68 million...

The programme will examine Rent Control's order on unassessed rent increases alongside the government's case for replacing its ageing presidential aircraft.

Sources: MyJoyOnline and 3News · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

This week's Newsfile will examine accommodation fees at the University of Ghana, the Attorney-General's 48-hour detention directive and the government's US$60.68 million purchase of a Dassault Falcon 6X. The University of Ghana Enterprises Limited, which runs the university's accommodation, initially proposed a 26% hostel-fee increase, then reduced it after consultations with student leaders.

The Rent Control Department has ordered the enterprise and private hostel operators to suspend increases that lack the required assessment, restore previous rates and refund excess payments. The university says its charges cover operating and maintenance costs, utilities and loan repayments for accommodation. The Attorney-General's directive also requires law enforcement agencies to report quarterly to his office on arrests, releases, court appearances and alleged rights violations.

The Falcon 6X arrived in Ghana on November 16. Government spokesperson Felix Kwakye Ofosu says replacing the Falcon 900EX is cheaper than continued maintenance and private charters; the government cites fuel leaks, corrosion and engine faults in the older aircraft. An earlier buyer ceded its order to Ghana, allowing earlier delivery.

Why it mattersStudents affected by hostel charges have been ordered to receive refunds of excess payments and restoration of previous rates where increases lacked assessment.
