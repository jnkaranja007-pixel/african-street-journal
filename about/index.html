About

The African Street Journal covers all 55 African countries, every day. Stories are short, written in plain English and built to load on any phone, over any connection.

Africa's news is reported by hundreds of local outlets, and most of it never travels beyond the country where it happened. Each morning we read that reporting, select what matters and publish a concise account of it, with the original outlet named and linked.

How the desk works

An automated desk reads public news feeds from outlets across the continent, groups reports of the same event and ranks them by public consequence. Elections, budgets, courts, health, food, power, jobs, trade and security lead. Gossip, sponsored content, opinion and tabloid items are left out.

AI language models write each story, using only the facts in the source report. Software checks every story before it runs: each figure must appear in the source, and the outlet must be named and linked. A story that fails is dropped, not published. A separate editor model then grades each story against our standards and holds back those that fall short.

We summarise; we do not report from the ground. Credit for every story belongs to the journalists who broke it. Read them, and support them.

On a small data bundle

The text-only edition carries the lead story from every country as plain text, with no maps or images. Story pages are mostly text as well.

A news reader app such as Feedly, Inoreader or NetNewsWire can deliver our headlines as they are published, using very little data. Add https://africanstreetjournal.com/feed.xml to the app; this is known as an RSS feed. To follow a single country, add its feed instead. Kenya's is https://africanstreetjournal.com/ke/feed.xml, and every country page links to its own.
