About

The African Street Journal exists so that people across Africa and in the diaspora can keep up with current events in all 55 countries: in plain language, on any phone and on any connection.

Most of the continent's news is spread across hundreds of outlets, and much of it is hard to find from outside the country it happened in. We use AI language models to read that reporting every day, pick out what matters most and summarise it, so that our people stay informed and knowledgeable about their continent. The journalists who did the reporting are always credited and linked.

How the desk works

Every morning an automated desk reads public news feeds from outlets across Africa, groups reports of the same event, and ranks them for public importance: elections, budgets, courts, health, food, power, jobs, trade and security come first. Gossip, sponsored copy, opinion columns and tabloid items are filtered out.

Each selected report is rewritten into a short original summary by an AI language model, working only from the facts in that report. Software then checks every story: figures must appear in the source, the story must name and link the outlet it came from, and anything that fails is dropped rather than published.

Stories are summaries, not original reporting from the ground. The outlet that broke the story is always credited and linked; please read and support them.

Reading on a small data bundle

The text-only edition lists the latest lead story from every country as plain text, with no maps or pictures. Story pages are mostly text too.

If you use a news reader app such as Feedly, Inoreader or NetNewsWire, add https://africanstreetjournal.com/feed.xml to it. The latest headlines then arrive in the app as they are published, using very little data. This kind of feed is called RSS. To follow one country only, add its feed instead: Kenya's is https://africanstreetjournal.com/ke/feed.xml, and every country page links its own.
