Congo holds aquaculture forum as local fish output falls short of demand

The country needs about 163 000 tonnes of fish a year, while local production reaches nearly 60 000 tonnes, leaving a gap of more than 100 000 tonnes.

Source: Journal de Brazza · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

The Republic of Congo held its first Forum on Investment in Aquaculture at the Grande foire agricole du Congo in Bambou Mingali, Ignié district, over the weekend. National fish needs are estimated at about 163 000 tonnes a year, while local production is nearly 60 000 tonnes.

The shortfall of more than 100 000 tonnes is met through imports, Journal de Brazza reported. Nearly 120 experts and sector participants gathered for two days to discuss barriers to aquaculture growth and hear findings from an international organisation’s study of the country’s aquaculture potential.

The forum received financial support from the World Bank and Proclimat and was organised ahead of the planned launch of the Congo Agriculture and Aquaculture Development Project. The initiative is intended to attract investment and increase production capacity.

Why it mattersFish consumers rely on imports to cover a shortfall of more than 100 000 tonnes against annual national needs.
