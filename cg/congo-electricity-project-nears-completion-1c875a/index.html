Congo electricity project nears completion

The World Bank-funded Pasel aims to cut losses and improve power supply across the nation.

Source: Journal de Brazza · 2026-11-13 · 1 min read

The Republic of Congo is nearing the end of its Project for the Improvement of Electricity Services (Pasel), a $100 million initiative funded by the World Bank. The project seeks to address technical and commercial issues hindering electricity transport and distribution. Minister of Energy and Hydraulic Resources, Bruno Jean Richard Itoua, met with a World Bank delegation led by Cheick Fantamady Kanté on November 10 in Brazzaville to discuss the project's progress.

Cheick Fantamady Kanté reported that the project's execution is proceeding normally, with work on electrical installations and the acquisition of technical equipment advancing. Despite significant production capacity, the country faces challenges in delivering reliable electricity to households and businesses. Measures to accelerate interventions on transport and distribution networks were also discussed.

The World Bank delegation expressed optimism that the Pasel works could be completed before the end of 2026. This timeline reflects the program's advancement and the efforts to enhance the national electrical system's performance. The project's success is expected to improve the quality of electricity service for Congolese citizens and businesses.

Why it mattersThe Pasel project, funded by a $100 million World Bank loan, is expected to be completed by the end of 2026, aiming to improve the national electricity supply.
