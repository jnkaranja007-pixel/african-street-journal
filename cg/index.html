Senate Approves Oil Contract Amendments

Four legislative proposals to modify existing oil production sharing agreements were adopted by the Senate on August 26, 2026.

Brazzaville senators approved four draft laws on August 26, 2026, that amend oil production sharing contracts. The approved texts concern the Nanga II bis, Nanga IV, Nanga V, and Marine XXIX-A permits. Amendments for Nanga II bis and Nanga IV, signed November 23, 2023, involve the Republic of Congo, SNPC, and China's Dingheng Mining Co. Ltd. The third amendment relates to Nanga V, and the fourth to Marine XXIX-A, under a contract signed April 25, 2025, between the Congolese state, SNPC, and Oriental Energy SAU.

These amendments focus on adjusting technical mechanisms tied to the "high price" clause within the oil contracts. The goal is to adapt contractual terms to current sector realities and encourage continued investment. Lawmakers were urged to consider the economic, financial, industrial, budgetary, and social consequences of these changes.

The stated objective is to provide greater clarity for operators and foster conditions conducive to the ongoing exploration, exploitation, and production of oil. This initiative aims to offer more visibility to industry players and ensure the continuity of petroleum activities.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the Senate's approval of oil contract amendments that aim to provide greater clarity and favorable conditions for continued exploration and production activities.

Belgium Offers Waste Management Expertise to Congo

Belgium's ambassador to Congo met with the Minister of Urban Sanitation to discuss sharing technical assistance in waste management and urban development.

Belgium intends to strengthen its cooperation with Congo in sanitation and urban development, with a focus on waste management and recovery. The Belgian ambassador, Emmanuelle Plissart De Foy, met with Minister Juste Désiré Mondelé on August 25 in Brazzaville to express her country's readiness to share its expertise.

Discussions centered on improving waste management practices and urban planning, including the concept of a circular economy to give waste a second life and reduce environmental impact. The talks also covered the recovery of waste from both land and aquatic environments, such as rivers and the sea.

While no specific projects have been finalized, both parties identified several areas for enhanced collaboration. This potential partnership aims to improve waste management and support Congolese authorities' efforts to create cleaner, better-organized cities.

Why it mattersInvestors may benefit from improved urban infrastructure and waste management practices that could lead to new business opportunities and a more attractive investment climate.

Coach Details Red Devils' Preparation for Upcoming Matches

National team coach Claude le Roy outlined the team's training strategy for the upcoming Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers against Namibia and Cameroon.

The Republic of Congo's national football team, the Red Devils, are undergoing preparations for their initial Africa Cup of Nations qualifying matches. Coach Claude le Roy held a press conference on August 26 at the Congolese Football Federation headquarters to discuss the team's strategy. This initial training camp focuses on local players, serving as a crucial stage for the technical staff to assess talent and identify potential squad members for international fixtures.

Le Roy detailed the team's readiness for the upcoming qualifiers, scheduled for late September and early October. The team will face Namibia and then Cameroon in their opening matches, which are considered vital for securing early points and a strong start to their qualification campaign. Beyond athletic performance, the coach emphasized the importance of building team cohesion, developing tactical plays, and improving player coordination during this preparatory period.

This training camp for local players acts as a preliminary assessment before the final squad is selected. The coaching staff has several weeks to refine their choices and assemble a competitive team capable of performing well in these critical early games. The focus is on establishing a solid foundation for the team's pursuit of qualification.

Why it mattersThe national football team's preparation and upcoming matches directly affect the country's sporting performance and national pride.

Naval and Gendarmerie Clash Near Makotipoko; Both Congos Urge Restraint

Naval and gendarmerie forces from the Republic of Congo and the Democratic Republic of Congo clashed near Makotipoko, prompting calls for calm from both governments.

An incident involving naval and gendarmerie elements from both Congos has been reported near Makotipoko, in the Nkéni-Alima department. Authorities from both nations are working to establish the exact circumstances of the events, which remain unclear. The clash occurred while the Congolese (DRC) Minister of Foreign Affairs, Thérèse Kayikwamba Wagner, was visiting Brazzaville.

Minister Wagner stated that exchanges were underway between the two countries' authorities to precisely determine the incident's details. "I am in Brazzaville in contact with my counterpart. The defense ministers of our two countries are in constant exchange. We are finalizing the triangulation of information," she said. She urged all parties to exercise restraint, emphasizing that the incident does not reflect the strong relations between the two nations.

The priority is to clarify the facts and prevent any escalation. The DRC's top diplomat stressed the need to strengthen existing dialogue and coordination mechanisms between the two states to better anticipate and prevent such incidents. Both governments aim to shed full light on the event and quickly restore calm in the Makotipoko area.

Why it mattersThe incident between naval and gendarmerie forces from the Republic of Congo and the Democratic Republic of Congo could potentially disrupt cross-border relations and stability.
