Rémy Ayayos Ikounga named deputy grand master of Congo's Masonic lodge

Colonel major Rémy Ayayos Ikounga replaces Jean-Dominique Okemba, who was removed in April.

Colonel major Rémy Ayayos Ikounga has been appointed the new deputy grand master of the Grande Loge du Congo. The ceremony took place on October 24-25 in Brazzaville during the lodge's general assembly. Ikounga, who is close to Edgar Nguesso, replaces Jean-Dominique Okemba, who was dismissed in April.

Ikounga, who also heads the presidential domain directorate, is described as a fervent Catholic and president of the football club L'Athlétic Club Léopards de Dolisie from 2008 to 2017. He also leads the association of former students of the military preparatory school Général-Leclerc. The appointment was made by Grand Master Denis Sassou-Nguesso.

The Grande Loge du Congo's assembly was attended by foreign delegations from the Grande Loge nationale française and the Grande Loge unie d'Angleterre. Claude Dohou, deputy grand master of the GLNF, officiated the ceremony. Maixent Raoul Ominga, director general of the national oil company, was named provincial grand master for the North.

Why it mattersThe appointment of Rémy Ayayos Ikounga as deputy grand master of the Grande Loge du Congo affects the leadership within a significant Masonic obedience in the country.

UBA Congo clients must update accounts by December 2026

The bank requires updated personal and business client information to ensure security and regulatory compliance.

UBA Congo is asking all individual and business clients to update their account files by December 31, 2026.

Clients need to provide a valid ID, recent proof of address if necessary, and updated personal or professional information for individuals. Businesses must submit current legal and administrative documents, details of directors, and information on beneficial owners.

Customers can visit their UBA branch or contact their account manager to verify and update their documents to maintain a secure and compliant banking relationship.

Sport · 2026-10-31

Congo football league expands to 18 clubs

The national championship will adopt a zonal format to manage costs for clubs.

The Congolese Football Federation (Fécofoot) will expand its top-tier league from 14 to 18 clubs for the upcoming season. The competition will be organized in a zonal format, a decision driven by economic realities and the financial burdens faced by clubs. This change aims to make the championship more manageable within the current financial constraints.

Fécofoot president Éric Alain Mantot stated that the zonal format is an economic necessity to reduce the costs associated with running the league. The federation also plans to improve the management capabilities of sports structures. A training session for club presidents and secretaries, supported by FIFA, will take place in Brazzaville from November 16 to 18, covering topics like leadership, finance, and marketing.

Following the training, clubs will receive an exceptional subsidy, contingent on signing an agreement that allows Fécofoot to monitor fund usage. Mantot clarified that Fécofoot's role is not direct club financing but rather to encourage clubs to seek sponsors and advertisers for greater autonomy. The federation's new orientation places club financing at the center of the next football season.

Why it mattersCongolese football clubs will face a new league structure and management training to address financial challenges.

Business · 2026-10-31

CEMAC countries to withdraw old coins

Seven categories of old FCFA coins minted between 1958 and 1985 will lose legal tender status on November 1, 2026.

The Bank of Central African States (BEAC) will demonetize seven categories of old FCFA coins starting November 1, 2026, across the CEMAC region. These coins, minted between 1958 and 1985, will no longer be accepted for transactions after this date. The operation aims to gradually withdraw these older currencies from circulation.

BEAC National Director Serge Dino Daniel Ngassakys announced on October 28 in Brazzaville that citizens have 12 months to exchange the targeted coins. The first phase, from November 1, 2026, to July 31, 2027, allows exchanges at BEAC, commercial banks, and microfinance institutions. The final phase, from August 1 to October 31, 2027, will be exclusively handled by the BEAC.

Coins from the 2006 and 2024 series will remain valid and continue to circulate within the six CEMAC countries. This measure provides a transitional period for holders of the old coins to exchange them before they are permanently removed from the monetary system.

Why it mattersCitizens in the six CEMAC countries, including the Republic of Congo, must exchange old FCFA coins within a 12-month period to avoid losing their value.
