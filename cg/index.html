Congo's energy loop project to expand to new countries

The Central Africa Energy Pool wants to connect more countries to a regional electricity grid.

The Central Africa Energy Pool (PEAC) plans to expand its energy loop project to include more countries in the sub-region. This initiative aims to boost electricity exchanges and strengthen grid interconnections across Central Africa. The project, initially planned for the Republic of Congo, Angola, and the Democratic Republic of Congo, could now incorporate additional states.

Discussions on November 5 in Brazzaville between Congo's Energy Minister Bruno Jean Richard Itoua and PEAC leadership, including Director Committee President Rui Pereira Do Amaral Gourgel, focused on this expansion. The PEAC seeks to accelerate regional energy integration amid ongoing challenges with electricity access and supply security in several nations.

The project also involves the potential construction of the 600 MW Chollet hydroelectric dam. The PEAC's goal is to promote better sharing of energy resources and speed up the development of common infrastructure, using grid interconnections as a tool for energy security and economic growth in Central Africa.

Why it mattersThe expansion of the energy loop project will facilitate cross-border electricity trade, potentially improving energy access and economic development for countries in Central Africa.

Brazzaville court opens criminal session with murder cases

The Court of Appeal in Brazzaville will hear dozens of criminal cases including murder and state security charges over three months.

The Court of Appeal in Brazzaville opened its criminal session on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, with proceedings set to run through January 2027. 10 jurors were sworn in to assist professional magistrates. The session will examine approximately 50 criminal cases, including the assassination of Anne Félicité Massamba-Débat, daughter of a former president, who was killed on June 13, 2023.

Five individuals are accused in the Massamba-Débat murder case. The court will also hear the case of lawyer Bob Kaben Massouka, detained since July 2025, who faces charges of criminal conspiracy and attempting to undermine state security with six co-accused. Another case involves about 20 people, mostly foreigners, charged with criminal conspiracy, passport forgery, and corruption.

The first day's hearings focused on aggravated theft and murder charges against several individuals, including a fugitive known as "Maréchal Mbibo." The session aims to address serious offenses against personal integrity over the next three months.

Why it mattersResidents of Brazzaville will see justice administered for serious crimes including murder and state security offenses.

Congo launches third phase of health security program

Prime Minister Anatole Collinet Makosso presided over the launch of the HeSP program's third phase in Brazzaville.

Republic of Congo Prime Minister Anatole Collinet Makosso officially launched the third phase of the West and Central Africa Health Security Program (HeSP) in Brazzaville on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. The launch occurred during a regional conference focused on strengthening health security, which gathered nearly 180 participants from various states, regional institutions, and international partners.

The third phase of the program involves the Congo, Cameroon, the Central African Republic, and Chad. Its aim is to improve epidemiological surveillance and enhance national capacities for preventing and responding to health emergencies. The initiative seeks to better anticipate health risks and coordinate interventions against epidemics that could cross borders.

The regional meeting provides a platform for exchanging experiences and best practices in preventing and managing health threats. The participating states intend to consolidate cooperation mechanisms and develop more effective early warning systems to address shared health challenges.

Why it mattersThe launch of the third phase of the HeSP program directly affects the health security of people in Congo and neighboring countries by improving epidemic response.

Congo plans stronger epidemic response

Brazzaville conference concludes with calls to boost surveillance and research for regional health security.

Republic of Congo's regional integration minister, Ludovic Ngatsé, announced on November 4 in Brazzaville that a recent conference will help the sub-region enhance its capacity to fight epidemics. The meeting's conclusions urge strengthening surveillance systems, consolidating the "One Health" approach, developing human resources, and promoting research and innovation to build a regional health security community.

Health professionals from Central and West Africa shared experiences over two days, formulating recommendations to prevent and manage health crises. These include sustainable financing, maintaining calm during crises, applying the "One Health" approach, and prioritizing prevention, as crisis management is more costly than prevention. CEMAC vice-president Assamba Angondo stated that health security is essential for stability and prosperity, as a country's epidemic prevention capacity contributes to its neighbors' security.

Minister Ngatsé praised the conference's exchanges and reaffirmed Congo's commitment to supporting this collective ambition and strengthening health response nationally and regionally. The conference highlighted that cooperation is a necessity for regional health security.

Why it mattersThe Republic of Congo is working with neighboring countries to improve epidemic prevention and response, which directly affects the health and safety of its citizens.

Fondation Mérieux urges unified viral threat approach

Alain Mérieux met with Congo's science minister to discuss a global 'One Health' strategy against epidemics.

Alain Mérieux, president of Fondation Mérieux, stated on November 3 in Brazzaville that a unified approach is needed to combat viral threats. He emphasized that African countries must open borders to face viruses that do not respect them, advocating for an interconnected and global response involving countries, doctors, veterinarians, and environmental specialists.

Mérieux explained that the response to epidemics can no longer be isolated, promoting the 'One Health' concept championed by Professor Ntoumi. Fondation Mérieux, active in 15 countries facing security or health crises, intends to serve Congo's needs, acknowledging the globalization of infection risks and the need for joint responses from foundations, laboratories like Institut Pasteur, and various nations, citing Ebola and monkeypox as lessons.

The Fondation Mérieux president assured that the Congolese government is open to collaboration and praised local expertise, particularly Professor Francine Ntoumi, for her ability to train scientists. He expressed confidence in Congo's scientific community, which is forming a school of doctors and biologists to meet these needs, concluding that a seven-year collaboration has been tacitly renewed.

Why it mattersThe president of Fondation Mérieux met with Congo's minister of scientific research to discuss a unified approach to viral threats, highlighting the need for collaboration between countries and specialists.
