Six Men Face Trial for Illegal Mining in National Park

Accused of illicit mineral extraction in Conkouati-Douli National Park, six men appeared before the Pointe-Noire court.

Six men stood trial on August 28 before the Pointe-Noire High Court, facing charges of illegal mineral exploitation within the Conkouati-Douli National Park in the Kouilou department. The proceedings highlight ongoing threats to protected natural areas in the Republic of Congo. The accused, who were provisionally detained, allegedly conducted mining activities outside any legal framework within the protected zone.

These alleged practices risk degrading ecosystems through habitat destruction and biodiversity disruption. The Conkouati-Douli National Park is a crucial protected area, vital for preserving Atlantic facade ecosystems and housing significant animal and plant diversity. The trial occurs amid intensified efforts to combat illegal activities in protected spaces.

Environmental and wildlife authorities emphasize that controlling economic activities and enforcing legal sanctions are key to preserving these territories. The court's action underscores the government's commitment to prosecuting illegal resource extraction within ecologically sensitive zones.

Why it mattersSix men are accused of illegal mineral exploitation, which authorities say degrades ecosystems and threatens biodiversity within a protected national park.

Communication Council Convenes Ordinary Session in Brazzaville

The Superior Council for Freedom of Communication is holding its second annual meeting to discuss media regulation amidst digital transformation.

The Superior Council for Freedom of Communication (CSLC) convened its second ordinary session of the year in Brazzaville from August 31 to September 1, 2026. The meeting, officially opened on Monday, focused on several issues related to the regulation of the communication sector. The conference hall of the Ministry of Justice served as the venue for the official opening ceremony, presided over by CSLC President Médard Milandou.

The session was attended by institutional and professional stakeholders from the communication sector, including leaders of media organizations, representatives of information and communication associations, media professionals, and CSLC collaborators. This gathering occurs as the media landscape undergoes significant changes, particularly with the rise of digital technologies, necessitating stronger oversight mechanisms while safeguarding communication freedom.

Over two days, CSLC members are tasked with reviewing agenda items and formulating guidelines to foster a more organized and responsible communication sector. The Council aims to continue its efforts in promoting a media environment where freedom of expression and the right to information are exercised in compliance with laws and professional principles.

Why it mattersThe Superior Council for Freedom of Communication is meeting to regulate the media sector, affecting how information is disseminated and accessed.

Experts Validate Public Procurement Manual to Standardize Controls

A new reference manual aims to unify practices and boost the effectiveness of public procurement oversight across the country.

Experts met on August 29 to review and approve a new manual intended to standardize public procurement control practices. This tool is designed to ensure uniform procedures and enhance the efficiency of oversight mechanisms. The manual provides a common framework, detailing specific practices and procedures for all actors involved in public procurement control.

The development of this manual addresses the need for a harmonized instrument that promotes a clearer understanding of public procurement requirements. Its validation marks a significant step in improving the control systems for public contracts. The meeting also facilitated discussions on the practical implementation of the document.

The objective is to establish this manual as a key reference for professionals responsible for ensuring the regularity and compliance of public procurement operations. Its successful implementation is expected to lead to more transparent and effective public spending.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the new manual aims to improve transparency and efficiency in public procurement, potentially leading to better contract management and reduced risks.

Congo Mayor Meets Traders on Tax and Market Plans

The administrator-mayor of Lumumba visited Ndji-Ndji market to discuss regulatory taxes and the upcoming central market.

The new administrator-mayor of Lumumba, Pointe-Noire's first district, visited the Ndji-Ndji market and surrounding commercial areas to engage directly with local traders. Accompanied by his teams, the mayor's visit focused on understanding traders' concerns and clarifying issues related to their business operations. Key discussions centered on the payment and adherence to regulatory taxes.

During the exchanges, the administrator-mayor assured traders that taxes must comply with current regulations. He urged them to fulfill their obligations while also acknowledging the difficulties they might face. The conversation also touched upon the highly anticipated opening of Pointe-Noire's new central market, which is expected to improve sales and purchasing conditions and enhance commercial organization.

Traders expressed palpable impatience regarding the new central market, seeking information on the next steps for its opening and the procedures for their eventual installation. The mayor's direct engagement aimed to foster a closer relationship with the business community and address their immediate concerns about compliance and future infrastructure.

Why it mattersLocal traders in Lumumba are directly affected by discussions on tax compliance and the delayed opening of the new central market, which impacts their business operations and future plans.
