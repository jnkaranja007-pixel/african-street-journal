UBA Congo clients must update accounts by December 2026

The bank requires updated personal and business documents to ensure security and regulatory compliance.

UBA Congo is asking all individual and business clients to update their account files by December 31, 2026. This action is part of the bank's commitment to secure banking operations and meet regulatory requirements. A complete and current client file helps the bank maintain reliable information, secure banking relationships, and prevent fraud.

Individual clients need a valid ID and updated personal and professional information, with recent proof of address if necessary. Business clients must provide up-to-date legal and administrative documents, details on directors and beneficial owners, and proof of business activity. Clients can visit their UBA branch or contact their account manager to complete these updates.

Updating client files strengthens account security, streamlines operations, and ensures transparent banking relationships. The bank urges clients not to wait until the last moment to verify and update their information before the 2026 deadline.

Congo pharmacists ask health ministry to revoke Propharma license

The National Order of Pharmacists cites alleged procedural irregularities and unauthorized activities by Propharma.

The National Order of Pharmacists in Congo has requested the Ministry of Health and Population to review Propharma’s operating license. Dr. MPANDZO Djagger, president of the Order, stated on October 27, 2026, that Propharma may have conducted business, including importation, without required prior authorizations, violating pharmaceutical regulations.

Dr. MPANDZO Djagger reported that the Order formally approached the Ministry of Health on June 1, 2026, with a request for clarification regarding Propharma's authorization, but received no response. He also noted that a prior inquiry to the General Inspectorate of Health on March 30, 2026, also went unanswered.

The Order seeks a formal response from the Minister of Health to ensure compliance with laws governing drug importation and sales, aiming to prevent illegal trafficking. They emphasize that strict adherence to regulations is vital for public health and medication safety.

Why it mattersThe health of citizens is at risk due to potential illegal sales and unsafe medications.

Congo football league expands to 18 clubs

The national championship will adopt a zonal format to manage club finances and operational costs.

The Congolese Football Federation (Fécofoot) will expand its top-tier national league from 14 to 18 clubs for the upcoming season. The competition will be organized in a zonal format, a decision driven by economic realities and the financial burdens faced by clubs. This change aims to adapt the championship's structure to the clubs' financial constraints.

Fécofoot president Éric Alain Mantot stated that the zonal format is an economic necessity to manage the costs associated with organizing the league. The federation also plans to enhance the management capabilities of sports structures through a training program supported by FIFA. This training, scheduled from November 16-18 in Brazzaville, will cover leadership, governance, financial management, and marketing for club officials.

Following the training, clubs will be eligible for an exceptional subsidy, contingent on signing an agreement that allows Fécofoot to monitor fund usage. Mantot clarified that Fécofoot's role is not direct club financing, encouraging clubs to seek sponsors and advertisers for additional resources. The federation's new direction focuses on rationalizing expenses and strengthening management to support the next football season.

Why it mattersFootball clubs in the Republic of Congo will face a new league structure and management training to address financial challenges.

Tech · 2026-10-29

Congo mobile networks falter from fuel shortage

Telecom operators struggle to power backup generators due to a lack of diesel, disrupting services.

Mobile network services across the Republic of Congo are experiencing disruptions because telecommunication operators lack diesel fuel for their backup generators. The shortage prevents essential equipment from running when the national electricity supply is insufficient. This situation is impacting phone calls, internet access, and mobile money transactions nationwide.

The Agency for Regulation of Posts and Electronic Communications (ARPCE) confirmed public electronic communications network disturbances on October 27. Operators report average daily electricity supply of only nine hours, with backup batteries lasting about six hours. This leaves several hours daily that require generator power, which is now hampered by insufficient diesel.

The ARPCE stated that the disruptions stem from inadequate power supply and the inability of backup generators to compensate due to the lack of gasoil. Businesses and individuals increasingly rely on mobile communications, internet, and electronic payments, making service continuity critical.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in the Republic of Congo are facing unreliable mobile phone, internet, and mobile money services due to a fuel shortage affecting telecom infrastructure.
