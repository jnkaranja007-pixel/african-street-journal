Congo launches energy study for special economic zones

The study, launched November 3, 2026, aims to secure energy supplies for industrial zones by 2030.

The Republic of Congo officially launched a study on November 3, 2026, in Brazzaville to secure energy supplies for its future industrial zones by 2030. The Ministry of Energy is overseeing the initiative, implemented by the National Agency for Rural Electrification with support from the UN Development Programme. This study will assess the energy needs of various industrial zones and identify suitable solutions, including solar power with storage, biomass, and grid connections.

Minister Michel Djombo stated that the study aims to strengthen electrification for Special Economic Zones (ZES). The goal is to propose reliable, sustainable, and economically viable energy solutions to meet industrial demands. Ensuring sufficient and regular energy is essential for attracting investors, developing production units, and processing raw materials locally.

This effort is part of the National Energy Pact and the international Mission 300 initiative. It seeks to identify projects for public and private funding, balancing industrial development with the energy transition and reduced greenhouse gas emissions. Expected outcomes include job creation, local value chain development, and strengthening the national production base by 2030.

Why it mattersThe study aims to improve energy supply for industrial zones, which will attract investors and boost local production and job creation.

Congo launches third phase of HeSP health security program

Nearly 180 participants gathered in Brazzaville for the regional meeting on strengthening health security.

Prime Minister Anatole Collinet Makosso officially launched the third phase of the Health Security Programme in West and Central Africa (HeSP) in Brazzaville on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. The launch took place during a regional conference focused on sharing knowledge and experiences among participating countries, with nearly 180 representatives from states, regional institutions, and international partners in attendance.

This phase of the HeSP program involves Congo, Cameroon, the Central African Republic, and Chad. Its aim is to strengthen cooperation mechanisms between these nations, improve epidemiological surveillance, and enhance national capacities for preventing and responding to health emergencies. The participating states intend to better anticipate health risks and coordinate their responses to epidemics and other threats that could cross borders.

The program seeks to foster collective responses and more effective monitoring systems for countries facing shared health challenges. Prime Minister Makosso stated that Africa is committed to uniting its forces to effectively combat epidemics.

Why it mattersThe Republic of Congo is participating in a regional health security program to improve epidemic prevention and response.

Health · 2026-11-05

Fondation Mérieux urges unified viral threat approach in Congo

Alain Mérieux stressed the need for a global, interconnected response to epidemics, involving multiple sectors and countries.

Alain Mérieux, president of Fondation Mérieux, called for a unified approach to viral threats in Brazzaville on November 3. He emphasized that addressing viruses, which disregard borders, requires open borders between African nations.

Mérieux stated that responses to epidemics must be interconnected and global, uniting countries, doctors, veterinarians, and environmental specialists under the "One Health" concept. Fondation Mérieux, active in 15 countries facing crises, aims to serve Congo's needs.

He noted the globalization of infection risks necessitates a joint response from foundations, laboratories like Institut Pasteur, and various countries, citing Ebola and monkeypox as lessons. Mérieux assured that the Congolese government is open to this collaboration, praising local expertise like Professor Francine Ntoumi.

Why it mattersThe president of Fondation Mérieux highlighted the need for a collaborative approach to combat viral threats, which affects public health in the Republic of Congo.

Culture · 2026-11-05

Congolese novel published after 50 years

Jean Malonga's 'Cœur d'Aryenne', written in 1953, is now available from Présence Africaine.

Jean Malonga's first novel from Brazzaville, 'Cœur d'Aryenne', has been published by Présence Africaine more than 50 years after it was written.

The novel, set in Mossaka in the Republic of Congo, tells the story of a young Congolese boy named Mambeke who saves the daughter of white colonists from drowning. The story explores themes of colonialism and racial ideology, drawing parallels to historical debates about the humanity of indigenous peoples.

The book was previously only accessible in specialized libraries. The narrative begins with Mambeke rescuing Solange, the daughter of colonist Roch Morax, from a caiman in the river.

Why it mattersThe publication of 'Cœur d'Aryenne' makes a significant work of Congolese literature accessible to a wider audience.

Congo validates preschool education standards guide

A five-day workshop in Brazzaville aims to refine a new guide for preschools to ensure child development.

A guide for preschool education standards is being validated in Brazzaville from November 2 to 6. The workshop, opened by Minister of Preschool, Primary, Secondary Education and Literacy Jean-Luc Mouthou, aims to improve the functioning of preschools. The document defines conditions for the well-being and development of young children, focusing on safe and hygienic learning environments.

Participants will review the guide's content over five days, offering feedback to create a unified and relevant document for the Congolese education system. The process involves examining four complementary areas to strengthen preschool regulations. Authorities intend to establish a better framework for early childhood education and promote supportive learning conditions from the earliest years.

The guide's application standards will define necessary conditions for child development and learning environments. This initiative seeks to ensure safety, hygiene, and protection for young children in preschool settings.

Why it mattersPreschool children in the Republic of Congo will benefit from improved learning environments and safety standards.
