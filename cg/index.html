Politics · 2026-08-05

Sassou Nguesso wins fifth term with 94.8% in Congo-Brazzaville vote

Republic of Congo's constitutional court confirmed President Denis Sassou Nguesso's re-election with 94.9% of the vote on a turnout of 65.9%, extending his rule to nearly 42 years. The March 15 election was boycotted by major opposition parties, with six lesser-known candidates on the ballot; one challenger's request to annul the vote was rejected by the court. The constitution bars Sassou Nguesso, 82, from running again when his new five-year term ends in 2031.

Why it mattersA confirmed fifth term locks in policy continuity for investors and creditors negotiating with Brazzaville, but keeps political-risk premiums elevated.

Business · 2026-08-05

Congo-Brazzaville seeks new IMF programme as debt tops 90% of GDP

The Republic of Congo has formally asked to open negotiations with the IMF for a new economic and financial programme, according to Africanews, with public debt exceeding 90% of GDP. Budget revenue remains highly dependent on oil output and exposed to swings in global commodity prices. Brazzaville said the move follows a January CEMAC summit at which regional leaders agreed to align economic policy with an IMF framework to defend the CFA franc's peg; an IMF mission is expected in Brazzaville in the coming weeks.

Why it mattersA fresh IMF deal would set the terms for future budget spending, debt relief and subsidy policy that directly affect fuel and food prices.

Business · 2026-08-05

Congo-Brazzaville finance minister projects 5.3% growth for 2026

Finance Minister Christian Yoka told the country's National Economic and Financial Committee that the economy is projected to grow 5.3% in 2026, driven by an oil-production rebound, rising liquefied-gas output and a 23% surge in private-sector credit, according to Brazzaville Insider. The banking sector's non-performing loan ratio fell to 13.5% from 16.5% in 2024, with provisioning coverage for bad loans rising to 65.1%. Independent verification of the government's figures remains limited given the country's statistical capacity.

Why it mattersFaster growth and a healthier loan book point to more available credit for local businesses, though the forecast still hinges on volatile oil prices.

Health · 2026-08-05

Cholera resurges on Brazzaville's Mbamou Island after nine-week lull

A cholera outbreak that had gone quiet resurfaced in February 2026 on Mbamou Island and in Congo-Oubangui department, with 196 suspected cases and eight deaths recorded since mid-February, according to a UNICEF flash update. A third hotspot with 14 alerts and five deaths was later identified in Impfondo, near the Central African Republic border. Children under 15 account for roughly a third of suspected cases.

Why it mattersRenewed cholera spread signals continuing gaps in clean water and sanitation access along the Congo River, a risk for river-trade and riverside communities.

Norwegian oil executives charged over $25 million in bribes to Congo president

Two executives of Hemla Africa Holding, a subsidiary of Norwegian energy firm PetroNor E&P, were charged on January 26 with paying $25 million in bribes to President Denis Sassou Nguesso and his family, according to Wikipedia's chronicle of 2026 events in the Republic of the Congo, citing Africanews reporting. The case adds to scrutiny of foreign oil companies' dealings with Congolese officials.

Why it mattersThe case puts foreign-investor compliance risk in Congo's oil sector under an international spotlight, with potential knock-on effects for licensing and partnerships.

Business · 2026-08-05

Russian-backed consortium advances Pointe-Noire fuel pipeline plan

The Republic of Congo approved a joint venture with Russian state-backed firms in June 2025 to build a petroleum-products pipeline between Pointe-Noire and Brazzaville under a build-own-operate-transfer deal, with Russia's ZNGS Prometey holding a 90% stake and Congo's national oil company SNPC the remaining 10%, according to industry tracker OG Analysis. Work on the project could begin in 2026, per Coface's country risk analysis, with construction expected to take about three years.

Why it mattersThe pipeline aims to ease chronic domestic fuel shortages by moving refined products inland, but ties Congo's energy infrastructure closely to Russian financing.

News · 2026-08-05

Congo, DR Congo advance Brazzaville-Kinshasa bridge tax accord

Officials from the Republic of Congo and the Democratic Republic of Congo validated a bilateral agreement in February 2026 setting a harmonized tax and customs regime for the proposed Brazzaville-Kinshasa road-rail bridge over the Congo River, according to Wikipedia's project entry. The agreement is meant to allow the stalled request-for-proposals process for the roughly $550 million, 1,575-metre span to relaunch.

Why it mattersA working tax framework removes a key hurdle to financing the bridge, which would be the first fixed link between the two capitals and a boost for cross-river trade.
