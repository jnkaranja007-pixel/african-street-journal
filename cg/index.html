Health · 2026-11-12

Republic of Congo records 261 Mpox cases and two deaths

Health authorities are stepping up awareness and surveillance as 11 departments and 13 districts are now affected.

Health authorities in the Republic of Congo are strengthening their response to a persistent Mpox outbreak, with 261 cases and two deaths recorded, a fatality rate of 0.76%. Men over 15 account for more than 59% of those infected.

At a meeting in Brazzaville on November 10, health district delegates and community relays discussed prevention, epidemiological surveillance and early detection of suspected cases. Congo-Oubangui, Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire are among the most affected areas, and the Lumumba district of Pointe-Noire concentrates a significant share of cases.

Mpox reappeared in the Congo in 2024 and again in 2026. Responders face shortages, with no vaccines available and medicines unavailable, community workers said. Two laboratories are now analysing samples from suspected cases.

Why it mattersMen over 15 make up most of the infected, and community workers say the shortage of vaccines and medicines is slowing the response.

Business · 2026-11-12

Republic of Congo oil sector drives growth

World Bank forecasts 4.4% growth in 2026, driven by oil production and prices.

The oil sector is regaining its prominence in the Republic of Congo's national growth, according to the World Bank's thirteenth economic and financial monitoring report. After years where non-oil sectors led economic activity, oil is once again becoming the primary driver.

Charlotte Ndaw Sako, the World Bank's representative in Congo, stated on November 10 in Brazzaville that increased oil production and rising international prices are expected to accelerate economic activity in 2026. This trend offers a temporary improvement in macroeconomic balances and a strategic opportunity for fiscal maneuverability, though the report cautions this recovery is fragile.

Despite the oil-driven boost, persistent challenges in non-oil sectors, including energy constraints and limited access to finance, hinder economic diversification. The report emphasizes that Congo's economic future hinges on consistent implementation of public finance management reforms and structural economic transformation, rather than solely on oil price fluctuations. Arrears remain a significant fiscal vulnerability, impacting public credibility, business cash flow, and investment.

Why it mattersThe Republic of Congo's economic growth in 2026 is projected at 4.4%, primarily due to the oil sector's recovery, which will improve public finances and external balances.

Sport · 2026-11-12

Congolese judokas win 17 medals in Cameroon

Junior and cadet judo teams secured seven gold, six silver, and four bronze medals at African competitions.

Young Congolese judokas returned to Brazzaville with 17 medals from competitions held in Yaoundé, Cameroon. The national junior and cadet teams earned seven gold, six silver, and four bronze medals, demonstrating strong performance on the continental stage.

The delegation's success included a second-place finish out of 14 nations at the Africa Cup on November 6, where nine medals were won. Chance Symphora Mankala Oyilantama took gold in the under 52 kg category, while Jhoey Joseph Mpenaya (-60 kg) and Rostand Itoua (-73 kg) also secured continental titles.

These achievements confirm the potential of the next generation of Congolese judo athletes. The results are an encouraging sign for the sport in the Republic of Congo, showcasing a new group capable of competing with top African judokas.

Why it mattersYoung athletes from the Republic of Congo have achieved success in international judo competitions, bringing home medals and showcasing national talent.

Sport · 2026-11-12

Prime Minister Makosso urges Red Devils to play with heart before Comoros

Anatole Collinet Makosso visited the squad at Alphonse-Massamba-Débat stadium on Tuesday 10 November and asked supporters to fill the stands for the home leg.

Prime Minister Anatole Collinet Makosso visited the Congo national football team, the Red Devils, at the Alphonse-Massamba-Débat stadium in Brazzaville on Tuesday 10 November. The squad is preparing for two matches against Comoros.

Speaking to the internationals, Makosso urged them to defend the national colours with heart, soul and spirit, and to show determination, commitment and patriotism. He also called on supporters to fill the stands at the stadium on 17 November to push the team towards victory.

The first leg is scheduled for 14 November in Moroni, with the return match on 17 November in Brazzaville.

Why it mattersSupporters in Brazzaville are asked to attend the 17 November home match, and the Red Devils must travel to Moroni for the first leg on 14 November.
