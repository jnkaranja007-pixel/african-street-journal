Ministers Testify on Makotimpoko Border Clash

Congolese Defense and Interior ministers briefed lawmakers on an incident involving RDC forces and territorial disputes.

The Defense and Security Commission of the National Assembly heard testimony on August 31 and September 1 from the ministers of Defense and Interior regarding events in Makotimpoko. The hearings aimed to clarify the circumstances of an attack on public force agents by elements of the Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo (RDC), the event's progression, and its casualties. Deputies also sought information on the legal status of disputed territories and border surveillance.

Defense Minister Raymond Zéphirin Mboulou presented colonial texts from 1908 to assert Congo's claim over territories like Mokoloko Island, acknowledging that some populations originate from the RDC. He described the Makotimpoko incidents as "regrettable" and condemned the broadcast of "humiliating" images, emphasizing the government's preference for diplomatic solutions and dialogue between the two nations.

Interior Minister Jean Olessongo Ondaye welcomed the parliamentary commission's initiative, stating it clarified misunderstandings and the role of public forces. He assured that the process of investigation and understanding the border incident continues, with the goal of preserving peace between the neighboring countries. The head of state is personally monitoring the situation, and contacts have been established with RDC authorities.

Why it mattersThe Congolese ministers' testimony informs lawmakers about a border incident and territorial dispute, potentially affecting national sovereignty and diplomatic relations.

Lékoumou leads Republic of Congo in 2026 BEPC exam results

The Lékoumou department achieved an 86.57% pass rate, while Congo-Oubangui recorded the lowest at 58.83% among fifteen regions.

The Lékoumou department has emerged as the top performer in the Republic of Congo's 2026 Brevet d’études du premier cycle (BEPC) examinations, with a pass rate of 86.57%. This places it significantly ahead of other regions, including Nkéni-Alima, which secured second place with 77.43%, and Sangha, rounding out the top three at 77.37%. The results offer a snapshot of educational performance across the country's fifteen administrative divisions.

At the lower end of the performance spectrum, the Congo-Oubangui department registered the lowest pass rate at 58.83%. Other departments also showed varying levels of success, with Brazzaville ranking seventh at 70.09% and Pointe-Noire, a major urban center, placing fourteenth with 63.65%. This disparity in results across departments could prompt discussions regarding learning conditions and educational resources.

The ranking highlights a considerable gap in academic achievement between the leading and lagging regions. Lékoumou's performance, with nearly nine out of ten candidates passing, suggests a strong educational framework within the department. This data serves as a key indicator for evaluating territorial educational outcomes and may inform future strategies for pedagogical support and resource allocation.

Why it mattersStudents in the Lékoumou department are achieving higher pass rates in the BEPC exams, while those in Congo-Oubangui face greater challenges.
