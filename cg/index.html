Six Men Tried for Illegal Mining in Conkouati-Douli National Park

Accused of illicit mineral extraction within a protected area, six men appeared before the Pointe-Noire tribunal on August 28.

Six men faced trial on August 28 at the Pointe-Noire High Court for alleged illegal mineral exploitation within the Conkouati-Douli National Park in the Kouilou department. The proceedings highlight ongoing threats to Congo's protected natural spaces. The accused, held in provisional detention, were presented with charges of conducting mining activities outside legal frameworks inside the park.

According to evidence presented, these alleged activities could lead to ecosystem degradation, including habitat destruction and biodiversity disruption. The Conkouati-Douli National Park is a significant protected area in Congo, vital for preserving Atlantic facade ecosystems and housing diverse plant and animal species.

This court appearance occurs amid intensified efforts to combat illegal activities within protected zones. Environmental and wildlife authorities emphasize that controlling economic activities and enforcing legal sanctions are crucial for preserving these territories.

Why it mattersSix men are accused of illegal mining activities within a national park, potentially harming ecosystems and biodiversity.

Experts Validate Public Procurement Control Manual

A new reference tool aims to standardize oversight practices and enhance the effectiveness of public procurement processes.

Experts convened on August 29 to review and approve a manual designed to guide public procurement control actors. The tool is intended to standardize practices and improve the efficiency of oversight. This initiative seeks to harmonize procedures and elevate the quality of control in public procurement operations.

The manual provides a common framework for stakeholders, detailing specific practices and procedures for control exercises. Its development addresses the need for a unified tool to foster a clearer understanding of public purchasing requirements. The meeting also facilitated discussions on the manual's implementation strategies.

The objective is to establish this manual as a practical, daily reference for professionals responsible for ensuring the regularity and compliance of public procurement activities. The validation marks a significant step in refining the mechanisms for overseeing these crucial public contracts.

Why it mattersThe manual standardizes public procurement control, which affects businesses and investors by clarifying regulations and improving the efficiency of government contracts.

Mayor Meets Merchants Over Taxes and New Market

The new administrator-mayor of Lumumba visited Ndji-Ndji market to address vendor concerns about taxes and the upcoming central market.

The administrator-mayor of Lumumba, Pointe-Noire's first district, visited the Ndji-Ndji market and surrounding commercial areas to engage directly with local traders. Accompanied by his teams, the mayor's visit focused on listening to the vendors' concerns and clarifying issues related to their business activities, particularly tax payments and regulatory compliance.

During the discussions, the administrator-mayor assured vendors that taxes must remain within the current legal framework. He urged them to fulfill their obligations while acknowledging the difficulties they might face. The conversation also touched upon the highly anticipated new central market of Pointe-Noire, which is expected to improve sales and purchasing conditions and enhance commercial organization.

Vendors expressed palpable impatience regarding the new market's opening, seeking clarity on the next steps and the procedures for their eventual installation. The mayor's direct engagement aims to foster a closer relationship with the business community and ensure a smoother transition for commercial activities in the district.

Why it mattersLocal merchants are directly affected by discussions on tax compliance and the operational details of a new central market.
