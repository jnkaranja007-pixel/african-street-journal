Congo resumes government evaluation seminars after two-year pause

The eighth seminar, opened in Brazzaville on 13 November by Prime Minister Anatole Collinet Makosso, reviews three planning documents that set priorities to 2031 and beyond.

After a pause of more than two years, the Congolese government has resumed its evaluation seminars. The eighth seminar opened on 13 November at the Palais des congrès in Brazzaville, with Prime Minister Anatole Collinet Makosso presiding. It is the first seminar of the new five-year term.

The seminar is examining three documents: an evaluation of the 2022-2026 National Development Plan (PND), the draft Congo-Vision 2063 strategic document, and the draft PND for 2027-2031. The prime minister said the evaluation must identify progress as well as difficulties, shortfalls and the factors that slowed results, not serve only as a report.

The 2022-2026 plan was adopted by law no. 3-2022 of 14 January 2022 and aimed at a strong, diversified and resilient economy. During its implementation an acceleration programme for 2024-2026 was adopted to speed up delivery, including youth aspirations.

Why it mattersMinistries and public bodies will use the outcome of these reviews to set the national development priorities for 2027 to 2031.

Congo seeks Francophonie backing for forest decade and UN Security Council seat

Brazzaville asked member states at a ministerial meeting in Cambodia to support a UN forestry decade, a cultural heritage bid and a 2030-2031 Security Council place.

At the 48th Ministerial Conference of the Francophonie (CMF), held on November 14 in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, the Republic of Congo presented three priorities it wants member states to back. Foreign Minister Constant Serge Bounda, who led the Congolese delegation, asked that the UN Decade on Afforestation and Reforestation, covering 2027 to 2036, be taken up as a Francophone initiative.

Brazzaville also sought support for its candidacy of the Kébé-Kébé to be inscribed on the UNESCO list of intangible cultural heritage. The country is running for a non-permanent seat on the UN Security Council for 2030-2031, citing its diplomatic experience in mediation and negotiated political solutions to crises.

The ministerial session finalised strategic documents for heads of state and government, who met on November 15 and 16 in Phnom Penh. The meeting was also due to address the choice of the organisation's next secretary-general. The conference theme was "Reinvesting in peace for shared prosperity and lasting stability".

Sport · 2026-11-17

Congo must beat Comoros at home to keep CAN 2027 hopes alive

The Red Devils drew 0-0 in Moroni and now host the Comoros in Brazzaville on Tuesday, needing a win to lift their total to five points.

The Republic of Congo's national football team, the Red Devils, drew 0-0 with the Comoros in Moroni on Friday, 13 November, in the third round of the Africa Cup of Nations (CAN) 2027 qualifiers. The result leaves Congo bottom of Group G with two points from three matches, behind Cameroon on 7, the Comoros on 4 and Namibia on 3.

Coach Claude Le Roy's side held firm at the back, with goalkeeper Melvin Zinga repelling several attempts. In the 85th minute, the crossbar saved Congo from a shot by Idris Mohamed. The attack, however, failed to convert its chances, and the team missed chances in the final moment.

The return match is scheduled for Tuesday, 17 November, at the Alphonse-Massamba-Débat stadium in Brazzaville. Congo must win to reach five points and overtake the Comoros in the table. Before the double fixture, Le Roy had said he wanted at least four points from the six available.
