Congo and Russia pledge to speed up military cooperation

The two governments agreed in Brazzaville to deepen military and technical ties, with Moscow floating on-site advisers for Congolese forces.

The Republic of Congo and Russia reaffirmed on November 13 in Brazzaville their intention to accelerate military and technical cooperation. The position was set out after talks between Russian Ambassador Dimitriy Korepanov and Congo's Minister of National Defence, Raymond Zéphirin Mboulou.

Ambassador Korepanov said the two countries have long-standing relations, pointing to the friendship between their presidents, and that Russia had helped Congo develop since independence. General Vladislav Kharchenko, Russia's representative to the Ministry of Defence, said the two sides had set out paths for future cooperation and hoped more agreements would follow.

Kharchenko said Russia was studying the idea of sending military advisers to help Congolese armed forces raise their capacity on the ground. He suggested this could cost less than sending trainees to Russia.

Diabetes affects 7% of Brazzaville residents

The Republic of Congo's Health Minister revealed the statistic during World Diabetes Day.

Seven percent of Brazzaville residents have diabetes, according to a recent survey revealed by the Republic of Congo's Minister of Health and Population, Professor Jean Rosaire Ibara. He stated that diabetes is a significant public health challenge, affecting nearly 589 million people globally and 24 million adults in Africa, many of whom are unaware of their condition.

The minister identified alcohol and tobacco consumption as key risk factors, leading to late diagnoses and severe complications like blindness, amputations, cardiovascular diseases, and kidney failure. To combat this, the ministry is enhancing early screening through community campaigns and free testing days, and integrating non-communicable disease management into primary healthcare.

The government plans to incorporate blood sugar and blood pressure tests into routine check-ups for adults over 40 and pregnant women, improve access to insulin and other diabetes medications, and train community health workers. The minister urged collective action from parents, educators, health professionals, and the private sector to address the disease.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Health will integrate blood sugar tests into routine adult check-ups and improve access to diabetes medication.

Women parliamentarians seek Isidore Mvouba's support for parity

A delegation from the Francophonie Parliamentary Assembly's women's network met with the National Assembly president in Brazzaville to discuss increasing female representation.

The Network of Women Parliamentarians of the Francophonie Parliamentary Assembly (APF) met with Isidore Mvouba, president of the National Assembly, on November 11 in Brazzaville to discuss gender parity in politics. The meeting aimed to advance the representation of women in the Republic of Congo's parliament, which remains below legal objectives.

The delegation, in the country for a two-day seminar on women's leadership and local governance, presented conclusions from their work to Mvouba. Discussions focused on legislative measures supporting women, mechanisms for promoting their presence in decision-making bodies, and future prospects for increasing their parliamentary representation.

Laure Baflan Donwahi, a member of the APF women's network steering committee, stated that exchanges covered legislative frameworks for women, parity, and future parliamentary perspectives. The Republic of Congo's law already enshrines gender parity.

Why it mattersWomen parliamentarians are seeking support from the National Assembly president to increase female representation in parliament, a goal enshrined in law.
