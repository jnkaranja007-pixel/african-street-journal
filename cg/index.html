Congo Senate approves four oil contract amendments

Senators in Brazzaville adopted four draft laws to adjust technical mechanisms related to "high price" clauses in oil production sharing contracts.

The Congolese Senate, led by President Pierre Ngolo, approved four draft laws on Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, 2026, during its third extraordinary session in Brazzaville. These laws concern amendments to oil production sharing contracts for the Nanga II bis, Nanga IV, Nanga V, and Marine XXIX-A permits.

The first two amendments, for Nanga II bis and Nanga IV, were signed on November twenty-third, 2023, between the Republic of Congo, the National Oil Company of Congo (SNPC), and China's Dingheng Mining Co. Ltd. The third amendment covers the Nanga V permit, and the fourth is for Marine XXIX-A, under a contract signed on April twenty-fifth, 2025, with Oriental Energy SAU.

These changes aim to adapt contractual provisions to current sector realities and encourage continued investment. The objective is to provide more visibility to operators and create favorable conditions for ongoing oil exploration, exploitation, and production activities, addressing economic, financial, industrial, budgetary, and social implications.

Why it mattersOil operators and the Congolese state are affected by these amendments, which aim to provide more visibility and create favorable conditions for continued oil activities and investments.

Belgium offers waste management expertise to Republic of Congo

Belgium's ambassador to the Republic of Congo, Emmanuelle Plissart De Foy, met with Minister Juste Désiré Mondelé to discuss cooperation in urban sanitation and development.

Belgium has expressed its readiness to share its expertise in waste management and urban development with the Republic of Congo. Ambassador Emmanuelle Plissart De Foy conveyed her country's availability to assist during a meeting on August twenty-fifth in Brazzaville with Juste Désiré Mondelé, the Minister of Urban Sanitation, Local Development, and Road Maintenance.

The discussions focused on strengthening cooperation, particularly in sanitation, territorial planning, and urban development. A significant part of the talks centered on waste management and valorization, including the concept of a circular economy to recover and reuse waste from both land and aquatic environments like rivers and the sea.

While no specific projects were finalized, both parties identified several areas for enhanced collaboration. This partnership aims to improve waste management practices and support Congolese authorities in their efforts to create cleaner and better-planned cities, according to the Belgian diplomat.

Why it mattersThis initiative could lead to new business opportunities and improved urban environments for residents in the Republic of Congo.

Diables Rouges Coach Details CAN Qualifiers Preparation

Claude le Roy, the national team coach, outlined the preparation strategy for the upcoming Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers against Namibia and Cameroon.

The Republic of Congo's national football team, the Diables Rouges, has begun preparations for their initial Africa Cup of Nations (CAN) qualifying matches. Coach Claude le Roy held a press conference on August twenty-sixth at the Congolese Football Federation (Fécofoot) headquarters to discuss the team's strategy.

The first training camp focuses on evaluating local players to identify those who could join the national squad for international competitions. Le Roy emphasized that these early sessions are crucial for building team cohesion, refining tactical options, and establishing automatisms before the final team selection.

The Diables Rouges are scheduled to play Namibia and then Cameroon in late September and early October. These matches are considered vital for securing early points in the qualification campaign, and the technical staff has several weeks to finalize their choices for a competitive team.

Why it mattersFootball fans and supporters of the national team are directly affected as the coach's plans will determine the team's performance in crucial international qualifiers.

AGL Congo and Congo Terminal celebrate independence, highlight logistics role

Africa Global Logistics Congo and Congo Terminal marked the 66th anniversary of Congo's independence, emphasizing the role of logistics in national development.

Africa Global Logistics (AGL) Congo and Congo Terminal celebrated the 66th anniversary of Congolese independence on August 15, 2026, gathering employees to honor the nation and the workforce driving the group. The event highlighted the local integration and team spirit uniting staff, with logistics activities contributing significantly to Congo's economic growth.

Festivities began with speeches from Cynthia Ouattara, Country Administrative and Financial Director for AGL Congo, and Kèhindé Salami, Technical Director for Congo Terminal. They underscored values such as humility, solidarity, agility, and a passion for enterprise, which foster a sense of belonging among employees. Ouattara stated that Independence Day unites them around history, identity, and a shared commitment to making logistics a development engine for Congo.

Congo Terminal used the celebration to stress the importance of teamwork and proximity among staff during a period of significant port transformation. Salami noted that the event strengthens team cohesion for future challenges, adding that the upcoming operation of the East pier will accelerate port volume growth, boosting economic development across the sub-region.

Why it mattersThis event affects employees of AGL Congo and Congo Terminal by reinforcing their sense of belonging and highlighting their collective contribution to the country's economic development through logistics.

Congo Minister Inspects LMC Mine Development in Kouilou

The Minister of Mining Industries and Geology, Urbain Fiacre Opo, visited the Luyuan des Mines Congo (LMC) site in Mpili to assess progress on the Mboukoumassi exploitation permit.

Minister Urbain Fiacre Opo recently conducted a field visit to the LMC site in Mpili, Kouilou department, to review the advancement of work related to the Mboukoumassi exploitation permit. The Chinese company, Luyuan des Mines Congo (LMC), operates on a surface area of 242 km² under this permit.

The visit aimed to monitor mining activities and ensure compliance with the commitments tied to the exploitation permit. The government seeks to ensure that the development of the country's mineral resources adheres to current regulations while generating economic and social benefits for local populations.

The Mboukoumassi project's development aligns with Congo's goal to increase the mining sector's contribution to economic diversification. Regular oversight of ongoing projects is seen as a way to support investors and confirm that operations meet sector requirements.

Why it mattersLocal populations near Mpili and Pointe-Noire are affected by the government's efforts to ensure economic and social benefits from the mining project.
