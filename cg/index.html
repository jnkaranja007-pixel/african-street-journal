Health · 2026-10-28

Pharmacists council challenges Propharma drug license

The National Order of Pharmacists says the health ministry ignored its negative assessment when granting Propharma wholesale drug sales approval.

The National Order of Pharmacists (CNOP) in the Republic of Congo is contesting the health ministry's decision to grant Propharma a license for wholesale pharmaceutical sales. CNOP President Jagger Mpanzo stated on October 27 in Brazzaville that the procedure violated existing laws governing pharmacists. He demanded a review of the authorization's legality, citing irregularities in the process.

Mpanzo explained that the ministry approved Propharma despite the CNOP issuing a prior, motivated refusal. He indicated that this oversight raises questions about the approval's regularity and could negatively impact public health. The CNOP had previously engaged with the health ministry and the general inspectorate of health between March 30 and July 1, 2026, without a satisfactory resolution.

The CNOP, established on April 29, 1992, regulates the pharmaceutical profession in Congo. Mpanzo indicated that the organization is now considering legal action to challenge the decision. The council reaffirmed its commitment to dialogue with the ministry and other state institutions to uphold the law and clarify institutional responsibilities.

Why it mattersCitizens' health could be negatively impacted by the irregular approval of a company for wholesale drug sales.

Education · 2026-10-28

Congo university talks suspended

Government and UMNG union negotiators failed to agree on salary arrears payments after over a week of talks.

Negotiations between the Republic of Congo government and the University Marien-Ngouabi (UMNG) inter-union college were suspended on October 19 in Brazzaville.

The discussions, which lasted over a week, did not yield a compromise on the demands of teaching and administrative staff, particularly concerning outstanding salary payments and a clear schedule for full arrears settlement.

Both sides expressed a desire to continue dialogue, but no date has been set for the resumption of talks, leaving the resolution of staff claims uncertain.

Business · 2026-10-28

Congo unions demand E2C general director's dismissal

Workers cite power outages, financial irregularities, and poor maintenance at the state electricity company.

Trade unions in the Republic of Congo are demanding the immediate dismissal of Jean-Bruno Danga Adou, the general director of Énergie électrique du Congo (E2C). Representatives of the workers issued a statement on October 23 in Brazzaville, denouncing alleged managerial and technical failures within the public company.

The unions highlighted deteriorating electricity supply, particularly in Brazzaville, leading to spoiled food, damaged appliances, and business slowdowns. They rejected blame on field agents, stating a lack of necessary equipment and alleged abandonment of preventive maintenance on production and distribution networks. The unions also cited alleged fund misappropriation and conflicts of interest in contract awards.

A general assembly is scheduled for November 18 at the company's Brazzaville headquarters to formalize the demand for the director general and his team to step down. The unions also reported intimidations against some of their members.

Why it mattersCitizens in Brazzaville are experiencing power outages affecting households and businesses.
