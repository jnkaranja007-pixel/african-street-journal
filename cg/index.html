Congo launches 1.28 billion FCFA Akou-Mbon road rehabilitation

The six-month project covers 69 km of road in the Plateaux department, linking Mbon district to larger markets and services.

The Congolese government is investing 1.28 billion FCFA to rehabilitate the 69 km Akou-Mbon road in the Plateaux department. Minister of Urban Sanitation, Local Development and Road Maintenance Juste Désiré Mondelé launched the works on November 7, with a planned duration of six months. The project aims to ease travel for residents of Mbon, who have faced circulation difficulties for several decades.

Francis Landry Ngueloundou, director general of road maintenance, said the works will make it easier to reach markets, schools and health facilities, and should reduce transport costs and travel times. Producers hope to move foufou, potatoes and safous to large urban centres more easily, since some farm produce currently rots for lack of suitable transport.

Residents used the launch ceremony to raise concerns about the lack of drinking water and electricity. Mondelé pledged to pass these concerns to the competent authorities and assured that the roadworks will be carried out on schedule and to standard.

Why it mattersFarmers in Mbon district who grow foufou, potatoes and safous now face spoilage because of the poor road, and the rehabilitation is meant to get their goods to urban markets.

Congolese judokas win 17 medals in Cameroon

Junior and cadet judo teams secured seven gold, six silver, and four bronze medals at African competitions.

Young judokas from the Republic of Congo returned to Brazzaville with 17 medals from competitions held in Yaoundé, Cameroon. The national junior and cadet teams earned seven gold, six silver, and four bronze medals. This performance confirms the potential of Congo's next generation of judokas on the continental stage.

The delegation achieved a successful outing, particularly at the Africa Cup on November 6, where 10 athletes secured nine medals, including three gold, five silver, and one bronze. This placed Congo second among 14 participating nations. Chance Symphora Mankala Oyilantama won gold in the under-52kg category, while Jhoey Joseph Mpenaya (-60kg) and Rostand Itoua (-73kg) also took continental titles.

These results are an encouraging sign for Congolese judo, demonstrating the ability of its new generation to compete with top African judokas. The team's success highlights their capabilities in both women's and men's categories.

Why it mattersYoung Congolese judokas have demonstrated their competitive ability by winning multiple medals at continental competitions.

Business · 2026-11-11

PRRU informs economic actors on market conditions

The urban resilience project aims to prepare businesses for contract bidding processes.

The coordinator of the Urban Resilience Reinforcement Project (PRRU), Guy Fulgence Mbengué, stated on November 10 in Brazzaville that businesses and economic operators need to understand the project's market procedures. This aims to prevent confusion when they prepare their bids.

Mbengué explained that the meeting with economic actors was to mobilize potential service providers and entrepreneurs to provide practical information on market passation. The PRRU, supported by the World Bank and the government, will focus on activities such as managing erosion, urban roads, and constructing socio-economic infrastructure like markets and schools.

The PRRU, which will operate in Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire, was launched on November 9. It intends to reduce erosion and flood risks and improve access to sustainable, climate-resilient infrastructure in specific districts of both cities.

Why it mattersEconomic actors in Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire will benefit from clearer procedures for bidding on infrastructure projects.

Climate · 2026-11-11

Congo president and UN General Assembly chief press for forest action before COP31

Denis Sassou-N’Guesso and Khalilur Rahman called for concrete measures to protect forest ecosystems ahead of the Antalya climate conference.

President Denis Sassou-N’Guesso received Khalilur Rahman, president of the 81st session of the UN General Assembly and Bangladesh's foreign minister, at the People's Palace in Brazzaville on Sunday, November 8, 2026. Rahman was leading a delegation of UN diplomats visiting the country.

The two men focused on environmental issues, particularly the preservation of forest ecosystems and the urgency of climate action. Their talks came as the effects of climate change intensify, and they stressed the need to turn international commitments into concrete measures rather than statements of intent.

The meeting took place a few days before the 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31), scheduled for Antalya, Turkey. The forests of the Congo Basin, described in the report as one of the world's main carbon reservoirs, were a central part of the discussion.

Why it mattersForests of the Congo Basin, which store carbon, are a focus of the talks that will shape the country's position at COP31 in Antalya.

Education · 2026-11-11

MEEC council opposes student body suspension

The Council of Elders of the Movement of Students and Pupils of Congo rejects the interim executive's suspension by the higher education ministry.

Brazzaville: The Council of Elders of the Movement of Students and Pupils of Congo (MEEC) opposed on November 9 the suspension of the national executive bureau's activities. The Ministry of Higher Education had decided to suspend the bureau's operations at Marien-Ngouabi University.

The Council of Elders stated it received with dismay the November 4 communiqué ordering the suspension, calling the decision arbitrary and a violation of fundamental freedoms. The council argued that no law grants the minister power to suspend student associations, asserting only judicial authorities can do so.

The MEEC Council of Elders has rejected the suspension as void and demanded its immediate withdrawal. They have contacted the Senate president, National Assembly president, and Prime Minister. The council also called for student mobilization and announced the MEEC national council will convene after teachers' strike ends.

Why it mattersStudents and pupils in the Republic of Congo face disruption to their association's activities and representation.
