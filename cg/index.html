Congo suspends four port officials over grave misconduct

The director general of the Port of Pointe-Noire and three other senior managers were suspended on November 2, 2026.

The director general of the Port of Pointe-Noire, Séraphin Bhalat, and three other senior officials were suspended from their duties on November 2, 2026, for "grave misconduct." The suspension order was issued by the Minister of Transport, Josué Rodrigue Ngouonimba. Also suspended are Émile Ngandzien, director of finance and accounting; Léopold Vincent Mopita, director of control and budget; and Edmond Jean de Dieu Otsoa, permanent secretary of the market procurement unit.

The suspensions follow questions raised by emails attributed to the officials regarding a financial operation under Bhalat's direction. The minister reportedly sought explanations for a transfer to a bank account not belonging to the company that executed the work. The exact circumstances, amount, and justifications for this operation remain unclear. Serges César Bouya, the deputy director general, has been appointed to act as interim director general.

The Port of Pointe-Noire is a strategic infrastructure for the Republic of Congo's trade. The simultaneous suspension of key financial and administrative managers raises governance and transparency concerns. This incident occurs as the port handles essential imports and exports for the country and the wider sub-region.

Why it mattersFour senior officials at the Port of Pointe-Noire, a key economic infrastructure, have been suspended due to alleged grave misconduct and financial irregularities.

Congo Terminal installs 65 dock fenders at Môle Est

The rubber and steel buffers protect ships from impact during docking operations at Pointe-Noire port.

Congo Terminal has begun installing 65 dock fenders along a 750-meter stretch of the Môle Est in Pointe-Noire. The first phase will see 35 fenders installed by year-end on 400 meters of the quay, with the remaining 30 to be fitted on the next 350 meters in early 2027.

These high-resistance rubber and steel fenders act as giant shock absorbers, safeguarding vessels from collision damage upon docking. Jérôme MOUILLE, QHSE Manager for the Môle Est Project at Congo Terminal, stated that these fenders are the primary protection for every ship calling at the quay, emphasizing that operational safety is the company's standard.

This installation is a concrete step for the Môle Est project, aimed at enhancing Congo Terminal's capacity and supporting the growth of port activities in Pointe-Noire and the wider sub-region. Congo Terminal, a subsidiary of Africa Global Logistics, is the exclusive operator for container and roll-on/roll-off vessel handling at the port.

Why it mattersThe installation of dock fenders at Pointe-Noire port ensures safer operations for ships and supports the development of port activities.

Jean Malonga's first novel published after 50 years

The 1953 novel "Cœur d'Aryenne" by Jean Malonga is now available from Présence Africaine.

After more than 50 years, Jean Malonga's first novel, "Cœur d'Aryenne," has been published by Présence Africaine. The novel, which deals with colonial-era racial ideology and master-slave dynamics, was previously only accessible in specialized libraries. The story is set in Mossaka, in the Centre-East of Republic of Congo.

The narrative begins with a 12-year-old Congolese boy named Mambeke saving Solange, the daughter of white colonists Roch Morax and Marie-Rose, from drowning in Mossaka. Mambeke's father, Yoka, works as a cook for the colonist family. This rescue is framed as an indelible debt for Roch Morax, highlighting the master-slave relationship.

The novel's title, "Cœur d'Aryenne," refers to the colonialist with a heart of stone, a metaphor for the harsh authority of the era. The publication brings to light a work that was confined to a review for decades, echoing historical debates about humanity and subjugation, such as the 16th-century Valladolid controversy.

Why it mattersThe publication of "Cœur d'Aryenne" makes a significant work of Congolese literature, previously difficult to access, available to readers.

Republic of Congo seeks UN Security Council seat

President Sassou N'Guesso asked African leaders to back the country's bid for a non-permanent seat on the UN Security Council for 2030-2031.

President Denis Sassou N’Guesso has asked fellow African leaders to support the Republic of Congo's bid for a non-permanent seat on the United Nations Security Council for the 2030-2031 term. He made this appeal during the third Russia-Africa summit in Moscow on October 28 and 29.

Sassou N’Guesso stated that African support would strengthen the continent's voice within the UN body responsible for international peace and security. He highlighted global challenges such as geopolitical tensions, terrorism, climate change, and armed conflicts as reasons for increased African representation.

The Congolese president emphasized his country's commitment to dialogue, mediation, and peaceful dispute resolution as its diplomatic stance for the Security Council seat. Brazzaville aims to leverage this approach if successful in its bid.

Congo studies energy for special economic zones

A new study aims to boost electricity supply to 805 MW for industrial zones and companies.

Minister Michel Djombo announced in Brazzaville on November 3 that a study on powering special economic zones (ZES) will identify solutions to increase electricity supply. The goal is to reach an estimated 805 MW capacity for ZES and industrial firms to ensure efficient operation.

Djombo stated that industries need energy for production, and many currently struggle to operate. The study will assess energy needs for ZES and support industries like forestry companies near these zones. He noted that energy constraints deter investors from developing projects in the ZES.

The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) is providing technical support for the study, which is part of a rural electrification program. UNDP representative Adama-Dian Barry pledged to help mobilize funding for the project's implementation and expressed hope for concrete outcomes to transform the industrial sector.

Why it mattersIndustrial companies and potential investors in the Republic of Congo face energy supply challenges that hinder production and project development.
