Jean Malonga's first novel published after 50 years

The 1953 novel "Cœur d'Aryenne" by Jean Malonga is now available from Présence Africaine.

Source: Congopage · 2026-11-04 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

After more than 50 years, Jean Malonga's first novel, "Cœur d'Aryenne," has been published by Présence Africaine. The novel, which deals with colonial-era racial ideology and master-slave dynamics, was previously only accessible in specialized libraries. The story is set in Mossaka, in the Centre-East of Republic of Congo.

The narrative begins with a 12-year-old Congolese boy named Mambeke saving Solange, the daughter of white colonists Roch Morax and Marie-Rose, from drowning in Mossaka. Mambeke's father, Yoka, works as a cook for the colonist family. This rescue is framed as an indelible debt for Roch Morax, highlighting the master-slave relationship.

The novel's title, "Cœur d'Aryenne," refers to the colonialist with a heart of stone, a metaphor for the harsh authority of the era. The publication brings to light a work that was confined to a review for decades, echoing historical debates about humanity and subjugation, such as the 16th-century Valladolid controversy.

Why it mattersThe publication of "Cœur d'Aryenne" makes a significant work of Congolese literature, previously difficult to access, available to readers.
