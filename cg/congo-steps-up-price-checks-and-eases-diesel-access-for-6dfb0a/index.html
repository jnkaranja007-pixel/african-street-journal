Congo steps up price checks and eases diesel access for essential-goods suppliers

Inspections from 25 October to 14 November found breaches of approved prices as the government works to restore a reference price for butane gas.

Source: Journal de Brazza · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

The Republic of Congo’s government has stepped up price surveillance and eased access to diesel for operators supplying essential goods, following inspections that found pricing irregularities. The measures were discussed at a cabinet meeting, alongside anti-corruption work and plans to modernise public administration.

Trade Minister Jacqueline Lydia Mikolo said checks from 25 October to 14 November found sellers charging above approved prices and some imported goods without approved prices. The government is revising the order governing butane-gas prices to restore a reference tariff that operators must follow.

The cabinet called for continued monitoring of prices, quality and availability, and action against speculation and the withholding of goods. The government also discussed preparations for Congo’s next anti-corruption evaluation, scheduled for 2027.

Why it mattersEssential-goods suppliers have been given easier access to diesel, and operators face a revised butane-gas reference tariff.
