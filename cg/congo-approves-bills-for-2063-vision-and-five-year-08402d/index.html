Congo approves bills for 2063 vision and five-year development plan

The proposals set out six development pillars and public investment plans, and now go to Parliament for examination and adoption.

Source: Journal de Brazza · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

The Republic of Congo’s government approved bills for a long-term development vision through 2063 and a five-year national plan for 2027-2031 at a cabinet meeting. The proposals will be sent to Parliament for examination and adoption.

Economy and Planning Minister Ludovic Ngatsé presented the bills. The vision centres on developing transport, logistics and services, alongside processing raw materials locally. Its six pillars include energy, digital services, infrastructure, industry, mining, hydrocarbons and agriculture. The 2027-2031 plan includes governance, human capital, energy, infrastructure, resource development and social protection, supported by a public investment programme and monitoring system.

The cabinet also approved an investment charter bill to improve legal protections for investors and credit access for small and medium-sized businesses. It adopted a decree to update national accounts using the 2008 system, with the aim of better measuring informal activity, natural resources and digital services.

Why it mattersParliament must examine and decide whether to adopt the two development bills, which set out the first five-year plan under the vision through 2063.
