President's new government tests his push to expand presidential power

A Tela Non commentary questions whether the president will apply the doctrine behind his January 2025 dismissal of Patrice Trovoada's government.

Source: Tela Non · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

A Tela Non commentary says São Tomé and Príncipe's president is now forming a government backed in parliament by the Movement for Change-Party of Social Progress (MCI-PS) and the Party of Unity and Nation (PUN), after dismissing Patrice Trovoada's government in January 2025. The author argues the president's actions have suggested he wants a role in shaping and influencing government policy, beyond serving as an arbiter of state institutions.

The president cited what he called the government's inability to solve economic and social problems, institutional disloyalty by Trovoada, the prime minister's lengthy absences abroad and public dissatisfaction. The commentary says those reasons helped start a drive to "presidentialise" the political system, but questions whether the president will follow that approach in forming the new government.

The author also warns that expanding presidential influence could weaken parliament's oversight role, while arguing that the president's earlier move had appeared motivated by a desire to address poverty and poor governance.

Why it mattersThe president and the new government must now show whether his influence over government formation and policy will continue, with MCI-PS and PUN providing parliamentary support.
