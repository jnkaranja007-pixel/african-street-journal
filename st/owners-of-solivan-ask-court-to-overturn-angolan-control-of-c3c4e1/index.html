Owners of SOLIVAN ask court to overturn Angolan control of Rosema brewery

The request was filed on 24 November, days after the new government took office, reopening a 20-year dispute over the brewery's management.

Source: Tela Non · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

The owners of SOLIVAN, a São Tomé and Príncipe company that has fought for control of the Rosema beer business for 20 years, asked the Constitutional Court on 24 November to cancel the ruling that returned the factory's possession, management and control to RIDUX, an Angolan private company. The filing came just after the new government took office.

SOLIVAN's lawyer confirmed to the outlet Téla Nón that the request had been submitted and said the company would make a public statement on the case in the coming period. The brewery had earlier been managed by RIDUX until parliament's majority, led by the ADI party, and the MCI-PS-PUN coalition used an interpretive law in 2023 to restore it to SOLIVAN's control.

Parliament later repealed that interpretive law after the ADI split in 2024, and newly appointed Constitutional Court judges overturned the ruling that had favoured SOLIVAN, returning the brewery to RIDUX. The report says the two companies have contested control of Rosema for 20 years.

Why it mattersThe Constitutional Court must now consider SOLIVAN's request to reverse the ruling that placed the brewery under RIDUX's control.
