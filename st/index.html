President Demands Modern, Principled National Police Force

Carlos Vila Nova calls for a police force that is firm, balanced, and above political disputes, emphasizing human capital for modernization.

President Carlos Vila Nova stated that São Tomé and Príncipe requires a modern national police force that remains faithful to republican principles. Speaking at the 51st anniversary of the National Police, he stressed the need for a police presence that is both firm and balanced, close to citizens yet respected, and prepared for current and future challenges.

The President highlighted that authority for the police is a duty, not a privilege, and advocated for investment in the national police, particularly in its human capital. He described the police as the most visible representation of the state, acting as first responders to accidents and interveners in conflicts, and protectors of families.

Vila Nova commended the police's professionalism during the July 19 presidential elections and urged for similar performance in the upcoming September 27 legislative, municipal, and regional elections. He also noted the incorporation of 185 new agents, bringing the total to approximately 900, and called for discipline, professionalism, courage, balance, and respect for the law from all officers.

Why it mattersThe President's call for a modern, apolitical police force directly impacts citizens by shaping their interactions with state authority and law enforcement.

Central Bank Issues 50 Dobras Note for National Identity

A commemorative 50 Dobras banknote, not for circulation, was released to mark 50 years of independence and 34 years of the Central Bank.

Sao Tome and Principe's Central Bank has officially presented a commemorative 50 Dobras banknote, intended to celebrate the nation's 50th anniversary of independence, which occurred last year. The release was delayed due to prevailing circumstances. Central Bank Governor Agostinho Fernandes stated the note is a collector's item for historical preservation, not for daily transactions or as a replacement for existing currency.

Prime Minister Américo Ramos emphasized the symbolic value of currency, asserting that a nation's money represents its identity. He highlighted that the Dobra remains a visible symbol of sovereignty and that this new note strengthens the connection between currency, memory, and national sovereignty, prompting reflection on the nation's past and future.

The issuance of the commemorative note, symbolizing history, identity, trust, and the future, coincides with the 34th anniversary of the Central Bank's institutionalization. This event follows other recent initiatives by the Central Bank, including programs aimed at economic growth and a realistic assessment of the country's economic performance.

Why it mattersThe release of a commemorative 50 Dobras note reinforces national identity and historical memory for citizens of Sao Tome and Principe.

Sao Tome and Principe Government Raises Minimum Pension by 8 Euros

The minimum pension in Sao Tome and Principe will increase from 1,200 to 1,400 dobras, benefiting approximately 6,500 elderly citizens starting in September.

The Sao Tome and Principe government will raise the minimum pension by nearly 8 euros, from 1,200 to 1,400 dobras, effective September. Prime Minister Américo Ramos announced the increase, which includes retroactive payments to July, stating it will bring improvements to pensioners. This marks the second such increase during the current legislative period, following a rise from 1,000 to 1,200 dobras in January 2025. The announcement comes two months before legislative, regional, and municipal elections scheduled for September 27.

Prime Minister Ramos, who also heads the Ministry of Labor and Solidarity, confirmed the sustainability of the archipelago's Social Security fund. He indicated that while the fund is secure, further improvements are necessary, including the digitalization and modernization of services and the provision of competent staff. The decision to increase the pension was under consideration for some time, according to Ramos, who visited the National Institute of Social Security services.

The pension adjustment is expected to benefit around 6,500 elderly individuals. The government views this measure as significant for improving the financial situation of its pensioners. The implementation with retroactive effect to July aims to provide immediate financial relief to beneficiaries, underscoring the government's commitment to social welfare ahead of the upcoming elections.

Why it mattersThe increase in the minimum pension directly affects elderly citizens by providing them with more income.

Sunken Ship Blocks Príncipe Port, Threatening Goods and Fuel Supply

The cargo vessel Rehoboth's sinking has halted all operations at Príncipe's sole port, raising fears of shortages in essential goods and fuel, with environmental concerns also.

Panic is spreading among the population of the Autonomous Region of Príncipe following the sinking of the inter-island cargo ship Rehoboth. The vessel's submersion has blocked the island's only port, jeopardizing the supply of goods and fuels. Merchants warn of an impending crisis, particularly concerning food and fuel availability, as residents begin to stockpile products.

Signs of hoarding are already visible at fuel stations, with long queues forming as people anticipate stockouts. Motorcyclist Wilson Santana reported seeing large quantities of fuel being purchased and stored in containers. Regional and central authorities are urgently seeking solutions to the supply disruption and the potential environmental hazards posed by the sunken ship.

Ezekio Napoleão, the Regional Secretary for Infrastructure, expressed concern about fuel potentially floating within the ship, which could lead to an environmental disaster. The incident has reignited discussions about the necessity of constructing a new port on the island, with one merchant suggesting a delay in elections until guarantees for a functional port and fuel reservoir are provided.

Why it mattersThe blocking of the sole port by a sunken ship directly impacts the supply chain for essential goods and fuels, affecting the daily lives and economic activities of Príncipe's residents.

IMF, World Bank Policies Drive Debt System Across Africa

Austerity measures tied to debt servicing are creating similar economic and social challenges in geographically distant African nations, according to academic analysis.

African nations are increasingly facing similar economic pressures due to a shared "debt system," which compels governments to prioritize debt repayment over social spending and public services. This phenomenon, driven by policies from institutions like the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the World Bank, results in a growing commitment of national budgets to debt servicing. The consequence is a persistent need for primary surpluses, which directly translates into cuts in social expenditures and reduced investment in essential public services across the continent.

This pattern was highlighted through an academic discussion where a doctoral student from Mozambique described her country's economic situation. She explained that the current economic policies in Mozambique are leading to a significant portion of the public budget being allocated to debt service. This necessitates a high primary surplus, which in turn forces cuts in social spending and a reduction in investments in public services, a situation that mirrors challenges faced in other nations.

The analysis suggests that despite geographical differences, many countries are subjected to a common economic framework. This framework, characterized by austerity measures aimed at managing public debt, creates a uniform impact on their economic and social dimensions. The article posits that this systemic approach to debt management is a determining factor linking the economic policies and social outcomes of numerous countries worldwide.

Why it mattersAfrican nations are affected by policies that require prioritizing debt repayment, leading to cuts in social spending and public services.
