Sao Tome president rejects MLSTP government proposal

President Carlos Vila Nova has asked the second-placed ADI party to form a government after the MLSTP failed to prove parliamentary support.

President Carlos Vila Nova of Sao Tome and Principe has rejected the Movimento de Libertação de São Tomé e Príncipe's (MLSTP) proposal to form a government. Vila Nova cited insufficient evidence of parliamentary sustainability for a government led by the MLSTP, which won the most seats in the September 27 elections but without an absolute majority.

Américo Barros, the MLSTP president, contested the decision, stating his party had presented a proposal for himself as prime minister with the support of 30 deputies, including 24 from MLSTP, two from BASTA, and four others who gave conditional support. The presidency formally notified the MLSTP on November 10 to present its proposal within 24 hours, which was submitted on November 11.

Following the MLSTP's rejection, President Vila Nova has now summoned the Acção Democrática Independente (ADI), the second-largest party with 23 deputies, to present its own prime ministerial candidate and proof of parliamentary backing within 24 hours. The ADI had previously formed a parliamentary agreement with MCI-PUN, securing 29 deputies.

Sao Tome president gives MLSTP 24 hours to name prime minister

President Carlos Vila Nova wants proof that the nominee would have enough support in the National Assembly to pass the government's programme.

President Carlos Vila Nova has invited the MLSTP party, which won the most seats in the September 27 legislative elections but without an absolute majority, to name a prime minister within 24 hours. The president's office said the nominee must be sent in writing and accompanied by evidence of parliamentary backing.

The presidency said the nominee must show enough political support in the National Assembly to secure approval of the government's programme and allow it to carry out its duties. The MLSTP, led by Américo Barros, won 24 seats and has claimed the right to form the next government. The presidency said the demand was made to safeguard national interests and promote political stability.

The outgoing XIX Constitutional Government remains in office until a new prime minister is appointed and sworn in, and may only carry out acts needed for routine public business. The government's resignation followed the opening of the new legislative term, which began on the same day the presidency issued its statement.

Central bank asks public to take part in emigration and currency survey

The Central Bank of Sao Tome and Principe is collecting views on emigration, the foreign exchange market and inflation expectations until November 22.

The Central Bank of Sao Tome and Principe is carrying out a survey between November 9 and November 22 on emigration, the foreign exchange market and inflation expectations. It aims to learn what people think, feel and have experienced on these subjects.

The bank is calling on everyone to take part, saying the contributions will be essential to reaching the survey's goals. Its appeal did not set out how the results will be published or used.

Other items in the same feed cover the Dobra banknote, which was produced in the Netherlands, and a Central Bank and academies initiative to support economic growth in the country.
