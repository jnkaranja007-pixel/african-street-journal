Voters Shun Democracy, High Abstention Reported

Sao Tome and Principe's presidential election saw 58.57% abstention, marking a significant distrust in the liberal democratic system.

A record 83,276 voters, or 58.57% of the electorate, did not participate in the July 19 presidential elections in Sao Tome and Principe. This widespread apathy signals a deep dissatisfaction with a political system that, since 1991, has failed to meet citizens' basic needs despite promises of change.

Voters have expressed fatigue and disbelief with a 36-year-old democratic routine that has seen politicians enrich themselves while essential services like energy, water, and healthcare remain inadequate. The European Union, a key promoter of liberal democracy in the country, has pledged to work with the government to encourage greater participation in upcoming legislative and local elections.

The low turnout suggests a growing sentiment that the system has become a "dictatorship of democracy," where citizens hold power only at the ballot box. Political actors, according to reports, use the quadrennial electoral cycle to secure personal and familial interests, transitioning from poverty to wealth within their terms.

Why it mattersCitizens are affected by a political system that fails to deliver basic services and allows politicians to enrich themselves, leading to widespread disillusionment.

Project WACA+ delivers two trucks and 30 bins to capital city

New waste management equipment aims to improve sanitation and boost tourism in Sao Tome's main district.

The city of Sao Tome has received two new waste collection trucks and 30 new bins, marking a significant upgrade in waste management. The handover ceremony took place on Friday in front of the Ministry of Environment building. The equipment was funded by Project WACA+ through an international public tender.

Project WACA+ coordinator Arlindo Carvalho stated that the initiative addresses key obstacles to sustainable tourism, with basic sanitation and waste management identified as major issues. The new assets will enhance collection efficiency, particularly in the densely populated Água Grande district, which serves as the country's primary tourist gateway.

Minister of Environment, Youth and Sustainable Tourism, Nilda da Mata, accepted the keys and urged responsible use of the new resources by drivers and officials of the Água Grande Council. She emphasized that clean environments are essential for tourism, and this delivery represents the first phase of a larger project that includes plans for a sanitary landfill.

Why it mattersThe city's sanitation is improved, which is important for tourism and the general living conditions of residents in the capital.

Journalists Trained on Parity Law for Election Coverage

Media professionals attended a workshop to understand and apply the Parity Law in their reporting for upcoming September elections.

Media professionals from public and private outlets participated in a workshop focused on the Parity Law, aiming to enhance their understanding and application of gender equality principles in political coverage. The event, held at the United Nations System in São Tomé and Príncipe, specifically prepared journalists to cover the September elections with a focus on parity.

The workshop, organized by the National Institute for the Promotion of Equality (INPG) and the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), sought to deepen knowledge of the legislation, combat gender stereotypes, and encourage reflection on how men and women are presented in public and political spheres. Participants included reporters, camera operators, and editorial staff from various media platforms.

Jailça Lima, Director of the INPG, emphasized the crucial role of journalists in informing, mobilizing society, and contributing to democracy and social justice. She urged media professionals to be free, attentive, and fair in their reporting, actively working to deconstruct prejudices and reduce gender stereotypes to strengthen the collective future.

Why it mattersJournalists are affected by the need to apply the Parity Law in their reporting, ensuring gender equality in election coverage.

UNESCO recognition boosts Sao Tome's historic plantations

Six former plantation estates, built on slavery and forced labor, have been added to the World Heritage List, drawing investor interest.

Six historic plantation estates, known as "rocas," on Sao Tome and Principe have been added to the UNESCO World Heritage List. These sites, established by Portuguese colonists in the late 19th century, were built using slave labor and later contracted workers who endured harsh conditions. Many of these "rocas" are now abandoned, posing significant preservation challenges for the island nation.

The recognition by UNESCO has sparked interest from private investors who see potential in developing the historical tourism aspects of these crumbling structures. The plantations historically cultivated sugar cane, cocoa, and coffee, with Sao Tome serving as a final staging post for slaves before their journey to the Americas.

Descendants of plantation workers, like Conceicao Moreno, view these sites as a vital part of their heritage, despite their difficult past. Moreno highlighted the crumbling walls of the former Sundy plantation on Principe island as a testament to her family's history, stating, "My grandmother lived here, my father was born here and I was born here too. It's our heritage, in a way."

Why it mattersThe UNESCO recognition of historic plantations may attract investors interested in tourism development, potentially impacting the economic landscape of Sao Tome and Principe.

Man's leg injury worsens from lack of consistent care

A 53-year-old man's leg wound, present for three decades, has become severely debilitating due to inconsistent medical attention and a shortage of supplies.

Idalécio Soares do Rosário, 53, has lived in Ponta Mina for approximately 30 years with a severe leg injury that has progressively worsened. The condition now significantly limits his daily activities and ability to work. His sister, Camila de Sousa Pires, brought the case to Tela Non, highlighting the prolonged illness, economic hardship, and difficulties in accessing necessary wound care.

The injury first appeared about 30 years ago, initially causing inflammation that worsened over time. Currently, Rosário requires regular wound dressing, but his family struggles to provide consistent care. His sister reported seeking assistance at health units in Água Grande district but faced challenges in maintaining continuous treatment, particularly a lack of necessary materials for the dressings.

The family states that Rosário has not received consistent medical assistance for his foot wound, which has deteriorated to its current state. The lack of available materials for wound care is a significant obstacle to his treatment. This situation has left him with a progressively worsening condition that impacts his basic ability to function.

Why it mattersIdalécio Soares do Rosário is affected by a worsening leg injury because his family cannot consistently access medical supplies and care for his wound.
