Nilda da Mata elected president of Sao Tome and Principe's National Assembly

The former environment minister won all 55 votes in the parliament's first session of the thirteenth legislature.

Nilda da Mata, a deputy from the ADI party and a former minister of environment, youth and sustainable tourism, was elected president of the National Assembly on November 10 with the votes of all 55 deputies. She was nominated by her party's parliamentary group in the first session of the legislature, which followed the September 27 legislative elections.

She is the second woman to preside over the parliament. Da Mata holds a degree in international relations and previously worked as an adviser to the President of the Republic. Deputies then elected ADI deputy Vasth Santos as vice-president and filled the rest of the parliamentary board.

The newly elected deputies were sworn in at a ceremony presided over by President Carlos Vila Nova. The MLSTP won the most seats with 24, ADI took 23, the MCI-PS/PUN coalition won six and the Basta movement won two. The president has invited MLSTP to propose a candidate for prime minister, and that name has not yet been confirmed.

Why it mattersADI and MCI-PUN, which signed a parliamentary agreement giving them 29 seats, now control the assembly's leadership, while MLSTP must still name a prime minister candidate.

Sao Tome and Principe elects 19 women to parliament

The number of female lawmakers more than doubled, reaching 34.5% of the new legislature.

19 women have been elected to Sao Tome and Principe's parliament for the first time, a significant increase from eight in the previous term. The new legislature, which begins its session on Tuesday, will see these 19 women comprise 34.5% of the 55 elected members. Japan and the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) have pledged support to ensure their full participation.

The election of these women follows the implementation of a 2022 parity law requiring a minimum of 40% representation for each sex on candidate lists. According to the Japanese ambassador Yoshio Ando and UNDP resident representative Luc Gnonlonfoun, 16 of the newly elected female deputies have no prior legislative experience. They will need to familiarize themselves with parliamentary procedures, committee work, and budget debates in an environment free from intimidation and violence.

Japan and the UNDP, who supported the implementation of the parity law through debates and training, will continue to work with national authorities to consolidate these gains. Their goal is to ensure that these elected women can effectively exercise their mandates and contribute to national priorities. This effort aims to preserve the achievements of the parity law and build confidence in democratic institutions for future generations of women leaders.

Why it mattersThe election of 19 women to parliament, a result of a new parity law, signifies a major step for women's political representation and democracy in Sao Tome and Principe.

São Tomé and Príncipe launches Resilands climate resilience initiative

The Green Climate Fund-financed project will fund early warning systems and disaster risk management, with the UN Economic Commission for Africa carrying out the work.

São Tomé and Príncipe has launched the Resilands initiative, aimed at protecting communities and public works from worsening climate change as a strong El Niño is forecast. The launch came during national consultations held in October, where the country moved forward on strengthening risk management and putting preventive alerts in place.

António Palazuelos, an expert on Small Island Developing States, spoke to UN News at the UN Biodiversity Conference, COP17, in Armenia. He said the initiative is meant to build rapid response capacity for emergencies, and that the minister had called it a state priority. He cited storms, floods and landslides among the threats the project addresses, and said infrastructure must be built to withstand floods and extreme weather.

The project is funded by the Green Climate Fund and executed by the UN Economic Commission for Africa. It brings together nine member countries of the Commission of Island States of Africa for Climate, including Cape Verde, Guinea-Bissau, Equatorial Guinea and São Tomé and Príncipe. It is led by the Ministry of Finance and the Planning Department, working with environment, blue economy, civil protection and foreign affairs bodies, universities and civil society.

Why it mattersInfrastructure and communities in the country are targeted for protection from floods and landslides under the programme.

Women achieve historic representation in Sao Tome and Principe parliament

18 women now sit in the 55-seat National Assembly, the highest number ever.

18 women now serve in Sao Tome and Principe's 55-seat National Assembly, marking the highest female representation in the country's history. This achievement follows the 2022 Parity Law, which mandates a minimum 40% quota for each gender on candidate lists for elected positions.

Vera Cravid, the Minister of Justice, Parliamentary Affairs, and Women's Rights, stated that this represents women's increased participation in defining societal issues and decisions. Many newly elected female deputies expressed determination to fulfill the expectations of the Santomean people and contribute to the nation's progress.

Some elected representatives have already identified key issues for parliamentary debate, including the rise in violence against women and the health challenges faced by individuals experiencing homelessness. The XIII Legislature's opening also saw a female majority in the National Assembly's Standing Committee, with Nilda da Mata elected president unanimously by all 55 deputies present.

Why it mattersThe new parliament composition directly affects all citizens by ensuring more diverse perspectives in lawmaking and policy decisions.

Brazilian director to lead masterclass in São Tomé

Juliane Almeida will discuss character creation in fiction on November 13.

The Instituto Guimarães Rosa São Tomé will host Brazilian filmmaker Juliane Almeida for a masterclass on Friday, November 13. The session, titled “Construção de Personagens na Ficção” (Character Construction in Fiction), will explore the creative processes behind developing fictional characters.

Almeida will guide participants through the techniques and considerations involved in bringing fictional beings to life. Following the masterclass, attendees will have the opportunity to watch the series “Criança de Raíz” (Child of Root), which was directed by Almeida.

The event aims to foster creative development within São Tomé and Príncipe's artistic community. The Instituto Guimarães Rosa serves as a cultural bridge, offering educational and artistic exchanges.

Why it mattersFilmmakers and aspiring writers in São Tomé and Príncipe can learn about character development.
