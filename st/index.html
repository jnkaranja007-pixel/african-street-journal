Women dominate São Tomé and Príncipe higher education

Data shows a consistent rise in female enrollment and graduation rates across all educational levels in the nation.

Women in São Tomé and Príncipe are increasingly dominating higher education, with recent graduation ceremonies at the University of São Tomé and Príncipe highlighting this trend. Out of 92 students who graduated across various fields, 79 were women, while only 13 men completed their higher education at the public university. Similar patterns are observed at private universities, indicating a significant shift in educational attainment.

This rise in female educational achievement is attributed to the "Leve-Leve" approach, characterized by patience and wisdom, which is guiding women's prudent growth. The Prime Minister, Américo Ramos, acknowledged this significant female presence, stating it honors the university and the country. He emphasized that this achievement should translate into real opportunities for these newly qualified women in leadership roles and active participation in decision-making positions.

Historically, São Toméan women were illiterate but possessed the life wisdom to build society. Now, these resourceful women are focusing on knowledge and empowerment to lead the nation they helped create. The Ministry of Education, Culture, and Higher Education data confirms a continuous increase in girls attending basic and secondary education, reinforcing the trend of female academic dominance.

Why it mattersNewly qualified women in São Tomé and Príncipe are affected by this trend as it signifies increased opportunities for leadership and active participation in decision-making roles in the workforce.

Sao Tome and Principe Democracy Model Fails Nation

A persistent critique suggests the nation's adopted democratic system does not suit local realities, leading to economic and social regression.

A persistent concern is the ongoing degradation of the political, economic, and social situation in Sao Tome and Principe, a condition observed for several years. All political parties, with the exception of recent formations, have participated in governance, either alone or in coalitions, leading to a shared responsibility for the country's misfortunes. The nation has experimented with various governance systems, excluding monarchy, yet none have functioned effectively.

The idea gaining traction in Africa, which the author supports, is that the current model of democracy practiced on the continent is a prototype imposed from outside and ill-suited to general African realities. Political scientists are urged to study alternative democratic models based on sociocultural realities, as insisting on what does not work is a significant error. Even Winston Churchill acknowledged democracy as the worst system, except for all others.

Despite over 35 years of democratic rule, Sao Tome and Principe has seen significant economic and social regression. While elections are held regularly, often with foreign observation and funding, the nation's institutions largely do not function. This situation suggests a pretense of democracy to meet unstated foreign objectives, raising questions about the suitability of the current model for the country's future.

Why it mattersThe current democratic model is failing to foster economic and social development, leading to national regression and questioning its suitability for Sao Tome and Principe.

Sao Tome Customs Space Opens to Modernize Services

A new customs facility at Porto de Ana Chaves aims to streamline operations amid increased cargo flow from diaspora remittances.

Minister of State for Economy and Finance Gareth Guadalupe inaugurated a new customs space at Porto de Ana Chaves on Thursday, August 27. The facility is designed to improve service conditions and expedite the release of consolidated cargo. This development comes as rising remittance flows from the diaspora place greater pressure on port services and infrastructure.

The new infrastructure is part of a broader strategy to modernize customs services, which includes the planned installation of non-intrusive inspection equipment. While construction for a pallet scanner is ongoing, the infrastructure for a container scanner has recently commenced. The initiative also necessitates enhanced coordination between the General Customs Authority, Fiscal Police, and ENAPOR to ensure greater organization and efficiency.

Minister Guadalupe explained that the new space addresses a significant increase in cargo movement, largely driven by goods sent by São Toméans living abroad. He noted that exempting certain remittances from customs duties has led to a 91% rise in weight and a 71% increase in service fee payments, highlighting the country's reliance on maritime transport for external supplies.

Why it mattersThe new customs space and modernization efforts directly impact the diaspora by facilitating the import of essential goods for their families in Sao Tome and Principe.

Shanghai Cooperation Organization Summit to Boost Economic Ties

The 2026 SCO summit in Bishkek aims to strengthen economic and technological cooperation among member states amid global tensions.

Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan, will host the 2026 Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) summit on August 31 and September 1, marking the organization's 25th anniversary. The summit aims to consolidate the SCO's role as a pillar of stability in a changing world order by strengthening regional ties and enhancing economic and technological cooperation. The 'Shanghai Spirit,' characterized by mutual trust, equality, common interest, consultation, and respect for civilizations, will be revitalized to promote multipolarity in global governance.

The summit seeks to foster a convergence of views among member states to counter hegemonic and protectionist sentiments that undermine international relations. The SCO, which has united countries against violent extremism and terrorism for 25 years, faces new challenges in economics and technology. Recent disruptions to global trade, including trade disputes and conflicts, necessitate stronger ties and compromise within the SCO to act more effectively and preserve common interests through solidarity.

Technological cooperation, particularly in artificial intelligence, is deemed crucial for SCO members to harness the benefits of this transformative technology. The organization aims to build bridges, especially for the Global South, to combat the digital divide and ensure equitable access to AI opportunities. By promoting multilateralism, the SCO, representing nearly half the world's population and 23.5% of global GDP, seeks to influence global decisions and advocate for a community of shared future for humanity.

Why it mattersInvestors may benefit from strengthened economic and technological cooperation among SCO member states, potentially leading to new trade opportunities and reduced trade barriers.
