Portuguese firm recruits 100 Sao Tome construction workers

A European Union-funded labour migration project aims to fill a shortage of skilled workers in Portugal.

Around 100 young Sao Tomean construction professionals are being recruited by a Portuguese company for work in Portugal. The recruitment drive, which is part of a European Union-funded labour migration project, began today in Sao Tome. The Portuguese company Cândido de José Rodrigues (CJR) is leading the effort.

Pedro Maia, director of CJR, stated that the company is seeking a wide range of skilled workers, including foremen, typographers, engineers, masons, and machine operators, due to a significant labour shortage in Portugal. CJR will provide logistical support for obtaining legal work visas and will cover accommodation, utilities, and food for workers if they are away from their residences.

Selected Sao Tomeans are expected to earn a monthly salary of 1,000 euros for their work on CJR's construction projects in Portugal. The recruitment process is scheduled to last for three days, with many young people gathering at a hotel in the capital seeking employment opportunities.

Sao Tome president congratulates Brazil's Lula on re-election

Carlos Vila Nova sent a message to his Brazilian counterpart wishing him health and success.

Sao Tome and Principe President Carlos Vila Nova congratulated Brazil's re-elected president, Lula da Silva, on his victory in Sunday's presidential elections. The congratulatory message was sent via the official presidency page of Sao Tome. Vila Nova expressed wishes for health and success in Lula's mission, stating the victory would strengthen historical relations and friendship between the two nations.

The Sao Tomean president affirmed that Lula da Silva's re-election reflects the Brazilian people's trust and hope in his leadership for development and prosperity. Lula da Silva secured his fourth presidential term with 51.7% of the vote in the second round, defeating Flávio Bolsonaro who received 48.23%.

Brazil is a key cooperation partner for Sao Tome and Principe, involved in sectors such as health, education, culture, and food security. This re-election solidifies existing cooperation ties between the two CPLP member states.

Sao Tome and Principe seeks firm for Azeitona solar project

The Ministry of Infrastructure wants a supervisor for the Azeitona solar photovoltaic plant construction.

The Ministry of Infrastructure and Natural Resources in Sao Tome and Principe is seeking a specialized company to oversee the construction of a solar photovoltaic park with batteries in Azeitona, Principe. This project is part of the Energy Transition, Efficiency, and Expansion Project (ETREEP), which is funded by the African Development Bank (AfDB). The selected firm will provide "Owner’s Engineer Services for Azeitona Solar PV Plant and BESS."

The ministry aims to contract a firm to supervise the construction works, ensuring the project's successful implementation. The Azeitona solar park is a key component of the ETREEP initiative, designed to enhance energy transition and efficiency across the islands. The African Development Bank is providing the necessary funding for this significant undertaking.

The recruitment process for the supervisory company is underway. The Azeitona solar park is expected to contribute to the expansion of renewable energy sources in Sao Tome and Principe. The ETREEP project aims to improve energy efficiency and expand access to power.

western nations tie africa aid to reforms

investment and trade deals now depend on democratic progress, anti-corruption measures, and human rights protections.

Western nations and international organizations are structuring their investments, financial packages, and trade agreements in Africa based on specific, measurable criteria rather than vague preferences. These conditions are directly incorporated into frameworks such as the United States' African Growth and Opportunity Act (AGOA), Millennium Challenge Corporation (MCC) pacts, and International Monetary Fund (IMF) loan agreements.

Global powers seek reforms across three essential pillars: democratic progress, anti-corruption and governance, and human rights. Democratic reforms include political pluralism, institutional stability, and peaceful power transfers, with conditions like regular, monitored elections and respect for constitutional term limits. Anti-corruption measures focus on financial transparency, independent anti-graft agencies, and open public procurement systems. Human rights requirements increasingly link duty-free market access and direct budget support to civil liberties, fundamental labor standards, and protections for vulnerable groups.

African nations must also shift their mindset towards work and productivity to address significant debt burdens, particularly from the IMF and China, which are conditioned on development. The continent faces a substantial annual infrastructure financing deficit, leading to frequent electricity cuts and high transport costs between neighboring countries, hindering development.

Why it mattersAfrican nations seeking investment and financial aid must implement democratic, anti-corruption, and human rights reforms to qualify for Western funding and trade agreements.

Sao Tome and Principe governments annul contracts

New governments in Sao Tome and Principe routinely cancel prior deals, citing 'damaging' terms.

New governments in Sao Tome and Principe consistently annul contracts signed by their predecessors, claiming the deals are damaging to the state. This practice, repeated with each change in administration, raises questions about potential corruption within the governmental process.

The core issue arises when an annulled contract leads to legal action by the aggrieved investor. The government is then compelled to appoint a lawyer to defend its decision. This lawyer requires payment, creating a financial arrangement potentially involving the head of government who initiated the contract annulment.

This cycle of contract cancellation and subsequent legal defense payments creates a win-win situation for both the appointed lawyer and the government official responsible for the annulment. The Tela Non outlet suggests this arrangement could be a form of governmental corruption.

Why it mattersNew governments in Sao Tome and Principe annul contracts, leading to legal fees that benefit government officials.
