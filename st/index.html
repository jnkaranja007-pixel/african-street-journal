Sao Tome and Principe professionals train in Portugal for clinical trials

Five health professionals from Sao Tome and Principe are in Portugal for practical training to develop clinical trials in their country.

Five professionals from Sao Tome and Principe are undergoing practical training in Portugal from November 8 to 14 to help establish conditions for clinical trials at home. The training aims to prepare institutions for evaluation, regulation, and research responsibilities. This practical stage is part of the CT-Luso project, which includes 30 professionals from Portuguese-speaking African countries.

The Sao Tomean team includes Hironisia Santos, Jeryson Costa, and Neurice Ramos from the Ministry of Health's Pharmaceutical Department, Eula Maquengo, president of the Health Ethics Commission for Scientific Research (CESIC), and Jessica Almeida, a researcher at the National Center for Endemic Diseases. They are engaging with Portuguese authorities, ethics committees, and research centers to refine procedures for evaluating, authorizing, and monitoring clinical trials for adaptation to Sao Tome's context.

This training follows two completed CT-Luso programs that certified 234 professionals on ethical and regulatory fundamentals and 87 on Good Clinical Practices. The current stage focuses on practical application through case analysis and observing host institutions' procedures. Recommendations for Sao Tome and Principe suggest developing a specific decree on clinical research to systematize existing norms.

Why it mattersThe training will enhance Sao Tome and Principe's capacity to conduct clinical trials by improving regulatory and ethical frameworks.

6 Setembro wins São Tomé championship

The team will now play Príncipe's 1º de Maio for the national title.

Desportivo 6 Setembro became the São Tomé island champion after defeating Ké-Morabeza 2-0 on Sunday. Castro scored in the 62nd minute and Agrós added a second goal in the 82nd minute. The team will now face Príncipe's 1º de Maio, the champion of the Príncipe region, in the grand final.

During the match, 6 Setembro's Tinho missed a penalty, with Ké-Morabeza's young goalkeeper Leu making a spectacular save. This victory secured 6 Setembro's place in the championship-deciding match.

The final match between 6 Setembro and 1º de Maio is scheduled to determine the overall national champion.

Why it mattersThe football team 6 Setembro has won the São Tomé island championship, advancing to a final match.

China donates scanning technology to Sao Tome and Principe

New 3D imaging scanners, the first of their kind in Africa, will enhance customs security at the port and airport.

Sao Tome and Principe has received advanced non-intrusive inspection equipment from China, a donation valued at seven million euros. The technology, including a container scanner for the Port of Ana Chaves and two baggage scanners for Nuno Xavier International Airport, is set to become operational on December 1, 2026. This upgrade aims to modernize customs services and bolster national security.

The new systems, model NUCTECH WooKong H, use high-resolution 3D imaging to analyze cargo and luggage with greater precision, reducing reliance on physical inspections. Chinese specialists are installing the equipment and training local customs and airport officials on its operation. Chinese Ambassador Xu Yingzhen stated the scanners are pioneering in Africa and will aid in combating smuggling and improving efficiency.

Minister of State for Economy and Finance Gareth Guadalupe highlighted that Sao Tome and Principe has never before possessed scanners with this capability. He noted the equipment will improve inspection procedures, offer greater passenger convenience, and help mitigate transnational crime, potentially improving the country's standing in the Mo Ibrahim Index. The Chinese cooperation also includes long-term maintenance support for the systems.

Why it mattersSao Tome and Principe's customs and border security will be significantly enhanced by advanced scanning technology, improving efficiency and national safety.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Sao Tome youth council demands effective public policies

The National Youth Council seeks real job opportunities, training, and housing for young people facing emigration.

The Sao Tome and Principe National Youth Council (CNJ) on Thursday demanded "effective public policies" to create real opportunities for young people. The council stated that young people need concrete results, including employment programs, vocational training aligned with market needs, entrepreneurship support, access to credit, affordable housing, and effective participation in decision-making processes.

The CNJ expressed its disappointment with the government's handling of Youth Day, withdrawing from the central event due to communication and organizational failures. The council emphasized the need for a National Youth Strategy that moves beyond paper to effect tangible change, urging institutions to listen more to youth concerns and commit to clear goals, resources, and deadlines. They called for youth to be placed at the center of national priorities.

The organization highlighted that many young Sao Tomeans lack employment, dignified opportunities, and the means to realize their life projects, often lacking hope for their future in their homeland. This reality, coupled with persistent issues in basic services like water, energy, health, and housing, forces many young people to see emigration not as an option but as a necessity, representing a loss of national growth and development capacity.

Why it mattersYoung Sao Tomeans are facing a lack of opportunities, leading many to consider emigration as a necessity rather than a choice.
