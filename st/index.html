Sao Tome and Principe, Angola sign higher education quality accord

The agreement between ANRES and INAAREES will facilitate the exchange of external evaluators and the recognition of academic degrees.

Sao Tome and Principe and Angola have signed an agreement to improve the quality of higher education in both nations. The accord, signed on October 29 in Luanda, Angola, between the National Agency for Regulation of Higher Education (ANRES) of Sao Tome and Principe and Angola's National Institute for Evaluation, Accreditation and Recognition of Higher Education Studies (INAAREES), aims to foster collaboration.

The agreement formalizes a strategic alliance focused on regulation, external evaluation, and accreditation of institutions and study cycles. It allows both regulatory bodies to share evaluation experts for assessment commissions in Angola and Sao Tome and Principe. Cooperation also covers quality assurance systems, academic degree recognition, and technical capacity building.

A joint Bilateral Technical Committee will be established to create bi-annual action plans and monitor set goals, with annual meetings alternating between Luanda and Sao Tome. The protocol takes immediate effect for an initial five-year period, renewable for equal terms, and aligns academic excellence policies within the CPLP space.

Why it mattersStudents and institutions in Sao Tome and Principe will benefit from harmonized academic recognition and improved quality assurance processes in higher education.

Sao Tome and Principe bans plastic bags

Law 8/2020 prohibits production, import, sale, and free distribution of plastic bags.

Sao Tome and Principe banned plastic bags through Law n.º 8/2020, published on September 21. The law prohibits the production, import, commercialization, and free distribution of plastic bags across the nation. This legislation aims to curb plastic waste and its environmental impact.

Neighboring countries offer comparative approaches to plastic regulation. Cabo Verde's Law n.º 43/X/2024 extends bans to all single-use plastics, including cups and disposable cutlery. Angola, via Presidential Decree n.º 170/25, implements a phased prohibition, starting with thinner plastic bags and straws after 12 months, and expanding to PET bottles and disposable tableware after 36 months.

The analysis suggests Sao Tome and Principe could expand its ban to include gradual prohibitions on plastic cups and cutlery in future revisions. The nation's Law n.º 8/2020 also mandates government incentives for private investment in alternative products, aligning with Cabo Verde's experience in promoting sustainable options.

Why it mattersThe ban on plastic bags directly affects consumers and businesses in Sao Tome and Principe.

Health · 2026-10-31

Kosmos Energy donates 10 ambulances to Sao Tome and Principe health ministry

The vehicles will bolster the national health system's response capacity and improve patient transport.

10 new ambulances were delivered to the Sao Tome and Principe Ministry of Health on Friday, October 30, 2026. The donation came from the oil company Kosmos Energy and was presented during a ceremony led by Prime Minister Américo Ramos.

Prime Minister Ramos stated that the investment addresses concrete needs within the health services, aiming to enhance the national health system's capacity. These ambulances will improve patient transport and facilitate faster, more effective medical assistance to the population.

The donation is part of the social commitments made by Kosmos Energy under its contract for the exploration of Block 5 in Sao Tome and Principe's exclusive economic zone.

Why it mattersThe donation of 10 ambulances will improve healthcare access and response times for the population of Sao Tome and Principe.

Príncipe islanders face rising home and shop robberies

Residents report stolen cash, phones, and even potatoes amid growing insecurity on the island.

Residents of Príncipe island are experiencing an unusual wave of robberies targeting homes and businesses, causing widespread concern. Domingas da Conceição reported thieves took recharge cards, cash, a mobile phone, and school backpacks from her home. Arzemiro Cassandra also lost money and a bag of potatoes in a separate incident.

The insecurity has altered the island's long-standing reputation for safety, with one merchant noting that valuables could once be left unattended. Residents attribute the rise in crime partly to frequent nighttime power outages that leave areas in darkness, creating opportunities for criminals. Some also believe the island is becoming a refuge for individuals fleeing criminal charges in São Tomé.

The escalating situation has led to calls for increased police patrols and a more effective security response. Merchants and residents alike are lamenting the change from a past where the island was considered safe enough to leave valuables exposed.

Climate · 2026-10-31

Sao Tome suspends climate and water project tenders

The PRIASA III project unit and the National Water Institute have halted three calls for expressions of interest.

The PRIASA III project unit and the National Water Institute (INA) have suspended three calls for expressions of interest that were announced on October 15. The suspended tenders were for institutional capacity building and climate finance mobilization, integrated water resource management plans, and hydrometeorological studies for the Rio Jorge and Rio d’Ouro river basins.

This information was communicated on Wednesday through a note sent to media outlets. The suspension is effective until further notice. The tenders were for consulting firms to undertake these specific studies and planning activities.

The PRIASA III project aims to strengthen institutional capacity and mobilize financial resources for climate-related initiatives. The INA is responsible for water resource management and related studies within the country.

Why it mattersThree tenders related to climate finance, water resource management, and hydrometeorological studies in Sao Tome and Principe have been suspended.
