President Vila Nova calls for modern, republican police force

President Carlos Vila Nova emphasized the need for a police force that is firm, balanced, and close to citizens while remaining loyal to republican principles.

President Carlos Vila Nova, speaking at the 51st anniversary of the National Police, stated that São Tomé and Príncipe requires a modern police force that is also faithful to republican principles. He highlighted the need for a force that is firm yet balanced, respected, and prepared for future challenges.

Vila Nova, who serves as the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces, argued that the country needs a police force that understands authority as a duty, not a privilege. He advocated for investment in the National Police, emphasizing that human capital should be central to its modernization.

The President noted that a well-prepared, disciplined, and motivated police officer is a crucial state investment. He commended the National Police for its conduct during the presidential elections on July 19 and requested a repeat of this professionalism for the upcoming legislative, municipal, and regional elections on September 27.

Why it mattersCitizens are affected by the President's call for a police force that is both modern and adheres to republican principles, as this impacts their daily interactions with law enforcement and the state's presence.

Shipwreck blocks Príncipe port, threatens island supplies

The sinking of the inter-island cargo ship Rehoboth has blocked Príncipe's only port, causing panic among residents and raising concerns about food and fuel shortages.

The island of Príncipe faces a potential crisis as the shipwreck of the cargo vessel Rehoboth has blocked its sole port, according to Tela Non. This incident, which occurred about a week ago, has halted new supply operations at the Santo António port, leading to fears of widespread shortages of essential goods and fuel.

Residents are already reacting to the disruption, with reports of long queues at fuel stations and people buying large quantities of products, as stated by motorcyclist Wilson Santana. Merchant Fernando Sousa warned that without a swift resolution, the island could face a crisis of goods, particularly food and fuel.

Regional and central authorities are seeking an urgent solution, not only to restore supply lines but also to address environmental risks. Ezequio Napoleão, Regional Secretary for Infrastructure, expressed concern about residual product floating inside the ship, which could lead to an environmental disaster if spilled. The incident has also reignited discussions about constructing a new port for the island.

Why it mattersThe blockage of the only port directly impacts all residents of Príncipe by threatening their access to essential goods like food and fuel.

Government increases minimum pension to 1,400 dobras for 6,500 people

The government of Sao Tome and Principe has raised the minimum pension by 200 dobras, effective retroactively from July, benefiting approximately 6,500 pensioners.

The minimum pension in Sao Tome and Principe has increased from 1,200 to 1,400 dobras, an announcement made by Prime Minister Américo Ramos. This change, which includes retroactive payments from July, will affect around 6,500 individuals.

Prime Minister Ramos stated that the increase would bring some improvement for pensioners. The new value began to be paid in August, with the retroactive portion covering July. This update was announced following the Prime Minister's visit to the National Social Security Institute.

During his visit, Prime Minister Ramos also advocated for the urgent digitization and modernization of social security services. This initiative coincides with the launch of the Single Social Registry program and the expansion of the vulnerable family program by the Directorate of Social Protection, Solidarity, and Family.

Why it mattersApproximately 6,500 pensioners will receive an additional 200 dobras per month, improving their financial stability.

Lusíada University mourns former Rector Fernanda Pontífice

The Lusíada University of São Tomé and Príncipe announced the passing of Professor Fernanda Pontífice, who served as Rector for sixteen years.

The Lusíada University of São Tomé and Príncipe expressed profound sadness over the death of Professor Fernanda Pontífice, a recognized figure in education and higher learning. She was celebrated for her dedication and leadership, leaving a lasting impact on the institution and the country.

During her sixteen years as Rector, Professor Pontífice was also a teacher, mentor, and inspiration to many students, faculty, and staff. Her tenure at the university is linked to a commitment to education, valuing knowledge, and advocating for a university that contributes to national development.

Beyond her university role, Professor Pontífice also served in public office as a Member of Parliament and Minister of Education. The university acknowledges her long and significant journey as Rector and faculty member as an essential part of its history, extending condolences to her family and the academic community.

Why it mattersStudents, faculty, and staff at Lusíada University are affected by the loss of a leader who shaped their educational and professional paths.

Journalists and Technicians Strike Over Unpaid Audiovisual Tax

Journalists and technicians from state media outlets will begin an indefinite strike on September two, protesting the government's failure to fulfill commitments regarding.

The Union of Journalists and Social Communication Technicians announced an indefinite strike starting September two, citing the government's non-compliance with agreements related to the Audiovisual Tax and other demands. The strike notice was submitted to the Prime Minister's Office on Tuesday, August twenty-fifth.

According to Jorge Do Ó, president of the Union of Journalists and Social Communication Technicians, the government has not honored commitments made to professionals at the three public media organizations: National Radio of São Tomé and Príncipe, TVS, and STP-PRESS. The union's concerns include the suspension of the Audiovisual Tax and the alleged breach of a memorandum of understanding concerning the transfer of tax revenues collected by the Water and Electricity Company (EMAE).

EMAE has made irregular deposits of Audiovisual Tax funds into public media accounts and owes more than a year's worth of accumulated debt. The union also seeks improved working conditions, professional training every two years, and enhanced capabilities for public media organizations, according to information obtained by Tela Non.

Why it mattersJournalists and technicians will cease work indefinitely, directly impacting their income and the public's access to information from state media.
