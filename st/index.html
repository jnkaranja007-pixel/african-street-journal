President Calls for Modern Police Force

President Carlos Vila Nova emphasized the need for a modern, yet republican-principled police force, highlighting human capital as key to its development.

President Carlos Vila Nova presided over the 51st anniversary of the National Police, stating that São Tomé and Príncipe requires a police force that is both modern and steadfast in its republican principles. He advocated for a firm yet balanced police presence, one that is accessible to citizens while also commanding respect.

The President stressed that authority is a duty, not a privilege, and called for investment in the national police, particularly in its human capital. He described well-prepared, disciplined, and motivated officers as one of the state's most significant investments, noting the police's role as the visible representation of the state.

Vila Nova acknowledged the National Police's professional conduct during the July 19 presidential elections and urged for a repeat of this professionalism in the upcoming September 27 legislative, municipal, and regional elections. He highlighted the police's crucial role in responding to accidents, intervening in conflicts, and protecting families.

Why it mattersThe President's call for a modern and professional police force directly impacts citizens by ensuring better state presence and response to incidents.

Government Raises Minimum Pension to 1400 Dobras

The minimum pension in São Tomé and Príncipe will increase from 1200 to 1400 dobras, affecting approximately 6,500 pensioners with retroactive payments starting from July.

The government of São Tomé and Príncipe has announced an increase in the minimum pension from 1,200 to 1,400 dobras, a rise of 200 dobras. This adjustment, confirmed by Prime Minister Américo Ramos following a visit to the National Social Security Institute, will be effective from September and includes retroactive payments dating back to July. The measure aims to provide financial improvement for pensioners.

Prime Minister Ramos stated that the pension increase will benefit around 6,500 individuals. He emphasized that this change will bring about improvements for the country's pensioners, noting the retroactive application of the new rate. The announcement was made on the same day that the Directorate of Social Protection, Solidarity, and Family launched the Unified Social Registry program and celebrated the expansion of the vulnerable family program.

During his visit, Prime Minister Ramos also highlighted the urgent need for the digitalization and modernization of social security services. He advocated for these improvements alongside the announcement of the pension hike, suggesting a broader agenda for enhancing the social welfare system in the country. The visit and subsequent announcements underscore a focus on improving social security provisions.

Why it mattersPensioners in São Tomé and Príncipe will receive an increased minimum pension payment, improving their financial situation.

Sunken Ship Blocks Príncipe Port, Threatening Supplies

The grounding of the Rehoboth cargo vessel has halted all activity at Príncipe Island's sole port, raising fears of shortages in essential goods and fuel.

Panic is growing on Príncipe Island as the sinking of the inter-island cargo ship Rehoboth has blocked its only port, jeopardizing the supply of goods and fuel. Merchants warn of an impending crisis, citing potential shortages of food and all essential commodities. Signs of hoarding have already appeared at fuel stations, with long queues forming as residents anticipate stockouts and purchase supplies in large quantities.

Regional and central authorities are urgently seeking a solution to the crisis, which not only threatens supply chains but also poses environmental risks. Ezequio Napoleão, the Regional Secretary for Infrastructure, expressed concern over the presence of fuel still floating within the sunken vessel, fearing a potential spill that could lead to an environmental disaster. The EMAE has reportedly removed its fuel reserves.

The accident has reignited discussions about the long-standing need for a new port on the island. One merchant suggested delaying the upcoming September 27 elections until assurances are given for a functional port and a fuel reservoir, highlighting these as critical issues causing hardship for the island's population.

Why it mattersThe sinking of the Rehoboth ship and the subsequent port closure directly impacts the supply of goods and fuel to Príncipe Island, affecting daily life and commerce.

Media Workers Strike Over Unpaid Audiovisual Tax

Journalists and technicians at state media outlets will strike indefinitely starting September 2, protesting the government's failure to honor agreements on audiovisual tax.

State media employees in Sao Tome and Principe will strike indefinitely from September 2, following a pre-strike notice filed by the Union of Journalists and Social Communication Technicians. The action is a protest against the government's alleged non-compliance with commitments regarding the Audiovisual Tax and other demands from public media professionals.

The union cited the suspension of the Audiovisual Tax and the government's alleged failure to implement a memorandum of understanding concerning the transfer of collected tax revenues as key grievances. The Empresa de Água e Eletricidade (EMAE) has been irregularly depositing funds, while still owing over a year's worth of accumulated audiovisual tax revenue to the media organizations.

Beyond financial concerns, the union also highlighted issues with working conditions, the need for regular professional training, and the reinforcement of public media capacities. Discontent among journalists and technicians regarding unfulfilled government commitments and their current working environment is widespread, according to reports.

Why it mattersJournalists and technicians are affected by the government's failure to fulfill agreements on audiovisual tax revenue and other professional demands, impacting their working conditions and pay.
