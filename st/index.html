Coimbra festival highlights Sao Tome and Principe culture

The Artes à Solta festival in Coimbra, Portugal, from November 13-15 will showcase Sao Tomean theatre, dance, and heritage.

The city of Coimbra, Portugal, will host the Artes à Solta festival from November 13 to 15, dedicating its second edition to the heritage and culture of Sao Tome and Principe. The event will feature traditional Sao Tomean theatre, known as tchiloli, and the popular dance bulawê. Organizers announced the festival's focus on the island nation after its first edition highlighted Cape Verde.

Diogo Coelho, executive director of Golden Skill, the company organizing the initiative, stated that the festival will include conferences on Sao Tome and Principe's historic plantations, called roças, and its biosphere reserve. Musician Hector Costa will present a conference on tchiloli, followed by a concert exploring the archipelago's sounds. The Sao Tome and Principe Embassy in Portugal is partnering with the festival.

Ambassador Esterline Gonçalves Género of Sao Tome and Principe in Portugal attended the press conference, viewing the festival as an opportunity to promote the country's culture and heritage. The festival program, which is free to enter, is still being finalized and will be announced on the event's social media channels.

Why it mattersSao Tomeans and their diaspora can learn about and engage with their cultural heritage being presented in Portugal.

OMS pledges continued support for Sao Tome and Principe health programs

The World Health Organization will bolster efforts against malaria, tuberculosis, and health emergencies.

The World Health Organization (OMS) will continue to support Sao Tome and Principe in combating malaria, tuberculosis, and other diseases, while also strengthening national capacities for preventing and responding to health emergencies. This commitment was affirmed by the new OMS representative, Marjorie Mupandaré, after presenting her credentials to the Minister of State for Foreign Affairs, Ilza Amado Vaz.

Mupandaré stated that Sao Tome and Principe is on a positive trajectory with its national public health program. She assured continued technical assistance and close collaboration with national authorities to address key sector challenges, particularly in public health and health emergency management. The OMS representative also met with the Minister of Health, Celso Matos, to discuss the country's health situation and cooperation priorities.

Key concerns include reinforcing epidemiological surveillance at national entry and exit points, especially given the threat of serious diseases like Ebola circulating in other African countries. Mupandaré emphasized the need to enhance the surveillance capabilities at airports and to improve the skills of health professionals in Sao Tome and Principe.

Why it mattersThe World Health Organization will continue to support Sao Tome and Principe in combating diseases and strengthening health emergency response.

Sao Tome and Principe statistics agency surveys businesses

The National Statistics Institute is collecting data on sales and economic outlook from companies and market vendors.

The National Statistics Institute (INE) began a qualitative survey on Tuesday, November 3, in the Autonomous Region of Príncipe to assess economic activity and business trends across Sao Tome and Principe. The survey, which started on October 15, targets companies, shops, service providers, and market vendors to gather information on sales, product commercialization, and economic forecasts for the coming months.

Antónia Sousa, head of the INE's Economic and Financial Statistics Department, explained that the collected data will enable comparisons of commercial activity between periods, identify trends, and evaluate the business environment. The INE aims to understand if economic activities are improving, stable, or worsening, and to identify key business challenges like financing issues and the rising cost of living. 10 surveyors are involved, with eight focusing on establishments and services and two on market vendors in Bobô Forro.

The INE also maintains monthly teams collecting product and price information from businesses and services to calculate inflation. Sousa emphasized the need for collaboration from businesses and vendors for the quality and reliability of statistical data. This survey compares the first half of 2026 with the same period in 2025 and looks at prospects for the end of the year.

Why it mattersBusinesses and market vendors in Sao Tome and Principe are being surveyed about their sales, product commercialization, and economic outlook to understand business performance and challenges.

Health · 2026-11-06

Sao Tome and Principe health system deteriorates

The health minister admitted the country is ranked second in Africa for health sector decline over the past decade.

Sao Tome and Principe's health sector has seen deterioration over the last 10 years, according to the Ibrahim Index of African Governance. Minister of Health Celso Matos acknowledged the poor results, stating that while some parameters have regressed, others have improved.

Matos clarified that the reported deterioration covers the period from 2016 to 2025, with his government taking office only two years ago. He highlighted a stabilization in 2025 and improvements in combating malaria, with cases reducing from over 7,000 in 2024 to four to 5,000 in 2025.

Progress was also noted in controlling communicable diseases like HIV and improving access to primary healthcare, with a goal of one doctor per health post. However, non-communicable diseases such as cardiovascular accidents and diabetes are a serious concern, straining the nation's limited resources.

Why it mattersThe health system's decline affects all citizens' access to adequate medical care and treatment for both communicable and non-communicable diseases.

Health · 2026-11-06

Sao Tome police hold cancer awareness fair

The National Police's Agua Grande district command hosted a fair promoting early detection of breast and cervical cancers.

The National Police held a fair at its Agua Grande district command to raise awareness about breast and cervical cancer prevention. The event, themed "Together for the prevention of breast cancer," coincided with October, the month dedicated to breast cancer awareness.

Sub-Commissioner Daniela Cunha, director of Public Relations and Cooperation, stated that the fair saw good participation and met expectations. The initiative aimed to educate security sector personnel, especially women, about the importance of early detection for these diseases affecting women in Sao Tome and Principe.

The fair also featured a commercial aspect, with the sale of handicrafts from women in the Prison and Social Reintegration Services, palm wine, fruits, vegetables, and pork. Agent Lisete Furtado noted that products made by inmates were also displayed and sold.
