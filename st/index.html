Sao Tome football matches suspended over unpaid referees

Referees went on strike on Saturday, halting all league games due to non-payment of their allowances by the football federation.

Football league matches in Sao Tome were suspended on Saturday, October 25, 2026, because referees went on strike over unpaid allowances. The referees did not show up for games, including a scheduled match between 6 de Setembro and Juba de Diogo Simão at Quartel field, which was part of the 21st round of the first division.

The strike meant that teams, police, and spectators had to leave the Quartel field. One street vendor selling drinks and chicken wings near the field expressed frustration, stating, "I spent so much to buy these things to sell and now I will lose my money."

This suspension affects the penultimate round of the league, known as the “deposto Rei.” The decision was made by the referees who are demanding payment from the Sao Tomean Football Federation (FSF).

Sao Tome and Principe urged to abandon 'leve-leve' lifestyle

A writer reflects on the island nation's relaxed pace of life and calls for a reevaluation of how the country is built.

A writer in Sao Tome and Principe is calling for a reevaluation of the nation's approach to life, work, and development, suggesting it is time to move beyond the 'leve-leve' (slowly, slowly) philosophy.

The author expresses admiration for poet Dona Alda do Espírito Santo and musician Kalu Mendes, whose work embodies the Santomean identity of tranquility and living without constant pressure. Observing life in Angolares, the writer notes the unhurried movements of people and fishermen, who wait for the right moment and the right wave before acting.

While acknowledging the beauty and intelligence found in patience and respecting natural rhythms, the writer questions whether this relaxed attitude hinders progress and the building of the country's future.

Sao Tome fishermen adopt sustainable longline fishing

A three-month project trained local fishermen in the longline technique, offering a sustainable alternative to small-mesh nets.

Small-mesh net fishing may soon end in Sao Tome and Principe as the longline technique gains ground. A three-month responsible fishing project by consortia Oikos, Marapa, and program Tatô has promoted this sustainable alternative. The project involved training fishermen in communities across the archipelago.

Alexander Ramos, a project consultant, explained that longline fishing is more selective, using accessible hooks and lines. He noted that while not all fishermen have mastered the technique, it can be learned. The project also included workshops to define responsible fishing practices and develop fishing gear with community input, according to Antunes Pina, Tatô program representative.

Fishermen have embraced the new method, with Marques Lima from Praia Gamboa stating, "We cannot continue to catch small fish, because, every day that passes, the fish are decreasing in the sea." The project's results are documented in a newly presented manual.

Agriculture · 2026-10-26

Sao Tome and Principe starts snail conservation experiment

12 endemic Archachatina bicarinata snails are being monitored in controlled conditions to boost their dwindling population.

The Principe Foundation and the Principe Natural Park have started an ex situ conservation experiment for the búzio-d’obô (Archachatina bicarinata) snail. This endemic species, found only in Sao Tome and Principe, is listed as threatened by the IUCN. 12 snails were collected under permit and are now housed in controlled conditions at Barracão and Porto Real.

This initiative aims to study the snails' survival, diet, growth, and reproduction, including egg-laying periods and clutch sizes. The experiment, supported by HBD and other partners, seeks to gather information due to the species' declining population and lack of data. If successful, snails born in captivity will be reintroduced into the Principe Natural Park.

The búzio-d’obô is a living treasure of the Sao Tome and Principe forests. Protecting this endemic species is a shared responsibility.
