Sao Tome and Principe to get first neurosurgeon

Elyane Neto, 26, returns to Russia for specialized neurosurgery training to address a critical need.

Sao Tome and Principe will have its first neurosurgeon within three years, identified as 26-year-old Elyane Neto. After seven years of general medical training in Moscow, Neto volunteered for two months at the Ayres de Menezes Central Hospital. She departs this Thursday for Russia to pursue specialized neurosurgery training.

Neto stated her interest in the central nervous system and the brain, calling it a significant challenge and a specialty absent in the country. She noted the lack of adequate care for patients with neurological issues like strokes and head trauma from motorcycle accidents. A group of eight Russian specialists recently assisted the hospital, performing dozens of surgeries.

Neto comes from a family of medical professionals, including her father, older brother, aunt, and sister-in-law. Her two-month voluntary internship highlighted the need for neurosurgical expertise, particularly for young patients suffering from strokes or head injuries. The hospital currently lacks this specialized medical service.

Why it mattersSao Tome and Principe citizens needing neurosurgery will soon have access to local expertise.

Sao Tome president sends condolences for Spain train crash

Carlos Vila Nova expressed sorrow to Pedro Sánchez over the accident that killed around 30 people.

Sao Tome and Principe President Carlos Vila Nova sent condolences to his Spanish counterpart, Pedro Sánchez, on October 29, 2026, following a high-speed train crash.

The accident, which occurred near the Urda station in Toledo, Spain, involved two high-speed trains and resulted in approximately 30 fatalities, according to the presidency's official page.

President Vila Nova conveyed his personal and national sympathy to the families of the victims, lamenting the loss of life in the tragic incident.

Sao Tomean soldiers train in advanced first aid

13 military personnel completed a five-day course on life-saving techniques for combat and civilian emergencies.

13 Sao Tomean soldiers learned advanced first aid techniques during a five-day course taught by Portuguese military health specialists. The training culminated in a simulation exercise involving a simulated ship explosion at Porto de Ana Chaves, where two crew members sustained severe injuries, including facial burns and a fractured leg with arterial bleeding.

The simulated casualties received initial care from four trained crew members before being transported to the hospital in stable condition. Lieutenant-Colonel Rui Alves, chief of the Portuguese mission, stated that the 13 trainees are now equipped to intervene in war scenarios, civilian catastrophes, and other emergencies.

One graduate, Corporal Reginaldo Cravid, expressed confidence in applying his new skills in both military and civilian life. Brigadier-General Virgílio Pontes, Chief of Staff of the Sao Tomean Armed Forces, noted that the training's impact extends beyond the military context.

Why it mattersSao Tomean soldiers are now better prepared to save lives during military operations and civilian emergencies.

Sao Tome and Principe citizens demand public service

Citizens want ministers to prioritize water, electricity, healthcare, and jobs over personal gain.

Following recent elections, citizens of Sao Tome and Principe are calling on new ministers to address their problems instead of engaging in partisan disputes. The public expects ministers to serve the people, not themselves, and to avoid treating public office as personal property or a blank check.

The public demands competence, honesty, transparency, humility, and patriotism from their leaders. They want reliable water and electricity, functional hospitals, adequate schools, and employment opportunities for young people. Ministers are expected to be close to communities, understand family difficulties, and prioritize national interests over personal luxury or expensive vehicles.

Citizens reject arrogance and lavish lifestyles funded by public money while people struggle to survive. They also oppose nepotism and party interests overriding competence in appointments. The focus should be on solving problems with expertise, not just giving speeches, as governing means serving the nation.

Why it mattersCitizens of Sao Tome and Principe are demanding improved public services like water, electricity, healthcare, and job creation from their newly appointed ministers.
