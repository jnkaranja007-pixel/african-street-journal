Gasoline and oil ship arrives Wednesday

A vessel carrying gasoline, jet fuel, and domestic oil is expected to dock Wednesday morning, ending a 15-day fuel shortage.

A ship carrying gasoline, jet fuel, and domestic oil is scheduled to arrive in Sao Tome and Principe on Wednesday around 10 a.m., according to Minister of State for Economy and Finance Gareth Guadalupe. The arrival of the vessel is expected to resolve a gasoline stockout that has lasted over 15 days, significantly impacting taxi drivers, fishermen, and motorcyclists who have been unable to work.

Minister Guadalupe acknowledged the hardship caused by the shortage, stating that governing involves making decisions and accepting responsibility. He assured that the incoming fuel supply is sufficient to last until next year and that the government has a strategy to prevent future shortages. This strategy includes reducing diesel imports, which are currently a major component of the country's electricity generation.

The government is also pursuing solar energy production, aiming to reduce diesel dependency by the end of 2026. The current fuel import has incurred extremely high costs, with over 50% attributed to diesel. The ADI government, led by Américo Ramos, has faced increased public discontent due to the fuel crisis.

Why it mattersTaxi drivers, fishermen, and motorcyclists are affected by the fuel shortage, which prevents them from working.

Police detain 55 protesters in Lisbon housing demonstration

Demonstrators arrested early Tuesday were released and will appear in court Wednesday, as housing protests spread nationwide.

Police arrested 55 people in Lisbon during housing protests early Tuesday, though all have since been released and are scheduled to appear in court on Wednesday. The demonstrations, which began peacefully in Porto over a week ago, have now spread to cities across the country.

In Lisbon, a popular assembly proposed re-establishing tent encampments starting Wednesday. Meanwhile, in Porto, a group that had been camped in Praça D. João I for a week departed without incident after complying with authorities' orders.

The protests, initially focused on housing issues, saw participation from various cities from the north to the south of the country. The arrests in Lisbon mark a significant escalation in the demonstrations.

Why it mattersResidents are affected by housing shortages and the response of authorities to their protests.

Fuel shortage halts water sales in Sao Tome

Residents of Sao Tome city and its outskirts face severe water scarcity as a gasoline shortage paralyzes motorbike-based water delivery services.

The water distribution system installed by EMAE in the peripheral neighborhoods of Sao Tome city has been dry for some time, leaving residents without running water. This has led to a lucrative trade in water, with citizens paying over 1,000 dobras for 1,000 liters transported by motorbike trucks.

However, a recent gasoline shortage has brought this vital business to a standstill. Motorbike trucks are no longer operational, leaving the population frustrated as they cannot access water for cooking, bathing, or drinking. Many motorbike operators, who are young men with families, are now unemployed and unable to provide for their households.

The crisis is widespread, affecting areas like Bobô Fôrro, Água Porca, Penha, and Boa Morte, where piped water has been unavailable for up to three months. Residents in these areas are forced to travel kilometers to obtain water, and the paralysis of motorbike transport means even this option is severely limited.

Why it mattersResidents of Sao Tome city and surrounding areas are affected by the inability to purchase water for daily needs due to a fuel shortage.

UN launches climate resilience initiative in Sao Tome and Principe

The RESIslands project aims to enhance climate risk assessment and investment readiness in the island nation.

The United Nations Economic Commission for Africa (UNECA) has launched the RESIslands initiative in Sao Tome and Principe, a project designed to bolster the nation's preparedness for climate change impacts. This initiative is part of a larger program encompassing eight African countries, with Sao Tome and Principe being a key participant. The project focuses on strengthening climate risk assessments, improving institutional capacity, and preparing for climate-related investments, with a particular emphasis on safeguarding communities and their essential services.

Funded by the Green Climate Fund (GCF) and implemented by UNECA, the RESIslands initiative has a 30-month duration. It provides support to nine member states of the African Island States Climate Commission (AISCC), including Portuguese-speaking nations like Cape Verde, Equatorial Guinea, and Guinea-Bissau. In Sao Tome and Principe, the program will enhance national capabilities for risk evaluation, bolster climate information systems, and assist in preparing investment proposals aligned with the country's specific priorities.

UNECA program manager Kjartan Fjeldsted highlighted the ambition to translate national priorities into tangible actions and sustainable investments. He underscored the importance of protecting the country's natural resources, such as its marine environment, forests, and biodiversity, which form the foundation for sustainable agriculture, fisheries, and tourism. Fjeldsted also acknowledged Sao Tome and Principe's active role in regional cooperation through the AISCC.

Why it mattersSao Tome and Principe's communities and essential services are directly affected by the initiative's focus on improving climate risk assessment and investment readiness.

Trindade FC reaches cup final after beating Guadalupe

Trindade FC secured their place in the São Tomé and Príncipe Cup final by defeating Desportivo de Guadalupe 2-0 in a semi-final match.

Trindade Futebol Clube has become the second finalist for the São Tomé and Príncipe Cup in the São Tomé region. The team qualified by defeating Desportivo de Guadalupe, a second-division side, with a score of 2-0 in a semi-final played at the Estádio Nacional 12 de Julho.

In a balanced match, the scoring was opened in the 13th minute of the first half by forward Peter Ube, who gave Trindade the lead before halftime. The same player scored again in the second half, completing his brace and setting the final score at 2-0, securing his team's qualification for the island of São Tomé's cup final.

Trindade FC will face Inter de Bombom in the final, after Inter de Bombom eliminated Caixão Grande with a 1-0 victory in the other semi-final. Meanwhile, in the Autonomous Region of Príncipe, 1.º de Maio and Operários have already secured their spots in the regional final. The winner of the São Tomé final will then compete in the national grand final against the winner from Príncipe.

Why it mattersFootball fans in São Tomé and Príncipe will watch Trindade FC compete in the cup final.
