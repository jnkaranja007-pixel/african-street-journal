Sao Tome president demands parliamentary support for prime minister nominee

The president gave the MLSTP 24 hours to name a prime minister candidate with sufficient parliamentary backing.

Sao Tome's President has asked the MLSTP party, which won the recent elections without an absolute majority, to nominate a prime minister within 24 hours. The head of state also demanded written proof of sufficient parliamentary support for the nominee to govern. The current government will continue managing public affairs until a new prime minister is appointed.

This move follows the opening of the new legislative session on November 11, 2026, after the September 27 legislative elections. The MLSTP, led by Américo Barros, secured 24 out of 55 seats in the National Assembly. The presidential publication stressed the importance of this process for national interest and political stability.

Nilda Borges da Mata, a former environment minister, was elected President of the National Assembly on November 10, 2026, with 55 votes. The MLSTP has claimed the right to form the next government. The Ação Democrática Independente (ADI) party won 23 seats, while other coalitions secured the remaining mandates.

Why it mattersThe president's demand for parliamentary support directly affects the formation of the new government and the country's political stability.

Nilda da Mata elected Sao Tome and Principe parliament president

The former environment minister secured unanimous support from 55 deputies in the new legislature.

Nilda da Mata, 50, a deputy from the ADI party, became the third woman to lead Sao Tome and Principe's National Assembly on November 11, 2026. She was elected unanimously by the 55 deputies sworn in for the XIII legislature.

Da Mata, a former minister of environment, youth, and sustainable development, stated it was an honour to occupy the same seat as Alda do Espírito Santo, the assembly's first female president. She promised a shared and unifying presidency, aiming for exemplary institutional relations in the country's semi-presidential system.

The National Assembly is the centre of political power in Sao Tome and Principe, with deputies influencing the executive, judicial, and legislative branches. Da Mata, as the second highest state figure, presides over this central sovereign body.

Why it mattersNilda da Mata's election as president of the National Assembly places her as the second highest figure in the state, influencing the country's political direction.

Women win historic representation in Sao Tome and Principe parliament

18 women now sit in the 55-seat National Assembly, the highest number ever.

18 of the 55 deputies in Sao Tome and Principe's new National Assembly are women, marking the largest female representation in the country's history. This increase follows the 2022 Parity Law, which mandates a minimum of 40% for each sex on party candidate lists for elected bodies.

Vera Cravid, the Minister of Justice, Parliamentary Affairs, and Women's Rights, stated that this signifies women's greater involvement in defining societal issues and decisions. Newly elected deputies, such as Dicla Almeida from the ADI parliamentary group, expressed determination to meet public expectations, while Conceição Raposo of the MLSTP group highlighted her commitment to the country's betterment.

Some elected deputies, including Nadiareth Barros from MCI-PS/PUN, plan to address issues like rising violence against women and the plight of homeless individuals with mental health problems. The XIII Legislature's opening also saw a female majority in the National Assembly's leadership, with Nilda da Mata elected president unanimously by all 55 deputies present.

Why it mattersThe increased representation of women in parliament means more focus on issues directly affecting women and the general population.

Sao Tome and Principe president calls for political stability

President Carlos Vila Nova stated that constitutional reform cannot be delayed any further.

President Carlos Vila Nova stated on Tuesday that São Tomé and Príncipe needs political stability to grow and attract investment. He spoke during the investiture ceremony for new deputies in São-Tomé.

Vila Nova emphasized that without stability, there are no plans, no completed projects, and no confidence. He argued that constitutional reform is overdue due to gaps and lack of clarity in the current constitution.

The president also called for reforms in justice, public administration, and electoral law, stressing that approved laws must improve people's lives. He highlighted the importance of the youth, diaspora, and sustainable development, particularly the blue economy.

Why it mattersPresident Vila Nova's call for stability and reform directly impacts citizens, businesses, and the country's future development plans.

São Tomé and Príncipe launches Resilands climate resilience initiative

The Green Climate Fund-financed project will fund early warning systems and disaster risk management, with the UN Economic Commission for Africa carrying out the work.

São Tomé and Príncipe has launched the Resilands initiative, aimed at protecting communities and public works from worsening climate change as a strong El Niño is forecast. The launch came during national consultations held in October, where the country moved forward on strengthening risk management and putting preventive alerts in place.

António Palazuelos, an expert on Small Island Developing States, spoke to UN News at the UN Biodiversity Conference, COP17, in Armenia. He said the initiative is meant to build rapid response capacity for emergencies, and that the minister had called it a state priority. He cited storms, floods and landslides among the threats the project addresses, and said infrastructure must be built to withstand floods and extreme weather.

The project is funded by the Green Climate Fund and executed by the UN Economic Commission for Africa. It brings together nine member countries of the Commission of Island States of Africa for Climate, including Cape Verde, Guinea-Bissau, Equatorial Guinea and São Tomé and Príncipe. It is led by the Ministry of Finance and the Planning Department, working with environment, blue economy, civil protection and foreign affairs bodies, universities and civil society.

Why it mattersInfrastructure and communities in the country are targeted for protection from floods and landslides under the programme.
