Seven Political Forces Confirmed for September 27 Legislative Elections

The Constitutional Court confirmed seven political parties will compete for 55 seats in the National Assembly on September 27.

Seven political forces will compete in the São Tomé and Príncipe legislative elections scheduled for September 27, the Constitutional Court announced Friday. The parties vying for seats are MCI, Nossa Terra, Basta, MLSTP, PCD, MDFM, and ADI, listed in their order on the ballot.

These elections will determine the 55 deputies for the National Assembly, who will subsequently influence the selection of the Prime Minister. The President of the Republic will appoint the Prime Minister after consulting with the parties that secure parliamentary representation.

In addition to the legislative elections, municipal elections for districts on the island of São Tomé and a regional election for the island of Príncipe are also scheduled for September 27.

Why it mattersSeven political forces will compete for seats in the National Assembly, influencing the country's governance.

Sao Tome Customs Space Opens to Modernize Services

A new customs facility at Porto de Ana Chaves aims to streamline goods clearance amid increased diaspora remittances.

Sao Tome and Principe's Minister of State for Economy and Finance, Gareth Guadalupe, inaugurated a new customs space at Porto de Ana Chaves on Thursday, August 27. The facility is designed to improve customer service and expedite the release of consolidated cargo. This development comes as rising remittance flows from the diaspora are placing greater pressure on port services and infrastructure.

This new infrastructure is part of a broader strategy to modernize customs operations, which includes the planned installation of non-intrusive inspection equipment. While the pallet scanner infrastructure is still under construction, the container scanner facility has recently begun construction. The initiative also highlights the need for enhanced coordination between the General Customs Authority, Fiscal Police, and ENAPOR to ensure efficiency and institutional cooperation.

Minister Guadalupe stated that the new space addresses a significant increase in goods movement, largely driven by shipments from Sao Tomeans living abroad. He noted that exempting certain remittances from customs duties has led to a 91% rise in weight and a 71% increase in service fee payments, underscoring the country's reliance on maritime transport for external supplies.

Why it mattersThe new customs space and modernization efforts aim to facilitate the clearance of goods, particularly those sent by emigrants to their families, improving the efficiency of port operations.

Project WACA+ Delivers Two Trucks and Thirty Bins to Capital

New waste management equipment aims to improve sanitation and support tourism in Sao Tome's main district.

The city of Sao Tome received a significant boost for its sanitation efforts with the delivery of two new trucks and 30 waste bins. The handover ceremony took place Friday in front of the Ministry of Environment building. Project WACA+, which funded the acquisition through an international public tender, aims to address key constraints hindering sustainable tourism.

Project coordinator Arlindo Carvalho stated that poor basic sanitation and waste management were identified as major issues affecting tourism. The new equipment is expected to streamline waste collection, particularly in the interior of Agua Grande district and along the city's main roads, where most of the population resides. Agua Grande, as the country's largest district and a primary tourist gateway, must maintain a clean appearance.

Minister of Environment, Youth and Sustainable Tourism Nilda da Mata urged responsible use of the new vehicles and bins by the Agua Grande municipality. She emphasized that tourism, a key sector, cannot thrive without effective daily waste management. This delivery represents the first phase of a larger project that also includes plans for a sanitary landfill.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because improved sanitation and waste management in the capital's main tourist district can enhance the country's image and attract more visitors.

Banana Festival Showcases Fruit's Many Uses

The KITXIBÁ Fest aims to demonstrate the diverse potential of bananas, transforming them into food, crafts, and fertilizer.

The first KITXIBÁ Fest was launched to highlight the versatility of the banana, a staple food in Sao Tome and Principe, under the theme "one product can offer a thousand possibilities." The event showcased how the fruit and the plant itself can be utilized beyond basic consumption, with organizers aiming to promote the banana and its derived products.

Entrepreneurs responded to the festival's challenge by presenting a range of innovations spanning gastronomy and crafts, blending tradition with creativity and new business opportunities. Offerings included banana smoothies, pancakes made with banana flour, dried bananas, and crepes incorporating banana with chia and flax seeds, demonstrating culinary applications.

Beyond food, the festival also featured the transformation of often-discarded parts of the banana plant. Leaves and fibers were turned into utilitarian and decorative items such as rope pictures, napkin holders, and placemat bases. Even banana peels found a new purpose, with an organic compost presented as a fertilizer, turning waste into a valuable resource.

Why it mattersThe KITXIBÁ Fest promotes new business opportunities for entrepreneurs by showcasing innovative uses for bananas and their byproducts.

Media Professionals Trained on Parity Law for Election Coverage

Journalists attended a workshop to understand and apply gender equality legislation in their reporting for upcoming elections.

Media professionals from public and private outlets participated in a workshop focused on the Parity Law, aiming to enhance rigor and equality in election coverage. The event, held at the UN System in São Tomé and Príncipe, specifically addressed the role of media in promoting equal participation of women and men in politics. The goal was to deepen understanding of the legislation, combat gender stereotypes, and prepare journalists for the implementation of parity in the September elections.

The workshop sought to familiarize media professionals with the content and objectives of the parity legislation. It also encouraged reflection on how women and men are portrayed in public and political spheres, particularly in the lead-up to legislative, municipal, and regional elections scheduled for September. The initiative was supported by the National Institute for the Promotion of Equality (INPG) and the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP).

Jailça Lima, Director of the INPG, emphasized the crucial role journalists play in informing, mobilizing society, and strengthening democracy and social justice. She urged media professionals to be free, attentive, and fair in their work, contributing to the deconstruction of prejudices and reduction of gender stereotypes. Lima stated that the quality of public debate and how citizens receive information can significantly impact the collective future.

Why it mattersJournalists are affected by the Parity Law workshop as it trains them to cover elections with greater gender equality and combat stereotypes in their reporting.
