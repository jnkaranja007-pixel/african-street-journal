Sao Tome and Principe bans plastic bags

Law 8/2020 prohibits plastic bag production, import, and sale, with government to create incentives for alternatives.

Sao Tome and Principe banned plastic bags on September 21, 2020, through Law 8/2020. The law, published in the Official Gazette, prohibits the production, import, commercialization, and free distribution of plastic bags.

The legislation aims to curb plastic waste, following similar actions in Cabo Verde and Angola. The government is tasked with creating economic and administrative incentives to encourage private investment in alternative products.

Future revisions of the law could progressively ban other single-use plastic items like cups and cutlery, mirroring Cabo Verde's broader approach. The quality of alternative products must also be verified as per the law's implementation monitoring clause.

Why it mattersThe ban on plastic bags directly affects consumers and businesses involved in their production, import, and sale in Sao Tome and Principe.

Sao Tome and Principe to get first neurosurgeon in 3 years

Elyane Neto, 26, returns to Russia for advanced training in a specialty currently unavailable in the nation.

Sao Tome and Principe's health system will gain its first neurosurgery specialist within three years. Elyane Neto, a 26-year-old doctor trained in Russia, will depart on Thursday for Moscow to pursue this specialization. Neto completed seven years of general medicine training at Moscow University and recently volunteered for two months at the Ayres de Menezes Central Hospital.

Neto stated that neurosurgery is a specialty that the country lacks, noting that many patients do not receive adequate care. She cited an increase in young people with neurological issues like strokes and head trauma from motorcycle accidents as reasons for the demand. Neto comes from a family of medical professionals, including her father, brother, aunt, and sister-in-law.

During two weeks in October, Neto collaborated with a team of eight Russian specialists who provided support in various surgical fields at the Ayres de Menezes Central Hospital. This group operated on dozens of Sao Tomean patients, highlighting a gap in local specialized surgical capacity.

Why it mattersSao Tome and Principe's population will soon have access to specialized neurosurgery care, addressing a critical gap in neurological and trauma treatment.

Sao Tome president sends condolences for Spanish train crash

Carlos Vila Nova expressed sorrow for the 30 deaths in a collision near Toledo.

Sao Tome and Principe President Carlos Vila Nova sent condolences to his Spanish counterpart Pedro Sánchez on October 29, 2026, following a fatal train accident.

The presidential office announced that Vila Nova conveyed his personal sympathy and that of the Sao Tomean people for the tragedy near the Urda station in Toledo, which claimed approximately 30 lives.

The message expressed regret for the loss of life and extended sympathy to the bereaved families, according to a statement from the presidency.

Príncipe residents face rising crime wave

Assaults on homes and businesses are causing concern on the island, with residents reporting stolen goods.

Residents of Príncipe island are experiencing a growing wave of robberies targeting homes and businesses, creating unusual insecurity. Domingas da Conceição reported the loss of phone credit, cash, a mobile phone, and school bags. Arzemiro Cassandra also had his wallet stolen, along with a bag of potatoes from a storage area.

The fear of crime is widespread among traders and residents, who note a change from the island's historically safe past. Merchant António Barros recalled a time when valuables could be left unattended, contrasting it with the present situation. Residents suggest that nighttime power cuts, due to energy shortages, are enabling criminals by leaving areas in darkness.

Arzemiro Cassandra also stated that the island is becoming a refuge for individuals fleeing crimes committed in São Tomé. Security forces are being called upon to increase patrols and provide a more effective response to the escalating situation.

Why it mattersResidents on Príncipe island are experiencing increased insecurity due to a rise in robberies.

Sao Tome and Principe, Angola sign higher education quality deal

The agreement between ANRES and INAAREES will allow for the exchange of external evaluators and accreditation procedures.

Sao Tome and Principe's National Agency for Higher Education Regulation (ANRES) and Angola's National Institute for Evaluation, Accreditation and Recognition of Higher Education (INAAREES) signed a cooperation protocol in Luanda on October 29, 2026. The agreement aims to strengthen the quality assurance systems for higher education in both nations. ANRES President Sónia dos Reis Magalhães and INAAREES Director-General Jesus António Tomé signed the protocol.

This partnership will facilitate the exchange of external evaluators and the sharing of accreditation procedures between the two countries. It also covers the harmonization of academic equivalence processes and technical capacity building. A bilateral technical committee with up to six members will be formed to develop biannual action plans and monitor goals, meeting annually in Luanda and Sao Tome.

The protocol takes immediate effect and will last for an initial five-year period, with options for renewal. This accord is designed to align academic excellence policies within the Portuguese-speaking Community (CPLP).

Why it mattersStudents and academics in Sao Tome and Principe will benefit from harmonized academic recognition and improved quality assurance in higher education.
