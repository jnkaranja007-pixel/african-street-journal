Sao Tome and Principe culture showcased at Portugal festival

The Artes à Solta festival in Coimbra, Portugal, will feature traditional theatre, dance, and music from the island nation.

The Artes à Solta festival in Coimbra, Portugal, will highlight the culture and heritage of Sao Tome and Principe from November 13 to 15. The event will include traditional Sao Tomean theatre, known as tchiloli, which is recognized by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage, and the popular dance bulawê. The festival, in its second edition, previously focused on Cape Verde.

Diogo Coelho, executive director of Golden Skill, the company organizing the event, stated that conferences will cover Sao Tome and Principe's historic plantations called roças and its biosphere reserve. Santomean researcher and musician Hector Costa will present on tchiloli, followed by a concert described as an 'acoustic journey' through the archipelago's sounds. The festival is a partnership with the Sao Tome and Principe Embassy in Portugal.

Esterline Gonçalves Género, the Sao Tome and Principe ambassador to Portugal, attended the press conference and noted the festival's role in promoting the country's culture and heritage. The festival program, which is free to attend, is still being finalized and will be announced on the event's social media channels soon.

Why it mattersThe festival in Coimbra will present Sao Tome and Principe's cultural heritage, including UNESCO-recognized tchiloli theatre, to an international audience.

OMS pledges continued support for Sao Tome and Principe health programs

The World Health Organization will bolster national capacity for disease control and health emergency prevention.

The World Health Organization (OMS) will continue to support Sao Tome and Principe in combating malaria, tuberculosis, and other diseases, while also strengthening national capabilities for preventing and responding to health emergencies. This commitment was affirmed by the new OMS representative, Marjorie Mupandaré, after presenting her credentials to the Minister of State for Foreign Affairs, Ilza Amado Vaz.

Mupandaré stated that Sao Tome and Principe is on a positive trajectory with its national public health program, noting the country's positive evolution in implementation. She assured ongoing technical support and close collaboration with national authorities to address key sector challenges, particularly in public health and health emergency management. The OMS representative also met with the Minister of Health, Celso Matos, to discuss the country's health situation and cooperation priorities.

Key concerns include reinforcing epidemiological surveillance at national entry and exit points, especially given the threat of serious diseases like Ebola in other African countries. The OMS plans to help the country enhance its airport surveillance and improve the skills of health professionals in Sao Tome and Principe.

Sao Tome health system deteriorates

Health minister admits system regressed over the last decade but points to recent improvements.

Sao Tome and Principe's health system has been ranked the second worst in Africa for deterioration over the past decade, according to the Ibrahim Index of African Governance. Health Minister Celso Matos acknowledged the poor results for the period 2016 to 2025, stating that while some health parameters have regressed, others have improved.

Matos, who took office two years ago, emphasized that the reported deterioration does not reflect his current government's performance, noting that the worst decline occurred in 2024, with stabilization in 2025. He highlighted progress in combating malaria, with cases dropping from over 7,000 in 2024 to four to 5,000 in 2025, and cited good results in controlling communicable diseases like HIV.

Despite improvements in primary healthcare access and efforts to increase doctors in districts, the minister admitted that non-communicable diseases like cardiovascular accidents, diabetes, and high blood pressure are a serious problem. He stated the country lacks sufficient funds to provide the necessary quality of care for these costly conditions.

Why it mattersThe health minister admitted that Sao Tome and Principe's health system has deteriorated over the last decade, impacting the quality of care available to citizens.

Sao Tome court to launch digital accounting system in 2027

The Tribunal de Contas will move to digital submissions for public entities and companies by April 30, 2027.

The Tribunal de Contas in Sao Tome and Principe will introduce an electronic system in 2027 for public entities and companies to submit their accounts digitally. This move away from paper submissions was announced by the court's president, Ricardino Costa Alegre.

Costa Alegre informed the press that a new instruction will be issued for entities to submit their accounts in a digital format to the Tribunal de Contas. He stated the tool is in its final stages and expects it to be operational by April 30, 2027, after the March 31 deadline for submissions.

The Sao Tomean Tribunal de Contas, established in June 2003, functions as the supreme and independent body for controlling public revenue and expenditure, judging state accounts and conducting preventive oversight of public contracts.

Central bank bans ASDIS micro-finance activities

The Central Bank of Sao Tome and Principe has ordered ASDIS – Cooperativa de Crédito to cease all micro-finance operations.

The Central Bank of Sao Tome and Principe has announced that ASDIS – Cooperativa de Crédito is not authorized to conduct micro-finance activities in the country. The bank issued a statement on April 11, 2026, informing the public and economic agents of the decision.

According to the Central Bank's statement, ASDIS – Cooperativa de Crédito, located at Edifício HB, Travessa do Pelourinho, lacks the necessary authorization to operate. The law requires prior approval from the Central Bank for any entity engaging in micro-finance.

To protect citizens, the Central Bank urges the public to avoid contractual relationships or new micro-finance operations with ASDIS. It recommends verifying an entity's authorization status before engaging financial services.

Why it mattersCitizens and economic agents in Sao Tome and Principe are directly affected as they are warned against conducting financial operations with an unauthorized micro-finance entity.
