Sao Tome and Principe elects 18 women to parliament

The number of female deputies in the National Assembly has more than doubled since 2022.

18 women won seats in the 2026 legislative elections in Sao Tome and Principe, more than double the eight elected in 2022. These new deputies represent 32.7% of the 55 members of the National Assembly. The final results were validated by the Constitutional Court on October 6, 2026.

The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) reported the figures, highlighting the increase in women's political participation. Among the elected are Adjoa Soares, Arminda Candô, and Kátia Ramos, representing various parties and constituencies across the islands. The UNDP noted that gender parity has not yet been achieved.

The organization stressed the need to ensure women can fully exercise their mandates and take on leadership roles. The list of elected women includes representatives from MLSTP, MCI-PS/PUN, ADI, and Movimento Basta parties.

Why it matters18 women have been elected to the National Assembly, increasing female representation in the country's parliament.

Sao Tome and Principe records second-worst health indicator decline

The nation's health system faces infrastructure, staffing, and rising non-communicable disease challenges.

São Tomé e Príncipe registered the second-largest decline in health indicators in Africa between 2016 and 2025, according to the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance. This deterioration contrasts with the continental trend, where 40 out of 54 African nations improved their health systems during the same period.

Health Minister Celso Matos acknowledged the decline, stating the signs were long evident. He cited fragile infrastructure, a shortage of health professionals, and an increase in non-communicable diseases like strokes and diabetes as major issues. Financial limitations further hinder adequate care provision.

The minister highlighted that challenges including infrastructure, equipment, medicines, endemic malaria, and financing have been identified for years. Mobilizing resources to address these issues, such as improving hospital response capacity and managing the epidemiological profile, is now urgent.

Why it mattersSão Tomé e Príncipe's health system is deteriorating, affecting the population's access to adequate care and treatment for rising non-communicable diseases.

Elackcio Afonso elected Cantagalo district president

The new president pledged rigorous management, internal controls, and accountability for the district.

Elackcio Afonso was sworn in on Wednesday as the new President of the Cantagalo District Council. He promised to transform the district into a model of perfect municipal functioning through rigorous management, internal controls, and accountability.

Afonso, who led the MLSTP list to victory in the September 27 local elections, stated his commitment to building a better future for Cantagalo. He announced plans to immediately mobilize resources to restore waste collection, repair vehicles, and implement effective cleaning plans in coordination with communities.

The new president also outlined a strategic combination of agriculture, fishing, culture, tourism, and hospitality as means to generate structural resources for the district's economy. He aims for transparent governance under his new administration.
