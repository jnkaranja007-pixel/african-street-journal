Sao Tome and Principe parliament adds 11 women lawmakers

Women now hold 34.5% of seats in the National Assembly following the 2026 legislative elections.

Following the 2026 legislative elections, Sao Tome and Principe's National Assembly will have 19 women among its 55 members, an increase from eight in the previous term. This change raises the proportion of seats held by women from 14.5% to 34.5%. The shift is attributed to the country's 2022 Parity Law, which mandates a minimum 40% representation for each sex on candidate lists.

The Parity Law's implementation was supported by a "Parity Law Monitoring Commission" that reviewed candidate lists and encouraged necessary adjustments. Electoral and judicial authorities, political parties, civil society groups, and women in public life all played roles. Over 72 women from seven political parties and five districts received training in political communication and negotiation.

A network of over 105 individuals, including parliamentarians, party members, and journalists, publicly advocated for parity. The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), with financial support from the Japanese government, assisted national institutions and stakeholders throughout the 2026 electoral cycle in applying the new legal framework.

Sao Tome and Principe team trains in Portugal for clinical trials

Five health professionals will gain practical experience in Portugal to develop clinical trial capabilities at home.

Five health professionals from Sao Tome and Principe are in Portugal for practical training in clinical trials. The training, from November 8 to 14, aims to prepare the nation's institutions for evaluating, regulating, and investigating clinical studies. This initiative is part of the CT-Luso project, bringing together 30 professionals from Portuguese-speaking African countries.

The São Toméan team includes specialists from the Ministry of Health's Pharmaceutical Department, the Health Ethics Commission for Scientific Research, and the National Center for Endemias. They are working with Portuguese authorities, ethics committees, and research centers to learn procedures for assessing, authorizing, and monitoring clinical trials. This knowledge will be adapted for Sao Tome and Principe's national research framework.

This hands-on training follows two previous CT-Luso programs that certified 234 professionals on ethical and regulatory foundations and 87 on Good Clinical Practices. The current stage focuses on practical application through case analysis and direct engagement with host institutions in Lisbon, Setúbal, and Porto. Recommendations from the project suggest Sao Tome and Principe's draft legislation is robust but needs a specific decree on clinical research.

Why it mattersFive health professionals from Sao Tome and Principe are receiving training to enhance the country's capacity for conducting and regulating clinical trials.

Sport · 2026-11-10

6 de Setembro wins Sao Tome island football title

The military team defeated Desportivo Ké Morabeza 2-0 to secure a spot in the national championship final.

6 de Setembro clinched the Sao Tome island football championship title by beating Desportivo Ké Morabeza 2-0 in the final match of the competition. This victory guarantees the military formation a place in the national championship final, where they will face 1.º de Maio, the champion from the island of Principe.

Despite the win, the final whistle did not spark widespread celebrations as the team's focus is already on the upcoming national final. "Our main objective is the finalissima. That's why we will save the party for the end," explained assistant coach Nilton Tomé, stating it would be premature to celebrate extensively with a significant obstacle still ahead.

Desportivo Ké Morabeza, already relegated to the second division, put up a fight, but 6 de Setembro secured their win with second-half goals. Castro scored the opener assisted by captain Tinho, and Agro added the second in the final minutes. 6 de Setembro finished the island league with 49 points.

Sao Tome and Principe schools face AI learning challenge

Artificial intelligence evolution outpaces educational and professional systems, demanding critical thinking over passive consumption.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly integrating into daily life, evolving at a pace that challenges educational and professional systems in Sao Tome and Principe to keep up. This transformation requires clear pedagogical and professional focus, urging the nation to confront this change with attention.

While AI can generate fluent and assertive responses, these are not always correct, posing a risk if trusted blindly. Andreas Schleicher, Director of Education and Skills at the OECD, noted a concerning correlation, not necessarily direct causality, between AI advancement and a decline in complex reading skills. However, AI can also be a tool to encourage sophisticated questions and free teachers from routine tasks, aiding in more precise diagnostics.

The future of education in Sao Tome and Principe will increasingly automate easily taught and tested subjects, elevating the importance of ethical decision-making, human relationships, and creativity. Students must become active learners, and educators will shift to roles as mentors and intellectual catalysts, with AI serving as a cognitive amplifier rather than a replacement for human reasoning.

Why it mattersStudents and educators in Sao Tome and Principe must adapt to AI's rapid evolution, which risks diminishing critical thinking and complex reading skills while demanding new pedagogical approaches.

Sao Tome youth council demands effective public policies

The National Youth Council seeks real opportunities, training, jobs, and housing, citing emigration as a necessity.

The Sao Tome and Principe National Youth Council (CNJ) on Thursday demanded "effective public policies" to create "real opportunities" for young people, including more training, employment, and housing. The organisation stated that many young people view emigration as a necessity due to a lack of prospects.

In a statement sent to Lusa, the CNJ appealed to the State's responsibility, stating that the youth need results, not repeated promises. They called for effective public policies, employment programs, vocational training aligned with market needs, support for entrepreneurship, access to credit, affordable housing, and effective participation in decision-making processes.

The CNJ also highlighted that investing in youth is the most important investment a country can make and urged development partners to continue supporting initiatives that promote youth opportunities, inclusion, and capacity building. The council noted that many young people lack employment, dignified opportunities, and the conditions to realise their life projects, leading to a growing emigration phenomenon that concerns the nation.

Why it mattersYoung people in Sao Tome and Principe face a lack of opportunities, leading many to consider emigration as a necessity.
