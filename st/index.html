Sao Tome moto-taxis resume after fuel shortage

Transport workers in Sao Tome and Principe restart operations after a two-week halt due to a gasoline stock rupture.

Moto-taxis, the primary transport for people and goods in Sao Tome and Principe, resumed services on October 23, 2026, after a 15-day halt caused by a gasoline stock rupture.

Motorcycle taxi driver João Paulo de Moura reported resuming activities with significant debt from missed weekly payments, stating he owed 2,000 dobras. Taxi driver Cabral Lima noted October 22 was his 'day zero' for service, having used his car's fuel money for food.

Both taxi and moto-taxi drivers highlighted that the gasoline's return does not solve all issues, pointing to the urgent need for government formation following the September 27 legislative elections.

Why it mattersTransport workers in Sao Tome and Principe faced financial hardship and operational halts due to a gasoline shortage.

Sao Tome gasoline stations restock fuel after two-week shortage

Gasoline and domestic kerosene are now available at all stations following the arrival of a tanker.

Gasoline and domestic kerosene are now on sale at filling stations across Sao Tome and Principe, ending a nearly two-week stockout of these products. Fuel became available on the morning of October 22, 2026, after a tanker discharged its cargo at the port in Lembá district on Wednesday night.

The sale of fuel resumed 24 hours after Gareth Guadalupe, the Minister of State for Economy and Finance, expressed regret over the shortages and their impact on the population. Guadalupe stated that over 50% of the cost of the recent import was for diesel, as a large part of the nation's energy matrix still relies on it.

Guadalupe also mentioned plans to mitigate the crisis by introducing 10 to 11 megawatts of solar energy by the end of the year and developing a factory to produce gas in Sao Tome and Principe for domestic use, transitioning away from petroleum.

Why it mattersResidents of Sao Tome and Principe can now purchase gasoline and domestic kerosene after a two-week shortage.

Project teaches 80% of Sao Tome and Principe to swim

Rita Fernandes' "Sao Tome and Principe a Nadar" project trains local instructors and teaches swimming skills to islanders.

The "Sao Tome and Principe a Nadar" project is teaching islanders to swim, aiming to equip over 80% of the population with this life skill. Rita Fernandes, a former professional swimmer, founded the initiative three years ago after observing the lack of swimming proficiency in the island nation where fishing is a significant activity.

Fernandes, who has a background in Sociology and Sports Management, partnered with local teacher Angélico Santos, who had recently established the Sao Tome and Principe Swimming Federation. The project addresses the absence of public swimming pools and the lack of swimming in the official education system, despite children's proximity to the sea.

The initiative has evolved into a structured cooperation with local and Portuguese institutions, including the Portuguese Association of Swimming Technicians (APTN). Through donations and partnerships, the project has provided training materials and conducted online and in-person training sessions, even sending young swimmers like José Dias and Tiago Pires to Portugal for pool training.

Why it mattersThe project directly impacts the population of Sao Tome and Principe by teaching them a vital life skill and improving water safety.

Sao Tome government seeks micro-enterprise expert

The Project Implementation Unit will receive support from a consultant to boost micro, small, and medium enterprises.

The Government of Sao Tome and Principe (GoSTP) has secured a grant from the International Development Association (IDA) to fund the Sao Tome and Principe Youth Employment Opportunities Project (POEJ). A portion of this grant will be used to hire a consultant specializing in micro, small, and medium enterprises.

This expert will support the Project Implementation Unit, aiming to enhance opportunities for small businesses within the country. The IDA grant is intended to cover the costs associated with this consultancy service.

The specific details of the consultant's role and the project's timeline for implementation are managed by the Project Implementation Unit.

Why it mattersThe government is hiring a consultant to support micro, small, and medium enterprises.
