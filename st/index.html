Sao Tome and Principe schools get solar power for meals

The World Food Programme installed solar energy and kitchen equipment in 15 schools to improve school feeding.

15 schools in Sao Tome and Principe started the new school year with solar-powered kitchens and modern equipment. The World Food Programme (WFP) initiative aims to improve food preparation and conservation in school canteens.

The project, called 'Green Infrastructure for Health and Education', directly benefits 4,075 people, including 4,000 students and 75 school staff. Solar panels, electric pressure cookers, and refrigerators were installed to enhance food management and efficiency.

School staff received training to strengthen canteen management. The WFP also supported the government with nutrition initiatives and participated in validating a national dietary guide and closing the COMPRAN Project.

Why it mattersStudents in 15 Sao Tome and Principe schools will have better-prepared and conserved meals due to new solar-powered kitchen facilities.

Sao Tome gasoline stations restock fuel after two-week shortage

Gasoline and domestic kerosene are now available at all stations following the arrival of a tanker.

Gasoline and domestic kerosene are now on sale at filling stations across Sao Tome and Principe, ending a nearly two-week stockout of these products. Fuel became available on the morning of October 22, 2026, after a tanker discharged its cargo at the port in Lembá district on Wednesday night.

The sale of fuel resumed 24 hours after Gareth Guadalupe, the Minister of State for Economy and Finance, expressed regret over the shortages and their impact on the population. Guadalupe stated that over 50% of the cost of the recent import was for diesel, as a large part of the nation's energy matrix still relies on it.

Guadalupe also mentioned plans to mitigate the crisis by introducing 10 to 11 megawatts of solar energy by the end of the year and developing a factory to produce gas in Sao Tome and Principe for domestic use, transitioning away from petroleum.

Why it mattersResidents of Sao Tome and Principe can now purchase gasoline and domestic kerosene after a two-week shortage.

Sao Tome and Principe government seeks communications advisor

The government is hiring a communications assistant for a youth employment project funded by the International Development Association.

The Government of Sao Tome and Principe (GoSTP) is seeking to recruit a communications assistant consultant for the Youth Employment Opportunities Project (POEJ). This recruitment is funded by a grant from the International Development Association (IDA).

The IDA grant will cover project costs for the POEJ, which aims to create employment opportunities for young people. The consultant will support communication efforts related to this initiative. Other related procurements include vehicles for the Directorate of Technical and Vocational Education and Training and a specialist consultant for micro, small, and medium enterprises.

The Project SIAS also announced an extension for applications for an agro-economy technician. These recruitments and extensions indicate efforts to develop various sectors within the country.

Why it mattersThe government is hiring a communications assistant for a youth employment project, impacting young people seeking jobs.

Sao Tome and Principe on alert for sheep and goat disease

A national contingency plan is being developed to prevent the spread of Peste des Petits Ruminants.

Sao Tome and Principe is on health alert due to the spread of Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR) in other countries, including those in Central and West Africa. The disease, which devastates goat and sheep farming, has not yet appeared in the archipelago, but officials acknowledge risk factors requiring constant vigilance.

Alfredo da Mata, a veterinary doctor and head of the Animal Health Department, stated that PPR significantly affects small ruminant production globally. He confirmed that while Sao Tome and Principe remains free of the disease, the country faces risks that could facilitate its introduction. The African Union is supporting the creation of a National Contingency Plan to outline surveillance, prevention, control, and response mechanisms for potential outbreaks.

Minister of Agriculture, Fisheries, and Rural Development, Nilton Garrido, emphasized that prevention involves strengthening surveillance systems and training national veterinary services. A three-day workshop is currently underway with national and international specialists to define strategies for preventing, preparing for, and controlling this serious disease affecting small ruminants, which is considered vital for the rural economy and food security.

Why it mattersSao Tome and Principe's animal health and rural economy are at risk from the potential introduction of Peste des Petits Ruminants.
