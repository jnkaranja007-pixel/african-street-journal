China donates scanning technology to Sao Tome and Principe

New 3D imaging scanners, the first of their kind in Africa, will enhance customs security at the port and airport.

Sources: Tela Non and Jornal Tropical · 2026-11-09 · 1 min read

Sao Tome and Principe has received advanced non-intrusive inspection equipment from China, a donation valued at seven million euros. The technology, including a container scanner for the Port of Ana Chaves and two baggage scanners for Nuno Xavier International Airport, is set to become operational on December 1, 2026. This upgrade aims to modernize customs services and bolster national security.

The new systems, model NUCTECH WooKong H, use high-resolution 3D imaging to analyze cargo and luggage with greater precision, reducing reliance on physical inspections. Chinese specialists are installing the equipment and training local customs and airport officials on its operation. Chinese Ambassador Xu Yingzhen stated the scanners are pioneering in Africa and will aid in combating smuggling and improving efficiency.

Minister of State for Economy and Finance Gareth Guadalupe highlighted that Sao Tome and Principe has never before possessed scanners with this capability. He noted the equipment will improve inspection procedures, offer greater passenger convenience, and help mitigate transnational crime, potentially improving the country's standing in the Mo Ibrahim Index. The Chinese cooperation also includes long-term maintenance support for the systems.

Why it mattersSao Tome and Principe's customs and border security will be significantly enhanced by advanced scanning technology, improving efficiency and national safety.
