Sao Tome health system deteriorates

Health minister admits system regressed over the last decade but points to recent improvements.

Source: Jornal Tropical · 2026-11-05 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Sao Tome and Principe's health system has been ranked the second worst in Africa for deterioration over the past decade, according to the Ibrahim Index of African Governance. Health Minister Celso Matos acknowledged the poor results for the period 2016 to 2025, stating that while some health parameters have regressed, others have improved.

Matos, who took office two years ago, emphasized that the reported deterioration does not reflect his current government's performance, noting that the worst decline occurred in 2024, with stabilization in 2025. He highlighted progress in combating malaria, with cases dropping from over 7,000 in 2024 to four to 5,000 in 2025, and cited good results in controlling communicable diseases like HIV.

Despite improvements in primary healthcare access and efforts to increase doctors in districts, the minister admitted that non-communicable diseases like cardiovascular accidents, diabetes, and high blood pressure are a serious problem. He stated the country lacks sufficient funds to provide the necessary quality of care for these costly conditions.

Why it mattersThe health minister admitted that Sao Tome and Principe's health system has deteriorated over the last decade, impacting the quality of care available to citizens.
