Sao Tome's finance minister leaves tax recovery against SOLIVAN unresolved

Gareth Guadalupe ordered the seizure of SOLIVAN's assets in March 2026 as the state sought to recover money owed over the Rosema brewery's tax arrears.

Source: Tela Non · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Gareth Guadalupe, Sao Tome and Principe's outgoing minister of state for the economy and finance, is leaving office after ordering the seizure of assets belonging to SOLIVAN, a private company, in March 2026. The move was aimed at recovering money the state said was owed by the Rosema brewery.

Guadalupe told the country's parliament that the seizure targeted SOLIVAN, not the brewery itself, so that the state would be repaid whichever taxpayer owed it. He had earlier accused the company's operators of evading tax on sums the report describes as millions of euros.

The report does not name a successor or say what will happen to the recovery action now that Guadalupe is leaving office.
