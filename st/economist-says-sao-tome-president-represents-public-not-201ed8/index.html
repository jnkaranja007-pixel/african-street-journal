Economist says São Tomé president represents public, not personal, interests

In an essay, the economist says scholarships are public support, not gifts from a president, and state assets belong to the collective.

Source: Tela Non · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

An economist writing as Não Acidental has argued that São Tomé and Príncipe’s president represents the public interest and is not the owner of state property. The essay, published by Tela Non, addresses claims that the country’s first president personally gave people scholarships to study abroad.

The writer says many São Toméans have publicly thanked the former president for scholarships that enabled them to study in Asia, Europe and Africa. The essay argues that public assets and support belong to the collective, and that the president’s role is to represent all citizens under the constitution.

The article is the first in a series explaining what the economist calls confusion between private property and public goods. It says the president has the highest responsibility for managing state assets.

Why it mattersPeople who receive public scholarships should understand them as collective support, rather than personal gifts from the president, the economist argues.
