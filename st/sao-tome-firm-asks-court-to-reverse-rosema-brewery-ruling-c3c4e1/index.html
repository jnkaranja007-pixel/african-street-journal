São Tomé firm asks court to reverse Rosema brewery ruling

The request seeks to undo the Constitutional Court ruling that restored possession, management and control of the brewery to Angola's private group RIDUX.

Source: Tela Non · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Owners of SOLIVAN, a São Tomé and Príncipe company that has fought for control of the Rosema brewery for 20 years, filed a request on 24 November with the Constitutional Court to cancel the ruling that returned the factory to RIDUX, a private Angolan company. The filing came just after the new government took office.

SOLIVAN's lawyer confirmed to the Téla Nón news outlet that the request had been submitted. The company wants the court to reverse its earlier judgment, which restored possession, management and control of the brewery to RIDUX.

The dispute has shaped political and court decisions for two decades, according to Téla Nón. SOLIVAN's lawyer said the company would make a public statement on the case at a later date.

Why it mattersThe Constitutional Court must now rule on whether to overturn the decision that gave RIDUX control of the brewery.
