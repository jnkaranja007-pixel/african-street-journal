At least 18 migrants drown off Comoros en route to Mayotte

At least 18 African refugees drowned after smugglers dropped them into the water off Comoros' Mitsamiouli coast in March 2026 while they tried to reach the French island of Mayotte, officials and rescuers said. About 30 others survived; many victims could not swim and had traveled from the Democratic Republic of Congo. The coastguard continued searching for four missing bodies, according to the interior minister.

Why it mattersUnderscores the deadly toll of the Comoros-Mayotte migration route that keeps drawing thousands of Africans despite repeated tragedies.

Police dismantle migrant-smuggling ring running Comoros-Mayotte crossings

French border police investigators broke up a large network organizing clandestine kwassa-kwassa boat crossings between the Comoros and Mayotte in a March 2026 operation. The organization dropped passengers on the islet of M'tsamboro before ferrying them onward, charging migrants multiple payments; investigators said the ring generated more than 210,000 euros in revenue.

Why it mattersSignals tighter law-enforcement pressure on the smuggling economy that fuels dangerous sea crossings from Comoros.

Comoros economy set to grow 3.4% in 2025 on services, easing inflation

Comoros' GDP growth is projected at 3.4% in 2025, edging up from 3.3% in 2024, driven mainly by the services sector supported by commerce and easing inflation, according to the World Bank. Industry is expected to expand 3.3%, led by construction in transport, tourism and health, while agriculture, the largest employer of the poor, grows a modest 2.9%.

Why it mattersModest growth signals limited fiscal room to cushion households from the fuel-price shocks now roiling the archipelago.
