Diaspora remittances exceed 20% of Comoros GDP

Money sent from abroad now represents over a fifth of the nation's total wealth produced annually.

Money sent from abroad by the Comorian diaspora now accounts for over 20% of the nation's gross domestic product, according to figures from the Central Bank of Comoros. While this does not mean the diaspora directly produces this portion of wealth, the sums transferred significantly bolster household consumption and economic activity. This represents a dramatic increase from 2000, when diaspora remittances were only about 3.7% of GDP.

Historical data from the World Bank illustrates this rapid growth, with remittances reaching 6.2% of GDP in 2005 and approximately 9.4% in 2010. By 2023, these transfers had climbed to around 23% of GDP, demonstrating a multiplication of their economic weight over two decades. This trend highlights the growing reliance on the diaspora as a pillar of the Comorian economy.

These substantial financial inflows are crucial for thousands of families, funding essential needs such as food, housing, healthcare, and children's education, as well as construction and other household expenses. The economic growth forecast for 2025 stands at 3.8%, a slight increase from 3.3% in 2024, alongside a slowdown in inflation.

Why it mattersThe diaspora's financial contributions are essential for household consumption and economic activity in Comoros.

Comoros Receives UNESCO Certificate for Historic Medinas

The official UNESCO certificate for the Comorian medinas was presented to President Azali Assoumani, marking a new phase of protection and conservation.

The Comorian medinas have officially received their UNESCO World Heritage certificate, presented on August 29 in Iconi. This recognition follows their historic inscription two months prior and was awarded to President Azali Assoumani during a ceremony blending popular enthusiasm with republican solemnity.

The certificate formalizes a decision made in June by the World Heritage Committee in Busan, South Korea, acknowledging years of collaborative work by the state, local authorities, heritage specialists, and residents. The medinas of Iconi, Itsandra, Moroni, and Ntsoudjini are the first Comorian sites to be listed.

UNESCO representative Louis Haxthausen emphasized that the certificate signifies a commitment to the protection, conservation, and transmission of the medinas' universal value, including their coral stone architecture and living community traditions. The Comorian Ambassador to UNESCO, Mohamed Bajrafil, echoed this, stating that preserving these historic sites is now a commitment to humanity.

Why it mattersThe Comorian medinas are now a UNESCO World Heritage site, requiring collective responsibility for their preservation and sustainable development, including tourism.
