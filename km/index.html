Reunion court orders Comorian soldier's expulsion after asylum denial

A Comorian soldier, one of seven who deserted during military exercises in March, faces expulsion from La Réunion after his asylum request was rejected, maintaining his return.

A Comorian soldier will be expelled to the Comoros after a La Réunion administrative court upheld a judgment to return him, nearly four months after he deserted during the Papangue 2026 inter-army exercise. The soldier, born in 1997, arrived on the island on March twenty-second with a Comorian delegation for maneuvers and disappeared on March twenty-fourth. Seven soldiers deserted in total, with two found by the end of March, one of whom was already expelled to Moroni.

The soldier was apprehended on July twenty-second during an identity check and requested asylum on July twenty-fourth. His request was denied by the Ofpra on August sixth, followed by a prefectural order for his removal a week later. Before the judges, he claimed fear of an "extrajudicial execution" and stated he fled "exactions" by the army against the population, alleging he had information about a former colleague's assassination and another's disappearance.

The magistrates found his claims insufficiently documented to prove a personal and present danger, noting no appeal had been filed with the National Court of Asylum by the judgment date. The expulsion order remains in effect, and if returned, the soldier faces potential arrest upon arrival in the Comoros, where authorities have previously mentioned severe sanctions, including imprisonment and dismissal, for deserters.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects the soldier, who faces potential arrest and severe sanctions, including imprisonment and dismissal, upon his return to the Comoros.

Mvola users credit Sonelec meters without payment, causing 200 million franc loss

A malfunction between Mvola mobile payments and Sonelec prepaid electricity meters in Mohéli allowed multiple subscribers to credit their accounts without corresponding.

A payment system malfunction involving Mvola mobile payments and Sonelec prepaid electricity meters was observed in Mohéli on Tuesday, August eighteen. Several subscribers reportedly managed to credit their electricity meters without the corresponding amounts being debited from their Mvola accounts, according to Comores Infos.

The incident occurred hours after the restoration of Yas's money transfer service, which had been interrupted for maintenance. A source close to the matter estimates the potential financial damage at over 200 million Comorian francs. This source claims some users made multiple transactions, with one meter reportedly credited with more than nine million francs of electricity.

Neither Sonelec nor Yas had issued official statements regarding the incident as of the report's writing. The companies have not confirmed the figures or addressed how many meters are affected, whether the credits will be canceled, or if a technical investigation has been launched, leaving questions for subscribers and stakeholders.

Why it mattersSonelec customers in Mohéli may have received electricity credits without payment, potentially leading to future service adjustments or investigations for those involved.
