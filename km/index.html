Comoros Medinas Receive UNESCO World Heritage Certificates

Six historic Comorian medinas, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list on July 25, 2026, were officially celebrated on August 29, 2026.

The official certificates recognizing six historic Comorian medinas as UNESCO World Heritage sites were presented on Saturday, August 29, 2026, in Ikoni. The sites, including Moroni, Ikoni, Itsandra, Ntsudjini, Domoni, and Mutsamudu, were added to the prestigious list on July 25, 2026, marking the first Comorian property to achieve this status. The certificate was handed to President Azali Assoumani by Louise Haxthausen, director of the UNESCO regional office.

This international recognition celebrates ancient cities shaped by centuries of history, migration, and trade, originating from the era of sultanates. These medinas are not merely historical relics but are living communities, continuously inhabited and passed down through generations. The event in Ikoni, held at Bichioni Square, formally acknowledged this enduring heritage.

The inscription signifies a major cultural achievement for the Comoros, highlighting the unique historical and architectural value of these urban centers. The ongoing habitation and transmission of traditions within these sites underscore their living heritage status, a key factor in their UNESCO designation.

Why it mattersSix historic Comorian medinas are affected by this UNESCO recognition, which celebrates their cultural and historical significance as inhabited sites.

Journalist receives death threat from governor's brother

Mohéli governor's brother allegedly threatened journalist Oubeid Mchangama with death for interviewing opposition figures.

Journalist Oubeid Mchangama reported receiving death threats from Belayid, the brother of Mohéli Governor Chamina Ben Mohamed. Belayid allegedly warned Mchangama not to return to the island, stating he would be a "dead man" if he did. The threats reportedly followed Mchangama's interviews with both opposition and government supporters during a recent trip to Mohéli.

Mchangama stated he interviewed various political actors without bias, fulfilling his journalistic duty to present different viewpoints. He shared his account publicly, highlighting the incident as a new challenge to press freedom in the Comoros. The journalist asserted that no criticism, however inconvenient for authorities or their associates, justifies a death threat.

This incident occurs within a context where individuals close to political leaders are frequently accused of intimidating journalists, often with a perceived sense of informal immunity. Such an environment is reported to foster self-censorship and weaken democratic institutions within the Comoros.

Why it mattersJournalists and their ability to report freely are directly affected by threats that can lead to self-censorship and a weakened democracy.
