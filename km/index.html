Mayotte Deputy Asks Paris to Halt Comoros Aid

Estelle Youssouffa, a deputy for Mayotte, accused the Comoros government of destabilization and called for Paris to suspend development aid.

Mayotte's representative in Paris, Estelle Youssouffa, has publicly called for France to suspend development aid to the Comoros. This statement follows a response from the Comorian Minister of Foreign Affairs, Mbaé Mohamed. Youssouffa asserted Mayotte's historical affiliation with France since 1841, referencing referendums from 1974 and 1976, and stated that UN General Assembly resolutions lack binding force and that the UN recognizes Mayotte as French.

Further escalating her stance, Youssouffa accused the Comorian government of interference and destabilization efforts, alleging it orchestrates migration flows. She proposed sanctions against the Comoros, specifically suggesting the suspension of development assistance. This proposal has been met with criticism, suggesting it amounts to collective punishment against the Comorian people and transforms cooperation into a tool of pressure.

Comorian officials argue that the dispute over Mayotte requires respectful dialogue grounded in international law and history, not financial threats. They maintain that Mayotte has remained a Comorian island under French administration since the Comoros gained independence. The call to suspend aid is seen as undermining prospects for de-escalation and peaceful resolution of the territorial disagreement.

Why it mattersThe proposal to suspend development aid directly impacts the Comorian population by potentially reducing financial resources available for projects and services.

Magistrate suspended over colleague violence allegations

A judicial official in Mutsamudu faces suspension following accusations of physical altercations with colleagues and an attorney.

A magistrate in Mutsamudu has been suspended from his duties following allegations of violent behavior towards colleagues. The decision comes after reports of serious incidents, including an alleged physical assault on an attorney from Domoni, during which the magistrate reportedly attempted to push her from the first floor. The attorney defended herself by biting the magistrate's arm.

The magistrate's conduct had raised questions about his continued service, with further incidents involving other colleagues also reported. While a more severe sanction, such as permanent removal from the profession, has been mentioned, it remains unconfirmed at this time. The suspension, however, marks a significant development in the ongoing investigation.

This suspension represents a turning point in the case, occurring after grave accusations and concerns about the judicial environment in Mutsamudu. The judicial authorities are expected to determine the final disciplinary actions to be taken.

Why it mattersThe suspension of a magistrate affects the stability and integrity of the local justice system, potentially impacting legal proceedings and public trust.

UNESCO Certifies Comoros' Historic Medinas as World Heritage

Iconi will host the official ceremony on Saturday where the Comoros president will receive the inscription certificate for six historic Sultanate medinas.

Comoros is preparing for a significant cultural milestone as the historic medinas of the Comorian Sultanates are officially recognized as UNESCO World Heritage sites. The town of Iconi will host the handover ceremony this Saturday, August 29, where President Azali Assoumani will receive the inscription certificate from the UNESCO Director-General. This recognition marks the first time Comoros has achieved World Heritage status.

The announcement was made by Minister Ali Mohamed Saïd Ali, who called for public mobilization to celebrate this national achievement. The inscribed sites include six historical cities: Iconi, Itsandra, Moroni, and Ntsoudjini on Grande Comore, and Mutsamudu and Domoni on Anjouan. These ancient urban centers, decided upon in July 2026 in Busan, South Korea, represent centuries of exchange between Africa, the Arab-Muslim world, and Indian Ocean trade routes.

The choice of Iconi for the ceremony is symbolic, highlighting its Swahili civilization, stone architecture, narrow streets, and living traditions. This designation is expected to boost cultural tourism, crafts, and heritage restoration, creating youth employment. However, it also brings a collective responsibility to preserve these sites, manage their development, and involve local residents in their stewardship.

Why it mattersThe historic medinas of the Comoros have been recognized by UNESCO, potentially boosting cultural tourism and heritage preservation efforts.

Business · 2026-08-29

Central Bank Modernizes Finance After 3.8% Growth

The Central Bank of Comoros aims to enhance financial modernization with new digital platforms and services following a 3.8% economic growth in 2025.

The Comorian economy grew by 3.8% in 2025, its best performance since the health crisis, driven by construction and electricity improvements. Inflation also decreased from 5.1% to 3.3%. The Central Bank of Comoros (BCC) presented these results and its 2026 priorities, emphasizing financial modernization.

The BCC's updated statutes strengthen its independence. New initiatives like Komor Pay, Komor Switch, a payment hub, and the 'Mali Ya Wakazi' agent network are designed to secure transactions, reduce cash usage, and make banking services more accessible to citizens.

Financial sector growth is evident, with bank balance sheets reaching 262.3 billion francs by the end of 2025, a 14% increase. Credits grew by 8% to 145.8 billion francs. The BCC also plans to facilitate SME financing through leasing and the Comoros Guarantee Company.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the Central Bank's modernization efforts which aim to secure transactions and facilitate SME financing, alongside overall economic growth and reduced inflation.
