Comoros radio journalist Mselem Ali dies

Mselem Ali, a prominent voice on Comorian radio, passed away after a career marked by significant media evolution and challenges.

Mselem Ali, a former journalist for Radio Comores and a notable figure in national radio journalism, has died. His passing marks the loss of a familiar voice to generations of listeners and a journalist who witnessed the evolution of Comorian media.

Ali was arrested on August 1, 2003, along with Radio Ngazidja director Abdou Djibaba, for an editorial criticizing the justice system and alleging corruption. He was detained for nearly two months, according to Reporters Without Borders, and released on September 13, 2003, highlighting the pressures faced by journalists.

Ali also participated in a 2011 program discussing the role of media in Comoros, demonstrating his continued interest in information dissemination. He spent his later years in France, leaving behind a legacy tied to the history of national radio and the audiovisual landscape of the Comoros.

Why it mattersThe death of Mselem Ali represents the loss of a significant figure in Comorian media history, impacting those who followed his career and the national narrative.

Education · 2026-11-03

Comorian schools face student malaise

Students experience fainting and behavioral issues, with explanations ranging from djinns to psychological distress.

Students in Comoros are experiencing unexplained malaise, including fainting spells and behavioral changes, leading to difficult situations for schools and families. While some attribute these episodes to djinns, others, like school heads, suspect simulation or underlying health and psychological issues.

At Éclairage school, principal Saïd Aboubacar suspects some students feign illness to skip lessons, particularly during math and physics tests, and has warned of exclusion. Meanwhile, psychiatrist Mistoihi Hassani Msoma emphasizes the need for individual psychological evaluations to address personal and family difficulties.

A mother shared her daughter's persistent absence from school, which has continued despite prayers and interventions from Quranic teachers. This situation highlights a suffering that extends beyond the school environment, requiring appropriate care beyond supernatural interpretations.

Why it mattersStudents in Comoros are experiencing unexplained physical and behavioral symptoms at school, affecting their education and well-being.
