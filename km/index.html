Comoros Rejects French Aid Tied to Migration Control

Moroni denounces French pressure to accept deportees from Mayotte, reaffirming its stance on the island's status.

The Comorian government has reacted strongly to France's announcement that it will link part of its development aid to cooperation on migration issues. Officials in Moroni described Paris's approach as "unbearable condescension" and rejected the use of aid as leverage to secure the return of individuals expelled from Mayotte. This dispute highlights the ongoing territorial disagreement between the two nations over the island.

The Comorian Minister of Foreign Affairs, Mbaé Mohamed, officially responded to the French position. The situation was further amplified by Mayotte's representative, Estelle Youssouffa, who publicly defended the island's French affiliation on the social platform X. Her comments added another layer to the public discourse surrounding the aid conditionality and the status of Mayotte.

This diplomatic friction centers on the readmission of Comorian nationals expelled from Mayotte, an island claimed by both France and the Comoros. The Comorian government's firm rejection of France's conditions signals a potential strain on bilateral relations and raises questions about the future of French development assistance to the archipelago.

Why it mattersThe Comorian government is affected by France's attempt to use development aid as a tool to pressure them into accepting deportees from Mayotte.

Comorian Soldier Deportation Upheld by French Court

A Comorian soldier who deserted during military exercises in La Réunion has been denied asylum and faces expulsion back to his home country.

A Comorian soldier, born in 1997, who disappeared in March after arriving for joint military exercises, will be deported from La Réunion to the Comoros. The administrative court in La Réunion ruled on August 19 to uphold his expulsion, nearly four months after he went missing. He was apprehended on July 22 during an identity check and subsequently requested asylum, which was rejected on August 6.

The soldier claimed he feared extrajudicial execution and spoke of army abuses against civilians, as well as information regarding the assassination of a former colleague. He also cited severe sanctions threatened by the Comorian defense director. However, the magistrates found his claims insufficiently documented to prove personal, present danger and noted no appeal had been filed with the national asylum court.

The court’s decision means the expulsion order remains in effect. If returned, the soldier is likely to face arrest upon arrival in the Comoros, with authorities having previously announced severe penalties including imprisonment and dismissal from service. This follows the disappearance of seven soldiers during the same exercise, two of whom were found earlier, with one already expelled.

Why it mattersThe soldier faces potential arrest and severe sanctions, including imprisonment and dismissal, upon his return to the Comoros.

UNESCO Certificate Arrives for Comoros' Historic Medinas

Iconi will host the official ceremony on Saturday, August 29, where the UNESCO Director-General will present the World Heritage inscription certificate for six historic Comorian.

Comoros is preparing for a significant cultural event as the historic medinas of the Comorian Sultanates are officially recognized by UNESCO. The city of Iconi will host the ceremony on Saturday, August 29, where the Director-General of UNESCO will present the inscription certificate to President Azali Assoumani. This marks the first time the Comoros has achieved World Heritage status.

The announcement was made by Minister Ali Mohamed Saïd Ali, who called for public mobilization to celebrate this national recognition. The inscription, decided in July 2026 in Busan, South Korea, covers six historical cities: Iconi, Itsandra, Moroni, and Ntsoudjini on Ngazidja island, and Mutsamudu and Domoni on Ndzuwani island. These sites represent centuries of exchange between Africa, the Arab-Muslim world, and Indian Ocean trade routes.

The choice of Iconi for the ceremony is symbolic, highlighting its Swahili civilization, stone architecture, narrow streets, and living traditions. This UNESCO designation is expected to boost cultural tourism, crafts, heritage restoration, and youth employment, while also imposing a collective responsibility to preserve these sites and involve local communities in their management.

Why it mattersThe inscription of historic medinas as UNESCO World Heritage sites opens new opportunities for cultural tourism, crafts, and employment, potentially benefiting local economies and youth.

MVola exploit flaw, Comoros electricity meters overcharged

A system glitch allowed some customers to add significant electricity credit without deducting funds from their mobile money accounts, impacting over 200 million Comorian francs.

A flaw in the MVola mobile money service allowed some customers to recharge electricity meters without their accounts being debited, an issue that has spread across all three Comorian islands. The anomaly reportedly began on August 18, following a system maintenance for MVola. Customers discovered they could credit their Sonelec electricity meters with amounts far exceeding their available balance, with some individuals reportedly adding millions of francs without any deduction.

The ease of access to meter numbers, often visible on external installations, facilitated the exploitation of this vulnerability. In Mohéli, young people were observed going door-to-door, recharging meters for unsuspecting residents. While some beneficiaries may have received credit without their knowledge or consent, others, including a prominent merchant who allegedly added seven million francs, appear to have knowingly taken advantage of the glitch.

Sonelec stated it incurred no direct financial loss, as all transactions were settled by MVola. The estimated loss of over 200 million Comorian francs is attributed to Yas Comores' mobile payment service. MVola has remained silent and is reportedly hesitant to pursue widespread legal action, needing to differentiate between fraudulent users and those who benefited unintentionally.

Why it mattersMVola, the mobile payment service, faces financial losses and potential legal complexities due to the exploitation of a system flaw by some users.

News · 08/28/2026

Magistrate suspended over colleague violence allegations

A judicial official in Mutsamudu faces suspension following accusations of physical altercations with colleagues, including an incident with an attorney.

A magistrate in Mutsamudu has been suspended from his duties following allegations of violent conduct towards several colleagues. The decision comes after a period of intense scrutiny within the local judiciary. Information obtained by Comores Infos from a reliable source confirms the suspension, marking a significant development in a case that has caused considerable disruption.

The situation escalated significantly due to accusations involving an altercation with an attorney from Domoni. Reports indicated that the magistrate allegedly physically assaulted the attorney and attempted to push her from the first floor. The attorney reportedly defended herself by biting her aggressor's arm. Further incidents involving other judicial colleagues had also been reported, raising questions about the magistrate's continued service.

While a more severe sanction, such as permanent dismissal, has been mentioned by sources, it remains unconfirmed at this time. The confirmed suspension represents a turning point, addressing the serious accusations and the resulting concerns about the integrity of the judicial system in Mutsamudu. Comores Infos will continue to monitor the case for further disciplinary actions.

Why it mattersThe suspension of a magistrate over violence allegations affects the stability and perceived fairness of the justice system for all citizens.
