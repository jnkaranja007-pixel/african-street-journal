Thirty Die From Substance Overdoses Since January

A total of thirty individuals have died from substance overdoses since January, with victims ranging in age from their early thirties to their sixties.

Thirty people have died from substance overdoses in Seychelles since January, according to figures released during a Sunday evening ceremony. The victims included individuals in their early thirties and others in their sixties, highlighting the broad age range affected by the crisis.

The statistics were shared as part of an effort to raise public awareness about the escalating overdose situation within the country. Officials aim to sensitize the population to the severity and prevalence of overdose-related fatalities.

This ongoing issue underscores a significant public health challenge. The ceremony served as a platform to discuss the critical need for increased awareness and preventative measures to address substance abuse and its fatal consequences.

Why it mattersThirty individuals have died from substance overdoses, affecting people across a wide age spectrum from their thirties to their sixties.

Posters Remain on Poles More Than Ten Months After Election

Campaign posters are still visible on Mahé's electrical poles and roadsides, defying electoral regulations.

More than ten months after the 2025 presidential and National Assembly elections, campaign posters remain affixed to electrical poles and along many roadsides on Mahé. This situation persists despite electoral regulations stipulating that campaign materials must be removed from public spaces within fourteen days of the election results being announced.

The continued presence of these posters indicates a lack of enforcement of electoral laws concerning campaign material removal. The Seychelles Broadcasting Corp reported on the persistent visibility of these election remnants across the island.

The electoral regulations clearly state the timeframe for removal, but the ongoing display suggests a gap between the law and its practical application in the post-election period. This oversight affects the visual landscape and potentially the adherence to electoral processes.

Why it mattersThe continued presence of election posters affects the visual environment and suggests a lack of adherence to electoral regulations for the general public.
