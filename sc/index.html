Air Seychelles staff protest salary increases

First officers are unhappy with the pay gap between them and captains after a March salary hike.

Some Air Seychelles staff are unhappy with recent salary increases, citing a disparity between captains' and first officers' pay. All Air Seychelles employees received a salary hike approved in March, ranging from 12% for lower earners to 7% for higher earners. The implementation was delayed until September due to the crisis in the Gulf region.

Veronique Laporte, the Minister for Transport, Ports, and Civil Aviation, explained the reasons for the pay difference between captains and first officers. The approved salary increases were implemented in September.

The salary adjustments were approved in March but could only be implemented in September due to the regional crisis.

Seychelles launches 41st Kreol Festival

The festival opened with a ceremony featuring a fashion show and a presentation on Creole culture.

Seychelles' Minister for Tourism and Culture, Amanda Bernstein, officially opened the 41st edition of the Festival Kreol on Saturday evening at the international conference centre. The opening ceremony included a presentation detailing the evolution of Seychellois Creole culture over the past 50 years.

The event also featured a fashion show with participation from various ministers, alongside other performances and celebrations. This year's festival aims to showcase and celebrate the rich Creole heritage of the islands.

The festival will continue with a series of events planned to highlight Creole traditions and arts. Further details on the festival's schedule and activities are expected to be released.

Why it mattersSeychelles residents will experience cultural performances and celebrations during the Festival Kreol.

Seychelles broadcasters meet on technology adoption

Public television and radio stations from Southern Africa are meeting in Seychelles to discuss technology.

Seychelles Broadcasting Corporation (SBC) faces challenges adopting new technologies while meeting audience needs. This is being discussed at the 10th conference for public television and radio stations in the Southern Africa region. The meeting is taking place at the Avani Barbarons hotel today and tomorrow.

The conference addresses how public broadcasters can adapt to technological changes. Delegates are discussing ways to integrate new platforms and methods to reach audiences effectively. This is part of the ongoing discussions for public broadcasting in the region.

The event gathers representatives from public broadcasting stations. They will share experiences and best practices to ensure broadcasters remain relevant.

Why it mattersSeychellois citizens who consume media will be affected by how their public broadcasters adopt new technologies.

News · 2026-10-24

PUC repairs La Digue manholes after floods

About 200 drainage system manholes have been fixed, with 300 more needing work on the island.

The Public Utilities Company (PUC) is repairing manholes connected to its drainage system on La Digue, which were damaged by floods. PUC officials and contractors from Anse Reunion and La Passe were observed working on the repairs.

Approximately 200 manholes have been fixed so far, with an additional 300 still requiring attention. The work is being carried out by PUC and contractors.

The repairs aim to restore the island's drainage system following the inundation.

Why it mattersResidents of La Digue are affected by the ongoing repairs to the drainage system manholes damaged by floods.
