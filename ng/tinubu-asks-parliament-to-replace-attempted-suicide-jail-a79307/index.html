Tinubu asks parliament to replace attempted-suicide jail terms with care

The proposal would offer medical support instead of prosecution, while a separate Senate bill on the issue has passed its second reading.

Source: BBC Africa · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Nigerian President Bola Tinubu has asked parliament to decriminalise attempted suicide, proposing medical support for people who survive rather than prosecution. Under current Nigerian law, attempted suicide is a crime punishable by up to one year in prison, a fine or both.

A separate bill to replace penalties with treatment, rehabilitation and support has passed its second reading in the Senate. Its sponsor, Senator Asuquo Ekpenyong, says prosecuting survivors could deter people experiencing suicidal thoughts from seeking help. He has also warned that fear of prosecution and social stigma could lead families to conceal attempts.

Mental health professionals have called for reform for years. Parliament must now consider Tinubu’s proposal; the Senate bill is already at a later stage of consideration.

Why it mattersPeople who survive suicide attempts face possible prosecution under the current law, while both proposals would direct them toward medical care and rehabilitation.
