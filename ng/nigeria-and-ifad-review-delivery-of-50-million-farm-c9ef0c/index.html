Nigeria and IFAD review delivery of $50 million farm programme

The mission will assess work in Ogun and Kano, where the programme targets 100,000 direct and 500,000 indirect beneficiaries.

Source: AgroNigeria · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Nigeria’s Federal Government and the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) began a joint review of the Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zones programme in Abuja on 24 November, seeking faster implementation of its $50 million investment.

The mission will examine contracts, spending, results and operational bottlenecks ahead of the programme’s first additional financing and mid-term review, said Emmanuel Oladipo Akogun, a director at the agriculture ministry. The programme supports rice and cassava value chains in Ogun and Kano, with targets of 100,000 direct and 500,000 indirect beneficiaries, particularly women and young people.

In Kano, a 2024 pilot supported more than 1,000 smallholder farmers and farmer organisations with inputs, market links and training. Field visits are scheduled through 2 December; the initial loan period runs to 30 September 2029.

Why it mattersFarmers in Kano and Ogun are covered by a programme with 100,000 direct-beneficiary places, while officials review delivery and bottlenecks.
