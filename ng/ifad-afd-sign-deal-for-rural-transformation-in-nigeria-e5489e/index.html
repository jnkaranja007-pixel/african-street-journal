IFAD, AFD sign deal for rural transformation in Nigeria

The co-financing agreement backs the Value Chain Programme in Northern Nigeria, which is designed to cut poverty and improve nutrition in rural areas.

Sources: AgroNigeria and Nairametrics · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

The International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) and the Agence Française de Développement (AFD) have signed a co-financing agreement in Abuja for the Value Chain Programme in Northern Nigeria (VCN). The signing, between IFAD Country Director Mohamed El-Ghazaly and AFD Country Director Jacky Amprou, marks a milestone in the programme's start-up.

VCN is a flagship programme of the Federal Government of Nigeria. Its co-financiers are IFAD, AFD and nine participating states: Bauchi, Borno, Jigawa, Kano, Katsina, Kebbi, Sokoto, Yobe and Zamfara. Both institutions have urged the federal and state governments to speed up remaining start-up work ahead of a programme start-up workshop planned for early December 2026.

Under the framework, IFAD will act as lead co-financier and provide technical, fiduciary, environmental and social support for AFD's financing. The programme is still in its inception phase, and the agreement sets out how the two institutions will jointly finance its components.

Why it mattersRural households in the nine northern states will see the programme's poverty, nutrition and income work begin only once start-up activities are finished before the December workshop.
