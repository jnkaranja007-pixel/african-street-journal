Nigeria targets $9.3bn care economy and seeks private investment

The Federal Executive Council approved a 2026–2030 policy, while a proposed bill would give it legislative backing.

Sources: Vanguard and Nairametrics · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Nigeria is targeting a $9.3 billion care economy and seeking private investment in childcare, elderly care and disability support after the Federal Executive Council approved the National Care Policy and Implementation Plan 2026–2030. The policy is intended to organise the sector and expand jobs and women’s participation in the economy.

Women’s Affairs Minister Hajiya Imaan Suleiman-Ibrahim said on Tuesday in Lagos that the council directed the justice and women’s affairs ministers on October 28 to draft an executive bill backing the policy. Citing UN Women’s 2026 modelling, she said closing unpaid care gaps and expanding universal childcare could add at least $9.3 billion to gross domestic product and create 9.4 million to 17 million jobs. Caregiving accounted for 20.16 per cent of women’s withdrawal from the workforce, against 0.6 per cent for men.

International Finance Corporation Nigeria head Christian Mulamula said 92 per cent of childcare facilities were small, independently owned businesses and 76 per cent of childcare entrepreneurs lacked formal finance. Lagos is developing an investment-oriented care policy framework, Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu said through his finance commissioner, and urged investors and financial institutions to support the sector.

Why it mattersChildcare entrepreneurs face a financing gap, with 76 per cent lacking access to formal finance, as the government seeks private investment.
