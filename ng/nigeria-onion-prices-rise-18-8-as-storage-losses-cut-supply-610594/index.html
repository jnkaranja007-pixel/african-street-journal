Nigeria onion prices rise 18.8% as storage losses cut supply

More than 51% of stored onions were lost between April and October, while annual demand of 2.44 to 2.5 million tons exceeds production.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Onion prices in Nigeria rose 18.8% in 2026, with traders in Enugu reporting daily increases, according to the National Agricultural Extension and Research Liaison Services (NAERLS). The increase contrasts with falls in other staples: garri prices declined 21% and white maize prices fell 13%.

The National Onion Producers, Processors and Marketers Association of Nigeria said more than 51% of stored onions were lost between April and October. It attributed losses to mud-and-thatch storage huts, fires at northern aggregation centres and reduced planting after weak returns last season. Nigeria produces roughly 2 million metric tons annually, against demand of 2.44 to 2.5 million tons.

Supplies are expected to rise with harvesting in Sokoto in November and December, then Aliero in Kebbi State between late December and January; the main harvest is scheduled for March to April. On November 18, Agriculture and Food Security Minister Abubakar Kyari said the government planned food releases through the Bank of Agriculture, but reserves focus on grains rather than fresh onions.
