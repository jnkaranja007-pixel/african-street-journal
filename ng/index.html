Sport · 2026-11-08

Nigeria U-17 women win World Cup in Morocco

The Flamingos defeated Japan 2-1 in the final, securing a historic title for Nigerian women's football.

Nigeria's U-17 women's national team, the Flamingos, have won the 2026 FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup in Morocco. They beat Japan 2-1 in the final match. Oluebube Umejiaku and Awawu Bashiru scored for Nigeria, while Fuki Ito scored for Japan.

Japan's coach, Sadayoshi Shirai, acknowledged Nigeria's speed and physical strength as key factors in the defeat. He stated that the Flamingos managed to break through their defence at moments when they got in behind. Shirai also noted that his players fought hard but struggled to cope with Nigeria's power.

The Deputy President of the Senate, Senator Barau Jibrin, congratulated the team on their triumph. First Lady Mrs Tinubu also celebrated the victory, calling it a reflection of the team's resilience and commitment. Nigeria reached the final after defeating Brazil in the semi-finals.

Why it mattersThe Nigerian U-17 women's football team has achieved a historic World Cup victory, bringing pride to the nation.

SERAP asks govt to reveal N50,000 relief payment details

The rights group wants information on beneficiaries, funding, and safeguards for the National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention.

The Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP) has asked Nigeria's Minister of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Reduction, Dr Bernard Doro, to disclose details about the ₦50,000 National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention (NERSI). SERAP's request, made on November 7, 2026, seeks information on funding, beneficiary selection, payments, and safeguards for the programme announced on October 30, 2026.

SERAP's deputy director, Kolawole Oluwadare, stated that Nigerians have a right to know how public funds for NERSI are spent and who receives the assistance. The organisation also urged the minister to ask the Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC) and the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) to monitor the programme and investigate any corruption allegations.

The rights group warned that public funds for social assistance must not be used to reward political supporters or influence electoral choices, especially ahead of the 2027 general elections. SERAP also stated that beneficiaries should not be required to demonstrate political loyalty to receive the ₦50,000 assistance.

Why it mattersNigerians are directly affected by the government's ₦50,000 relief payment programme, and SERAP is seeking transparency on how these public funds are managed and distributed.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Nigerians demand power minister's resignation over blackouts

Civil society groups accuse Joseph Tegbe of incompetence amid persistent electricity outages and high bills.

Nigerians and civil society organisations are calling for the resignation of Power Minister Joseph Tegbe due to ongoing electricity blackouts. They accuse him of incompetence and failing to resolve the worsening power situation across the country.

Tegbe attributed recent outages to planned maintenance at Egbin Power Station and gas supply issues affecting thermal plants, promising restoration within 48 to 72 hours. He also announced the release of 98 stranded transmission equipment containers, which he said would help complete stalled projects and strengthen the grid.

However, groups like the Committee for the Defence of Human Rights and the Human Rights Writers Association of Nigeria have dismissed the minister's explanations as unacceptable excuses. They cited exorbitant bills despite poor or non-existent supply and called for investigations into alleged financial abuses within the ministry.

Why it mattersNigerians are facing persistent electricity outages and paying high bills without adequate service, leading to calls for the minister's resignation.

Agriculture · 2026-11-08

GIZ urges stronger Nigerian education capacity for farm innovations

German agency calls for embedding agricultural innovations into training systems beyond just boosting output.

The German Corporation for International Cooperation (GIZ) has called for stronger institutional capacity to embed agricultural innovations in Nigeria’s education, training, and extension systems. Alexis Brakhan, Project Manager of GIZ’s AgSys programme, stated on Wednesday in Abuja that lasting transformation requires more than increased farm productivity.

Brakhan made the call at the close-out workshop of the Strengthening Institutional Frameworks for Transformative Agricultural Systems (SIFTAS) project, implemented by the Sasakawa Africa Association (SAA) with GIZ funding. He explained that the German Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development (BMZ) sees transforming agricultural and food systems as a strategic priority, requiring interventions beyond output increases to address food insecurity and climate change.

The SIFTAS project incorporated sustainable agricultural practices into institutional structures, developing crop-specific agroforestry curricula for cassava, potato, and rice. Preparations for the next phase include a post-project Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning (MEL) Transition Framework and signing MoUs to ensure continued progress monitoring and learning.

Why it mattersNigerian farmers and agricultural students will benefit from improved training and access to sustainable farming innovations.

Business · 2026-11-08

Nigeria summit demands growth beyond theory

Leaders at the 32nd Nigerian Economic Summit agreed reforms must bring tangible economic benefits to households.

The 32nd Nigerian Economic Summit concluded on Tuesday with a consensus that Nigeria's economic reforms must deliver tangible growth impacting households. Public and private sector leaders attended the two-day event.

Participants at the summit, themed “Growth That Works,” called for macroeconomic reforms to move from theoretical frameworks to practical, household-level economic expansion. The summit served as a platform for policymakers and industry captains.

The discussions focused on achieving economic growth that demonstrably benefits citizens. International development representatives also participated in the summit.
