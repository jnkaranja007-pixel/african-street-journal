US revokes visas for asylum seekers, targets 200,000

The US government will revoke visas for foreigners who entered on temporary visas and then applied for asylum, with the White House suggesting up to two hundred thousand visas.

The United States State Department announced plans to revoke visas for individuals who entered the country on tourist or business visas and subsequently sought asylum. This action is part of the Trump administration's effort to reduce immigration, with State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott stating that obtaining a visa to seek asylum constitutes fraud and is grounds for revocation.

The White House indicated that this policy could affect up to two hundred thousand visas, describing it as potentially the largest mass visa revocation in history. The State Department did not confirm this specific number, noting that revocations would occur on a rolling basis. This follows earlier actions where approximately 175,000 foreign visas were revoked since January 2025 for various reasons, including criminal offenses.

This new measure targets those who overstay their visas after expiration, building on the administration's broader immigration enforcement. A federal judge recently struck down a ban on visas for nationals from 75 countries, including Nigeria, who wished to settle in the US indefinitely. The State Department emphasized that a visa is considered a privilege, not a right.

Why it mattersNigerians who have entered the US on temporary visas and then applied for asylum, or those considering such a path, are directly affected by the US government's new policy to revoke visas for asylum fraud.

Dolly Parton dies at 80, ending six-decade career

American country music star Dolly Parton, known for hits like “Jolene” and “9 to 5,” passed away on Tuesday in Nashville, Tennessee, at the age of 80.

Dolly Parton, the American country music star, has died at 80, her family announced. Her career spanned nearly six decades across music, film, television, and business, establishing her as a cultural icon.

Parton's family did not disclose the cause of death, though the singer had publicly discussed dealing with health issues. She was remembered for her extensive legacy in music, film, and philanthropy, according to The Punch.

The news of her passing was reported by Nairametrics, Premium Times, and The Punch, all confirming her death in Nashville. Parton's influence extended beyond entertainment, encompassing significant business ventures throughout her life.

Why it mattersFans of Dolly Parton and those who appreciate her contributions to music, film, and business will be affected by the loss of a prominent cultural figure.

FCT Police detain officers over alleged N40,000 extortion in Abuja

The Federal Capital Territory Police Command has identified and taken into custody officers seen in a viral video allegedly extorting N40,000 from a resident.

The FCT Police Command has detained officers identified in a viral video showing alleged extortion along Wuye-Utako Road in Abuja. The officers are currently in custody, according to a statement from SP Josephine Adeh, the FCT Police Public Relations Officer.

The Commissioner of Police, CP Ahmed Muhammed Sanusi, ordered an investigation after the video surfaced on social media. The Command has also invited the complainant to its headquarters to provide an official statement, which will assist in the ongoing investigation.

Adeh stated that the Command will not tolerate extortion, abuse of authority, misconduct, or unprofessional conduct by its personnel. Any officer found culpable after due investigation will face appropriate disciplinary and legal action, reaffirming the Command's commitment to accountability.

Why it mattersResidents of Abuja are affected as the police command investigates alleged misconduct by its officers, impacting public trust and safety.

Analyst: Terrorists exploit social media, remittances for funding

Terrorist groups in Nigeria are using social media and international remittance services to raise and move funds, according to security analyst Dr. Bulama Bukarti.

Terrorist financing is becoming more sophisticated, with foreign-based individuals soliciting donations through platforms like Telegram and Instagram, disguised as humanitarian or educational appeals. Dr. Bulama Bukarti, a security analyst and lawyer, stated on Channels Television that these donations, ranging from $50 to $500, are accumulated in master accounts before being broken into smaller amounts and transferred to recipients in Nigeria via international remittance platforms.

This method allows groups to evade Nigeria's tightening financial regulations, Bukarti explained. He cited a Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit (NFIU) report detailing these activities. Bukarti urged Nigerians to perform due diligence before donating online, emphasizing that the warning is not against genuine humanitarian assistance but a call for caution.

He also called for stronger linkage between Bank Verification Numbers (BVN) and SIM cards, noting that deceased persons' accounts and third-party controlled accounts have allegedly been exploited for terrorism financing. Bukarti stressed that the NFIU and the Nigerian Communications Commission must collaborate to ensure only properly registered and verified SIM cards are linked to bank accounts for financial transactions, stating that following the money is crucial to defeating terrorism.

Why it mattersNigerians are affected by the increased risk of unknowingly funding terrorist activities through online donations and remittance platforms, which could lead to financial and security implications.

Nigeria's new health policy improves doctor working conditions and patient care

The National Health Sector Reform Initiative II (NHSRII) aims to ensure doctors are rested after shifts and locum employees receive clear contracts and regular pay.

Nigeria's new health policy, the NHSRII, intends to improve health outcomes by ensuring doctors can finish a call, hand over to a rested colleague, and go home. This initiative addresses the working conditions of medical professionals, aiming for a more sustainable and effective healthcare system.

Under the new policy, locum employees are expected to have clear contracts, receive regular pay, and transition away from temporary status. This change seeks to provide greater stability and fairness for doctors working in temporary capacities, ensuring they are properly integrated into the healthcare workforce.

The ultimate goal of the NHSRII is for patients to be treated by properly staffed teams, rather than overworked individuals. This focus on adequate staffing and rested medical personnel is designed to enhance the quality and safety of patient care across the country, according to the Premium Times.

Why it mattersThis policy directly affects doctors by improving their working conditions and contract stability, and patients by ensuring they are treated by well-rested and properly staffed medical teams.
