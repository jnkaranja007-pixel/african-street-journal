Nigeria faces power cuts over N639bn unpaid gas debt

Unpaid gas debts totaling N639 billion threaten Nigeria's electricity supply, impacting power generation.

Nigeria's electricity supply is at risk due to an accumulated unpaid gas debt of N639 billion owed by power generation companies.

This significant debt has hampered the ability of gas suppliers to provide the necessary fuel for power plants, leading to reduced electricity generation across the country.

The situation highlights ongoing challenges in the energy sector, where financial liabilities directly impact the reliability of essential services for millions of Nigerians.

Gunmen demand N500m for abducted FUNAAB graduate, three others

Four people, including a recent university graduate, were taken from a farm in Ogun State on Tuesday night.

Kidnappers are holding a recent graduate of the Federal University of Agriculture, Abeokuta (FUNAAB), Adedoja Hannah, together with three other people, and have asked for N500 million for their release. Suspected gunmen seized the four on Tuesday night at a farm on the Akintobi-Alabata axis of Abeokuta, Ogun State, where they had been working.

The university's Students' Union Government (SUG) disclosed the demand in a statement posted on Facebook on Thursday, voicing concern for the victims' safety. Hannah, a graduate of the Department of Agricultural Administration, was last seen on Monday, and her phone has been unreachable since the abduction. Police have been working on the case since Tuesday.

The union appealed to the public to stay calm and to avoid unverified reports that could interfere with the security response. Security efforts are ongoing to locate the victims and secure their safe return.

Why it mattersThe four captives, one of them a recent graduate, are being held until a N500 million ransom is paid or they are found.

Nigeria hands Port Harcourt airport to private firm for 30 years

Port Harcourt Airport Company Ltd. will run the Rivers State airport under a concession agreement signed by the Federal Government.

Port Harcourt Airport Company Ltd. has taken over the Jeremiah Obafemi Awolowo International Airport in Port Harcourt, Rivers State, under a 30-year concession from the Federal Government. Officials say the deal is meant to ease the cost of running airports that lose money and to draw private capital into the aviation sector.

Festus Keyamo, Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, said the signing opens a new phase in the government's plan to bring private money into airport infrastructure. The concession is also expected to improve how selected airports across the country are managed.

The operator will run the airport for three decades. The government has assured workers that their jobs are secure under the agreement.

Why it mattersWorkers at the airport keep their jobs under the new operator, and the private firm now carries the airport's running costs for 30 years.

Four UNN students die at off-campus party

A survivor recounts losing consciousness after consuming alcohol at a gathering in Nsukka, Enugu State.

Four students at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka (UNN) died on Wednesday at an off-campus party in Nsukka, Enugu State. A 24-year-old survivor, an Art Education student, lost consciousness after a participant vomited and later found his friends motionless. The gathering was organized by a friend named David to celebrate the end of final examinations, with two bottles of alcoholic drinks, Magic Moment and Best, shared among about 10 people.

The survivor described the party starting around 11:30 a.m. in a closed parlour with a generator running outside. He stated that alcohol was the only substance consumed, dismissing claims of illicit drugs. He recounted feeling his legs stiffen before losing consciousness and waking up around 9 p.m. to find others on the floor. He sought help from friends who transported the affected students to Bishop Shanahan Hospital and other medical facilities.

The university's Vice-Chancellor, Prof. Simon Otuanya, visited the survivor, who reported gradual improvement despite ongoing leg stiffness. Local government representatives and police officers also visited, with police inquiring about potential drink tampering or prior disagreements. The survivor confirmed that the drinks were shared in small quantities, not even a quarter of a party cup per person.

Why it mattersFour students died after consuming alcohol at a party in Nsukka, prompting a police inquiry into the circumstances.

Nigeria's shift to fenced ranches could let investors start with two cattle

Jerry Cunningham, managing director of American West African Agro (AWAA), says the reform would also improve productivity and security.

Jerry Cunningham, managing director of American West African Agro (AWAA), told AgroNigeria that moving from open pastoralism to fenced ranches and feedlots could make commercial cattle production far more accessible. Investors could begin a ranching business with as few as two animals, and a single facility could house as many as 15,000.

Cunningham said the transition, which he expects to take several years, would give small-scale producers new opportunities, raise cattle productivity and improve security. He said Nigerian cattle have good genetic potential but need better feeding to perform, so controlled production is the first step. The Ministry and L-PRES are already working on this, he said.

Better feeding changes the economics: a farmer could raise cattle to between 400 and 500 kilogrammes within 24 months, instead of selling them at seven months at about 250 kilogrammes. In the South-West, small ranchers already keep 10 to 50 cattle in confined systems, and AWAA's work in Kogi State, where it bought local cattle and feed, showed the model could work.
