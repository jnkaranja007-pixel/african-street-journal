Tinubu seeks $20.5bn foreign loan for projects

President Bola Tinubu has asked the Senate to approve a borrowing plan of $20.52 billion and €1.045 billion for national development.

President Bola Tinubu has requested the Nigerian Senate's approval for a supplementary external borrowing plan totaling $20.52 billion and €1.045 billion. The funds are intended to finance infrastructure and development projects across the nation.

The proposed loans come as Nigeria's debt burden faces increased scrutiny, with rising refinancing costs and minimal savings to cover maturing obligations. Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has called for detailed project-specific information on the loan plan, citing concerns about transparency.

The Federal Government and the Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC) have agreed on measures to alleviate economic hardship for workers, including a ₦70,000 transport intervention and payment of arrears to health workers. Separately, a ministerial committee recommended sanctions and prosecution for NSCDC officers over the deaths of 33 detainees in Niger State.

Why it mattersPresident Bola Tinubu is seeking foreign loans that will increase Nigeria's national debt.

Dangote Refinery cargo dominates Nigerian port traffic

Refinery-linked shipments accounted for 30.4% of Nigeria’s total port throughput in the first half of 2026.

Cargo linked to the Dangote Refinery represented 30.4% of Nigeria’s total port throughput between January and June 2026. Nigerian ports handled 68.29 million metric tons of cargo during this period. Lekki port, which began operations as Nigeria’s first deep-sea port, processed 27.32 million metric tons, about 40% of the national total.

Operations at the Dangote Refinery accounted for 76% of Lekki’s throughput, with refinery-linked cargo totalling approximately 20.76 million metric tons. This single industrial complex significantly influences the port’s performance. Port calls at Lekki rose by 48.4% in the first half of 2026, while average vessel turnaround time increased from 2.1 to 3.3 days.

The Nigerian Ports Authority has not disclosed the proportion of its revenue attributable to Dangote-related cargo. Meanwhile, container traffic rose by 10.3% to approximately 815,000 TEUs in the first half of 2026. Outbound cargo shipments grew by 23.5% to about 29.2 million metric tons.

Why it mattersDangote Refinery's significant port activity impacts Nigeria's maritime services and cargo volumes.

Dangote Refinery IPO attracts investors from 120 countries

The Lagos-based refinery's initial public offering exceeded its $1.6 billion target, drawing participation from all 54 African nations.

Dangote Petroleum Refinery, based in Lagos, has concluded its initial public share offering, reporting investment from 120 countries. The offering, initially set to close on October 13, was extended by two weeks to encourage African participation. While the total funds raised were not disclosed, Aliko Dangote stated the target of $1.6 billion was surpassed by "more than three, four times".

Aliko Dangote described the IPO as a "historic achievement for Africa," demonstrating continental self-sufficiency in financing and industrial development. Investors from all 54 African countries and five other continents participated, with Dangote noting the extraordinary international interest and confidence in the refinery's future. The company had previously secured $2.5 billion in private investment in July.

The refinery, with a current capacity of 700,000 barrels per day, aims to double its output to 1.4 million barrels per day, potentially becoming the world's largest. Africa currently imports over 70 percent of its refined fuel, according to an Africa Finance Corporation report.

Why it mattersInvestors from 120 countries participated in the IPO for the Lagos-based Dangote Petroleum Refinery.

NASHGODAN urges feed banks for livestock farmers

The National Sheep and Goat Development Association of Nigeria calls for government support to combat rising feed costs.

The National Sheep and Goat Development Association of Nigeria (NASHGODAN) has asked the Federal Government to create community-based feed banks across the country. This aims to ensure livestock farmers can access affordable and quality animal feed, addressing concerns over rising costs and the sustainability of sheep and goat farming.

NASHGODAN National President Abdullateef Ahmed stated in Abuja that all government levels should partner with financial institutions, development partners, feed manufacturers, and research bodies. He believes this collaboration, combining government support with private sector investment, can lead to affordable livestock feed and allow farmers to sustain their businesses.

Ahmed warned that high feed prices threaten sheep and goat farming's sustainability, potentially discouraging young people from entering the sector and reducing market-ready animal supply. He noted that smallholder farmers, who produce a significant share of Nigeria’s sheep and goats, are particularly vulnerable to reduced animal growth, disease, and lower profits when feed becomes too expensive.

Why it mattersLivestock farmers in Nigeria face challenges with rising feed costs, threatening their businesses and food production.
