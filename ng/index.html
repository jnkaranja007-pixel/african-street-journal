Health · 2026-11-01

Nigeria Centre for Disease Control confirms negative Ebola tests

Two suspected cases in Adamawa State tested negative for Ebola Virus Disease after two rounds of laboratory tests.

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) announced that two suspected Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) cases in Adamawa State have tested negative. The individuals, who showed symptoms suggestive of Ebola in Yola, were isolated while investigations took place. The NCDC confirmed that the index case in the Federal Capital Territory (FCT) remains the only confirmed infection in Nigeria.

NCDC Director-General Dr Jide Idris stated that the report of the suspected cases was received by the Adamawa State Public Health Emergency Operations Centre (PHEOC) on October 30, 2026. Samples from the individuals underwent two rounds of laboratory testing, both yielding negative results for Ebola. Idris commended the Adamawa State Commissioner for Health and their team for their proactive approach and assured continued technical support.

This development follows the confirmation of a Bundibugyo Ebola case in Abuja, involving a traveler from Uganda. The negative results in Adamawa have eased concerns about a potential spread. Idris urged the public to remain calm and avoid spreading unverified information, advising them to seek official communication channels for updates. He also stressed the importance of continued vigilance and seeking immediate medical attention for anyone experiencing Ebola-like symptoms.

Politics · 2026-11-01

Sokoto ultimatum issued over attack on ADC candidate

Ekiti police arrest 11 after Omoyele Sowore abduction; conflicting relief payment terms emerge.

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has given Sokoto security authorities 24 hours to respond to an alleged attack on its governorship candidate. In Ekiti, police announced 11 arrests following an incident involving Omoyele Sowore, who was forcibly taken from a restaurant in Ado-Ekiti by armed men. Meanwhile, two government publications presented different terms for the ₦50,000 relief programme.

Sowore's official social media accounts confirmed his abduction. Abba Atiku also pledged to provide buses for President Tinubu. The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has outlined its preparations for upcoming elections.

The ADC's ultimatum highlights ongoing political tensions in Sokoto. The conflicting relief payment terms could affect citizens awaiting financial assistance. INEC's preparations signal the electoral body's readiness for future electoral processes.

Why it mattersPolitical actors and security incidents in Sokoto and Ekiti, alongside government relief programs and electoral preparations, directly affect Nigerian citizens.

Politics · 2026-11-01

Group urges southern presidency until 2031

BTO For PBAT '27 Support Group says power rotation promotes equity and political balance in Nigeria.

The BTO For PBAT ’27 Support Group has called for the Nigerian presidency to remain in Southern Nigeria until 2031. Director-General Ogbeni Adojutelegan Adesuyi Gideon stated in Akure that this would promote equity, fairness, and political balance. He argued that allowing the South to complete an eight-year tenure would strengthen national unity and democratic stability.

Adojutelegan acknowledged that power rotation is not in the constitution but is a vital political arrangement for national cohesion. The group, led by the Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo, believes equitable arrangements are essential for mutual trust and deepening democracy. They stated their position is anchored on equity, fairness, and justice, not sectionalism.

The group advocates for the continuation of a southern presidency until 2031 to reinforce fairness and political accommodation. They stressed that Nigeria's democratic stability depends on leaders upholding inclusion and mutual respect. This call is for political fairness and democratic consolidation, not to deny any Nigerian the right to contest elections.

Why it mattersThe group's call for the presidency to remain in the South until 2031 aims to ensure equity, fairness, and political balance among Nigeria's geopolitical zones.

Agriculture · 2026-11-01

Nigeria cocoa exports to US jump 554%

Shipments reached $142 million in 2025, up from $21.7 million the previous year, according to the US Mission in Nigeria.

Nigeria's cocoa bean exports to the United States surged by 554.4% to $142 million in 2025, a significant increase from $21.7 million in 2024. The United States Mission in Nigeria announced these figures, highlighting growing trade between the two nations.

The mission stated that US demand for Nigerian cocoa is booming, with exports to the United States rising sharply. This growth in trade occurs as Nigeria aims to diversify its foreign exchange earnings and boost agricultural production.

Despite the export surge, Nigeria's cocoa industry faces challenges including old plantations, limited financing, and low productivity. Over 80% of Nigeria's cocoa is exported raw, limiting local value addition and job creation.

Why it mattersNigerian farmers and businesses are seeing increased demand for their cocoa beans in the United States, but structural issues hinder the sector's full potential.

Sport · 2026-11-01

Nigeria Flamingos target U-17 World Cup semi-final

A win against Venezuela would send the Flamingos to the semi-finals for the first time since 2022.

The Nigeria U-17 women's football team, the Flamingos, are set to play Venezuela with a place in the semi-finals of the World Cup at stake.

Victory for Nigeria would mark their first semi-final appearance since achieving a third-place finish at the 2022 edition held in India.

This match could also see the Flamingos achieve four wins in regular time at a single tournament, setting a new national record for the team.

Why it mattersNigerian women footballers are aiming for a historic semi-final spot in the U-17 World Cup.
