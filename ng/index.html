Nigeria’s stocks rise about 60% as inflation falls for four months

The Central Bank has started cutting rates, but the improved macroeconomic picture remains mixed beneath the headline figures.

Nigeria’s inflation fell for a fourth straight month and the Central Bank began cutting rates as the country’s stock market climbed about 60% for the year, according to Nairametrics on Nov. 17.

The report described Nigeria as entering the final weeks of 2026 in better macroeconomic shape than at any point in three years, while saying conditions beneath the headline gains were mixed.

The report also cited a harvest discount, but gave no details about its size or who was offering it.

Why it mattersInvestors in Nigerian stocks are looking at a market up about 60% for the year as the Central Bank starts cutting rates.

AFEX forecasts 1.9 million-tonne fall in Nigeria rice output

The exchange also projects a 1.5 million-tonne decline in maize production during the 2026/2027 market season, based on a survey of about 41,000 farmers.

AFEX Commodities Exchange forecasts Nigeria’s rice production will fall by 1.9 million metric tonnes in the 2026/2027 market season, according to its 2026 Wet Season Crop Production Report. The exchange also projects maize output will decline by 1.5 million metric tonnes.

The report draws on surveys of approximately 41,000 randomly selected farmers nationwide, alongside data from AFEX and other agricultural research bodies. AFEX said improved fertiliser availability during the 2026 wet season was partly linked to subsidy interventions by federal and state governments.

AFEX chief executive Akinyinka Akintunde called for stronger agricultural data systems and reforms to lift production capacity. The exchange said improved access to finance, agricultural infrastructure and targeted interventions were needed to address the projected declines.

Why it mattersRice and maize farmers face projected output losses, while AFEX calls for improved access to finance and agricultural infrastructure.

Nigeria targets $9.3bn care economy and seeks private investment

The Federal Executive Council approved a 2026–2030 policy, while a proposed bill would give it legislative backing.

Nigeria is targeting a $9.3 billion care economy and seeking private investment in childcare, elderly care and disability support after the Federal Executive Council approved the National Care Policy and Implementation Plan 2026–2030. The policy is intended to organise the sector and expand jobs and women’s participation in the economy.

Women’s Affairs Minister Hajiya Imaan Suleiman-Ibrahim said on Tuesday in Lagos that the council directed the justice and women’s affairs ministers on October 28 to draft an executive bill backing the policy. Citing UN Women’s 2026 modelling, she said closing unpaid care gaps and expanding universal childcare could add at least $9.3 billion to gross domestic product and create 9.4 million to 17 million jobs. Caregiving accounted for 20.16 per cent of women’s withdrawal from the workforce, against 0.6 per cent for men.

International Finance Corporation Nigeria head Christian Mulamula said 92 per cent of childcare facilities were small, independently owned businesses and 76 per cent of childcare entrepreneurs lacked formal finance. Lagos is developing an investment-oriented care policy framework, Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu said through his finance commissioner, and urged investors and financial institutions to support the sector.

Why it mattersChildcare entrepreneurs face a financing gap, with 76 per cent lacking access to formal finance, as the government seeks private investment.

OPay names five finalists for ₦18m National Innovation Challenge

The Nigerian digital bank says more than 11,000 students took part in selection, training and mentorship for the 2026 competition.

OPay, a Nigerian digital bank, has named five finalist teams for its 2026 National Innovation Challenge, which carries ₦18m in prizes. The competition is run with Google and the Federal Government’s 3 Million Technical Talent (3MTT) Programme.

More than 11,000 students across Nigeria entered the selection, training and mentorship process, OPay said. The announcement identifies five teams as finalists after that process.

The challenge is part of the OPay Scholars Programme 2026, which also includes a ₦1.2 billion, 10-year scholarship initiative and OPayFutures.

Why it mattersThe five finalist teams are selected from a process that drew more than 11,000 students across Nigeria.

Politics · 2026-11-18

London deletes posts praising Nigeria-US security partnership

The American strategist had called Nigeria Washington’s greatest ally in Africa and said the cooperation could widen economic and trade opportunities.

American political strategist Diana London deleted social media posts praising Nigeria’s security partnership with the United States, according to The Punch’s report on Wednesday. London had described Nigeria as Washington’s greatest ally in Africa and said cooperation under Presidents Bola Tinubu and Donald Trump was producing results.

In posts on X, London credited the two governments with working to tackle terrorism and strengthen bilateral relations. She also said the partnership could open economic and trade opportunities. The Punch’s assigned report says the posts were deleted but gives no reason for their removal.

President Tinubu has also given London an exclusive interview, according to Imran Muhammad, a senior aide to the chairman of the governing All Progressives Congress. Muhammad said the interview was expected to be released soon.

Why it mattersThe promised interview has not yet been released, leaving readers awaiting Tinubu’s account of the security relationship London praised.
