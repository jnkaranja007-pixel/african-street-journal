Nigeria's headline inflation falls to 14.06% in October

Food inflation also eased to 19.13 percent, though prices rose 0.43 percent month on month.

The National Bureau of Statistics said Nigeria's headline inflation rate dropped to 14.06 percent in October 2026, down from 14.63 percent in September. The fall marks the fifth consecutive monthly decline, according to The Punch.

Food inflation also slowed, to 19.13 percent year on year from 19.59 percent in September, the bureau said in its latest Consumer Price Index report. On a month-on-month basis, however, headline inflation rose 0.43 percent in October, up from 0.06 percent the previous month, meaning average prices climbed faster than in September.

Year on year, food inflation was highest in Ondo at 31.74 percent and Lagos at 31.43 percent, and lowest in Nasarawa at 3.7 percent. Month on month, food prices rose fastest in Yobe, at 18.41 percent, and fell most in Lagos, by 5.93 percent. Items driving the monthly movement included vegetable oil, sardines, rice and catfish.

Why it mattersHouseholds buying staples such as rice, cooking oil and fish face a month-on-month price rise even as the annual rate eases.

Plateau records 5,457 suspected diabetes cases in nine months

The nine-month total already comes close to the 5,546 suspected cases the state recorded across the whole of 2025.

Plateau State has recorded 5,457 suspected diabetes cases in the first nine months of this year. The state Commissioner for Health, Nicholas Ba’amlong, disclosed the figure in Jos on Monday.

The total is close to the 5,546 suspected cases the state recorded throughout 2025. The commissioner's disclosure did not break the cases down further or say exactly which months the nine-month period covers.

Separately, the National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in Persons (NAPTIP) has recorded 1,508 human trafficking cases and 1,434 arrests, its Director-General, Binta Bello, told a stakeholders' forum in Abuja.

Why it mattersPatients in Plateau face a disease burden that, at this pace, will exceed last year's count before the year closes.

Nigerian stocks gain N351bn on Monday

The Nigerian Exchange Limited's All-Share Index rose 0.21 per cent to 252,809.78 points, with industrial, consumer goods and insurance stocks leading the gains.

Investors on the Nigerian Exchange Limited gained N351.06bn on Monday as buying interest returned to industrial, consumer goods, and insurance stocks. The All-Share Index closed at 252,809.78 points, a 0.21 per cent increase from Friday's close. Total market capitalisation also rose by 0.21 per cent, reaching N164.15tn.

The market breadth was positive, with 34 stocks advancing against 27 declining. Nigerian Exchange Group Plc led the gainers, surging 10.00 per cent to N182.60. LivingTrust Mortgage Bank Plc followed with a 9.87 per cent gain to N2.56. On the losing side, R. T. Briscoe (Nigeria) Plc dropped 9.84 per cent to N8.25.

Sectoral performance was mostly bullish, with the NGX Consumer Goods Index up 0.80 per cent and the NGX Industrial Index up 0.60 per cent. The NGX Banking Index dipped 0.26 per cent, and the NGX Oil and Gas Index declined 0.10 per cent. Trading activity involved 673.59 million shares exchanged across 38,218 deals.

NNPC probes fire at Lagos filling station

The Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited has launched an investigation into a fire that engulfed a filling station in Lagos.

The Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPC) has launched an investigation into a fire that broke out at a filling station in Lagos on Monday. The blaze engulfed the station, causing significant damage and disruption.

NNPC officials went to the scene to assess the damage and gather information on what caused the fire. The company is still reviewing the scale of the damage and the exact circumstances of the incident.

The inquiry is meant to establish the root cause of the fire and any safety lapses. The NNPC has said it will release further details once the investigation is concluded.

Why it mattersMotorists and residents who buy fuel at the station are left waiting on the findings for any change in safety at the site.

Fountain of Life Church removes Jimmy Odukoya as senior pastor

Trustees say they are enforcing the succession plan left by the late founder, Taiwo Odukoya, who died in 2023.

The Board of Trustees of the Fountain of Life Church in Lagos has removed Pastor Jimmy Odukoya as its senior pastor, with the decision taking immediate effect. The board announced it in a statement on Tuesday.

The trustees said the move followed a review of the church's constitution and the recorded instructions of its late founder, Pastor Taiwo Odukoya, who died in 2023. They described it as an institutional decision, but did not disclose the succession arrangements or name who will take over.

Odukoya and the church have rejected the decision. They maintain that the trustees acted outside the church's constitution and that his 2023 appointment remains valid.

Why it mattersOdukoya's congregation now has no recognised senior pastor, and the trustees have not yet said who will lead it.
