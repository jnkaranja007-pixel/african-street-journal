Bolt Stays in Nigeria Despite Uber Exit and Industry Challenges

Ride-hailing firm Bolt has committed to continuing its operations in Nigeria, a market proving difficult for mobility services.

Ride-hailing company Bolt has announced its intention to remain in Nigeria, even as its competitor Uber ceases operations in the country. Bolt stated that Nigeria is a crucial market for its expansion and assured its users, drivers, and stakeholders of its continued focus on strengthening its services.

The company's decision comes amidst significant challenges within Nigeria's mobility sector, which has been described as a tough market for ride-hailing services. Africa's most populous nation presents unique operational hurdles for companies in this industry.

Bolt's commitment signals a strategic choice to navigate the difficulties rather than withdraw. The company aims to solidify its position and continue serving the Nigerian market, emphasizing its long-term growth prospects within the country.

Why it mattersBolt's decision to stay in Nigeria affects riders and drivers by ensuring continued service availability and potential employment opportunities within the ride-hailing sector.

Vice President Shettima Turns Sixty, Offers Lessons

Nigeria's Vice President Kashim Shettima marked his 60th birthday with a life story offering valuable lessons for younger Nigerians.

Vice President Kashim Shettima, a former governor of Borno State, banker, politician, and businessman, reached the milestone of 60 years on Wednesday, September 2. His life journey is presented as a source of inspiration and practical learning for young Nigerians.

While acknowledging that no individual is perfect, the narrative surrounding Shettima's career highlights aspects that can be emulated. His multifaceted background, spanning public service and the private sector, provides a unique perspective on navigating professional and personal challenges.

The occasion serves as a moment to reflect on the experiences of a prominent figure in Nigerian public life. His story is intended to offer tangible takeaways for those aspiring to achieve success in their own endeavors, emphasizing that lessons can be drawn from both achievements and imperfections.

Why it mattersYoung Nigerians can learn from Vice President Shettima's career path and experiences as he turns sixty.

Traffickers Force Nigerians into Cyber-Scams in Asia

Youths lured by job offers in Asia face beatings, electrocution, and forced participation in criminal schemes.

Nigerian youths seeking employment in Asia have been trapped in compounds where they are allegedly forced to conduct online scams, including romance fraud, fake online stores, and cryptocurrency schemes. Victims reported threats of severe physical punishment, including beatings and electrocution, for failing to meet demanding financial targets, some as high as $300,000 per person monthly. Salaries were often reduced, and infractions like sleeping at a desk incurred fines that further diminished earnings.

Survivors described horrific conditions in detention areas known as the “Dark Room,” where individuals were chained in cramped spaces, denied food, and subjected to electrocution. Some detainees died under duress, with bodies allegedly dismembered for organ harvesting before being disposed of in rivers. One Nigerian man reportedly died after being forced to run for two hours non-stop, while others suspected organ harvesting from a Liberian detainee who died after severe beatings.

After months of forced labor and abuse, a group of 83 African detainees, including Nigerians, Ugandans, and Kenyans, were eventually marched into Thailand. While many were convicted of illegal entry and faced fines or short prison sentences, only a fraction were formally recognized as trafficking victims by Thai authorities. Efforts by organizations like NAPTIP and the Nigerian Embassy in Bangkok, alongside local NGOs, played a role in securing the release of some individuals.

Why it mattersNigerian youths are affected by being lured with false job promises and then forced into criminal activities under threat of violence and death in Asian cyber-slave camps.
