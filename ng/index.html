Nigerian Breweries profit rises 33% to N114.1 billion

Revenue grew 5% to N1.10 trillion in nine months ended September 30, 2026, as finance costs fell.

Nigerian Breweries Plc reported a 33% increase in profit after tax to N114.1 billion for the nine months ending September 30, 2026. This growth was driven by a 15% rise in gross profit to N477.9 billion and a significant 78% reduction in finance expenses. The company's revenue also saw a 5% increase, reaching N1.10 trillion during the same period.

Managing Director Thibaut Boidin stated that the nine-month performance demonstrated business resilience and disciplined execution, despite ongoing consumer spending pressures. The brewer achieved a net profit of N21 billion in the third quarter, marking its first profitable third quarter since 2022. Boidin called this return to quarterly profitability an important milestone.

Nigerian Breweries experienced declines in volumes and net revenue in the third quarter, though premium brands like Heineken contributed to market share gains. The company reported stronger cash generation and improved working capital management. Boidin confirmed a continued focus on brand investment, cost control, and cash management for the remainder of 2026.

Why it mattersNigerian Breweries Plc's financial results show increased profitability and revenue, alongside a return to quarterly profit, indicating business recovery.

IAR wins N1.2bn for climate-smart agriculture

Four grants will fund research into drought-tolerant crops, irrigation, and livestock feed for Nigerian farmers.

The Institute for Agricultural Research (IAR) at Ahmadu Bello University (ABU), Zaria, has secured four grants worth N1.213 billion for climate adaptation research. The funding from the Action Research for Climate Adaptation in Agriculture (ARCAA) programme will support practical solutions for farmers facing climate challenges.

The grants, awarded by RTI International and RUFORUM with Gates Foundation support, will fund research into locally sourced livestock feed, solar-powered irrigation, drought-tolerant maize, and resilient groundnut varieties. IAR secured four of the eight successful Nigerian proposals, making it the only institution in the country to win multiple grants.

These initiatives aim to help farmers improve production, adapt to changing climatic conditions, and adopt sustainable practices, particularly in Northern Nigeria. IAR Executive Director Prof. Ado A. Yusuf stated the grants reflect the institute’s research strength and commitment to addressing farmers' challenges and contributing to national food security.

ADC says Atiku won't pick Senate President, Speaker

Dino Melaye stated the African Democratic Congress will allow lawmakers to elect their own leaders for independence.

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) Presidential Campaign Council's Deputy Director-General, Dino Melaye, stated that a presidency under Atiku Abubakar would not dictate the selection of the Senate President or the Speaker of the House of Representatives. Melaye announced on Arise Television that the ADC party is committed to democratic principles and will permit lawmakers to choose their leaders independently. This approach, he explained, aims to foster legislative independence and enhance the National Assembly's oversight capabilities over the executive branch.

Melaye specified that the ADC would empower relevant National Assembly committees, including those for Establishment, Finance, and Budget and Planning, to scrutinize government spending. These committees will be tasked with questioning government officials and monitoring expenditure, particularly concerning State House operations. The ADC official emphasized that this independent selection of leaders would strengthen the National Assembly's oversight functions.

Separately, Tijjani Kaura was declared the winner of the All Progressives Congress (APC) rerun senatorial primary election for Zamfara North. This primary followed violence at voting centers, and Senator Sahabi Ya’u Kaura was announced as the victor ahead of the 2027 elections.

Why it mattersNigerian lawmakers will have the freedom to elect their leaders without presidential interference, according to Dino Melaye of the ADC.

Police rescue 11 kidnapped victims

19 suspects arrested and 91 livestock recovered in security operations across several states.

Nigeria Police Force rescued 11 kidnapped victims and arrested 19 suspects in operations over the past 48 hours. The security forces also recovered 91 rustled livestock, including 48 cows and 43 sheep, along with firearms and stolen valuables. These actions are part of sustained efforts to combat kidnapping and other criminal activities.

In Zamfara State, five elderly women were rescued from armed bandits in Dansadau after a gun battle, though a 35-year-old farmer was killed during the attack. In Niger State, six farmers abducted in Rafi Local Government Area were freed by a joint security team. Police in Kano arrested five suspects connected to the abduction of an APC Chairman, recovering 48 cows and 43 sheep.

Security forces in Kebbi State repelled an attack on Ribah town on October 30, following an earlier incident where nine people were killed and property destroyed in Dan-Indo village. Coordinated search-and-rescue operations are underway in Zamfara for nine persons abducted near Kaura Namoda. Tactical teams in Katsina foiled an attempted bandit attack on Karofi village.

Jigawa commits N4.5bn to cattle ranches

The state plans three ranches with modern facilities to boost livestock and reduce farmer-herder conflicts.

The Jigawa State Government has committed N4.5 billion to establish three cattle ranches across the state. The Commissioner for Livestock Development, Salim Abdulrahman, announced the funding, which is part of the N17.2 billion allocated to livestock development in the 2026 fiscal year. The ranches will be located in Sankara Grazing Reserve (Ringim), Kashamai Grazing Reserve (Kafin Hausa), and Kukar Mekiya (Kiyawa).

Each 510-hectare ranch will accommodate 3,000 households and feature modern facilities including pasture, water points, schools, healthcare, veterinary clinics, markets, electricity, and water supply. Abdulrahman stated the initiative aims to combat frequent clashes between farmers and herders in Jigawa. The government also plans veterinary clinics in all 27 local government areas and mobile clinics across the state.

The state has earmarked N350 million for livestock vaccination and is upgrading three livestock breeding centres. MACBAN National Deputy Treasurer Abdullahi Abubakar supported the transition to modern ranching, suggesting it could reduce land and resource competition that triggers disputes.

Why it mattersJigawa State's N4.5 billion investment in cattle ranches will directly affect livestock farmers by providing improved facilities and reducing conflicts.
