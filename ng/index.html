Nigeria's power minister names gas, grid and metering failures

Joseph Tegbe told senior editors in Lagos that gas supply, ageing equipment, vandalism and poor metering are holding back the electricity sector.

Nigeria's Minister of Power, Joseph Tegbe, has named gas supply constraints, ageing infrastructure, vandalism, transmission limits, poor metering and commercial problems as systemic faults in the country's electricity sector. Speaking at an interactive luncheon with senior editors in Lagos on Friday, November 13, 2026, he listed them as the underlying causes of the sector's weakness.

Tegbe sought media partnership to build public trust in the sector and said the problems together hold back its performance and stability. He argued that fixing them needs government, private operators and the public to work together.

The minister did not outline specific government plans for tackling the problems at the luncheon. Power supply has been inconsistent for households and businesses across the country.

Why it mattersHouseholds and businesses that already face unreliable power now have the minister's own list of the faults behind it, and no timetable for fixing them.

Analyst says Nigeria is already a one-man state, not a one-party state

The ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) now controls 31 of Nigeria's 36 states, up from 21 at the end of the 2023 election cycle.

Suleiman A. Suleiman, writing in Vanguard, argues that Nigeria is already a one-man state under President Bola Ahmed Tinubu. The APC has gained 10 sitting governors who defected from their original parties in the past year, while none have moved in the other direction.

Suleiman says the Peoples Democratic Party is now a one-man show, with little clarity on what it stands for beyond FCT Minister Nyesom Wike. The Labour Party and the New Nigeria Peoples Party won 63 National Assembly seats between them in 2023, but Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso have since left both parties to meet on one presidential ticket in the Nigeria Democratic Congress.

The African Democratic Congress is the opposition party in the most trouble, he writes, with its candidates from the presidential to the state assembly races in limbo for almost a year after court rulings on the dispute of one party member.

Why it mattersOpposition parties are losing their candidates and their platforms, which leaves voters in the 2027 election with fewer real choices.

Nigeria signs $3m deal for 1,000 climate-smart tomato greenhouses in Kano

The deal, signed at COP31 in Türkiye, is funded by the Bank of Agriculture and Turkish firm Direkci Group, which will each put in $1.5 million.

Kano State will host 1,000 climate-smart greenhouses for tomato growing under a $3 million investment agreement that Nigeria has signed. Turkish firm Direkci Group and the Bank of Agriculture are the partners, and the memorandum of understanding was witnessed by Agriculture Minister Abubakar Kyari at the COP31 climate summit in Türkiye on Wednesday.

Under the Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zones (SAPZ) programme, the bank will fund a financing mechanism for farmers taking part, while Direkci Group supplies technology, engineering, installation and technical support. Kyari said the project showed how state institutions and private investors could work together to modernise farming, and said women and smallholder farmers would be prioritised for assets, finance, mentoring and market links.

The partners will hold a joint performance review after nine months. A commercial expansion worth at least $30 million in greenhouse systems and equipment could follow, depending on agreed purchases and performance targets. Kyari warned that success would depend on transparent farmer selection, timely infrastructure delivery and measurable results.

Why it mattersSmallholder and women farmers in Kano get first access to greenhouses, finance and mentoring, and a nine-month review decides whether the project expands.

IFAD, AFD sign deal for rural transformation in Nigeria

The International Fund for Agricultural Development and Agence Française de Développement will co-finance a programme supporting nine northern states.

The International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) and the Agence Française de Développement (AFD) signed a co-financing agreement in Abuja to support the Value Chain Programme in Northern Nigeria (VCN). IFAD Country Director Mohamed El-Ghazaly and AFD Country Director Jacky Amprou signed the deal, which aims to reduce poverty and improve nutrition in rural communities.

The VCN programme, a flagship initiative of the Nigerian Federal Government, will be co-financed by IFAD, AFD, and the governments of Bauchi, Borno, Jigawa, Kano, Katsina, Kebbi, Sokoto, Yobe, and Zamfara states. IFAD will act as the lead co-financier, providing technical and support services for AFD's contribution, establishing a coordinated framework for joint financing and implementation.

IFAD and AFD urged the federal and state governments to accelerate remaining start-up activities, including preparations for a programme workshop scheduled for early December 2026. Both institutions reaffirmed their commitment to inclusive agricultural growth and resilient food systems in northern Nigeria.

Why it mattersIFAD and AFD have committed to co-financing a programme in nine northern Nigerian states to boost rural incomes and food security.

US basketball player arrested with 34kg cannabis in Lagos

Greene Da'monte Khiree was detained at the airport after abandoning luggage containing a potent strain of cannabis.

US basketball player Greene Da'monte Khiree, 28, was arrested at Lagos airport on Wednesday with 33.95kg of cannabis.

The National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) said Khiree arrived from Thailand via the United Arab Emirates and was found with 32 packs of a potent cannabis strain known as "Loud" in his checked bags.

The agency stated that Khiree, who played professionally in Thailand, claimed he was visiting Nigeria to build career networks and that his phone data indicated his involvement in drug trafficking.
