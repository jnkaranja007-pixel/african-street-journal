Osun building collapse kills three

Three people died and five were rescued after a two-storey building collapsed in Osun State.

A two-storey commercial building under construction collapsed at Owode-Ede, in Ede North Local Government Area of Osun State, on Friday night.

Three people died and five were rescued alive from the incident at Zone 8, Oke Alaafia Community. The Federal Fire Service received a distress call at 11:30pm and found the building had six shops on the upper and middle floors and two stores on the ground floor. Structural defects and substandard materials are suspected causes. The five rescued victims were taken to Idera Specialist Hospital, Ede.

The rescue operation concluded at 9:26am on Saturday. The estimated property loss was N100 million, while N150 million worth of property was saved. The operation involved the Osun State Fire Service, the Nigeria Police Force, and the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps.

Abuja hosts African prosperity conference

Leaders from government, business, and tech will meet on November 3 to discuss economic growth strategies.

Prominent African leaders, including former African Development Bank President Dr. Akinwumi Adesina, Emir of Kano Muhammadu Sanusi II, and Abia State Governor Alex Otti, are set to gather in Abuja on November 3 for the second Oxford Global Think Tank Leadership Conference. The event aims to devise strategies for a prosperous, resilient, and inclusive African economy, bringing together experts from government, business, technology, finance, and civil society.

The conference, themed “Leadership for a Prosperous Future,” will examine leadership, institutional reforms, and technological innovation. Dr. Adesina will deliver the keynote address, while Sanusi and Atedo Peterside will co-chair. Discussions will cover artificial intelligence, global power shifts, demographic changes, and economic challenges, with a focus on strengthening institutions, mobilizing investment, and expanding economic opportunities.

Organizers stated the conference seeks practical steps and partnerships to boost Africa's economic potential, improve productive capacity, and promote inclusive growth. The event, hosted by the Oxford Global Think Tank, will take place at the Transcorp Hilton Hotel in Abuja from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., featuring an exhibition on emerging technologies and the launch of an AI Virtual Centre.

Why it mattersAfrican leaders will discuss strategies for economic growth, institutional reforms, and technological innovation to build a prosperous continent.

MTN MoMo revenue jumps 102.2% in nine months

Customer wallet balances for MTN Nigeria's mobile money service more than quadrupled to N43.8 billion.

MTN MoMo Payment Service Bank (MoMo PSB) in Nigeria saw its revenue climb by 102.2% in the nine months leading up to September 30, 2026. This growth occurred as customer wallet balances surged to N43.8 billion.

The financial results released by MTN Nigeria Communications Plc show that the mobile money business experienced significant expansion. Wallet balances, a key indicator of customer engagement, increased by more than four times their previous value.

These figures reflect the growing adoption of mobile money services in Nigeria.

Nigerian TVET programme makes UNESCO awards final

A Nigerian Technical and Vocational Education and Training programme has been selected as a finalist for the UNESCO global awards.

A Nigerian Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) programme has reached the final stage for the prestigious UNESCO global awards.

The programme, which focuses on skills development, was recognised for its innovative approach to vocational training. Officials stated the selection highlights the quality of education and training initiatives within Nigeria.

The final award ceremony is scheduled to take place at a later date, with further details on the specific TVET programme to be announced.

Why it mattersA Nigerian TVET programme has been recognised internationally by UNESCO, potentially elevating the profile of vocational education in the country.

Nigeria cocoa exports to US jump 554%

Shipments reached $142 million in 2025, up from $21.7 million the previous year, according to the US Mission in Nigeria.

Nigeria's cocoa bean exports to the United States surged by 554.4% to $142 million in 2025, a significant increase from $21.7 million in 2024. The United States Mission in Nigeria reported this growth, attributing it to rising demand in the American market and expanding trade opportunities.

The US mission highlighted that this export boom from Nigeria is evidence of a fair, two-way trade relationship. Despite this surge, Nigeria faces structural challenges in its cocoa industry, including ageing plantations and limited access to financing, which affect productivity and farmer investment.

More than 80% of Nigeria's cocoa is exported raw, limiting local value addition. The growth in US exports occurred as global cocoa prices fell from approximately $10,950 per tonne to around $5,400 by November 2025, posing further challenges for producers.

Why it mattersNigerian cocoa farmers and businesses are affected by increased export demand from the US, but face challenges from ageing farms, poor financing, and falling global prices.
