Nigeria quarantines Ebola contacts

The Federal Government has placed 46 contacts of an Ebola patient under quarantine and restricted their travel.

Nigeria's Federal Government has quarantined 46 high-risk contacts of a confirmed Ebola Virus Disease patient in Abuja and restricted their air travel. The government is also strengthening preparedness across states and health facilities to prevent wider community transmission. The index patient, who arrived from Uganda, is showing signs of recovery, and there is currently no evidence of community spread, according to the Coordinating Minister of Health and Social Welfare, Prof. Ali Pate.

Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, directed the Federal Airports Authority of Nigeria (FAAN) to place the 46 contacts on a no-fly list. This measure aims to prevent individuals who may develop symptoms from travelling. The Nigeria Civil Aviation Authority (NCAA) has been notified to inform domestic and international airlines of the restriction. Heightened surveillance, including temperature checks and digital health declarations, is in place at international airports.

Contact tracing has identified individuals at the hospital where the patient was treated, her domestic contacts, and a private medical facility visited. High-risk contacts are isolated in a designated facility, while others are monitored. The patient's travel history indicates the case was imported, with symptoms developing days after arrival in Nigeria.

Why it mattersNigeria has confirmed one case of Ebola Virus Disease and is taking measures to prevent its spread.

24 states approve state police bill

Nigeria's constitution requires 24 state approvals for the bill to become law, with 10 northern and 14 southern states already endorsing it.

Nigeria's push for state police has reached a benchmark with 24 states approving the constitutional amendment bill. This number meets the endorsement required for the bill to become law, following approvals from 14 southern and 10 northern states. The National Assembly transmitted the bill to state legislatures on September 16, seeking concurrence within 30 days.

The Kaduna State House of Assembly unanimously endorsed the bill yesterday, joining the 23 other states that have given their approval. Mr. Henry Marah, Chairman of the House Committee on Information, stated that the proposed state police system would strengthen community security and improve protection of lives and property. He assured that funding would be prioritised, even if it meant reducing spending on other projects.

While 24 states have approved, nine northern states including Kogi, Niger, Borno, Gombe, Bauchi, Jigawa, Katsina, Kano, and Sokoto, are still deliberating. Lagos, Ondo, and Ebonyi are the southern states yet to conclude their reviews. State legislatures are expected to submit their final resolutions by October 29.

Why it mattersThe establishment of state police will affect the security of all Nigerians by potentially improving protection of lives and property.

Interpol arrests two Nigerians over $2.4m fraud

Operation Serengeti III targeted cybercrime, leading to arrests in phishing and investment scams.

The International Criminal Police Organisation (INTERPOL) arrested two suspects in Nigeria as part of Operation Serengeti III, a cybercrime enforcement operation. One suspect is linked to a phishing and business email compromise operation that amassed 2.1 million email addresses, with investigators recovering a luxury vehicle. A second suspect was arrested over an international investment fraud scheme promoted through messaging applications.

Financial investigations connected the investment fraud scheme to the movement of more than $2.4m in suspected criminal proceeds, much of which was allegedly laundered through cryptocurrency transactions. INTERPOL stated that the operation, coordinated between June 29 and August 31, 2026, involved law enforcement agencies from 21 countries. The names of the two Nigerian suspects were not disclosed.

Operation Serengeti III targeted cyber-enabled fraud, ransomware, business email compromise, online scams, and digital extortion. The 21 participating countries arrested 1,462 suspects and recovered more than $105m in illicit funds. Authorities identified 65,929 victims targeted by cybercriminal networks, with financial investigations linking activities to losses exceeding $385m.

Why it mattersTwo Nigerian suspects were arrested for involvement in international fraud schemes that moved over $2.4m in suspected criminal proceeds.

Nigeria plans framework for farmer climate finance

A new system aims to guide investments for adaptation and resilience against drought and flooding.

Nigeria is developing a framework to improve access to climate finance for small-scale farmers, guiding investments in agricultural adaptation and protecting rural livelihoods. The initiative will provide financial institutions with clear guidelines for supporting farming activities resilient to climate challenges like drought and flooding. This framework was discussed at a validation workshop in Abuja organised by the Presidential Implementation Committee on Technology Transfer (PICTT) and YAPU Solutions.

PICTT Chairman Dahiru Muhammed stated the framework aims to translate Nigeria’s national climate taxonomy into a practical system for accessing and channeling funds. He explained that while the Federal Ministry of Finance and the UN Development Programme developed the taxonomy, PICTT and partners are establishing implementation mechanisms for rural communities. Muhammed stressed that defining eligible climate adaptation activities is essential for farmers and agricultural enterprises to benefit from financing.

Country Representative of WorldFish in Nigeria, Charles Nyangbe, noted the need for financing solutions tailored to varying regional environmental conditions, such as drought in the north and flooding in the south. YAPU Solutions Managing Director Christoph Junghleisch estimated smallholder farmers could need up to $30 billion annually to respond to climate risks, describing climate adaptation as a significant investment opportunity.

Why it mattersSmall-scale farmers in Nigeria will be directly affected by a new framework designed to improve their access to climate finance for adaptation.

Agriculture · 2026-10-23

Nigeria cocoa exports to US reach $142 million

Trade value surged more than sixfold in 2025, despite ongoing domestic production challenges.

Nigeria's cocoa bean exports to the United States dramatically increased to $142 million in 2025, up from $21.7 million in 2024. This sixfold rise signals growing trade opportunities, though the domestic industry faces production constraints. The US Mission in Nigeria announced these figures on Tuesday, citing increased demand for Nigerian cocoa.

The export growth occurs as Nigeria aims to boost non-oil exports and revitalize its agricultural sector. Challenges include ageing plantations, low farm productivity, limited financing, and inadequate processing. Many cocoa farmers are around 60 years old, with trees aged 20 to 40 years, further hindering production. High input costs and poor-quality seedlings also discourage youth involvement.

Despite the higher export values, farmers may not see improved earnings due to falling global prices and declining production. Global cocoa prices dropped from $10,950 per tonne to approximately $5,400 by November 2025. Nigeria's production was also projected to fall by 11 per cent. Stakeholders suggest strengthening cooperatives, improving input access, and investing in processing facilities to enhance farmer returns and bean quality.

Why it mattersNigerian farmers face challenges in benefiting from increased export values due to falling global prices and production issues.
