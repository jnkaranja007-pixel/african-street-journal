IAR secures N1.2bn for climate-smart farming

Four grants will fund research into drought-tolerant crops, irrigation, and livestock feed for Nigerian farmers.

The Institute for Agricultural Research (IAR) at Ahmadu Bello University in Zaria has won four grants totaling N1.213 billion to support climate-smart agriculture in Nigeria. The funding comes from the Action Research for Climate Adaptation in Agriculture (ARCAA) programme, a collaboration between RTI International and RUFORUM, with support from the Gates Foundation.

These grants will finance research into practical solutions for farmers facing climate challenges, including livestock feed shortages, irrigation water access, and drought-tolerant crop varieties. IAR secured four of the eight successful Nigerian proposals, making it the sole institution in the country to receive multiple grants from the programme, which had 149 initial concept notes.

The funded projects will explore locally sourced livestock feed alternatives, solar-powered irrigation systems, and the adoption of drought-tolerant maize and resilient groundnut varieties. IAR Executive Director Prof. Ado A. Yusuf stated the grants reflect the institute's research strength and commitment to addressing farmers' challenges and contributing to national food security.

Nigeria's power sector sale fails to end generator reliance

13 years after privatization, most Nigerians still depend on generators for electricity, with diesel costs acting as the real tariff.

Most Nigerians continue to rely on generators for electricity 13 years after the power sector was sold. The generator remains the primary source of power, and diesel expenses function as the actual electricity tariff for many households and businesses.

Professor Barth Nnaji, founder of Geometric Power and a former Minister of Power, addressed this persistent issue at the Lagos Country Club Business Forum 3.0. He explained the reasons behind the continued dependence on private generators despite the sector's privatization.

The forum highlighted that the sale of the power sector has not yet fulfilled its promise of reliable national electricity supply. The ongoing reliance on generators indicates a significant gap between the intended outcomes of privatization and the lived reality for Nigerians.

Why it mattersNigerians continue to spend money on diesel for generators because the privatized power sector has not provided consistent electricity.

ASUU threatens strike over UniAbuja land allocation

The union says FCT Minister Nyesom Wike allocated university land to private individuals despite a court order.

The Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU) at the University of Abuja has threatened industrial action if the FCT Minister, Nyesom Wike, does not reverse the alleged unlawful allocation of university land. ASUU Chairperson, Prof. Sylvanus Ugoh, stated on Tuesday in Abuja that the union could no longer guarantee industrial harmony on campus.

Ugoh cited a court order from August 18, 2026, from Justice F.E. Mesiri of Maitama High Court, restraining the FCT Minister from encroaching on university land. Despite this, Wike allegedly allocated portions of the university's 11,824 hectares to companies and individuals on September 26, 2026. The minister had previously announced on June 30, 2025, the retrieval of 7,000 hectares from the university's allocation.

The union argued that the land, allocated in 1988, is federal government property intended for long-term development, research, and environmental conservation. ASUU pointed to other universities like the University of Ilorin (15,000 hectares) and Obafemi Awolowo University (13,000 hectares) to justify the University of Abuja's land size.

Why it mattersUniversity staff and students face potential disruption to academic activities if the land dispute escalates into industrial action.

Agriculture · 2026-11-04

Ogun govt backs Tinubu's livestock plan

Ogun State pledges to boost livestock sector and economic chances through collaboration with Northern regions.

The Ogun State Government has pledged to continue supporting President Bola Ahmed Tinubu's livestock development plans. Special Adviser on Agriculture, Dr. Angel Adelaja-Kuye, stated this on October 31, 2026, at a rally in Kaduna. She affirmed the South's commitment to collaborating with Northern Nigeria to strengthen the livestock sector and create economic opportunities.

Dr. Adelaja-Kuye commended the Federal Ministry of Livestock Development for its positive impact on farmers, including cattle ranchers, pastoralists, and poultry farmers, particularly in Ogun State. She noted that ministry programs have also aided peaceful coexistence between farmers and pastoralists through livestock development initiatives.

The Ogun official acknowledged past challenges in the livestock sector but highlighted that current interventions are yielding progress. She expressed appreciation for the President's commitment to empowering citizens and expanding economic opportunities through the livestock industry, calling for four more years of support.

Why it mattersOgun State government officials are supporting federal initiatives to improve the livestock sector, which affects farmers and pastoralists.
