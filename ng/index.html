Nigerian court voids Atiku Abubakar's presidential candidacy

A Federal High Court ruling has invalidated the nomination of former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar as the ADC's presidential candidate.

Nigeria's Federal High Court on November 5, 2026, nullified the leadership of the opposition African Democratic Congress (ADC) party, including the nomination of its presidential candidate, Atiku Abubakar. The court ruled that the party's interim leadership, headed by David Mark, was unlawfully constituted. This decision came after a challenge by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former ADC deputy chairman, who argued that Mark and others were not elected at a national convention.

The ruling throws Abubakar's seventh presidential bid into uncertainty ahead of the January 16 elections. The ADC, which had aimed to unite opposition forces against President Bola Tinubu, is now embroiled in a leadership dispute. Abubakar, who served as vice-president from 1999 to 2007, had joined the ADC in 2025 along with other politicians disillusioned with the Peoples Democratic Party.

The Mark faction plans to appeal the judgment, asserting that a previous court ruling in July had addressed leadership issues favorably. However, the differing legal arguments and plaintiffs in the two cases mean the relationship between the judgments requires further judicial review. The Supreme Court had previously ordered an expedited hearing at the Federal High Court.

Why it mattersAtiku Abubakar's presidential candidacy is in doubt, potentially affecting the main opposition's challenge in the upcoming elections.

Nigeria loses $1.6bn to avoidable blindness yearly

Professor Ada Aghaji stated that vision impairment costs the nation billions in lost productivity annually.

Nigeria is losing an estimated $1.6 billion each year due to vision impairment, according to Professor Ada Aghaji. This loss stems from reduced productivity among individuals who suffer from conditions like cataracts or uncorrected refractive errors. The problem affects farmers unable to distinguish crops from weeds, traders struggling with figures, and children whose education is hindered by blurred vision.

Delivering the University of Nigeria's 254th Inaugural Lecture on October 29, 2026, Aghaji, a Professor of Ophthalmology and Eye Health Systems, highlighted that much of this vision loss is preventable or treatable. She contrasted the case of a Nigerian child facing treatment barriers with a Somali refugee child in England who received timely surgery and aids, illustrating systemic disparities in eye care access.

Aghaji argued that eye health in Africa is an economic, development, and justice issue, with Africa losing about $9 billion annually and globally the figure reaches $410 billion. She identified poverty, a shortage of trained health workers, weak referral systems, and inadequate integration of eye care into primary health services as key barriers to treatment.

Why it mattersNigeria loses $1.6 billion annually in productivity due to vision impairment, affecting livelihoods and economic output.

Politics · 2026-11-07

Aguda: Tinubu reforms need time to yield results

Businessman Oluwole Aguda stated that Nigerians must see a credible path to relief from economic adjustments.

Businessman Oluwole Aguda stated that President Bola Tinubu's economic reforms, including the removal of fuel subsidy and naira devaluation, have not yet translated into improved living conditions for most Nigerians. Aguda, a former governorship aspirant, noted that the cost of food, transportation, housing, and healthcare continues to pressure households.

Aguda explained that while the intention behind the reforms is clear, execution has been challenging, and economic recovery should be measured by affordability of necessities, job creation, and living standards. He added that the administration's case for a second term must demonstrate these tangible improvements, not just the courage to implement reforms.

He also pointed to the Lagos-Calabar Coastal Highway as a project promising reduced travel time and costs. Aguda stressed that policy continuity is crucial for national growth, warning that policy reversals hinder development and that calls to restore fuel subsidy must consider public finance implications.

Senate advances bill to modernise Nigerian agriculture

The proposed legislation aims to strengthen food security, productivity, and climate-resilient farming practices across the nation.

Nigeria's Senate has passed a bill to establish a comprehensive legal framework for modernising the agricultural sector, moving it to the next stage of legislative review. Deputy Senate President Jibrin Barau sponsored the bill, which seeks to improve food security, agricultural productivity, mechanisation, irrigation, financing, agro-industrialisation, rural development, and climate-resilient farming.

Senate President Godswill Akpabio referred the bill, titled 'A Bill for an Act to Provide a Comprehensive Legal Framework for the Transformation and Modernisation of Agriculture in Nigeria,' to the Senate Committees on Agricultural Production, Services and Rural Development, and Agricultural Colleges and Institutions. These committees have four weeks to submit their reports. Barau stated that agriculture, once a major economic driver, had declined, and that Nigeria lacked effective structures to harness its potential despite possessing human and natural resources.

Senate Whip Mohammed Monguno noted that agriculture accounts for about 40 per cent of Nigeria’s Gross Domestic Product and provides approximately 70 per cent of employment, but budgetary allocations have been low. The bill aims to address constraints in mechanisation, agricultural financing, rural infrastructure, and post-harvest management.

Why it mattersFarmers and those involved in agricultural production will benefit from potential improvements in food security, productivity, and infrastructure.

Business · 2026-11-07

FourthCanvas expands to UK market

The Nigerian design consultancy has established a UK operation and secured its first fintech client.

Nigerian design consultancy FourthCanvas has launched operations in the United Kingdom, appointing Tunji Ogunoye as Country Director. This marks the company's first international expansion as it celebrates its 11th anniversary.

The Lagos-headquartered firm has incorporated in the UK and already secured its first client in the fintech sector. Ogunoye, a longtime team member, will lead the new UK operation.

FourthCanvas's expansion into the UK follows 11 years of operation since its founding. The company's move aims to establish a formal international presence.

Why it mattersNigerian company FourthCanvas has expanded its business operations into the United Kingdom.
