Opposition suit against Tinubu's eligibility stalls over service

A legal challenge to President Bola Tinubu's eligibility for the 2027 presidential election has been postponed due to issues with serving the president.

A legal challenge filed by Atiku Abubakar against President Bola Tinubu's eligibility for the 2027 presidential election has been stalled. The court adjourned the case to a new date following a disagreement between lawyers regarding the proper service of the suit on President Tinubu. This development means the legal question of Tinubu's eligibility remains unresolved for now.

The opposition's attempt to unite against President Tinubu for the 2027 election is seen by some as a recurring tactic in Nigerian political history, with past efforts failing against incumbents. Unlike the 2015 election, where a strong opposition coalition formed and made significant sacrifices, the current opposition landscape is described as far from that level of unity and preparedness.

The ruling party, under President Tinubu, currently controls a substantial number of states and possesses a formidable nationwide structure, including a large representation in the National Assembly. This contrasts with the situation in 2015, when the ruling party was fractured and faced significant challenges, including internal defections and external pressures, which paved the way for the opposition's victory.

Why it mattersThe stalled legal challenge affects the political landscape and the potential for opposition parties to unseat the incumbent president.

Driver's Fatigue Causes Fatal Crash, Leads to Six-Year Sentence

A Nigerian-British taxi driver, who drove for nearly three days straight, fell asleep at the wheel and killed a man in the UK.

Kolawole Erunkulu, a taxi driver with joint British and Nigerian nationality, has been sentenced to six years in prison for causing the death of 59-year-old Philip Dray. The fatal collision occurred when Erunkulu, who had been driving for approximately 53 hours with minimal rest, experienced a microsleep. His Audi veered into a layby, striking Mr. Dray as he was about to re-enter his parked Volkswagen. Mr. Dray died at the scene.

Surrey Police revealed that Erunkulu's longest rest period in the three days prior to the crash was only seven hours. In the 12 hours immediately preceding the incident, he had been working as a taxi driver with only short breaks. CCTV footage from inside his vehicle showed Erunkulu nodding off before the car drifted into the layby. He continued driving for about ten seconds after the impact before stopping.

Erunkulu pleaded guilty to causing death by dangerous driving and was sentenced at Guildford Crown Court. He received an eight-year driving ban. The judge noted that Erunkulu was clearly extremely tired and should have stopped driving. Police highlighted the case as an example of the severe dangers associated with driving while fatigued, especially during the early morning hours.

Why it mattersThe driver's extreme fatigue and prolonged driving hours led to a fatal accident, resulting in a prison sentence and driving ban for the driver.

Nigeria's Forex Liquidity Strengthens Amidst Record Diaspora Inflows

External reserves surpass $53 billion as July diaspora remittances reach $947 million, nearing the central bank's target.

Nigeria is experiencing improved foreign exchange liquidity as September begins, with external reserves now exceeding $53 billion. July saw record formal diaspora inflows totaling $947 million, falling just $53 million short of the Central Bank of Nigeria's (CBN) monthly goal of $1 billion. This strengthening liquidity is occurring as Nigeria anticipates a return to the FTSE Russell Frontier Market universe this month.

The naira traded at varying rates across official and parallel markets on September 2, 2026. The official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market (NFEM) recorded the naira at N1,329.43 to the US dollar on September 1, with a closing rate of N1,329.00 per dollar. This official rate is derived from the volume-weighted average of transactions within the formal banking system.

In contrast, the parallel market continued to trade the dollar at higher rates. Aboki Forex reported dollar buying at approximately N1,400 and selling at N1,405 on September 2, while NairaToday placed the rate around N1,410. This disparity highlights the ongoing gap between regulated transactions and rates determined by supply and demand in the informal sector, with actual rates varying by dealer, location, and transaction size.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the strengthening foreign exchange liquidity and the narrowing gap between official and parallel market rates, which can impact the cost of imports and the repatriation of profits.

Journalist's death casts pall over Nigerian media industry

Dotun's extensive career spanned multiple prominent Nigerian newspapers before he launched his own online publication.

The Nigerian media industry is mourning the loss of Dotun, a journalist whose death has cast a significant shadow over the profession. His career was marked by a diverse range of experiences, contributing to publications such as Newswatch, The PUNCH, The Sun, The Nigerian Tribune, and The Compass.

Beyond his work at established outlets, Dotun also founded The Eagle Online, demonstrating an entrepreneurial spirit within the media landscape. His journey through various journalistic roles highlights a deep commitment to reporting and a broad understanding of the industry's different facets.

The impact of his passing is felt across the sector, as his contributions and the breadth of his professional journey are recognized. His career trajectory serves as a testament to his dedication and influence within Nigerian journalism.

Why it mattersThe death of Dotun affects the Nigerian media industry by removing a seasoned storyteller and entrepreneur who had a significant career across multiple publications.
