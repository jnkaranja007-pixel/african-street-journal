Nigeria confirms Ebola Bundibugyo strain

The World Health Organisation stated that the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, confirmed in Nigeria's capital, has no vaccine or specific treatment.

Nigeria has confirmed its first case of the Ebola virus, identified as the Bundibugyo strain, in the Federal Capital Territory. The patient, a woman, arrived in Abuja on October 3 via Ethiopian Airlines Flight ET333 from Uganda. She received care at a private hospital before diagnosis and has since been moved to an isolation facility. The World Health Organisation has stated that this strain of the virus has no vaccine or specific treatment.

Health authorities have initiated extensive contact-tracing efforts, identifying 44 close contacts including healthcare workers, patients, and caregivers. The Federal Government has placed health institutions and port health surveillance units nationwide on high alert. Uganda has requested detailed travel and clinical records from Nigeria, asserting the patient did not contract the virus within its territory as there are no current Ebola cases there.

The National Assembly has urged the Federal Government to release emergency funds to relevant agencies to combat the disease and to make passenger screening mandatory at all entry points and borders. They also called for an immediate amendment to the Quarantine Act of 1926. Health officials are urging the public to remain calm and follow advice from health authorities.

Why it mattersNigerians are at risk of contracting the Bundibugyo Ebola strain, which has no specific treatment or vaccine, potentially leading to severe illness.

Standard Chartered names Adelagun CEO

Ayodeji Adelagun has been confirmed as the new Chief Executive Officer of Standard Chartered Bank Nigeria Limited after serving as acting CEO since January.

Standard Chartered Bank Nigeria Limited has confirmed Ayodeji Adelagun as its Chief Executive Officer following regulatory approval by the Central Bank of Nigeria. Adelagun, who has over 26 years of banking experience in Africa, has been acting CEO since January 2026. He previously held senior roles at the bank, including Head of Markets for Nigeria and Africa, and Head of Rates and Credit Trading for West Africa.

Adelagun stated that Nigeria remains a significant long-term growth market for Standard Chartered, with an ambition to deliver greater value for clients and the economy. His focus will be on deepening client relationships, driving sustainable growth, and leveraging the bank's international network for Nigerian clients. He is also Chairman of Standard Chartered Capital and Advisory Nigeria.

A Fellow of the Financial Markets Dealers Association, Adelagun was instrumental in introducing benchmark bonds in Nigeria, which supported the country's inclusion in the JP Morgan Emerging Markets Index. He also pioneered bond trading desks at FSDH and Standard Chartered Bank and previously worked in various roles at Access Bank Plc.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as Standard Chartered Bank Nigeria Limited has appointed a new CEO focused on deepening client relationships and driving sustainable growth in the Nigerian market.

Nigeria places 46 Ebola contacts on no-fly list

Authorities have imposed a 21-day travel restriction on individuals who had contact with an Ebola-infected person as part of containment efforts.

Nigeria's federal government has ordered 46 individuals who were in contact with an Ebola-infected person to be placed on a 21-day no-fly list. This measure is part of intensified efforts by authorities to contain the spread of the virus.

The aviation minister confirmed the restriction, stating that the 46 contacts will face a 21-day no-fly restriction. This action aims to prevent potential transmission of the virus through travel.

The government is implementing stringent measures to manage the Ebola outbreak. The no-fly order for exposed individuals is a key component of the public health strategy to limit further spread.

Why it matters46 individuals are affected by a 21-day no-fly restriction due to their contact with an Ebola-infected person.

Comedian Broda Shaggi recounts stray bullet injury

Nollywood actor Samuel Perry, known as Broda Shaggi, described his fear and uncertainty after being shot by a stray bullet while filming a skit.

Nollywood actor and comedian Samuel Perry, known as Broda Shaggi, has detailed his recovery from a stray bullet injury that halted his career for months. The incident occurred while he was filming a comedy skit under the Sango-Ota bridge. Perry recounted being overwhelmed by fear, seeing blood, and praying for survival until he received first aid at a hospital. He expressed concern about his ability to continue performing, as dancing and physical activity are integral to his work.

The injury led Perry to worry about his future in entertainment and whether fans would forget him during his absence. He underwent surgery in Nigeria and later sought further treatment and physiotherapy in Dubai, where his recovery progressed rapidly. Perry stated he could stand with support two days after the Dubai operation and walk with crutches by the third day, describing his survival as a second chance.

Perry, who had maintained a demanding work schedule since 2018, feared his prolonged absence would lead to public amnesia. He is now able to stand without crutches but still uses them for support. The comedian expressed gratitude for being alive and promised fans new projects, urging them to anticipate his return to their screens with exciting content.

Why it mattersThe comedian and his fans are affected as the injury disrupted his career and left him uncertain about his ability to perform.
