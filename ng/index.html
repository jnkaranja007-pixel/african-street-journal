Court voids atiku's 2027 candidacy

A federal high court in Abuja has nullified the process that selected Atiku Abubakar as the African Democratic Congress presidential candidate.

The Federal High Court in Abuja has removed the executive body of the African Democratic Congress led by Senator David Mark. The court also voided all decisions made by this group, including the selection of former vice president Atiku Abubakar as the party's presidential candidate for the 2027 general elections.

This court decision impacts the selection process for Atiku Abubakar's presidential ticket, which occurred hours after he campaigned in Kebbi. In a separate incident, terrorists attacked Akwando village in Kaduna State, resulting in two deaths and the abduction of 18 villagers.

Families of 10 travellers, including former NYSC member Olusoji Adedapo, are seeking their release after being kidnapped between Ogun and Oyo states, demanding N25 million. Reports also indicate a nationwide decline in electricity supply, with Distribution Companies receiving 2,926 megawatts due to operational issues.

Why it mattersThe court ruling directly affects Atiku Abubakar's presidential aspirations for 2027, while other reports highlight security challenges and a worsening power situation.

Afreximbank targets over $540m deals at Lagos CANEX

The African Export-Import Bank aims to finance African music, film, and fashion businesses.

The African Export-Import Bank (Afreximbank) aims to secure creative industry deals exceeding $540m at the Creative Africa Nexus Weekend 2026 in Lagos. The bank seeks to expand financing for African music, film, fashion, and other cultural businesses.

Nigeria's creative industry is set to receive a financing boost from Afreximbank and Nigerian development finance. This initiative aims to unlock the potential of Nigeria’s creative economy.

The Creative Africa Nexus Weekend 2026 is scheduled to take place in Lagos. Afreximbank is committed to supporting the growth of cultural enterprises across the continent.

Why it mattersNigerian creative businesses will benefit from increased financing opportunities.

Politics · 2026-11-06

INEC removes 1.27 million questionable voter records

Nigeria's electoral commission aims to prevent a repeat of 2023 presidential poll failures ahead of 2027.

Nigeria’s electoral commission has removed over 1.27 million questionable voter records from its register. The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) stated this action is part of efforts to address challenges that affected the 2023 presidential election. These measures are intended to improve processes for the 2027 general elections.

INEC Chairman Prof. Joash Ojo Amupitan met with traditional rulers in Kogi State on Thursday to seek their support for peaceful and credible elections. He visited Lokoja to assess the commission’s personnel and infrastructure preparedness for the 2027 general elections and an upcoming off-cycle governorship election. Amupitan also commissioned a storage facility for Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) devices at the INEC state headquarters.

The commission is committed to strengthening its operational capacity through infrastructure upgrades and technological tools for seamless electoral processes. Traditional rulers in Kogi State pledged to communicate the INEC chairman’s message to their subjects, assuring the commission of their dedication to peaceful, transparent, and credible elections in the state.

Why it mattersNigerian voters will experience improved electoral safeguards in the upcoming 2027 general elections.

Nigeria targets global creative economy

Vice President Kashim Shettima urged investors to fund African creative businesses for future growth.

Nigeria aims to boost its standing in the global creative economy by increasing trade, investment, and research. The nation is focused on developing this sector for future economic expansion.

Vice President Kashim Shettima called on investors and financial institutions to provide early funding for African creative businesses. He described the sector as a significant source of future global companies and employment.

The creative sector is identified as a major driver for economic growth across the continent. Nigeria's strategy involves strengthening its participation through enhanced trade and investment initiatives.

Why it mattersNigerian creative businesses and investors stand to benefit from increased funding and trade opportunities.
