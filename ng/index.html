President Tinubu orders comprehensive reform of Nigerian football after NFF resignations

President Bola Tinubu has directed a complete overhaul of Nigerian football, hours after the Nigeria Football Federation's leadership resigned due to team failures.

President Bola Tinubu has ordered a comprehensive reform of Nigerian football, citing the national teams' repeated failures to qualify for major international competitions. This directive came on Thursday, following the resignations of Nigeria Football Federation (NFF) President Ibrahim Musa Gusau, General Secretary Mohammed Sanusi, and other Executive Committee members.

The NFF confirmed the mass resignations, stating that Gusau's decision aimed to facilitate an orderly reform process to address long-standing institutional, governance, and developmental issues. Bayo Onanuga, Special Adviser to the President on Information and Strategy, announced the presidential order, noting Tinubu's view that structural weaknesses within the system have caused the decline.

President Tinubu emphasized that the reform must strengthen governance, administration, stakeholder representation, development pathways, domestic competitions, and accountability across Nigerian football. He highlighted that despite significant public support and investment, football in Nigeria has struggled due to these systemic deficits, contrasting it with progress seen in other sports after the restructuring of the sports sector.

Why it mattersThe reform directly affects Nigerian football players, coaches, and staff by restructuring their professional environment and potentially altering career paths and opportunities within the sport.

First Lady's Initiative Supports 1,638 Families of Fallen Police Officers

The Renewed Hope Initiative (RHI) provided financial aid to 1,638 families of police officers who died in the line of duty since President Bola Tinubu’s administration began.

The Renewed Hope Initiative (RHI) Social Investment Programme, led by First Lady Senator Oluremi Tinubu, delivered financial support to 1,638 families of police officers who lost their lives while serving. This intervention, the second edition of the initiative, specifically targets families of security personnel who died in the line of duty since the current administration commenced.

The financial empowerment packages, presented at the Presidential Villa in Abuja, aim to help these families rebuild their livelihoods and care for their dependents. Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, attended the ceremony and expressed appreciation for the gesture, describing it as a demonstration of national gratitude.

The event also saw the attendance of the wife of the Vice President, Hajia Nana Shettima, the wife of the Senate President, Mrs Ekaette Unoma Akpabio, service chiefs, and the President of the Police Officers’ Wives Association (POWA). The IGP assured the bereaved families that the sacrifices of their loved ones would not be forgotten and affirmed the police force's commitment to personnel welfare.

Why it mattersFamilies of deceased police officers receive financial support to help them rebuild their lives and care for their dependents.

Anchoria Capital Group secures SEC approval for holding company structure

Anchoria Capital Group has received approval from the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) to operate as a non-operating Capital Market Holding Company.

Anchoria Capital Group (ACG) has secured approval from the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) to function as a non-operating Capital Market Holding Company. This regulatory nod brings Anchoria Asset Management Limited (AAM), Anchoria Securities Limited (ASL), and Anchoria Advisory Services Limited (AASL) under a single regulated Group structure.

The new structure means that the three entities will now operate under the oversight of Anchoria Capital Group. This change is intended to streamline operations and regulatory compliance for the financial services group.

The approval signifies a formal consolidation of the group's various financial services arms, impacting how these companies are managed and regulated within the capital market.

Why it mattersThis development affects investors by consolidating financial services under a single regulated entity, potentially simplifying their engagement with the group's offerings.

Nigeria's financial future is heavily influenced by its young population, with nearly three-quarters of citizens under the age of 24, according to the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). This demographic reality prompts questions about the preparedness of young Nigerians to engage in investment activities and build long-term wealth.

The SEC's statistics underscore the importance of equipping this large youth demographic with the necessary knowledge, access, and discipline for investing. The financial sector faces the challenge of ensuring that this significant portion of the population can actively participate in and benefit from the country's economic growth.

A decade of fund returns is being examined to understand how it can inform young Nigerians about starting early in investment. This analysis aims to provide practical insights into wealth creation for a population segment that will largely shape the nation's economic landscape in the coming years.
