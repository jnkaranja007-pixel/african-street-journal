Nigeria records no new Ebola cases after 62 contacts clear monitoring

The World Health Organization hailed the result after a woman arriving from Uganda tested positive for the virus last month.

Nigerian authorities say no further cases of Ebola have been detected since a woman arriving from Uganda tested positive for the virus last month. The World Health Organization (WHO) praised Nigeria after 62 contacts of the patient completed 21 days of monitoring without signs of infection.

Health authorities said the monitoring of all identified contacts has now ended, with none of them showing symptoms. The woman's case was the only one confirmed in the country during the period reported.

The outlet did not say whether monitoring will continue for anyone else, or whether any further travel measures are planned for arrivals from Uganda.

Why it matters62 people who had contact with the patient are now free of the monitoring requirement and can return to normal daily life.

Dele Oye says Nigeria's budget gap threatens public finances

The chairman of Alliance for Economic Research & Ethics says improving economic indicators are not matched by budget delivery.

Nigeria is approaching the end of 2026 with growing questions over the gap between better macroeconomic indicators and actual budget delivery. Dele Kelvin Oye, chairman of Alliance for Economic Research & Ethics Ltd/GTE, is examining the Federal Government's budget performance in an interview with Nairametrics.

Oye's review covers revenue generation, deficit financing, capital expenditure and mounting debt obligations. He has set out five reforms he says Nigeria needs before 2027 to close that gap.

The interview examines how these pressures bear on the 2027 budget cycle, which the reforms are meant to shape.

Why it mattersThe five reforms Oye proposes would shape how the 2027 budget is spent and how much debt the government must service.

Nigeria plans disease-free livestock zones and six new abattoirs

Government partners aim to lift meat processing capacity and vaccine output, with the national institute targeting six times its current production.

Dr. Olumide Ojo, Senior Special Assistant to the Minister of Livestock Development, said the federal government is building modern abattoirs, expanding local vaccine production and developing disease-free zones to attract private investment in livestock. He spoke in an interview with AgroNigeria about the reforms.

Ojo said a single modern abattoir could need about 1,000 cattle a day to run, and six such facilities across the country could generate demand for roughly 6,000 cattle daily. The disease-free zones are intended to help livestock meet the health and certification rules needed for export, including to Gulf countries, which he said have been hard to supply because of animal disease concerns.

Nigeria's National Veterinary Research Institute currently meets only 10 to 20 percent of the country's livestock vaccine needs, with imports covering as much as 80 percent. With support from the United Kingdom government and the World Bank, the institute is being expanded to about six times its current output, which Ojo said could also supply West African countries.

Why it mattersPastoralists, traders and transporters are expected to gain demand for their cattle and services once the planned abattoirs are running.

Health · 2026-11-14

Nigeria to lead global health system reform

The World Health Organisation says Nigeria can accelerate its own progress and demonstrate country-led transformation.

Nigeria is set to play a leading role in transforming the global health system, following a Member State-led process initiated by the 79th World Health Assembly in May 2026. This initiative, hosted by the World Health Organisation (WHO), aims to create a more coherent, country-led system with clear mandates and financing aligned with national priorities, involving UN agencies, Gavi, the Global Fund, the Pandemic Fund, and the World Bank.

Dr. Pavel Ursu, WHO Representative to Nigeria, stated that the global health architecture reform itself will not immediately change health outcomes for Nigerians, but its success depends on strengthening primary healthcare, supply chains, surveillance, and equipping health workers. He emphasized that countries can accelerate progress by investing domestic resources through national plans and strengthening accountability, citing Nigeria's Health Sector Renewal Investment Initiative and Sector-Wide Approach as existing foundations.

Reform options will be presented to governing boards between late 2026 and February 2027, with recommendations to the World Health Assembly in May 2027, aligning with the UN80 reform and Africa’s Accra Reset promoting health sovereignty. Ursu noted that Nigeria has an opportunity to demonstrate what country-led transformation can achieve through initiatives like the Joint Annual Review and quarterly performance reviews to identify and correct bottlenecks.

Why it mattersNigeria's health sector reforms aim to strengthen services for citizens, with reform options to be presented by February 2027.
