Naira slides against dollar as Thursday trading opens

Official and street rates diverge by roughly ₦68 to ₦80 per dollar as the currency faces pressure from importers and portfolio outflows.

On Thursday, November 12, 2026, the naira opened trading under sustained demand pressure in both the official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market (NFEM) and the parallel market. Entering the session, the official window stood around the ₦1,350.36 to ₦1,353.50 per dollar band, while international currency feeds showed spot quotes near ₦1,345.72. Dealers in Lagos and Abuja quoted the dollar at ₦1,411 to ₦1,420 for buying and ₦1,419 to ₦1,430 for selling.

Market analysts link the softening to seasonal foreign exchange demand from importers and travellers ahead of the year-end, along with foreign portfolio investment outflows and a firmer US dollar globally. The parallel market touched a 10-month low of ₦1,430 per dollar this week, yet gross external reserves stand at about $54.9 billion, above the CBN's full-year target of $51.04 billion.

Traders and businesses are watching daily NFEM liquidity injections and CBN updates as trading continues. Earlier in the week, the official window moved between ₦1,349.25 and ₦1,354 per dollar, with interbank turnover of $248.07 million across 144 deals.

Why it mattersImporters and businesses paying in dollars face a widening gap between official and street rates, as the packet reports.

Police target AI fake news farms before 2027 polls

Nigeria's police are deploying cyber-intelligence to find phone farms used to spread AI-generated disinformation.

Nigeria's police are launching operations to find phone farms, where hundreds of mobile phones are used to create artificial online engagement and manipulate public opinion ahead of the 2027 elections. Inspector-General of Police, Olatunji Disu, stated that the police have deployed cyber-intelligence and digital forensic capabilities to tackle AI-generated misinformation, deepfakes, and voice cloning.

Disu explained that the police have acquired cyber solutions capable of detecting AI manipulation with up to 99 per cent accuracy, and these tools can verify information and identify deepfakes. He noted that coordinated attempts to interfere with the electoral process through such facilities have been uncovered, leading to targeted raids on phone farms.

The police have arrested an individual accused of spreading false information about a candidate from the South-South region to negatively influence voters. Disu said the suspect was identified through a complaint, investigation, and digital attribution exercise, highlighting the police's efforts to monitor credible threats and identify coordinated networks involved in malicious information manipulation.

Nigeria seeks to cut wheat imports with new project

Project WHEAT NESS aims to boost local production, processing, and distribution of wheat.

Nigeria's Minister of State for Industry, John Owan Enoh, expressed concern over the nation's continued reliance on imported wheat, despite its capacity for local cultivation and significant investment in milling infrastructure. He stated that improved local production, stronger agricultural partnerships, and targeted support for farmers and businesses could reduce this dependence.

Enoh launched Project WHEAT NESS to address challenges in wheat production, processing, distribution, and affordability. He noted that milling facilities operate at half capacity because locally grown wheat is insufficient, lacks quality, or is too expensive for processors. The project requires a coordinated approach involving farmers, millers, manufacturers, bakers, financial institutions, and government agencies.

Project WHEAT NESS will focus on raw material supply, industrial processing, downstream production, consumer markets, and the financial and policy framework. It is a comprehensive strategy to improve value chain efficiency, not a subsidy. The government will use an evidence-based approach, with a dedicated unit coordinating activities and ensuring accountability through measurable performance indicators.

Why it mattersThe government is launching a new initiative to improve local wheat production and processing, aiming to reduce reliance on imports and make food products more affordable.

Nigeria's cattle ranches could lower production costs

Commercial cattle farming may become accessible to small investors with as few as two animals, according to AWAA.

Nigeria's livestock sector is shifting towards fenced ranches and feedlots, a move expected to reduce barriers to commercial cattle production. Jerry Cunningham, Managing Director of American West African Agro (AWAA), stated that this transition could allow investors to start ranching businesses with as few as two animals. The change, anticipated to take several years, aims to create opportunities for small-scale producers, boost cattle productivity, and enhance security.

Cunningham explained that Nigerian cattle possess good genetic potential but require better feeding for optimal productivity, making controlled production systems a crucial first step. Under improved conditions, cattle could reach 400-500 kilogrammes within 24 months, compared to the current practice of selling them at seven months weighing around 250 kilogrammes. This faster cycle offers better returns for farmers, with small-scale ranchers in the South-West already operating with 10 to 50 cattle in confined systems and generating income.

The reforms aim to address grassland deterioration caused by overgrazing, drawing a parallel to historical land disputes in the United States. Cunningham believes the transition will change, not eliminate, jobs in the sector, creating more roles in organised livestock operations. AWAA's experience in Kogi State, where they sourced local cattle and feed, demonstrated the viability of collaborative approaches within two to three months.

Why it mattersThe reforms aim to make commercial cattle production more accessible and profitable for Nigerian farmers and investors.

Gowon memoirs praise Awolowo's economic leadership

General Yakubu Gowon's book highlights Chief Obafemi Awolowo's financial management during the civil war.

General Yakubu Gowon's memoirs, My Life of Duty & Allegiance, detail the governance approach during his regime, singling out Chief Obafemi Awolowo for praise.

Gowon, who appointed Awolowo as Vice Chairman of the Federal Executive Council and Commissioner of Finance, wrote glowingly of his "business acumen, political sagacity, economic wizardry and thorough man-management ability." The memoirs state that Awolowo's contributions were invaluable in keeping national finances intact during and after the 30-month civil war.

The book also credits colleagues in the Supreme Military Council and civil service mandarins for the administration's successes, particularly its ability to prosecute the war without endangering the national economy.

Why it mattersThe memoirs offer insights into Nigeria's past governance and economic management, particularly during a critical period.
