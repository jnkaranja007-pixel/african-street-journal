Peter Obi visit prompts Bayelsa political shifts

Ijaw leaders demand development and unity following the Nigeria Democratic Congress presidential candidate's visit.

Presidential candidate Peter Obi's recent visit to Bayelsa State has triggered a political realignment, with Ijaw leaders calling for development and unity. The visit, which coincided with Senator Henry Seriake Dickson's homecoming as National Leader of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), included the commissioning of the NDC state secretariat in Yenagoa and political engagements.

Professor Benjamin Okaba, former President of the Ijaw National Congress, stated that Obi's visit introduced a significant new dimension to Bayelsa politics and the wider Ijaw Nation. He noted that Senator Dickson's experience as a former governor and senator provides the NDC with an important political foothold in the state.

Okaba emphasized that cultural celebrations, like the traditional Ijaw war canoe and boat regatta at Toru-Orua, must be accompanied by a sustained commitment to the welfare and development of riverine communities. He also highlighted Obi's comments on the marginalisation of oil-producing communities.

Why it mattersThe visit by presidential candidate Peter Obi and the subsequent calls for development and unity by Ijaw leaders directly affect the political landscape and the welfare of communities in Bayelsa State.

Nigeria adopts SOP to boost food systems

The unified procedure aims to improve coordination and policy implementation across the agricultural value chain by 2030.

Nigeria's Food Systems Technical Committee has adopted a unified Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) to strengthen coordination and ensure effective policy implementation across the country’s agricultural value chain. This initiative aims to guarantee nutrition security by 2030, addressing challenges like low productivity, high food prices, and post-harvest losses.

The SOP will provide clear guidance for actors from production to processing, helping stakeholders understand their roles for a coordinated approach to food systems transformation. Dr. Clementina Ebere-Okoro, National Convenor of the UN Food Systems Implementation Team, announced the development at the Agri-Food System Transformation and Innovation (AFTI) Programme meeting.

Former National Convenor Dr. Sanjo Faniran described the SOP adoption as a paradigm shift, noting increased state prioritization of agro-processing to reduce losses and maximize produce value. The initiative also seeks to boost local food production, make nutritious food affordable, create jobs, and improve farmers’ incomes.

Why it mattersFarmers' incomes and access to affordable, nutritious food will be improved by the new SOP.

Naira trades at ₦1,347.30 in official market

The naira weakened for a second consecutive trading session, closing at N1,348 per dollar on Wednesday.

The naira traded at different rates against the US dollar on Thursday, November 5, 2026. In the official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market (NFEM), the dollar was valued at approximately ₦1,347.30. At the parallel market, Bureau De Change operators bought the dollar at approximately ₦1,380 and sold it at ₦1,390.

The figures show Nigerians buying dollars through the parallel market paid more than those accessing foreign exchange officially. The difference between the official rate and the parallel market selling rate was approximately ₦42.70 per dollar. Exchange rates fluctuate based on dollar supply, demand, market liquidity, and trading activities.

The naira weakened for a second consecutive trading session in November, closing at N1,348 per dollar on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, compared with N1,347.75/$ recorded on Tuesday. Nigerians seeking foreign exchange for international travel, school fees, business transactions and other payments are advised to confirm prevailing rates with their banks and licensed Bureau De Change operators.

Why it mattersNigerians seeking foreign exchange for international travel, school fees, business transactions and other payments face varying rates.

Kano state to irrigate 2,000 acres for farming

11 new mini dams will expand year-round cultivation, boosting farmer productivity and rural incomes.

The Kano State Government plans to develop 11 mini dams to bring an additional 2,000 acres of farmland under irrigation for year-round production. Commissioner for Agriculture and Natural Resources, Dr. Danjuma Mahmoud, stated this aims to increase cultivable land and improve farmer productivity. The government has already restored over 1,200 acres by rehabilitating irrigation facilities at Jermambaka, Kadawa, and Bunkure A and B.

A further 200 acres were recovered through a partnership with the Federal Government and the ATAPS project. The state is also developing an irrigation facility at Donsushiya, comprising a two-kilometre dam with a three billion cubic litre capacity, designed to serve 500 hectares. This project is 40 per cent complete, with its 14-kilometre access road finished.

The proposed mini dams will be located in Bebeji, Kibiya, Sumaila, Doguwa, Shanono, Dawakin Kudu, Gaya, Rogo, Dala, Dawakin Tofa, and Ungogo local government areas. Compensation has been paid in at least six locations, and contractors are mobilised to some sites, including Bebeji. The expanded irrigation network is expected to generate employment and discourage rural migration.

Why it mattersFarmers in Kano will benefit from increased cultivable land and improved productivity through new irrigation projects.
