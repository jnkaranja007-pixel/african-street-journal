Nigeria Returns to Frontier Market Status on September 21

FTSE Russell has confirmed Nigeria's reclassification from Unclassified to Frontier Market status, effective September 21, 2026, marking a significant milestone for the nation's.

Nigeria's capital market will officially rejoin the global Frontier Market universe on September 21, 2026, following confirmation from global index provider FTSE Russell. The reclassification, announced in a market notice on Thursday, August 27, 2026, signifies Nigeria's return to this investment category after a period of being unclassified.

The Federal Government confirmed the development through a statement issued by the Minister of Finance and Coordinating Minister of the Economy, Taiwo Oyedele, on Friday. This decision by FTSE Russell represents an important milestone for the country's capital market, signaling renewed confidence and potential for increased foreign investment.

This move by FTSE Russell is expected to enhance Nigeria's visibility on the global financial stage and potentially attract more capital inflows. The reclassification from Unclassified to Frontier Market status is a key step in integrating Nigeria's financial markets more deeply into the international system.

Why it mattersInvestors will be affected by Nigeria's reclassification to a Frontier Market, potentially leading to increased foreign investment and capital inflows.

Former Minister Praises Kalu's Abia Leadership, Cites Decline

Osita Chidoka stated that Orji Uzor Kalu's tenure as Abia governor put the state in the national conversation, but infrastructure and standing diminished afterward.

Former Minister of Aviation, Osita Chidoka, described the administration of ex-Abia State Governor Orji Uzor Kalu as a defining period that placed the state firmly in national discussions. Chidoka noted that Kalu's assertive leadership during the early Fourth Republic gave Abia a strong voice and visibility, helping the South East region find relevance in the new democratic order.

However, Chidoka observed that the momentum recorded during Kalu's eight-year tenure faded after he left office. He stated that infrastructure deteriorated and Abia's national standing diminished in the subsequent years, with Aba appearing to succeed independently of government efforts.

Chidoka, speaking at Abia State's 35th anniversary, contrasted the Kalu era with the decline that followed, suggesting current Governor Alex Otti has an opportunity to restore confidence, rebuild infrastructure, and revive the state's sense of possibility.

Why it mattersInvestors may be interested in the assessment of past infrastructure deterioration and the current opportunity to rebuild, which could impact business environments.

Two rescued from Kubwa building collapse

Emergency services pulled two residents alive from rubble after a one-storey residential building partially collapsed in Kubwa, Abuja, on Friday morning.

Two residents were rescued alive from a collapsed one-storey building in Kubwa, Abuja, and are receiving treatment at Kubwa General Hospital. The Federal Capital Territory Emergency Management Department (FEMD) confirmed the rescue, stating the victims were on the ground floor when the right side of the residential property, comprising six blocks of flats, gave way around 11:30am on Friday. Other occupants were not in the building at the time, limiting the number of people trapped.

The FEMD received a distress call at 11:38am and mobilized rescue teams to the scene. The two individuals were extracted from the rubble without fatalities, though significant personal belongings and household items were lost. The Department of Development Control and the FCT Police Command also responded to secure the area and begin an investigation into the cause of the structural failure.

The incident highlights potential structural integrity issues in residential areas. While no lives were lost in this specific collapse, the loss of property and the need for investigation underscore the importance of building safety regulations and enforcement in urban centers like Abuja.

Why it mattersThe collapse of a residential building in Kubwa, Abuja, directly affects residents by causing loss of property and potential displacement, and necessitates investigations into structural safety.

Atiku Abubakar Questions President Tinubu's Age, Reno Omokri Responds

Presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar claimed President Bola Tinubu is 90 years old, prompting a response from Reno Omokri who shared a 1996 photo of Tinubu and Dele Momodu.

Presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has dismissed concerns about his own age, stating he is fit for leadership and questioning President Bola Tinubu's age, suggesting he is 90 years old. Abubakar made these remarks in an interview with BBC Hausa when asked about his age and its potential impact on his ability to govern if elected in 2027. He asserted that he does not appear to be nearing 80, contrasting his perceived fitness with the age he attributed to the current president.

In response to Abubakar's comments, Reno Omokri, an Ambassador-designate, shared a photograph of President Tinubu and Dele Momodu taken in London in 1996. Omokri used the image to challenge the claim that Tinubu is 90, arguing that if that were true, Dele Momodu, who appears younger in the photo, would logically be in his eighties. Omokri urged objective observers to examine the photograph and assess the ages of the individuals depicted.

Omokri further questioned the appearance of President Tinubu in the 1996 photo, suggesting he looked significantly younger than 60 at the time. He also addressed Abubakar's assertion that he initiated the opposition coalition, noting that Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso have since left the group. Abubakar expressed optimism that the remaining members would continue to work together for the country's benefit.

Why it mattersThe age claims made by Atiku Abubakar and the subsequent challenge by Reno Omokri, using a 1996 photo, directly involve political figures and could influence public perception and electoral dynamics.

Nigerian Student Wins Global Chinese Proficiency Prize

Faith Emmanuel secured the Global Third Prize at the 25th “Chinese Bridge” competition, excelling among 157 contestants from 138 countries with less than two years of study.

Faith Emmanuel, a student at Nnamdi Azikiwe University (UNIZIK), has won the Global Third Prize at the 25th “Chinese Bridge” Chinese Proficiency Competition. Competing against 157 students from 138 nations in Xiamen, China, Emmanuel represented the Confucius Institute at UNIZIK. The Chinese Embassy in Nigeria acknowledged her achievement, calling her an “outstanding Nigerian youth” and highlighting the growing cultural exchange between the two countries.

Emmanuel's success is particularly noteworthy as she began learning Chinese in March 2025, a significantly shorter period than many competitors who had studied for five to fifteen years or lived in China. Despite this, she progressed through multiple rounds to reach the Global Top 30 before securing the third prize. This marks the twelfth time a UNIZIK Confucius Institute participant has reached the Global Top 30 since 2018.

University officials attributed Emmanuel's performance to her passion, hard work, and confident presentation. Qin Fangfang of the Confucius Institute stated that Emmanuel's journey reflects the determination of Nigerian youth to pursue their dreams. The “Chinese Bridge” competition aims to foster Chinese language proficiency and cultural understanding among international students, with UNIZIK's institute having a strong record of success in previous years.

Why it mattersThis event highlights the success of Nigerian youth in international cultural and educational competitions, potentially encouraging further cross-cultural engagement and educational pursuits.
