Fulani groups demand 30% agric budget for livestock

Pastoralist associations want the livestock ministry funded and 417 grazing reserves gazetted to end farmer-herder conflict.

Fulani pastoralist groups on Sunday in Abuja rejected being labelled bandits and demanded President Bola Tinubu initiate a national dialogue on farmer-herder conflicts. The Confederation of Pastoralist Non-Governmental Organisations CONPA, representing millions of pastoralists across Nigeria, stated that hundreds of their members have been killed in recent months in coordinated attacks. They called for an end to ethnic profiling and for criminals to be arrested and prosecuted as individuals.

The groups reported that women, children, and the elderly were among hundreds killed in Plateau, Niger, Sokoto, Kebbi, and Zamfara states, with thousands of cattle lost and settlements burned. They asked the Federal Government to direct NEMA and NCFRMI to provide relief to displaced pastoralists and ensure their protection as Nigerian citizens. The coalition commended the creation of the Federal Ministry of Livestock Development but noted it remains unfunded.

CONPA appealed for the livestock ministry to receive at least 30 per cent of the agricultural budget and for the immediate gazetting and protection of 417 grazing reserves. They also requested pilot ranches and fodder production across the six geo-political zones, citing the livestock sector as a 30 trillion naira economy that should not be treated lightly.

Why it mattersFulani pastoralist groups are affected by killings and displacement due to farmer-herder conflicts and seek government intervention.

Nigeria identifies 53 Ebola contacts, offers weekly support

The federal government will provide N100,000 weekly to individuals in isolation as it tracks 53 contacts of the confirmed Ebola case.

Nigeria's federal government has identified 53 contacts linked to the country's confirmed Ebola case in the Federal Capital Territory (FCT). The government announced it would provide N100,000 weekly to individuals placed in isolation or quarantine to support their upkeep. This measure aims to prevent further transmission of the virus, with health authorities intensifying efforts to contain the outbreak.

Dr. Jide Idris, Director-General of the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC), stated that the contacts include healthcare workers and the patient’s spouse. He confirmed that contact tracing is ongoing and the number of identified individuals may change. Idris clarified that being identified as a contact does not mean an individual has contracted the virus, but requires monitoring for early detection of any possible infection.

The NCDC has activated its national incident management system at the highest level to coordinate the response. So far, Nigeria has recorded one confirmed Ebola case in the FCT, with a suspected case in Rivers State testing negative. Investigations into another suspected case in the FCT are ongoing, but no contacts have tested positive for the virus.

Why it matters53 individuals in Nigeria are being monitored for Ebola and will receive financial support while in isolation.

Nigeria plans 30% value-addition for industrial growth

The RMRDC Director-General stated the plan aims to boost local manufacturing and resource utilization.

Nigeria's government is implementing a 30% value-addition plan to foster industrial wealth across the nation. This initiative was announced by RMRDC Director-General, Prof. Nnanyelugo Ike-Muonso, at the Second Africa Raw Materials Summit held in Abuja.

The plan focuses on increasing the local processing of raw materials to create more finished goods within Nigeria. Prof. Ike-Muonso highlighted the importance of this strategy for developing the country's manufacturing sector and reducing reliance on imported products.

The summit provided a platform for discussions on leveraging Africa's raw materials for economic development. The value-addition target is expected to stimulate investment in local industries and create new employment opportunities.

Why it mattersThe value-addition plan aims to create industrial wealth and boost local manufacturing in Nigeria.

Nigerian govt disburses N38.93 billion to 166 institutions

The Federal Government released funds to improve infrastructure and teaching facilities at universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education.

The Nigerian government has disbursed N38.93 billion to 166 public tertiary institutions across the country. The funds are intended to address infrastructure gaps and enhance teaching and learning facilities.

Mrs Efe Ovuakporie, Head of Media at the Federal Ministry of Finance, stated on Sunday in Abuja that the disbursement falls under the Needs Assessment Revitalisation Fund. This initiative covers 79 universities, 46 polytechnics, and 41 colleges of education.

The funds aim to improve conditions within these educational establishments. The specific breakdown includes support for universities, polytechnics, and colleges of education.

Why it matters166 public tertiary institutions in Nigeria will receive funds to improve infrastructure and teaching facilities.
