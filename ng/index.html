Nigeria's central bank to decide on interest rates on November 23

The Monetary Policy Committee meets on November 23 and 24 to set the Monetary Policy Rate, now 23% after a cut from 26.5% in September.

The Central Bank of Nigeria's Monetary Policy Committee meets on November 23 and 24 to decide whether to raise, hold or cut interest rates. The Monetary Policy Rate, the benchmark the bank sets, currently stands at 23%, after the bank reset it from 26.5% in September.

The decision turns on more than that one figure, according to the Nairametrics explainer that sets out the process. The committee weighs a range of economic indicators before choosing a direction for the rate.

The meeting falls in the final days of November, and its outcome will determine the rate that applies from the announcement onward.

Why it mattersBanks and businesses that borrow at rates tied to the Monetary Policy Rate will face whatever the committee sets when it announces its decision after the November 24 meeting.

PCN denies shielding Kano drug dealers, says it sealed 1,631 outlets

The Pharmacy Council of Nigeria says it sealed 1,631 pharmaceutical premises in Kano State this year and has inspected 2,304 drug outlets.

The Pharmacy Council of Nigeria (PCN), the federal body that regulates pharmacies, has denied claims that it is shielding illicit drug dealers and facilitating the distribution of controlled substances in Kano State. It called the allegations made by Muhuyi Magaji Rimin-Gado, chairman of the Kano State Multi-Agency Taskforce on Drug Abuse and Illicit Trafficking, baseless and challenged him to produce evidence of staff involvement.

In a rebuttal signed by Pharm. Hasiya Ladan, a consultant pharmacist at the PCN's Kano office, the council said it had sealed 1,631 pharmaceutical premises in 2026 after inspecting 2,304 outlets. It said 15 investigations into suspected violations are ongoing and that its national enforcement team visited Kano in April and September. Rimin-Gado had accused the PCN and the National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) of protecting dealers and questioned import licences for pregabalin, a drug NAFDAC rescheduled as controlled on September 14.

The council said regulating pharmaceutical premises is a federal responsibility under the 1999 Constitution and the Pharmacy Council of Nigeria (Establishment) Act 2022. It also disputed a claim that it registered an outlet in a residential area in breach of the Land Use Act, saying the task force chairman had not identified the provisions allegedly broken.

Why it mattersResidents and pharmacy operators in Kano face ongoing PCN inspections and 15 open investigations, while the dispute over pregabalin licensing remains unresolved.

IFAD, AFD sign deal for rural transformation in Nigeria

The co-financing agreement backs the Value Chain Programme in Northern Nigeria, which is designed to cut poverty and improve nutrition in rural areas.

The International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) and the Agence Française de Développement (AFD) have signed a co-financing agreement in Abuja for the Value Chain Programme in Northern Nigeria (VCN). The signing, between IFAD Country Director Mohamed El-Ghazaly and AFD Country Director Jacky Amprou, marks a milestone in the programme's start-up.

VCN is a flagship programme of the Federal Government of Nigeria. Its co-financiers are IFAD, AFD and nine participating states: Bauchi, Borno, Jigawa, Kano, Katsina, Kebbi, Sokoto, Yobe and Zamfara. Both institutions have urged the federal and state governments to speed up remaining start-up work ahead of a programme start-up workshop planned for early December 2026.

Under the framework, IFAD will act as lead co-financier and provide technical, fiduciary, environmental and social support for AFD's financing. The programme is still in its inception phase, and the agreement sets out how the two institutions will jointly finance its components.

Why it mattersRural households in the nine northern states will see the programme's poverty, nutrition and income work begin only once start-up activities are finished before the December workshop.

Agriculture · 2026-11-15

Nigeria secures $3m deal for 1,000 tomato greenhouses

The agreement, signed at COP31 in Türkiye, is meant to bring private money into climate-smart tomato farming in Kano State.

Kano State is set to host 1,000 climate-smart greenhouses for tomato production, under a $3 million investment deal Nigeria has signed at the COP31 climate summit in Türkiye. The pilot is designed to draw private capital into sustainable food production.

Agriculture Minister Abubakar Kyari presided over the signing of a memorandum of understanding under the Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zones (SAPZ) programme. The Bank of Agriculture will put $1.5 million into a financing mechanism for participating farmers, while Turkish firm Direkci Group will supply another $1.5 million along with greenhouse technology, engineering and technical support. Kyari said women and smallholder farmers will be prioritised for productive assets, financing and market links.

Kyari said the partners will hold a joint performance review after nine months, when a commercial expansion worth at least $30 million could follow, subject to agreed acquisitions and performance targets. He warned that success depends on transparent farmer selection, timely infrastructure delivery and measurable results. Direkci Group said it is ready to extend its technology to other parts of Nigeria.

Why it mattersFarmers chosen for the scheme will gain access to $1.5 million in bank financing for greenhouses in Kano State.
