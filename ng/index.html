Borno community urges Tinubu to rescue 78 abducted children

The Margi community in Borno State pleaded with President Bola Tinubu and Governor Babagana Zulum to secure the release of 78 schoolchildren and a teacher.

The Margi community in Borno State has appealed to President Bola Tinubu and Governor Babagana Zulum to rescue 78 schoolchildren and one teacher abducted from Askira/Uba Local Government Area. The victims were taken in two separate incidents: 43 pupils from Mussa on May 15, 2026, and 35 students and a teacher from Lassa on June 26, 2026.

Malla Gadzama, the community’s Secretary, stated in Abuja that the families are in anguish and urged authorities to provide regular updates on the rescue efforts. He called on President Tinubu to mobilize security and intelligence resources and on Governor Zulum to prioritize the rescue and establish a reliable communication channel with affected families.

The community also appealed to Senator Ali Ndume and other elected representatives from Askira/Uba to maintain pressure on the authorities. They requested better school security and support for the victims upon their return, citing the government's constitutional responsibility for the security and welfare of its people.

Why it mattersThe abduction of 78 schoolchildren and a teacher affects the families of the victims in Borno State, who are appealing to the Nigerian government for their safe return.

Police arrest two masquerades for extortion in Akwa Ibom

Suspects allegedly terrorized residents and assaulted women while demanding money.

Police in Akwa Ibom State arrested two Ekpo masquerade participants on Monday, October 26, for allegedly extorting money and assaulting the public in Ikot Ekpene. The arrests occurred along the Ukana/Abak highway around 6pm.

State police spokesperson Timfom John identified the suspects as Ine Isong, 29, and Emmanuel Ekpenyong, 20, who were found with masquerade costumes, machetes, spears, and arrows. Preliminary reports suggest the suspects terrorized residents and assaulted people, particularly women, while extorting money.

Investigations are ongoing to identify other involved individuals, according to John. The Commissioner of Police, Baba Azare, warned that cultural practices would not be used to cover criminality and that the command would ensure residents' peace and security.

Why it mattersTwo individuals were arrested for extorting money and assaulting the public while disguised as Ekpo masquerades.

Nigerian households weigh timing over rates for savings

Money not spent today has a purpose, influencing investment choices for Nigerian households and businesses.

Nigerian households and businesses face a key decision when saving temporarily unused funds: timing is more important than the interest rate offered. Money set aside is typically earmarked for future expenses such as rent, school fees, taxes, or emergencies.

This approach suggests that the immediate need for funds dictates savings strategy, rather than solely pursuing the highest yield. The purpose of the money influences how long it can be invested and the type of account chosen.

Understanding these short-term needs is crucial for effective financial planning. The decision-making process prioritizes accessibility and planned expenditures over maximizing returns on idle cash.

Why it mattersNigerian households and businesses must consider when they will need their savings before deciding where to invest.

ADC urges ECOWAS to warn Nigeria on election crisis

Senator David Mark asked the regional bloc to caution the Federal Government and INEC against actions that could trigger poll conflict.

The National Chairman of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), Senator David Mark, asked the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) on Thursday to warn Nigeria’s Federal Government and the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) against actions that could cause a crisis over the 2027 general elections.

Mark made the appeal in Abuja while receiving an ECOWAS delegation on a pre-election fact-finding mission, stating that the credibility of Nigeria’s elections is vital for peace in West Africa. The ADC has raised concerns about INEC’s preparedness, alleged government control of institutions, and threats of violence, according to the party’s National Publicity Secretary, Mallam Bolaji Abdullahi.

The Head of the ECOWAS Mission, former Sierra Leone Vice-President Mr Victor Bockarie Foh, said the delegation would report the ADC’s concerns to the regional body. The mission is assessing Nigeria’s election preparedness and the political atmosphere, noting that peace in Nigeria is important to the West African region.

Why it mattersThe ADC warns that the Federal Government and INEC must ensure a free, fair, and transparent 2027 general election to prevent electoral conflict in Nigeria.

Fulani leaders demand probe into pastoralist attacks

Hundreds killed and cattle lost in Plateau, Niger, Sokoto, Kebbi and Zamfara states, leaders say.

Fulani leaders are calling on the Federal Government to investigate attacks on pastoralists in several Nigerian states and provide aid to displaced families. The Kautal Pulaku Fulbe Association of Nigeria, represented by General Secretary Abdullahi Ardo, stated on Sunday in Abuja that hundreds of people have died and cattle have been lost in recent months. The coalition identified Plateau, Niger, Sokoto, Kebbi, and Zamfara states as areas where these deadly attacks have occurred.

Ardo, speaking for a coalition of Fulani leaders, urged the government to strengthen security for herders and ensure victims receive justice. He noted that women, children, and the elderly were among those killed, forcing many families to flee their homes. The group requested an independent probe and called on the National Emergency Management Agency and the National Commission for Refugees, Migrants and Internally Displaced Persons to provide relief materials.

The leaders also asked President Bola Tinubu and the National Assembly to increase funding for the livestock sector, proposing that at least 30 per cent of the agricultural budget go to the Ministry of Livestock Development. They seek formal recognition of Nigeria’s 417 grazing reserves and the establishment of pilot ranches and fodder production facilities. The coalition described Nigeria’s livestock industry as an economic sector worth about N30tn.

Why it mattersFulani pastoralists are directly affected by deadly attacks, displacement, and loss of livestock, and are seeking government protection and aid.
