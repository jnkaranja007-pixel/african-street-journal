Edo Governor vows to sign death warrants for kidnappers

Governor Monday Okpebholo stated he would sign execution orders for convicted kidnappers, even if they were family.

Edo State Governor Monday Okpebholo has declared he will sign death warrants for convicted kidnappers, stating he would even sign his father's warrant if found guilty. The governor made this vow amid efforts to enforce the state's law which mandates the death penalty for kidnapping offences that result in the victim's death.

Okpebholo emphasized his commitment to removing "bad elements" from society, citing the trauma inflicted on victims and the severe injuries sustained by some, including the state's Chief Judge who lost the use of one hand. He confirmed he has instructed the state Attorney-General to expedite relevant documents for the executions.

The Edo State Kidnapping Prohibition (Amendment) Law, 2025, signed by Okpebholo in February 2025, prescribes life imprisonment for kidnappings where victims survive and capital punishment for those resulting in death. In September 2026, four men were sentenced to death for a kidnapping in Benin City.

Why it mattersThe governor's strong stance on capital punishment for kidnappers directly impacts the lives of those convicted and their families.

FG moves to boost local milk production

The government plans to expand Ikun Dairy Farm to cut Nigeria's $1.5 billion annual dairy import bill.

Nigeria's Federal Government plans to expand Ikun Dairy Farm in Ekiti State to boost domestic milk production. The move aims to reduce the country's annual expenditure of $1.5 billion on imported dairy products. Minister of Livestock Development, Idi Mukhtar Maiha, visited the farm and identified it as a key investment for dairy development.

The Ikun Dairy Farm, operated by Promasidor in partnership with the Ekiti State Government, currently produces over 6,000 litres of milk daily. Maiha stated the government is ready to collaborate with Promasidor and the state government to scale up operations. This expansion is expected to support job creation, food security, and growth in the livestock sector.

The farm, which occupies over 530 hectares and houses more than 700 cattle, has already seen investment, including a $5 million injection from Promasidor. Efforts to improve local cattle breeds have resulted in generations better adapted to Nigeria's climate, overcoming earlier challenges with imported stock. The farm also produces its own maize and soybean for cattle feed.

Why it mattersThe Federal Government's plan to expand domestic milk production aims to reduce Nigeria's reliance on imports and bolster the local agricultural sector.

Nigerian-born Emehelu becomes New Jersey's first immigrant lieutenant governor

Shirley Emehelu, who emigrated from Nigeria as a child, was sworn in on Monday and also took on the role of Secretary of State.

Nigerian-born American lawyer Shirley U. Emehelu has become the first immigrant to serve as Lieutenant Governor of New Jersey. Governor Mikie Sherrill appointed her on Monday, making her the fifth person to hold the post. The appointment also means the state's two highest executive offices are held by women at the same time for the first time.

Sherrill said Emehelu's record in fighting financial crime, protecting consumers, defending civil rights and tackling government waste shaped the choice. Emehelu also took on the duties of Secretary of State. Chief Justice Stuart Rabner swore her in at the Statehouse in Trenton, with her husband and children present.

Emehelu said she hopes her appointment will encourage younger people to enter public service. She pledged to support the governor's work on living costs and economic opportunity. She previously served as New Jersey's Acting State Comptroller, overseeing efforts against fraud and waste involving public money.

Why it mattersNigerian-Americans gain one of the highest executive positions in US state government, and New Jersey's administration now includes two women in its top executive offices.

Nigeria ministry flags 89 issues for $500m AGROW project

Federal officials met in Minna to resolve grant, governance, and risk issues before the World Bank-backed project begins.

Nigeria's Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security has identified 89 issues in draft manuals for the $500 million Sustainable Agricultural Value Chains for Growth (AGROW) Project. Dr Emmanuel Akogun, Federal Director of the Department of Development Partners Projects, stated on Monday in Minna that these issues require clarification before the project can start.

The identified issues, grouped into six areas, concern fund flow for matching grants, grant facility governance, agreement provisions, eligibility criteria, results verification, and environmental and social risks. The two-day consultation, hosted by Niger State Government, aimed to finalize the manuals for the World Bank-backed initiative.

The AGROW project, approved in March 2026, will help smallholder farmers improve productivity and access markets through a performance-based matching grant facility. Initial priority commodities include rice, maize, soybeans, and cassava, with support for improved crop varieties and digital advisory services.

Why it mattersSmallholder farmers in Nigeria will benefit from improved productivity and market access through the AGROW project, which focuses on rice, maize, soybeans, and cassava.

Senate donates N100m to U-17 women's football team

The Nigerian Senate recognised the Flamingos' World Cup triumph with a significant financial reward.

Nigeria's U-17 women's national football team, the Flamingos, received a N100m donation from the Senate on Tuesday. This recognition follows their historic victory at the 2026 FIFA U-17 Women’s World Cup held in Rabat, Morocco.

Senate President Godswill Akpabio announced the donation during a plenary session, acknowledging a motion by Senator Abdul Ningi that highlighted the team's achievement. Senator Adams Oshiomhole supported the motion, commending the Flamingos for defeating Japan in the final and urging recognition of national successes.

The Senate adopted prayers to congratulate President Bola Tinubu and the National Sports Commission, urging the Federal Government to adequately reward the players. Akpabio called on the Nigeria Football Federation to sustain and develop the team for future competitions, with the N100m intended to motivate the players.

Why it mattersThe Senate's N100m donation directly benefits the U-17 women's football team following their World Cup win.
