Court Voids EFCC Probe Report on London/Paris Club Refunds

A High Court judge ruled the EFCC overstepped its authority by investigating court-ordered payments to a consultant.

A Federal Capital Territory High Court has nullified an Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) report alleging fraud in the London/Paris Club refunds for Nigeria's 774 local government areas. Justice Suleiman Belgore stated the Federal Government acted improperly by directing the EFCC to probe the matter, especially after two prior court decisions had already addressed the payments. The court found the EFCC's report constituted an affront to judicial authority.

The judge emphasized that the government should have pursued legal avenues to set aside court judgments rather than initiating an investigation. He noted that negotiated terms for legal services rendered by consultant Dr. Ted Iseghohi-Edwards were renegotiated in open court. The court recognized the validity of ten promissory notes issued to the consultant, authorized by former President Muhammadu Buhari, following a Federal High Court order.

The ruling upheld a suit filed by Dr. Iseghohi-Edwards, confirming his retainer by the Incorporated Trustees of the Association of Local Governments of Nigeria (ALGON) for the refund matter. The court also prohibited the Federal Government and its agencies from arresting, detaining, or prosecuting the lawyer concerning these refunds, safeguarding his fundamental rights.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the court ruling clarifies the legality of payments related to the London/Paris Club refunds, impacting financial settlements.
