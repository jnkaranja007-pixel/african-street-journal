Obaigbena says he received no police invitation over First Bank dispute

The chairman of THISDAY (Nigerian newspaper group) and ARISE (Nigerian television network) says settlement talks with First Bank and GHL are continuing after a Supreme Court.

Sources: Vanguard and Nairametrics · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Nduka Obaigbena, chairman of Nigerian media companies THISDAY, a newspaper group, and ARISE, a television network, said he had received no police invitation or other law-enforcement communication over a dispute involving First Bank and his media organisations. In a statement issued by his office in Ikoyi, Lagos, he denied reports that police had declared him wanted and denied threatening First Bank chief executive Olusegun Alebiosu.

Obaigbena's office said it had checked all his addresses and found no invitation related to an investigation. It linked the reports to a financial dispute involving First Bank, GHL, THISDAY Newspapers and ARISE Television, and said talks were under way to settle outstanding issues. The statement said the Supreme Court ruled unanimously, 5-0, for GHL in its case against First Bank on July 3, 2026.

The statement said First Bank and GHL had explored an out-of-court settlement since the ruling, while efforts to resolve debts allegedly owed to the media companies were also continuing. Obaigbena said he had spoken with senior police officials on November 19 about the proposed state police system.

Why it mattersFirst Bank and GHL are pursuing settlement talks after the Supreme Court's July 3 ruling for GHL, while debts allegedly owed to the two media companies remain under discussion.
