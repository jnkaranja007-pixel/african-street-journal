Madagascar's Catholic bishops call for inclusive national consultation

In a statement after their November session, the bishops warned that decisions imposed in advance would not reflect the voice of the people.

Sources: Midi Madagasikara and Madagascar Tribune · 2026-11-16 · 1 min read

The Conference of Catholic Bishops of Madagascar has urged leaders to make the upcoming national consultation genuinely inclusive, reaching beyond Antananarivo and the big cities to grassroots communities. The bishops issued their statement at the end of their ordinary November session, held in Ambatoroka, Antananarivo.

The bishops said the process must reflect the voice of the people, not opinions already formed and imposed in advance. They also criticised leaders for selfish disregard or systematic, indiscriminate opposition, and denounced false information and personal attacks on social media, particularly Facebook. They listed insecurity, drug trafficking, serious illnesses and economic hardship among the problems facing the population, including low prices paid to farmers while daily living costs keep rising.

The bishops told citizens they must not hand their lives entirely over to leaders, and called for just and effective laws to govern the exploitation of land, water and subsoil resources.

Why it mattersRural households and farmers, who receive low prices for their produce while living costs rise, are among the groups the bishops say must be heard in the national consultation.
