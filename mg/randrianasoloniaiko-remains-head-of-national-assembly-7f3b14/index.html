Randrianasoloniaiko remains head of National Assembly

Vice-president denies rumors of a move to oust the permanent bureau, citing normal legislative work.

Source: Madagascar Tribune · 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

Rumors of a leadership change at Madagascar's National Assembly have been denied by Fanomezantsoa Andrianjanahary, the institution's vice-president. Andrianjanahary stated on Thursday, November 12, that no initiative to remove the permanent bureau was underway, aiming to quell speculation circulating on social media about internal crisis. Legislative activities, including the examination of the 2027 finance bill, continue normally, with deputies from the Vondron'ny Fihavaozan'i Madagasikara (VFM) maintaining their support for the current president, Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko.

The denial comes amid reports of a low parliamentary presence during budget sessions, which Andrianjanahary attributed to deputies visiting their constituencies to present their work. Despite these assurances, Randrianasoloniaiko's political positioning has raised questions, particularly his reduced public communication compared to earlier stages of the current administration. This shift fuels speculation about a potential cooling of relations with Colonel Michaël Randrianirina, though no verified information confirms a dispute between them.

Randrianasoloniaiko played a decisive role in the current power structure, having approached the High Constitutional Court prior to the October 14, 2025, decision that tasked Colonel Michaël Randrianirina with presidential duties. The National Assembly itself experienced a significant leadership change on October 15, 2025, when 127 deputies voted to remove the previous permanent bureau. Currently, no similar scenario is anticipated, with the existing bureau maintaining its functions and the 2027 budget review officially prioritized.
