National Consultation process launches in Andohatapenaka

The National Consultation, themed "recovery and renewal," began today in Andohatapenaka, marking a more concrete phase for the process with the presentation of its steps and.

The National Consultation officially commenced this Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, at Village Voara Andohatapenaka. The event started with an ecumenical service at three o’clock in the afternoon, led by the Ecumenical Council of Christian Churches of Madagascar (FFKM). This launch follows weeks of anticipation from political, social, and institutional actors.

The day’s agenda includes the presentation of the process's main stages and the designation of a national coordinator to oversee its implementation. This coordinator will help identify the structure responsible for monitoring and organizing the entire consultation system. The ceremony is expected to conclude at five fifteen in the afternoon, followed by an official photo at five thirty.

The consultation aims to gather expectations, proposals, and concerns from the population, extending to the fokontany level. These contributions will be centralized, examined, and synthesized to form a national vision, intended to guide the country's Refoundation process. This initiative occurs amidst political upheavals and mobilizations from September and October two thousand twenty-five, which altered the institutional landscape.

Why it mattersThe National Consultation directly affects citizens by gathering their input from the fokontany level to shape a national vision and influence future political and institutional processes.

Madagascar Central Bank installs four gold collection points

The Banky Foiben'i Madagasikara (BFM) will establish new gold collection points in four high-potential areas to formalize the gold sector and boost national reserves.

The Banky Foiben'i Madagasikara (BFM) plans to open gold collection points in Ambilobe, Maevatanana, Mananjary, and Miandrivazo. This initiative aims to integrate monetary gold into Madagascar's foreign exchange reserves, a policy started by the Central Bank in 2023. The new structures will bring official channels closer to artisanal gold miners, facilitating access to formal collection points in production zones.

The BFM intends to better regulate gold commercialization and reduce reliance on informal circuits. This system will also enhance quality control and purity of collected metal, adhering to current standards. The project focuses on the entire supply chain, from extraction by artisans to official collection and integration, to improve traceability and transparency of transactions.

Beyond economic goals, the project includes social and financial dimensions. The BFM seeks to support the formalization of artisanal miners, improve their access to banking services, and fund cleaner extraction technologies. Mechanisms for guarantees and financial products tailored to artisanal mining are also under consideration, aligning with commitments made under planetGOLD Madagascar to eliminate mercury use.

Why it mattersThis development affects artisanal gold miners by formalizing their operations, providing access to banking services, and promoting safer extraction methods.

Ministry supports Fami NGO with aid for disabled children

The Ministry of Population and Solidarity provided food, daily necessities, and equipment to the Fami NGO, which offers free treatment to children with lower limb disabilities.

The Ministry of Population and Solidarity has provided support to the Fami NGO, which treats children with lower limb disabilities free of charge at the CRMM Antsirabe. This assistance included food aid, daily necessities, and equipment for people with disabilities.

Minister Rakotoniaina Voahanginirina affirmed the collaboration to implement projects supporting the center and improving care for disabled children. This initiative aligns with the vision set by the President of the Renovation for human-centered and non-discriminatory development.

Since 1996, approximately 400 children have received treatment from Fami, with many fully recovering, including surgical interventions when necessary. The Minister and her team also offered condolences to a family in Antananarivo following a recent tragedy.

Why it mattersChildren with lower limb disabilities and their families are directly affected by the free treatment and support provided by the Fami NGO and the Ministry.

National Assembly debates future amid calls for dissolution

Madagascar's National Assembly begins an extraordinary session today, facing pressure from political groups and youth organizations advocating for its dissolution.

The National Assembly commenced an extraordinary session today, August 25, to address seven proposed laws. The legislative agenda includes new frameworks for electronic communications and infrastructure, a revised cybercrime law, and ratification of a United Nations convention adopted in Hanoi in October 2025. Other proposals cover the general status of military personnel, the safe and peaceful use of nuclear technology, an agreement on productive investments in the SADC area, and gambling regulations.

This session occurs amidst political uncertainty, with calls for the Assembly's dissolution. A youth consultation in late July at CCI Ivato and the Assedu-Mada organization have publicly demanded the Assembly's dissolution. Additionally, Gen Z activists are seeking the departure or removal of Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko from the institution's presidency.

In response to the potential for institutional restructuring, a new parliamentary force, "Vovonan'ny fihavaozan'i Madagasikara" (VFM), has formed. Comprising independents and Irmar dissidents, the VFM aims to participate in political consultations related to the Refoundation, concerned that a future political dialogue might replace the current Parliament with a transitional body.

Why it mattersThe political uncertainty surrounding the National Assembly's future could impact the stability of governance and the legislative process for all citizens.

Madagascar Film Festival awards filmmakers in five categories

The second annual Madagascar Film Festival – ISSM Awards concluded in Havoria Anosy, recognizing directors and creators across animation, fiction, and documentary films.

The Madagascar Film Festival – ISSM Awards recently celebrated Malagasy cinematic talent, culminating in an awards ceremony in Havoria Anosy. The festival, which included several days of screenings and professional exchanges, honored distinguished works in five competitive categories.

Awards were given for animation, fiction short film, fiction long film, documentary short film, and documentary long film. Louisette Ratsivahiny's "Fara" won the top prize in animation, while Franco Clerc's "Mandrakizay Doria" received the first prize for fiction long film. "Viavy" by Gilde Zafitsihadinoina secured the top award in the documentary long film category.

Zafindrazana Ralaiaridimby Edwella, general director of the ISSM, stated that the festival aims to professionalize Malagasy cinema and showcase local talent. The jury evaluated entries based on creativity, cultural dimension, image quality, and mastery of cinematic techniques to highlight works that demonstrate true artistic creation.

Why it mattersThe festival provides a platform for Malagasy filmmakers to gain recognition and professional development, impacting their careers and the local film industry.
