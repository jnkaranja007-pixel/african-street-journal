Health · 2026-10-22

Rotary and WHO organize march against polio

A public march will take place in Antananarivo on October 24, 2026, to raise awareness and combat polio.

The World Health Organization and the Rotary Club are organizing a public march in Antananarivo on Saturday, October 24, 2026, to combat polio. The event aims to raise awareness and encourage community participation in eradicating the disease, which primarily affects children under five years old.

Rotary has been involved in the fight against polio since 1979, supporting vaccination efforts and community outreach. This march is part of a national campaign to stop the spread of polio in Madagascar. The Ministry of Public Health is also supporting the initiative through disease surveillance and vaccination oversight.

Organizers are urging everyone to participate in the march to contribute to the eradication of polio. This event follows a recent national vaccination and Vitamin A supplementation campaign for children, which took place from October 5-9 across all 23 regions of Madagascar. Rotary emphasized that parental and community involvement is crucial for achieving the goal.

Why it mattersChildren under five years old in Madagascar are at risk of contracting polio, and the march aims to encourage participation in its eradication.

Ratovomanana Jaovolahasy receives arts and culture award

The founder and leader of the gospel band Jaws Band was honored for his contributions to Malagasy arts.

Ratovomanana Jaovolahasy, founder and leader of the gospel band Jaws Band, received the "Officier de l'ordre des arts, des lettres et de la culture" award yesterday. The Jaws Band, known for its energetic performances and gospel music, has been active for eight years. The band recently won the "Feo lafika mendrika indrindra" award from Radio Fiotazantsoa in 2026.

The Jaws Band is recognized for its powerful singing and stage presence. Popular songs by the band include "Tsy hiala aminao," "Hanaraka anao," and "Tsy manary anao Jéhovah." This award from the Ministry of Communication and Culture acknowledges Ratovomanana Jaovolahasy's significant impact on Malagasy arts.

The Jaws Band celebrated its seventh anniversary in 2025, having performed over 40 concerts during its existence. Ratovomanana Jaovolahasy, the band's founder, remains committed to advancing gospel music in Madagascar and hopes to take the genre much further.

Why it mattersRatovomanana Jaovolahasy is affected by receiving an award recognizing his artistic achievements.

Health · 2026-10-22

Rotary clubs march against polio

Community groups in Antananarivo will hold a four-kilometer walk to raise awareness for polio eradication efforts.

Rotary, Rotaract, and Interact clubs are organizing a four-kilometer citizen march in Antananarivo on October 24, coinciding with World Polio Day. This event follows a national vaccination campaign held from October 5 to 9 across all 23 regions of Madagascar.

The integrated campaign, which included vitamin A supplementation, aimed to protect nearly six million children under five years old from polio and measles. The World Health Organization is supporting the Ministry of Public Health in case surveillance, outbreak response, and vaccination planning.

Rotary International has been fighting polio since 1979, and its clubs in Madagascar continue community initiatives. The organization emphasizes that vaccination, epidemiological surveillance, and collective mobilization are crucial to stop poliovirus transmission and protect future generations.

Why it mattersChildren under five are at risk of irreversible paralysis from polio if not vaccinated.

Politics · 2026-10-22

Madagascar finalizes 2027 budget priorities

Electricity, agriculture, and education are among the key areas targeted for increased funding in the upcoming national budget.

Madagascar's government is in the final stages of approving its 2027 budget, with a strong emphasis on electricity, water, roads, health, education, and agricultural development. Prime Minister Mamitiana Rajaonarison stated that budget evaluations are nearly complete before the finance bill is sent to the National Assembly. Addressing persistent electricity outages, the Prime Minister suggested intentional sabotage may be a factor, pending investigation results. The Ministry of Economy and Finance also highlighted the energy sector's importance in the 2027 financial projections.

Lawmakers are advocating for increased state support for agriculture and education, while suggesting reductions in spending on institutions to free up resources for essential services. Concerns remain that current agricultural support, including seed distribution, is insufficient for sustainable production increases. A lack of agricultural technicians to guide farmers on best practices hinders the effectiveness of material aid, suggesting a need for a more comprehensive approach combining inputs, technical support, and producer monitoring.

Financial constraints also affect the education sector, with some initiatives relying on external funding. National resources are reportedly insufficient to cover the costs of school infrastructure and teacher recruitment. This raises questions about the tangible outcomes of the 2026 budget, emphasizing the need to align allocated funds for schools, roads, health centers, and other vital infrastructure with actual expenditures and completed projects.

Why it mattersThe 2027 budget priorities will affect Malagasy citizens through planned improvements in essential services like electricity, water, and education, alongside agricultural development.

Education · 2026-10-22

Scouts launch new academic year

Madagascar's Scouts opened the 2026-2027 academic year with an official ceremony and the distribution of neckerchiefs.

The Malagasy Scout organization, Kiadin’i Madagasikara (KIM), launched the 2026-2027 academic year at the Imam Reza Cultural Center in Ivato this week. The ceremony included the distribution of neckerchiefs to central office members and the official reception of deputy leaders. This event also marked the beginning of a three-year term for General Commissioner Okapi Droit.

Separately, Malagasy comedian Joely conducted a charitable event in Anosibe for children at the Association Meal Espoirs and the Association l’Île aux Enfants – Madagascar. This activity is part of the "Mbola Mijoro ho an’ny zaza malagasy" project, which Joely stated is a key goal alongside his performances in Madagascar.

From October 20 to 22, 2026, a consultative and coordination workshop involving multiple sectors and partners is taking place. The focus is on mental health and psychological support, with the Ministry of Public Health's Director for Non-Communicable Diseases emphasizing the importance of collaboration in advancing mental health initiatives.

Why it mattersChildren in Madagascar are affected by charitable initiatives and educational programs.
