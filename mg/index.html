Madagascar president meets Putin in Moscow

Michaël Randrianirina seeks Russian partnerships in energy, tech, and infrastructure at the Russia-Africa Summit.

Madagascar's Refondation of the Republic president, Michaël Randrianirina, met Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday morning. The meeting occurred during the third Russia-Africa Summit, which aims to strengthen political, economic, and diplomatic ties between Russia and African nations. Approximately 30 heads of state and government are attending the summit.

This marks the second meeting between Randrianirina and Putin, following their February 19, 2026, encounter at the Kremlin. During that meeting, both leaders expressed a desire to enhance cooperation in defense, energy, agriculture, and infrastructure. Putin outlined Russia's ambitions for African cooperation, emphasizing education, health, economy, agriculture, and defense.

Russia plans to establish logistics platforms for agricultural products to boost trade and food sovereignty. The country also aims to process African mineral resources locally. For Madagascar, this summit offers a chance to continue discussions from February and explore new cooperation avenues, particularly in attracting investors for energy, technology, and infrastructure projects.

Why it mattersMadagascar's president is seeking new partnerships in key development sectors like energy and technology.

Roxicomania Festival to showcase 18 artists

The two-day event on October 31 and November 1 will feature 13 emerging talents alongside established rock musicians.

The Roxicomania Festival will take place on October 31 and November 1 at the Anosy National Library parking lot in Madagascar.

The event aims to demonstrate the diversity of rock music, including subgenres like 'beko rock', 'alternatif', and 'soft rock', featuring 18 established artists and 13 new talents.

Performers will include Reko Band, Iraimbilanja, Rolf, Nully Gafunk, Andriamaninto, Shiny, Insidz, Blue’d & Njiva, Highomellow, Neverfall, Sidina, Load, Taoro, Behind The Twelves, Tsilavina Rakoto, Ultime Vibration, and Mozika Fototra.

Why it mattersMadagascar's rock music scene and its diverse artists will be showcased at the Roxicomania Festival.

Madagascar court to try ex-president Rajoelina

The High Constitutional Court confirmed the anti-corruption unit can prosecute former president Andry Rajoelina and ex-PM Christian Ntsay.

The High Constitutional Court (HCC) confirmed yesterday that the Anti-Corruption Unit (PAC) has the authority to try former president Andry Rajoelina, former Prime Minister Christian Ntsay, and several ex-ministers. This decision pertains to three judicial cases where these former leaders face accusations of corruption and related offenses.

The HCC has partially lifted the jurisdictional privilege for former president Andry Rajoelina and other former high-ranking officials from his administration. The court's ruling allows the PAC to proceed with legal action against them.

The HCC's decision means that these former officials will now face prosecution for alleged corruption. The specific details of the three judicial dossiers remain under review by the PAC.

Why it mattersFormer president Andry Rajoelina, former Prime Minister Christian Ntsay, and several ex-ministers will face corruption charges.

Politics · 2026-10-29

Tim party calls for election talks

The party secretary general stated that rumours of cancelled elections are unfounded.

The Madagascar political party Tiako i Madagasikara (Tim) has called for discussions on the upcoming elections. Tim's secretary general, Randriamasinoro Ny Rina, stated that rumours suggesting the elections will not happen are baseless. The party, founded by former president Ravalomanana, acknowledged existing political disagreements.

Ny Rina urged for dialogue to ensure the electoral process proceeds as scheduled, warning that delaying discussions could prolong the country's situation. He encouraged Tim supporters to register to vote, emphasizing the party's commitment to its promises and its role as a responsible political entity.

The Tim political bureau has instructed its followers to prepare for the elections and to register on the electoral rolls. The party also reminded all political parties of their duty to engage in political activity, educate citizens, and participate in elections, stating Tim has always been present through difficult and easy times.

Why it mattersMadagascar's political stability and the process of upcoming elections are directly affected.

GNBC women win third Madagascar basketball title

The Gendarmerie nationale team defeated JEA Vakinankaratra 47-42 in the final to secure their latest championship.

The women's basketball team of the Gendarmerie nationale (GNBC) has been crowned champions of Madagascar for the third year in a row, following their victories in 2024 and 2025. The team, coached by Eli Michaël Rakotonindrina, secured their latest title by defeating JEA Vakinankaratra 47-42 in a closely contested final match.

General Solofoson Lucien Rabialahy, president of GNBC, attributed the team's success to discipline, physical prowess, and solidarity, stating that players put individual skills to collective use. Coach Rakotonindrina, who has led the team to all three consecutive titles, expressed a desire to compete against top teams on the African continent.

In its five years of existence, the GNBC women's section has advanced from the N1B to the N1A league and now dominates the national basketball scene. The club also focuses on youth development with teams from U14 to N1A and plans to introduce football and handball sections. International competition participation is being considered, pending financial resources and federation support.

Why it mattersThe GNBC women's basketball team has won their third consecutive national championship, solidifying their dominance in Madagascar.
