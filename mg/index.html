Madagascar Presidency Convenes Election Talks

Political parties, civil society, and other groups will meet from September 1 to 8 to discuss the 2027 elections.

The Malagasy presidency is initiating a new phase of preparation for the 2027 elections by hosting a week-long consultation in Iavoloha. From September 1 to 8, political parties, associations, and civil society organizations focused on electoral matters will gather. Participants must demonstrate legal standing to attend these discussions, which aim to build a shared vision for the upcoming polls and avoid prolonging the current political period.

This meeting follows earlier engagements, including discussions between the Head of State and former leaders in July, and a confirmation of the 2027 election objective on August 18. The Independent National Electoral Commission (Ceni) is central to the process, with efforts to update the electoral roll by July 2027. However, the official timeline faces potential conflicts with the national dialogue led by the FFKM, whose conclusions are not expected until after May 2027, potentially delaying constitutional texts.

Authorities aim to prevent scheduling conflicts from hindering electoral preparations, advocating for simultaneous work on reforms. The goal is transparent, credible, and peaceful elections with broad acceptance. Key discussion points in Iavoloha include electoral governance, ballot guarantees, and the order of elections, with the possibility of a presidential election preceding a constitutional vote being considered. Eligibility criteria for the presidential race are also a sensitive topic.

Why it mattersPolitical actors and civil society groups are affected by the discussions on electoral governance and the order of future elections.

Four murder suspects die during police operation in Ambohimanga

Four adolescents arrested in connection with the double murder of two young women died after allegedly attempting to escape during a crime scene reconstruction.

The investigation into the deaths of Stéphanie Nohasoavina and Haingotiana Rasamison has ended in violence, with four teenage suspects dying during a police operation. The four were arrested on August 25 following the discovery of the bodies on August 23 in a ravine in Ambohimanga Rova. The suspects, some of whom were minors, were taken to the crime scene for a reconstruction when they allegedly attempted to flee or attack officers, leading to the fatal shooting.

The incident has sparked widespread controversy and public outcry, with many questioning the security measures surrounding the custody of young suspects and alleging summary execution. The deaths have extinguished the legal proceedings, preventing a public trial and leaving the families of the victims without definitive answers about the exact circumstances and motives behind the double murder. This outcome has created a judicial void and raised suspicions about potential accomplices.

The events in Ambohimanga Rova have resulted in the loss of six young lives, including the two victims and the four suspects. The premature end to the legal process deprives the nation of the truth and leaves a lingering mystery. The authorities' handling of the case has been criticized, with concerns raised about the procedures followed and the ultimate consequences for justice and societal trust.

Why it mattersThe deaths of the four suspects prevent a public trial, leaving the families of the victims without answers about the double murder and its motives.

Madagascar Artists Draw Crowds with Diverse Weekend Performances

Rija Ramanantoanina, Erick Manana, Feo Gasy, Samoëla, and Rebika captivated audiences in Antananarivo with varied musical styles.

The capital city of Antananarivo buzzed with musical energy this past weekend as multiple artists delivered performances across different genres. Rija Ramanantoanina, accompanied by the thirty-piece Bandy Baraka Big Band, revisited his hits at La Balançoire Ivandry. Erick Manana and Feo Gasy presented traditional folk music from the Highlands at CCI Ivato, marking a reunion for Feo Gasy after a hiatus. These artists indicated plans for future international tours.

Samoëla, performing at the Antsahamanitra amphitheater, drew a large audience for over four hours, focusing on his lyrical compositions. Meanwhile, Rebika performed at Esca Antanimena, maintaining a lively atmosphere despite reported difficulties among some members. The group celebrated forty years of career with a fireworks display, concluding a day that featured a total of seventy songs.

Despite varying attendance numbers for some shows, the overall public engagement remained high, with audiences eager for live music. Artists from different generations and musical backgrounds, including Erick Manana who has a career spanning four decades, showcased the vibrant local music scene. The weekend's events highlighted the enduring appeal of live performances and the diverse talent within Madagascar.

Why it mattersMadagascar's artists are engaging audiences and planning international tours, indicating potential growth in the cultural export sector.

Madagascar State Fails to Spend $2 Billion in Foreign Funds

Government ministries are operating at engagement rates as low as two percent, leaving infrastructure projects unfunded.

Madagascar's government is failing to spend billions in allocated funds, creating a paradox of available money and stalled projects. In the first quarter of 2026, internal investment execution stood at only 2.3%, with the Ministry of Public Health dropping from a 40.3% engagement rate to 2.9%. Similar low engagement levels were reported in the Ministry of National Education and the Ministry of the Interior, contributing to a sense of national regression.

Despite having nearly two billion dollars in external financing unspent, equivalent to more than a year of the country's tax revenue, the state is not disbursing these funds. The Ministry of Finance acknowledged in May that less than a third of World Bank funds are being utilized. This inability to absorb available financing occurs even as the government actively seeks more international funding.

The issue is not a lack of funds, as tax revenues are meeting expectations and salaries are being paid. Instead, the problem lies with the state's capacity to execute spending. This spending paralysis, combined with an inability to effectively collect revenue, forms a double bind for the Malagasy state, hindering development and public services.

Why it mattersThe Malagasy state's inability to spend allocated funds affects the entire country by preventing infrastructure development and the implementation of public services.

Raboussa's 25-year rap career fills Palais des Sports

The rapper celebrated a quarter-century on stage with a sold-out concert featuring live musicians and guest artists, including his sons.

The Palais des Sports et de la Culture de Mahamasina was full yesterday for Raboussa's 25th anniversary concert. The artist demonstrated that rap, especially with live musicians, can still fill a large venue of over 7,000 people. Event organizer SR Event reported a sell-out hours before the show, highlighting the rarity of a solo artist filling such a large space in the rap genre.

Raboussa performed with a live band, including Nino on keyboards, Fy on drums, Rapa and Nanja on guitar, Andry on saxophone, and DJ Raez on turntables. Vocalists Volamihanta and Natacha covered parts previously sung by former collaborator Fy. The concert featured guest appearances from groups 18,3 and Double’NN, as well as Marion, who performed the hit "My Girl."

The evening's most significant moments included the appearance of Raboussa's two sons, who joined him on stage, signaling a potential continuation of the family's musical passion. The concert, marked by nostalgia and shared moments, celebrated Raboussa's enduring connection with his audience after 25 years. He also announced an upcoming collector's album documenting his career.

Why it mattersThe event's success and the announcement of a collector's album indicate continued commercial viability and cultural relevance for the artist.
