Madagascar Overhauls Oil Sector Architecture

The government is restructuring the downstream hydrocarbon sector to ensure supply security and address regional energy access disparities.

The Malagasy government is undertaking a comprehensive overhaul of its downstream hydrocarbon sector to mitigate critical logistical vulnerabilities and shield the national economy from global price volatility. This initiative, guided by the Ministry of Energy and Hydrocarbons, aims to secure a sustainable energy supply and rectify territorial inequalities that hinder energy access in remote districts.

Key to this strategy is the gradual establishment of state strategic reserves to buffer against potential supply disruptions and geopolitical shocks. Concurrently, the government plans to develop a national refining capacity, with technical studies to determine optimal locations and financial viability for modern refineries. This aims to locally process resources like the Tsimiroro heavy oil deposits, thereby reducing the import bill for finished products.

To invigorate the market and attract private investment, the state is dismantling historical barriers, opening the entire value chain from importation to retail distribution to all operators meeting legal and environmental standards. Priority investment zones with incentive frameworks will be identified to improve supply to remote regions, while the Office Malgache des Hydrocarbures will see its powers enhanced with advanced technological oversight.

Why it mattersThe government's restructuring of the oil sector aims to improve energy security and access, particularly for remote districts, and reduce reliance on international markets.

Official Corruption Thrives Amidst Government Silence

Widespread 'bizna,' or petty corruption, is undermining national integrity and the government's reform promises.

A culture of pervasive corruption, termed 'bizna,' is flourishing across Madagascar, impacting daily life and public trust. This practice involves officials from police officers demanding bribes to customs agents requiring 'voan-dalàna' (a form of bribe), and even judges manipulating verdicts for financial gain. The issue extends to parliamentarians swayed by financial incentives and public officials facilitating corrupt contracts. This systemic corruption, fueled by illicit gains, operates in plain sight, with those involved benefiting from a culture of silence and impunity.

The recent case involving jurist Harotsilavo Rakotoson, who spoke out about the prevalence of 'bizna' during a podcast, has brought this issue into sharp focus. Rakotoson faced scrutiny for his candid remarks, highlighting a pattern of silencing critics who expose uncomfortable truths. The article suggests that the Head of State and Prime Minister should intervene against those who distort their reform agendas by perpetuating these corrupt practices, distinguishing 'bizna' from legitimate business activities.

Authorities have historically used the vague charge of 'threat to state security' to suppress dissent and silence individuals who speak out against corruption or mediocrity. Examples cited include individuals being targeted for wearing subversive t-shirts, making critical gestures, or photographing the perceived excessive baggage of state officials. This tactic effectively stifles free speech and prevents public discourse on critical national issues, further entrenching the corrupt system.

Why it mattersThe prevalence of corruption affects all citizens by undermining fair processes and the rule of law, making daily interactions with officials unpredictable and costly.

Researchers Seek Funding for Commercializing Discoveries

Madagascar's researchers are exploring strategies to fund their work, including turning discoveries into companies and partnering with the private sector.

Researchers in Madagascar are actively seeking funding for their work, with strategies including the UniPod project aimed at transforming research findings into viable companies. A portion of incoming funds will be reinvested into research activities. Partnerships with the private sector are also being pursued as a secondary solution to sustain the research sector.

The government is collecting data on research outputs from 18 universities and nine research centers nationwide to improve dissemination. Annually, hundreds of research results are produced. This effort aims to enhance the distribution of these findings across the country.

The University of Antananarivo will host its "Salon de l’étudiant" on September 3 and 4 at Esplanade Ankatso. This event will showcase the university's academic programs and feature collaborations with private sector entities. These partners will participate in exhibitions and conduct workshops for students preparing for careers that align with market demands.

Why it mattersResearchers and universities are seeking private sector partnerships and commercialization strategies to fund their work and make research outputs more accessible.

Power cuts return to Antananarivo, disrupting businesses

Daily electricity interruptions are intensifying across the capital, affecting key economic sectors and reviving memories of past instability.

Electricity outages have become a daily reality in Antananarivo, with increasing frequency over recent weeks. The disruptions now impact significant economic zones like Ankorondrano and Behoririka, alongside residential areas. Merchants, companies, and individuals are facing shared difficulties that directly affect their operations and daily lives, stirring up unwelcome memories of past service failures.

This situation revives memories of widespread discontent fueled by power and water shortages before September 25, 2025. The demand for reliable electricity and potable water was a key part of the protest movement that led to the previous regime's downfall. Nearly a year later, the return of these cuts is more than a technical issue; it touches a sensitive point for the capital's residents, who are showing signs of weariness and resignation.

The state-owned utility JIRAMA attributes the problems to breakdowns, technical issues, and the dry season's impact on hydroelectric production. While these factors may explain some disruptions, consumers question the benefits of JIRAMA's direct fuel imports, previously touted as a cost-saving measure. For households and businesses, the continuity of power supply remains the critical indicator of service improvement.

Why it mattersBusinesses and residents in Antananarivo are affected by daily electricity outages that disrupt operations and daily life.

Pintade Amo­ron’i Mania Defeats Ascut Atsinana­na in Close Basketball Match

The Pintade Amo­ron’i Mania girls' U16 team secured a narrow 58-53 victory over Ascut Atsinana­na in a tightly contested national championship quarterfinal.

Pintade Amo­ron’i Mania emerged victorious against Ascut Atsinana­na with a final score of 58-53 in the U16 girls' national basketball championship quarterfinal held in Amoron’i Mania. The match was closely fought until the end, with both teams exchanging the lead throughout the game. Pintade benefited from playing on their home court and in front of their supporters.

In the boys' U16 category, several favored teams were eliminated. JSB defeated Cosfa 77-55, while AS Montfort beat MB2 All 67-44. Fandrefiala lost to NAS 53-50, and AS ST Michel advanced by defeating NBE 65-41. Ascut also secured a spot in the next round, winning against UBCM with a score of 69-43.

The national championship quarterfinal matches for the U16 age group, both boys and girls, took place last Thursday in Amoron’i Mania. The event saw several upsets in the boys' division, while the girls' competition featured a nail-biting finish in one of the key matchups.

Why it mattersInvestors may be interested in the performance of local sports teams as indicators of community engagement and potential sponsorship opportunities.
