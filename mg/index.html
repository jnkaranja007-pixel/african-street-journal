Madagascar court allows constitutional referendum before presidential vote

The High Constitutional Court ruled that a referendum to adopt a new constitution can proceed before the presidential election.

Madagascar's High Constitutional Court (HCC) ruled on November 4, 2026, that a referendum to adopt a new constitution can be held before the presidential election. The court, responding to a request from the President of the Refoundation, determined that a popular consultation for a new constitution does not violate current constitutional provisions.

The HCC clarified that while a simple revision of the existing text is different from adopting a new constitution to establish a new institutional order, the latter is legally possible. This process would begin with the President of the Refoundation, followed by a Council of Ministers deliberation. The proposed constitution would then require approval from three-quarters of the National Assembly members before being submitted to a popular vote.

The project law for 2027 allocates over 199 billion ariary to the CENI for electoral preparations, including the constitutional referendum and presidential election. The HCC also stated that a referendum offering multiple options to voters is feasible, provided that legal and regulatory frameworks are amended beforehand. The official electoral calendar is yet to be established.

Why it mattersMalagasy citizens will vote in a referendum on a new constitution before electing a president.

Russia and US intensify influence efforts in Madagascar

Both nations are increasing diplomatic and security engagements across the island nation.

Madagascar is experiencing heightened diplomatic activity from both Russia and the United States, with both powers increasing contacts on military, security, and economic fronts. Recent visits by representatives from both nations to the northern part of the island highlight this growing competition for influence in the Indian Ocean region.

Military cooperation is a key area of focus between Madagascar and Russia, with plans to continue training Malagasy soldiers by Russian instructors and exchange defense equipment. Discussions also cover agriculture, health, higher education, and mining governance. Meanwhile, U.S. officials from AFRICOM, led by General John Brennan, met with Malagasy leaders on November 3-4 to discuss maritime surveillance, combating illicit trafficking, piracy, and military training, alongside the importance of critical minerals for economic security.

Russian interim chargé d'affaires Alexeï Buriak visited Nosy Be to discuss tourism, agriculture, and trade in vanilla and ylang-ylang essential oils, renewing an agreement on maintaining Russian monuments. The U.S. delegation's visit focused on maritime security and critical minerals, indicating a strategic interest in Madagascar's resources and location.

Why it mattersMadagascar's government is engaging with both Russia and the United States on military, economic, and security matters.

Madagascar parliament to review 2027 budget

The finance bill proposes 5.1% economic growth and targets energy, roads, agriculture, education, and health.

Madagascar's National Assembly will examine the 2027 finance bill, which outlines the government's economic recovery ambitions. The Ministry of Economy and Finance submitted the draft law on time, allowing deputies to review the economic and budgetary choices. This submission adheres to institutional procedures and prioritizes parliamentary debate.

The 2027 budget, prepared under the guidance of Minister Dr. Ramiarison Herinjatovo Aimé, aims for 5.1% economic growth, up from an estimated 4.1% in 2026. Key priorities include revitalizing the energy sector, particularly the Jirama company, improving road infrastructure, and supporting agriculture, education, and health. The budget also allocates funds for a constitutional referendum and presidential elections planned for 2027.

The government plans to strengthen fiscal discipline by increasing tax and customs revenue collection and combating fraud. Public spending will focus on investments expected to yield concrete economic and social returns. Regional budget conferences have helped identify territorial priorities for the decentralization agenda.

Why it mattersMadagascar's parliament will debate a budget aimed at economic recovery, with specific allocations for key sectors and upcoming elections.

Madagascar central bank holds key rate at 12.50%

The Bank of Madagascar will maintain its benchmark interest rate at 12.50% for three months to combat rising inflation.

The Central Bank of Madagascar (BFM) decided to keep its benchmark interest rate at 12.50% following a monetary committee meeting on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. This decision, effective November 4 for three months, aims to continue fighting inflation which is impacting household purchasing power and economic activities.

The BFM's monetary policy report on November 3 stated that maintaining this rate will help keep monetary conditions restrictive enough to gradually bring inflation back to its 5% medium-term target. Annual inflation rose to 8.6% in September from 6.8% in March, driven by food and energy prices.

The central bank forecasts inflation could reach 9.3% in December 2026 before slowing in the second quarter of 2027. This sustained high rate may increase borrowing costs for Malagasy businesses, particularly SMEs needing finance for investments, and could also make credit less accessible for households.

Why it mattersMalagasy households and businesses face continued high borrowing costs and persistent inflation impacting their finances.

Madagascar taekwondo championships end in Fianarantsoa

320 taekwondo athletes competed in combat and poomsae events over two days.

The Madagascar taekwondo WT championships concluded today at the Ambatomena gymnasium in Fianarantsoa after two days of competition. 320 taekwondo athletes participated in the national summit, featuring combat ('kyorugi') and poomsae disciplines.

Five regional leagues, including Analamanga, Vakinankaratra, Atsinanana, Boeny, and the host Matsiatra-Ambony, sent representatives. The event included poomsae tests in seven categories for various age groups and genders, alongside kyorugi bouts from junior to master levels.

The final day featured remaining kyorugi categories for seniors and juniors, as well as team combats. The championships were preceded by a training session for referees led by Nicolas de Gonzague Randriamiandrisoa, an international referee from Nantes.

Why it matters320 taekwondo athletes from five Malagasy regions competed in national championships.
