Politics · 2026-10-31

Madagascar president seeks economic ties with Russia

Colonel Michaël Randrianirina highlighted energy, agriculture, and health as key sectors for new partnerships.

Madagascar's President, Colonel Michaël Randrianirina, attended the 3rd Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow, aiming to strengthen economic cooperation with Russia. He stated that Madagascar faces destabilization attempts due to this partnership but remains committed to its growth.

Randrianirina emphasized the role of the Madagascar-Russia Chamber of Commerce in attracting investors and seeks Russian companies that can process raw materials locally. Potential areas for new partnerships include energy, agriculture, health, water supply, digital technology, and scientific research.

Russia presented its A7 cross-border payment platform to the Malagasy delegation, a system that has become significant in Russia's financial ecosystem. The platform is expanding into Africa, with existing offices in Nigeria and Zimbabwe.

Why it mattersMadagascar's president is seeking new economic partnerships with Russia, focusing on sectors like energy and agriculture, which could lead to local industrial development and job creation.

Business · 2026-10-31

Madagascar and Turkey seek more investment

The two nations aim to boost economic ties and development projects, focusing on energy, education, and trade.

Madagascar and Turkey aim to deepen their economic partnership by attracting more investment and development projects. This goal was stated on Thursday, October 29, in Antananarivo during a reception celebrating the 103rd anniversary of the Turkish Republic. Discussions focused on opportunities within the Malagasy economy.

Minister of Public Security Erick Michel Wouli Soumah Idrissa affirmed the authorities' commitment to strengthening bilateral relations. Ambassador Halime Ebru Demircan confirmed Turkey's interest in increased collaboration in energy, education, and trade. A significant past achievement is the Ambohimanambola thermal power plant, built by Turkish group Aksa for over $80 million.

Sectors like agriculture, infrastructure, mining, tourism, health, and renewable energy are now targeted for Turkish capital. The government seeks to promote industrialization, process national products, and create jobs. Discussions are ongoing for agreements on fishing, electricity, and investment promotion. Since 1992, 181 Malagasy students have received Turkish university scholarships.

Why it mattersMadagascar's government hopes to foster industrialization, develop processing of national products, and create jobs through increased Turkish investment.

Climate · 2026-10-31

Madagascar pledges women in ocean conservation

The island nation joins Belize in a global initiative to integrate women into marine resource decision-making.

Madagascar has pledged to place women at the centre of its ocean conservation efforts and blue economy strategies. The commitment was made at the UN Biodiversity Conference (CoP17) as part of the Women Ocean Guardians Initiative. This global partnership aims to integrate women's leadership into formal decision-making systems for marine resources. Madagascar joins 14 other governments that have made similar pledges.

Andrianirina Harinaivo Fanomezantsoa, Madagascar’s Coordonnateur Général for Green Diplomacy, stated that women in Madagascar are key contributors to marine conservation and coastal development. He highlighted their roles as rangers, saving group members, park managers, healthcare workers, scientists, and village chiefs. The initiative has secured over 50 commitments, including those from 16 governments and more than 30 partners.

The Women Ocean Guardians Initiative aims to address gaps in the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, particularly Target 23 on gender equality, which remains the least advanced global goal. Challenges in achieving the 30x30 ocean protection goal, such as management effectiveness and inclusive governance, are also being targeted.

Why it mattersMadagascar's commitment ensures women's leadership is integrated into national marine conservation strategies and blue economy development.

Madagascar university officials detained over fake diplomas

Three leaders at Itasy University in Soavinandriana face pre-trial detention for alleged diploma fraud.

three senior officials at Itasy University in Soavinandriana, Madagascar, have been placed in pre-trial detention following an investigation into fake diplomas. Two other individuals are under judicial supervision. The case, revealed by the Independent Anti-Corruption Bureau (Bianco), involves the alleged use of falsified degrees to secure positions within the institution.

The implicated officials include the former university president, the engineering school director, the general coordinator, the accounting director, and the director of finance and general affairs. Investigations revealed that fake license and master 2 diplomas, purportedly issued by the university, were used to justify administrative appointments. The finance director is accused of issuing these fraudulent documents.

The charges include forgery, misuse of public office, complicity, and illicit gain. The Public Prosecutor's Office stated that investigations are ongoing and may uncover further irregularities and responsibilities. The case highlights alleged fabrications and usage of false documents, abuse of function, and obtaining undue advantages.

Why it mattersthree university officials in Madagascar are in detention and two are under judicial supervision due to allegations of using and issuing fake diplomas for positions.

Health · 2026-10-31

US donates technology to Madagascar health ministry

American officials provided digital tools to support frontline health workers in data collection and transmission.

American officials have donated modern technology to Madagascar's Ministry of Health to aid frontline health workers. The equipment will support the collection, input, and transmission of health data. This initiative aims to streamline and accelerate data processes while improving accuracy and reducing errors.

The donation is intended to enhance the efficiency of health information management across the country. Health workers will use the new technology to manage patient records and health statistics more effectively. This move is expected to improve the overall quality of health services by ensuring more reliable data.

The Ministry of Health received the equipment on October 31, 2026. The ministry anticipates better tracking of public health trends and improved response to health challenges.

Why it mattersMadagascar's health workers will use new technology to improve the accuracy and speed of collecting and transmitting health data.
