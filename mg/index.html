Madagascar Boeing 777 trial delayed again

The Aviation Civil of Madagascar requested more investigations into the 2025 Boeing 777 shipment to Iran.

The trial concerning the controversial shipment of Boeing 777s to Iran has been postponed again, with no substantive hearings held. The Aviation Civil of Madagascar, a plaintiff, and the prosecution requested further investigations. The case, which began with 38 defendants, may now include up to 51 individuals, and a new court date is set for November 5.

The hearing, delayed by the late arrival of Indian national Khushwinder Singh, saw the ACM lawyers highlight insufficient evidence, including a need for US documents regarding the aircraft transfer to Iran, a country under US sanctions. They also seek a criminal classification of the acts, suggesting a threat to national security. The prosecution noted 13 additional ACM agents identified by Bianco in December 2025 who have not all been questioned.

Lawyers for the defendants protested the repeated delays, which have kept some, including Singh, detained for 13 months without a final judgment. Requests for provisional release for key defendants, including former Transport Minister Valéry Ramonjavelo, were denied by the prosecution due to the charges' gravity. ACM Director General Alban Rakotoarisoa expressed concern about potential international sanctions against Madagascar.

Why it mattersThe trial delay affects 51 potential defendants and prolongs the uncertainty for those detained.

Former MP Naivo Raholdina jailed for 10 years

Naivo Raholdina and former fokontany chief Jean Nirina Rafanomezantsoa convicted for land deals.

Former deputy Naivo Raholdina was sentenced to 10 years in prison and fined 200 million ariary on Tuesday, October 27, at the Anosy court. Jean Nirina Rafanomezantsoa, former head of the Anosizato-Est fokontany, received a five-year prison sentence. Both were found guilty of abuse of power and illegal land transactions.

The convictions stem from the irregular occupation of a plot in Anosibe Angarangarana and the controversial sale of a football field in Anosizato. Raholdina and Rafanomezantsoa, detained since May, were sentenced in a common law case, unrelated to state security charges against other former regime officials. Raholdina, a close associate of former president Andry Rajoelina, was absent for the verdict.

In a separate case, Raïssa Razaivola received a five-year prison sentence and a 10.8 million ariary fine. Razaivola, previously sentenced to seven years in 2023, had been released after the October 2025 power change. She was re-arrested in February and transferred to Avaradrano.

Why it mattersFormer deputy Naivo Raholdina and former fokontany chief Jean Nirina Rafanomezantsoa were convicted for abuse of power and illegal land transactions.

Madagascar to compete in men's three-person petanque world championship

13 African nations will represent the continent at the upcoming world championship in Thailand.

Madagascar will send a team to compete in the men's three-person petanque world championship. The tournament is scheduled to take place in Thailand from November 20-24. 13 African nations will participate in the event.

The participating African countries include Algeria, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Côte d'Ivoire, Madagascar, Mali, Mauritania, Mauritius, Niger, Senegal, and Benin. The competition is for the men's three-person team category.

In other sports news, the CosPN Madagascar basketball team finished fourth in their group at the 'Road to Bal' tournament in Hoima, Uganda. They secured one win against Ushindzi Comores with a score of 88-55, and suffered three losses.

Why it mattersMadagascar will be represented by its national team in an international petanque competition.

ministry rejects tourism director nomination

the tourism ministry publicly disagreed with the ONTM's choice for interim executive director.

The Ministry of Tourism and Handicrafts has publicly rejected the Office National du Tourisme de Madagascar's (ONTM) appointment of Jean-Marc Razakazafy as interim executive director. The ONTM's board announced the nomination on October 26, stating it aimed to revitalize the office, but the ministry stated it had not approved the decision. This disagreement follows the departure of former director Danny Barivelo on October 21.

The ONTM board said its activities and partner commitments would continue normally despite the leadership change. However, the ministry expressed reservations about Razakazafy's candidacy even before the announcement. The ministry emphasized that leadership changes must adhere to good governance and the principles of 'Refondation'. Razakazafy previously served as a permanent mission officer in former President Andry Rajoelina's cabinet, a parliamentary advisor, and a technical advisor in the Ministry of Energy and Hydrocarbons.

Both the ONTM and the ministry invoked the 'Refondation' policy to support their opposing stances on the nomination. The ONTM maintains Razakazafy's appointment, while the ministry has not offered its support. The situation leaves the ONTM's operational capacity in question, with its interim director's legitimacy challenged by its own supervising ministry.

Why it mattersThe public disagreement between the Ministry of Tourism and the ONTM over a key appointment creates uncertainty for the tourism sector's leadership and operations.

Madagascar president rejects social media pressure on cabinet changes

Colonel Michaël Randrianirina stated he will not dismiss ministers based on online criticism, asserting his authority.

President Colonel Michaël Randrianirina declared he will not yield to social media pressure to remove government members. He specifically stated that the Minister of Water and Sanitation would retain her position without needing re-evaluation. This stance comes amid online criticism, particularly on Facebook, following an incident involving 11 Chinese nationals who left prison irregularly.

The president addressed the issue after a journalist questioned him about a minister implicated in a controversial case. He emphasized that decisions would not be made under Facebook's influence, noting an ongoing investigation. The case involves the Minister of Justice, Fanirisoa Ernaivo, whose name was mentioned by the secretary-general of the prison administration union, who is currently in Dubai and claims the minister gave him instructions regarding the detainees' release.

Minister Fanirisoa Ernaivo denies the accusations and suggests a network is involved in these operations. In contrast, President Randrianirina publicly praised the Minister of Water and Sanitation, Minosoa Elia Razafindraniaina, stating her position is secure. Online commentators have drawn parallels to past leaders who dismissed social media criticism, recalling how platforms like Facebook were instrumental in mobilizing protests that led to regime changes.

Why it mattersPresident Randrianirina's decisions on government appointments and his response to public opinion on social media directly affect the administration and its officials.
