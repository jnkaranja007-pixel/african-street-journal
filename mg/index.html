Ravalomanana sentenced to ten years for 2025 protest violence

The former Senate president was convicted of complicity in murder and intentional injury related to the repression of demonstrations in Antananarivo.

Former Senate president General Richard Ravalomanana has been sentenced to ten years of forced labor for his role in violence during September and October 2025 protests in Antananarivo. The court found him guilty of complicity in murder and intentional injury, alleging he instructed security forces to use violence and facilitated abuses against demonstrators. The case included accusations of dragging a person and gendarmes burning protesters' hair, though Ravalomanana denied any involvement.

The defense argued that no direct evidence, such as written or verbal orders, linked Ravalomanana to the violence, and that he was not part of the operational chain of command. An earlier charge of threatening a former director of Sirama was dismissed due to prescription and the complainant withdrawing his complaint. Despite the defense's arguments, the court convicted the former Senate president, who is to be imprisoned at Imerintsiatosika high-security prison.

Ravalomanana's lawyer has announced an appeal to the Court of Cassation, seeking to overturn the verdict. The conviction stems from events between September 25 and October 11, 2025, where authorities responded to demonstrations. The prosecution's case focused on Ravalomanana's alleged instructions to security forces, which he and a former national police director denied, stating operations were managed by structures under the Prime Minister and a mixed operational staff.

Why it mattersFormer Senate president Richard Ravalomanana was sentenced to ten years of forced labor for his role in violence during 2025 protests, affecting his political standing and freedom.

Madagascar Airlines grounds ATR plane due to engine shortage

The national carrier faces continued flight disruptions as two essential reserve engines undergo maintenance in Europe.

Madagascar Airlines is experiencing ongoing flight disruptions because two reserve engines for one of its five ATR aircraft are delayed in delivery. These engines are undergoing regulatory maintenance in European workshops. The airline's management stated yesterday that they are awaiting the engines' return after multiple postponements.

The delivery was initially scheduled for August 27, then moved to September 2, but has been delayed again. The airline's director general, Rado Rabarilala, indicated a further one-week delay is expected without a precise new date. French and German embassies have intervened to expedite repairs.

With only four aircraft now operational, Madagascar Airlines must adjust flight schedules, impacting regularity and punctuality. The company apologizes for the inconvenience and is covering hotel costs for affected passengers. The director general acknowledged a lack of specific delivery timelines in the contract, noting engine maintenance can exceed two million dollars, of which approximately 375,000 dollars remain outstanding.

Why it mattersMadagascar Airlines is affected by the engine delays, which disrupt flight schedules and incur financial costs for passenger accommodation and outstanding maintenance bills.

Magistrates Union Challenges Justice Ministry Over Suspensions

The Magistrates Union of Madagascar contests the Justice Ministry's handling of three anti-corruption magistrates' suspensions, escalating a dispute over disciplinary procedures.

The Magistrates Union of Madagascar (SMM) has publicly challenged the Ministry of Justice regarding the suspension of three magistrates from the Anti-Corruption Pole (PAC) in Antananarivo. SMM President Mbintanarivo Andriantsihorisoa stated that the magistrates should have been informed of the specific grievances against them and allowed to present their defense before any action was taken. The union alleges this constitutes "harassment" and that their request for a prior review of the situation was ignored.

The SMM is also raising broader concerns about directives issued to magistrates on sensitive cases, demanding that any instructions influencing decisions or legal interpretations be formalized in writing. This demand directly addresses concerns about judicial independence and protection from hierarchical pressure. The union is not limiting its response to public protest and is seeking the suspension of the measure against the three magistrates, initiating a new conflict with the Ministry.

The Ministry of Justice justified the sanctions by citing alleged failures in case management and professional responsibilities, following an investigation by the Anti-Corruption Pole Monitoring and Evaluation Committee. The ministry affirmed its commitment to strictly applying regulations without differential treatment. This opposition presents two conflicting views: the Ministry emphasizes professional accountability and adherence to rules, while the SMM criticizes the procedure and defends the sanctioned magistrates, signaling a wider institutional confrontation.

Why it mattersThe suspension of three magistrates and the ensuing dispute between their union and the Ministry of Justice could impact the perceived independence and stability of the judiciary, potentially affecting legal processes and investor confidence.

New Rice Variety 'Besalohy' Promises 6 Tons Per Hectare

Developed through collaboration, the Besalohy rice variety offers high yields and adaptability to various Malagasy soil conditions.

Madagascar's agriculture sector sees a significant advancement with the introduction of the new rice variety, 'Besalohy'. This development is the result of a partnership between the Research Center for Rural Development (Fofifa) and the Japan International Research Center for Agricultural Sciences (Jircas). The variety is noted for its quality, distinct appearance, and aroma, and is suitable for local climate conditions.

Besalohy rice contributes to productive and sustainable rice cultivation, particularly in less fertile lands. It can yield up to six tons per hectare and can be planted in any season. Fofifa and Jircas conducted research and trials from 2021 in locations including Kianjasoa, Itasy, and Toamasina, with successful outcomes.

The development of Besalohy utilized molecular techniques to improve rice varieties, ensuring good yields even in challenging or infertile soils. The variety has a life cycle of approximately three months and its seeds can be reused. Fofifa's Director General, Pr Jean Michel Leong Pock Tsy, stated the goal is to continuously improve yields by listening to farmers' needs and observing current conditions.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by the introduction of the Besalohy rice variety because it offers higher yields and adaptability to less fertile lands, potentially improving their harvests.

Madagascar Sees Spike in Fire Alerts

Satellite data reveals 17,485 fire alerts between August 2025 and August 2026, an exceptionally high number compared to previous years.

Madagascar is experiencing its peak season for bush and forest fires, a period that typically lasts seventeen weeks. Between August 18, 2025, and August 24, 2026, a total of 17,485 fire alert points were recorded across the country. This figure, derived from reliable data from VIIRS and Global Forest Watch, is noted as exceptionally high when compared to previous years, reaching back to 2012.

The platform also indicates that from 2001 to 2025, Madagascar lost 350,000 hectares of forest due to fires, with the year 2014 marking the largest loss of forest cover during the high season, at 49,000 hectares destroyed. In response to this trend, Madagascar National Parks (MNP) has begun distributing protective equipment to its field agents as part of its fire management strategy.

This initiative, conducted in partnership with the French association Baroudeur Massilia Pompiers du Monde, aligns with national policies for disaster risk management. Eighty-three individual protective equipment kits were distributed across eleven Madagascar National Parks sites, supporting the national contingency plan for bush and forest fires signed in December 2024.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by the increased risk of bush and forest fires, which can destroy crops and agricultural land.
