Madagascar launches National Consultation process in Andohatapenaka

The National Consultation, themed "recovery and renewal," began today in Andohatapenaka, aiming to clarify its methodology and designate a national coordinator.

The National Consultation process officially started this Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, at Village Voara Andohatapenaka. The event, which included an ecumenical service led by the Ecumenical Council of Christian Churches of Madagascar (FFKM), is set to outline the process's key stages and introduce the national coordinator responsible for its implementation.

Political, social, and institutional actors have been awaiting this launch for several weeks, seeking clarity on the consultation methods that will be used across the country. The process is designed to gather input from the grassroots level, extending to the fokontany, to collect public expectations, proposals, and concerns.

These contributions will be centralized, examined, and synthesized at various levels to inform a national vision, according to the organizers. The initiative follows political upheavals and mobilizations in September and October 2025, which significantly altered Madagascar's institutional landscape and increased calls for state reform.

Why it mattersThe National Consultation aims to gather public input from all levels of society to inform future political and institutional processes, directly affecting citizens' participation in governance.

Madagascar's Central Bank Installs Four Gold Collection Points

The Banky Foiben'i Madagasikara (BFM) will establish four gold collection points to boost national gold reserves and formalize the artisanal gold mining sector.

The Banky Foiben'i Madagasikara (BFM) plans to install four gold collection points in Ambilobe, Maevatanana, Mananjary, and Miandrivazo, all areas with high gold potential. This initiative aims to strengthen the country's gold reserves and formalize the gold supply chain by bringing official channels closer to artisanal miners.

These new structures are intended to improve the framework for gold commercialization and reduce reliance on informal circuits. The BFM also seeks to enhance quality control and purity of collected metal, aligning with current standards. This effort is part of a policy started in 2023 to integrate monetary gold into Madagascar's foreign exchange reserves.

The project also includes social and financial dimensions, with BFM intending to support the formalization of artisanal miners, improve their access to banking services, and facilitate funding for cleaner extraction technologies. This aligns with commitments made under planetGOLD Madagascar to progressively eliminate mercury use in artisanal gold mining.

Why it mattersArtisanal gold miners will gain access to formal collection points and banking services, potentially improving their livelihoods and working conditions.

National Assembly convenes extraordinary session amid political uncertainty

Madagascar's National Assembly began an extraordinary session on Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, to address seven legislative projects and confront calls for its dissolution.

The National Assembly is holding an extraordinary session at Tsimbazaza, focusing on seven legislative projects, including new frameworks for electronic communications, cybercrime, and the ratification of a UN convention from October 2025. Other items on the agenda include military status, peaceful nuclear technology use, an investment agreement for the SADC region, and gambling regulations. This session follows the relocation of the SADC Parliamentary Forum plenary, freeing up the parliamentary schedule.

The session occurs amidst political pressure, with calls for the Assembly's dissolution from youth consultations held in late July at CCI Ivato and reaffirmed by Assedu-Mada. Additionally, Gen Z activists are demanding the departure or removal of Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko from the institution's presidency.

A new parliamentary force, "Vovonan'ny fihavaozan'i Madagasikara" (VFM), comprising independents and Irmar dissidents, has formed to participate in political consultations related to the Refondation. Their concern is that a future political dialogue might replace the current Parliament with a transitional structure, a worry discussed in an August eleventh meeting between VFM members and Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko.

Why it mattersThe political uncertainty surrounding the National Assembly's future directly affects citizens by potentially altering their representation and the legislative process.

Madagascar Film Festival crowns new cinematic talent

The second annual Madagascar Film Festival – ISSM Awards concluded in Havoria Anosy, recognizing filmmakers and creators across five competitive categories.

The Madagascar Film Festival – ISSM Awards recently celebrated Malagasy cinematic talent, culminating in an awards ceremony in Havoria Anosy. The festival, which included several days of screenings, discussions, and industry networking, honored distinguished works and creators with trophies and recognition.

Awards were presented in five categories: animation, short fiction, feature fiction, short documentary, and feature documentary. The jury evaluated entries based on creativity, cultural dimension, image quality, and mastery of cinematic techniques, aiming to highlight works that demonstrate true creative effort beyond simple image or information production.

Notable winners included Louisette Ratsivahiny for "Fara" in animation, Herizo Bashy for "The Encounter" in short fiction, and Franco Clerc for "Mandrakizay Doria" in feature fiction. Documentaries saw Aina Rasamoelina win for "Harmony" in short form and Gilde Zafitsihadinoina for "Viavy" in the feature category. Éclat Studio Visuel also received the top prize for content creation, according to L'Express de Madagascar.

Why it mattersThis event directly impacts Malagasy filmmakers and content creators by providing recognition and professional development opportunities within the industry.

Ministry supports NGO Fami with food and disability equipment

The Ministry of Population and Solidarity provided food aid, daily necessities, and equipment for people with disabilities to the NGO Fami.

The Ministry of Population and Solidarity recently provided support to the NGO Fami, which offers free treatment and surgery to children with lower limb disabilities at the CRMM Antsirabe. Minister Rakotoniaina Voahanginirina affirmed the collaboration to support the center's projects and improve care for these children.

Since nineteen ninety-six, approximately four hundred children have received treatment through Fami, with many achieving full recovery. This initiative aligns with the vision set by the President of the Renovation for human-centered development and non-discrimination.

The Minister of Population and her team also visited Antananarivo to offer condolences and support to a family following the tragic death of two sisters. The public awaits official explanations and investigation results from the authorities regarding this incident.

Why it mattersChildren with lower limb disabilities receive free treatment and surgery, improving their health and mobility, while a grieving family receives support after a tragic loss.
