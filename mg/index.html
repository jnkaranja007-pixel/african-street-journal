Madagascar economist urges digital law for growth

Jonah Andrianantoanina says updated legislation will help Madagascar leverage AI and improve internet access.

Economist Jonah Andrianantoanina is pushing for a significant law to drive Madagascar's digital transformation, stating that digital technology can become a main engine for economic growth. He believes reforming laws on electronic communications and digital infrastructure will speed up this change. Andrianantoanina described the draft law n°040/2026 on electronic communications and digital infrastructure as having major importance for consumers and the country. He said the goal is to enable Madagascar to benefit from the fourth industrial revolution, including artificial intelligence.

Madagascar already participates in the AI value chain through data annotation, with a large part of training data for French AI companies prepared in the country, according to Andrianantoanina. He noted that these strengths need better internet connectivity to be realized, stating that without accessible internet for all, they will remain useless. The proposed legislative reform will update regulations from 2005, when less than 1% of Malagasy people used the internet, to current realities where over 20% now use it, with smartphones and mobile payments transforming habits.

The new law aims to create equal rules for all actors, introducing five license categories awarded through competitive processes and strengthening the independence of the Regulatory Authority for Communication Technologies (ARTEC). Andrianantoanina stated these measures could improve the investment environment and broaden access to digital services for Malagasy citizens.

Why it mattersMadagascar's economic growth and ability to benefit from AI are directly linked to new digital infrastructure laws and improved internet access.

Madagascar and Russia sign bilateral agreements

Agreements cover agriculture, health, education, defense, and mining, with a potential $10 billion Eurasian fund.

Colonel Michaël Randrianirina announced several concrete outcomes from Madagascar's participation in the Russia-Africa Summit upon his return. Agreements were signed by accompanying ministers covering agriculture, health, higher education, defense, and mining resource management. Randrianirina stated that the visit opens new economic and diplomatic perspectives for Madagascar.

Specific projects include mobile medical laboratories for rural diagnostics and a joint agricultural training center. Russia also plans to increase scholarships for Malagasy students, with a focus on recognizing national diplomas for balanced university exchanges. Discussions also addressed strengthening military ties and combating illegal mining.

The visit included a meeting with Vladimir Putin on October 30, exploring additional projects and a potential $10 billion Eurasian financial mechanism for investments. Randrianirina emphasized pragmatic, equal cooperation, with technical negotiations and financing mobilization crucial for implementation in Madagascar.

Why it mattersMadagascar's government has secured agreements with Russia to advance sectors like agriculture, health, and education, potentially impacting citizens through improved services and opportunities.

Antananarivo traffic chaos as roadworks begin

Road construction on two key arteries will last until January 16, 2027, causing severe congestion.

Roadworks for the "PIAA 2" project have paralyzed the center of Antananarivo, Madagascar, creating gridlock on major routes. The closure of the road between the Ministry of Public Health and Befelatanina forces traffic to reroute, while work in Analakely, including Independence Avenue, further restricts movement. These extensive urban development works are scheduled to continue until January 16, 2027.

The disruption intensified as it coincided with the busy holiday season, a critical period for traders stocking up in the capital. Transport operators, street vendors, and commuters are experiencing significant delays. Narindra Rakotobe reported that the construction, occurring day and night, has led to extreme traffic tensions.

The city's inhabitants face a difficult period balancing urban improvements with economic disruption. The ongoing roadworks are expected to continue impacting daily life and commerce until early next year.

Why it mattersResidents and commuters in Antananarivo are trapped in severe traffic jams due to ongoing road construction projects.
