Electoral Commission Submits Synthesis Report to FFKM

The CENI handed over its preparatory workshop findings to the FFKM to aid in structuring national dialogue for upcoming elections.

The National Independent Electoral Commission (CENI) has officially submitted its synthesis report from preparatory workshops to the FFKM, a council of churches. CENI President Thierry Rakotonarivo presented the document to FFKM President Mgr Samoela Jaona Ranarivelo, signaling a commitment to support the FFKM's mediation efforts for national consultations. These workshops, held in late May, gathered civil society and political parties to lay groundwork for constructive dialogue.

The report details eleven axes examined during the pre-consultation, including electoral lists, calendars, candidacies, campaigns, voting procedures, results tabulation, appeals, observation, inclusivity, electoral laws, and CENI's general organization. Recommendations address issues like inscription difficulties, duplicate entries, and deceased voters on electoral lists, proposing digitalization and biometrics. The electoral calendar should be clear and predictable, considering national realities such as rainy seasons and agricultural activities.

The CENI expressed readiness to collaborate with the FFKM throughout the national consultation process, emphasizing that the report will serve as a foundation for future discussions and preparatory exchanges. The document aims to identify legal, administrative, technical, and operational challenges while gathering improvement proposals. The FFKM views the report as a valuable tool for future national consultations on reforming the electoral legal framework.

Why it mattersThe CENI's report submission to the FFKM directly impacts the preparation of national consultations for electoral reform, affecting how future elections will be organized.

Madagascar State Fails to Spend Foreign Funds Amidst Budget Paradox

Nearly two billion dollars in external financing remains unspent, while domestic investment execution hovers at two percent, highlighting a state capacity crisis.

Madagascar's government is failing to disburse significant funds, despite having approved budgets and secured external financing. In the first quarter of 2026, domestic investment execution was only two point three percent, with ministries like Health showing a drastic drop in engagement from over forty percent to two point nine percent. This near paralysis extends to education and interior ministries, contributing to a sense of national regression.

The issue is not a lack of funds, as tax revenues are meeting projections and salaries are being paid. The core problem lies in the state's inability to spend, a situation exacerbated by the previous government's six-year halt on budget execution reviews. The current administration has resumed these reviews, revealing that only a quarter of the two thousand twenty-six billion ariary investment budget has been committed halfway through the year.

Furthermore, nearly two billion dollars in financing from international donors, equivalent to over a year's tax revenue, remains unused. Less than a third of World Bank funds allocated for projects have been spent. This paradox of seeking international funding while being unable to absorb it points to a critical constraint in the state's execution capacity, rather than a shortage of financial resources.

Why it mattersThe Malagasy state's failure to spend allocated funds directly impacts the implementation of public projects and services, affecting citizens who rely on these initiatives.

Magistrates' Union Challenges Justice Minister Over Suspensions

The Syndicat des magistrats de Madagascar disputes the procedure used to suspend three anti-corruption court judges, escalating a dispute with the Ministry of Justice.

The Syndicat des magistrats de Madagascar (SMM) has openly contested the Ministry of Justice's handling of the suspension of three judges from the Anti-Corruption Pole (PAC) in Antananarivo. SMM President Mbintanarivo Andriantsihorisoa stated that the affected magistrates should have been informed of the specific grievances against them and allowed to present their defense before any decision was made. The union is demanding the suspension of the measure, calling it "harassment" and requesting that directives influencing judicial decisions be formalized in writing to protect judicial independence.

The Ministry of Justice, however, justified the sanctions by citing alleged failures in case management and professional responsibilities, following an investigation by the Anti-Corruption Pole Monitoring and Evaluation Committee. The ministry affirmed its commitment to strictly applying regulations without differential treatment. This stance directly contrasts with the SMM's position, which criticizes the procedure and defends the suspended judges, framing the situation as a broader institutional confrontation between the union and the Ministry.

This renewed conflict between the SMM, led by Mbintanarivo Andriantsihorisoa, and the Ministry of Justice, headed by Minister Fanirisoa Ernaivo, signals a significant institutional standoff. The dispute over the three PAC judges' fate highlights underlying tensions regarding judicial procedures, accountability, and the potential for hierarchical pressure on magistrates handling sensitive cases.

Why it mattersThe suspension of magistrates and the ensuing dispute between their union and the Ministry of Justice could impact the perceived independence and stability of the judiciary, potentially affecting legal processes and public trust.

ZRK Event Hosts University and Professional Training Fair

The third edition of the fair, featuring over thirty university programs and professional training options, opened today and runs through Thursday.

ZRK Event is organizing the University and Professional Training Fair, which began today and will continue until Thursday at the Ankoay Ankorondrano gymnasium. This event aims to guide students after their baccalaureate exams, offering insights into more than thirty university programs and mentions. The fair also includes conferences and workshops led by experts to discuss in-demand job market skills, alongside demonstrations and testimonials from professionals.

The organizer, Zina Rakotoseheno of ZRK Event, highlighted the unique offerings of this third edition, stating that while visitors might find familiar elements, the fair presents distinct opportunities not found elsewhere. The event is a collaborative effort involving institutions such as the Higher Polytechnic Institute of Madagascar (ISPM), Advancea Madagascar University Polytechnic Institute, the Higher Institute of Social Work (ISTS), NTTC Madagascar, and Web Academy, among others.

The fair provides a platform for students to explore educational pathways and understand career prospects. The inclusion of expert-led sessions and professional insights is designed to bridge the gap between academic learning and the practical demands of the contemporary job market. This initiative seeks to equip young people with the knowledge needed to make informed decisions about their future studies and careers.

Why it mattersStudents graduating from high school are affected by this event as it provides them with information on higher education and professional training options to guide their future career choices.

Madagascar Leaders Convene for 2027 Election Talks

Political parties, civil society, and national representatives will meet in Iavoloha from September 1 to 8 to discuss electoral reforms and the 2027 election timeline.

Madagascar's preparation for the 2027 elections enters a new phase with a week-long consultation in Iavoloha, starting September 1. The presidency of the Republic of Refoundation is gathering political parties, groups, civil society organizations focused on electoral issues, and representatives of vital forces. Only legally recognized entities can participate in these discussions, which extend a process initiated by Colonel Michaël Randrianirina in late July.

The central aim is to forge a shared position for organizing future ballots without prolonging the current political period. President confirmed the objective of 2027 elections on August 18, stressing the need for swift progress. The National Independent Electoral Commission (Ceni) is key, with electoral list revisions intended to enable it to organize a vote by July 2027, though the official timeline is tight. Authorities aim to prevent this constraint from delaying overall preparations.

Discussions will focus on electoral governance, ballot guarantees, and their sequencing. The possibility of a presidential election preceding a constitutional vote may be debated, alongside eligibility criteria for presidential candidates. The ambition is for transparent, credible, and peaceful elections with broad acceptance, addressing sensitive political questions to shape the rules of the next political cycle.

Why it mattersPolitical parties, civil society, and national representatives are affected by discussions on electoral governance and the sequencing of future ballots.
