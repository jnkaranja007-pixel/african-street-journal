Madagascar university officials detained over fake diplomas

Three senior staff at Itasy University in Soavinandriana face pre-trial detention for alleged diploma fraud.

Three senior officials at Itasy University in Soavinandriana, Madagascar, have been placed in pre-trial detention following an investigation into fake diplomas. Two other individuals are under judicial supervision. The case, initiated by the Independent Anti-Corruption Bureau (Bianco), centers on the alleged use of falsified degrees to secure positions within the university.

The implicated officials include the former university president, the director of the Higher School of Engineering, and an administrative and financial manager. Investigations revealed a system of issuing and using fake Bachelor's and Master's 2 degrees from Itasy University, reportedly to justify administrative appointments. The charges include fabrication and use of false documents, abuse of function, and illicit gains.

The Public Prosecutor's Office stated that investigations are ongoing, with potential for further examination of irregularities and responsibilities. The case involves alleged offenses such as forgery, abuse of office, and complicity in obtaining undue advantages.

Politics · 2026-11-01

Madagascar president attends Russia-Africa summit

The president of Madagascar's Republic Refoundation Party met Vladimir Putin at the second Russia-Africa summit in Moscow.

The president of the Republic Refoundation of Madagascar (PRRM) attended the third Russia-Africa summit in Moscow on October 29, 2026, meeting with Vladimir Putin. The summit saw 48 African delegations, 22 led by heads of state, a rise from 17 in 2023.

Analysts debated whether Africa is becoming a 'Russafrique' after 'Françafrique,' questioning if the continent is at the table or on the menu. Russia's trade with Africa grew from $18 billion in 2022 to over $27 billion in 2025, falling short of its goal to double exchanges to $40 billion. Russian arms imports to Africa decreased from 24% in 2019-2023 to 15% in 2021-2025.

Madagascar's military cooperation with Russia, initiated in 2018 and updated in 2022, reportedly scaled up after an unspecified rupture in October 2025. The summit's final declaration and an action plan for 2027-2029 were adopted, alongside a separate, non-binding action plan between the African Union Commission and Russia.

Why it mattersThe president of Madagascar's party attended a summit with Russian leadership, and military cooperation between Madagascar and Russia has reportedly increased.

Culture · 2026-11-01

Madagascar and Italy boost film heritage ties

Minister Gascar Fenosoa visited Bologna to discuss preserving and digitizing Malagasy cinema archives.

Madagascar is strengthening its cultural cooperation with Italy to safeguard, restore, and digitize its film and audiovisual heritage. Minister of Communication and Culture Gascar Fenosoa traveled to Bologna, Italy, to meet with the Fondazione Cineteca di Bologna and its laboratory, L’Immagine Ritrovata. The discussions focused on conserving archives, digitizing films, restoring old movies, upgrading equipment, and training Malagasy professionals.

The initiative aims to improve the preservation of audiovisual materials held at the Auditorium Andohibe, making them more accessible to the public. Minister Fenosoa stated that understanding history and protecting its documentation are essential for consolidating Malagasy identity and preparing the country's future. This vision links heritage preservation with education, creation, and cinema development.

The collaboration builds on existing economic and technical cooperation between Madagascar and Italy, established by an agreement on July 1, 1964. This new focus on audiovisual heritage seeks to deepen these bilateral relations. Officials from the Cineteca di Bologna, including Director Gian Luca Farinelli and Deputy Director Davide Pietrantoni, participated in the meetings.

Why it mattersMalagasy citizens will benefit from better preservation and access to their country's historical films and audiovisual records.

Health · 2026-11-01

US donates technology to Madagascar health ministry

American officials provided digital tools to support frontline health workers in data collection and transmission.

American officials have donated modern technology to Madagascar's Ministry of Health to aid frontline health workers. The equipment will support the collection, input, and transmission of health data. This initiative aims to streamline and accelerate data processes while improving accuracy and reducing errors.

The donation is intended to enhance the efficiency of health information management across the country. Health workers will use the new technology to manage patient records and health statistics more effectively. This move is expected to improve the overall quality of health services by ensuring more reliable data.

The Ministry of Health received the equipment on October 31, 2026. The ministry anticipates better tracking of public health trends and improved response to health challenges.

Why it mattersMadagascar's health workers will use new technology to improve the accuracy and speed of collecting and transmitting health data.
