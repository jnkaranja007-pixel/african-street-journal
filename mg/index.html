Madagascar Shifts Drought Response to Anticipation Strategy

The nation presented its Drought Investment Plan at COP17 in Mongolia, aiming to redirect funding towards sustainable resilience efforts for vulnerable territories and populations.

Madagascar is transitioning from reactive drought crisis management to a proactive anticipation policy. At the COP17 of the UN Convention to Combat Desertification in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, the country unveiled its Drought Investment Plan. Developed with the Global Mechanism, this plan seeks to better channel financing into actions that can build lasting resilience for at-risk regions and communities.

The plan focuses on three main areas: ecosystem and forest restoration, including degraded watershed rehabilitation and mangrove preservation; agriculture and water, through modernizing irrigation, promoting agroecological practices, and using short-cycle, climate-resilient seeds; and empowering farmers and communities. The goal is to reduce reliance on repeated humanitarian aid by building capacity before new droughts strike.

Minister of Environment and Sustainable Development, Luk Aristide Nolave Andriatsihala, emphasized investing in people, particularly women and youth, aligning with international discussions on preventive financing. The COP17, themed "Restoring land, restoring hope," brings together 197 Parties, scientists, economic actors, civil society, and local communities to discuss drought resilience and explore mechanisms for mobilizing capital for resilience, water infrastructure, and land restoration.

Why it mattersFarmers and rural communities are directly affected by the shift to proactive drought measures, aiming to reduce their dependence on repeated humanitarian aid.

Madagascar Russia Cooperation Deepens Amidst Concerns

The island nation is accelerating its ties with Moscow, mirroring strategies seen in the Central African Republic, raising concerns about resource control and dependency.

Madagascar is intensifying its relationship with Russia, engaging in military cooperation, exploring mining interests, and seeking electoral assistance. This strategy, according to observers, echoes Russia's approach in the Central African Republic and raises concerns about increasing dependence on the Kremlin and potential capture of strategic resources during a period meant to restore constitutional order.

The trend follows a pattern observed in other African nations where new leadership often revisits traditional alliances. Madagascar's Colonel Michaël Randrianirina has reportedly visited Moscow, where President Vladimir Putin outlined sectors of Russian interest including agriculture, geology, mining, health, and training. Russian Africa Corps instructors have arrived at the Ivato parachute battalion camp, ostensibly for military training.

Within Madagascar's political institutions, the President of the National Assembly, Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko, is noted as a key figure promoting closer ties with Moscow. He has engaged in multiple discussions with Russian representatives, advocating for expanded parliamentary, economic, and strategic cooperation. Analysts suggest this alignment between state authorities and Russian interests facilitates the Kremlin's growing influence in mining, energy, and infrastructure sectors on the island.

Why it mattersThis developing relationship affects Madagascar by potentially increasing its reliance on Russia and allowing for the capture of strategic national resources.

Madagascar Football Team Faces U23 African Cup Qualification Amidst Poor Preparation

With less than a month before their first match against Comoros, the Malagasy U23 football team lacks any organized competition or player selection process.

Madagascar's U23 national football team, the Barea, is set to begin its qualification campaign for the Africa Cup of Nations (CAN) against the Comoros on September 28. This continental tournament serves as a pathway to the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. However, the team's preparation is in question, with no national competitions or player selections having taken place in the months leading up to the crucial first-round matches.

The Malagasy Football Federation appears to have made no concrete preparations for this international fixture. The draw, held in Cairo, Egypt, places Madagascar against the Comoros, with the first leg hosted by the Comoros and the return leg scheduled for October 6, likely to be played abroad by Madagascar. Should Madagascar advance, they would face South Africa in the second round.

The federation's lack of activity raises concerns about how a competitive U23 squad will be assembled and trained in just a few weeks. This situation mirrors a previous attempt in 2023 for CAN and 2024 Olympic qualifiers, where a collaboration with external associations failed to achieve qualification and ended due to financial issues. The federation's executive committee is scheduled to discuss Madagascar's participation this Thursday.

Why it mattersMadagascar's U23 football team faces an uncertain path to Olympic qualification due to a lack of preparation and player selection, potentially impacting national sporting representation.

IMM Provides Free Pediatric Ultrasounds for 25 Children

The Institut Médical de Madagascar is offering free pediatric ultrasounds to 25 children under 12 years old who require urgent scans for health monitoring or life-saving.

The Institut Médical de Madagascar (IMM) is conducting a health initiative, "Sunday for Life, Health for the Community 100%," to provide free pediatric ultrasounds. This service is available to 25 children, from newborns up to 12 years old, who need urgent scans for health monitoring or life-saving care.

This initiative is part of IMM's commitment to its social responsibility and corporate social responsibility (CSR) policies. Recognizing that many families struggle to afford private medical imaging, IMM is reinforcing its support for vulnerable households. This first edition specifically focuses on the health and care of children, with pediatricians and sonographers offering their services pro bono.

To access this service, individuals can register by calling the green number 617 or by presenting a referral letter from a doctor. IMM has established a dedicated system for participants, with clear signage from the main entrance to the CSR room, ensuring a separate and organized process away from the emergency department.

Why it mattersChildren under 12 years old in Madagascar can receive free pediatric ultrasounds for health monitoring and life-saving interventions.

National Consultations Chronogramme Puts 2027 Elections Under Pressure

The timeline for national consultations, meant to inform a new constitution, may delay critical 2027 elections, including the presidential vote.

Madagascar's national consultation process, intended to lay the groundwork for a new constitution, faces a critical timeline challenge. The resolutions from these consultations are not expected until after May 2027, the same period slated for a constitutional referendum or election. This compressed schedule leaves little time for drafting a new constitution and organizing a vote, potentially impacting the entire 2027 electoral calendar.

The process, officially launched after several postponements, involves training facilitators and conducting consultations at various administrative levels from September to November. The national consultation phase itself is scheduled between March and May 2027. A committee of experts will then need to transform the consultation outcomes into a constitutional text, followed by procedures for adoption and the organization of elections, including the presidential vote due by the end of 2027.

Authorities maintain the schedule is feasible, with plans for parallel workstreams on electoral governance and preparations not dependent on consultation outcomes. The Prime Minister has reaffirmed government support, with a technical committee to manage the process. The core challenge remains synchronizing the conclusion of consultations, the establishment of a new constitution, and the organization of both the constitutional vote and the presidential election before the December 2027 deadline.

Why it mattersThe timeline for national consultations and constitutional reform could delay the 2027 presidential election, affecting the country's political transition.
