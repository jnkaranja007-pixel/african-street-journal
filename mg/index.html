Electoral Project Closes with Positive Assessment

The RPEDEM initiative, funded by over $13 million, officially concluded its support for Madagascar's electoral processes.

The six-year project Reinforcing Electoral and Democratic Processes in Madagascar (RPEDEM) has officially ended, with its final steering committee meeting held in Antananarivo. The initiative, launched in March 2023, provided assistance for the 2023 presidential, 2024 legislative, and 2024 municipal elections. The project aimed to rebuild a strong social contract where every voice matters and the electoral system reflects the sovereign will of the Malagasy people, contributing to stable, transparent, and inclusive governance.

Key achievements highlighted include the introduction of braille ballots, enabling visually impaired voters to cast their ballots independently for the first time. The project also supported sub-projects focused on increasing the participation of women and youth, enhancing the national electoral commission's biometric data management capabilities, and preventing tensions through youth civic and political engagement. Digital platforms like iParticipate, iReport, and iVerify were established to further citizen involvement.

With a total budget exceeding $13 million, the RPEDEM project has concluded its operations. A successor project, APERM (Support for the Electoral Process for the Refoundation of Madagascar), is currently being prepared. The final review presented both technical and financial outcomes of the RPEDEM initiative, with participation from the CENI President, UN Resident Coordinator, UNDP Resident Representative, and technical and financial partners.

Why it mattersThe conclusion of the RPEDEM project affects citizens by ensuring continued support for stable and inclusive electoral processes, with new tools for participation and accessibility.

Scissors murder suspect granted conditional release, family protests

A woman accused of fatally stabbing a 26-year-old man with scissors has been granted provisional liberty, sparking outrage from the victim's family.

The case of a 26-year-old man murdered with scissors in a bar in Ambohimahitsy Ambohimangakely on August 23 has reached the Anosy court. Following proceedings, one of the women implicated in the incident was placed in pre-trial detention at the Antanimora central prison, while the other received provisional liberty. The victim had gone to the bar with two friends, and an argument escalated into a fatal altercation.

According to investigation details, during the fight, one woman allegedly used scissors to stab the victim in the temple and chest. He was rushed to the hospital but died from his injuries. Both women fled the scene and remained at large until apprehended by investigators. The victim's family disputes the prosecutor's decision to grant provisional liberty to the second suspect.

A source close to the victim's family stated that the woman granted provisional liberty is as implicated as the other, having participated in the assault and assisted in hiding after the event. The family is urging authorities to conduct a thorough investigation and take appropriate action, especially as they claim the woman on provisional liberty is making intimidating and threatening remarks towards them.

Why it mattersThe victim's family is directly affected by the court's decision to grant provisional liberty to a suspect in their relative's murder, leading to fear and intimidation.

Prime Minister Appoints National Consultation Technical Committee

The newly formed committee will coordinate the nationwide consultation process aimed at refounding the Republic.

Prime Minister Mamitiana Rajaonarison signed an order on September 3, 2026, in Antananarivo, designating members to the Technical Committee for National Consultation. This action advances the broad consultation process initiated for the Refoundation of the Republic. The committee, established by decree on June 17, 2026, will support the Ecumenical Council of Christian Churches of Madagascar (FFKM), which is leading the consultations across the country.

Based at the Prime Minister's office, the committee is tasked with monitoring the timeline, providing technical coordination, and relaying debate conclusions to the government for implementation. Its mandate concludes with the investiture of the elected President following the refoundation process. This appointment occurs amidst a political transition that began in October 2025, with the consultation process gaining operational momentum in late August 2026.

The FFKM has outlined a schedule for progressive consultations, starting at the local level in mid-September and moving up through communes, districts, and regions, with a national synthesis expected by March 2027. Resolutions will be presented after May 2027, preceding elections scheduled between May and July 2027, followed by a presidential election later that year. This timeline aligns with the twenty-four-month plan for establishing a new institutional framework, as submitted to the Southern African Development Community (SADC).

Why it mattersThe appointment of the Technical Committee for National Consultation affects citizens by moving forward a process that will lead to the election of a new President and the establishment of a new institutional framework.

Colonel Randrianirina Consults Political Groups on Election Preparations

President of the Refoundation of the Republic is meeting with various political parties and associations to gather input for upcoming elections.

Political consultations aimed at preparing for upcoming elections are ongoing at the Iavoloha State Palace. Colonel Michaël Randrianirina, president of the Refoundation of the Republic, has been meeting with numerous political formations and associations over several days. These discussions, which began on Tuesday, September 1, involve listening to participants' analyses of the electoral situation and their proposals for future polls.

The initial meetings included the Firaisankina political group, a women's association, and KFIM. On Wednesday, Colonel Randrianirina met with IRMAR, the Gasikara movement, and SIAP. The focus of these exchanges is to gather diverse perspectives and suggestions for improving the electoral process and its environment, with the stated goal of fostering transparency and acceptance of results.

The presidency aims to involve a wide range of political viewpoints and societal components in these discussions to ensure inclusivity. International observers from the United Nations, African Union, and SADC are attending these consultations. Further meetings with other political groups and civil society representatives are scheduled, intended to identify points of convergence and divergence regarding election preparations.

Why it mattersPolitical parties and associations are affected by these consultations as they are providing input and making proposals on the organization and process of future elections.

Counterfeiter Captured After Mother Arrested

A young man involved in printing fake currency was apprehended in Vohiposa after evading authorities for two days.

Authorities apprehended a young man in Vohiposa on Wednesday night, two days after he evaded capture during his mother's arrest in Ankarinera Andrefana. The suspect is implicated in a counterfeit money operation in the Manandroy district and is also linked to burglaries, fraud, and kidnapping. His mother was placed under detention after appearing in court. Among 15 counterfeit 20,000 ariary notes found at their home, only two were genuine.

The Special Security Detachment (DSS) Ambalakindresy pursued the suspect after he escaped during his mother's arrest. The family's patriarch was excluded from the counterfeiting investigation due to a chronic illness. In a separate but related case, two women arrested on Sunday in Ambohimanjaka Ambositra for possessing counterfeit money were also detained. These women admitted to holding the fake bills for a year before attempting to use them.

The Ilaka Afovoany brigade is actively searching for another individual named by the two women. This second case is not directly connected to the Ambohimahasoa incident. During the search of the women's residences, authorities found 161 counterfeit 20,000 ariary notes, totaling 3,200,000 ariary. The investigation into the counterfeit currency operations is ongoing.

Why it mattersIndividuals involved in the circulation of counterfeit currency are being arrested and detained, impacting the local economy and potentially leading to further investigations.
