Ladies Makis win Madagascar's first Olympic medal

The Madagascar women's rugby sevens team secured the bronze medal at the JOJ Dakar 2026 by defeating the Czech Republic 26-10.

The Ladies Makis 7s team has won Madagascar's first-ever Olympic medal, securing bronze at the JOJ Dakar 2026. The Malagasy rugbywomen defeated the Czech Republic 26-10 in Dakar on Tuesday evening. This historic achievement marks the first time Madagascar has won a medal since its first Olympic participation in 1964 and the first time a Malagasy collective team has qualified for an Olympic competition.

The team's journey included three group stage victories against the United States, Spain, and the Czech Republic, followed by a 5-15 semi-final loss to Kenya. Coach Dina thanked God and supporters, stating the team exceeded expectations and encouraging the players to continue their studies. Player Safy expressed happiness despite the semi-final loss, highlighting the team's performance against taller opponents.

The Ladies Makis had previously trained in Tunisia for a week. Other Malagasy athletes competed in Dakar: judoka Bryan Minoharintsoa Tokiniaina finished 7th in the under-100kg category, and swimmer Océane Rakotonanahary placed 6th in her 100m breaststroke heat, with Tendry Rakotobe set to compete in the 50m butterfly.

Russia and US intensify influence efforts in Madagascar

Both Moscow and Washington are increasing diplomatic and security engagement with Malagasy authorities.

Madagascar is experiencing heightened diplomatic activity from both Russia and the United States, with both nations increasing contacts with Malagasy officials in military, security, and economic sectors. Recent visits by representatives from both powers to the northern part of the island highlight this competition for influence in the Indian Ocean region.

Military cooperation is a key focus between Madagascar and Russia, with President Michaël Randrianirina confirming strengthened relations after the Russia-Africa summit on October 31. This cooperation includes training Malagasy soldiers by Russian instructors and exchanges on defense equipment. Russia's interim chargé d'affaires, Alexeï Buriak, also visited Nosy Be to discuss tourism, agriculture, and trade in vanilla and ylang-ylang essential oils, renewing an agreement on maintaining Russian historical sites.

The United States is also deepening its ties with Madagascar, particularly in maritime defense and security. A U.S. Africa Command delegation, led by General John Brennan, visited Antananarivo on November 3 and 4 to discuss maritime surveillance, combating illicit trafficking, piracy, and military training with Malagasy officials, including Colonel Michaël Randrianirina and Minister of Armed Forces Ely Maminirina Razafitombo. Critical minerals and their economic security were also discussed.

Why it mattersMadagascar's government is engaging with both Russia and the United States on military, security, and economic cooperation, impacting the nation's foreign relations and strategic positioning.

Madagascar grandmother sentenced to life for murder

The Anosy court sentenced Raissa's grandmother to life imprisonment with forced labor for her murder.

The Anosy Criminal Court sentenced Raissa's grandmother to life imprisonment with forced labor for the murder of the 8-year-old girl. One of her cousins received a 10-year prison sentence for his role in the crime. The court delivered its verdict yesterday, more than a year after the girl's disappearance.

Raissa's body was discovered on May 11, 2025, in a bag near her home in Antohomadinika IVO, a day after she was reported missing. A message on the family's gate read, "Vengeance is accomplished, but we still have two to kill…". The trial involved the victim's grandmother, her cousin, and another boy.

The court's decision follows investigations into the tragic events of May 2025. The grandmother was found guilty, and her cousin was also convicted. The case has been before the Anosy criminal jurisdiction since the discovery of the child's body.

Politics · 2026-11-04

Madagascar digital minister resigns

Mahefa Andriamampiadana leaves his post, with the minister of higher education taking interim charge.

Madagascar's Minister of Digital Development, Posts, and Telecommunications, Mahefa Andriamampiadana, has officially resigned from his position. The Presidency of the Refoundation announced the departure yesterday, stating that health reasons prevent the minister from continuing his duties. Ndaohialy Manda-Vy Ravonimanantsoa, the minister of Higher Education and Scientific Research, will temporarily oversee the digital development ministry until a successor is appointed.

Andriamampiadana confirmed his resignation on social media, reflecting on his year-long commitment and unfulfilled ambitions for his department. He expressed regret that circumstances prevented him from completing several planned initiatives. While official reasons cite medical grounds, his departure comes as discussions continue on two draft laws concerning the digital sector.

The two draft laws are one on cybersecurity and cybercrime (042/2026) and another on electronic communications (040/2026). Concerns have been raised regarding potential impacts on freedom of expression, content management, and personal data protection for the cybersecurity law. The electronic communications law faces questions about regulatory and financial conditions for operators and their effect on competition.

Why it mattersMinister Mahefa Andriamampiadana resigned citing health, but his departure occurs as two digital sector laws face scrutiny over freedom of expression and competition.

Madagascar photography festival marks CERES anniversary

The SAR'nao Festival in Antananarivo celebrates 20 years of the CERES education program and contemporary art.

The SAR'nao Festival launched its annual Month of Photography yesterday, November 3, at the Alliance Française d’Antananarivo. This year's event highlights photography's role in memory, transmission, and contemporary creation with two exhibitions. The first, "20 ans du programme CERES," runs until November 8 and showcases 20 years of the Centre de Renforcement Éducatif et Scolaire's work with rural youth.

This CERES retrospective features black and white photographs from 2015 by Pierrot Men alongside images from 2025 by Guillaume de Tapol. Their combined work illustrates the evolution of young beneficiaries and the challenges of education access and equal opportunity. A second exhibition, "AFFEKT" by Faly Randrianjatovo, will run from November 10 to 14, exploring intersections of dance, movement, body, and fashion.

Complementary workshops on fashion photography, led by Faly Randrianjatovo, will take place from November 11 to 13. The festival also includes outreach activities for Alliance Française learners and schoolchildren to promote photographic art. The Month of Photography aims to foster photography as a space for reflection and creation in Madagascar.

Why it mattersMadagascar's youth and cultural engagement are directly affected by the Month of Photography festival and its educational outreach.
