Ravalomanana sentenced to ten years for 2025 protest violence

The former Senate president was convicted of complicity in murder and voluntary injury related to the repression of demonstrations in Antananarivo.

Former Senate president General Richard Ravalomanana has been sentenced to ten years of forced labor in connection with violence during September and October 2025 protests in Antananarivo. His lawyer announced an appeal to the Court of Cassation, contesting the evidence against his client. While initially facing three accusations, including a dismissed threat charge, the case focused on Ravalomanana's alleged role in the capital's unrest.

Prosecutors accused Ravalomanana of complicity in murder and voluntary injury, alleging he instructed security forces to use violence and facilitated abuses against protesters. Specific incidents mentioned included a person being dragged and others whose hair was allegedly burned by gendarmes. Ravalomanana denied any involvement, a position supported by former National Police Director General Dany Marius Rakotozanany, who testified Ravalomanana was not in the operational command chain.

The defense argued there was no direct proof, written or verbal, linking Ravalomanana to the violence, asserting no material act or formal order attributable to him constituted the alleged offenses. The court did not accept this argument, finding the former Senate president guilty. He is to be held at the Imerintsiatosika high-security prison, and the defense intends to challenge the verdict through the appeal process.

Why it mattersThe conviction of a former Senate president for violence during protests affects political stability and the rule of law in Madagascar.

Expatriates Represent Madagascar at African Table Tennis Championship

A team composed entirely of Malagasy players living abroad will compete in the ITTF African Championships in Morocco.

Madagascar's national table tennis team will compete in the ITTF African Championships in Tanger, Morocco, from October 18 to 25. The squad is entirely made up of expatriate players who have been trained and honed in European facilities. This selection highlights the current reality of high-level Malagasy table tennis, where top talents develop their skills internationally before returning to represent their country.

The men's team includes Fabio Rakotoarimanana, Antoine Razafinarivo, and Setra Rakotoarisoa. The women's team features Karen Raharimanana and Edena Prévot, joined by Vony Ange Randriantsoa, who received a wildcard. These athletes will participate in seven events: men's and women's team, singles, doubles, and mixed doubles.

The championships offer a pathway to the 2027 World Championships, with qualification spots determined by the ITTF. Success in matches, sets, and advancing in the main draw will be crucial for the team's final standing. The competition format includes team events with a round-robin group stage followed by knockout rounds, and singles matches with a similar group stage before a main draw.

Why it mattersMalagasy expatriate table tennis players are affected by competing in the African Championships, which serve as a qualifier for the World Championships.

Madagascar Takes Third in MX2 at African Nations Motocross

Jérémy Rakotoarimanana and Steven Karnezis secured individual podium spots, contributing to the team's overall success in Namibia.

Madagascar's motocross team achieved a significant third-place finish in the MX2 category at the 2026 Motocross of African Nations held in Namibia. Jérémy Rakotoarimanana, riding a KTM 250, and Steven Karnezis, on a Fantic 250, both earned third place overall in their individual standings, propelling the Malagasy team to a strong collective result. In the MX125 category, Leung Yen finished eighth, while Lucas Szmurlo placed twenty-third.

The MX1 category saw setbacks for Madagascar, with Mathias Plan­tive and Clau­dio Tida both retiring from their races due to technical issues. Despite these mechanical failures, the team's performance is considered a major success, demonstrating their ability to compete at the highest continental level. The delegation returned home yesterday, having proven their competitive edge.

A team spokesperson noted on social media that Madagascar will return stronger, acknowledging the experience gained even with the challenges. The team's performance signifies a positive step for Malagasy motorsport, with riders showing they can contend with the continent's best. This achievement was celebrated upon their return to the airport.

Why it mattersMadagascar's motocross team's third-place finish in MX2 demonstrates the nation's growing capability in international motorsport.

Madagascar State Strengthens Cybercrime Controls

A new bill to combat cybercrime will grant the state significantly enhanced investigative powers, including a dedicated digital police unit.

Madagascar's National Assembly is set to debate and vote on a new bill, n°042/2026, aimed at combating cybercrime this Friday. The proposed legislation is presented as an update to existing laws to address emerging digital threats.

A key feature of the reform is the establishment of a new entity called the Unit for Digital Protection and Investigation (Upin). This specialized digital police force will be tasked with preventing and detecting cyber offenses, conducting technical investigations, and assisting the judiciary.

The Upin will possess broad authority to carry out its mandate. The bill's progression through the legislature signifies a move by the state to bolster its capacity in addressing online criminal activities and enhancing digital security measures.

Why it mattersThe new cybercrime law affects citizens by potentially increasing state surveillance and investigative capabilities in the digital realm.

Madagascar Hosts First Women's IHF Nation Trophy Zone 7

Six Indian Ocean nations will compete in the inaugural senior women's handball tournament from September 29 to October 4.

Madagascar will host the first-ever IHF Nation Trophy for senior women in Zone 7, marking a significant step for the island nation. The event, scheduled from September 29 to October 4, will take place at the Cnaps sports complex in Vontovorona. This tournament is a crucial opportunity for the development of women's handball in the region and will serve as preparation for the upcoming Africa Cup of Nations.

The participating nations include Reunion, Seychelles, Comoros, Mayotte, and the host country, Madagascar. The Malagasy Handball Federation (FMHB) president, Andriamanga Herilovasoa Andrianisainana Ratsimandao, stated that hosting the competition is vital for the growth of women's handball and showcases the talent within the Indian Ocean.

To assemble the strongest possible national team, the ongoing championship in Madagascar, held at the Palais des Sports Mahamasina, is being used as a major selection tournament. This event provides a direct platform for players to demonstrate their skills and vie for a spot on the national squad.

Why it mattersThe tournament brings together six nations for a regional handball competition, impacting sports development and national team selection.
