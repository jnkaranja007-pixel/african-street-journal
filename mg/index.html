Russian Africa Corps troops in Madagascar: official count 154, sources say 400

Journalist Yasmina Perrière questions why Russian soldiers are needed in Madagascar and what Moscow wants in return.

Russia's Africa Corps has installed military personnel in Madagascar, and the official figure is 154 soldiers, although several sources put the number at 400 or more. Independent journalist Yasmina Perrière, writing in the Madagascar Tribune, argues the deployment is part of a strategy combining economic interests, control of resources and political influence.

The official reasons given are the close protection of transition leader Michaël Randrianirina and the training of the armed forces. Perrière says these do not explain the presence, because Madagascar faces no jihadist insurgency or civil war of the kind that has drawn foreign forces into the Sahel. She notes Russia used a similar model in the Central African Republic, Mali and Burkina Faso.

Perrière says the transition leadership came to power after a popular uprising in October 2025 that demanded democracy, and that it has not moved quickly to organise elections. In her view, the Russian protection force risks becoming a tool for keeping the current team in power, and the economic terms of the arrangement remain largely unclear.

Madagascar and Russia advance diploma recognition

A memorandum of cooperation on higher education and scientific research was signed between the two nations.

Madagascar and Russia are progressing on mutual recognition of academic diplomas, a key step for student mobility. This educational cooperation was a focus at the Russia-Africa summit held on October 28 and 29. Alexeï Buriak, Russia's interim chargé d'affaires in Madagascar, stated on November 10 in Antananarivo that nearly 150 agreements were signed at the summit, including memorandums for Madagascar in agriculture, health, and higher education.

The Russian government fully funds 45 scholarships currently being developed with CONABEX for Malagasy students. Beyond government scholarships, direct institutional agreements, such as one between IST d’Antananarivo and Saint Petersburg University, are being explored to facilitate student movement. While a foreign media outlet reported a mutual diploma recognition agreement in late October 2026, the Malagasy Ministry of Higher Education confirms a cooperation memorandum, and Russian accounts mention an intergovernmental agreement in preparation.

This technical dossier is advancing gradually, according to Buriak, who expressed satisfaction with the educational dialogue. A 1973 protocol on equivalence between Madagascar and the USSR exists, though its current applicability is unclear. For Malagasy graduates, mutual recognition of diplomas is crucial for further studies, qualification recognition, and employment without facing uncertain equivalence procedures.

Why it mattersMalagasy students and graduates will benefit from easier recognition of their qualifications for further study or employment in Russia.

Madagascar plans constitutional referendum

Officials propose changes to voter registration, campaign rules, and diaspora participation.

Officials in Antananarivo concluded two days of discussions on proposals for Madagascar's constitutional referendum. Key changes discussed include keeping a 90-day period between voter notification and the referendum date. Voter lists will be publicly displayed in each fokontany 60 days before the vote and accessible online via the CENI website.

The proposals also suggest removing the voting ordinance and potentially assigning poll station members to local assemblies to boost participation. A 30-day campaign period is recommended without spending limits, though civil society organizations and high-ranking officials would be barred from campaigning. The distribution of gifts and state resources for campaigning would be prohibited and penalized.

Malagasy citizens abroad may vote at consulates, requiring specific arrangements for overseas operations. A single ballot paper is also proposed, with provisional results expected within 12 days. Discussions continue on whether to present multiple constitution drafts or a single text for the vote.

Why it mattersMadagascar's citizens will vote in a constitutional referendum to shape the country's foundational laws before the 2027 presidential election.

Madagascar's rugby sevens team wins first Olympic medal at Dakar Youth Games

The Ladies Makis 7's took bronze after beating the Czech Republic 26-10, the country's first Olympic medal since it began competing in 1964.

Madagascar closed its campaign at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games with a bronze medal for the Ladies Makis 7's rugby team, their first event in a collective Olympic discipline. The side won three group matches, lost the semi-final to Kenya, then beat the Czech Republic 26-10 to take third place.

The delegation's report counts six disciplines in total. Sprinter Jauslin Anjara Laura finished fifth in her 200 m semi-final in 27.46, missing the final. Swimmer Tendry Rakotobe set a Madagascar record of 27.35 in the 50 m backstroke but did not reach a final. Judoka Bryan Minoharintsoa Tokiniaina reached the quarter-finals and placed seventh in the under-100 kg category.

Table tennis player Tina Gracia Andriantahina lost two group matches. Beach wrestler Francia Nomenjanahary Randriatiana won one bout and lost two, stopping short of the semi-finals. A weightlifting demonstration event was also held.

Why it mattersThe medal is the first Olympic podium finish in the nation's history, a result the delegation presents as the high point of the Games.

Madagascar's pétanque team targets a double in Thailand

The four-player squad, led by coach Herinirina Edmond Ratsimba, flies out on November 18 for the 52nd World Championships in Khon Kaen.

Madagascar's national pétanque team will compete at the 52nd world championships in triplette and precision shooting, held from November 20 to 25 in Khon Kaen, Thailand. The four players selected after trials in Ankatso from October 10 to 14 are Olivier Rakotomanana, Faralahy Urbain Joseph Ramanantiaray, Tendry Fitia Fanjamanovofifaliana and Lova Satamandimby Rakotoarisoa.

The squad has been in final training in Antsirabe since November 2, and the two-week camp ends on Wednesday. National technical director Juan Éric Rakotondralambo said the aim is a double, and that sessions focused on precision shooting and team cohesion. The delegation returns to the capital on Thursday and flies out on November 18, with a stopover in Addis Ababa.

The team is again led by coach Herinirina Edmond Ratsimba, known as Hery Morondava. Madagascar has won two triplette world titles, in 1999 and 2016, and in 2024 in Dijon its triplette side lost the final 11-13 to Italy.

Why it mattersPlayers and their families in Madagascar face a two-week wait for results from Khon Kaen, with the squad departing November 18.
