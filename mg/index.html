Madagascar seeks Russian investment in energy and agriculture

President Randrianirina wants Russian firms to process raw materials locally, creating jobs and industrial activity.

Madagascar's President, Colonel Michaël Randrianirina, attended the 3rd Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow, outlining economic priorities and seeking stronger ties with Russia. He stated that despite destabilization attempts, Madagascar is committed to developing this partnership. The President highlighted the Madagascar-Russia Chamber of Commerce as a tool to connect investors.

Key sectors for new partnerships include energy, agriculture, health, water supply, digital technology, and scientific research. President Randrianirina aims to attract Russian companies willing to process raw materials directly in Madagascar, boosting local employment, tax revenue, and industrial development. Russia presented its A7 cross-border payment system, which has expanded in Africa.

The effectiveness of this summit will be measured by tangible benefits for the population, including mobilized funding, established businesses, created jobs, and completed infrastructure projects. The A7 payment platform, launched in late 2024, is expanding into Africa with existing offices in Nigeria and Zimbabwe.

Why it mattersMadagascar's economic priorities are energy, agriculture, health, water, digital, and research, with a focus on local processing of raw materials to create jobs.

Madagascar and Turkey seek more economic cooperation

Ankara is interested in increased collaboration in energy, education, and trade, with agriculture and mining also targeted.

Madagascar and Turkey aim to expand their economic partnership to attract more investments and development projects. This goal was stated on Thursday, October 29, in Antananarivo during a reception celebrating the 103rd anniversary of the Turkish Republic. Prime Minister Mamitiana Rajaonarison and several government officials attended the event.

Discussions focused on opportunities within Madagascar's economy, with the Minister of Public Security, Erick Michel Wouli Soumah Idrissa, expressing a desire to deepen bilateral ties. Ambassador Halime Ebru Demircan confirmed Turkey's interest in sectors like energy, education, and trade, highlighting the $80 million Ambohimanambola thermal power plant as a key achievement.

Future collaboration could extend to agriculture, infrastructure, mining, tourism, health, and renewable energy, supporting industrialization and job creation. Several agreements on fishing, electricity, and investment promotion are under discussion, with 181 Malagasy students having received Turkish university scholarships since 1992.

Why it mattersMadagascar's economy could benefit from increased Turkish investment and development projects in key sectors.

Madagascar pledges to centre women in ocean conservation

The island nation joins Belize in a global initiative to integrate female leadership into marine strategies and blue economies.

Madagascar has pledged to integrate women's leadership into national marine conservation strategies and blue economies. This commitment was made at the UN Biodiversity Conference (CoP17) as part of the Women Ocean Guardians Initiative. The global public-private partnership aims to place women at the forefront of ocean conservation and the sustainable use of marine resources.

Andrianirina Harinaivo Fanomezantsoa, Madagascar’s Coordonnateur Général, stated that women in Madagascar are vital team players in marine conservation and coastal community development. They serve in roles such as rangers, saving group leaders, park managers, and technical department leads. Madagascar joins 14 other governments that have made similar pledges.

The Women Ocean Guardians Initiative seeks to address gaps in the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, particularly Target 23 on gender equality, which remains the least advanced global goal. Management effectiveness and inclusive governance are identified as primary challenges for achieving the 30x30 ocean protection goal.

Why it mattersMadagascar's commitment will integrate women into national marine conservation strategies and blue economies, impacting coastal communities and resource management.

Madagascar braces for heavy rains and cooler temperatures

The national weather service issued a red alert for rainfall in specific regions starting October 30, 2026.

Madagascar will experience a significant drop in temperatures across the island starting October 30, 2026, and continuing through Sunday. The southern part of the country is expected to have dry weather, while other areas will see unstable atmospheric conditions.

A red alert for heavy rainfall has been issued for the Matsiatra Ambony, Fitovinany regions, and the Ifanadiana district. The Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research (Mesupres) also stated that documents for all scholarship applications are valid for one year, with no charge for processing.

In preparation for the rainy season, the Agency for Public Investment Projects (Apipa) cleared debris and trash from the Andriantany canal in Ampefiloha to ensure water flow. This cleaning operation is ongoing in other locations.

Why it mattersResidents in Matsiatra Ambony, Fitovinany, and Ifanadiana face potential flooding due to heavy rainfall alerts.
