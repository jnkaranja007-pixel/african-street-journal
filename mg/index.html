Madagascar's National Assembly vice-president denies plot to oust leadership

Fanomezantsoa Andrianjanahary says no move to remove the permanent bureau is under way, and the 2027 finance bill is being examined.

Rumours of a shake-up at the top of Madagascar's National Assembly have been denied. Fanomezantsoa Andrianjanahary, a vice-president of the chamber representing the former Fianarantsoa province, said on Thursday, 12 November, that no initiative to dismiss the permanent bureau was under way. The claims had circulated on social media for several days.

He said lawmakers were continuing normal work, including examination of the 2027 finance bill. He said the Vondron'ny Fihavaozan'i Madagasikara (VFM) group still backs the current speaker, Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko, and attributed low attendance at some budget sittings to deputies touring their constituencies to report on their activities.

Speculation persists because Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko has been more discreet than at the start of the transition, when he was a leading spokesman for the new government. Reports of tension with Colonel Michaël Randrianirina have not been verified, and the report confirms no dispute between the two men.

Why it mattersLawmakers must complete examination of the 2027 finance bill, with the permanent bureau remaining in place for now.

170 teachers complete training in Malagasy language and mathematics

The Ministry of National Education awarded certificates to 170 teachers who finished training in Malagasy language and mathematics.

A ceremony in Farafangana awarded certificates to 170 teachers who completed training in Malagasy language and mathematics. The training was organised by the Ministry of National Education through its basic training department, in collaboration with Family Health International 360 and funded by the Fondation Hempel as part of the Fianarana tsara orina project.

Of the 170 teachers, 131 were from Farafangana and 39 from Vondrozo. The CRFRP Farafangana coordinated the training, which covered Zap Ambatolampy within Cisco Farafangana. The ceremony was attended by the head of the Atsimo Atsinanana region, Randrianasolo Volatiana Pauline, and the acting Director General of INFP.

The training aims to develop teachers' ability to impart knowledge in the core subjects of Malagasy and mathematics, as part of a programme to strengthen the quality of education.

Why it mattersTeachers in Farafangana and Vondrozo have completed training that will change how they teach Malagasy language and mathematics.

Agriculture · 2026-11-13

Madagascar and Russia to build farm school

An agreement signed in Moscow will also see Russian agricultural technology transferred to Madagascar.

Madagascar and Russia are set to establish an agricultural school and transfer Russian farming technology. The initiative aims to boost the country's agricultural sector, which is key to its economic development. This cooperation was formalized in Moscow on October 29, 2026, through an agreement signed by Madagascar's Minister of Agriculture and Food Sovereignty, Gaëtan Ramindo, and his Russian counterpart, Oksana Lut.

The project intends to enhance the technical skills of young Malagasy people in agriculture and livestock farming, introducing them to modern production methods. Beyond training, the partnership focuses on transferring Russian expertise in mechanization, cultivation techniques, and the organization of large farms. Russia's experience in agricultural research, including seed improvement and genetic resources, may also benefit Madagascar, as could its significant production of fertilizers.

Trade in agricultural and livestock products between the two nations is also a focus for this expanding partnership. To ensure these initiatives are coordinated, a joint Malagasy-Russian technical committee will be formed to oversee the implementation and commitments of both parties.

Why it mattersYoung Malagasy people will receive training in modern agricultural techniques and technology transfer from Russia, starting with the establishment of a new school.

Sport · 2026-11-13

Warren Caddy's goal is cancelled out as Madagascar draws 1-1 with Guinea-Bissau

Madagascar remain on two points from three CAN 2027 qualifiers after a late equaliser in Meknès on Thursday.

Madagascar and Guinea-Bissau drew 1-1 on Thursday at the Stade d'Honneur in Meknès, Morocco, in the third round of CAN 2027 qualifiers. Warren Caddy headed the Barea in front in the 59th minute, but a hesitant sortie by the Malagasy goalkeeper in the 83rd minute let Renato Nhaga equalise for Guinea-Bissau.

Coach Corentin Martins said the team deserved more points, after defeats and draws in its earlier matches against Nigeria and Tanzania. Captain Rayan Raveloson said the players had applied their plan and kept creating chances after the goal conceded.

Madagascar have two points from three matches, while Guinea-Bissau lead Group L with seven. The Barea must win the return match in Bissau on 17 November to keep qualification alive.

Why it mattersMadagascar must now win in Bissau on 17 November to keep its qualifying hopes alive.
