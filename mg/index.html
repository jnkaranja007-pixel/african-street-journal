Madagascar's Catholic bishops call for inclusive national consultation

In a statement after their November session, the bishops warned that decisions imposed in advance would not reflect the voice of the people.

The Conference of Catholic Bishops of Madagascar has urged leaders to make the upcoming national consultation genuinely inclusive, reaching beyond Antananarivo and the big cities to grassroots communities. The bishops issued their statement at the end of their ordinary November session, held in Ambatoroka, Antananarivo.

The bishops said the process must reflect the voice of the people, not opinions already formed and imposed in advance. They also criticised leaders for selfish disregard or systematic, indiscriminate opposition, and denounced false information and personal attacks on social media, particularly Facebook. They listed insecurity, drug trafficking, serious illnesses and economic hardship among the problems facing the population, including low prices paid to farmers while daily living costs keep rising.

The bishops told citizens they must not hand their lives entirely over to leaders, and called for just and effective laws to govern the exploitation of land, water and subsoil resources.

Why it mattersRural households and farmers, who receive low prices for their produce while living costs rise, are among the groups the bishops say must be heard in the national consultation.

Frédéric Rabekoto wins Madagascar rally title in Moramanga

Rabekoto takes the 46th Grand Rallye de Madagascar by Orange, his first national rally championship, after a close duel with Olivier Ramiandrisoa.

Frédéric Rabekoto won the 46th Grand Rallye de Madagascar by Orange in Moramanga and was crowned Madagascar's rally champion for the first time. The result was decided only in the final kilometres of the event.

Olivier Ramiandrisoa, his main rival, set the fastest times on the final day's special stages and cut the gap sharply, despite running off the road near the end of the Power Stage. He still finished the rally. Mathieu Andrianjafy completed the podium.

Crew member Rabekoto's win ends a season that the organisers' coverage describes as one of the most open in recent years.

Why it mattersRabekoto now holds the national rally title, and Ramiandrisoa and Andrianjafy, who were in contention, remain in the frame for next season.

Ismaëlle Sanda goes to Tashkent to represent Madagascar in Our Generation 2026

The young singer will perform a song written for the contest in the international final on 20 November.

Ismaëlle Sanda, chosen through a national selection, left Madagascar for Tashkent in Uzbekistan to represent the Indian Ocean island in the final of the Our Generation 2026 international music competition, scheduled for 20 November. The contest is open to young talents aged 9 to 16 from several countries.

Sanda will sing "Nateraka ho Mpandresy" ("Born to Win"), a song created specially for the competition that carries a message of hope, perseverance and self-belief. She says she feels fully prepared and approaches the contest with confidence and determination. She is accompanied by Coach Kenny and by Melky, who is also part of the Malagasy delegation as a jury member.

The final brings together international contestants for an event that celebrates youth creativity and artistic expression. The delegation aims to show Malagasy young talent on the world stage.

Why it mattersMadagascar's delegation, including Sanda, Coach Kenny and Melky, will perform on the international stage in Tashkent on 20 November.
