Madagascar seizes Enelec power plant in Toamasina

Government takes control of thermal plant for two years to fix electricity shortages and underproduction.

The Malagasy state has officially requisitioned the Enelec thermal power plant in Toamasina for up to 24 months. The decree, issued on November 3, places the plant's personnel, facilities, and services under state control, with production now managed by Jirama. A government delegation, including the Minister of Energy and Hydrocarbons, formally notified Enelec officials in Toamasina.

This action follows persistent electricity supply disruptions in Toamasina, where the Enelec plant's production has reportedly fallen to 14 megawatts from its contracted 32 megawatts. The government's decision came after previous warnings to Enelec regarding electrical issues in Toamasina and other cities. Enelec had cited technical failures as the cause for its reduced output.

The state's seizure of the plant aims to ensure the continuity of public service and improve electricity supply in the economic capital. The government intends to bring more production capacity online to reduce recurring power outages that affect daily life and economic activities in the port city.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in Toamasina will experience improved electricity supply and reduced power outages.

Madagascar wins 14 gold medals at handisport championship

The island nation dominated the Indian Ocean Clubs Handisport Championship 2026 held in Antananarivo.

Madagascar athletes secured 14 gold, 7 silver, and 6 bronze medals at the Indian Ocean Clubs Handisport Championship (CCCHOI) 2026 in Antananarivo. The six-day event saw Malagasy competitors excel in para-athletics, wheelchair tennis, and para-pétanque. The national team's performance confirmed the growing strength of Malagasy handisport in the region.

The home crowd's support fueled the Malagasy team, which finished first in the overall standings. Notable performances came from Faravavy Aurélie, Randriamanantsoa, Randrianarindra Nancy Carnel, Rakotomanana Tolojanahary Fridon, and Raholijaona Njaka in para-athletics. Mariamo Ali and Rakotonirina Jean Olivier won wheelchair tennis titles, while Malagasy duos triumphed in para-pétanque.

The CCCHOI 2026 also featured a victory for Club Atsimo-Andrefana in wheelchair basketball, defeating Mauritius in the final. Club Haute Matsiatra took third place. Despite Madagascar's overall success, Mauritian athlete Gregori was the top scorer with 48 points. La Réunion placed second in the medal standings, followed by the Comoros and Mauritius.

Why it mattersMalagasy athletes achieved significant success and demonstrated regional dominance in handisport.

Business · 2026-11-07

Air Madagascar's debt burden resurfaces

A former director warns that reviving the old airline risks repeating past financial mistakes.

Air Madagascar's operational return on October 31 was marked by national pride, but a former director claims this masks a deeper financial reality. The airline, legally distinct from the newer Madagascar Airlines, carries over $100 million in historical debt. The state's announced abandonment of $50 million in claims on Air Madagascar leaves the remaining debt unresolved.

This move to rehabilitate the old company, according to former Air Madagascar General Director Thierry de Bailleul, could reinstate costly staff benefits like termination indemnities. He suggests this benefits corporate interests and historical creditors over the airline's economic viability. The new management has announced positive financial results without providing audited accounts or specifying the reporting period.

De Bailleul argues that Air Madagascar's core issues were its economic model, debt, and governance, not its name. He calls for the publication of audited 2026 accounts and previous years' financials to assess the company's true state. The concern is that past practices leading to bankruptcy could be revived under the guise of national pride.

Why it mattersMadagascar's national airline is facing a potential return to its previous financial struggles due to unresolved debt and the reinstatement of costly employee benefits.
