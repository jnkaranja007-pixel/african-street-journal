Madagascar presents drought investment plan at COP17 in Mongolia

Madagascar unveiled its Drought Investment Plan at the COP17 conference in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, aiming to shift from crisis response to proactive drought anticipation.

Madagascar presented its Drought Investment Plan at the COP17 of the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. The plan, developed with support from the Global Mechanism, seeks to direct funding towards actions that sustainably strengthen exposed territories and populations. The international conference, running from August seventeen to August twenty-eight, two thousand twenty-six, focuses on "Restoring Land, Restoring Hope."

The Malagasy plan centers on three main intervention areas. These include restoring ecosystems and forests, modernizing irrigation systems, developing agroecological practices, and using short-cycle seeds. The third area places farmers and communities at the core, aiming to reduce their reliance on repeated humanitarian aid by building their capacities before new droughts.

Luk Aristide Nolave Andriatsihala, the Minister of Environment and Sustainable Development, emphasized the need for upstream investment in populations, particularly women and youth. This aligns with COP17 discussions on preventive financing, with participants examining mechanisms to mobilize more capital for resilience, hydraulic infrastructure, and land restoration, including a new investment facility for drought resilience.

Why it mattersThe plan directly affects Malagasy farmers and communities by aiming to reduce their dependence on humanitarian aid and strengthen their resilience to drought.

Madagascar deepens Russia ties, risking resource control and dependence

Madagascar's transitional authorities are strengthening military and economic cooperation with Russia, particularly in mining, mirroring Russia's strategy in the Central African.

Madagascar is increasing its alignment with Moscow, a move that analysts say risks growing dependence on the Kremlin and potential capture of the country's strategic resources. This strategy is similar to Russia's approach in the Central African Republic, according to Madagascar Tribune.

The Malagasy junta leader visited Moscow in February 2026, where Russian President Vladimir Putin outlined areas of interest including agriculture, geology, mining, health, and training. Russian Africa Corps instructors have since arrived in Madagascar, stationed at the Ivato paratrooper battalion camp to train the Malagasy army.

The President of the National Assembly, Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko, is actively promoting stronger ties between Antananarivo and Moscow, advocating for deeper parliamentary, economic, and strategic cooperation. This convergence of high-level authorities and Russian interests supports the Kremlin's expanding military, economic, and diplomatic influence in Madagascar, with a focus on mining, energy, and infrastructure.

Why it mattersThis deepening relationship affects Malagasy citizens by potentially shifting control over the nation's strategic resources and increasing its reliance on a foreign power.

Madagascar faces Comoros in U23 Africa Cup qualifiers

Madagascar's national U23 football team will play Comoros in the first round of qualifications for the Africa Cup of Nations U23, despite lacking recent preparation.

Madagascar's Barea U23 team is scheduled to face the Comorian Coelacanths on September twenty-eighth in the first leg of the Africa Cup of Nations U23 qualifiers. The return match will be hosted by Madagascar on October six, likely at an away venue. This continental tournament serves as a qualifier for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympic Games.

The Malagasy Football Federation has not conducted any national competitions or player scouting for this age category in recent months, raising concerns about the team's readiness. The Federation's executive committee will discuss Madagascar's participation this Thursday, with less than a month remaining until the first match.

In the previous Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers in 2024, the Barea lost to the Comoros. If Madagascar advances past Comoros, they are set to play South Africa in the second round, with matches scheduled for November nine and November seventeen.

Why it mattersMalagasy football fans and players are directly affected by the lack of preparation for this international competition, which could impact their Olympic qualification hopes.

Madagascar Medical Institute offers 25 free pediatric ultrasounds

The Institut Médical de Madagascar (IMM) will provide free pediatric ultrasounds to 25 children, from newborns to 12 years old, this Sunday.

The Institut Médical de Madagascar (IMM) is launching a health initiative this Sunday, offering free pediatric ultrasounds to 25 children aged newborn to 12 years. This program targets children who need urgent medical imaging for health monitoring or life-saving purposes, as part of the IMM's social responsibility policy.

The initiative, themed "Sunday for Life, 100% Health for the Community," focuses specifically on child health and care. Pediatricians and sonographers from the IMM will volunteer their services for the health of these children. This effort aims to support vulnerable households facing difficulties in accessing specialized medical examinations.

To access the service, families can register via the green number 617 or present a doctor's referral. The IMM has established a dedicated system to manage participants, with clear signage from the main entrance to the RSE room, separate from the emergency department.

Why it mattersTwenty-five children and their families will receive free, urgent medical imaging that they might not otherwise afford, directly addressing a health access issue.

Business · 2026-08-27

Madagascar signs international cocoa agreement, boosting fine cocoa status

Madagascar signed the International Cocoa Agreement 2026 today, August twenty-seventh, at the Colbert Hotel in Antananarivo, further solidifying its position in global cocoa.

Madagascar is seeking to enhance the value of its cocoa quality in international markets. Today, August twenty-seventh, the country hosted Michel Arrion, executive director of the International Cocoa Organization (ICCO), for discussions and the signing of the International Cocoa Agreement 2026. This visit occurs while Madagascar holds the vice-presidency of the ICCO, aiming to strengthen its role in international discussions regarding the future of the sector.

The agreement is particularly important for Malagasy cocoa because its Annex C recognizes Madagascar as the only African country where one hundred percent of its cocoa bean exports are classified as “fine or aromatic cocoa.” This international recognition is a major asset for better positioning Malagasy cocoa in global markets.

For the Government and the National Cocoa Council (CNC), the current challenge is to translate this distinction into concrete benefits for the sector, especially for producers. Discussions with the ICCO will focus on quality, sustainability, traceability, markets, and enhancing the origin of Madagascar cocoa.

Why it mattersThe signing of this agreement directly impacts Malagasy cocoa producers by providing a framework to better value their fine cocoa on international markets and potentially increase their income.
