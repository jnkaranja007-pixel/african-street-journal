Japan Donates Rice Worth 7.9 Billion Ariary to Madagascar

A non-reimbursable donation of 300 million yen aims to bolster food security and fund development projects through local sales.

Japan has provided Madagascar with a non-reimbursable donation of rice valued at approximately 7.9 billion ariary (300 million yen) to enhance food security. The agreement, signed on Friday, September 4, is part of the 2026 Food Assistance Program. The ceremony was attended by the Japanese Ambassador to Madagascar, Tojima Hitoshi, and the Malagasy Minister of Foreign Affairs, N'Diaye Alice, alongside other government officials.

This contribution targets a nation where nearly 80% of the population relies on agriculture, facing challenges like recurrent droughts in the Grand South and cyclones in eastern regions. Chronic malnutrition affects nearly half of children under five. The donated rice, a staple food, will be sold by the Malagasy government at a reasonable price on the local market. Proceeds will be channeled into "Counterpart Funds" to finance socio-economic development projects in collaboration with Japan.

These "Counterpart Funds" have previously supported initiatives aimed at strengthening food security, improving agricultural sector resilience, and enhancing living conditions. Japan's continued assistance underscores its commitment to ensuring a more stable, secure, and affordable food supply for Madagascar. The program aims to address the country's vulnerability in agricultural production and persistent malnutrition issues.

Why it mattersThe donation of rice and its subsequent sale will generate funds for development projects, impacting the Malagasy economy and socio-economic landscape.

Madagascar Assembly Passes Two of Seven Proposed Laws

Lawmakers failed to advance five critical bills during an extraordinary session, prompting questions about efficiency and public spending.

The National Assembly concluded its second extraordinary session of 2026 with a meager output, adopting only two out of seven proposed laws. The remaining five bills, which covered topics ranging from SADC finance and investment to cybercrime and nuclear technology, have been deferred to a future session. This outcome raises concerns about the effectiveness of extraordinary parliamentary sessions and the associated public expenditures.

The two adopted laws pertain to the general status of military personnel and the SADC finance and investment agreement. The National Assembly cited the importance and complexity of the deferred legislation, such as the cybercrime bill, as reasons for the delay, emphasizing a desire to avoid hasty decisions with significant public impact. However, this approach leads to questions about the necessity of convening such sessions if a substantial portion of the agenda remains incomplete.

The parliamentary session, which incurs public costs including potential deputy indemnities, has highlighted a lack of transparency in state financial matters. While careful deliberation on crucial legislation is acknowledged, the failure to adequately prepare for and examine all scheduled bills during an extraordinary session suggests a need for better planning. The Assembly president has requested another extraordinary session to address the outstanding legislative items, potentially leading to further state expenses.

Why it mattersCitizens are affected by the inefficient use of public funds and the delayed consideration of laws impacting national development and security.

Electoral Project Concludes with Over 13 Million Dollar Investment

The RPEDEM initiative, launched in March 2023, officially ended with a final review of its technical and financial components.

The Reinforcement of Electoral Processes and Democracy in Madagascar (RPEDEM) project has officially concluded following its sixth and final steering committee meeting in Antananarivo. The initiative, which began in March 2023, aimed to strengthen the electoral system and foster a more inclusive governance structure. Key stakeholders, including the President of the CENI, Thierry Rakotonarivo, and representatives from the United Nations and UNDP, attended the final review.

During its tenure, RPEDEM supported several key elections, including the 2023 presidential polls, the May 2024 legislative elections, and the December 2024 municipal elections. Notable achievements include the introduction of braille ballots for visually impaired voters, enabling independent voting for the first time. The project also incorporated sub-projects focused on increasing women and youth participation, enhancing biometric data management, and preventing tensions through citizen engagement.

With a total budget exceeding 13 million dollars, the RPEDEM project facilitated the creation of three digital platforms: iParticipate, iReport, and iVerify, developed with support from UNDP-Brussels. A successor project, APERM, is currently in preparation to continue the work of supporting Madagascar's electoral processes and democratic foundations.

Why it mattersThe conclusion of the RPEDEM project and the preparation of a successor project indicate ongoing efforts to stabilize and improve Madagascar's governance and electoral systems.

Prime Minister Appoints National Consultation Technical Committee

The newly appointed committee will oversee the organization and technical coordination of a national consultation process aimed at refounding the Republic.

Prime Minister Mamitiana Rajaonarison signed an order on September 3, 2026, in Antananarivo, designating members for the Technical Committee of the National Consultation. This committee, established by decree on June 17, 2026, will support the Ecumenical Council of Christian Churches of Madagascar (FFKM) in piloting consultations across the country. Based at the Prime Ministry, the committee is tasked with monitoring the timeline, providing technical coordination, and transmitting debate conclusions to the government for implementation.

The committee's mandate concludes with the investiture of the elected President following the refoundation process. This appointment occurs amidst a political transition initiated in October 2025. The national consultation, which gained operational momentum in late August 2026, aims to establish a new institutional framework within twenty-four months, as outlined in the Refoundation Program submitted to the Southern African Development Community (SADC).

Preparations are accelerating with facilitator training underway and political consultations on electoral governance continuing. The Independent National Electoral Commission (CENI) is also revising the electoral roll. The September 3 order signifies a shift from preparatory to operational phases, confirming the concrete implementation of the National Consultation process.

Why it mattersThe appointment of the Technical Committee of the National Consultation directly impacts the political process by establishing a structure to guide consultations and implement their outcomes.

Education · 2026-09-05

Youth violence prompts questions about parental and societal influence

The recent murder of two young girls by drug-addled youths has sparked debate on who is responsible for educating children in Madagascar.

The brutal murder of two young girls, Stéphanie and Haingotiana, by a group of young people under the influence of drugs has raised critical questions about the education of children in Madagascar. While parents are naturally seen as the primary educators, the complexity of influences from teachers, peers, extended family, and community members means they are not solely responsible for shaping a child's values and behavior.

The incident prompts a deeper examination of how young people become involved with drugs and lose their moral compass to the point of committing violent crimes. It highlights the need to educate youth on recognizing dangers and making protective choices, without shifting blame from the perpetrators. The inherent youthful spirit of rebellion and experimentation adds layers of complexity to this challenge.

Modern influences, including social media, globalized trends, and even undesirable role models from street gangs, present a constant stream of information and norms to Malagasy youth. This influx of potentially harmful influences, alongside concerns about the lyrical content and on-stage behavior of some local artists, leads to public dismay over deviations from traditional Malagasy values, though the definition of these values remains debated.

Why it mattersThe recent murders highlight how societal influences and a lack of guidance can lead young people to harmful behaviors and violence, affecting the safety and moral fabric of communities.
