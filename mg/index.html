Madagascar updates national health security plan

The revised National Action Plan for Health Security aims to strengthen the country's response to health emergencies.

Madagascar has updated its national health security plan to better prevent, detect, and respond to health emergencies. The revised strategy aligns with global health changes and the country's specific situation, addressing increasing public health threats like epidemics and potential outbreaks of diseases such as plague, cholera, and Ebola.

The updated Plan d’Action National Pour la Sécurité Sanitaire de Madagascar (PANSS) seeks to enhance Madagascar's readiness for health crises. It aims to improve coordination and collaboration between human health, animal health, and environmental sectors under the "One Health" approach, recognizing that collective efforts are essential for preparedness.

This revision focuses on adapting prevention measures to current realities and strengthening the nation's capacity to handle health emergencies. The goal is to protect public health by improving the country's ability to face and mitigate health risks.

Why it mattersMadagascar's national health security plan was updated to improve the country's response to health emergencies.

Madagascar police celebrate 65 years of service

The national police force marked its 65th anniversary with public outreach and recruitment drives.

Madagascar's national police force celebrated its 65th anniversary this year, focusing on community engagement and infrastructure development. The Ministry of Public Security equipped officers to better serve the public under the theme 'Malagasy National Police protecting and helping the people'. Events included an open house at the National Library in Ampefiloha, which concluded yesterday, showcasing police services.

Community outreach activities featured health screenings, including free tests for breast and cervical cancer, and diabetes checks, organized by the Association of Police Wives and Female Police Officers (Fivapova). A nationwide blood donation drive was also initiated on September 30th. To increase police presence, recruitment efforts are underway, with 500 agent trainees heading to Eniap in Antsirabe and further selections for 20 commissioners, 30 inspectors, and 850 police agents.

The goal is to reach an international standard of three police officers per 100 people, compared to the current ratio of one officer per 3,000 people. Regional public security directors were officially installed in Boeny, Melaky, Betsiboka, and Sofia on October 16th, strengthening local police accessibility.

Why it mattersThe national police force is expanding its ranks and services to better protect and assist the Malagasy people.

Madagascar U15 team wins three matches in Baku

The Barea secured victories against Mozambique, New Zealand, and Puerto Rico in the FIFA U15 World Cup.

Madagascar's U15 national football team, the Barea, achieved a perfect record of three wins in three matches at the FIFA U15 World Cup & Festival in Baku, Azerbaijan. The team, composed of players from the Centre technique national de Carion, defeated Mozambique 2-0, New Zealand 4-2, and Puerto Rico 4-3 over the weekend.

Toky Niaina Fenohery Razafinimanana scored Madagascar's first goal against Mozambique, with Francisco Rafanomezanjanahary adding the second. Mendrika Salomon Randriamanantena scored twice in the win against New Zealand, with Toky and Riciano Fanomezana also finding the net. Against Puerto Rico, Yann Willy Nirintsoa and Barthélémy Andrianotahiana scored, before Riciano and Francisco secured the late victory.

The Barea will play two more matches in the initial phase on Monday against Venezuela and Somalia. The non-eliminatory evaluation phase continues until Saturday in Baku. Coach Jean-Jacques Pierre leads the Malagasy squad.

Why it mattersYoung Malagasy footballers are competing in an international tournament in Azerbaijan.

Madagascar U23 team wins fifth friendly match

The national U23 team, the Barea, defeated Bongolava 8-0 in their sixth preparation match for the U23 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers.

The Madagascar U23 team, known as the Barea, secured their fifth victory in a series of preparation matches by defeating the Bongolava selection 8-0. This win occurred on the synthetic pitch in Tsiroanomandidy, as the team continues its training camp two weeks before the U23 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics.

Coach Herimanitra Rabeharisoa, also known as "Hery Be," noted improvements in the team's game organization and scoring but highlighted that some details still require attention. The day prior, a reserve team beat the Itasy senior selection 2-1 in Analavory, though performance declined in the second half due to the unlevel dirt pitch.

The local pre-selection has now recorded five wins and one draw, with their next challenge being against South Africa's Bafana Bafana in the second qualifying round, scheduled for November 13 in Mauritius and November 17 in South Africa. Head coach Boualem Mankour has joined the staff in France to finalize preparations for expatriate players.

Why it mattersMadagascar's U23 football team is preparing for the U23 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers, which are also qualifiers for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympic Games.

Madagascar police numbers increase

The Ministry of Public Security aims to reduce the ratio of one police officer to 3,000 inhabitants.

Madagascar's national police force is expanding its ranks through two recruitment drives this year. The first wave added 500 student officers to the National School for Inspectors and Police Officers in Antsirabe. A second recruitment drive, with initial stages on September 26 and 27, seeks 20 commissars, 30 inspectors, and 850 police agents.

These new recruits address a significant shortage, as Madagascar currently has one police officer for every 3,000 citizens, falling short of international standards. The ministry stated these efforts aim to bring the police closer to the population, acknowledging the long road ahead. Regional security directorates have also been established in Boeny, Melaky, Betsiboka, Sofia, Diana, and Sava.

The ministry inaugurated new and rehabilitated infrastructures this year, including regional directorates, police stations, and posts. These facilities have been equipped with vehicles, motorcycles, and computer hardware to enhance community engagement. Police Inspector General Erick Michel urged these locations to become centers for strategy development to ensure security.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Public Security is increasing police numbers and improving infrastructure to better serve the population.
