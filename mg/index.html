Madagascar requisitions Enelec power plant

The government takes control of the Enelec fuel oil plant in Toamasina for up to 24 months, with the final cost and scope yet to be determined.

The Malagasy government has requisitioned Enelec's fuel oil power plant in Toamasina, transferring operational control to the state-owned utility JIRAMA. A decree dated November 3 placed the plant and its operational resources under state control, excluding solar power, for a maximum of 24 months. The exact financial implications, including who will cover fuel, salaries, maintenance, and potential compensation, remain unclear.

This measure follows a period of recurring power outages in Antananarivo and Toamasina. While new turbines in the capital had temporarily reduced cuts in late 2025, power disruptions returned by November 5 due to issues at the Andekaleka plant. In Toamasina, cyclone Gezani damaged the network in early 2026, and Enelec's plant was operating at only 30% capacity.

The state's action is authorized by the Electricity Code in cases of service disruption. JIRAMA will now manage the plant's operations and can directly purchase maintenance parts. The Enelec personnel will remain in place under JIRAMA's direction, with reports indicating JIRAMA will contribute to salaries. The transfer of control does not include ownership of the company.

Why it mattersThe government's requisition of the Enelec power plant means JIRAMA will manage operations and maintenance, impacting the cost and reliability of electricity for residents.

Randrianasoloniaiko remains head of National Assembly

Vice-president denies rumors of a move to oust the permanent bureau, citing normal legislative work.

Rumors of a leadership change at Madagascar's National Assembly have been denied by Fanomezantsoa Andrianjanahary, the institution's vice-president. Andrianjanahary stated on Thursday, November 12, that no initiative to remove the permanent bureau was underway, aiming to quell speculation circulating on social media about internal crisis. Legislative activities, including the examination of the 2027 finance bill, continue normally, with deputies from the Vondron'ny Fihavaozan'i Madagasikara (VFM) maintaining their support for the current president, Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko.

The denial comes amid reports of a low parliamentary presence during budget sessions, which Andrianjanahary attributed to deputies visiting their constituencies to present their work. Despite these assurances, Randrianasoloniaiko's political positioning has raised questions, particularly his reduced public communication compared to earlier stages of the current administration. This shift fuels speculation about a potential cooling of relations with Colonel Michaël Randrianirina, though no verified information confirms a dispute between them.

Randrianasoloniaiko played a decisive role in the current power structure, having approached the High Constitutional Court prior to the October 14, 2025, decision that tasked Colonel Michaël Randrianirina with presidential duties. The National Assembly itself experienced a significant leadership change on October 15, 2025, when 127 deputies voted to remove the previous permanent bureau. Currently, no similar scenario is anticipated, with the existing bureau maintaining its functions and the 2027 budget review officially prioritized.

Why it mattersThe National Assembly's permanent bureau continues its functions as lawmakers examine the 2027 budget, following denials of internal crisis.

Photographer killed in Andraharo road accident

A taxi-ville driver who fled the scene was arrested by police early Friday, and tested above the legal alcohol limit.

A 28-year-old photographer named Faneva died on Thursday evening in Andraharo, Antananarivo, after a taxi-ville struck his motorcycle near Cap 3000 at about 7:30 PM. The taxi driver fled toward Tsarasaotra, and witnesses who tried to chase him did not catch him.

Police from the public security station of the capital's Fifth arrondissement took the badly injured rider to the Joseph Ravoahangy-Andrianavalona University Hospital Centre (HJRA), where doctors could only confirm his death. His body was placed in the hospital morgue. Investigators identified the taxi quickly, and the 45-year-old driver was arrested at about 5:30 AM on Friday and placed in custody. Early findings indicate his blood alcohol level was above the legal limit.

On the same evening, another motorcyclist died on the bypass between Ambohitrimanjaka and the Andranotapahana road exit after being hit by a 4x4. On Friday morning at about 7:30 AM, a pedestrian crossing at a zebra crossing in Ankorondrano was knocked down by a motorcycle, and the rider was seriously hurt. The exact circumstances and responsibilities for the first crash are still to be established by the investigation.

Why it mattersThe driver's arrest and alcohol finding mean he now faces the investigation into who was responsible for the death.
