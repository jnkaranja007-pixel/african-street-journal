Madagascar's government and parliament face decision on whistleblower bill

The draft protecting whistleblowers, corruption reporters and witnesses is complete, but its adoption depends on government and parliamentary bodies.

Sources: Madagascar Tribune and Midi Madagasikara · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Madagascar’s Committee for the Safeguarding of Integrity (CSI), the country’s integrity body, urged the government and parliament to adopt a completed bill protecting whistleblowers, people who report corruption and witnesses. The committee’s executive secretary, Tsiry Razafimandimby, made the appeal on Wednesday, 25 November, at a debate in Mahamasina.

Razafimandimby said fear of reprisals can deter citizens from reporting public-fund theft and abuses of power. The bill’s drafting is finished, but government and parliamentary bodies must still examine and adopt it. The CSI says protection from pressure and intimidation is central to the proposed law.

The CSI also called for cooperation between state institutions and civil-society organisations. Its effectiveness would depend on the safeguards adopted and how authorities apply them.

Why it mattersThe government and parliament must now examine and adopt the completed bill, with its safeguards and enforcement still to be determined.
