Madagascar allocates $13 million to fight financial crime in 2027

The budget covers corruption, money laundering and drug trafficking as the finance minister leads the regional council through August 2027.

Source: Midi Madagasikara · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Madagascar will spend about $13 million, equal to 0.3% of planned public expenditure in 2027, on fighting organised crime, including corruption, money laundering and drug trafficking. The announcement came at the opening of a sixth meeting of a Southern African Development Community (SADC) committee on money laundering and terrorist financing in Nosy Be.

Prime Minister Mamitiana Rajaonarison said Madagascar would no longer rank last in the fight against money laundering. Finance Minister Herinjatovo Aimé Ramiarison chairs the council of ministers of the Eastern and Southern Africa Anti-Money Laundering Group (ESAAMLG) from September 2026 to August 2027. Illicit financial flows into and out of Madagascar were estimated at $330 million in 2024, equal to 18.5% of tax revenue.

Madagascar is the first SADC member to begin the third round of mutual evaluations, ESAAMLG executive secretary Fikile P. Zitha said. Its financial intelligence unit, the SAMIFIN, joined the Egmont Group of financial intelligence units in July, giving it secure channels to exchange financial intelligence with partners worldwide.

Why it mattersThe government has set aside $13 million for 2027 enforcement against corruption, money laundering and drug trafficking.
