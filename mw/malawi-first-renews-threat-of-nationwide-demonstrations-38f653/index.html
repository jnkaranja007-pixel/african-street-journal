Malawi First renews threat of nationwide demonstrations

The group says government missed a two-week deadline to set out responses to fuel shortages, hunger, blackouts, forex scarcity and water disruptions.

Sources: The Nation Malawi and Nyasa Times · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Malawi First, a group led by Bon Kalindo, renewed its threat to hold nationwide demonstrations after a two-week period for government to outline responses to socio-economic problems expired. The period followed a November 4 meeting, after which the group suspended a seven-day ultimatum issued on October 29.

Kalindo said in an interview that government had not communicated proposed interventions to the group. Malawi First has called an emergency meeting to decide its next move, which it plans to announce. Kalindo said demonstrations would proceed if the group did not receive answers. The demands cover fuel, electricity, water, food and foreign-exchange shortages.

Government previously said the problems were structural and would take time to resolve. Information Minister Shadric Namalomba said President Peter Mutharika had indicated stabilising the situation could take two years. Nyika Institute director Moses Mkandawire urged Malawi First to continue dialogue, saying the challenges require carefully planned interventions.

Why it mattersMalawi First must decide its next course of action after the government’s two-week period to outline interventions expired.
