Malawi medical referrals cost K43.4 billion a year

Reserve Bank records show K16 billion went to treatment abroad from March to July 2026, while a professor estimates wider costs are higher.

Source: Nyasa Times · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Malawi spends about K43.4 billion a year on medical treatment abroad, business leaders said at an economic forum in Lilongwe. They argued that investment in specialist healthcare at home could fill gaps in the country’s services and keep scarce foreign exchange in Malawi.

Investment, Trade and Tourism Minister Simon Itaye said reliance on foreign hospitals was adding pressure to strained foreign-exchange reserves. Reserve Bank of Malawi payment records show K16 billion was spent on overseas referrals from March to July 2026. The referrals cost about K3.6 billion a month, according to the report.

Gowokani Chijere-Chirwa, an Extraordinary Professor at North-West University in South Africa, put the wider cost—including guardians’ expenses and lost productivity—at between K17.6 billion and K19.5 billion. The Press Corporation Limited and the Malawi Confederation of Chambers of Commerce and Industry held the forum to discuss investment in strategic sectors, including healthcare.

Why it mattersThe health system and foreign-exchange reserves absorb about K3.6 billion in referral spending each month, while business leaders call for specialist care investment at home.
