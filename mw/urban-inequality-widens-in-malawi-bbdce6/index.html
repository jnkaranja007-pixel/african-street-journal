Urban inequality widens in Malawi

New data shows a widening gap in household consumption in Malawi's cities over the past five years.

Malawi's urban areas have seen a significant increase in inequality over the last five years, according to the National Statistics Office (NSO). The Gini index for urban consumption rose from 39.0 in the 2019-20 financial year to 45.6 in 2024-25, a sharper rise than the national increase from 37.9 to 39.9. This widening gap occurred alongside a rise in national food poverty from 20.5 percent to 24 percent, even as overall national poverty declined.

The NSO's findings, based on the Sixth Integrated Household Survey, indicate that economic gains have not been evenly distributed among urban households. Urban poverty itself increased from 19.2 percent to 20.9 percent, though it remains lower than rural poverty. This trend exacerbates the challenges Malawians face with the rising cost of living, with the cost for an average household of six exceeding K1.11 million in August 2026.

Economist Marvin Banda stated that this urban inequality reflects an economy with a divide between those with secure employment and assets and those relying on informal trade and irregular incomes. He noted that poorer households are forced to spend most of their income on necessities, leaving little for savings or investment, while non-food inflation reached 31.9 percent in August 2026.

Why it mattersUrban households face widening consumption disparities, impacting their ability to save and invest due to rising costs and informal work.
