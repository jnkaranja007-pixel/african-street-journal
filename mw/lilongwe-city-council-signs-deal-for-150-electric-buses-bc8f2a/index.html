Lilongwe City Council signs deal for 150 electric buses

The Memorandum of Understanding with Indian firm Mercury EV-Tech Limited also covers an electric vehicle assembly plant in the capital.

Source: Malawi24 · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Lilongwe City Council has signed a Memorandum of Understanding with Mercury EV-Tech Limited, an Indian electric mobility company, covering the proposed deployment of 150 electric buses in the capital. The first buses are expected to arrive between February and March 2027.

Council Chief Executive Officer Clement Stambuli confirmed the expected arrival period and said Mercury EV-Tech had already registered its operations in Malawi. He described the partnership as an investment opportunity that could bring modern transport technology and open doors for local vehicle assembly and job creation.

The proposed assembly plant is still subject to feasibility assessments, further discussions and regulatory approvals, and commercial terms and an implementation schedule are yet to be finalised. In a disclosure to India's stock exchanges on November 16, 2026, Mercury EV-Tech said the agreement also covers technology transfer, technical support, skills development and capacity building.

Why it mattersResidents of Lilongwe would gain a new public transport option if the first buses arrive between February and March 2027 as expected.
