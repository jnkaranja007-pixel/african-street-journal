Politics · 2026-11-01

Mutharika returns, blames fuel and power issues on saboteurs

President Peter Mutharika stated he was in full control of Malawi during his absence and promised consequences for electricity disruptions.

President Peter Mutharika returned to Malawi on Saturday after a private trip to South Africa and asserted he remained in charge of the country. He stated he was in constant contact with the Chief Secretary and ministers, dismissing speculation about other officials running the government in his absence. Mutharika also warned individuals deliberately causing problems in the electricity sector that they would face consequences.

The President attributed persistent electricity disruptions to man-made issues, promising that blackouts would end by December as machines were being repaired. Regarding fuel shortages, Mutharika explained that supplies were available at ports in Tanzania and Mozambique, including Nacala, Beira, Tanga, and Dar es Salaam, but logistics were hindering transportation into Malawi. He asked for patience, stating these fuel transportation challenges were beyond government control.

Mutharika, who has made five private trips since October 2025, thanked citizens for messages during his absence and called for unity using the Swahili expression 'Harambee'. He arrived home on Saturday, November 1, 2026, following his nearly month-long trip.

Why it mattersMalawians are directly affected by persistent electricity disruptions and fuel shortages, with the President promising resolutions by December.

Sport · 2026-11-01

Malawi U-15 team finishes 79th at Fifa World Cup

The team's performance in Baku exposed significant gaps in the nation's youth football development structures.

Malawi's Under-15 boys national football team concluded the inaugural Fifa Under-15 World Cup and Festival in Baku, Azerbaijan, ranked 79th out of 189 nations. The team secured 15th place in Tier C after achieving five victories against Chinese Taipei, Oman, Suriname, Hungary, and Rwanda. Takutzwa Maluwe was Malawi's top scorer with eight goals, contributing to the team's overall performance.

Despite individual successes like Maluwe's eight goals, defeats against Algeria, Argentina, Luxembourg, Panama, and Thailand highlighted areas needing substantial improvement in Malawi's youth football development. Namasala Academy owner Wesley Namasala observed that international competitiveness requires sustained investment in coaching, facilities, nutrition, and competitive opportunities, noting that foreign players were often more physically developed.

Namasala proposed establishing at least 10 residential academies across Malawi to build a strong foundation, stating that short training camps cannot replace childhood development. Zambia's success in winning Tier B by assembling a squad from 11 schools also suggests schools football as a viable alternative pathway for talent development in Malawi.

Why it mattersMalawi's youth football development structures were exposed as lacking compared to other nations, impacting the future of young players.

Politics · 2026-11-01

Malawi parliament meeting postponed after presidential recommendation

President Mutharika recommended postponing the National Assembly's session, with the Speaker announcing the change.

Malawi's National Assembly meeting, scheduled from November 9 to December 11, 2026, has been postponed. President Peter Mutharika sent a letter on October 23, 2026, recommending the postponement to redirect public resources to national challenges. The Speaker subsequently announced the postponement of the Fourth Meeting of the 52nd Session.

The use of the word 'directed' in the President's letter has raised constitutional questions. Section 59 of the Constitution states that the Speaker, in consultation with the President, appoints the time for every meeting of the National Assembly. The President can also, in consultation with the Speaker, summon extraordinary meetings or prorogue the Assembly.

The Constitution does not expressly provide for postponement, but outlines the consultative relationship between the President and the Speaker regarding parliamentary sittings. The Speaker takes part in fixing when a meeting starts, and the President has an express power to prorogue, subject to consultation.

Why it mattersThe postponement of the National Assembly meeting affects the legislative process and the constitutional roles of the President and the Speaker.

Health · 2026-11-01

Malawi expands cancer treatment services

Kamuzu Central Hospital registered over 2,100 new cancer patients in 2025, offering chemotherapy and radiotherapy.

Malawi has increased access to specialised cancer care at the National Cancer Centre within Kamuzu Central Hospital (KCH) in Lilongwe. Patients can now receive surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy locally, reducing the need for expensive overseas treatment. Health officials noted progress in cancer services despite challenges like late diagnoses.

In 2025, KCH registered over 2,100 new cancer patients, with more than 1,600 receiving chemotherapy and over 600 undergoing radiotherapy. Amos Msekandiana, KCH Director General, stated that the centre also provided brachytherapy to 70 women. He highlighted the need for more oncologists, nurses, and technicians to improve care, noting that many patients still present with late-stage disease.

The Ministry of Health and Sanitation aims to strengthen cancer control through the National Cancer Control Strategic Plan 2019-2029. Benson Chisamile, Secretary for Health and Sanitation, mentioned progress in prevention, including HPV vaccination and screening, and training over 2,000 health workers in childhood cancer detection. Msekandiana also called for investment in psychosocial support for patients.

Why it mattersMalawians needing cancer treatment now have expanded local options for surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy at Kamuzu Central Hospital.

Business · 2026-11-01

Malawi central bank raises borrowing costs

The Reserve Bank of Malawi increased its Lombard Rate to 2 percent to manage liquidity amid inflation risks.

The Reserve Bank of Malawi (RBM) has raised its Lombard Rate from 0.2 percentage points to 2 percentage points, increasing borrowing costs for businesses and consumers. This move by the Monetary Policy Committee aims to manage liquidity amidst inflation risks, with economist Velli Nyirongo warning of tough times ahead. Businesses relying on imported materials and fuel will face higher operational costs, potentially limiting expansion and new ventures.

Nyirongo stated that while the rate hike may help contain inflation risks, its direct impact will be limited because external factors like high fuel prices and imported costs also drive inflation. He emphasized the need for government coordination on food supply, agricultural production, and fiscal discipline to support the fight against rising prices. The current inflation rate dropped to 20.1 percent in the third quarter from 22.9 percent.

The RBM's Monetary Policy Committee noted that broad money growth slowed to 19.7 percent in the third quarter of 2026, down from 21.2 percent in the previous quarter and significantly lower than 51 percent a year earlier. Private sector credit growth also slowed to 21.7 percent. However, the use of the Lombard facility rose to an average of K72.3 billion per day, posing a risk to money supply growth.

Why it mattersMalawians will face elevated prices and higher borrowing costs due to the central bank's decision to increase the Lombard Rate.
