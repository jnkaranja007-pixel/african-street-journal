Activist disputes NGO regulator's power to deregister HRDC

Veteran activist Redson Munlo argues the Non-Governmental Organisations Regulatory Authority (NGORA) lacks legal authority to deregister the Human Rights Defenders Coalition.

Veteran activist Redson Munlo has challenged the Non-Governmental Organisations Regulatory Authority's (NGORA) move to deregister the Human Rights Defenders Coalition (HRDC). Munlo stated that civil society organisations are not legally required to register with NGORA, therefore the body cannot deregister them.

Munlo described NGORA as an association without regulatory powers over independent civil society groups. He noted that many prominent organisations in Malawi’s civic space operate without being NGORA members and continue their work. This dispute could concern international donors monitoring Malawi's democratic standing.

Development partners, including the European Union, the United Kingdom, and UN agencies, rely on groups like HRDC to monitor transparency and public funds. NGORA announced the revocation of HRDC's status, citing operational shortcomings.

Why it mattersThe dispute over deregistering the Human Rights Defenders Coalition (HRDC) by the NGO regulator NGORA could alarm international donors who rely on such groups for transparency monitoring.

Malawi plane crash evidence vanishes

Forensic samples and photographs from the June 10, 2024 crash that killed Saulos Chilima are missing.

Forensic samples and photographs from the June 10, 2024 plane crash that killed former vice-president Saulos Chilima and eight others have disappeared. Senior medical officer Andrew Master Mwale told the plane crash enquiry that samples he collected and stored at Sun Set Funeral Services could no longer be traced. The missing evidence raises serious questions about the investigation's transparency and completeness.

Mwale expressed surprise that materials from such a sensitive investigation could go missing without explanation. He noted that eight bodies were kept at Sun Set Funeral Services, which had reportedly inadequate security compared to Goodwill Funeral Services, where Chilima's body was held. The disappearance of photographs taken during recovery and postmortems also hinders independent specialists from reconstructing the process and verifying forensic observations.

Substantial human tissue, including muscle and backbone fragments, was recovered, but some materials, including a large tissue sample believed to belong to Abdul Lapukeni, remain unaccounted for. Questions also surround the identification of body parts attributed to Chilima, specifically an amputated leg and foot. Mwale stated that independent DNA testing, potentially in South Africa or Zambia, is increasingly important to confirm victim identities.

Why it mattersThe disappearance of evidence from the plane crash investigation hinders the accurate identification of victims and clarification of the accident's circumstances.

Malawi council launches K3.6 billion development projects

Lilongwe City Council's Reformed Constituency Development Fund package includes a health facility, secondary school, and road improvements.

Lilongwe City Council has launched K3.6 billion in development projects for the Mtandire-Mtsiriza constituency, aiming to improve lives within five months. The Reformed Constituency Development Fund (RCDF) package includes a new health facility, a secondary school, and infrastructure upgrades like box culverts and drainage to combat flooding. Besphat Construction Company, Fukumule Construction, and Lukam Construction Company will carry out the work.

Member of Parliament Alfred Gangata stated that residents identified the projects to meet their development needs and urged community ownership. Lilongwe City Council's Director of Administration, Mussa Mwale, assured that quality and compliance mechanisms are in place. The projects are intended to address long-standing challenges in healthcare, education, and infrastructure.

Residents have anticipated improvements in public services and infrastructure for years. The successful and timely completion of these projects within the five-month timeframe will determine how quickly these developments translate into tangible changes in their daily lives.

Why it mattersResidents of Mtandire and Mtsiriza will benefit from improved access to healthcare, education, and better road infrastructure.
