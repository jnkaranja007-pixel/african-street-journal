Malawi construction cost inflation slows

General Construction Input Price Index rose 4.4 percent in Q2 2026/27, down from 6.2 percent.

Malawi's construction cost inflation slowed in the second quarter of the 2026/27 financial year, with the General Construction Input Price Index (GCIPI) rising by 4.4 percent between July and September 2026. This marks a decrease from the 6.2 percent increase recorded in the preceding quarter, according to figures from the Construction Industry Regulatory Authority (Cira) and the National Statistical Office (NSO). Annually, construction cost inflation eased to 21.3 percent from 28.8 percent in the first quarter, a reduction of 7.5 percentage points.

Cira chief executive officer Engineer Gerald Khonje stated that the pace of price increases has moderated significantly, reflecting improving stability within the industry, although construction inputs remain more expensive than a year ago. Civil engineering saw quarterly input price inflation decline to 3.4 percent from 8.1 percent, while building construction recorded a slight quarterly increase to 5.1 percent from 5.0 percent.

Despite the easing cost indicators, the Reserve Bank of Malawi (RBM) revised its construction sector growth projection for 2026 downwards to 4.2 percent, citing foreign exchange shortages, rising fuel prices, and supply constraints. Economist Velli Nyirongo noted that imported materials, financing costs, and transport remain significant constraints for contractors and developers.

Why it mattersMalawi's construction sector growth projection has been revised downwards due to persistent foreign exchange shortages and fuel supply challenges.

Grandfather jailed 10 years for rape

A 59-year-old man was sentenced for raping his 12-year-old granddaughter in Salima District.

Richard London, a 59-year-old grandfather from Chenyama Village in Salima District, has been sentenced to 10 years imprisonment with hard labour. The Salima Senior Resident Magistrate’s Court convicted him of raping his 12-year-old granddaughter.

The court found London guilty of the offence, which contravenes Section 138 of Malawi's laws. The sentence includes hard labour, as handed down by the magistrate.

Separately, a High Court judge in Lilongwe ordered the release of a man jailed for 16 years after being tried as an adult when he was a child. This man, identified as CM, had pleaded guilty to sexual intercourse with a 12-year-old girl in Dedza.

Malawi parliament budget review postponed

President Mutharika ordered the delay to focus resources on El Niño threat preparations.

Malawi's Mid-Year Budget Review Meeting, scheduled from November 9 to December 11, 2026, has been postponed by presidential order. The decision, announced on October 28, aims to redirect government resources and attention towards preparing for the anticipated El Niño weather threat.

Opposition MCP youth director Moses Malondera accused President Peter Mutharika of misleading the public, stating that the government disguised a cancellation as a postponement by not providing an alternative date. National Assembly Speaker Sameer Suleman confirmed the postponement followed a directive from the President.

The government's statement cited the need to mitigate El Niño's potential impact on food security, livelihoods, and the economy. This directive has drawn mixed reactions from legal and governance experts who question its legality, according to a letter from State House seen by the Times Group Malawi.

Why it mattersThe postponement of the budget review meeting affects parliamentary oversight and the allocation of resources for national priorities.

WFP support boosts Malawi farmer's maize harvest to 21 bags

Levison Kamleka harvested 21 bags of maize in the 2025-2026 season, up from four, after receiving anticipatory action intervention.

Levison Kamleka, a 45-year-old farmer in Zomba District, harvested 21 bags of maize in the 2025-2026 farming season, an increase from his usual three to four bags. This improvement followed support from the World Food Programme (WFP) through an anticipatory action intervention. Kamleka used six bags for food and has 14 remaining.

Kamleka, from Group Village Mkanda, Senior Chief Mulumbe, stated that he sees a future in such initiatives because he can now secure food and plans for relish daily, ending the struggle for maize. His experience is part of WFP Malawi’s Zero Hunger Village (ZHV) model, launched in September 2025, which targets 1,200 villages and 54,400 beneficiaries across 14 districts.

In Nsanje District, another drought-prone area, irrigation support under the ZHV model is helping farmers like Chrissy Edward grow crops. Edward, 35, harvested nine bags of rice from her 0.1-hectare plot using irrigation, up from two to three bags previously, enabling her to sell surplus and pay school fees for her daughters. Aline Bingara, 52, also uses irrigation to grow sweet potatoes, maize, rice, and vegetables.

Why it mattersMalawian farmers are experiencing increased food security and income through WFP's Zero Hunger Village model, which provides agricultural support and irrigation.

Malawi president skips Russia-Africa summit

22 African heads of state attended the two-day summit in Moscow discussing trade, security, and food security.

Malawi President Peter Mutharika did not attend the third Russia–Africa Summit in Moscow, which began on Wednesday and concluded on Thursday, October 29. 22 African heads of state were present at the summit, where leaders discussed trade, food security, energy, investment, and security cooperation.

The summit brought together delegations from 48 of Africa’s 54 countries, according to Kremlin foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov. Russian President Vladimir Putin and Burundi’s President Évariste Ndayishimiye, the current African Union chairperson, co-chaired the event. Leaders from Zimbabwe, Ghana, Mali, South Sudan, Mozambique, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, and the Republic of the Congo were among those reported in Moscow.

Uganda, Rwanda, Algeria, Burkina Faso, and South Africa sent senior representatives instead of their heads of state. The agenda included discussions on trade and investment, agriculture, energy, transport, education, science, technology, and humanitarian cooperation. Participants were expected to adopt a Russia–Africa partnership action plan for 2027 to 2029.

Why it mattersMalawi's head of state was absent from a summit where African nations discussed trade, food security, energy, investment, and security cooperation with Russia.
