Malawi health workers threaten walkout

A coalition of health workers has given the government a 14-day ultimatum to address critical shortages and staffing issues.

Healthcare workers in Malawi are planning to withdraw their services from public health facilities after a 14-day ultimatum to President Peter Mutharika’s administration expired without a satisfactory response.

The Human Resources for Health Coalition, which petitioned the government on October 27, 2026, demanding the recruitment of over 14,400 qualified health professionals, warned of potential service shutdowns. Coalition chairperson Solomon Chomba stated that the group has not received a reply from the Office of the President and Cabinet, indicating a likely tactical labour withdrawal and potential court action.

Chomba described dire conditions in clinics and hospitals, citing a lack of gloves and drugs, and overburdened staff, and stated the coalition would proceed step-by-step before a nationwide withdrawal. The Ministry of Health and Sanitation has also reportedly ignored the coalition's request for a meeting, though a ministry spokesperson claimed recruitment is underway.

Why it mattersHealthcare workers are threatening a nationwide withdrawal of services, impacting all public health facilities across Malawi.

Court fines three men K800,000 each over 670 litres of petrol

The fuel has been forfeited to the government after the men were found with it in Dwangwa, Nkhotakota District.

Three men have each been fined K800,000 by a court in Malawi for possessing 670 litres of petrol, and the fuel has been forfeited to the government. The men, identified as Hamza Mdoka, 27, Chikondi Zulu, 23, and Molle Banda, 23, were found with the petrol in Dwangwa, Nkhotakota District, on Tuesday.

The men had faced a possible 12-month jail term. The court chose a fine instead, and the fuel now belongs to the state.

The report does not give further details of the proceedings.

Why it mattersMdoka, Zulu and Banda must each pay K800,000, and the 670 litres of petrol is no longer theirs to sell or keep.

Malawi court delays ex-army chief Phiri's corruption trial

The Anti-Corruption Bureau cited unpreparedness as prosecutors failed to open the case against General Paul Phiri on five charges.

The prosecution of General Paul Valentino Phiri, the former commander of the Malawi Defence Force, was delayed on Thursday when the Anti-Corruption Bureau (ACB) informed a Lilongwe court it was not ready to proceed with the case. Phiri arrived at the Magistrate’s Court expecting the first hearing after his arrest last month, but proceedings did not begin.

Phiri's lawyer, Powell Nkhutabasa, confirmed the delay, stating that the prosecution was unprepared. The retired general faces five charges related to abuse of office and dealing in terrorist property, with investigators alleging he facilitated illegal firearms trafficking between June and July 2025.

Inside the court, Phiri appeared composed in a blue safari suit, speaking with supporters. The ACB's request for a delay means the case against the former army chief, which has drawn significant public attention, will not move forward as scheduled.

Why it mattersThe Anti-Corruption Bureau's delay means the trial of former army chief Paul Phiri on charges of abuse of office and illegal firearms trafficking will not proceed as planned.

Politics · 2026-11-13

Mkaka dares accusers to produce evidence of corruption

Former Malawi Congress Party Secretary General Eisenhower Mkaka says he will open his assets to anti-corruption investigators if they have reasonable grounds to suspect wrongdoing.

Eisenhower Mkaka, former Secretary General of the Malawi Congress Party (MCP), has challenged anyone accusing him of corruption to present evidence. He said anti-corruption agencies were free to investigate his affairs if there were reasonable grounds to suspect wrongdoing.

Mkaka said he would provide documents proving the legitimate ownership of his assets, and insisted he had never stolen from anyone or abused his position for personal gain. In a strongly worded statement, he criticised people who judge others on rumours and unverified allegations.

He compared hatred based on hearsay to a contagious disease that spreads from person to person without any firsthand experience or verified facts behind it. His remarks come as he pushes back against negative perceptions and urges critics to back their claims with evidence rather than speculation.

Why it mattersMkaka's offer to hand over asset documents gives investigators a concrete route to clear or confirm the allegations against him.

Security · 2026-11-13

Malawian pilot Andrew Meki earns helicopter rating in Lusaka

Lieutenant Meki finished rotary-wing conversion on the Enstrom 480B, moving on from his fixed-wing training in Livingstone.

Lieutenant Andrew Meki, a Malawian military pilot, has finished helicopter conversion training on the Enstrom 480B at the Zambia Air Force Helicopter Training School in Lusaka. He had first flown the SF-260 fixed-wing aircraft at the Zambia Air Force Flying Training School in Livingstone before switching to rotary wings.

The course mixed ground school with practical flying, covering aerodynamics, flight controls, hover work, take-offs and landings, navigation and standard procedures. It also drilled emergency responses, including autorotation, the manoeuvre a pilot uses to land safely after an engine failure in flight.

The Zambia Air Force said Meki's completion showed his determination to widen his knowledge. The force added that the training showed how military co-operation can build aviation skills and strengthen ties between Zambia and Malawi.

Why it mattersMeki's rating adds a qualified helicopter pilot to the Malawian military, and the training was carried out through Zambian military cooperation.
