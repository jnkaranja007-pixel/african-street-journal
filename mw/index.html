Parliament rejects MRA briefing on drug theft probe

Lawmakers demanded a more comprehensive report from the Malawi Revenue Authority on missing medicines.

Lawmakers on Malawi's Parliamentary Committee on Health rejected a presentation from the Malawi Revenue Authority (MRA) on March 11 regarding missing and unsafe medicines. The committee ordered the MRA delegation, led by Commissioner General Felix Tambulasi, to return with a more thorough report.

The MRA officials were turned away because their initial briefing was deemed insufficient by the committee members. This parliamentary investigation into the handling of medicines is ongoing.

Separately, the Human Rights Defenders Coalition (HRDC) plans protests on November 17 demanding the resignation of Energy Minister Jean Mathanga, despite the Non-Governmental Organisations Regulatory Authority deregistering the HRDC and 35 other organizations for failing to meet reporting requirements.

Why it mattersParliamentary committees are investigating the handling of medicines and the actions of government officials.

Business · 2026-11-04

Chinese steel firm eyes Malawi market

Hongwang New Materials plans to establish manufacturing operations and transfer technology to Malawi.

Chinese steel manufacturer Hongwang New Materials Technology Co., Ltd. is expanding its investment plans into Malawi to establish manufacturing operations. The company is developing factories in Malawi, alongside agreements in Tanzania and Madagascar.

Hongwang New Materials Deputy General Manager Deng Weiquan said the company is working with China’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs on a ‘Two Countries, Two Parks’ initiative. This program links an industrial park in Loudi, Hunan, with one in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, to process products for local use or export.

Economist Maury Siyasiya expressed optimism that the proposed investment could reduce Malawi’s reliance on imported steel and create opportunities for local businesses. The Hunan factory, established in September 2021, began operations in March 2023 and records annual sales of 3.5 billion yuan.

Why it mattersMalawians may benefit from reduced reliance on imported steel and new business opportunities if the investment translates into actual production.

Mourinho mourns Tenerife striker's death

Real Madrid coach Jose Mourinho called the on-pitch collision that killed Jesus de Miguel 'shocking' and 'sad'.

Tenerife striker Jesus de Miguel, 30, died in hospital on Tuesday after a collision with FC Andorra goalkeeper Jesus Owono during a Spanish second division match.

Spanish media reported the striker suffered cardiac arrest and was resuscitated before passing away the next day. Mourinho described the death as shocking and sad for everyone, sending condolences to the player's family and sympathy to Owono, who was sent off.

Mourinho, whose Real Madrid side faces AEK Athens on Wednesday, also confirmed winger Vinicius Junior will start despite a four-match domestic suspension, stating squad depth is a strength.

Politics · 2026-11-04

Malawi police charge man over fake presidential leave statement

A 39-year-old man faces charges for allegedly creating a false press release attributed to the Office of the President and Cabinet.

Malawi police arrested 39-year-old Emmanuel Soko from Kasungu District for allegedly producing a fake press release.

The document, bearing forged Office of the President and Cabinet (OPC) credentials, falsely claimed President Peter Mutharika was taking 10 days' leave. The OPC later dismissed the statement as untrue. Police suspect Soko breached sections of the Penal Code and the Electronic Transactions and Cyber Security Act.

Soko is expected to appear in court in due course. The specific charges are related to alleged breaches of law.

Why it mattersEmmanuel Soko faces legal charges for creating and distributing a false official statement.

Education · 2026-11-04

Unicef report flags Malawi child spending concerns

Malawi's government allocated billions to education and health but lacks specific child funding details.

The United Nations Children’s Emergency Fund (Unicef) 2026-27 Citizens’ Budget report reveals Malawi's government allocated billions of Kwacha to education, healthcare, water, sanitation, and social protection. However, the report does not specify how much of the national budget is directed towards children’s essential needs. This comes as the broader social protection allocation reduced from K217 billion to K123 billion.

Unicef Malawi Chief of Policy, Mathews Tasker, linked the social protection reduction to fewer contributions from development partners, raising concerns for vulnerable households. The budget includes K58 billion for primary and secondary education, K45.7 billion for school operations, and K11 billion for learning materials. The health sector received K108.3 billion for drugs and K91 billion for hospital operations, with K380 billion allocated for water and sanitation.

The report lacks separate allocations for child nutrition, early childhood development, and child protection. Chairperson for the Parliamentary Committee on Social and Community Affairs, Savel Kafwafwa, previously voiced concern over the funding gap in early childhood development. The NGO Coalition on Child Rights urged the government to increase funding for primary and secondary education administration, the P Class, the National Children’s Commission, and child protection institutions.

Why it mattersChildren in Malawi face potential underfunding for essential services like nutrition and early development due to unclear budget allocations.
