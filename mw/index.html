President Mutharika Pledges More Funding for Human Rights Commission

President Peter Mutharika has pledged to ensure the Malawi Human Rights Commission is adequately resourced to safeguard freedoms and protect vulnerable groups.

President Peter Mutharika has pledged to provide better funding for the Malawi Human Rights Commission (MHRC), describing it as a critical watchdog for safeguarding freedoms. Speaking to outgoing commissioners at Kamuzu Palace, Mutharika emphasized the commission's central role in protecting vulnerable populations and holding duty bearers accountable. He highlighted their work on disability and elderly rights, and the operationalization of the Access to Information law as key achievements.

The President stated that human rights are fundamental to democracy and development, promising to ensure the MHRC receives sufficient resources to combat abuses. Human rights groups have consistently pointed to underfunding as a major impediment to the MHRC's ability to investigate complaints, conduct civic education, and monitor detention facilities. Analysts suggest a well-funded commission is vital for upholding the constitution and Malawi's international human rights commitments.

Outgoing MHRC chairperson Chikondi Chijozi Jere commended the President for the audience and his commitment to resource allocation. She noted that significant challenges remain, particularly in addressing lengthy pre-trial detentions and fully implementing the Access to Information law, which requires additional funding for transparency and accountability. The commissioners' three-year tenure concluded on August 26, 2026.

Why it mattersThe Malawi Human Rights Commission and its ability to investigate complaints and uphold constitutional obligations are directly affected by the President's funding pledge.

Sulom Fines FCB Nyasa Big Bullets K3.5 Million for Fan Misconduct

The Super League of Malawi has penalized the club after supporters threw objects onto the pitch, causing a match stoppage.

The Super League of Malawi (Sulom) has fined FCB Nyasa Big Bullets K3.5 million for misconduct stemming from an incident during their FDH Bank Premiership match against Ekhaya Football Club on July 19, 2026. The game at Mpira Stadium was temporarily halted when fans threw bottles onto the pitch in protest of a referee's decision. Sulom's Disciplinary Committee found the club guilty, and the statement warned of potential point deductions for future similar offenses. The league body urged clubs to educate their supporters on maintaining order.

FCB Nyasa Big Bullets CEO Albert Chigoga condemned the fans' actions, stating that football does not condone such behavior and that perpetrators will be reported to the police. He expressed disappointment that the club continues to incur significant fines for disciplinary issues, emphasizing the need for supporters to reflect on their actions. Last season, the club faced multiple fines totaling K15 million for various fan-related incidents.

This latest penalty comes despite the club's off-season campaign to curb hooliganism, which included establishing disciplinary and security committees. The club's director responsible for supporters met with leadership in March to strategize, aiming for zero tolerance on indiscipline. The national supporters committee general secretary acknowledged efforts to address acts that tarnish the team's image.

Why it mattersFCB Nyasa Big Bullets is affected by a K3.5 million fine and the threat of point deductions due to fan misconduct during a league match.

Mzuzu Airport navigation blackout revealed after fatal crash

A parliamentary committee heard that Mzuzu Airport has operated without basic navigation equipment for over a decade, impacting air traffic management.

A parliamentary ad hoc committee has revealed significant structural vulnerabilities in Malawi's aviation safety following the June 10 military plane crash. Senior air traffic information officer Joseph Moyo testified that Mzuzu Airport has been operating without functional air navigation instruments for more than ten years and lacks systems for recording radio communications. Controllers currently rely on visual flight rules and pilot-reported coordinates in unradar-controlled airspace.

Moyo stated that the airport's very high frequency omnidirectional range (VOR) navigation instrument stopped working over a decade ago, forcing controllers to depend entirely on pilot declarations in non-radar airspace. This lack of verification capability was highlighted when the aircraft's reported position differed from its actual flight path before the crash. Lawmakers also questioned Moyo's professional scope regarding weather updates.

Further revelations indicated that Moyo personally relayed altered weather information to the crew, warning of five-kilometer visibility without consulting meteorological officers or updating official records. This occurred despite official reports indicating an eight-kilometer visibility range at 10 am. The committee is probing these critical gaps in air traffic management procedures and infrastructure deficits.

Why it mattersThe lack of functional navigation equipment and potential alteration of weather data at Mzuzu Airport directly impacts the safety of air travel for all passengers and crew.

Blantyre Water Board CEO Suspended Amid Inquiry

Yeremia Chihana faces allegations of misrepresentation and unauthorized appointments, marking his second suspension in five months.

The Blantyre Water Board (BWB) has suspended its chief executive officer, Yeremia Chihana, for the second time in five months. The suspension follows a disciplinary inquiry into allegations of misrepresentation, unauthorized appointments, and securing a K500 million overdraft without board approval. Chihana claims the suspension is retaliation for refusing to travel to Lilongwe with a board member to meet a contractor for a $13 million World Bank-funded project.

Board chairperson Stanely Chirwa accused Chihana of falsely claiming board endorsement for an institutional modernization program in a letter to the Secretary to the Treasury. Chirwa stated that no such meeting occurred and the board was unaware of the program. Chihana is also questioned over the appointment of Lon Zizwe as acting director of finance, a move allegedly made without board knowledge or approval.

During his previous suspension, Chihana obtained a court order to be reinstated. He asserts that his current suspension is linked to his insistence on proper procedures for a multimillion-dollar World Bank project and his refusal to comply with certain demands from powerful interests. The inquiry will examine these serious allegations against the CEO.

Why it mattersThe suspension of the Blantyre Water Board CEO impacts the utility's leadership and ongoing projects, potentially affecting service delivery and financial oversight.

Professor Dzanja Calls for Investment in Malawi's Agricultural Marketing Systems

Lilongwe University of Agriculture and Natural Resources Professor Joseph Dzanja argues that Malawi's agricultural potential is hindered by fragmented market systems.

Newly appointed Professor Joseph Dzanja of the Lilongwe University of Agriculture and Natural Resources (LUANAR) has urged significant investment in agricultural marketing systems to unlock Malawi's full potential. Speaking during his inaugural lecture, Professor Dzanja challenged policymakers and stakeholders to re-evaluate the organization and support of agricultural markets within the country. He emphasized that Malawi's agricultural challenges extend beyond mere production, encompassing the critical systems that link farmers to consumers, processors, and other participants in the agricultural value chain.

Professor Dzanja identified five key dimensions where Malawi's market system is fragmented: information, coordination, space, time, and power. He noted that farmers and other market actors frequently lack access to timely and reliable data regarding prices, demand, and market opportunities, which impedes their ability to make informed decisions. The absence of collective action among producers and stakeholders, he explained, results in limited bargaining power and an inability to achieve economies of scale for input purchases or produce sales.

The lecture also highlighted the 'space' dimension, characterized by a disconnect between rural areas and urban centers, leaving many farmers situated far from major markets, processors, and consumers. Professor Dzanja's analysis suggests that addressing these systemic issues is crucial for improving the overall agricultural landscape and ensuring that farmers can effectively connect with and benefit from market opportunities.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by fragmented market systems that limit their access to price information, reduce their bargaining power, and create a disconnect from major markets, hindering their ability to make informed decisions and benefit from their produce.
