Malawi Revenue Authority launches five-year plan to boost domestic taxes

The Malawi Revenue Authority (MRA) unveiled its 2026-31 Corporate Strategic Plan, aiming to increase the country's tax-to-GDP ratio from 16.8 percent to 20 percent.

The Malawi Revenue Authority (MRA) launched a new five-year Corporate Strategic Plan yesterday, challenging itself to sustain revenue performance and strengthen domestic resource mobilization. The Minister of Finance, Economic Planning and Decentralisation, Joseph Mwanamvekha, urged the MRA to build on recent gains by strengthening compliance, widening the tax base, and improving efficiency without solely relying on increasing taxes.

The new plan focuses on revenue mobilization and sustainability, digital transformation, data and analytics, risk-based compliance, an expanded tax base, and improved taxpayer services. MRA board chairperson MacFussy Kawawa stated the authority maintained an average revenue performance rate of about 99 percent between 2020 and 2026, collecting approximately K4.4 trillion against a K4.32 trillion target in the final year.

The MRA faces a K6.2 trillion domestic revenue collection target for the current fiscal year ending March 31, 2027. In the first quarter, the MRA collected K1.39 trillion against a target of K1.37 trillion, resulting in a K20 billion surplus. The United Nations Development Programme resident representative, Fenella Frost, emphasized that strengthening domestic resource mobilization is a necessity given tighter global financing conditions.

Why it mattersMalawian citizens and businesses are affected as the government seeks to increase domestic revenue through a wider tax base and improved efficiency, rather than solely increasing existing taxes, to fund public services and reduce reliance on external financing.

Malawi delays power projects, risking 2030 energy target

Malawi is unlikely to meet the World Bank's Mission 300 power compact goal by 2030 due to slow implementation of energy projects and a lack of specific proposals for the.

Malawi faces a significant challenge in its energy sector, with officials from the Public Private Partnership Commission (PPPC) and the Ministry of Energy confirming no specific projects have been submitted for the World Bank-supported Mission 300 initiative. This initiative aims to connect 300 million people in Africa to electricity by 2030, with the World Bank providing up to $30 billion for bankable projects.

Ministry of Energy spokesperson Joan Thaundi stated that the government is implementing the Accelerating Sustainable and Clean Energy Access Transformation In Malawi (Ascent Malawi) Project, which began on July twenty-fifth, two thousand twenty-six. This project, managed by the Ministry and Electricity Supply Corporation of Malawi (Escom), aims to connect eight hundred eleven thousand customers through home systems, with three thousand two hundred twenty-four connections already made.

Despite these efforts, the country's energy challenges stem from power shortages. The Mpatamanga project, expected to add three hundred fifty-three megawatts to the grid, remains the flagship initiative. Public Private Partnership Commission Chief Executive Officer Arthur Nanthuru noted the potential for private sector involvement, stating that the government has created an enabling environment for participation through Power Market Limited.

Why it mattersMalawian citizens and businesses are directly affected by the ongoing power shortages, which lead to interruptions in daily life and economic vulnerability.

Former Finance Minister Chithyola faces High Court over K29 billion abuse-of-office all...

Malawi's Anti-Corruption Bureau has escalated the case against former Finance Minister Simplex Chithyola Banda, moving it to the High Court for alleged misuse of K29 billion.

The abuse-of-office case against former Finance Minister Simplex Chithyola Banda has been referred to Malawi’s High Court, intensifying legal scrutiny over his tenure. The Anti-Corruption Bureau (ACB), represented by acting Director General Gabriel Chembezi, requested the transfer, which was approved by Chief Resident Magistrate Austin Banda.

At the core of the allegations is K29 billion in funds that prosecutors claim were misused. Investigators are examining decisions made by Chithyola during his time as Minister of Finance, focusing on whether he exceeded his lawful authority or violated procedures for handling public finances.

The former minister’s lawyer, George Kadzipatike, accepted the formal position that Chithyola will report to the ACB on August 30. This referral to the High Court signals a serious approach by authorities to the high-profile case involving a former Cabinet minister.

Why it mattersMalawian citizens are affected as the case scrutinizes the alleged misuse of K29 billion in public funds, impacting government accountability and resource allocation.

Malawi prisons report 702 inmates severely undernourished

A new report on food provision in 27 of Malawi's 28 prisons found 702 out of 16,237 assessed prisoners are severely undernourished, highlighting significant nutritional challenges.

Malawi's prison system is struggling to provide adequate food, with 702 prisoners identified as severely undernourished. A report covering 27 of the country's 28 prisons revealed that most inmates receive only one meal of beans and nsima daily, and some facilities have experienced periods with no food at all.

In response, the Malawi Prison Service is changing its approach to inmate feeding, moving from one meal to three meals a day. Prison Service spokesperson Steve Meke stated that nearly two-thirds of prison stations have already implemented this new arrangement, with remaining stations expected to follow once issues like cooking pot shortages are resolved.

Meke explained that this shift reflects a move from a punitive to a rehabilitative approach, aiming to treat prisoners as human beings and prepare them for reintegration into their communities. The Service seeks government funding and plans to commercialize prison industries to generate revenue and support programs that equip inmates with practical skills.

Why it mattersThe report directly affects the 702 severely undernourished prisoners by highlighting their critical need for sufficient food to maintain health and dignity.

Malawi environmental groups demand increased climate financing for vulnerable communities

Environmental sector stakeholders in Malawi are calling for authorities and development partners to mobilize more climate financing and sustainability programs to strengthen.

Malawian environmental groups are urging increased climate financing to protect vulnerable communities from climate change impacts. At a workshop on climate justice, Alfred Kambwiri of the Centre for Environmental Policy and Advocacy stated that implementation partners should prioritize empowering vulnerable communities to participate in climate resilience efforts.

Kambwiri emphasized that communities need not only to benefit from climate interventions but also to be equipped with the knowledge, resources, and capacity to develop and implement their own solutions. Tadala Chumachiyenda of NCA-DCA Malawi highlighted the significance of climate financing for countries like Malawi, which are highly vulnerable to climate change effects, noting that successful interventions depend on adequate and accessible funding.

Tarcizio Kalaundi of Trocaire Malawi acknowledged progress in unlocking climate financing and strengthening accountability but called for greater cooperation with international partners to ensure equitable distribution of funds to communities. Participants at the workshop, including representatives from the Department of Environmental Affairs, faith communities, the private sector, and the media, advocated for transparency, accountability, and community participation in managing climate resources.

Why it mattersVulnerable communities in Malawi are directly affected as their ability to develop and implement climate solutions depends on increased and equitably distributed climate financing.
