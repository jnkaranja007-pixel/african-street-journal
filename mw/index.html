Malawi Law Society questions president's power to postpone Parliament

The society argues President Mutharika lacks constitutional authority to direct the Speaker on parliamentary sittings.

The Malawi Law Society (MLS) has questioned the constitutionality of President Peter Mutharika’s directive to postpone Parliament’s Fourth Meeting of the 52nd Session. The society stated yesterday that Section 59(1) of the Constitution does not grant the President power to direct the Speaker on when Parliament should meet.

MLS chairperson Davis Njobvu and honorary secretary Francis M’mame signed a statement clarifying that Section 59(1) only allows the President, in consultation with the Speaker, to summon extraordinary meetings or prorogue Parliament. They declared the President’s directive illegal and unconstitutional, as it falls outside his granted authority.

The postponement, directed by Mutharika on October 23 2026, was intended to redirect resources towards El Niño and fuel challenges, affecting the scheduled November 9 meeting. This action follows Speaker Sameer Suleman’s establishment of 12 ad hoc committees, creating tensions between the Executive and Legislature.

Sport · 2026-10-31

Malawi U-15 finish 79th at World Cup

The team secured 15th place in Tier C after beating Rwanda 2-0 in their final match.

Malawi's Under-15 National Football team concluded the Fifa Under-15 World Cup & Festival in Baku, Azerbaijan, in 79th position overall. They secured 15th place in Tier C after defeating Rwanda 2-0 in their final match yesterday. Takutzwe Maluwe and Lameck Kamanga scored the goals for Malawi.

Coach Joseph Malizani expressed satisfaction with the team's performance, highlighting the valuable skills and experience gained by his players. The eight-a-side competition featured 189 under-15 teams from six confederations, playing on 68x47metre pitches with 40-minute fixtures.

Malawi began the tournament with three consecutive wins against Chinese Taipei, Oman, and Suriname in Group B. They later lost to Algeria and Argentina, placing third in their group and advancing to Tier C. In placement matches, they lost to Panama and Thailand before their final victory over Rwanda.

Why it mattersMalawi's Under-15 football team participated in the Fifa Under-15 World Cup & Festival, gaining experience for future senior play.

Business · 2026-10-31

Sky Energy launches 100 electric taxis in Malawi

The company also unveiled 40 charging stations and a ride-hailing app to combat fuel shortages.

Malawian energy company Sky Energy launched 100 electric taxis, 40 public charging stations, and a ride-hailing app on Friday evening in Lilongwe. The move aims to address the country's ongoing fuel shortages.

Sky Energy's managing director, Schizzo Thomson, stated that the Sky E-Ride app will allow users to book electric rides via their phones. He emphasized that replacing petrol cars requires supporting infrastructure, not just the vehicles themselves.

The company celebrated its 10th anniversary at the launch event held at Umodzi Park, Presidential Hotel. Malawi imports all its petroleum, requiring foreign currency for purchases.

Why it mattersMalawians will have a new way to travel and potentially avoid disruptions caused by fuel shortages.

Health · 2026-10-31

Malawi expands cancer treatment services

Kamuzu Central Hospital registered over 2,100 new cancer patients last year, offering chemotherapy and radiotherapy.

Malawi has increased access to specialised cancer care at the National Cancer Centre in Lilongwe, offering surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy at Kamuzu Central Hospital. Previously, patients needing these services often travelled abroad for treatment. Health Secretary Benson Chisamile stated on Thursday that the country has improved cancer services despite challenges like late diagnosis and unequal access.

The centre registered over 2,100 new patients in 2025, with more than 1,600 receiving chemotherapy and over 600 receiving radiotherapy. KCH Director General Amos Msekandiana noted that many patients still present late due to limited awareness and diagnostic delays. He highlighted a need for more oncologists, nurses, and technicians to strengthen services.

Msekandiana also called for an independent National Cancer Institute to support training, research, and policy, potentially positioning Malawi as a medical tourism destination. He stressed that cancer care extends beyond medication, requiring psychosocial support for patients coping with the physical and psychological effects of the disease.

Why it mattersMalawians with cancer can now access surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy locally, reducing the need for expensive overseas treatment.

Business · 2026-10-31

Malawi central bank raises borrowing rate

The Reserve Bank of Malawi increased the Lombard Rate to 2 percent to manage liquidity amid inflation risks.

The Reserve Bank of Malawi (RBM) has raised the Lombard Rate from 0.2 percentage points to 2 percentage points, increasing borrowing costs. This move aims to manage liquidity and combat inflation risks, which economists warn will lead to elevated prices and tough times for consumers. Businesses relying on imported materials and fuel will face higher operational costs, potentially limiting expansion and new ventures.

Economist Velli Nyirongo stated that while the rate hike may help contain inflation, its direct impact will be limited due to external factors like high fuel prices and imported costs. He stressed the need for government coordination in managing food supply, supporting agriculture, and maintaining fiscal discipline to address inflation effectively. The Monetary Policy Committee noted that annual broad money growth slowed to 19.7 percent in the third quarter of 2026.

Banking system liquidity tightened, with excess reserves averaging K82.4 billion per day, down from K191.3 billion. However, recourse to the Lombard facility rose to an average of K72.3 billion daily, posing a risk to money supply growth. Inflation dropped to 20.1 percent in the third quarter from 22.9 percent in the previous period.

Why it mattersMalawians will face higher prices and increased costs for borrowing money due to the central bank's policy change.
