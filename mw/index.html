Parliament interviews candidates for judiciary watchdog

Six shortlisted individuals are being vetted by lawmakers to fill positions on a new commission intended to oversee judicial conduct.

Parliament's Public Appointments Committee (PAC) has begun interviewing candidates for the Independent Complaints Commission (ICC), a body established to monitor the behavior of judges and court staff. Six individuals were selected from an initial pool of 23 applicants for the commissioner roles.

Four of the six shortlisted candidates appeared for interviews on the first day. A fifth candidate is scheduled to be interviewed later, while the sixth person did not attend, leaving the committee uncertain about their withdrawal or other circumstances. Committee vice-chairperson Joshua Malango confirmed these details.

The Malawi Law Society (MLS) has welcomed the progress, with chairperson Davis Njobvu stating that the interviews represent a step forward in implementing judicial reforms. Reform advocates are closely observing the appointment process for the new watchdog.

Why it mattersMembers of parliament are vetting candidates for a new commission that will oversee judges and court officers, impacting the accountability of the judiciary.

Man dies after 28-year court battle for unpaid salary

Rodwell Zimba pursued his former employer, the government, for 28 years over unpaid salary and benefits before his death.

Rodwell Zimba, who pursued a 28-year court case against the government for unpaid salary and benefits, has died without resolution. His son, Gerald Zimba, confirmed his father's death, stating his condition worsened on Tuesday while en route to the hospital. Zimba had been seeking payment of his withheld salary, allowances, pension, and gratuity since initiating the case after Malawi's transition to multiparty democracy.

Zimba, formerly an under-secretary in the Ministry of Sports, Arts and Culture, was arrested during the rule of former president Hastings Kamuzu Banda on accusations of tribalism. Following his detention, he turned to the courts after Malawi transitioned to multiparty democracy to reclaim financial benefits he believed were owed. However, the case remained unresolved for 28 years, concluding only with Zimba's death.

His death leaves the long-standing claim unresolved, raising questions about whether his family can continue the legal pursuit through his estate. The outcome will depend on the legal status of the proceedings and the laws governing claims after a claimant's death. A statement from the source expressed shame and called for the current government to review the case and prevent future occurrences.

Why it mattersRodwell Zimba and potentially his family are affected by the government's failure to resolve his unpaid salary and benefits claim for 28 years.

El Niño threatens Malawi with deeper economic crisis

Economists warn that inadequate government financing for El Niño preparedness could worsen the country's economic outlook.

Malawi faces significant economic damage if the government fails to adequately fund and implement measures against a severe El Niño, economists have warned. Marvin Banda, an economist, cautioned that a projected GDP impact of 1-7 percent signals potential severe economic damage, not an automatic recession. He stressed that interventions must precede crop losses and food shortages, stating that Malawi's vulnerability lies in its slow response, which risks occurring after damage is done.

As of September 2026, Malawi had mobilized K163.7 billion against the K488.5 billion needed for the El Niño Preparedness Plan, leaving a K324.8 billion shortfall. This lack of funding raises concerns about the country's ability to implement preventive measures effectively and at the necessary speed. While the government's preparedness plan is commended, economists fear inadequate financing could undermine its success.

Economist Velli Nyirongo highlighted Malawi's high dependence on rain-fed agriculture, classifying the potential El Niño as a major economic risk. This situation could force increased government spending on food aid and disaster response, straining an already limited fiscal space. Nyirongo called for immediate, well-funded action, including investments in irrigation, climate-smart agriculture, and timely access to affordable farm inputs.

Why it mattersMalawi's economy is threatened by potential agricultural catastrophe and increased disaster spending due to El Niño, exacerbated by insufficient government funding for preparedness measures.

Minister tells off parliament speaker over corruption claims

Information Minister Shadric Namalomba has demanded evidence from Parliament Speaker Sameer Suleman regarding alleged corruption within the government.

Information Minister Shadric Namalomba has challenged Parliament Speaker Sameer Suleman to provide proof of corruption within the government, stating that no one is above the law. Namalomba issued a letter demanding that Suleman present evidence within seven days, asserting that President Peter Mutharika will not shield any corrupt officials. The minister urged those with evidence of graft to report it to the Anti-Corruption Bureau (ACB) or the police, rather than making public accusations.

Namalomba's remarks appear to contradict recent events where the government blocked a proposal by Suleman to establish special committees to investigate the operations of some government departments. This move by the speaker was seen by some as an attempt to probe government functions, which officials like Namalomba have described as an overreach by parliament. The minister insists that the government is not protecting any thieves and that all allegations should be substantiated with concrete evidence.

Despite the minister's call for formal evidence, many Malawians have publicly sided with Suleman. A recent online poll conducted by the publication showed that over 3,500 out of approximately 5,000 participants supported the speaker's stance on investigating alleged corruption. This public sentiment suggests a disconnect between the government's official position and the people's perception of widespread corruption within state institutions.

Why it mattersMalawian citizens are affected by the government's stance on corruption allegations, as it impacts their trust in public institutions and the accountability of their leaders.

Viyuyi and Kondowe share top scorer lead

Standard Bank Blue Eagles striker Tonic Viyuyi has drawn level with FCB Nyasa Bullets striker Ephraim Kondowe on 10 goals each in the FDH Bank Premiership.

Tonic Viyuyi scored twice on Sunday to reach 10 goals for the season, matching FCB Nyasa Bullets' Ephraim Kondowe who also has 10 goals. Kondowe had led the league with nine goals after the first round and added one more in his team's second-round opener. Viyuyi's brace in Eagles' 3-0 win over Chitipa United brought him level.

Kondowe did not score in Bullets' 3-0 victory against Mafco FC on the same afternoon Viyuyi reached his tally. Marco Chiwaya of Goshen City Dedza Dynamos is currently third in the scoring charts with six goals, while six other players are tied on five goals.

Football analyst Charles Nyirenda stated that the race is still open but considers Kondowe and Viyuyi the frontrunners. He noted that the four-goal gap between the leading pair and the third-placed player suggests the competition will likely remain between the top two if they maintain their current form.
