US investors target Malawi's minerals with new Chamber of Commerce

The American Chamber of Commerce in Malawi (AmCham) launched in Blantyre, aiming to strengthen commercial ties and increase US investment in the country's mining sector.

The American Chamber of Commerce in Malawi (AmCham) officially launched in Blantyre on Tuesday, with US Embassy Chargé d’Affaires Melania Arreaga stating that American investors are targeting Malawi’s mineral resources. The new chamber seeks to boost America’s supply chains by utilizing Malawi’s critical minerals, thereby reducing reliance on other nations for resources essential to economic and security needs.

Arreaga anticipates growing US investment in Malawi’s mining sector, with AmCham serving as a key link for American companies to these opportunities. Nico Holdings plc managing director Vizenge Kumwenda described AmCham as a vital platform connecting Malawi to global markets, particularly the US, offering opportunities for export, technology, skills, and capital.

Kumwenda highlighted tourism, agriculture, and newly discovered critical minerals as major opportunities, but cautioned that Malawi must improve regulation, governance, and investor confidence. He also called for a more effective judicial system to protect investors' rights, faster court processes, and stronger anti-corruption measures to enhance the investment climate.

Why it mattersMalawian businesses and the government are affected by the potential for increased foreign investment, new market access, and the need to improve regulatory frameworks.

Unima Vice-Chancellor urges graduates to combat corruption

Professor Samson Sajidu, Vice-Chancellor of the University of Malawi, called on over two thousand new graduates to be problem-solvers and uphold integrity to advance national.

The University of Malawi (Unima) held its second graduation ceremony of two thousand and twenty-six yesterday in Zomba, where its Vice-Chancellor, Professor Samson Sajidu, encouraged graduates to be sources of solutions and not perpetrators of problems. He emphasized that education with character builds trust in society and urged them to connect with and uplift others.

Professor Sajidu also warned the graduates about the dangers of corruption, stating that it hinders national development and disproportionately affects vulnerable people. He advised them to choose honesty over shortcuts and integrity over personal gain, and to repay student loans to benefit future students.

Unima Council chairperson Benedicto Malunga supported these statements, stressing that laziness and corruption impede national progress. He challenged the graduates to be hardworking and innovative in their professional roles, noting that the value of education is measured by contributions to society. Of the graduating class, forty-two percent were females.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects the over two thousand graduates who are entering the workforce and are being called upon to contribute to Malawi's development and combat corruption.

LUANAR reaffirms agribusiness hub role for Malawi's agricultural economy

The Lilongwe University of Agriculture and Natural Resources aims to unlock greater value from Malawi's agricultural economy through research, youth investment, and strategic.

The Lilongwe University of Agriculture and Natural Resources (LUANAR) has reaffirmed its commitment to becoming a national and global hub for agribusiness transformation in Malawi. This move is intended to help the country extract more value from its agricultural sector, focusing on improving markets, rural development, and the commercialization of farming.

LUANAR Council Chairperson Professor James Seyani stated that the institution remains dedicated to investing in young people and equipping them with research skills to address agricultural challenges. He highlighted the importance of partnerships, including those supported by IFAD, in guiding the university's direction and empowering young researchers to create practical solutions for agricultural earnings.

LUANAR Vice Chancellor Professor Emmanuel Kaunda emphasized the university's core mandate to generate knowledge that transforms society. He noted that academic work on agricultural markets and rural development is crucial for moving Malawian farmers beyond subsistence production and building stronger connections between producers, markets, and consumers.

Why it mattersMalawian farmers and the national economy are affected by LUANAR's efforts to commercialize farming and improve agricultural markets, potentially increasing earnings and moving beyond subsistence production.

Japanese Businesses Explore Malawi Investment Opportunities

Six Japanese companies are engaging with local counterparts and authorities in Malawi to identify investment prospects in agriculture, manufacturing, and processing.

Japanese entrepreneurs have expressed interest in investing across various sectors of Malawi's economy, as Lilongwe and Tokyo work to enhance trade and investment ties. About six Japanese companies are currently in the country, meeting with local businesses, operators, and government officials to explore opportunities in agriculture, manufacturing, and processing.

Japanese Ambassador to Malawi Naito Yasushi hosted a networking event in Lilongwe for the visiting Japanese and local businesses. Industrialisation, Business, Trade and Tourism Minister Simon Itaye, along with other Cabinet ministers, attended the event. Ambassador Yasushi affirmed his office's commitment to strengthening development cooperation, including in infrastructure, energy, education, agriculture, manufacturing, and social sectors.

Minister Itaye highlighted agriculture, mining, tourism, and manufacturing as priority investment areas, inviting Japanese investors to explore these opportunities, including in food supply chain management, logistics, and technology transfer. The Malawi Confederation of Chambers of Commerce and Industry stated that the private sector is ready to partner with Japanese counterparts to achieve growth goals.

Why it mattersMalawian businesses and workers in agriculture, manufacturing, and processing sectors could see new investment and job creation from Japanese companies.

Malawi Ministry of Health reveals 79 percent of diabetes cases undiagnosed

The Ministry of Health disclosed that nearly 79 percent of diabetes cases nationwide remain undiagnosed, prompting efforts to expand screening and treatment access, especially.

Malawi's Ministry of Health has revealed that 79 percent of diabetes cases across the country are currently undiagnosed, according to Public Relations Officer Benedicto Mbewe. This includes an estimated 3,126 children and adolescents aged 0 to 19 living with Type 1 diabetes, a figure that Mbewe warned could be significantly higher.

The Ministry is taking steps to decentralise diabetes services and bring essential medical supplies closer to patients. However, clinical audits show that only 16 percent of children achieve reasonable blood sugar control, with a median diagnosis age of 10 years and a median glycated haemoglobin level of 11.4 percent, which is dangerously high.

Mbewe confirmed that Malawi has only one specialised paediatric endocrinologist for the entire country, contributing to persistent shortages of insulin, syringes, and testing strips at public hospitals. These shortages are attributed to inadequate government funding, supply chain challenges, and heavy reliance on donor support, impacting thousands of young patients needing specialist care.

Why it mattersThousands of Malawian children and adults are living with undiagnosed or poorly managed diabetes due to a lack of resources and specialists, leading to severe health complications and deaths.
