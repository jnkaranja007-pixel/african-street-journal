Malawi fuel black market fuels deadly fires

Fuel shortages in Malawi's formal supply chain are driving a dangerous black market trade that has led to a vendor's death.

Malawi's fuel crisis has worsened, with long queues at filling stations and widespread shortages. This scarcity is pushing transport costs higher and disrupting businesses. Meanwhile, fuel is readily available on the black market at two to three times the official pump price, raising questions about diversion and regulatory failures.

A fuel vendor died last Monday in Kanengo, Lilongwe, after his motorcycle, carrying fuel in 20-litre containers, crashed during a police pursuit. His companion suffered serious burns in the incident. This tragedy highlights the public safety emergency created by the illegal fuel trade, beyond economic damage.

Civil society groups like the Human Rights Consultative Committee (HRCC) have spoken out about the crisis. Authorities, including the Malawi Energy Regulatory Authority (Mera) and the Malawi Police Service, are urged to investigate fuel diversion and enforce laws against illegal trading. No profit from this trade is worth a human life.

Why it mattersA fuel vendor died and his companion was burned in a fire during a police pursuit related to illegal fuel trading.

Malawi president in good health, government says

Information Minister Shadric Namalomba confirmed President Mutharika is on a private trip in South Africa.

Malawi’s government has confirmed President Peter Mutharika is in good health and on a private trip in South Africa. Minister of Information and Communications Technology Dr Shadric Namalomba issued a statement on Friday, October 23, 2026, addressing public speculation about the president’s whereabouts.

The statement assured Malawians that the president is enjoying his time abroad and that there is no cause for concern. Namalomba noted that the trip has taken slightly longer than usual, which he said has created room for speculation. The government stated that the president would return home when the time is right and that any health issues would be communicated through official channels.

The government did not provide a specific date for President Mutharika’s return. The statement was issued following public questions about his location since his departure.

Why it mattersMalawians were concerned about the whereabouts and health of their president, Peter Mutharika.

Malawi chief dies in India after treatment

Inkosi Chindi, 49, passed away on October 23, 2026, while receiving medical care arranged by the Malawi Government.

Inkosi Chindi of Mzimba died in India on Friday, October 23, 2026, where he had traveled for medical treatment facilitated by the Malawi Government. The traditional leader, born May 5, 1977, was 49 years old at the time of his death. His passing was confirmed by Mmbelwa District Council District Commissioner Emmanuel Bulukutu and Ngoni Chiefs Council Secretary Inkosi Jalavikuwa.

Inkosi Jalavikuwa stated that details surrounding the chief's illness and death were not disclosed but confirmed he died while receiving treatment. Burial arrangements will be announced once the process of repatriating the chief's remains to Malawi is complete. His death is considered a significant loss for the traditional leadership in Mzimba.

The Ngoni Chiefs Council and relevant authorities are expected to provide further details on the repatriation process and the funeral programme. The nation awaits official communication regarding arrangements to honour the late traditional leader.

Why it mattersThe death of Inkosi Chindi affects the traditional leadership and communities under his influence in Mzimba.

vandals cut water at Malawi's largest hospital

Queen Elizabeth Central Hospital suspended activities as dry taps hit patients and staff during 35°C heat.

Patients and guardians at Queen Elizabeth Central Hospital (QECH) in Blantyre scrambled for water on Friday as temperatures reached 35°C. The hospital's taps ran dry due to vandals damaging Blantyre Water Board (BWB) pipelines, forcing a reduction in pumping capacity.

The water shortage led QECH, Malawi's largest referral hospital, to suspend some activities. Director General Professor Patrick Kamalo confirmed the hospital had run out of water, impacting patients, guardians, and health workers struggling with hygiene and the heat. Some patients were reportedly turned away.

One guardian, Lucia Jasi, was instructed by nurses to fetch water from her home borehole, noting that patients from distant districts could not do the same. Another guardian, Maureen Kagona, said she had not bathed due to the shortage. Health sciences students also struggled to concentrate while searching for drinking water.

Why it mattersPatients and staff at Malawi's largest hospital faced severe hygiene and comfort issues due to a lack of water during extreme heat.

Malawi withdraws ICC judicial candidate

Tujilane Chizumila was nominated in March but removed from the race before December elections.

Malawi has withdrawn its candidate, Ms Tujilane Chizumila, from the running for a position as a judge at the International Criminal Court. The Embassy of the Republic of Malawi announced the decision on 20 October 2026, removing the country from contention in the court’s ninth election of judges. The ICC’s governing body, the Assembly of States Parties, recorded the withdrawal in official documents on the same day.

Ms Chizumila was nominated by the Government of Malawi in a communication dated 27 March 2026. The notice of withdrawal provided no specific reason for the decision. Judges at the ICC, which is based in The Hague, are elected by member states for nine-year terms.

The election of new judges is scheduled to occur during the twenty-fifth session of the Assembly of States Parties in New York. This session is set to run from 7 to 17 December 2026. The ICC prosecutes genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes, and the crime of aggression.

Why it mattersMalawi will not have a representative on the International Criminal Court after withdrawing its nominated candidate.
