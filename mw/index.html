Malawi FA proposes K2.8 billion for youth football

The Football Association of Malawi's 2027 budget includes a significant allocation to boost grassroots development and talent identification.

The Football Association of Malawi (FAM) has proposed allocating K2.8 billion to youth football in its 2027 budget, aiming to strengthen the sport's foundations and improve development pathways. The National Youth Football Association (NYFA) expects to receive the largest share of this funding, which chairperson Tiyenkhu Chavula stated is essential for developing competitive national teams.

Young Malawian footballers currently rely on grassroots competitions like the Nthanda Under-14 League and M’mera Mpoyamba Under-16 League, but face challenges with equipment, coaching, and organised play. FAM president Fleetwood Haiya presented the proposed K21.8 billion total budget, with K21.2 billion for expenditure, during a consultation meeting in Lilongwe.

This proposed K2.8 billion for youth football will be submitted to the FAM General Assembly for approval next month. The funding's ultimate impact on young players across Malawi will depend on its distribution and the opportunities created for talent progression.

Why it mattersYoung Malawian footballers could gain access to better coaching, equipment, and talent identification programs through the proposed K2.8 billion youth football budget.

Health · 2026-11-09

Malawi HIV infections drop by three-quarters

New infections fell from 60,000 in 2010 to 14,000 in 2025, health officials announced.

New HIV infections in Malawi have fallen by 76 per cent, health officials announced on Friday. The National AIDS Commission (NAC) reported that annual new infections decreased from 60,000 in 2010 to 14,000 in 2025. This achievement reflects years of prevention, testing, and treatment efforts.

NAC Monitoring and Evaluation Officer Shadreck Sulani presented the figures in Lilongwe, stating that the progress demonstrates the impact of implemented interventions. AIDS-related deaths also dropped from 36,000 to 13,000, and mother-to-child transmission fell from 40 per cent to 6 per cent. Officials aim to reduce new infections to 6,000 by 2030.

Sulani noted that continued investment and public awareness remain critical for controlling the epidemic. The NAC's data shows Malawi is making strides in eliminating AIDS as a public health threat.

Why it mattersMalawians are directly affected by the significant reduction in new HIV infections and AIDS-related deaths.

Politics · 2026-11-09

HRDC challenges NGO regulator in court

The Human Rights Defenders Coalition seeks to halt its deregistration by NGORA at a High Court hearing on November 12.

Malawi’s NGO Regulatory Authority (NGORA) and the Human Rights Defenders Coalition (HRDC) will face off in the High Court in Lilongwe on November 12, 2026. HRDC is seeking a judicial review and an injunction to reverse NGORA's decision to deregister the coalition.

NGORA, the body that regulates NGOs in Malawi, stated it deregistered HRDC due to repeated breaches of regulatory requirements. HRDC has called the decision "unreasonable" and is challenging it in court. NGORA spokesperson Laston Chinkudzu confirmed the regulator is prepared to defend its action.

The court will hold an inter-parte hearing, allowing both NGORA and HRDC to present their arguments. HRDC had initially requested an ex-parte hearing, which would have excluded the regulator from the proceedings.

Why it mattersThe Human Rights Defenders Coalition, a civil society group, is directly affected by the NGO Regulatory Authority's decision to deregister it.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Muwua declares bid for DPP leadership

The 33-year-old PhD scholar aims to contest for the party's presidential nomination in 2030.

Chriss Connex Muhuwa, 33, has announced his intention to seek the Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) presidential nomination for the 2030 elections. Muhuwa, a PhD scholar in renewable energy, stated his ambition to succeed Professor Arthur Peter Mutharika. He has prepared a program to transform Malawi’s economy and strengthen public institutions.

Muwuwa’s early experiences in informal trade, including selling used clothing, exposed him to the economic difficulties faced by ordinary Malawians, particularly farmers and small-scale entrepreneurs. He previously served as Deputy National Youth Director of the DPP and as a Special Assistant on Policy Analysis in the Office of the Vice President. His academic background includes research in energy materials.

His proposed agenda focuses on reliable electricity, industrialisation, job creation, and strengthening public institutions. Muhuwa aims to build support through policies, preparation, and integrity, believing leadership should be judged by ideas, accountability, and results. He views Mutharika as a political mentor.

Why it mattersChriss Connex Muhuwa, a 33-year-old politician, is seeking to lead the Democratic Progressive Party in the 2030 elections, which will affect the country's political direction.

Health · 2026-11-09

Malawi health recruitment faces scrutiny over staffing gaps

Stakeholders question if the exercise addresses Malawi’s persistent shortage of skilled medical personnel.

The Malawian Ministry of Health and Sanitation announced walk-in interviews for health workers from November 20–22, 2026, with support from the Global Fund. The recruitment drive aims to fill positions across 14 cadres of health workers but has drawn criticism for a lack of detail on vacancy numbers and deployment plans.

Seasoned nurse-midwife Dorothy Ngoma stated that the recruitment announcement does not disclose the number of vacancies or deployment strategies, making it hard to assess if it meets actual healthcare needs. The Human Resources for Health Coalition has called for over 14,400 professionals, while the 2026/27 budget allows for nearly 6,000, raising questions about the impact of the current exercise.

Malawi Health Equity Network Executive Director George Jobe added that recruitment should offer career progression for existing staff who have upgraded qualifications. He proposed that serving health workers should have a fair chance at higher positions matching their new skills, rather than new entrants taking senior roles.

Why it mattersMalawians seeking healthcare may face continued service delivery issues due to a recruitment process that lacks transparency on specific needs and deployment.
