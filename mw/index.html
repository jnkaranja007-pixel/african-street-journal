Malawi Aviation Deficiencies Revealed After Vice President's Plane Crash

A parliamentary inquiry heard testimony that Malawi's aviation infrastructure suffers from long-standing structural deficiencies, risking failure in an upcoming international.

An inquiry into the June 10, 2024 plane crash that killed Vice-President Saulos Chilima and eight others has revealed significant structural deficiencies in Malawi's aviation infrastructure. Fredrick Chisepeya, director of Aerodromes and Air Navigation Standards at the Civil Aviation Authority of Malawi, told a parliamentary committee that the country risks failing an upcoming International Civil Aviation Organisation audit unless urgent investment is made in safety and security.

Testimony indicated that Malawi possesses only one secondary flight surveillance radar, located at Kamuzu International Airport, which has diminished coverage at lower altitudes. This gap meant the aircraft carrying the Vice-President was outside radar coverage when it crashed. Furthermore, Mzuzu Airport, the intended destination, operated at a safety level of three on a ten-point scale, well below the required six for medium-sized aircraft, with deficiencies in fire response and runway conditions.

Compounding these issues, Mzuzu Airport was operating without a valid license at the time of the crash, only receiving one in 2025 with attached conditions. The Department of Climate Change and Meteorological Services also stated that no airport weather forecaster was deployed to Mzuzu Airport on the day of the flight, as the department was unaware of the planned flight due to Mzuzu Airport not receiving commercial flights at the time. Mandatory weather forecast checks were not conducted for the flight.

Why it mattersThe parliamentary inquiry's findings directly impact the safety and reliability of air travel within Malawi, affecting all passengers and the operational capacity of aviation services.

Alliance Demands Increased Investment for Non-Communicable Disease Care

The Non-Communicable Diseases Alliance Malawi is calling for sustained funding and improved services to address the growing burden of diseases like cancer and diabetes.

The Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD) Alliance Malawi has urged for continuous investment in essential services to enhance treatment access for NCDs across the country. This call comes ahead of the Global Week for Action on NCDs 2026, an event focused on tangible improvements in healthcare delivery. NCDs, encompassing conditions such as cardiovascular diseases, cancers, diabetes, and chronic respiratory illnesses, are responsible for approximately 40 percent of all deaths in Malawi.

The alliance specifically requested accelerated action to ensure World Health Organization (WHO) PEN and PEN-Plus strategies are well-resourced, functional, and accessible, particularly in rural areas. These WHO strategies aim to manage NCDs in low-resource settings. The call emphasizes the need for investment in healthcare workers, medicines, diagnostics, screening, referral systems, and health information systems, alongside efforts to reduce risk factors like tobacco use, unhealthy diets, and inactivity.

Public health advocate Maziko Matemba stated that the Global Week for Action serves as a vital platform to move from political promises to active healthcare delivery. The event aims to bridge global commitments with local realities, seeking more consistent access to affordable medicines, reliable diagnostic tools, and comprehensive care networks. Participants will endorse the Malawi GW4A NCD Charter 2026, which will be presented to the Parliamentary Committee on Health to advocate for stronger political leadership and accountability in addressing NCDs.

Why it mattersPeople living with non-communicable diseases in Malawi are affected by the call for sustained investment in healthcare services, which aims to improve their access to treatment and care.

Ministry Awaits Funding for Bingu Stadium Fence Repairs

Security lapses at Bingu National Stadium persist due to unaddressed perimeter fence vulnerabilities, as the Ministry of Youth, Sports and Culture seeks funding for repairs.

The Ministry of Youth, Sports and Culture has acknowledged ongoing security issues at Bingu National Stadium, citing a lack of funding to repair the perimeter fence. Gaping holes in the fence have allowed fans to enter the venue without tickets, contributing to poor gate management. The ministry has proposed building a brick fence in vulnerable sections to address these security gaps.

These security concerns were highlighted during the recent Airtel Top 8 final between Mighty Wanderers and Ekhaya, which generated K87 million in gate revenue. Despite the presence of security personnel, some fans gained unauthorized entry by exploiting weak points in the fence. Long queues and a mismatch between ticket sales and available security staff exacerbated the problem, making it easier for gatecrashers to slip through.

The stadium is scheduled to host a Caf Champions League match between Mighty Wanderers and Simba SC this Sunday, adding urgency to the security situation. Mighty Wanderers has pledged to implement enhanced security measures for the upcoming continental fixture. The ministry continues to await funding to implement the proposed fence repairs and address the stadium's security vulnerabilities.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the stadium's security lapses and poor gate management could deter future events and impact revenue generation.

Power outages disrupt Rumphi Hospital services

Frequent blackouts at the district's referral hospital are impacting patient care and health worker operations.

Patients at Rumphi District Hospital are experiencing disruptions to essential health services due to persistent power blackouts. The district's key referral facility lacks a reliable backup power system, leading to prolonged electricity interruptions that negatively affect operations and patient care. Health workers face significant challenges as the entire hospital is plunged into darkness when the national power grid fails, hindering their ability to perform duties and potentially risking patient lives.

The absence of a dependable alternative electricity source means hospital operations are severely impacted by national grid failures. These disruptions affect critical services requiring continuous power, including patient care, lighting, medical equipment operation, and laboratory services. The Rumphi District Council is considering engaging local Members of Parliament to find solutions for a dependable backup power supply.

Concerns are mounting over the hospital's vulnerability to power outages, with civil society groups urging the council to prioritize installing a reliable backup system. Such an investment is crucial to protect patients, support health workers, and ensure vital services remain operational. Reliable electricity is essential for adequate lighting, operating medical equipment, preserving medicines, and ensuring safe patient care, especially during emergencies and at night.

Why it mattersPatients at Rumphi District Hospital are affected by power blackouts which disrupt essential health services and put lives at risk.
