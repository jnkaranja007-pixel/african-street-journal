National Bank of Malawi launches electric vehicle

The K74 million vehicle is part of the bank's push for environmental, social, and governance principles.

National Bank of Malawi (NBM) plc has introduced an electric vehicle costing K74 million as part of its commitment to environmental, social, and governance (ESG) principles and to encourage green financing.

SME Consultant Micheal Chikapa stated on Tuesday that the vehicle signifies NBM's aspiration to lead Malawi's transition to a greener economy and integrate sustainability into its operations.

The bank's Green Finance Product provides accessible credit for renewable energy solutions through collaborations with Sky Energy Africa and Energy 265, Chikapa added.

Why it mattersMalawi's economy is affected as the National Bank of Malawi launches an electric vehicle to promote green financing and sustainability.

Malawi skips Russia-Africa summit amid recruitment concerns

Activists criticize government absence from Moscow meeting, citing deceptive recruitment of youth for war.

Malawi did not send a presidential delegation to the third Russia-Africa Summit held on October 28-29, prompting criticism from political and human rights campaigners. The absence raised questions about the country's diplomatic priorities and its stance on the recruitment of African youth into Russia's war efforts. While 22 African presidents attended, Malawi was among the 48 listed delegations, though only senior government representatives were present.

Authorities cited President Peter Mutharika's private visit to South Africa as justification for the low-level representation, despite campaigners arguing a cabinet minister could have attended. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs had initially stated Malawi would not send a delegation without providing an official explanation. This decision occurred amidst austerity measures and travel restrictions imposed by the government to address economic challenges.

Concerns over deceptive recruitment schemes intensified in November 2025 after reports of young Malawian women being trapped in Alabuga, a Russian military-industrial zone. Political scholar Jani Gray Kasunda stated Malawi should have used the summit to raise these issues, emphasizing the need for answers on government actions. Ghana, however, secured a commitment from Moscow to stop recruiting its nationals through deceptive offers.

Malawi NGOs challenge deregistration

Civil society groups argue the regulator overstepped by cancelling registrations without prior warnings or corrective actions.

A coalition of 36 Malawian civil society organisations is challenging their deregistration by the Non-Governmental Organisations Regulatory Authority (NGORA). Groups including the National Advocacy Platform and the NGO Gender Coordination Network argue that NGORA has overreached its powers and should reverse the decision.

The organisations stated they support accountability but questioned if deregistration was a proportionate response to alleged violations. They contend that the NGO Act allows for warnings and corrective measures before cancelling registration, and that NGORA should consider the circumstances of each case.

The dispute centres on how regulation should be enforced, with the coalition arguing the law aims to foster an environment for effective operation, not just police the sector. They believe authorities should have discretion in imposing penalties for compliance failures.

Why it mattersMalawian civil society organisations are directly affected by the deregistration of 36 NGOs.

Malawi private schools lose leader

Henry S. Kumwenda, General Secretary of the Private Schools Association of Malawi (PRISAM), died on Monday.

Henry S. Kumwenda, the General Secretary of the Private Schools Association of Malawi (PRISAM), has died. Kumwenda passed away on Monday, leaving a void in the leadership of the association he helped to manage. His death was announced by PRISAM president Ernest Kaonga.

Kumwenda was instrumental in coordinating programmes and supporting member schools within PRISAM. He was also a champion for private education in Malawi. His role involved ensuring the smooth operation of the association and advocating for its members.

PRISAM president Ernest Kaonga expressed his condolences following Kumwenda's death. The association is now facing a leadership gap due to the loss of its General Secretary.

Malawi court to decide bishop's bail on November 20

Prosecutors do not oppose bail for Bishop Medram Felix Chirwa in a murder case but seek strict conditions.

The High Court’s Criminal Division in Lilongwe will rule on November 20 whether Bishop Medram Felix Chirwa of the Fountain of Victory Church can be granted bail. Chirwa is involved in a murder case concerning FDH Bank employee Tathokoza Salimu.

Prosecutors, represented by lawyer Hassan Ayami, told the court on Monday that they do not oppose bail but requested stringent conditions. These include reporting to the DPP Criminal Registry every two weeks, a property bond of K300 million, two sureties of K20 million each, surrendering travel documents, and court approval for any travel outside Malawi.

Chirwa’s lawyer, Joseph Chiume, argued that fortnightly reporting is an unnecessary burden and proposed monthly reporting instead. Justice Ngwira Mwakibinga reserved his ruling after hearing arguments from both sides.
