Agriculture · 2026-11-07

Malawi farmers await fertiliser as planting season nears

Prospective beneficiaries in the Southern Region report no NPK and UREA, risking crop yields.

Malawian farmers in the Southern Region are still waiting for NPK and UREA fertiliser for the Farm Input Subsidy Programme (FISP) as the planting season approaches. Rains for planting maize could arrive as early as next week, but basal fertiliser is most effective when applied within seven days of planting.

The Fertiliser Suppliers Association of Malawi reports available fertiliser stocks stand at approximately 148,000 metric tonnes against annual national requirements of 448,000 tonnes, a shortfall of about 300,000 tonnes. Farmers are concerned about what they will plant with if the inputs do not arrive on time.

Despite a shift in the financial year to April 1 for better planning, the timely delivery of essential inputs remains a challenge. Forecasts of possible El Niño conditions and erratic rainfall add to the urgency for farmers who have been advised to diversify crops.

Why it mattersFarmers in Malawi face potential crop failure due to delays in receiving essential fertiliser inputs needed for the upcoming planting season.

Politics · 2026-11-07

Malawi Electoral Commission recognised for voter campaign

The 'Weaving the Web of Truth' campaign was selected as a top case study by the UN Development Programme.

Malawi's voter education campaign has earned international recognition, with the Catholic Commission for Justice and Peace (CCJP) praising the Malawi Electoral Commission (MEC) for building public confidence ahead of the 2025 General Election. MEC's 'Weaving the Web of Truth' campaign was selected as one of 20 Spotlight case studies in the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) Atlas of Innovation in Public Communication, from 173 applications submitted by 50 countries.

The campaign used radio, television, SMS, social media, and community networks to spread verified voter information in different languages, working with civil society organisations to monitor rumours and respond to misinformation. Over 9,450 radio and television slots were used, and voter turnout increased to 76.4 percent in the September 16, 2025 General Election from 64.8 percent in the 2020 Fresh Presidential Election.

CCJP National Coordinator Lewis Msiyadungu said the recognition showed the value of cooperation among electoral stakeholders in protecting election credibility. MEC has dedicated the recognition to its staff, civil society organisations, media houses, and community partners, stating their contribution was key to the campaign’s success.

Why it mattersMalawians are directly affected by voter education campaigns that aim to increase public confidence and participation in elections.

Sport · 2026-11-07

Ekhaya FC builds fan loyalty beyond match days

The club engages students and visits hospitals to foster community ties and financial sustainability.

Ekhaya FC is actively building relationships with supporters beyond football matches to strengthen its fan base and financial future. Many football clubs in Malawi neglect fan engagement, but Ekhaya FC uses community outreach and direct interaction. This strategy aims to create a sense of belonging and loyalty for long-term financial health.

Ekhaya FC chief executive officer Thandose Mhango stated the club's goal is a meaningful year-round presence in the community. Recent activities included engagement with students at Malawi University of Business and Applied Sciences and a visit to Queen Elizabeth Central Hospital. The club also enhanced its media capacity and digital platforms to connect with younger audiences and offer merchandise.

This approach has seen Ekhaya FC record K23.85 million in gate revenue for the first round of the FDH Bank Premiership season, surpassing established teams. Mighty Wanderers led earnings with K93.73 million, followed by FCB Nyasa Big Bullets at K88.20 million. Ekhaya FC's strategy aims to develop a loyal supporter community that grows with the club.

Why it mattersFootball fans in Malawi are affected by clubs' varying efforts to build loyalty and financial stability.

Education · 2026-11-07

Malawi primary school classes cleaned of used condoms

Mponela 2 Primary School learners in Dowa District are forced to clean up used condoms left in classrooms each morning.

Students at Mponela 2 Primary School in Malawi's Dowa District begin their day by cleaning used condoms from classrooms, a consequence of the school's lack of security and poor infrastructure. Over 3,000 learners share only two toilets, and the absence of a fence allows intruders to vandalize property and use classrooms for illicit activities at night. Marium Mira, treasurer for the Parents Teacher Association, called the situation heartbreaking.

The deputy head teacher, Dickson Muyaba, stated that the lack of a fence leaves the school exposed, creating a health and safety crisis and potentially triggering disease outbreaks due to poor sanitation. He appealed for government intervention to provide a fence and protect school property. Florence Ndovi of the Community Social Accountability Committee confirmed the council has plans to provide two classroom blocks, a teacher's house, two toilets, and a fence, but stressed the need to see the fence implemented.

Clinical psychologist Professor Chiwoza Bandawe explained that forcing children to clean up used condoms violates their dignity and can have serious psychological effects, making them feel unsafe and confused. Children's reactions vary by age and understanding, potentially leading to embarrassment, disgust, fear, or curiosity about sexual behaviour. The school has over 3,000 learners.

Why it mattersChildren in Malawi are being exposed to adult sexual activity and its remnants, compromising their dignity and sense of safety at school.

Culture · 2026-11-07

Film on period stigma to premiere in Malawi

The film 'Impure' will premiere on November 27, 2026, during the 16 Days of Activism campaign.

Join for Joy Malawi and the German Development Cooperation (GIZ) will premiere the film Impure on November 27, 2026, in Malawi. The film is part of the 16 Days of Activism against gender-based violence campaign.

The German-Kenyan co-production, inspired by a real-life tragedy in Kenya, follows a 12-year-old girl named June who experiences her first period at school. Join for Joy Malawi country director James Wilson Phiri stated the premiere aims to spark conversations among young people and key stakeholders, including educators, parents, community leaders, government officials, development partners, and the private sector.

Discussions at the premiere will explore the connection between menstrual health, gender equality, girls' rights, dignity, and education. Participants will consider how families, schools, communities, men, and boys can create more supportive environments for girls.

Why it mattersThe film premiere and subsequent discussions in Malawi aim to address period stigma and its impact on girls' education and social life.
