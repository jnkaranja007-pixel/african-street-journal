Malawi presents automatic fuel pricing model at Windhoek regional meeting

Officials say the mechanism, adopted in 2012, has helped government save public money and free fiscal space for other priorities.

Malawi has presented its automatic fuel pricing mechanism to senior government officials and economists from East and Southern African countries in Windhoek, Namibia. Rhino Mchenga, director of fiscal decentralisation at the Ministry of Finance, Economic Planning and Decentralisation, said the model was introduced in 2012 after mounting fiscal pressures and foreign exchange shortages made fuel subsidies unsustainable.

Mchenga said the mechanism lets domestic pump prices adjust automatically to changes in international import costs, exchange rates and levies, so government no longer accumulates subsidy costs. He acknowledged that the shift raised fuel prices as subsidies that had kept petroleum products affordable were withdrawn. The conference was organised by the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa's Southern and Eastern African subregional offices.

Malawi has suffered recurring fuel shortages, mainly because of scarce foreign currency for imports, and tobacco, its main earner, is seasonal. Zambia, Namibia, Tanzania, Mozambique, Botswana and South Africa also use the mechanism, which revises pump prices whenever international oil prices, the exchange rate or freight charges move by five percent.

Why it mattersFuel users in Malawi face price changes whenever oil prices, the exchange rate or freight charges shift by five percent, and a Price Stabilisation Fund cushions importers from losses.

Lilongwe council buys three electric vehicles to beat fuel shortages

The city council has spent K615 million on three electric cars so officers can keep collecting revenue during fuel shortages.

Lilongwe City Council has invested K615 million in three electric vehicles to keep municipal work running through recurring fuel shortages. The council says the cars will let officers travel across the city for revenue collection and administrative duties without relying entirely on petrol and diesel.

Chief Executive Officer Clement Stambuli said recurring fuel shortages had made it increasingly hard to keep the council's transport fleet running smoothly, and the purchase was prompted by that problem. The investment is expected to improve revenue collection and field operations and reduce the risk of activities stopping whenever fuel runs out.

The council will need to establish whether the K615 million spend produces measurable savings and better service delivery over time. Its success will depend on reliable electricity supply, charging infrastructure, battery maintenance and how well the vehicles are managed.

Why it mattersCouncil officers who collect revenue and carry out field work will be able to keep moving when fuel is scarce, and the council must show whether the K615 million pays off.

Chakwera given 7 days on MCP convention

Petitioners demand emergency convention, suspension of restructuring, and withdrawal of election fees.

Malawi Congress Party (MCP) president Lazarus Chakwera has been given seven days to call an emergency convention and suspend disputed party restructuring. A group led by former MCP Southern Region chairperson Dennis Nanthumba delivered a petition on Tuesday demanding these actions and the withdrawal of candidature fees for committee elections.

The petitioners, who include aspiring regional chairpersons, argue that Chakwera's term as party president has expired and he is no longer eligible for another term. They threaten to seek court intervention, including injunctive relief, if the party does not respond satisfactorily within the seven-day deadline.

The petition also calls for the immediate suspension of proposed demarcations of party regions and districts, arguing it could affect membership allocation and representation. MCP presidential hopeful Alex Major and 42 district chairpersons had previously called for a convention, but the National Executive Committee resolved to retain Chakwera.

Why it mattersMalawi Congress Party members are challenging the tenure of their president, Lazarus Chakwera, and demanding changes to party structure and election processes.

Nachipanti victim speaks 17 years after family killings

Margaret Twalibu still suffers health issues from a 2009 attack that killed her sister and nephew.

Margaret Twalibu, who survived an attack by Jack Bandawe, known as Nachipanti, revealed 17 years later that she cannot forgive him for killing two family members and leaving her with permanent injuries. The October 2009 assault in Ndirande Township, Blantyre, left Twalibu with persistent headaches and impaired eyesight, affecting her health and daily life.

Twalibu was 16 when Bandawe killed her 18-year-old sister, Shira Twalibu, and her four-year-old nephew, Lakazi Mpasa, while she sustained serious head injuries. Her brother, Yusuf Twalibu, stated the family questions the case's handling, alleging Bandawe was a scapegoat for main perpetrators who were never held accountable. Bandawe was apprehended after a resident struck his knee with a stone, leading to his conviction for murder and attempted murder.

Bandawe received a death sentence in November 2010 for the 2009 killings and attempted murder, and a prior 14-year sentence for a 2008 assault. He was known for nocturnal home invasions, targeting households with absent men and using sharp objects. Bandawe confessed to sucking blood, claiming he was hired for K80,000 to collect human blood, which led to arrests of several individuals. However, in August 2012, three suspects were acquitted due to insufficient evidence.

Why it mattersThe story details the lasting physical and emotional trauma experienced by Margaret Twalibu, a survivor of a brutal attack that resulted in the deaths of her sister and nephew.

FAM revives FA Cup with knockout format

The Football Association of Malawi will launch a new national cup competition involving teams from all levels of Malawian football.

The Football Association of Malawi (FAM) announced plans to launch a new national FA Cup competition, played on a knockout basis. FAM revealed the competition details through a press release on November 11, with the full package to be unveiled on November 20. This introduction follows Castel Malawi Limited's decision not to renew sponsorship for the Ibongetse Castel Challenge Cup and FDH Bank plc's shift of its sponsorship to the top-flight league.

FAM spokesperson Collins Nsuza stated the cup aims to be a flagship national knockout competition and a strategic platform for football development, talent identification, club growth, and commercial opportunities. The competition will include teams from the FDH Bank Premiership, National Division League, regional leagues, and district leagues. For the current season, participation is limited to 16 FDH Bank Premiership teams, 12 second-tier league sides, top teams from regional leagues, and district league champions due to time constraints.

The FA Cup will feature a regional preliminary phase, with district league champions and regional league teams joining National Division League teams for playoffs to determine 16 national phase qualifiers. The 16 Premiership teams will enter the draw from the round of 32. Football analysts George Chiusiwa and Parry Chinyama welcomed the development, noting it will bolster the football package and provide much-needed exposure for teams, especially in the elite tier.
