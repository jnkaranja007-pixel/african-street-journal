HRDC questions police over journalists' assault

The Human Rights Defenders Coalition demands an investigation into officers who allegedly failed to intervene during an attack on two journalists.

The Human Rights Defenders Coalition (HRDC) is demanding an investigation into police inaction during the assault and intimidation of two journalists in Blantyre on Sunday. Journalists Francis Chamasowa and Francis Nkawihe were allegedly manhandled without intervention from police officers who were present.

HRDC vice chairperson Madalitso Banda stated that the coalition condemns the attack and the damage to a vehicle belonging to Moses Kunkuyu. The coalition also referenced videos of the Blantyre incidents, urging police to act and investigate the officers involved.

The HRDC's statement follows an attack on the convoy of former President Lazarus Chakwera in Blantyre. The coalition's call for action highlights concerns over the safety of journalists and the response of law enforcement during public incidents.

Why it mattersJournalists and the public in Malawi are affected by the alleged failure of police to protect individuals during an incident.

Malawi imports 1,000 vehicles monthly amid forex crisis

Governance expert Henry Chingaipe urged import restrictions on luxury vehicles to conserve foreign exchange.

Malawi is importing approximately 1,000 vehicles each month despite a severe foreign exchange shortage, according to governance expert Henry Chingaipe. He argued for import restrictions, including on luxury vehicles, to alleviate pressure on the country's scarce forex reserves. Chingaipe made these remarks during a presentation on Developmental State Philosophy in Lilongwe.

Chingaipe stated that the continued outflow of foreign exchange is alarming and requires decisive policy action. AFIDEP Executive Director Eliya Zulu suggested that much of the forex is absorbed through the black market rather than a fundamental shortage. National Planning Commission (NPC) Director General Frederick Changaya proposed shifting away from liquid fuel vehicles.

Changaya also recommended restricting the importation of fuel-powered cars and promoting electric vehicles. He further suggested exploring local lithium battery manufacturing, noting Malawi's significant deposits of lithium and graphite. These minerals are essential for battery production.

Why it mattersMalawi's continued import of vehicles exacerbates its foreign exchange crisis, affecting the availability of forex for essential goods and services.

football fans assault officials over juju fears

Chitipa United supporters invaded a tunnel and attacked Masters FC officials believing juju prevented their team from scoring.

Masters FC officials were reportedly assaulted by Chitipa United supporters at Chitipa Stadium on Saturday during an FDH Premiership match. The supporters allegedly invaded the tunnel leading to the dressing rooms at half-time, searching for juju they believed hindered their team.

The incidents began during Masters FC’s Friday training session when an individual allegedly watched and poured liquid on the pitch. On Saturday, the same individual reportedly spread liquid at the stadium gate and collected soil from where Masters coach Peter Mponda stepped, escalating tensions before the match.

Videos show ball boys spreading liquid on the goal line where Masters were to defend in the second half. The assault on Masters officials highlights concerns about stadium security and the entanglement of football with suspicions about supernatural forces.

Why it mattersFootball fans in Chitipa attacked visiting officials due to beliefs that juju was preventing their team from scoring.

Mwaungulu questions Attorney General's Parliament case intervention

Former judge Dunstan Mwaungulu says the Attorney General's move into a Parliament legal case lacks instruction from the institution.

Former Court of Appeal judge Dunstan Mwaungulu has questioned the Attorney General's intervention in a court case involving Parliament. The dispute centres on disagreement between the Attorney General and Speaker of the National Assembly, Sameer Suleman, over who should represent Parliament. Mwaungulu stated that reports of the Attorney General replacing Parliament's lawyers are hard to reconcile with the Speaker's public statements.

Mwaungulu argued that while the Constitution names the Attorney General as the government's principal legal adviser, cases involving public offices are governed by the Suits by and Against Government and Public Officers Act. Under this law, Parliament had already retained lawyers, and they remain on record unless Parliament authorises a change. "You do not change lawyers by force or by your position," Mwaungulu wrote, adding that the litigant, Parliament, must instruct any change.

He questioned how the Attorney General could take over the case without instructions from Parliament. Available information suggests Parliament neither started nor agreed to the move. Mwaungulu believes this situation exposes deeper tensions over institutional independence.

Why it mattersFormer judge Dunstan Mwaungulu has raised concerns about the Attorney General's actions in a court case involving Malawi's Parliament, highlighting potential issues with institutional independence.

Sport · 2026-10-26

Wanderers draw with Karonga United

FCB Nyasa Big Bullets remain three points clear at the top of the FDH Bank Premiership.

Defending champions Mighty Wanderers drew 1-1 with Karonga United on Sunday at Karonga Stadium in the FDH Bank Premiership. The result leaves Wanderers on 36 points from 18 matches, three behind leaders FCB Nyasa Big Bullets.

Clement Nyondo scored for Wanderers in the 15th minute, but Michael Mwalilino equalised for Karonga United with a free kick in the 84th minute. Wanderers coach Bob Mpinganjira accepted the draw.

Karonga United came close to scoring on several occasions before their late equaliser. Wanderers were unable to find a second goal in the remaining time.

Why it mattersMighty Wanderers and FCB Nyasa Big Bullets are competing in the FDH Bank Premiership.
