Malawi hospital gets power after social media outcry

Local Government Minister Ben Phiri confirmed electricity was restored to Makanjira Rural Hospital within 48 hours.

Makanjira Rural Hospital in Malawi has regained electricity after a 48-hour deadline set by Local Government Minister Ben Phiri. The restoration followed a public outcry on social media platforms like TikTok and Instagram. Phiri announced the power had been reconnected through an update on his official Facebook account.

Minister Phiri thanked Malawians who assisted in the power restoration efforts. He stated that the hospital's power supply issue was due to a commissioning mix-up. The minister had promised to resolve the situation within the 48-hour timeframe.

The hospital's power supply was a concern for local residents. The intervention by social media users and the subsequent action by the minister highlight the impact of public pressure on service delivery.

Why it mattersMakanjira Rural Hospital now has electricity, resolving a service delivery issue for local residents.

Castel Malawi pauses plan to cut 100 jobs for three weeks

The brewer, which says a tough trading climate is hurting its operations, agreed with its union to suspend the retrenchments while talks with government take place.

Castel Malawi, which the Nyasa Times describes as the country's second-largest taxpayer, had planned to retrench 100 employees in the second week of November, citing a difficult business environment. The company has now put that decision on hold after talks with the Castel Malawi Workers Union (CAMAWU).

The two sides agreed to suspend the retrenchment for three weeks. Management has reportedly blamed a 110 percent excise tax on spirits, beer smuggling and a growing supply of illicit alcoholic products on the local market. CAMAWU president Israel Mughogho said the union wants roundtable talks with the Ministry of Labour, the Malawi Revenue Authority, the Ministry of Trade and the Ministry of Finance.

Mughogho said the illicit products evade taxes and also pose a health and safety risk to Malawians. The union is seeking policy changes that could reverse the planned job losses.

Why it mattersThe 100 affected workers keep their jobs for now, and the union has three weeks to win policy changes before any cuts can go ahead.

Politics · 2026-11-16

PAC asks President Mutharika for a meeting

The Public Affairs Committee has written to the president seeking an audience, and says it is still waiting for a date.

The Public Affairs Committee (PAC), a quasi-religious grouping, has written to President Peter Mutharika asking him to grant it an audience. PAC spokesperson Bishop Gilford Matonga confirmed the letter.

Speaking on Times Television's "Times Exclusive" programme, Matonga said the group wanted to discuss some issues with the president. He said the meeting would be one of the group's regular meetings with the head of state.

The group has not yet received a date for the meeting, according to Matonga.

Why it mattersThe request leaves the PAC without a confirmed date, so its concerns stay unheard until the president's office responds.

Health · 2026-11-16

Malawi prison officers sign mental wellness pact

A survey revealed widespread alcohol, gambling, and debt issues among officers, prompting a new initiative.

Malawi’s prison service in the Southern Region signed a mental wellness pact with Young Achievers for Development (YAD) and the Gift of Khonde Initiative on Thursday. This action follows warnings that suicide is significantly impacting prison officers.

Jefferson Milanzie, executive director of YAD and a suicide attempt survivor, is training officers and inmates on stress and depression management. He reported that 567 Malawians died by suicide last year, including three prison officers in the Southern Region, with four others surviving attempts. YAD's Abambo Tibe Khonde Initiative handled six anonymous cases from prison officers last year.

A survey of 4,142 officers in the region identified 145 alcohol-related cases, 68 gambling cases, 105 family conflicts, 149 debt cases, and 18 disciplinary hearings linked to social issues.

Why it mattersPrison officers in Malawi's Southern Region are receiving stress and depression training following a pact aimed at addressing mental wellness issues linked to debt and social problems.

Sport · 2026-11-16

Malawi trail Egypt and Angola in Afcon qualifiers

Angola's victory over South Sudan leaves Malawi four points behind Group B leaders.

The Malawi National Football Team's hopes of reaching the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations finals have suffered a setback. Angola defeated South Sudan 2-0 in Luanda on Saturday night.

This result means Malawi now trails Group B leaders Egypt and Angola by four points. Both Angola and Egypt have secured seven points from their first three matches in the qualifying group.

Malawi's qualification chances are significantly diminished with only three matches played in the group stage.
