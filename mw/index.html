Speaker Suleman Defends Parliamentary Scrutiny Amid DPP Warning

Speaker Sameer Suleman asserts his right to probe national issues, including alleged corruption and service delivery, despite a warning from the ruling party.

Speaker of Parliament Sameer Suleman has defended his decision to proceed with parliamentary scrutiny of national issues, stating he sees no issue with his actions. This comes after Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) Secretary General Peter Mukhito warned that parliamentary oversight should not become an alternative center of executive authority. Mukhito emphasized respect for President Peter Mutharika's mandate and the Executive's authority, even while acknowledging Parliament's constitutional oversight role.

Suleman's defense follows his recent press briefing where he raised concerns about the Salima water project, pension schemes, electricity supply, ESCOM and EGENCO, NOCMA, passport services, justice administration delays, prison conditions, mining, and the tobacco sector. He stated he communicates directly with President Mutharika and consults him on many decisions, urging that his actions be understood within his office's responsibilities. Parliament plans to establish committees to investigate matters of concern, including passport challenges and alleged financial irregularities at the National Oil Company of Malawi.

Mukhito, however, suggested that issues involving contracts, public finances, and procurement should be handled by legally mandated investigative institutions. He stated that passport challenges should be addressed by responsible government bodies and, where necessary, examined by relevant parliamentary committees. The DPP and Suleman appear to be on a collision course regarding the scope and handling of these planned parliamentary inquiries.

Why it mattersMalawian citizens are affected as the Speaker of Parliament initiates investigations into issues like passport services, alleged financial theft, and service delivery, potentially leading to corrective actions.

Portable Labs Detect Cholera Before Malawi Outbreaks

Compact water testing equipment distributed to district councils aims to prevent waterborne diseases from devastating rural communities.

Portable water quality testing labs are being distributed across Malawi to identify cholera contamination before outbreaks occur. Funded by Charity Water and implemented by Self Help Africa, the initiative provides compact labs to district councils to strengthen water, sanitation, and hygiene services. This effort addresses the critical need for tested water sources in rural areas where communities often rely on boreholes and shallow wells.

District Commissioner James Jones Kanyangalazi stated at a handover event for Dowa District Council that the donation is timely, as waterborne diseases continue to impact rural populations. He explained that the new labs will enable councils to test water bodies within hours, confirming the quality of water from newly drilled boreholes and identifying the source of potential cholera outbreaks.

The new portable labs ensure that no water source will be provided to communities without prior testing. This commitment aims to guarantee that all water made available for public use is of good quality and safe for drinking. The initiative seeks to significantly reduce the incidence of cholera, diarrhea, and other waterborne diseases that have historically claimed lives in these areas.

Why it mattersRural communities in Malawi are affected by the risk of cholera and other waterborne diseases due to untested water sources.

Ex-aviation chief highlights airport safety flaws

A former aviation authority director told lawmakers that Malawi's airports suffer from critical safety and infrastructure deficits, impacting operations.

Malawi's airports are facing significant safety and infrastructure challenges, according to former acting Director of the Civil Aviation Authority Samuel Kalua. Appearing before a parliamentary committee investigating a recent military aircraft crash, Kalua cited years of financial and bureaucratic hurdles that have hindered the maintenance of safety standards and essential equipment.

Kalua detailed a faulty landing system at Kamuzu International Airport that sometimes misdirected aircraft, forcing authorities to disable it. He also noted the absence of communication recording devices and a 27-year period without radar at the airports, with radar only installed in 2019. Bakili Muluzi International Airport, he added, lacks other landing systems, leaving pilots to rely on personal judgment.

Reforms are needed to bolster civil aviation oversight and ensure proper upkeep of airport infrastructure, Kalua stated. Earlier, a former Kamuzu International Airport briefing officer recounted a pilot mentioning difficulties with a flight route during a previous journey, shortly before the crash that claimed the lives of the vice-president and eight others.

Why it mattersThe safety and infrastructure issues at Malawi's airports directly affect the reliability and safety of air travel for all passengers and cargo.

UK Provides $3 Million for Malawi El Niño Preparedness

The United Kingdom has committed funding to help Malawi prepare for anticipated dry conditions during the upcoming rainfall season.

The UK has committed approximately $3 million (about K5.3 billion) to support Malawi’s response and preparedness for a forecast severe El Niño event during the 2026/27 rainfall season. British Deputy High Commissioner Ben Nicholson stated the funding aims to translate climate forecasts into early action, assisting communities and the agricultural sector in preparing for anticipated dry conditions. The support includes bringing climate information directly to communities, working with government and agribusinesses, promoting drought-resistant crop varieties, and monitoring market prices to anticipate possible changes in maize availability.

Nicholson announced the funding in Lilongwe on Wednesday at the fifth National Climate Outlook Forum (Nacof-5) and an Impact-Based Forecasting workshop. He emphasized the UK's collaboration with the Malawi government, the UK Met Office, and the Wiser climate programme to strengthen forecasting capacity and ensure climate information leads to practical, actionable interventions on the ground.

Malawi's Minister of Natural Resources, Patricia Wiskes, described the El Niño forecast as an opportunity for preparation rather than a prediction of disaster. She stated, “A forecast such as this one is not a prophecy of hardship; it is an invitation to act early, act together and act smart.” The government is treating the forecast as a call to proactive measures.

Why it mattersFarmers and agribusinesses in Malawi will receive support to prepare for anticipated dry conditions and potential changes in maize availability due to the El Niño forecast.

Malawi Musicians Silent Amid Economic Hardship and Political Actions

Once vocal artists who championed the poor and criticized governance now largely support politicians for personal gain.

Malawian musicians, historically known for their fearless critique of political and economic issues, have largely fallen silent. This shift contrasts sharply with the 1990s and early 2000s when artists like Lucius Banda used their music to address societal grievances and advocate for the common person. The current economic squeeze and the actions of politicians have not spurred a similar wave of protest songs from the country's artistic community.

Instead of speaking truth to power, many contemporary artists are observed aligning with politicians during election campaigns, often seeking personal benefits. This behavior is seen as a departure from the tradition of using music as a tool for social commentary and unity. The spirit of protest, however, is not entirely extinguished, as evidenced by artist Nepman's 2022 song criticizing the government during severe economic challenges.

The change in artistic engagement raises questions about the motivations behind artists' choices to support political figures and whether they prioritize personal gain over their historical role as voices for the people. While some artists have transitioned into politics themselves, the broader trend points to a diminished role for music in holding power accountable in Malawi.

Why it mattersMalawian artists are affected by a shift towards supporting politicians for personal gain, diminishing their role in critiquing governance.
