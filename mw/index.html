Malawi attorney general and law society clash over parliament delay

The Attorney General and Malawi Law Society interpret the same constitutional clause differently regarding the president's decision.

Attorney General Frank Mbeta and the Malawi Law Society (MLS) disagree on the legality of President Peter Mutharika's decision to postpone Parliament. The dispute centres on Section 59(1) of the Constitution, with both sides using the same provision to support their opposing views. The postponement moved the parliamentary session from November 9 to December 11.

Speaker of the National Assembly Sameer Suleman has sided with the President, stating Parliament will not challenge the postponement. Suleman indicated that he has taken no action against the President's directive. He advised listening to the President's reasoning.

The Attorney General's office and the MLS have conflicting interpretations of the constitutional provision. The President's decision to delay the parliamentary sitting has created this legal divergence.

Why it mattersMalawi's Attorney General and the Law Society have differing interpretations of the constitution regarding the postponement of Parliament, affecting the legislative process.

Malawi cities face unlicensed motorbike rider crisis

Motorcycle taxis are vital for transport and livelihoods in Lilongwe, Blantyre, Mzuzu and Zomba.

Unlicensed motorcycle riders are causing serious road safety concerns in Malawi's cities. Motorcycle taxis, known as kabaza, are a key part of urban transport in Lilongwe, Blantyre, Mzuzu, and Zomba. These bikes provide affordable mobility and support thousands of jobs.

The growing number of riders without licenses is raising questions about regulatory enforcement and owner responsibility. Road safety advocates are worried about the potential for accidents and lack of accountability on the roads.

The Malawi24 article highlights the dual nature of kabaza services: essential for daily commutes and income, yet operating outside of established licensing regulations.

Lawyer accuses Malawi Law Society of bias

Counsel Tupochele Mponda says the society has been inconsistent in its criticism of presidential actions.

Counsel Tupochele Mponda has accused the Malawi Law Society (MLS) of political bias for criticizing President Peter Mutharika's suspension of Parliament. Mponda stated that the MLS remained silent when former presidents made similar decisions but has now challenged Mutharika.

Mponda defended Mutharika's decision, citing constitutional powers under Section 59 (1) and (2) for presidential authority over Parliament. She pointed to prorogations by Bingu wa Mutharika in 2010 and 2011, and by Lazarus Chakwera between 2020 and 2025, which she claims did not draw similar MLS condemnation.

The lawyer challenged the MLS to explain its differing treatment of administrations and to seek judicial intervention if it believes the President violated the Constitution. Mponda warned that selective criticism could undermine public confidence in the society's independence and credibility.

Bullets and Wanderers win FDH Bank Premiership matches

FCB Nyasa Big Bullets beat Masters 2-1 and Mighty Wanderers defeated Goshen City Dedza Dynamos 2-0.

The wins keep both teams' paces in the league title race. Bullets claimed maximum points in an entertaining contest against Masters Football Club. Wanderers secured their win at Mpira Stadium.

DPP official threatens Malawi Congress Party director

Maria Mainja accused Moses Kunkuyu of spreading false rumours about President Mutharika's health.

Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) official Maria Mainja has accused Malawi Congress Party (MCP) campaign director Moses Kunkuyu of spreading false rumours about President Peter Mutharika's health.

Mainja stated that Kunkuyu falsely claimed Mutharika was critically ill and on the brink of death, while the president had been on a private visit to South Africa and returned healthy.

The DPP official even suggested that DPP supporters would attend Kunkuyu's funeral dressed in party colours, highlighting the increasingly bitter political atmosphere ahead of future elections.

Why it mattersMalawi's political arena is experiencing a war of words between DPP and MCP officials.
