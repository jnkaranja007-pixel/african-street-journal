Malawians plan nationwide protests over fuel, power and forex shortages

Demonstrations are expected on November 17 in Lilongwe, Blantyre, Zomba and Mzuzu, as the fuel crisis enters its 10th week.

Malawians in Lilongwe, Blantyre, Zomba and Mzuzu are expected to take to the streets on November 17 to protest growing economic hardship. The complaints centre on fuel shortages, electricity blackouts and a scarcity of foreign exchange.

The fuel shortage, now in its 10th week, has left motorists queuing for hours at filling stations and pushed up fares and commodity prices, according to the commentary. Importers struggle to access dollars, and manufacturers find it hard to get raw materials.

Organisers describe peaceful demonstrations as a constitutional right and urge the government to listen rather than dismiss the protests as politically motivated.

Why it mattersMotorists, transport operators and small businesses face queues, higher fares and interrupted power, and the protests are set to test how the government responds.

Health groups demand fair recruitment

Malawi's health sector faces a critical recruitment drive from November 20 to 22, with advocacy groups pushing for transparency.

Two health advocacy organisations, the Malawi Health Equity Network (MHEN) and the Universal Health Coverage Coalition (UHCC), are demanding a fair and transparent recruitment process for health workers. The exercise, scheduled for November 20 to 22, 2026, aims to strengthen the country's health workforce. MHEN and UHCC have urged the government to prevent favouritism and political interference, ensuring all candidates have an equal opportunity.

The organisations welcomed the recruitment drive as a chance to improve healthcare delivery but called for measures to prevent overcrowding and ensure candidate safety during interviews. They specifically requested that serving health workers who have upgraded their qualifications be given a fair chance to compete for senior positions matching their new skills and experience. A strictly merit-based process, free from unfair treatment, is essential for boosting staff morale and career progression.

MHEN and UHCC stressed that a transparent recruitment process promotes equal opportunities and strengthens healthcare delivery. They are urging the government to uphold fairness and accountability throughout the exercise, ensuring qualified candidates can serve in the nation's health sector. The groups highlighted that a transparent process would encourage career progression and boost staff morale.

Why it mattersThe upcoming recruitment of health workers from November 20 to 22, 2026, will determine who gets a chance to serve in Malawi's health sector.

Malawi wins African Court case against Tsaperas

The African Court on Human and Peoples’ Rights dismissed a K233 billion compensation claim against Malawi.

Malawi has won a significant legal battle at the African Court on Human and Peoples’ Rights, with the court throwing out a K233 billion (US$133.7 million) compensation claim against the country. Attorney General Frank Mbeta confirmed the victory in Arusha, Tanzania, following the ruling.

The case involved South African businessman Patroklos Tsaperas and stemmed from the collapse of a telecommunications deal with Malawi Mobile Limited (MML), a company in which Tsaperas is a shareholder. MML was granted a mobile phone licence in 2002 but failed to launch services within 12 months, leading regulators to revoke the licence in 2005.

Malawi’s High Court had previously awarded MML US$66.85 million in damages in 2012. This latest ruling spares Malawian taxpayers from a potentially massive financial payout after more than two decades of legal proceedings.

Why it mattersMalawian taxpayers are spared a K233 billion payout following the African Court's ruling.

Egypt thrash Malawi 5-0 in Afcon qualifier

Malawi's Flames conceded five goals in Cairo, exposing defensive and midfield frailties.

Egypt defeated Malawi 5-0 in a 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifier in Cairo on Friday, November 13, 2026. Nasser Maher opened the scoring in the sixth minute, followed by Omar Marmoush in the tenth and Mohamed Hany in the 39th. Mohamed Salah added two more goals, one from a penalty in stoppage time, to complete the rout.

The Flames conceded two goals within the first 10 minutes, disrupting their tactical plan. Egypt's technical players controlled the midfield tempo, while Malawi struggled with physical presence and winning second balls. Richard Mbulu tested Egypt's goalkeeper in the 22nd minute, but his effort was saved. Coach Kalisto Pasuwa stated, "We made mistakes and we were punished. We respected them too much and ended up conceding too many goals."

Malawi's qualification hopes for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations are now in doubt following the heavy defeat. The team struggled to combine and support their striker in the final third. Pasuwa made five changes to the lineup that faced Angola, but the reshuffle failed to contain Egypt's pace and movement.

Why it mattersMalawi's national football team suffered a significant defeat, impacting their chances of qualifying for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations.
