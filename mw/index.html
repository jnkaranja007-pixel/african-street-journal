Malawi government to appeal K300bn Finance Bank award

Attorney General Frank Mbeta calls the compensation to the defunct bank excessive and plans a Supreme Court review this week.

Malawi's government will challenge a K300 billion compensation award to the defunct Finance Bank Malawi (FBM), Attorney General Frank Mbeta said. Mbeta told an interview that he believes the award is excessive and will seek a review before a full bench of the Supreme Court of Appeal within this week.

The award, made on November 4, 2026, by Supreme Court assistant registrar Ibrahim Hussein, orders the Reserve Bank of Malawi (RBM) to pay FBM for revoking its banking licence in 2005. It splits into K157.5 billion for loss of business and profit, K138.12 billion for malfeasance in public office, and K150 million for breach of constitutional rights. The bank had first claimed K1.1 trillion.

The Supreme Court ruled on February 3, 2026, that RBM's revocation was unconstitutional and ordered damages assessed. The dispute began in May 2005, when Finance Bank challenged the licence revocation.

Why it mattersThe K300 billion payout is a cost to the public purse, which the government is now trying to reduce through an appeal.

Malawi hauliers ground trucks over fuel transport dispute

Local transporters protest the return of a court-outlawed Delivery Duty Unpaid system favouring foreign firms.

Local fuel transporters in Malawi have parked their tankers nationwide to protest the reintroduction of the Delivery Duty Unpaid (DDU) procurement system. The Transporters Association of Malawi (TAM) argues this arrangement allows fuel suppliers to select transporters, effectively shutting out domestic companies. This dispute revives a conflict over fuel transport contracts that a 2024 High Court ruling had previously addressed.

TAM spokesperson Frank Banda stated that Malawian operators are being disadvantaged while foreign transporters continue to handle the business. Truck Drivers Association of Malawi president Francis Mkandawire questioned the use of scarce foreign currency to pay foreign firms for transport services. An economics lecturer noted that these forex expenditures could be redirected to essential imports like fuel and medicine.

The DDU system, which began in 2017, contrasts with the previous ex-tank arrangement where local importers managed transit risks. Under DDU, suppliers bear delivery risks to local depots, with local importers taking charge only after delivery. TAM maintains that local transporters are capable and ready to resume services if engaged by the government.

Why it mattersMalawian transporters are directly affected by losing contracts and income due to the DDU system, while the country's foreign exchange reserves are strained by payments to foreign hauliers.

Temwa Chawinga nominated for BBC award

Malawi's First Lady urges citizens to vote for the national team striker.

Malawi's First Lady Gertrude Mutharika has urged all Malawians to vote for national team striker Temwa Chawinga in the BBC Women's Footballer of the Year Award 2026. Chawinga is one of five finalists and the only African player nominated for the prestigious award. Voting is open until November 18.

Mutharika stated that Chawinga's nomination is a national record and makes the entire continent proud. She called for unity in casting votes to bring the award home. Chawinga also recently finished in the top 10 of the Ballon d'Or award.

Temwa Chawinga is also a finalist for the US-based Women's National Soccer League (NWSL) Most Valuable Player award, where she is seeking a third consecutive win. Voting for the BBC award closes on November 18, with the winner announced on December 13.

Why it mattersMalawians are directly affected by the opportunity to support their national sports hero in an international competition.

Education · 2026-11-11

UNIMA students protest power cuts

Students say ongoing blackouts are hindering exam preparation and causing security fears.

University of Malawi (UNIMA) students are frustrated by continuous electricity blackouts that disrupt their studies and assignment completion. Collidah Kachale, a fourth-year student, stated that power outages prevent students from charging devices needed for research and group discussions, impacting their academic performance.

The disruptions are particularly worrying during the examination period, with students like Kachale appealing to the Minister of Education, Bright Nsaka, to intervene. Another student, Staphel Nyirenda, highlighted security concerns in dark areas and difficulties in preparing meals due to the frequent power interruptions.

Students are struggling to study adequately and fear that the blackouts will negatively affect their examination results. They have urged authorities to address the persistent electricity outages urgently.

Why it mattersUniversity students in Malawi are facing academic difficulties and security risks due to prolonged electricity outages.

Politics · 2026-11-11

Malawi arrests ex-MP Chilapondwa over K884m unpaid VAT

Revenue authority says the former Ntchisi South MP collected VAT on government electrification contracts without being registered to do so.

The Malawi Revenue Authority (MRA) has arrested Ulemu Chilapondwa, a businessman and former ruling party Member of Parliament for Ntchisi South, over allegations that he collected and failed to pass on K884.6 million in Value Added Tax linked to government-funded electrification contracts.

MRA says Chilapondwa, who trades as Chamachete General Dealers and J.U.C Investments, took the tax from the Ministry of Energy for supplies under the Malawi Rural Electrification Programme (MAREP) in November 2024, when he was not registered for VAT. MRA Head of Corporate Affairs Wilma Chalulu said the case concerns an alleged breach of Section 109 of the Tax Administration Act, 2021.

The matter is now before the courts, and MRA says due process will be followed. Chilapondwa is presumed innocent until proven guilty.

Why it mattersGovernment electrification contracts funded under MAREP are the source of the disputed VAT, so any shortfall in tax paid on those contracts directly affects public funds.
