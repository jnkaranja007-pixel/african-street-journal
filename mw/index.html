Malawi NGOs earned K1.3 trillion in 2026

Non-governmental organisations in Malawi generated K1.30 trillion in income in 2026, with foreign currency making up 76.7 percent of this amount.

Non-governmental organisations (NGOs) in Malawi generated K1.30 trillion in income in 2026, an increase from K1.02 trillion in 2025, according to the 2026 NGO Sector Report. Foreign currency accounted for K994.6 billion, or 76.7 percent, of this total income. The report also noted that the top 20 NGOs received half of the total income generated.

Despite the income surge, paid employment within NGOs dropped to 22,440 in 2026 from 28,680 in 2025, though salaries totalled K337.2 billion. NGO Regulatory Authority (Ngora) CEO Voice Mhone stated that this income is a massive injection into the economy, almost rivaling the national budget. Minister of Gender, Children, Disability and Social Welfare Mary Navicha highlighted that nearly 39 percent of women are employed by NGOs.

Bertha Phiri, Ngora Board Chairperson, reported that NGO compliance levels are around 70 percent and that the sector has overtaken tobacco in foreign exchange earnings. The report, introduced by Ngora in 2021, consolidates financial and compliance records annually. Minister Navicha acknowledged the drop in employment but pledged continued government support to strengthen NGO systems and ensure transparent fund usage.

Why it mattersThe NGO sector's K1.30 trillion income in 2026, with a significant portion in foreign currency, makes it a robust contributor to Malawi's economy, impacting employment and development efforts.

Malawi reforms fail to survive government changes

Eliya Zulu of Afidep says policy decisions are driven by short-term political interests, undermining development.

Economic reforms in Malawi are failing to survive government changes, according to Eliya Zulu, executive director of the African Institute for Development Policy (Afidep). Speaking at the Economics Association of Malawi (Ecama) 2026 Annual Economic Conference in Mangochi, Zulu stated that the lack of reform continuity weakens institutional memory and hinders long-term development.

Zulu noted that Malawi struggles economically despite having sound development strategies and qualified public servants, attributing this to policy decisions driven by short-term political interests and frequent changes in senior officials. Patrick Kabambe, Principal Secretary for Economic Planning and Development, also acknowledged institutional weaknesses affecting implementation.

The World Bank presented a mixed rating, stating that while Malawi shows signs of economic recovery with easing inflation, gains remain fragile and growth is projected to lag behind the sub-Saharan African average due to persistent macroeconomic challenges and external pressures.

Why it mattersMalawians are affected by the failure to implement consistent economic reforms, which hinders long-term development and economic stability.

High court frees boy convicted as adult

Lilongwe court rules boy was a minor when sentenced to 16 years for sexual assault.

The High Court in Lilongwe has ordered the immediate release of a boy convicted and sentenced as an adult. The court determined that CM was a minor when he was tried and sentenced.

CM had been jailed for 16 years after pleading guilty to having sexual intercourse with a 12-year-old girl in Dedza. He was first brought before a lower court on October 30, 2025, for the offense.

The ruling overturns the previous conviction and sentence, allowing CM to be freed from prison.

Malawi farmers harvest more maize with WFP support

Levison Kamleka increased his maize yield from four to 21 bags after receiving anticipatory action intervention.

Levison Kamleka, a 45-year-old farmer in Zomba District, harvested 21 bags of maize in the 2025–2026 season, a significant increase from his usual three to four bags. This improvement followed support from the World Food Programme (WFP) through an anticipatory action intervention. Kamleka has used six bags for food and has 14 remaining, stating his days of seeking maize for food are over.

Kamleka's experience is part of WFP Malawi’s Zero Hunger Village (ZHV) model, launched in September 2025 and targeting 1,200 villages and 54,400 beneficiaries. The model integrates agricultural support, irrigation, school meals, nutrition services, climate information, and anticipatory action to combat hunger. In Nsanje District, irrigation support is enabling farmers like Chrissy Edward, 35, to grow crops despite unreliable rainfall.

Edward, from Bithi 1 Village, increased her rice harvest from two to three bags to nine bags from a 0.1-hectare plot using irrigation from the Shire River. Aline Bingara, 52, also in Nsanje, uses irrigation to grow sweet potatoes, maize, rice, and vegetables, enabling her to feed her family and sell surplus produce. The ZHV model aims to improve food availability, household incomes, children’s health, and school attendance.

Why it mattersMalawi farmers are able to secure food supplies and generate income through WFP's agricultural and irrigation support initiatives.

Sky Energy launches 100 electric taxis in Malawi

The company aims to save foreign exchange and cut fossil fuel emissions with its new e-mobility system.

Sky Energy Africa is introducing 100 electric taxis and 40 fast chargers across Malawi, creating about 300 jobs. The sustainable energy enterprise, founded by Schizzo Thomson, is launching this e-mobility ecosystem to mark its tenth year in the country. A full charge for an electric vehicle (EV) covering 400 kilometres costs about K5 000 in electricity, while rapid charging sites will cost K25 000 for the same distance.

Thomson stated that after three years of learning, e-mobility is seen as the future solution for forex and fuel shortages, noting Malawi imports fuel worth $60 million monthly. Sky Energy, Malawi's premier EV dealer since 2023, developed the Sky e-Ride smart app, similar to Uber, allowing users to hail an e-taxi for as low as K1000 per kilometre. The company expects the 100 Geely EX2 electric taxis to transport approximately 5 500 people daily in Lilongwe, Mzuzu, and Blantyre.

Malawi Energy Regulatory Authority (Mera) spokesperson Fitina Khonje indicated that the country is collaborating with neighbours on EV regulations and aims to integrate EV chargers into existing fuel stations. The Ministry of Energy plans to launch the National E-Mobility Framework next Wednesday, with spokesperson Joan Thaundi expressing pleasure at the private sector's leading role in achieving e-mobility goals.

Why it mattersMalawians will have access to cheaper transport options and new job opportunities.
