Ekhaya FC Wins Airtel Top 8 Cup

Ekhaya FC secured the 2026 Airtel Top 8 Cup with a 2-1 victory over Mighty Wanderers at Bingu National Stadium.

Ekhaya Football Club has won the 2026 Airtel Top 8 Cup after defeating Mighty Wanderers 2-1 in a final match held at Bingu National Stadium in Lilongwe. The victory marked an emotional occasion for the Mpinganjira family, as William Mpinganjira celebrated with the winning team while his father, Thomson Mpinganjira, was on the losing side.

The club's forward, Allen Chihana, was confirmed as the tournament's top goal scorer, having scored in all four matches Ekhaya played on their way to lifting the trophy. Chihana's consistent performance was a key factor in the team's successful campaign.

This win continues Ekhaya FC's fairy tale run in the tournament, culminating in their championship title. The Airtel Top 8 Cup is a significant domestic football competition, and Ekhaya's triumph represents a major achievement for the club.

Women Dig Canal, Tap River Water for Dry-Season Farming

A group of 28 women in Mwandambe Village dug a 2km canal to irrigate fields, ensuring harvests during dry spells.

In Malawi, where 80 percent of farmers depend on rain, a group of 28 women and four men in Mwandambe Village have constructed a 2km canal to draw water from the Mudi River. This initiative, started in 2017, aims to combat food insecurity exacerbated by erratic rainfall and dry spells, with up to 2.6 million people at risk of hunger between October and March 2027. The women contributed funds for plastic sheeting and cement to line the canal and prevent water loss, though the cementing project remains incomplete due to economic challenges.

The women's efforts are already yielding visible results, providing food and income during dry months. Agnes Notesi, a founding member, stated that the canal took a year to construct and faced skepticism from others. Dorothy Thepheya, a widow, shared how the canal initiative allowed her to grow vegetables and maize, securing food and money for her grandchildren instead of relying on relatives. Village Head Mwandambe expressed pride in the women's self-sufficiency through farming.

The Ministry of Agriculture's Deputy Director of Irrigation Services, Christopher Bodole, highlighted the Mwandambe women as exemplary farmers the government wishes to support. This community-led irrigation project contrasts with the slow execution of national irrigation masterplans, demonstrating a grassroots approach to tackling food insecurity in a nation heavily reliant on rain-fed agriculture.

Why it mattersFarmers in Malawi are affected by the success of the Mwandambe women's canal project, which provides a model for overcoming drought and ensuring harvests.

Attorney General Appeals Court Ruling on Electoral Commission Relocation

Attorney General Frank Mbeta is challenging a High Court decision that denied the State a hearing on a key application in the Malawi Electoral Commission relocation dispute.

Attorney General Frank Mbeta has filed an appeal against a High Court ruling concerning the relocation of the Malawi Electoral Commission. The AG’s Chambers argues that the court, presided over by Justice Kenyatta Nyirenda, made a critical determination on a government application without affording the State an opportunity to present its case.

The appeal, lodged on behalf of the Chief Secretary and the Minister of Lands, Housing and Urban Development, contests a judgment from August 20, 2026. This ruling dismissed the government's attempt to lift a stay of proceedings in the ongoing relocation saga. Mbeta stated that the judge ruled on an application to discharge permission for judicial review without a hearing.

The government's appeal asserts that the judge erred by deciding the application to vacate leave without hearing the applicants and by recognizing the respondents' locus standi based on a prior decision. The government also contends that the case involves fundamental jurisdictional issues that should have been addressed before proceeding further.

Why it mattersThe Attorney General's appeal affects government operations and legal processes by challenging a court's procedural handling of a significant administrative relocation case.

Malawi State Arrests Activist Agape Khombe

The Malawian state arrested activist Agape Khombe, citing cyberbullying allegations, which critics label an assault on free speech.

Activist Agape Khombe has been arrested by the Malawian state, an action described by independent news platform Malawi24 as a "blatant, illegal assault on the constitutional right to free speech." The arrest was made under the pretext of protecting lawyer Edgar Kachere from alleged defamatory attacks framed as cyberbullying.

Malawi24 asserts that this move exposes the state's hypocrisy and contempt for the law. The platform highlights the arrest as a significant event concerning web regulation and its potential weaponization against citizens exercising their rights.

The incident raises concerns about the government's approach to online discourse and the potential for such actions to stifle dissent. The state's justification for the arrest is being challenged as a means to suppress free expression.

Why it mattersThe arrest of activist Agape Khombe affects citizens by potentially limiting their constitutional right to free speech online.

Kunkuyu May Face Grilled Over Chilima Black Box Claims

A parliamentary committee investigating the plane crash that killed the vice president may subpoena former minister Moses Kunkuyu to explain his statements about the aircraft's.

Former Information Minister Moses Kunkuyu may be summoned to testify before the parliamentary committee probing the June 10, 2024 plane crash that killed former Vice-President Saulos Chilima and eight others. This follows mounting pressure for Kunkuyu to clarify his claims that the aircraft's black box was recovered and preserved. Committee chairperson Walter Nyamilandu Manda confirmed the possibility remains open, pending the committee's assessment of key witnesses during the inquiry's final phase.

Kunkuyu, who previously served as Information Minister, had himself requested to be among the first witnesses called. He argued that the persistent mystery surrounding the black box warranted public interest testimony from all individuals linked to the device. Despite his request, Kunkuyu was not invited during the first or second phases of the inquiry.

Nyamilandu stated that Kunkuyu could be summoned during the third and final phase, specifically when lawmakers plan to engage with politicians connected to the tragedy. The committee is currently focused on witnesses in the ongoing second phase, but Nyamilandu did not rule out Kunkuyu's eventual appearance, emphasizing that the decision would be made at the appropriate time.

Why it mattersThe public and political figures are affected by the ongoing investigation into the circumstances surrounding the plane crash and the status of crucial evidence like the black box.
