Attorney General Joins Parliament Committee Dispute

Frank Mbeta's intervention in a case blocking parliamentary committees raises questions about his role as chief legal advisor.

Attorney General Frank Mbeta has joined court proceedings concerning an injunction that halted the formation of 12 parliamentary committees. These committees were established to investigate statutory bodies. The injunction was initially obtained by a private citizen, Austin Mseka, but the Attorney General's entry into the case has drawn criticism.

Legal analyst Peter Makossah, writing in The Daily Times, argues that Mbeta's involvement risks undermining the perception of impartiality. Makossah contends that the Attorney General, appointed by the executive but expected to offer detached legal advice, should not appear to take sides in disputes involving Parliament's oversight functions.

Makossah further cautioned that parliamentary committees are for scrutiny and investigation, not for determining guilt, a role reserved for the courts. He suggested that Parliament risks turning inquiries into mere theater if it oversteps these boundaries, and that the Attorney General's role is to advise the government and protect constitutional governance, not act as a private lawyer for officeholders.

Why it mattersThe Attorney General's involvement in a dispute over parliamentary oversight committees affects the balance of power between the executive and legislature, impacting governance.

Activists demand Speaker resigns amid party divisions

Civil society groups are intensifying calls for Parliament Speaker Sameer Suleman to step down following controversial remarks on religion.

Malawian civil society activists are demanding the resignation of Parliament Speaker Sameer Suleman, accusing him of lying and attacking the Agriculture Minister. Billy Malata, chairperson of Mbadwa Zokhudzidwa Malawi, made these accusations during a press briefing in Lilongwe. Social media users have questioned Malata's motives and suggested he may have been paid to target the Speaker.

The controversy has exposed divisions within the ruling Democratic Progressive Party (DPP). One DPP parliamentarian has publicly defended Suleman, breaking ranks with those calling for his removal. This internal dissent highlights a growing row over Suleman's remarks on religion.

The calls for Suleman's resignation are intensifying as the debate over his statements on religion continues. The situation has led to public questioning of the motives behind the demands for the Speaker's departure.

Why it mattersMalawians are questioning the motives and potential payment behind demands for the Speaker of Parliament's resignation.

Reserve Bank of Malawi Shuffles Top Management

Deputy Governor Kisukyabo Simwaka and two directors have been reassigned to other government roles in a significant shake-up at the central bank.

The Reserve Bank of Malawi has announced a major management reshuffle, with Deputy Governor for Operations Kisukyabo Simwaka and two directors being moved to new positions with immediate effect. The changes were detailed in Administrative Notice Number 1127, issued on October 20, 2026, and signed by RBM Governor George Partridge.

Deputy Governor Simwaka will join the Ministry of Finance, Economic Planning and Decentralisation, where he will be assigned to the Department of Economic Planning and Development. Boston Maliketi Banda, formerly the bank’s Director of Public Relations, is set to become a lecturer at the Malawi College of Accountancy.

Esau Banda, the Director of Human Resources, has been seconded to the Ministry of Industrialisation, Business, Trade and Tourism. He will serve as a Special Advisor on Trade and Industrialisation in his new role. The reshuffle impacts three senior positions within the central bank.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as senior leadership changes at the central bank could signal shifts in monetary policy or regulatory approaches.

Urban inequality widens in Malawi

New data shows a widening gap in household consumption in Malawi's cities over the past five years.

Malawi's urban areas have seen a significant increase in inequality over the last five years, according to the National Statistics Office (NSO). The Gini index for urban consumption rose from 39.0 in the 2019-20 financial year to 45.6 in 2024-25, a sharper rise than the national increase from 37.9 to 39.9. This widening gap occurred alongside a rise in national food poverty from 20.5 percent to 24 percent, even as overall national poverty declined.

The NSO's findings, based on the Sixth Integrated Household Survey, indicate that economic gains have not been evenly distributed among urban households. Urban poverty itself increased from 19.2 percent to 20.9 percent, though it remains lower than rural poverty. This trend exacerbates the challenges Malawians face with the rising cost of living, with the cost for an average household of six exceeding K1.11 million in August 2026.

Economist Marvin Banda stated that this urban inequality reflects an economy with a divide between those with secure employment and assets and those relying on informal trade and irregular incomes. He noted that poorer households are forced to spend most of their income on necessities, leaving little for savings or investment, while non-food inflation reached 31.9 percent in August 2026.

Why it mattersUrban households face widening consumption disparities, impacting their ability to save and invest due to rising costs and informal work.

Prophet Hara's book launch raises K70 million

Renowned Prophet Justice Hara launched four books at a Lilongwe event that also celebrated his birthday.

Prophet Justice Hara's book launch and birthday celebration at the Pearl Convention Centre in Lilongwe generated K70 million over two days. The event, held from Wednesday through Thursday, attracted religious leaders and creatives. Among the notable attendees, Prophet Shepherd Bushiri purchased a book for K8 million, and Pastor Khumbo Joram, founder of the Golden Nation Church, bought another for K7.5 million.

Bushiri described Hara's books as "treasures of wisdom" that will guide believers, emphasizing that ministry involves documenting truth for the future. Joram stated his purchase was to encourage Hara's continued production of knowledge for the church, noting the current need for such resources. The launched titles included "The Ever Increasing Success," "Who is Man?" and "Destiny is a Mystery."

Hara, also known as "The Game Changer," explained that the books stem from revelations he has received since secondary school. He expressed gratitude for the grace enabling him to share these insights, which he said teach about enduring success in Christ, human identity and authority, and encourage believers to pursue greater vision. The event featured musical performances by Joe Ikon, Onesimus, and Kamuzu Barracks Gospel Singers.

Why it mattersThe book launch generated K70 million, indicating a significant financial transaction and interest in the published works.
