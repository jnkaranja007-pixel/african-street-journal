Malawi's Silver Strikers Ladies reach CAF Women's Champions League semi-finals

Silver Strikers Ladies made history by becoming the first Malawian team to reach the semi-finals of the CAF Women’s Champions League COSAFA Qualifier after defeating Nsingizini.

Adelaide Migogo, chairperson of the National Women’s Football Committee, praised the Silver Strikers Ladies for their historic run to the semi-finals, calling it proof of the rising quality within Malawi’s domestic women’s football league. The team's 5-1 victory over Nsingizini Hotspurs secured their place in the last four, marking the first time a Malawian side has won multiple matches in the competition.

Migogo stated that the local league is getting stronger and that the progress is visible, pointing to Silver Strikers’ continental success as tangible evidence of development in Malawian women’s football at both grassroots and elite levels. The team, representing Malawi after winning the inaugural National Bank of Malawi Women’s Premiership, will now face Zambia’s Zesco Ndola Girls in the semi-final.

This achievement places the standard of Malawi’s women’s football firmly in the regional spotlight, according to Migogo. The team's performance, including their win against Costa do Sol and their overall advancement, reflects a significant step forward for the sport in the country.

Why it mattersMalawian women's football players and fans are directly affected by the Silver Strikers Ladies' historic advancement, which highlights the growing strength of the local league and provides a platform for regional recognition.

Malawi delays power projects, risks missing World Bank's 2030 target

Malawi has not yet presented any projects for support under the World Bank-backed Mission 300 initiative, potentially jeopardizing its goal to connect 300 million Africans to.

Malawi's slow progress on power project implementation may cause it to miss the 2030 target for the World Bank-supported Mission 300 power compact. The Public Private Partnership Commission (PPPC) and the Ministry of Energy confirmed that no specific projects have been fronted for support under the initiative, which aims to provide 250 million electricity connections to combat severe energy deficits.

Despite the lack of Mission 300 projects, the government is implementing the Accelerating Sustainable and Clean Energy Access Transformation In Malawi (Ascent Malawi) Project, according to Ministry of Energy spokesperson Joan Thaundi. This project, managed by the Ministry and the Electricity Supply Corporation of Malawi (Escom), began on July twenty-fifth, two thousand twenty-six, and has connected three thousand two hundred twenty-four customers through home systems, with a goal of eight hundred eleven thousand connections.

The country's energy challenges stem from power shortages leading to interruptions, with the Mpatamanga project remaining the flagship initiative, expected to add three hundred fifty-three megawatts to the grid. The government welcomes private sector investment in the energy sector and has created an enabling environment through Power Market Limited, Thaundi added.

Why it mattersMalawian citizens and businesses face ongoing power shortages and interruptions due to the slow pace of energy project development, impacting daily life and economic activity.

Football Legends Association faces scrutiny over K58 million fundraising gap

The Football Legends Association (FLA) is under fire for planning another fundraising event despite being K58 million short of its K250 million target, raising questions about.

The Football Legends Association (FLA) has drawn criticism for its decision to host a fundraising dinner this Saturday, aiming to reach a K250 million target for its members. This comes after the association announced it had already raised K192 million, leaving a K58 million gap that some social media users argue could be covered without a new event.

FLA chairperson Jim Kalua confirmed preparations are complete for the event at the Bingu International Conference Centre (BICC) in Lilongwe, with Minister of Youth, Sports and Culture Alfred Gangata expected to attend. The association plans to sell 300 imported tracksuits during the dinner.

Public comments, including those from Wellington Wa Maule Bango and Samson Zinyowe Chimchere, question the necessity of the new event for the remaining K58 million. Concerns have also been raised regarding the allocation of the K192 million already collected and how much of the total funds will directly benefit FLA members.

Why it mattersMalawi's former football players face uncertainty regarding the financial support intended for their welfare, as the Football Legends Association's fundraising strategy is being questioned.

Mtumbuka brands K700m loss an 'avoidable failure of corporate governance'

Malawi's state-backed fuel importer lost K700 million in a suspected fraudulent transaction, prompting calls for executive removal and an independent audit.

Malawi's state-backed fuel importer is facing political backlash after K700 million ($404,000 / £300,000) of state funds disappeared in a suspected fraudulent transaction. Dr. Matthews Mtumbuka, vice president of the opposition United Transformation Movement (UTM) and a Member of Parliament, described the incident as an "unacceptable and avoidable loss of public money."

Dr. Mtumbuka called for the immediate removal of executives involved in authorizing the transfer, stating, "Those involved should pack and go home." Civil society groups, including the Human Rights Defenders Coalition (HRDC), have also condemned the incident, demanding the immediate suspension of all officials who verified, approved, or processed the funds.

HRDC chairperson Michael Kaiyatsa and national coordinator Kelvin Chirwa warned against dismissing the event as external cybercrime without examining systemic internal failures. They advocate for an independent audit to determine if internal negligence or procedural breaches enabled the loss, and for the complete recovery of the missing funds.

Why it mattersThe disappearance of K700 million from a state-backed fuel importer directly affects Malawian citizens through potential impacts on fuel prices and the misuse of public funds.

High Court adjourns Dr Bobe murder trial until November

The murder trial of seven individuals accused of killing Dr Victoria Bobe has been adjourned by the High Court in Blantyre until November 30, 2026, after the State concluded its.

The High Court in Blantyre has adjourned the murder trial of seven people accused of killing Dr Victoria Bobe until November 30, 2026. Judge Ruth Chinangwa announced the adjournment after the State finished presenting all ten of its witnesses, with the final testimony coming from a security guard who was on duty the night of the attack.

Julius Mwanamanga, a guard employed by Mart Security Services, testified that armed robbers with covered faces attacked him and two others, tying them up before breaking into the house. He recounted hearing a gunshot shortly after being seized and another after the assailants left the property, but maintained he could not identify them.

Mwanamanga was assigned to protect Bobe’s residence that night because the regular guard was on leave. State lawyer Abdul Limbe pressed Mwanamanga on identifying the assailants, but the guard reiterated his inability to do so due to their covered faces, according to the Nyasa Times.

Why it mattersThe adjournment of this high-profile murder trial means that the accused individuals and the victim's family will face a prolonged wait for legal resolution.
