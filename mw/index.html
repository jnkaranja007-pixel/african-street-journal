Malawi protest group suspends fuel and blackout demonstrations for talks

The Concerned Citizens of Malawi-People Power Movement had planned nationwide protests on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, over fuel shortages and electricity blackouts.

The Concerned Citizens of Malawi-People Power Movement (CCM-PPM) has postponed nationwide demonstrations over fuel shortages and persistent electricity blackouts. The protests had been planned for Tuesday, November 17, 2026, to press government to address the energy crisis.

The organisers chose to engage government in dialogue instead of going ahead with the demonstrations. The energy crisis affects households and businesses alike, and the group said it would seek talks rather than street action.

The postponement opens a period of discussion between the group and government.

Why it mattersHouseholds and businesses still face blackouts and fuel shortages, and the outcome of the talks will decide whether the planned street protests return.

Four in five Malawians with diabetes never diagnosed, campaigners say

Health groups want government to fund screening, early diagnosis and awareness, especially at primary healthcare level.

Roughly 78 per cent of diabetes cases in Malawi go undetected, according to the Malawi Health Equity Network (MHEN). Its executive director, George Jobe, put the number of adults aged 20 to 79 living with the condition in 2024 at an estimated 540,100, a prevalence of 5.5 per cent.

Speaking alongside Diabetes Association of Malawi president Clement Mandala, Jobe said the high number of missed cases shows people must be identified early and started on treatment without delay. He cited a 2024 study in which none of the 14 primary healthcare facilities assessed in Blantyre had insulin, and only 57 per cent could diagnose diabetes and offer selected management services.

The two bodies are urging government to raise domestic health financing and build diabetes prevention, screening and management into primary healthcare, so services sit closer to communities. Jobe warned that these gaps could make timely diagnosis and treatment hard to reach for patients in underserved areas.

Why it mattersPatients at primary healthcare facilities in Blantyre cannot get insulin or a diabetes diagnosis there, and people in underserved communities face the greatest difficulty reaching treatment.

Malawi prison officers sign mental wellness pact

A survey of 4,142 officers in the Southern Region revealed widespread distress from debt, alcohol, and family issues.

Prison officers in Malawi's Southern Region have signed a mental wellness pact with the Young Achievers for Development (YAD) and the Gift of the Givers Foundation. The agreement follows warnings about the pressure faced by officers, with a survey revealing significant distress. In 2025, suicide claimed 567 Malawians, including three prison officers.

A survey of 4,142 officers in the region identified 145 alcohol-related cases, 68 gambling cases, 105 family conflicts, and 149 debt cases. Demster Chimatiro, a Southern Region prison mental health clinical officer, stated that officers face high societal expectations alongside occupational stress, financial pressure, and substance abuse. Deputy commissioner Zacheus Malekano M’bawa acknowledged that the Service has prioritized security over mental health.

Jefferson Milanzie, executive director of YAD, whose Abambo Tibe Khonde Initiative handled six anonymous cases from prison officers last year, plans to expand the training nationally. He noted that prisoners' mental health also remains unaddressed. Gift of the Givers country director Sherifa Mia highlighted that officers are often perceived as "iron men in uniform" and their mental health needs are neglected.

Why it mattersPrison officers in Malawi's Southern Region will receive mental health support and counselling following a pact aimed at addressing stress and depression.

Politics · 2026-11-15

Chakwera stays silent on 2030 presidential bid

Former president Lazarus Chakwera says Malawi Congress Party members will decide his political future, despite the party's constitution barring him from contesting again.

Lazarus Chakwera, Malawi's former president and leader of the Malawi Congress Party (MCP), declined on Friday to disclose his political ambitions ahead of the 2030 General Elections. He said party members would determine his political future. His remarks come after the MCP lost the September 2025 General Election.

Questions have grown over whether Chakwera intends to seek another presidential mandate, even though the party's constitution bars him from contesting. Reporting by Times Group Malawi and Nyasa Times notes that his refusal to say whether he will run has revived speculation about his future.

Nyasa Times also asks whether the MCP is ready for genuine renewal after its 2025 defeat, or remains trapped by the internal dynamics that reformers have been fighting.

Why it mattersMalawi Congress Party members will decide Chakwera's candidacy, and the party's constitution bars him from standing, so the MCP must settle its leadership before 2030.

Security · 2026-11-15

Street racers turn wet Malawi road into a racetrack

A video shows cars speeding at night on a rain-soaked dual carriageway with spectators metres from the traffic.

Street racers used a rain-soaked dual carriageway in Malawi as a racetrack at night, with spectators standing on the tarmac near speeding vehicles. A video published on Malawi24's YouTube channel captured the dangerous activity under streetlights as rain fell.

Onlookers lined the lanes, some filming with their phones, as cars sped past them on the wet road. The Directorate of Road Traffic and Safety Services (DRTSS) states that speeding is a leading cause of road crashes in Malawi. Reckless and dangerous driving are offences under Malawi's Road Traffic Act.

There has been no indication that police or road traffic officers have apprehended the racers involved. The Directorate of Road Traffic and Safety Services (DRTSS) has previously identified speeding as a major contributor to road accidents in the country.

Why it mattersSpeeding on wet roads is a leading cause of road crashes in Malawi, endangering drivers and spectators.
