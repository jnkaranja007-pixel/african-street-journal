High Court acquits Egnat Katengeza of murder

The court found insufficient grounds to convict Egnat Katengeza of murdering her sister, Agnes Katengeza.

The High Court in Lilongwe has acquitted Egnat Katengeza of murdering her sister, Agnes Katengeza, a former Reserve Bank of Malawi employee. Agnes was found dead in the boot of her car behind Gateway Mall in Lilongwe on September 24, 2023, a day after she was reported missing.

Egnat’s lawyer, Khumbo Soko, welcomed the decision, stating the prosecution lacked sufficient evidence. The State had suggested a financial motive, pointing to Egnat as the sole beneficiary of Agnes’ pension benefits.

However, a separate ruling by Judge Bruno Kalemba found that Amos Katengeza, Egnat's co-accused, has a case to answer for the same murder charge.

Why it mattersEgnat Katengeza has been acquitted of murder charges, freeing her from the accusation.

Malawi farmers fear hunger over fertiliser delays

Thousands of growers have not received subsidised inputs, threatening the next harvest as planting rains approach.

Thousands of Malawian farmers face potential hunger as delays in receiving subsidised fertiliser threaten the upcoming harvest. The Farm Inputs Subsidy Programme (Fisp) has been criticised for failing to deliver vital inputs before the planting rains begin.

Joseph Mwasiya, chairperson of the Nzeru N’chuma Farmers Club in Lunia Village, Blantyre, reported that many farmers have not received their Fisp fertiliser or training on alternative blended fertilisers like Mbeya. This is critical as basal fertiliser should be applied at planting, with rains expected in the Southern Region soon.

Agriculture Minister Roza Mbilizi stated on Friday that the Fisp distribution is on track, with efforts prioritised for hard-to-reach areas and regions with early rainfall, citing 70 percent implementation in Chitipa. The programme, worth K140 billion, aims to benefit 1.2 million farming households.

Why it mattersMalawian farmers face hunger if they cannot plant their crops due to delayed fertiliser subsidies.

Ecama backs Malawi's tight monetary policy

The Economics Association of Malawi supports the Reserve Bank's decision to keep interest rates high due to inflation and El Niño.

The Economics Association of Malawi (Ecama) has endorsed the Reserve Bank of Malawi (RBM)’s decision to maintain a tight monetary policy. Ecama stated that ongoing inflationary pressures and potential El Niño impacts make lowering interest rates unadvisable.

At its fourth meeting of 2026, held between October 27 and 28, the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) decided to keep the Policy Rate at 24 percent. The committee also maintained the Liquidity Reserve Requirement (LRR) for local currency deposits at 12 percent and for foreign currency deposits at 3.75 percent.

Ecama's backing comes as the RBM aims to control inflation and mitigate risks associated with the El Niño weather phenomenon.

Why it mattersMalawi's tight monetary policy, supported by Ecama, affects the cost of borrowing and the overall economic climate for businesses and consumers.

Malawi opposition leader attacks divisive politics

Simplex Chithyola-Banda says rewarding loyalty over competence hinders national development.

Opposition Leader Simplex Chithyola-Banda stated that Malawi will remain underdeveloped unless leaders stop prioritizing political loyalty, tribal identity, and regional affiliation over competence. The Malawi Congress Party figure argued that progress is sabotaged by leaders who judge citizens based on their origin, political support, or family ties rather than their qualifications.

Chithyola-Banda expressed concern in a Facebook post about whether Malawi can escape these divisions and offer opportunities based on skills and ability. He identified this toxic culture as a major barrier to national development, noting that new governments often discard projects from previous administrations due to political opposition.

This practice of discarding existing projects and starting anew wastes resources and time, according to Chithyola-Banda. He asserted that Malawi continues to struggle for meaningful progress over 60 years after independence because political rivalry, tribal loyalties, and regional interests have repeatedly been placed above national development.

Why it mattersMalawians are affected because divisive politics and rewarding loyalty over competence are hindering the country's development and progress.

Business · 2026-11-03

Cfao Malawi car sales drop 30 percent

The company invests K24 billion in new showrooms and service centres despite an economic slowdown.

Malawi's automobile industry is facing an economic slowdown, with Cfao Malawi reporting a 30 percent drop in car sales. The country imports approximately 1,000 vehicles weekly, many of them secondhand, particularly in Lilongwe and Blantyre.

Despite the downturn, Cfao Malawi is investing K24 billion in modern showrooms and service centres in Blantyre and Lilongwe, including an K11 billion facility in Lilongwe unveiled on Thursday. Managing Director Kennedy Kabaghe expressed confidence in an eventual economic recovery and increased vehicle demand.

The company also noted challenges in introducing electric vehicles due to a lack of charging infrastructure. Deputy Minister of Industrialisation Edgar Tembo urged local manufacturing of spare parts using mineral resources to promote value addition and reduce import dependence.

Why it mattersMalawi's automobile sector is experiencing reduced sales and investment challenges due to the economic slowdown.
