Malawi opposition attacks government over corruption and fuel chaos

The Malawi Congress Party claims leadership vacuum and economic turmoil are deepening the nation's crisis.

Malawi’s main opposition party, the Malawi Congress Party (MCP), has accused the government of failing to provide leadership amidst widespread corruption, fuel shortages, and blackouts. MCP campaign director Moses Kunkuyu stated in Lilongwe that President Peter Mutharika's private trip to South Africa has increased public anxiety while essential medicines are also scarce in hospitals.

Kunkuyu claimed public confidence is eroding due to controversial appointments and the handling of legal cases, warning of damage to Malawi’s international reputation. He highlighted that prolonged fuel shortages have disrupted businesses and public services, while foreign currency scarcity hinders the import of critical supplies.

The MCP urged First Vice-President Jane Ansah to address the nation on these critical issues, including the fuel crisis. Chief Secretary Justin Saidi dismissed the MCP's concerns as political rhetoric, stating the party lacks the mandate to speak for all Malawians. President Mutharika departed for South Africa on October 6, 2026, for his fifth private visit.

Why it mattersMalawians face disruptions to businesses, public services, and healthcare due to government inaction and economic problems.

Police warn miners after two die in collapse

Central East Region Deputy Commissioner of Police Monica Katemba Msongole visited the site and urged safer practices.

Police have warned artisanal miners against unsafe practices after a mine wall collapse killed two people at the Makwenje mining site in Kasungu. Central East Region Deputy Commissioner of Police Monica Katemba Msongole visited the site on Wednesday to assess the situation following the fatal incident.

Msongole expressed condolences to the bereaved families and urged artisanal miners and the public to avoid dangerous mining activities. Her visit was part of a supervisory tour that also included inspections of Mthabua and Chatoloma Police Units and discussions on safety at the Mota-Engil construction site.

At the police units, she interacted with officers and praised their efforts in providing security to rural communities, urging them to maintain discipline and strengthen community policing. She also stressed cooperation with the Mota-Engil construction company for safety and effective traffic management.

Why it mattersTwo miners died in a mine wall collapse at Makwenje mining site in Kasungu.

Malawi poverty rises by 600 000 amid food price surge

World Bank report shows 10.5 million Malawians cannot meet basic needs as food poverty climbs to 24 percent.

Nearly 600 000 Malawians have fallen into poverty, bringing the total unable to meet basic needs to 10.5 million in 2025, according to the World Bank. This increase, from 9.9 million in 2020, is driven by rising food prices, climate shocks, and persistent inflation. The proportion of people living in food poverty also rose to 24 percent from 20.5 percent during the same period.

The World Bank's October 2026 Poverty and Equity Brief attributes the worsening poverty to the 2024 El Niño-induced drought and successive cyclones, which disrupted agriculture. An average inflation rate of 28.4 percent in 2025 squeezed purchasing power, with the impact most severe in rural areas where 86 percent of households rely on farming.

The Gini index for inequality rose from 37.9 to 39.9, indicating a widening gap. Centre for Social Concern economic governance officer Agnes Nyirongo urged policymakers to strengthen measures protecting purchasing power and improving food security, noting that food inflation erodes the income of the poor.

Why it mattersMalawians are struggling to afford basic necessities and food due to rising prices and climate impacts.

Malawi urged to demand answers on Russian job traps

Activists want safeguards for youths recruited for work in Russia ahead of the upcoming Russia-Africa Summit.

Malawi faces calls to seek accountability for alleged deceptive recruitment schemes that may be exposing Malawian youths to Russian war-related industries. As leaders prepare for the Russia-Africa Summit on October 28-29, 2026, in Moscow, human rights advocates are urging the government to secure protections for citizens. These include safeguards on recruitment, information sharing, consular access, trafficking investigations, and safe return for affected individuals.

Legal commentator Benedicto Kondowe stated that Malawi must prioritize its citizens' safety over diplomatic ties, emphasizing that no economic or political relationship justifies deceiving young people into war-zone industries. Concerns were heightened by reports from November 2025 alleging Malawian girls were among hundreds of African women recruited to work at Alabuga, a Russian industrial zone linked to military drone production for the Ukraine war.

Minister of Homeland Security Peter Mukhito previously expressed concern in August 2026 over deceptive employment schemes after four Malawian youths were reportedly trapped in Russian war-related industries. The Alabuga Start program targets young women with offers of work-study opportunities, including free travel and accommodation, but investigative reports suggest recruits face passport confiscation and pressure to assemble military equipment.

Why it mattersMalawian youths are at risk of being deceived into working in Russian war-related industries.

Malawi court cancels arrest warrants for ex-HRDC leaders

Court records show the case against Timothy Mtambo, Gift Trapence and McDonald Sembereka was withdrawn in 2020.

Lilongwe chief resident magistrate Austin Banda cancelled arrest warrants for three former Human Rights Defenders Coalition (HRDC) leaders on October 23, 2026. The State had sought the warrants, claiming Timothy Mtambo, Gift Trapence, and McDonald Sembereka breached bail conditions from a 2020 case. Banda found that court records confirmed the State withdrew the criminal case against the trio on October 1, 2020.

The HRDC leaders were arrested in March 2020 for allegedly plotting to march to State House amid protests against the 2019 election results. Their lawyer, Khwima Mchizi, argued that the case was discharged, meaning bail conditions no longer applied. Banda stated that the withdrawal meant there were no conditions for the three to observe, rendering the warrants unattainable.

Despite the cancellation, State prosecutor Prescott Mwayiulipo informed the court of the State's intention to revive the matter. Magistrate Banda advised the State to make a formal application to do so. Mchizi welcomed the ruling, stating they were prepared to challenge any attempt to revive the withdrawn case.

Why it mattersThe former HRDC leaders Timothy Mtambo, Gift Trapence, and McDonald Sembereka faced arrest warrants that were subsequently cancelled by a court.
