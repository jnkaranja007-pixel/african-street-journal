FAM tightens stadium security for Airtel Top 8 final

Football Association of Malawi officials have assured fans of enhanced security measures and gate management at Bingu National Stadium for the Airtel Top 8 final.

The Football Association of Malawi (FAM) has pledged to implement stringent security measures at Bingu National Stadium for the Airtel Top 8 final match between Mighty Wanderers and Ekhaya FC. This assurance follows concerns about existing security vulnerabilities and poor gate management at the venue, which were noted during a previous high-profile match. FAM club licensing and competitions manager Clement Kafwafwa stated that security, gate management, and access control will be significantly tightened to address these issues.

Kafwafwa urged supporters to arrive early and purchase tickets in advance to prevent congestion at the stadium entrances. He also called for discipline, fair play, and mutual respect among fans, players, and officials during the event. The Ministry of Youth, Sports and Culture confirmed that repairs have been completed on the perimeter fence, addressing the loopholes that allowed unauthorized entry during the NBS Bank Charity Shield match in April.

Sponsors Airtel Malawi have organized pre-match entertainment, including a performance by artist Lulu, to enhance the fan experience. The final is anticipated to be an exciting and competitive contest, with FAM aiming to provide a well-organized event for the expected large turnout of supporters eager to witness the clash between the established Mighty Wanderers and the emerging Ekhaya FC.

Why it mattersFootball fans attending the Airtel Top 8 final are directly affected by the promised tightened security and improved gate management at Bingu National Stadium.

Erratic Rains Collapse Maize Harvests, Threaten Food Security

Malawian households face worsening food insecurity as erratic rainfall and anticipated El Niño conditions decimate harvests and deplete reserves.

Christina Njala of Matapira village in Lilongwe has seen her maize harvest collapse, yielding only seven bags from land that once produced over forty. With only one bag remaining by August, she anticipates needing food assistance by September and fears the impact of an impending El Niño. Her plight is echoed nationally, with the Department of Disaster Management Affairs (Dodma) activating anticipatory drought interventions across twenty-three districts.

Dodma's preparedness plan, developed with stakeholders, aims to cushion households ahead of the new farming season, which is expected to be characterized by El Niño conditions. Data from the National Statistical Office’s Sixth Integrated Household Survey (IHS6) indicates that nearly half of Malawian households have experienced climatic shocks, including drought (forty-nine point nine percent), late onset of rains (forty-seven point nine percent), and early cessation (forty-four point six percent).

The Thirty-Third Southern Africa Regional Climate Outlook Forum (Sarcof-33) forecasts warmer than normal temperatures and mixed rainfall prospects, with below-normal rainfall expected in the south. Innocent Pangapanga, Director of the Centre for Agricultural Research and Development (Card), warned that these shocks erode livelihoods, particularly those dependent on agricultural production. He stressed the need for adequate funding for the National Food Reserve Agency and Agricultural Development and Marketing Corporation (Admarc) to ensure food affordability.

Why it mattersMalawian households are affected by collapsing harvests and depleted food reserves due to erratic rainfall and anticipated El Niño conditions, leading to potential food insecurity.

Malawi Hospitals Show Systemic Failure Amid Funding Gaps

Four central hospitals are experiencing systemic failures including broken infrastructure and staff shortages due to years of underfunding.

Malawi's health system is facing a crisis marked by systemic failure, with four central hospitals exhibiting severe issues. Cracked floors, non-functional equipment, and frequent power outages are physical manifestations of chronic underfunding that health workers say forces them to improvise in unsafe conditions. Patients are experiencing long waits for routine procedures, indicating a significant decline in service delivery.

This deterioration stems from a long-standing reliance on donor funding, which has decreased as external partners shifted priorities. The country's own financial mechanisms have not compensated for the reduced contributions, leaving critical gaps in the health budget. Malawi continues to fall short of the Abuja Declaration target, which recommends allocating 15 percent of the national budget to health.

The problems at Kamuzu Central, Queen Elizabeth, Mzuzu, and Zomba hospitals are not isolated incidents but symptoms of a deeper structural issue. The financing model no longer meets the nation's healthcare needs, highlighting a critical gap between resource allocation and the demands placed on the healthcare infrastructure. This has led to a situation described as a 'slow-burn collapse' that is now impossible to ignore.

Why it mattersMalawi's health sector is experiencing systemic failure due to underfunding, affecting patients with long waits and compromised safety, and health workers with inadequate resources.

Airport Officer Says 8km Visibility Was Safe for Landing

An air traffic officer told a parliamentary committee that 8 kilometers visibility was compatible with landing, despite his own observation of 5 kilometers.

An air traffic information officer at Mzuzu Airport testified before a parliamentary committee that the official weather report indicated 8 kilometers visibility, drizzle, fog, and overcast conditions, which he stated were compatible with landing under visual flight rules. The officer, who has 16 years of experience at the airport, said he communicated both the official meteorological data and his personal observation of 5 kilometers visibility to the pilot of the aircraft carrying former Vice President Saulos Chilima.

The testimony raises questions about the weather information available to the flight, which was expected at Mzuzu around 10:02 local time but contacted the officer at 10:12. The officer's personal observation of 5 kilometers visibility was to the east, south, and west of the airport. He clarified that the 8 kilometers figure originated from the Meteorological Department, while the 5 kilometers was his own assessment.

The pilot later indicated to the officer that the aircraft was proceeding towards Ekwendeni, located approximately 20 nautical miles north of Mzuzu. The committee questioned whether the 5 kilometers figure was an official meteorological measurement, to which the officer responded it was his personal observation.

Why it mattersThe testimony affects aviation safety investigations and potentially the understanding of the circumstances surrounding a plane crash involving public figures.

Sport · 2026-08-30

Silver Ladies finish fourth in CAF Women's Champions League/Cosafa Qualifier

The Malawian team's historic debut ended with a 4-0 loss to hosts Gaborone United Ladies in the third-place playoff.

Silver Strikers Ladies concluded their maiden CAF Women’s Champions League/Cosafa Qualifier appearance with a fourth-place finish after a 4-0 defeat to hosts Gaborone United Ladies in Botswana. The team reached the semi-finals in their debut, a feat head coach Patricio Kulemeka described as historic and indicative of Malawi women's football readiness for continental competition.

Despite the scoreline in the third-place playoff, the Malawian side showed resilience against Gaborone's early scoring and sustained pressure. Jessica Maponga opened the scoring for the hosts, with another goal confirmed by VAR. The team's journey included a 2-1 semi-final loss to Zesco Ndola Girls and earlier tournament wins against Costa Do Sol and Nsingizini Hotspurs.

The achievement marks the first time a Malawian women's team has reached the semi-finals of the tournament, generating excitement among supporters and setting higher expectations for future campaigns. Coach Kulemeka expressed pride in the squad's performance, stating their experience gained will lead to a stronger challenge for the gold medal next year.

Why it mattersMalawian women's football has demonstrated its capacity to compete at the continental level, potentially attracting more support and investment.
