Malawi health groups warn of stampede risk at job interviews

Health Equity Network and Universal Health Coverage Coalition urge crowd management for November 20-22 recruitment.

Health organisations in Malawi have raised concerns about potential stampedes during walk-in interviews for healthcare workers scheduled from November 20 to 22, 2026. The Malawi Health Equity Network (MHEN) and the Universal Health Coverage Coalition (UHCC) stated that the Ministry of Health must implement crowd management measures to ensure candidate safety.

MHEN and UHCC, in a joint statement signed by Yandura Chipeta and George Jobe, warned of chaos if the process is not properly managed, citing past experiences with large numbers of applicants. They recommended staggered reporting times, organised queues, adequate security, clear entry and exit points, and sufficient interview panels.

The groups also highlighted the need for fair career progression for serving health workers who have upgraded their qualifications, urging that they be given a chance to compete for senior positions. The Ministry of Health announced the recruitment drive, funded by the Global Fund, to improve service delivery and access to healthcare across all four regions.

Why it mattersThousands of job seekers attending walk-in interviews for healthcare positions face potential safety risks due to inadequate crowd management.

Joseph Njobvuyalema joins DPP from MCP

Veteran politician Njobvuyalema cited the Democratic Progressive Party's focused leadership and experienced team as reasons for his defection.

Veteran politician Joseph Njobvuyalema officially joined the Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) on Saturday, leaving the Malawi Congress Party (MCP) after many years. The move occurred during a ceremony at Ming’ongo Ground in Lilongwe, under Senior Chief Kalolo.

Njobvuyalema stated that the DPP's focused leadership and the maturity of its senior officials attracted him to the party. He believes the DPP's experienced leaders are well-positioned to address national challenges and drive development. The DPP welcomed Njobvuyalema, with Deputy Regional Governor Grey Kapute saying his experience will strengthen the party.

Njobvuyalema previously served as an MCP Member of Parliament for his constituency. His defection adds a new dynamic to Malawi's political landscape as parties prepare for future contests.

Why it mattersVeteran politician Joseph Njobvuyalema has switched political parties, moving from the Malawi Congress Party to the Democratic Progressive Party.

Business · 2026-11-08

CDH Investment Bank wins Malawi's Bank of the Year award

The London magazine award marks the 13th time CDHIB has received the honour.

Global Banking and Finance Review Magazine has named CDH Investment Bank (CDHIB) Malawi’s Investment Bank of the Year 2026.

The London magazine recognised the bank for its performance, innovation and leadership, marking the 13th time CDHIB has received the award. Chief executive Thoko Mkavea credited the bank’s 107 employees, clients, and business partners for the achievement. CDHIB is a subsidiary of Malawi Stock Exchange-listed Continental Holdings plc.

Mkavea stated that CDHIB will continue investing in talent, technology, and institutional integrity to elevate standards within the financial sector. The bank reported strong earnings growth and a deeper presence in Malawi’s capital markets as its after-tax profit climbs toward K41 billion in the first half of the year.

Why it mattersCDH Investment Bank's repeated award and strong financial performance benefit Malawi's financial sector.

Education · 2026-11-08

Malawi primary school children clean used condoms

Mponela 2 Primary School in Dowa District faces health and dignity crisis due to lack of security and sanitation.

Learners at Mponela 2 Primary School in Malawi's Dowa District are forced to clean up used condoms from their classrooms each morning before lessons begin. The school, serving over 3,000 students, has only two toilets and lacks a fence, exposing it to vandalism and creating a health hazard. Marium Mira, treasurer for the Parents Teacher Association, called the situation heartbreaking.

Deputy head teacher Dickson Muyaba stated that the absence of a fence leaves the school vulnerable to intruders and property damage, while the poor sanitation poses a risk of disease outbreaks. He appealed to the government for urgent assistance, specifically a fence to protect school property. Florence Ndovi from the Community Social Accountability Committee confirmed that the council has plans to provide two classroom blocks, a teacher's house, two toilets, and a fence.

Clinical psychologist Professor Chiwoza Bandawe warned that forcing children to clean up discarded condoms violates their dignity and can have serious psychological effects, including confusion, embarrassment, disgust, fear, or curiosity about sexual behaviour. He stressed that a classroom should be a safe and respected environment for learning.

Why it mattersOver 3,000 primary school children in Malawi are subjected to unsanitary and undignified conditions, including cleaning used condoms, due to inadequate school infrastructure and security.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Malawi human rights body faces leadership gap

Commissioners await swearing-in as executive secretary's contract nears expiry.

The Malawi Human Rights Commission (MHRC) faces a potential leadership vacuum as newly appointed commissioners have not yet been sworn into office. This situation is complicated by the impending departure of the current executive secretary, Habiba Osman, whose 36-month contract ends on November 1, 2026.

Osman's contract will not be renewed despite her interest in continuing, a decision made by the previous commissioners led by Chikondi Chijozi-Jere. While no reasons were given, the commission thanked Osman for her service and will begin recruiting a new executive secretary. Osman stated her contract runs until December 2026 and that the deputy executive secretary can manage operations.

Presidential Press Secretary Cathy Maulidi referred questions to the Office of the President and Cabinet, which is preparing the swearing-in ceremony programme. Chief Secretary Justin Saidi assured Malawians there would be no leadership gap. Parliament's Legal Affairs Committee chairperson Gilbert Khonyongwa stressed the importance of MHRC's operations and stated the committee would seek clarification on transitional arrangements.

Why it mattersMalawians may experience a disruption in the oversight of human rights protections due to a potential leadership gap at the Malawi Human Rights Commission.
