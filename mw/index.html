Malawi steps up gold purchases to fund fuel imports

Information Minister Shadric Namalomba says the government expects fuel supplies to improve, but cannot give a date for ending the crisis.

Malawi is increasing purchases of locally mined gold to earn foreign exchange for importing fuel and other essential goods, Information Minister Shadric Namalomba said Tuesday. The government expects fuel supplies to improve significantly as the purchases rise.

Speaking on Times Radio’s Kulinji Programme, Namalomba said the government had increased investment in local gold buying. He said the Ministry of Finance had released US$30 million for fuel procurement; Petroleum Importers Limited had also bought fuel.

Namalomba said the government could not give a firm date for ending the fuel crisis. The gold purchases are intended to add foreign exchange for imports, but he gave no timetable for improved supply.

Why it mattersFuel buyers and importers still have no firm end date for the crisis, while the government has released US$30 million for procurement.

Business · 2026-11-18

EGENCO suspends William Liabunya over K2.6 billion payment

The board also suspended company secretary Videlia Mluwira as the state-owned power generator faces scrutiny over the payment.

The board of Malawi’s Electricity Generation Company (EGENCO) has suspended William Liabunya over a controversial K2.6 billion payment. The move comes barely a year after Liabunya returned to lead the state-owned power generator.

EGENCO also suspended Videlia Mluwira, its director of corporate services and company secretary, according to Malawi24. The report describes Liabunya as being back in trouble at the company but gives no further details about the payment or the suspensions.

The suspensions place the company’s chief executive and company secretary out of their roles, but the report does not state who will replace them or what action the board plans next.

Why it mattersEGENCO has suspended its leader and company secretary over the K2.6 billion payment, leaving the board’s next steps unstated.

Malawi urges irrigation push ahead of potential Super El Niño

An opinion article says farmers should expand dry-season crops and repair irrigation schemes to build food reserves before extreme weather hits.

A Times Group Malawi opinion article by MacPherson Tadziwa Mdalla urges the government, businesses and farming communities to expand irrigation-based food production ahead of a potential Super El Niño. It proposes winter cropping around Lake Malawi and along major river systems, as well as rehabilitating existing schemes.

Mdalla says Malawi relies heavily on rain-fed agriculture and that droughts, floods and crop failures have left millions vulnerable to food insecurity. Smallholders already grow crops along riverbanks and wetlands in the dry season, but limited access to irrigation equipment, finance, quality inputs and storage constrains their output.

The article calls for farmers to receive seeds, fertiliser, extension services and market access, with the aim of producing food for reserves and supporting household incomes. It also identifies Lake Chilwa, Lake Malombe, the Shire, Songwe and Ruo rivers, and dams and wetlands as water resources that could support irrigation.

Why it mattersSmallholder farmers growing along rivers and wetlands lack irrigation equipment, finance, quality inputs and storage, while the article calls for expanded winter cropping before a potential climate shock.

Climate · 2026-11-18

Storms strip roofs from Mangochi health centre and school classroom

Strong winds blew off the roofs at Phirilongwe Health Centre and nearby staff houses, and a classroom at Majuni Secondary School has been closed.

Stormy rains and strong winds have torn roofs from Phirilongwe Health Centre and staff houses in Chilipa, Mangochi District, leaving patients and health workers exposed to harsh weather. A classroom at Majuni Secondary School in Namwera was also damaged, disrupting learning.

Bishop Witmos, public relations officer for Mangochi District Council, said the council has begun assessing how badly the health centre and staff houses were hit. No students or teachers were hurt, and the damaged classroom was shut temporarily to avoid risk.

The classroom had lost its roof three days earlier in similar storms, Witmos said. The council is urging residents across the district to stay alert as the rainy season continues, to watch changing conditions and to follow safety guidance from authorities.

Why it mattersPatients and staff at Phirilongwe are now without full shelter from the weather, and the classroom's closure has interrupted lessons at Majuni.

Sport · 2026-11-18

FAM appoints Randy Waldrum as Scorchers coach

The American replaces Lovemore Fazili, whose tenure ends after he guided Malawi’s women to historic achievements.

The Football Association of Malawi has appointed American coach Randy Waldrum to lead the Scorchers, Malawi’s women’s national football team, ending Lovemore Fazili’s tenure. FAM president Fleetwood Haiya announced the appointment on Wednesday at a press briefing in Lilongwe.

Waldrum previously coached Nigeria’s women’s national team, known as the Super Falcons. Fazili’s tenure ended despite his role in guiding the Scorchers to historic achievements, Malawi24 reported.

Fazili was also nominated for the 2026 Confederation of African Football Coach of the Year award. Caf released its 10 nominees on Tuesday, ahead of the ceremony in Lagos, Nigeria.

Why it mattersWaldrum now takes charge of the Scorchers, while Fazili’s nomination puts the outgoing coach among Caf’s 10 award contenders.
