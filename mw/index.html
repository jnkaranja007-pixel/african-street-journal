Speaker Suleman Orders Amaryllis Hotel Inquiry Report Release

Parliamentary Speaker Sameer Suleman has directed the release of a report into the controversial acquisition of Amaryllis Hotel within seven days.

Parliamentary Speaker Sameer Suleman has ordered the release of a report concerning the acquisition of Amaryllis Hotel within seven days. This directive follows a long-running controversy surrounding the hotel's purchase, bringing the matter into the public domain. The move signals a potential shift in how parliamentary inquiries are handled, with a focus on making findings accessible.

Critics, however, remain skeptical about the effectiveness of such inquiries. Political scientist Augustine Magolowondo noted that while joint committees could pool expertise, their success hinges on the implementation of recommendations. Willy Kambwandira of the Centre for Social Accountability and Transparency stated that parliamentary investigations have often failed to produce consequences, becoming politicized public relations exercises.

Speaker Suleman has advocated for effective, non-ceremonial committees, suggesting a desire for tangible outcomes. This latest directive regarding the Amaryllis Hotel report could be a test case for his commitment to ensuring that parliamentary oversight leads to accountability and justice, rather than just gathering dust.

Why it mattersInvestors may be affected by the potential for increased transparency and accountability in government acquisitions, which could influence business dealings and regulatory oversight.

Malawi Minerals Fuel Polish Industrial Growth, Offer Few Local Jobs

Rare earth elements extracted in Malawi will be processed in Poland, creating high-tech jobs there while offering limited, low-wage employment in the African nation.

Natural resources from Malawi's Songwe Hill Rare Earths project are set to drive industrial development and create high-paying jobs in Poland, over 8,000 kilometers away. Canadian firm Mkango Resources, operating through its local entity Lancaster Exploration, will conduct only basic extraction processes like mining and crushing at the Phalombe site. The resulting unrefined Mixed Rare Earth Carbonate (MREC) will be shipped to Puławy, Poland.

In Poland, Mkango Resources has partnered with Grupa Azoty to build a $212 million separation plant. This facility will perform the advanced chemical refining and separation of valuable elements such as neodymium, praseodymium, dysprosium, and terbium. This process is expected to generate hundreds of skilled, long-term career opportunities for chemical engineers, metallurgists, and specialized plant operators.

Malawians involved in the Songwe Hill project are likely to secure only short-term, low-wage positions primarily involving manual labor, site clearing, driving, and basic equipment maintenance. The significant value addition and industrial rewards from the extracted rare earth minerals will predominantly benefit Poland, contrasting with the limited economic gains for the local Malawian workforce.

Why it mattersMalawians are affected by the export of raw materials that are processed abroad, leading to limited job creation and economic benefits within their own country.

Ex-Mera CEO Bail Ruling Looms Amid Hacking Allegations

A Lilongwe court will decide Thursday on bail for Collins Magalasi, accused of aiding phone hacking of President Mutharika and officials.

The Chief Resident Magistrate’s Court in Lilongwe is scheduled to rule on Thursday regarding bail for former Malawi Energy Regulatory Authority (MERA) CEO Collins Magalasi. Magalasi faces accusations of aiding an individual to hack the phones of President Peter Mutharika and several senior government officials, including ministers and police leadership. The alleged offenses occurred between June and August this year, and Magalasi was arrested on Monday, charged under the Electronic Transactions and Cyber Security Act.

A key point of contention during the bail hearing involved conflicting accounts of the police search at Magalasi’s residence. His lawyer, Andy Kawonga, stated he observed at least 35 officers in five vehicles, while Superintendent Isaac Ndala reported approximately 16 officers in three vehicles. Kawonga argued that with all relevant items, including laptops, seized, there was little justification for continued detention due to concerns about witness tampering.

Conversely, Superintendent Ndala asserted that Magalasi’s financial means could enable him to interfere with witnesses if released. He also raised the possibility of Magalasi recruiting further IT experts for his alleged scheme, an assertion that Magalasi’s lawyer dismissed as speculative. The court’s ruling on Thursday will address these arguments and determine Magalasi's pre-trial custody status.

Why it mattersCollins Magalasi's alleged involvement in hacking the phones of President Mutharika and senior government officials raises concerns about the security of sensitive communications and data.

Malawi Officials Tour BURN Clean Cooking Operations

Government delegation visits clean cooking company BURN's facilities in Blantyre as Malawi pursues climate goals.

A delegation from Malawi's Ministry of Natural Resources and Climate Change toured BURN's clean cooking operations in Njuli area, Blantyre. The visit, led by Deputy Minister Chipiliro Mpinganjira and Principal Secretary Dr. Misheck Muthali, aimed to assess the company's contribution to the nation's climate objectives. BURN, a significant distributor of clean cooking appliances, has distributed over 160,000 biomass stoves in Lilongwe, Mwanza, and Blantyre, some equipped with digital sensors for usage monitoring and carbon credit activities.

The delegation also visited Ndirande Ward in Blantyre to meet with BURN customers and observe the direct impact of clean cooking technologies on households. Margaret Chirwa, an ECOA Char stove user, reported significant savings on fuel expenses. She stated that her daily fuel costs have decreased from approximately K4,000 to K2,000, while still managing to prepare three meals daily.

Malawi has committed to reducing greenhouse gas emissions by 51 percent by 2040, a target that necessitates substantial investment in practical climate solutions and access to international climate finance. BURN's work with clean cooking technologies aligns with these national climate goals by offering alternatives that reduce reliance on traditional fuels and potentially contribute to carbon credit initiatives.

Why it mattersThe government's tour of BURN's operations highlights efforts to meet climate goals, potentially impacting the market for clean energy solutions.

No arrests made 15 months after Cricket Malawi manager's murder

Family of Arjun Menon, 48, awaits answers as police investigation into his May 2025 death stalls without any arrests.

Fifteen months have passed since Arjun Menon, former Cricket Malawi Operations Manager and national coach, was killed, yet his family continues to seek justice with no arrests made in the case. Menon, aged 48, was attacked and died on May 11, 2025, in Nancholi, Blantyre, while reportedly on his way home after a night out.

The lack of progress in the investigation has led to mounting questions regarding the probe into Menon's death. The incident occurred over a year ago, and the absence of any arrests or public updates from authorities has left the family in distress and uncertainty.

Malawi24, an independent news platform, reported on the ongoing situation, highlighting the family's persistent wait for answers. The case remains open, with the circumstances surrounding Menon's death still under investigation by local police.

Why it mattersThe family of Arjun Menon is affected by the lack of arrests and answers 15 months after his death, leaving them in distress and uncertainty.
