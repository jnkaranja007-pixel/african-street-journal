Ex-meteorologist says plane could have landed despite weather

A former weather officer testified that Mzuzu Airport had adequate visibility for landing on the day Vice-President Saulos Chilima died in a plane crash, despite overcast.

A former Mzuzu Climate Change and Meteorological Station officer-in-charge stated that the military plane carrying Vice-President Saulos Chilima and eight others could have landed at Mzuzu Airport on June 10, 2024. Olive Chavunguma told a parliamentary committee that her station recorded visibility of eight kilometers that morning, which she described as adequate for landing. She added that the plane had previously operated and landed under similar conditions, suggesting that any limiting factors were encountered en route to Chikangawa.

Chavunguma, who has 35 years of aviation experience, noted that while the weather was generally overcast with drizzles and fog, the visibility at the airport was sufficient. She clarified that Mzuzu Airport’s weather records should not be directly compared to conditions in Chikangawa due to significant differences in terrain, elevation, and atmospheric conditions. She could not confirm if bad weather or very low visibility caused the crash in Chikangawa.

The parliamentary inquiry also heard that Mzuzu Airport has operated for over ten years without functional air navigation equipment or systems to record radio communications. This revelation raises questions about the airport's safety standards and its role in the fatal crash. Chavunguma maintained her station's eight-kilometer visibility reading, even when questioned about a report indicating less than eight kilometers, attributing the difference to external assessments.

Anti-Corruption Bureau Questions Nocma CEO Over Fraudulent Payment

The National Oil Company of Malawi CEO was summoned for questioning after revelations of a $403,605 payment made based on fraudulent correspondence, while two other officials.

The Anti-Corruption Bureau (ACB) has summoned National Oil Company (Nocma) chief executive officer Engineer Emmanuel Matapa for questioning regarding a $403,605 (about K700 million) payment made based on fraudulent correspondence. ACB spokesperson Jacqueline Ngongonda confirmed Matapa was questioned yesterday morning about the payment to scammers. The bureau initiated an investigation into suspected abuse of office by those involved in the transaction, based on information from Nocma’s own press release.

The investigation follows the arrest of Nocma deputy CEO Miklas Reuben and senior operations manager Thokozani Jacob Sesani by the Malawi Police Service on Wednesday. Police stated the two may face charges including negligence in preserving money and misuse of public office. Nocma spokesperson Raymond Likambale previously stated that the government had not lost money, as the recipient bank confirmed receipt of the funds.

The payment in question was for services rendered by Mozhandling for the processing of the MT Flora, a vessel that transported 15.1 million litres of fuel procured through a Government-to-Government system. Nocma was designated a central role in petroleum product importation following the government's transition from an open tender system in November 2024.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the state-owned oil company is under investigation for a fraudulent payment, raising concerns about financial management and potential misuse of public funds.

Village leader fences musician's mausoleum over disrespect

A wooden fence has been erected around the mausoleum of late musician Lucius Banda in Sosola Village, Balaka, to prevent visitors from engaging in disrespectful behavior.

A wooden fence now surrounds the mausoleum of musician Lucius Banda in Sosola Village, Balaka, following a decision by the local village headman. The traditional leader stated that the fence was necessary to address growing disrespect at the gravesite, citing instances of visitors drinking alcohol, taking pictures, and stepping on other graves.

The village headman explained that the structure, built by Banda's relatives and friends within the Sosola Village Cemetery, was intended to restore order and protect the dignity of the burial site. He acknowledged that he was not consulted on the mausoleum's construction but did not object at the time and attended its opening ceremony.

While the village headman aims to preserve cultural respect for the deceased, the family of the late musician has not yet commented on the newly installed fence. The action by the traditional leader highlights local concerns about maintaining decorum at prominent burial sites.

Why it mattersThe action taken by the village headman to fence off the mausoleum directly impacts the cultural practices and respect shown at the burial site of a prominent Malawian musician.

Truck tyre burst kills four on Mchinji road

A Volvo articulated truck lost control after a tyre failure, colliding with a motorcycle and causing multiple fatalities in Mchinji District.

Four people died in a road accident in Kankhande Village along the M12 Road in Mchinji District, when a truck tyre burst and the vehicle collided with an oncoming motorcycle. Mchinji Police Publicist Limbani Mpinganjira confirmed the incident occurred yesterday involving a Volvo articulated truck and an unregistered Lifan motorcycle.

Police stated the truck driver lost control after a front tyre burst, leading to a head-on collision with the motorcycle before the truck overturned. The motorcycle rider, Charles Petro, a 29-year-old Zambian national, and his passenger, Judith Derick, sustained fatal injuries and were pronounced dead at Mchinji District Hospital. Francis Mwafongo, 42, who was travelling in the truck, also died from his injuries.

In a rare positive note, Judith Derick's three-year-old child, who was strapped to her back, miraculously survived the devastating crash. Police investigations into the cause of the accident are ongoing.

Why it mattersThe accident directly impacted rural livelihoods and transport safety, affecting individuals and families involved in the crash.

Malawi Agriculture Sector Shifts Focus to Commercialization

Stakeholders at the National Agriculture Fair agreed to move from production-only to a value-driven industry to boost incomes, jobs, and exports amidst declining trade.

Malawi's agriculture sector is set to transform from a production-centric model to a commercially-driven industry, aiming to increase incomes, create jobs, and expand exports. This shift was agreed upon by sector players during the opening of the 22nd National Agriculture Fair in Blantyre. The move addresses declining export growth and persistent business challenges, aligning with the agricultural commercialization pillar of Malawi 2063, the nation's long-term development strategy.

Minister of Agriculture Roza Mbilizi emphasized the need to change how agricultural commodities are produced, processed, valued, and marketed to achieve the country's development aspirations. This comes as Malawi's exports have fallen by 31 percent between 2014 and 2023, with the number of exporting firms significantly declining. The survival rate for new exporters is also critically low, with tobacco dominating the export basket at 61 percent.

The World Bank attributes Malawi's export weakness to low production capacity, insufficient skills, and limited foreign exchange, compounded by non-tariff barriers that raise trade costs. In response, the government is implementing programs like the Agriculture Commercialisation Project 2, which provides grants to producer organizations and small businesses, alongside initiatives supporting irrigation, mechanization, processing, and market information access.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by the shift towards commercialization, which aims to increase their incomes and create stronger businesses through improved production, processing, and market access.
