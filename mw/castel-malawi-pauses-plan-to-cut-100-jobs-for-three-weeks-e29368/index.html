Castel Malawi pauses plan to cut 100 jobs for three weeks

The brewer, which says a tough trading climate is hurting its operations, agreed with its union to suspend the retrenchments while talks with government take place.

Source: Nyasa Times · 2026-11-16 · 1 min read

Castel Malawi, which the Nyasa Times describes as the country's second-largest taxpayer, had planned to retrench 100 employees in the second week of November, citing a difficult business environment. The company has now put that decision on hold after talks with the Castel Malawi Workers Union (CAMAWU).

The two sides agreed to suspend the retrenchment for three weeks. Management has reportedly blamed a 110 percent excise tax on spirits, beer smuggling and a growing supply of illicit alcoholic products on the local market. CAMAWU president Israel Mughogho said the union wants roundtable talks with the Ministry of Labour, the Malawi Revenue Authority, the Ministry of Trade and the Ministry of Finance.

Mughogho said the illicit products evade taxes and also pose a health and safety risk to Malawians. The union is seeking policy changes that could reverse the planned job losses.

Why it mattersThe 100 affected workers keep their jobs for now, and the union has three weeks to win policy changes before any cuts can go ahead.
