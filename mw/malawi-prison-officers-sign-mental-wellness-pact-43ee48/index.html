Malawi prison officers sign mental wellness pact

A survey of 4,142 officers in the Southern Region revealed widespread distress from debt, alcohol, and family issues.

Sources: The Nation Malawi and Nyasa Times · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Prison officers in Malawi's Southern Region have signed a mental wellness pact with the Young Achievers for Development (YAD) and the Gift of the Givers Foundation. The agreement follows warnings about the pressure faced by officers, with a survey revealing significant distress. In 2025, suicide claimed 567 Malawians, including three prison officers.

A survey of 4,142 officers in the region identified 145 alcohol-related cases, 68 gambling cases, 105 family conflicts, and 149 debt cases. Demster Chimatiro, a Southern Region prison mental health clinical officer, stated that officers face high societal expectations alongside occupational stress, financial pressure, and substance abuse. Deputy commissioner Zacheus Malekano M’bawa acknowledged that the Service has prioritized security over mental health.

Jefferson Milanzie, executive director of YAD, whose Abambo Tibe Khonde Initiative handled six anonymous cases from prison officers last year, plans to expand the training nationally. He noted that prisoners' mental health also remains unaddressed. Gift of the Givers country director Sherifa Mia highlighted that officers are often perceived as "iron men in uniform" and their mental health needs are neglected.

Why it mattersPrison officers in Malawi's Southern Region will receive mental health support and counselling following a pact aimed at addressing stress and depression.
