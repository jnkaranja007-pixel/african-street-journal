Malawi's doctors' union calls for health-sector emergency

Shortages of medicines, fuel and staff have left public hospitals unable to deliver essential care, the union says.

Source: Malawi24 · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

The Medical Doctors Union of Malawi (MDUM) wants the government to declare a national emergency in the health sector, pointing to shortages of medicines, medical supplies, fuel, protective equipment and healthcare workers. In a statement dated November 26, 2026, titled "A Health System on Its Knees", the union said public hospitals are finding it hard to keep essential services running.

Some ambulances have been grounded for lack of fuel, leaving patients stranded when they need urgent care. MDUM cited a mother who reportedly died with her stillborn baby at Mponela Rural Hospital after two functional ambulances were grounded by fuel shortages. It also said a man from Thandaza Village in Traditional Authority Kasakula, Ntchisi District, died on October 20, 2026, while walking about 25 kilometres in search of medical attention.

The union warned that more lives could be lost unless the government moves quickly to restore essential health services.

Why it mattersPatients needing urgent care in public hospitals are being left stranded as fuel shortages ground ambulances, and the government is being asked to act.
