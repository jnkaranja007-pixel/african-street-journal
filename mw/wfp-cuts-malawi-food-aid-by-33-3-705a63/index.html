WFP cuts Malawi food aid by 33.3%

200,000 people risk hunger as the World Food Programme faces a $142.3 million funding shortfall.

Source: Times Group Malawi · 2026-10-28 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The World Food Programme (WFP) has reduced its planned humanitarian assistance for Malawi's lean season by 33.3 percent, cutting the target from 600,000 to 400,000 people. This reduction means 200,000 individuals face potential hunger between October 2026 and March 2027 due to funding constraints. WFP operations face a $142.3 million shortfall, forcing a narrowed geographical focus to only Chikwawa, Nsanje, and Phalombe districts.

The funding gap threatens essential programs including emergency food aid, nutrition support, school feeding, and refugee assistance. WFP stated that vulnerable households will lack support during a period of high food insecurity, likely leading them to eat fewer meals and adopt negative coping strategies. Centre for Agricultural Research and Development Director Innocent Pangapanga Phiri warned this could worsen food insecurity and increase reliance on climate shock coping mechanisms.

Centre for Humanitarian Assistance and Social Protection Executive Director Kenneth Sakala recommended the government reallocate resources and expand the Social Cash Transfer Programme. He also advised pre-positioning maize and cash before January price peaks and funding last-mile transport. Phiri urged strengthening food price monitoring and market stabilisation, including maize price monitoring and addressing supply bottlenecks.

Why it matters200,000 Malawians face hunger due to reduced food aid from the World Food Programme.
