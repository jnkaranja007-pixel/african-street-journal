Blantyre-area water customers pay extra during supply cuts

Chatha resident Elizabeth Tembo says interruptions leave her buying bottled water and paying workers to fetch borehole water, on top of her monthly bills.

Source: The Nation Malawi · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Residents of Chatha, on the outskirts of Blantyre City, say piped water is often cut despite monthly bills. Elizabeth Tembo, a single mother of two, said the service providers give a month rarely passes without interruptions, and that she is often told pipes have been vandalised or burst, or that power blackouts have stopped pumping.

Tembo said that when supply fails she buys five-litre bottles of drinking water at K4 000 to K6 000 from supermarkets, roughly K40 000 a week if she buys bottled water all week. She uses a community borehole when she has no money, paying part-time workers K700 to K1 000 per bucket, and says the borehole water sometimes upsets her stomach.

The Consumers Association of Malawi's executive director, John Kapito, described the constant disruptions faced by customers who pay for piped water as appalling. Water for People data cited in the report shows only 18% of Malawians have access to safely managed drinking water services.

Why it mattersChatha households paying piped water bills are also spending on bottled drinking water and borehole buckets whenever supply fails.
