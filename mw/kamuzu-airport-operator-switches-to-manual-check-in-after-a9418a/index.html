Kamuzu airport operator switches to manual check-in after storm damage

Airport Development Limited says storm damage to local network infrastructure has disrupted its check-in system, while passengers report long queues and slow starts by staff.

Source: Malawi24 · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Storm damage to local network infrastructure has disrupted check-in at Kamuzu International Airport in Malawi, forcing staff to process passengers manually, Airport Development Limited (ADL), the company that runs the airports, said. The airport has advised travellers to arrive four hours before departure and carry printed copies of their travel documents.

Passengers say arriving early makes little difference because queues keep growing and staff are slow to start. Bampsy Chiumia said some travellers arrived at 2am for an 8:10am flight, but workers came around 6am. Eddy Blum recalled staff handwriting flight tickets four days earlier.

Solomon Arthur Williams Chilemba said the delays make travelling by coach to Blantyre look like a better option for passengers connecting there. Some social media users have also questioned whether ADL's chief executive, Zione Themba, has the experience to run the airports; the Malawi Unfiltered platform made claims about political and tribal influence in appointments but provided no evidence.

Why it mattersTravellers connecting through Blantyre face a choice between flying with four hours of check-in and taking the road, and must carry printed documents to get through.
