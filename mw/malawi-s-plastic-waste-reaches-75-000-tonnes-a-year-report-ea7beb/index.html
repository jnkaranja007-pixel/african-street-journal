Malawi’s plastic waste reaches 75,000 tonnes a year, report estimates

A 2021 estimate put about 80% of the country’s plastic waste in single-use plastics, amid concern over disposal along the Lilongwe-Kasungu road.

Source: The Nation Malawi · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

A 2021 report estimated Malawi generates about 75,000 tonnes of plastic waste each year, with roughly 80% made up of single-use plastics. An editorial in The Nation Malawi raised concern about plastic disposal in a village along the road between Lilongwe and Kasungu.

The editorial says discarded plastics can block drains, contribute to flooding and contaminate water sources. It also warns that livestock and other animals can be harmed by swallowing plastic, while open burning exposes communities to harmful substances. The World Health Organisation has raised concerns about plastic pollution’s risks to human health and ecosystems.

Malawi has taken legal steps to restrict thin plastics after years of court battles and advocacy by environmental activists. The editorial argues that practical implementation, rather than laws alone, is needed to protect the environment.

Why it mattersFarmers and livestock owners face risks from plastic waste that can contaminate water and harm animals; Malawi’s annual waste is estimated at 75,000 tonnes.
