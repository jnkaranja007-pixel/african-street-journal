Nurse takes newborn home for oxygen after Malawi clinic loses power

The baby was stabilised at the nurse’s home and referred to Mitundu Community Hospital, while the clinic’s outage has lasted about two and a half months.

Source: Malawi24 · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

A nurse at Dickson Health Centre in Lilongwe took a newborn to his home for oxygen after the baby was born in critical condition at 1:28am on November 27 and the clinic had no electricity. Nurse Tonex Mkwezalamba said the baby’s Apgar score was 3 out of 10 at birth and rose to 5 after resuscitation.

The facility’s power had been off for about two and a half months following an electrical fault, and ambulance contacts were unreachable, according to information from Mkwezalamba. Security officer Thomas Kavalo helped carry an oxygen concentrator to the nurse’s home, where the baby stabilised. By Saturday morning, the newborn was referred to Mitundu Community Hospital for further care.

Mkwezalamba said the health centre also lacks a newborn weighing scale and protective equipment, including gloves. He wants the electricity fault fixed, saying many of the facility’s services depend on power.

Why it mattersHealth workers and newborn patients at Dickson Health Centre face another emergency without clinic power, which has been out for about two and a half months.
