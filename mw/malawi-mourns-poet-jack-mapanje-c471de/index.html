Malawi mourns poet Jack Mapanje

The writer, known for exploring freedom and power, died in the United Kingdom.

Source: Nyasa Times · 2026-11-05 · 1 min read

Distinguished Malawian poet, linguist, and academic Jack Mapanje has died in the United Kingdom. Mapanje, born on March 25, 1944, built an international reputation for his metaphorical and subtly subversive poetry. His work often examined themes of freedom, power, and belonging, drawing attention during Malawi's one-party era.

The Malawi Writers Union of Academic and Non-fiction Authors paid tribute to Mapanje, hailing his significant contributions to scholarship and literature. His acclaimed collections include "Of Chameleons and Gods" and "The Chattering Wagtails of Mikuyu Prison." His memoir, "And Crocodiles Are Hungry at Night," detailed his experiences of imprisonment, exile, and resilience.

Mapanje's legacy will endure through his body of work and his example as a writer who used language for artistic expression and quiet defiance. For many readers, students, and writers, his influence is profound.

Why it mattersMalawi has lost one of its most influential literary voices, Jack Mapanje, whose writings explored themes of freedom and power.
