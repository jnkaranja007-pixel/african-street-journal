Malawi presents automatic fuel pricing model at Windhoek regional meeting

Officials say the mechanism, adopted in 2012, has helped government save public money and free fiscal space for other priorities.

Source: The Nation Malawi · 2026-11-12 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Malawi has presented its automatic fuel pricing mechanism to senior government officials and economists from East and Southern African countries in Windhoek, Namibia. Rhino Mchenga, director of fiscal decentralisation at the Ministry of Finance, Economic Planning and Decentralisation, said the model was introduced in 2012 after mounting fiscal pressures and foreign exchange shortages made fuel subsidies unsustainable.

Mchenga said the mechanism lets domestic pump prices adjust automatically to changes in international import costs, exchange rates and levies, so government no longer accumulates subsidy costs. He acknowledged that the shift raised fuel prices as subsidies that had kept petroleum products affordable were withdrawn. The conference was organised by the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa's Southern and Eastern African subregional offices.

Malawi has suffered recurring fuel shortages, mainly because of scarce foreign currency for imports, and tobacco, its main earner, is seasonal. Zambia, Namibia, Tanzania, Mozambique, Botswana and South Africa also use the mechanism, which revises pump prices whenever international oil prices, the exchange rate or freight charges move by five percent.

Why it mattersFuel users in Malawi face price changes whenever oil prices, the exchange rate or freight charges shift by five percent, and a Price Stabilisation Fund cushions importers from losses.
