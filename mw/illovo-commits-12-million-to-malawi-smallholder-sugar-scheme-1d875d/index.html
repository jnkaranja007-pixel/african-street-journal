Illovo commits $12 million to Malawi smallholder sugar scheme

The Kazilira project covers 308 hectares at Dwangwa and is expected to add 4 500 metric tonnes of sugar each year.

Source: The Nation Malawi · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

At Dwangwa Sugar Estate in Nkhotakota District, 308 hectares of irrigated land are being opened to smallholder cane growers under the Kazilira scheme. Illovo put $12 million, about K21 billion, into the expansion, which should lift annual sugar output by 4 500 metric tonnes on top of the estate's current 100 000 metric tonnes.

Ronald Ngwira, the managing director of Illovo Sugar (Malawi) plc, the Malawi Stock Exchange-listed producer, presented the scheme at its launch and called it the first phase of a wider effort to expand cane supply through smallholders. Senior Chief Kanyenda, who chairs the scheme, said participating communities would gain a long-term income by joining the sugar value chain.

Industrialisation, Business, Trade and Tourism Minister Simon Itaye said Illovo had already met local demand, so most of the extra output would go to export markets to help ease foreign-exchange shortages. Illovo's profit after tax rose 250% to K77 billion in the year to August 31 2025, though sugar output fell to 203 786MT after heavy rains disrupted harvesting.

Why it mattersSmallholder cane growers in Kazilira gain irrigated land and a place in the sugar value chain, while most of the extra output is aimed at export markets.
