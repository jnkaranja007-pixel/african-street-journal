Donors pledge $221 million to Malawi recovery projects

The World Bank expects the money to flow within days of agreements signed in Lilongwe, despite persistent fuel and foreign-exchange shortages.

Source: The Nation Malawi · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Development partners have pledged $221 million, roughly K414 billion, for economic recovery and social protection in Malawi. Financing agreements were signed in Lilongwe on Friday for two projects under the government’s National Economic Recovery Plan, and the World Bank said disbursement is expected within days.

The $165.9 million INSPIRE-O project will reach vulnerable households in all 25 districts and four cities, combining direct aid, climate-smart public works and economic inclusion schemes for women and young people. 10 drought-prone districts will get stronger support against climate shocks. The M-THRiVE project aims to help small and medium-sized exporters win buyers, access finance and enter new markets.

World Bank country manager Firas Raad said Malawi’s foreign-exchange problems were structural and would need long-term fixes, chiefly better export performance. The World Bank, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Germany, Ireland and Iceland are backing the projects, even as fuel queues and currency shortages continue.

Why it mattersExporters and vulnerable households are the targets, with $221 million expected to reach them within days.
