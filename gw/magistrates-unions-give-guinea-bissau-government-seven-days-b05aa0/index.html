Magistrates' unions give Guinea-Bissau government seven days to negotiate

The unions say seven magistrates have worked for 15 months without pay because the case is blocked at the Finance Ministry.

Sources: O Democrata GB and Conosaba do Porto · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Two magistrates' unions in Guinea-Bissau gave the transition government seven days to negotiate over 13 demands, warning they could call a strike if talks do not begin. The unions submitted their demands on Monday, 23 November, to Public Administration Minister Assucénia Donate de Barros.

The unions say seven magistrates have served for 15 months without salaries because the case is blocked at the Finance Ministry. Their list also seeks payment of housing and isolation allowances, reopening regional and sectoral courts, and better equipment, internet access, vehicles and security for courts.

Spokesman Mamadu Bailo Baldé said the document updates proposals discussed in four rounds of talks with the government last year. The demands also include applying a 2018 law and granting retired status to magistrates with more than 25 years of service.

Why it mattersThe transition government must open negotiations with the magistrates' unions within seven days to avert a possible strike.
