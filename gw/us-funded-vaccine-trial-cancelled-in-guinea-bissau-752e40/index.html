US-funded vaccine trial cancelled in Guinea-Bissau

Accusations of unethical and neocolonial practices led to the cancellation of a hepatitis B vaccine trial on newborns.

Source: BBC Africa · 2026-11-05 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

A proposed trial of a hepatitis B vaccine for newborn babies in Guinea-Bissau has been cancelled. Police seized a batch of vaccines from the Bandim Health Project research centre, which has operated in the country since 1978. The trial was funded by the US under Health Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr.

The trial faced international criticism for planning to withhold a World Health Organization-recommended vaccine from half of the participating newborns. Some public health experts questioned whether vulnerable populations in African countries were being subjected to risks not accepted in wealthier nations. The US department of health and human services has been asked for comment.

Guinea-Bissau, like many neighbouring countries, typically waits until babies are six weeks old for hepatitis B vaccination, while the WHO recommends vaccination at birth.
