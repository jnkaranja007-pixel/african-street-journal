Senegal minister visits Bissau to discuss election date

Cheikh Niang arrived Sunday to assess Guinea-Bissau's political situation and a potential new date for general elections.

Senegalese Minister of African Integration, Foreign Affairs, and Senegalese Abroad, Cheikh Niang, arrived in Bissau on Sunday, October 25, 2026. His working visit aims to analyze the political situation in Guinea-Bissau and discuss setting a new date for the general elections. The current government and the National Electoral Commission acknowledged the impossibility of holding elections on the planned December 6 date.

Niang, who also chairs the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) Council of Ministers, was received by Justice Minister João Bernardo Vieira. ECOWAS designated Senegal as the mediator for Guinea-Bissau's political crisis, with Senegal currently holding the rotating presidency of the sub-regional organization. A new presidential decree is still awaited to officially postpone the vote and set a new date.

Niang is scheduled to meet with Prime Minister Ilídio Vieira Té, the President of the Transitional Council and Chief of General Staff Tomás Djassi, and the Transitional President Horta Inta-a. He will also hold separate meetings with leaders from various political parties, including PAIGC, MADEM-G15, PRS, APU-PDGB, PTG, and FREPASNA, as well as civil society representatives.

Why it mattersThe political situation and election date in Guinea-Bissau are being discussed by a Senegalese minister and various political actors.

Guinea-Bissau police rescue 71 trafficking victims

Operation ASTRA dismantled a human trafficking ring that lured victims with promises of work abroad.

Police in Guinea-Bissau rescued 71 people from various West African nations on October 22, 2026, in an operation that dismantled a human trafficking network. The operation, named ASTRA, led to the arrest of four suspects. The victims, originally from Mali, Guinea, Burkina Faso, Togo, Senegal, and Sierra Leone, were lured with promises of jobs in Canada, Europe, and the United States.

According to the Judicial Police (PJ) spokesperson Braima Cissé, the victims were brought to Bissau and held against their will. PJ agent António Gomes told AFP that the victims faced physical and psychological pressure, including threats and abuse, and were forced into pyramid schemes to recruit new members via social media. The network allegedly provided false office details in Canada, the United States, and Portugal to appear legitimate.

Interpol's information aided the dismantling of the network, which investigators believe operated across several countries in the sub-region. The rescued individuals will be handed over to their respective embassies, with others needing to contact family for assistance to return home.

Chinese medical team treats Bissau schoolchildren

18 health professionals offered free consultations and health education on scalp infections.

The 21st Chinese Medical Team held a health talk and offered free medical consultations to children at the SOS School in Bissau on Friday, October 23, 2026. The team, comprising 18 health professionals from the Military Hospital and Canchungo Regional Hospital, also promoted Traditional Chinese Medicine.

The health talk focused on preventing scalp ringworm, a common childhood illness during the rainy season. Doctors explained symptoms, transmission, and prevention, emphasizing early diagnosis and treatment. The team also donated footballs, school supplies, and other items to the children.

Doctor Wang Weicheng advised students to avoid contact with infected animals, refrain from sharing personal items, and keep their scalps clean and dry. SOS Basic School Director Agostinho Bolamu thanked the Chinese team for their initiative, highlighting its importance for the children's and teachers' health.
