Guinea-Bissau signs Hajj 2027 agreement with Saudi Arabia

An accord was signed in Jeddah on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, to organize the pilgrimage for Guinean Muslims.

Guinea-Bissau and Saudi Arabia signed a general pilgrimage agreement for Hajj 2027 on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, in Jeddah. The deal aims to strengthen cooperation in organizing the journey for Guinean Muslim pilgrims to Islam's holy sites.

Infali Coté, Guinea-Bissau's High Commissioner for Pilgrimage, and Saudi deputy minister Abdul Fattah Bin Suleimane Mashat signed the accord. The agreement commits both nations to ensuring the pilgrimage occurs with safety, organization, and dignity, providing Guinean pilgrims with better support and assistance.

This signing marks the official start of preparations for Guinean pilgrims to participate in Hajj 2027. The agreement reinforces institutional cooperation and the shared interest in improving services for pilgrims and meeting organizational requirements.

Why it mattersGuinean Muslims planning to perform the Hajj in 2027 will benefit from improved organization and assistance.

Rights group urges swift justice for three deaths

The LGDH called for accountability in the cases of Luís Balanta, Mamadu Tano Bari, and a military health technician.

The Guinean League for Human Rights (LGDH) on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, called for faster investigations into the deaths of vicar Luís Balanta, Mamadu Tano Bari, and a military health technician electrocuted in Bôr.

LGDH president Bubacar Turé stated that justice must be impartial and treat all cases equally, after meeting with the Attorney General. The organization welcomed the inquiry into Balanta's death but urged reinforced efforts in other unresolved cases, also expressing concern over rising human trafficking incidents.

The LGDH also requested the government increase human, logistical, and financial resources for the Public Prosecutor's Office to combat impunity and defend citizens' rights more effectively.

Why it mattersThe LGDH is pushing for accountability and faster resolution in cases of death and human trafficking, affecting citizens and the justice system.

Armed men invade Guinea-Bissau politician's home

MADEM-G15 party reports six intruders overpowered guards at Soares Sambú's Bissau residence early Monday.

The Movement for Democratic Alternation (MADEM-G15) reported that armed and hooded men allegedly invaded the Bissau home of Soares Sambú, a senior party official, on Monday, November 9, 2026.

The party stated that approximately six heavily armed individuals neutralized Sambú's personal security before entering his residence, where they remained for over an hour, causing panic among his family members.

MADEM-G15 has called on judicial authorities to urgently identify those responsible for the attack and determine their motives, demanding accountability for the incident.

Why it mattersThe incident directly affects Soares Sambú and his family, raising concerns about personal security for political figures in Guinea-Bissau.

Military court frees National Guard officers

Colonel Victor Tchongo and two other officers were released on November 10, 2026, after being detained since December 2023.

Colonel Victor Tchongo, former commander-general of the National Guard, Major Ramalhano Mendes, ex-operations commander, and Major Lamine Camará, his deputy, were freed on the afternoon of Tuesday, November 10, 2026, by order of the Superior Military Court of Guinea-Bissau. The three officers had been held since December 1, 2023, following accusations of attempting a coup.

Their detention stemmed from events where elements of the National Guard's Intervention and Reserve Brigade forcibly removed former officials Suleimane Seidi and António Monteiro from police custody. This action led to a military intervention classified by authorities as a coup attempt, occurring between November 30 and December 1, 2023.

In June 2025, a Regional Military Court had convicted Tchongo, Mendes, and Camará. A source from the Guinean Human Rights League confirmed their release, stating that four other individuals detained in the same case, associated with Tcherno Barri, were also freed.

Why it mattersThe release of high-ranking National Guard officers and others detained since 2023 offers a potential shift in the ongoing legal and political situation.

11 charged with Olof Sambú murder in Guinea-Bissau

Suspects face charges of murder and qualified damage following a public lynching on September 27, 2026.

Guinea-Bissau's Public Ministry has formally charged 11 suspects in connection with the death of Olof João Gomes Sambú. The incident occurred on September 27, 2026, in the Chapa area of Bissau. The accused are currently in preventive detention and have eight days to contest the charges.

The suspects are accused of murder and qualified damage under Guinea-Bissau's Penal Code. The Public Ministry stated that sufficient evidence has been gathered to show the 11 acted together in committing the crimes. Olof Sambú died after being falsely accused of attempting to kidnap a child, leading to a public lynching where his vehicle was intercepted and destroyed before he was fatally assaulted.

The National Guard Command clarified that Olof Gomes Sambú was not a member of their corps but a civilian employee of the General Directorate of Customs, serving as head of Anti-Fraud in the Gabú Delegation. If the suspects do not challenge the charges within the legal timeframe, the case will proceed to trial.

Why it matters11 individuals face trial for murder and property damage, impacting justice and public safety.
