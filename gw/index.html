Guinea-Bissau Parliament Leader Calls for Referendum Boycott

Domingos Simões Pereira, elected president of the National People's Assembly, urged citizens to stay home from the constitutional vote.

Domingos Simões Pereira, the leader of the PAIGC party and newly elected president of Guinea-Bissau's Parliament, has called for a complete boycott of the upcoming constitutional referendum. In a video message released from Portugal, Pereira urged citizens to remain in their homes on Sunday, the scheduled day of the vote.

Pereira described the popular consultation as "a coup against democracy and freedom," asserting that the necessary conditions for its proper execution are not met. He stated that Guineans cannot participate in a process he deems illegitimate, emphasizing his opposition to the referendum's current framework and timing.

The call for a boycott comes as Guinea-Bissau prepares for the constitutional referendum. Pereira's stance, articulated through social media, signals significant political division regarding the legitimacy and readiness for the vote, potentially impacting its turnout and perceived validity.

Why it mattersThe constitutional referendum is directly affected by the call for a boycott, potentially undermining its legitimacy and turnout.

Guinea-Bissau Voters Approve New Constitution Granting President More Powers

The "yes" camp secured 70 percent of the vote in a referendum held Sunday, ahead of a general election intended to restore civilian rule.

Guinea-Bissau voters have approved a constitutional change in a referendum, granting the president increased powers. The National Electoral Commission announced Tuesday that 70 percent of voters supported the "yes" camp. This constitutional amendment comes as the country prepares for a general election scheduled for December 6, aimed at returning power to civilian leadership after a military coup.

The referendum vote took place less than 10 months after the military seized power and just months before the presidential election. The junta stated the constitutional changes are intended to stabilize institutions before the elections. However, the political environment remains tense in the nation, with opposition groups urging a boycott due to perceived restrictions on public freedoms.

Transitional president General Horta N'Tam, who is barred from running in the upcoming election under the current transitional charter, had encouraged young people to vote in large numbers. Guinea-Bissau's move follows similar constitutional overhauls in other African nations like Zimbabwe, Guinea, Togo, Uganda, and Chad in recent years, often aimed at extending term limits or expanding executive authority.

Why it mattersThe constitutional changes will affect the balance of power within the government, potentially impacting future political stability and the transition to civilian rule.
