Guinea-Bissau falls behind on UEMOA reforms

The Secretary of State for the Treasury admitted a slight setback in implementing community policies and projects.

Guinea-Bissau has seen a slight decline in its implementation of reforms, policies, and projects for the West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA), according to Treasury Secretary Justino Vieira. He stated on November 4, 2026, that the results were less satisfactory than those recorded in 2024.

Vieira emphasized the need for better coordination between national authorities and technical departments to transpose UEMOA directives. The 12th annual evaluation, taking place in Bissau from November 4-6, aims to assess reform execution and find ways to strengthen national ownership of the sub-regional organization's initiatives.

The government aims to improve the implementation of UEMOA's additional acts and regulations, speed up directive transposition, and enhance the execution quality of funded programs. UEMOA Resident Representative Aly Coulibaly noted that the evaluation's modalities were set in Bamako, Mali, in June.

Why it mattersGuinea-Bissau's government is working to improve its adherence to UEMOA regulations and policies.

US-funded vaccine trial cancelled in Guinea-Bissau

Accusations of unethical and neocolonial practices led to the cancellation of a hepatitis B vaccine trial on newborns.

A proposed trial of a hepatitis B vaccine for newborn babies in Guinea-Bissau has been cancelled. Police seized a batch of vaccines from the Bandim Health Project research centre, which has operated in the country since 1978. The trial was funded by the US under Health Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr.

The trial faced international criticism for planning to withhold a World Health Organization-recommended vaccine from half of the participating newborns. Some public health experts questioned whether vulnerable populations in African countries were being subjected to risks not accepted in wealthier nations. The US department of health and human services has been asked for comment.

Guinea-Bissau, like many neighbouring countries, typically waits until babies are six weeks old for hepatitis B vaccination, while the WHO recommends vaccination at birth.

Guinea-Bissau debt hits 76.9% of GDP

Prime Minister Té acknowledges fiscal pressure despite projected GDP growth of 5.2% for 2026.

Prime Minister Ilídio Vieira Té stated on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, in Bissau that Guinea-Bissau's public debt has reached 76.9% of its GDP. He also noted that the current fiscal pressure of 9.1% is insufficient to meet the state's financing needs.

Speaking at a press conference with the International Monetary Fund (IMF), Té called for greater financial discipline, modernization of tax and customs administration, and increased transparency in public management. He highlighted that GDP is expected to grow by 5.2% in 2026 and 5.8% in 2027, with the budget deficit projected to fall from 7% in 2025 to 4.2% in 2026 and 2.8% in 2027.

The IMF mission is in the country to finalize the review of an economic and financial program started in 2023 and to negotiate a new agreement. IMF mission chief Niko Hobdari reported that inflation has dropped to 0.7% and recommended strengthening tax collection and the financial sector.

Why it mattersGuinea-Bissau's public debt and fiscal pressure affect the nation's ability to fund state operations and investments.

Lawyers accuse Supreme Court judges of crimes

A group of lawyers filed a criminal complaint against the former president and two counselors of the Supreme Court of Justice.

A collective of lawyers filed a criminal complaint on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, with the Public Prosecutor's Office against members of the leadership of the Supreme Court of Justice (STJ). The complaint targets former STJ president Lima André and counselors Arafam Mané and João Mendes Pereira, who are accused of several crimes.

Lawyer Nansambe stated that the complaint stems from accusations by citizens Marcelino Intupe and Ussumane Sanhá, and called for an investigation into the facts. Intupe listed alleged crimes including obstruction of justice, usurpation of public functions, dereliction of duty, denial of justice, embezzlement, malicious denunciation, qualified damages, and forgery. Mané was also accused of suspending judges and cases without proper justification.

The lawyers are seeking accountability for the accused if the charges are proven. Marcelino Intupe urged the Public Prosecutor's Office to investigate the case and determine any responsibilities.

Why it mattersMembers of the Supreme Court of Justice in Guinea-Bissau are facing criminal accusations from lawyers.

Agriculture · 2026-11-05

Guinea-Bissau scientists push for sardinella management plan

Researchers advocate for full implementation of a plan to protect vital fish stocks, approved over a year ago.

Researchers in Guinea-Bissau are urging the full implementation of a Sardinella Fisheries Management Plan, approved more than a year ago, to preserve the country's fish resources. The call came during a two-day seminar in Bissau on November 4, 2026, focused on lessons learned from a sustainable sardinella management project.

The seminar, funded by the UN's Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) through the EAF-Nansen Programme and involving the National Network of Community Radios (RENARC) and the National Institute of Fisheries and Oceanographic Research (INIPO), aims to identify project challenges and strengthen partner commitment. INIPO researcher Cadidjatu Djaló stated the institute remains available to collaborate on protecting this fundamental resource.

RENARC president Demba Sanhá explained his organization's role in communicating and raising awareness among local communities over the past 18 months. The meeting will review activities and formulate recommendations for INIPO and FAO.

Why it mattersFisheries researchers and community radio networks are working to ensure the long-term availability of sardinella, a key resource for the country.
