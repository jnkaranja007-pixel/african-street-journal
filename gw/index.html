Guinea-Bissau Voters Show Low Turnout in Constitutional Referendum

Nine hundred fourteen thousand four hundred twenty-eight registered voters began casting ballots on August 30, 2026, for a new constitution.

Guinea-Bissau began voting on a new constitution Sunday, August 30, 2026, with nine hundred fourteen thousand four hundred twenty-eight eligible voters participating. The National Transition Council approved the proposed constitutional changes, which aim to strengthen presidential powers and reduce the number of deputies in the National People's Assembly from one hundred two to sixty-five. The National Electoral Commission organized the referendum, which is being conducted through universal, direct, secret, and personal suffrage across the nation.

Voting stations opened at seven in the morning and will close at five in the afternoon, with three thousand five hundred sixty-two polling stations established nationwide. Early reports from Bissau, specifically in the Antula neighborhood, indicated a low turnout in the initial hours. Journalists observed only one candidate delegate present at polling station number one in electoral district number five.

The new constitution, approved by the National Transition Council, is the subject of this national referendum. The electoral process is underway across the country, with The Democrata providing ongoing coverage via its digital platforms and social media. The organization of the vote is managed by the National Electoral Commission, ensuring the process adheres to established electoral laws.

Why it mattersThe constitutional changes could affect governance and representation, impacting how the country is run and who holds power.

Guinea-Bissau Voters Consider Expanded Presidential Powers

Citizens cast ballots in a referendum that could grant the president more authority ahead of scheduled elections to restore civilian rule.

Voters in Guinea-Bissau participated in a referendum on Sunday that may expand presidential powers, with polls opening at 0700 GMT after heavy rains. Security was heightened in the capital, Bissau, with national guard and police present at key locations. The vote concludes at 1700 GMT, and provisional results are expected from Tuesday. This referendum follows a military coup less than ten months ago that saw the armed forces seize control of the nation.

The outcome of the vote could reshape the country's governance structure as it moves towards a general election intended to re-establish civilian leadership. Shopkeeper Halimatou Traore expressed hope for positive changes, stating that the country's recent crises have negatively impacted her earnings and that a successful referendum could set Guinea-Bissau on a new course.

Braima Seidy, a 76-year-old veteran of the independence war against Portugal, was among those who voted. The referendum is a significant step in the nation's transition following the recent coup, with citizens weighing in on the potential for increased presidential authority as the country aims to return to democratic governance.

Why it mattersCitizens are voting on a referendum that could increase presidential powers, potentially altering the country's political landscape.

Guinea-Bissau President Urges Youth to Vote in Referendum

Transitional President Horta Inta-a called for massive youth participation in the constitutional referendum, warning against further delays to their future.

Transitional President General Horta Inta-a appealed to Guinean voters, especially the youth, to participate in large numbers in the referendum. He cautioned young people against allowing their future to be postponed again, emphasizing that the vote is about the nation's future, not party politics.

The referendum concerns the adoption of a new Constitution, approved by transitional authorities. A total of 914,428 Guinean voters are eligible to cast ballots across 3,562 polling stations nationwide. The process, organized by the National Electoral Commission, aims to end political conflicts and institutional gridlock in Parliament, enabling future governments to complete their terms.

President Horta addressed journalists after casting his vote, accompanied by the First Lady and other officials. He dismissed speculation that the referendum was intended to facilitate the return of Umaro Sissoco Embaló, stating that all Guinean citizens, including Embaló, Braima Camará, and Domingos Simões Pereira, have the right to return and live in the country.

Why it mattersThe referendum on a new constitution directly affects all Guinean citizens by aiming to resolve political conflicts and institutional blockages, potentially leading to more stable governance.

Civil Society Groups Hail Referendum Boycott

Espaço de Concertação and Frente Popular stated that high abstention reflects civic awareness and political maturity among Guineans.

Civil society groups Espaço de Concertação (EdC) and Frente Popular (FP) expressed satisfaction with the boycott of the August 30, 2026, referendum in Guinea-Bissau. The organizations asserted that no referendum should override the sovereign will of the people or institutionalize autocracy. They believe the high abstention rate across the nation demonstrates the civic consciousness, political maturity, patriotism, and commitment to democracy and the rule of law among Guineans.

According to a statement, EdC and FP view the referendum's rejection as a response to falsehoods, manipulation, and political instrumentalization. They contend that citizens rejected the agenda of Umaro Sissoco Embaló and those who falsely represent civil society to serve the current regime's interests. The groups maintain that the population refuses to have its sovereign will manipulated to legitimize personal and authoritarian power projects.

The organizations also characterized the referendum's rejection as a setback for the military command, which they accuse of betraying its oath and the legacy of the national liberation struggle. EdC and FP argue that the People's Revolutionary Armed Forces should not be transformed into an entity serving private interests. They maintain that no constitution should be used to perpetuate the power of a select group.

Why it mattersCivil society groups are affected by the perceived manipulation of the referendum process to serve personal and authoritarian power projects.

Prime Minister Calls for Constitutional Revision Amidst Institutional Crises

Ilídio Vieira Té stated that the constitution has been a source of conflict and proposed amendments to reduce institutional disputes.

Prime Minister Ilídio Vieira Té announced on Sunday, August 30, 2026, that Guinea-Bissau's constitution has consistently been a source of conflict among state institutions. To address this, the National Transition Council and the Government have decided to implement changes aimed at minimizing existing disputes between state bodies. Té emphasized that any election outcome will be respected, as democracy is determined by the majority.

Speaking to journalists after voting in Biombo, Té urged citizens to participate in the referendum by voting either 'Yes' or 'No', criticizing calls for a boycott. He stressed the need for all Guineans to contribute to the nation's development through mutual cooperation and dialogue, stating that external help is not the solution. The Prime Minister recalled Amílcar Cabral's vision for national liberation and development.

The Prime Minister highlighted that Guinea-Bissau requires the collective effort of its citizens across various sectors to achieve development. He advocated for unity and collaborative work, warning against actions that could destabilize the country. Té reiterated that national progress depends on internal commitment and planning, rather than external intervention, to fulfill the population's desire for peace and advancement.

Why it mattersThe Prime Minister's call for constitutional revision and his statements on respecting election outcomes directly affect the political stability and governance of Guinea-Bissau.
