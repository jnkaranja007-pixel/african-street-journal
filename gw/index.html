Guinea-Bissau Votes to Expand Presidential Powers

A referendum proposing a stronger presidential system and a smaller parliament was held Sunday as the military government nears the end of its transition.

Guinea-Bissau held a referendum Sunday on a new constitution designed to significantly increase presidential authority and reduce the size of parliament. The vote is seen as a key step by the military government to alter the political landscape before scheduled December elections. The proposed changes would shift the country from a semi-presidential to a presidential system, granting the head of state more control over the executive branch, including the power to appoint and dismiss the prime minister.

Under the draft constitution, the president would lead the Council of Ministers, and the number of lawmakers would decrease from 102 to 65, with electoral constituencies reduced from 29 to 12. Supporters of the reforms, including General Tomás Djassi, head of the armed forces, argue these changes will clarify the hierarchy of power and prevent the recurring disputes that have fueled political instability. Critics, however, question the legitimacy of transitional authorities to enact such fundamental constitutional changes.

The opposition, including the African Party for the Independence of Guinea and Cape Verde (PAIGC), has rejected the proposed constitution, urging voters to boycott the referendum. They contend the draft would weaken the prime minister, subordinate parliament and political parties, and excessively concentrate power within the presidency. Concerns have also been raised about the military government's authority to rewrite the constitution during a transitional period.

Why it mattersThe proposed constitutional changes in Guinea-Bissau could concentrate power in the presidency, affecting the balance of authority between branches of government and potentially altering the political stability that influences economic and social conditions.

Prime Minister Demands Committed National Football Team

Guinea-Bissau's Prime Minister Ilídio Vieira Té expects a more professional and committed national football squad to qualify for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations.

Prime Minister of Transition Ilídio Vieira Té stated that the government expects the national football team, the Djurtus, to be more committed and professional during the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualification phase. The team begins its campaign this September, aiming to reach the final tournament after failing to qualify for the 2025 edition. Vieira Té assured that the Executive will continue to provide the necessary conditions for the team to compete at the highest level.

Speaking at a reception for the under-17 national team, Vieira Té emphasized the public's expectation for a team that can reconnect with fans and achieve positive results. He noted a visible distance between supporters and the squad, stressing that while defeat is part of football, the team must always give its maximum effort and represent the country with dignity. The Prime Minister also called for a greater sense of responsibility from the players.

Vieira Té reiterated the desire for a team deeply committed to the Guinean crest and composed of true professionals, stating that the government's role is to create the conditions for competition. These remarks were made on August 31st, and he expressed confidence in the Djurtus' qualification for CAN 2027, urging greater unity around the team led by Emiliano Té.

Why it mattersThe national football team's performance and commitment directly impact national pride and fan engagement, affecting how the country is represented internationally.

Guinea-Bissau Approves Constitutional Overhaul with 70% 'Yes' Vote

A new constitution, strengthening presidential powers and reducing parliamentary seats, passed with broad support across most regions.

Guinea-Bissau's provisional constitutional referendum results show 70% of voters approved the proposed changes on August 30. The National Elections Commission reported 383,116 "yes" votes against 160,954 "no" votes. Voter turnout was 63%, with 572,712 citizens participating out of 914,428 registered voters, resulting in 37% abstention.

Support for the constitutional revision was strongest in the interior, with Gabú region leading at 90% approval and Bafatá following at 85%. Biombo and Quinara recorded 79% support, while Cacheu and Tombali reached 70%. Oio registered 60% "yes" votes, and Bolama-Bijagós had 58%. Bissau was the sole region to reject the proposal, with 52% voting "no".

Key changes in the new constitution include enhanced presidential authority, a shift from a semi-presidential to a presidential system, and a reduction in the National People's Assembly from 102 to 65 deputies. CNE president Carmem Isaura Tavares Batista Lobo stated the count adhered to the Referendum Law, which deems approved any proposal receiving a majority of affirmative votes.

Why it mattersThe approval of a new constitution with strengthened presidential powers and a reduced legislature directly impacts the country's governance structure and the roles of its political leaders.

Civil society groups hail referendum boycott

Espaço de Concertação and Frente Popular stated that high abstention demonstrated civic awareness and rejected personal power agendas.

Civil society groups Espaço de Concertação (EdC) and Frente Popular (FP) expressed satisfaction with the boycott of Guinea-Bissau's August 30, 2026, referendum. The organizations stated that no referendum can override the sovereign will of the people and that constitutions should not institutionalize autocracy or perpetuate group power.

According to EdC and FP, the high abstention rate across the nation reflects the civic consciousness of Guineans, demonstrating political maturity, patriotism, and commitment to democracy, the rule of law, and justice. The groups asserted that citizens responded to lies, manipulation, and political instrumentalization, rejecting an agenda they attributed to Umaro Sissoco Embaló and others.

The organizations further argued that the referendum's rejection signifies the population's refusal to have their sovereign will manipulated to legitimize personal and authoritarian power projects. They also viewed the outcome as a setback for the military command, which they accused of betraying its oath and the legacy of the national liberation struggle, warning against transforming the People's Revolutionary Armed Forces into an entity serving private interests.

Why it mattersCivil society groups are affected by the referendum boycott as it challenges the legitimacy of the current government and its constitutional agenda.
