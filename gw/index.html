Pope names new Bishop of Bissau

Dom Victor Luís Quematcha will take charge of the country's main Catholic diocese on November 15.

Pope Leo XIV appointed Dom Victor Luís Quematcha as the new Bishop of the Diocese of Bissau, following the health-related resignation of Dom José Lampra Cá. The Apostolic Nunciature of Guinea-Bissau announced the appointment on November 7, 2026.

Dom Victor Luís Quematcha previously served as Bishop of the Diocese of Bafatá and will temporarily administer that diocese. His installation in Bissau is set for November 15 at the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Candelaria.

The Catholic Church thanked Dom José Lampra Cá for his service and invited the faithful to welcome and pray for their new pastor.

Lawyer urges faster inquiry into Olof Sambu lynching

Olof Sambu died on September 27, 2026, after being attacked by a mob on false kidnapping rumours.

The lawyer for Olof João Gomes Sambú's family urged the Guinean state to speed up investigations into his death. Suleimane Cassamá stated that the state must take responsibility for clarifying the circumstances of the killing and holding perpetrators accountable. Cassamá spoke on Friday, November 6, 2026, after the family testified at the Ministry of Justice as part of the ongoing inquiry.

Olof João Gomes Sambú died on September 27, 2026, in the Chapa de Bissau area. He was allegedly lynched by a crowd after false information circulated accusing him of attempting to kidnap a child. Witnesses reported that the mob damaged his vehicle and assaulted him following these suspicions.

Cassamá described the family's testimony as encouraging, showing the case remains under investigation. The family hopes for justice and that the truth about the incident will emerge. The investigation is proceeding with witness testimonies to establish the facts of the case.

Why it mattersOlof Sambu was killed by a mob on false rumours, and his family is seeking justice through an accelerated investigation.

Health · 2026-11-08

Guinea-Bissau cancels US-funded hepatitis B vaccine trial

The proposed trial for 14,000 newborns was halted after international criticism over its ethical implications.

Guinea-Bissau has cancelled a US-funded hepatitis B vaccine trial planned for 14,000 newborn babies. The decision followed international criticism that the study was unethical and potentially exploitative. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. funded the trial, which planned to give half the infants the vaccine and deny the other half.

Health experts warned that withholding the vaccine could expose babies to infection in Guinea-Bissau, where nearly one in five people has hepatitis B. The World Health Organization recommends all babies receive the vaccine at birth. The government had previously prohibited vaccine trials involving children, and police seized a batch of vaccines from the research centre.

The hepatitis B virus can cause chronic infection, leading to long-term liver damage and liver cancer, with no known cure. The WHO recommends a booster dose for infants, which provides years of protection. The trial was planned to involve newborns, who are at the greatest risk of chronic infection passed from mother to baby.

Why it mattersNewborn babies in Guinea-Bissau are at risk of chronic hepatitis B infection due to the cancellation of a vaccine trial.

Guinea-Bissau military partially lifts political activity ban

Political parties can hold internal meetings from November 24 but public gatherings remain prohibited.

Guinea-Bissau's High Military Command announced on Friday, November 6, that some restrictions will be eased, allowing internal political party activities to resume from November 24, 2026.

Legal political parties can now conduct internal meetings, consultations, assemblies, and congresses, provided they notify authorities beforehand and adhere to security conditions. However, marches, demonstrations, rallies, and other public events that could threaten national security or public order are still forbidden.

The military stated that any violation of these measures could lead to intervention by competent authorities, urging political actors and the public to avoid inciting violence or disturbing peace.

Why it mattersPolitical party activities are partially restricted, affecting the ability of politicians and party members to organize and engage publicly.

Education · 2026-11-08

Guinea-Bissau students demand teacher oversight

Student confederation also protests government ban on graduation parties, citing economic hardship.

The Confederation of Associations of Students of Public and Private Schools of Guinea-Bissau (CAAEPP-GB) asked the transitional government to strengthen oversight of contracted teachers on Friday, November 6, 2026. The students' organization alleges that many teachers abandon public schools to prioritize work in private institutions.

Alfa Umaro Só, president of CAAEPP-GB, stated during a press conference that the lack of attendance by some contracted teachers disrupts classes and compromises the quality of education in public schools. He also criticized the government's decision to prohibit graduation parties for final-year students, arguing the measure lacks economic justification.

Só defended that 12th-year students wish to hold their ceremonies at their schools, describing them as voluntary initiatives organized by the students themselves. The student leader accused the Association of Parents and Guardians of influencing the Council of Ministers' decision to ban these celebrations.

Why it mattersStudents in Guinea-Bissau are directly affected by the quality of teaching in public schools and the ability to celebrate their academic achievements.
