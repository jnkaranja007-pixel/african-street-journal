Guinea-Bissau votes on constitution to boost presidential powers

A referendum proposes a new constitution that would significantly strengthen presidential powers and reduce the size of parliament before December elections.

Guinea-Bissau held a referendum on Sunday to vote on a proposed new constitution that would consolidate presidential authority and diminish the role of parliament. This vote is viewed as a critical test for the current military administration's efforts to restructure the nation's political framework ahead of scheduled elections in December.

The draft constitution seeks to transition the country from a semi-presidential system to a more powerful presidential model, granting the head of state enhanced control over the executive branch. Under the proposed changes, the president would preside over the Council of Ministers and gain the authority to appoint and dismiss the prime minister. Additionally, the number of parliamentary seats would be reduced from 102 to 65, with electoral constituencies decreasing from 29 to 12.

Supporters of the reforms, including General Tomás Djassi, head of the armed forces, argue that these changes are necessary to clarify the hierarchy of power and prevent the recurring disputes between the presidency, government, and parliament that have historically led to political instability. However, the opposition, including the PAIGC, has rejected the proposals, asserting that the draft would weaken the prime minister, subordinate parliament and political parties, and excessively concentrate power in the presidency. Critics also question the legitimacy of transitional authorities to enact such fundamental constitutional changes.

Why it mattersThe proposed constitutional changes could concentrate power in the presidency, potentially affecting the balance of governance and the stability of the political system for all citizens.

Civil Society Groups Hail Referendum Boycott

Two Guinean organizations celebrated high abstention rates, viewing it as a rejection of autocratic agendas and political manipulation.

The Espaço de Concertação (EdC) and Frente Popular (FP) expressed satisfaction with the boycott of the August 30, 2026, referendum in Guinea-Bissau. They stated that no constitution should legitimize autocracy or perpetuate group power, and that a referendum cannot override the people's sovereign will. The groups asserted that the high abstention rate across the nation demonstrated civic awareness, political maturity, patriotism, and a commitment to democracy, the rule of law, and justice.

According to the organizations, the public responded to falsehoods, manipulation, and political instrumentalization by rejecting the agenda of Umaro Sissoco Embaló and those who claim to represent civil society for regime interests. The EdC and FP believe this rejection signifies the population's refusal to allow their sovereign will to be manipulated for personal and authoritarian power projects.

The statement further indicated that this outcome represents a setback for the military command, which the organizations accuse of betraying its oath and the legacy of the national liberation struggle. The EdC and FP maintain that the People's Revolutionary Armed Forces should not be co-opted to serve private interests.

Why it mattersCivil society groups are affected as they view the referendum boycott as a rejection of autocratic agendas and a demonstration of political maturity.

Fernando Dias Demands National Dialogue After Referendum

A faction leader of the Social Renewal Party criticizes transitional authorities for lacking national consensus and transparency in the recent referendum.

Fernando Dias da Costa, a leader within the Social Renewal Party (PRS), called on transitional authorities to foster conditions for genuine national dialogue to restore democratic legality. Dias stated that the Guinean people desire peace and stability founded on legitimacy, not just formal voting processes.

Dias, who previously ran for president in annulled elections, made his appeal via Facebook following a national referendum. He urged citizens to remain calm and not allow political differences to escalate into conflict. He also implored the transitional authorities and the international community to heed the population's message, warning against interpreting low turnout as a political victory.

The politician highlighted the low participation in the referendum as a political signal that should not be disregarded. He argued that citizens are unwilling to legitimize a process conducted without national consensus, broad political and social engagement, or legal transparency. Dias criticized the current transitional government for proceeding with constitutional changes outside of meaningful dialogue and linked to the former president's political agenda.

Why it mattersFernando Dias's call for dialogue and criticism of the referendum process directly impacts citizens' ability to influence the country's political direction and legitimacy.

Prime Minister Calls for Constitutional Revision to Curb Institutional Conflict

Ilídio Vieira Té stated that the constitution has been a source of conflict between state institutions and advocated for amendments.

Prime Minister Ilídio Vieira Té announced on Sunday, August 30, 2026, that the constitution has consistently been a source of conflict among the nation's sovereign bodies. He explained that the National Transition Council and the Government have decided to implement changes aimed at reducing these institutional disputes. Té emphasized that any election outcome will be respected, as democracy is determined by the majority.

Speaking to journalists after voting in Biombo, Té urged citizens to participate in the referendum, whether voting 'Yes' or 'No.' He criticized calls for boycotting the process, deeming it undemocratic. The Prime Minister stressed the need for all Guineans to contribute to national development, working together peacefully and constructively, rather than dragging the country down.

Té recalled the foundational goals set by Amílcar Lopes Cabral, the father of Guinean nationality: the Minimum Program for liberation and the Major Program for national development. He reiterated that the country's development must come from its own people, requiring unified planning and a shared objective to achieve progress and stability.

Why it mattersThe Prime Minister's call for constitutional revision and emphasis on national unity directly impacts political stability and governance in Guinea-Bissau.
