Security Forces Vote Early in Guinea-Bissau Referendum

Around ten thousand defense and security personnel cast early ballots for the constitutional referendum in Bissau, with election officials reporting strong turnout since polls.

Early voting for Guinea-Bissau's constitutional referendum saw a strong turnout among defense and security forces in Bissau on Thursday. Approximately ten thousand personnel were expected to vote at the Autonomous Sector of Bissau polling stations. The Regional Election Commission (CRE) reported high engagement from the opening of the polls at seven AM.

Abubacar Sidico Sambú, president of the CRE for the Autonomous Sector of Bissau, stated that technical and financial issues are not hindering the referendum process. He confirmed that organizational matters have been resolved, and payments to polling station staff are being managed with fifty percent disbursed before deployment and the remainder upon completion of duties.

The early voting is scheduled to continue until five PM, with results to be posted at the voting locations. Sambú explained that this early process allows security personnel to fulfill their duties on referendum day across the nation. The law also permits ordinary citizens on official missions or traveling to vote in advance.

Why it mattersSecurity forces are participating in an early vote for a constitutional referendum, impacting their availability and duties on the main election day.

Guinea-Bissau Votes on Constitution Expanding Presidential Powers

A referendum on August 30 will determine if the president gains significant new authorities, including appointing the prime minister and dissolving parliament, ahead of December.

Guinea-Bissau is scheduled to vote on a new constitution this Sunday, August 30, which proposes to significantly expand presidential powers and alter the nation's political structure. The proposed changes would grant the president the authority to appoint and dismiss the prime minister and cabinet, establish or dissolve ministries, and preside over the Council of Ministers. Furthermore, the president could dissolve parliament during severe political crises and dismiss the government under specific circumstances.

The reform also includes reducing the number of parliamentary seats from 102 to 65 and consolidating 29 electoral constituencies into 12. Supporters argue these changes could reshape political institutions, while critics, like Professor Manuel Nobre de Barros, warn of concentrating excessive power in the presidency, potentially leading to virtually unchecked authority. A new requirement for presidential candidates to have resided permanently in Guinea-Bissau for the preceding five years is also introduced.

This referendum follows a recent military coup and precedes general elections slated for December. The vote is being closely observed as the country transitions from military rule back to an elected government. The outcome will indicate whether the proposed constitutional amendments are seen as strengthening institutions or consolidating power at the top of the executive branch.

Why it mattersThe proposed constitutional changes could concentrate power in the presidency, affecting the balance of power and potentially limiting legislative oversight.

Guinea-Bissau and Gambia Establish Technical Platform for Cooperation

A new technical consultation platform is planned to address contemporary challenges and boost exchanges between Bissau and Banjul, following a diplomatic meeting.

Guinea-Bissau and The Gambia are set to create a Technical Consultation Platform aimed at tackling current issues across various sectors and increasing interactions between their capitals, Bissau and Banjul. This initiative follows a meeting between Guinea-Bissau's ambassador to The Gambia, Luís Camará de Barros, and Lamin Faati, The Gambia's Director for African Affairs at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

The diplomatic meeting, held on Tuesday, August 25, 2026, occurred in a cordial atmosphere where both nations reaffirmed their commitment to strengthening historical ties of friendship and good neighborliness. Ambassador de Barros noted that discussions focused on the progress of bilateral relations and a review of existing legal agreements between the two countries.

Both Guinea-Bissau and The Gambia expressed a shared interest in identifying new strategic areas and collaborative opportunities for economic, technical, and institutional cooperation. The diplomats also emphasized their dedication to ensuring the full implementation of all previously signed agreements between Bissau and Banjul.

Why it mattersInvestors may benefit from new economic, technical, and institutional cooperation opportunities identified between Guinea-Bissau and The Gambia.

Guinea-Bissau Football Team Aims for 2027 Africa Cup of Nations Qualification

National team coach Emiliano Té expressed confidence and ambition as the Djurtus prepare to begin their qualifying campaign for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations in September.

Guinea-Bissau's national football team, the Djurtus, will commence their qualification journey for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (CAN) in September with renewed determination. Coach Emiliano Té stated that the primary objective is to secure a place in the continent's premier football competition, aiming to advance as far as possible in the tournament. The team faces a challenging group but is approaching the qualifiers with seriousness, determination, and belief in their potential to succeed.

The qualifying phase begins with the first two matchdays across twelve groups in September. Guinea-Bissau is drawn in Group L and will play their opening match away against Tanzania on September 25. Four days later, on September 29, they will host Nigeria in their second fixture. Coach Té acknowledged the strength of both opponents, noting the individual talent of the Nigerian squad and the home-field advantage Tanzania will likely leverage.

Emiliano Té, who took over coaching duties in July 2025, has been tasked with guiding the national team back to the CAN, having missed qualification for both the 2025 edition and the 2026 World Cup. He highlighted the importance of belief among the players, emphasizing that "in football, nothing is impossible" and that "in difficulty, a Guinean always appears." The team is actively analyzing their opponents' strengths and weaknesses to prepare for the demanding matches ahead.

Why it mattersThe national football team's qualification campaign for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations directly affects Guineans' national pride and interest in the sport.

Tigres de São Domingos Accuses FFGB of Disregarding Member Will

The football club Tigres de São Domingos claims the Guinean Football Federation (FFGB) disrespected its members by recognizing a former president over elected officials.

The leadership of Tigres de São Domingos has declared the Guinean Football Federation's (FFGB) decision to continue recognizing Mamadu Lenin Dabo as club president an "affront." This comes after Tigres elected new social bodies in January, whose representatives were reportedly barred from an FFGB congress.

Tigres' vice-president, Catenca Djeme, was allegedly prevented from attending the FFGB's Ordinary Congress near Bissau. The club stated in a press release that this action is unacceptable and harms its rights as a member of national football structures, questioning why an unelected former official is recognized over legitimate representatives.

The club's management, led by Carlos Mendes Teixeira, views the FFGB's stance as a disregard for the will of its associates and internal reorganization efforts. Tigres did not participate in the recent congress and reserves the right to pursue institutional and legal avenues to assert its representation.

Why it mattersFarmers are not directly affected by this dispute over football club leadership and federation recognition.
