Guinea-Bissau Parliament Leader Urges Boycott of Constitutional Referendum

Domingos Simões Pereira calls the vote a "coup against democracy" and urges citizens to stay home.

Domingos Simões Pereira, leader of the PAIGC and president-elect of Guinea-Bissau's Parliament, has called for a complete boycott of the constitutional referendum scheduled for Sunday. In a video released from Portugal, Pereira urged citizens to remain home, labeling the popular consultation a "coup against democracy and freedom" due to unmet conditions for its realization. He stated that voting would equate to participating in the "assassination" of Vigário Balanta and the "disappearance" of Tano Bari, linking the referendum to recent political violence and disappearances.

The PAI-Terra Ranka coalition, led by PAIGC, argues that the current institutional and security circumstances are unsuitable for this process. Pereira advised against voting no, warning that simply being in public could be presented by authorities as proof of popular support. He encouraged "active abstention" to defend freedom, preserve democracy, and maintain hope for truth.

A self-proclaimed military High Command seized power in November, establishing a National Transition Council to review the constitution. The new charter reduces parliamentary seats from 102 to 65 and disenfranchises Guineans abroad in presidential elections. Opposition parties, restricted from public gatherings since the coup, have used social media to call for a boycott, labeling military meetings as intimidation tactics to ensure a "yes" vote.

Why it mattersGuinean citizens are affected by the call to boycott the referendum, which could be seen as a protest against the current government and its constitutional changes.

Guinea-Bissau, Gambia Forge Technical Platform for Cooperation

A new technical consultation platform will address contemporary challenges and boost exchanges between Bissau and Banjul.

Guinea-Bissau and The Gambia plan to establish a Technical Consultation Platform to tackle current issues across various sectors and enhance interactions between their capitals, Bissau and Banjul. This initiative was announced following a meeting between Guinea-Bissau's ambassador to The Gambia, Luís Camará de Barros, and Lamin Faati, The Gambia's Director for African Affairs at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

The meeting, described as cordial, saw both sides reaffirm their commitment to strengthening historical ties of friendship and good neighborliness. Ambassador de Barros noted that discussions focused on the dynamics of bilateral relations and the review of existing legal frameworks. Both nations are dedicated to ensuring the full implementation of agreements already in place.

Furthermore, Guinea-Bissau and The Gambia expressed a mutual interest in identifying new strategic areas and joint opportunities for economic, technical, and institutional cooperation. This platform aims to formalize and expand these collaborative efforts between the two West African nations.

Why it mattersGuinea-Bissau and The Gambia are establishing a technical platform to address shared challenges and boost economic, technical, and institutional cooperation.
