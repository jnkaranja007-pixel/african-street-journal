African Union Envoy Seeks Transparent Elections in Guinea-Bissau

Patrice Trovoada, the African Union's mediator, met with Guinea-Bissau's transitional president to discuss the upcoming presidential elections and the new constitution.

Patrice Trovoada, the African Union's mediator for the Guinea-Bissau crisis, expressed his hope for transparent and inclusive presidential elections. Trovoada met with transitional President General Horta Inta-a in Bissau to review the political process following the recent referendum that approved a new constitution. He emphasized that the new constitution, approved by Guineans on August 30, is a significant step forward and must be respected by all.

The mediator stated that the transition process should continue to progress in line with established commitments. He highlighted the importance of the new constitution, which was approved by popular referendum, as a framework for the future leadership chosen by the people. Trovoada’s visit aims to engage with both civilian and military authorities guiding the country’s political transition.

The current political situation in Guinea-Bissau follows a military coup on November 26, 2025, which led to the removal of the then-elected President Umaro Sissoco Embaló. A new constitution was introduced, granting more power to the head of state. The opposition has alleged that the coup was orchestrated to facilitate Embaló's return to power through new elections scheduled by the military for December 6.

Why it mattersThe upcoming presidential elections and the new constitution directly impact the political future and stability of Guinea-Bissau.
