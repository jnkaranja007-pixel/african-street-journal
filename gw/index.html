Guinea-Bissau prime minister highlights economic growth

Public debt reaches 76.9% of GDP, while fiscal pressure remains insufficient for state needs.

Prime Minister Ilídio Vieira Té stated on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, in Bissau that Guinea-Bissau's public debt has reached 76.9% of its GDP. He also acknowledged that the current fiscal pressure of 9.1% is insufficient to meet the state's financing requirements.

During a press conference with the International Monetary Fund (IMF), Té called for greater financial discipline, modernization of tax and customs administration, and increased transparency in public management. He noted that GDP is projected to grow by 5.2% in 2026 and 5.8% in 2027, with the budget deficit expected to decrease from 7% in 2025 to 2.8% in 2027.

The IMF mission is in the country to finalize the review of a program started in 2023 and negotiate a new agreement. IMF mission chief Niko Hobdari reported that inflation has fallen to 0.7% and that the program has helped lay foundations for development, impacting infrastructure and the energy sector.

Why it mattersThe prime minister acknowledged challenges with public debt and fiscal pressure, impacting the nation's financial stability.

Lawyers accuse Supreme Court members of crimes

A group of lawyers filed a criminal complaint against the former STJ president and two counselors.

A collective of lawyers filed a criminal complaint on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, with the Public Prosecutor's Office against members of the Supreme Court of Justice (STJ) leadership. The complaint targets former STJ president Lima André and counselors Arafam Mané and João Mendes Pereira, accusing them of various crimes.

Lawyer Nansambe stated that the complaint stems from accusations by citizens Marcelino Intupe and Ussumane Sanhá, requesting an investigation into the facts. Intupe listed alleged crimes including obstruction of justice, usurpation of public functions, prevarication, denial of justice, embezzlement, slander, and falsification.

The lawyers are seeking accountability for the accused if the charges are proven. Mané was also criticized for suspending judges and cases without proper justification. The Public Prosecutor's Office has been urged to investigate and determine any responsibilities.

Why it mattersCitizens and lawyers are seeking justice and accountability from members of the Supreme Court of Justice for alleged criminal acts.

Guinea-Bissau falls to 45th in African governance index

The country is now among the 10 worst-ranked nations on the continent, showing increasing deterioration.

Guinea-Bissau has dropped to 45th place in the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance (IIAG), placing it among the 10 worst-governed countries in Africa. The index assesses governance quality across security, justice, transparency, citizen participation, human development, and economic opportunities.

The nation has lost five positions over the past decade and is among 11 African states showing the most significant regression. The report highlights a sharp decline in Security and Rule of Law, Justice, and Transparency, with education also showing the second-greatest setback in Africa over the last 10 years.

The IIAG 2026, published by the Ibrahim Foundation, covers 54 African countries using 96 indicators from 2016 to 2025. Cabo Verde ranks highest among Portuguese-speaking African countries at third overall, while Mozambique is 29th and Angola is 37th, having made progress in areas like anti-corruption and gender equality.

Why it mattersGuinea-Bissau's governance decline affects its citizens' access to security, justice, and educational opportunities.

Guinea-Bissau to compete in West African draughts tournament

Mamadu Aliu Djaló will represent the nation in Dakar, Senegal, from October 31 to November 5, 2026.

Guinea-Bissau will participate in the first West African Sub-Regional Draughts Tournament from October 31 to November 5, 2026, in Dakar, Senegal. The Senegalese Draughts Federation invited the country based on recent achievements in the sport.

The national team will be represented by Mamadu Aliu Djaló, considered a top player in the region. Bacar Dabó, president of the Guinea-Bissau Draughts Federation, stated that the athlete has extensive international experience and strong reasoning abilities, aiming for a podium finish.

Other participating countries include Senegal, Burkina Faso, Mali, Mauritania, and Guinea-Conakri. Dabó expressed confidence that Djaló can compete equally with other participants and secure a top-three position in the tournament, which follows international regulations.

Why it mattersGuinea-Bissau's participation in the tournament affects its national sports representation and the athlete competing.
