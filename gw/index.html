Guinea-Bissau charges 11 over Olof Sambú death

11 suspects face charges of murder and qualified damage for the September 27, 2026, public lynching.

Guinea-Bissau's Public Ministry has formally charged 11 suspects in connection with the death of Olof João Gomes Sambú on September 27, 2026, in the Chapa area of Bissau. The accused are in preventive detention and have eight days to contest the charges. The Public Ministry stated it has gathered sufficient evidence to prove the 11 acted together in committing the crimes of homicide and qualified damage, as defined by the Guinean Penal Code.

Olof Sambú died following a public lynching after being falsely accused of attempting to kidnap a child. Investigators found that a group of people pursued Sambú, intercepted and destroyed his vehicle before fatally assaulting him. The National Guard Command clarified that Olof Gomes Sambú was not a member of their corps but a civilian employee of the Directorate-General of Customs, where he served as head of Anti-Fraud in the Gabú Delegation.

If no legal challenges are filed within the deadline, the case will proceed to trial. The charges include murder and qualified damage. The investigation concluded that the victim was falsely associated with a child abduction attempt before the assault.

Why it matters11 individuals are facing trial for the murder of Olof Sambú, a civilian customs official, following a public lynching.

Guinea-Bissau rights group seeks swift justice for three deaths

The LGDH urged the Public Prosecutor's office to speed up investigations into recent fatalities.

The Guinean League for Human Rights (LGDH) called for faster investigations into the deaths of vicar Luís Balanta, Mamadu Tano Bari, and a military health technician who died from electrocution in Bôr. The organization wants perpetrators held accountable and the innocent protected.

LGDH president Bubacar Turé stated this position on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, following a meeting with the Attorney General. While the LGDH welcomed an inquiry commission for Luís Balanta's death, they requested intensified efforts in other unresolved cases.

Turé emphasized that justice must be impartial, treating all cases equally without distinguishing between first or second-class crimes. The LGDH also raised concerns about alleged home invasions and a rise in human trafficking cases, urging the government to bolster the Public Ministry's resources.

Why it mattersThe LGDH's call for swift justice directly affects citizens awaiting resolution for deaths and other serious crimes.

Armed men invade home of Guinea-Bissau politician

MADEM-G15 party reports six intruders held Soares Sambú's family for over an hour.

A group of six armed and hooded men allegedly invaded the home of Soares Sambú, a senior member of the Movement for Alternating Democracy (MADEM-G15) party, in Bissau early on Monday, November 9, 2026.

The intruders reportedly overpowered Sambú's security guards before entering the residence, where they remained for over an hour, causing panic among his family members. The MADEM-G15 party described the incident as a serious violation of domicile and an attack on Sambú's personal safety.

The party has urgently appealed to judicial authorities to identify the perpetrators and those who may have ordered the attack, to determine the motives, and to hold those responsible accountable.

Why it mattersThe invasion of Soares Sambú's home by armed men raises concerns about the personal safety of political figures in Guinea-Bissau.

RGB party unveils six-pillar pact to end Guinea-Bissau's political crises

Fernando Mendes proposes a fixed electoral calendar and limits on dissolving the National People's Assembly.

Fernando Mendes, president of the Resistance of Guinea-Bissau Ba-fata Movement (RGB/MB), announced on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, that his party wants a Regime Pact to end the recurring political, governmental and institutional crises that have marked the country for decades.

Presenting the document at a press conference, Mendes said the pact rests on six pillars: constitutional reform and balance of powers, stabilisation of the electoral and party system, defence and security reform, independent justice and the fight against impunity, administrative reform and decentralisation, and an economic model with social cohesion. He described it as a patriotic, inclusive commitment meant to shield state institutions from partisan disputes.

On the institutional side, Mendes called for clearer rules on the semi-presidential system, defining the powers of the president and prime minister more precisely. He also proposed minimum terms and strict criteria to prevent arbitrary dissolution of the National People's Assembly, and a fixed electoral calendar for presidential, legislative and local elections.

Why it mattersCitizens and investors would gain a predictable election timetable and a limit on sudden dissolutions of parliament.

Guinea-Bissau and Saudi Arabia sign Hajj 2027 deal

The agreement in Jeddah aims to improve pilgrimage conditions for Guinean Muslims.

Guinea-Bissau and Saudi Arabia signed a pilgrimage agreement in Jeddah on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, for the 2027 Hajj. The deal aims to strengthen cooperation in organizing the journey for Guinean Muslim pilgrims to Islam's holy sites.

Infali Coté, Guinea-Bissau's High Commissioner for Pilgrimage, and Saudi Vice-Minister of Hajj Abdul Fattah Bin Suleimane Mashat signed the accord. The agreement ensures security, organization, and dignity for pilgrims, promising better support and assistance.

This signing marks the official start of preparations for Guinean participation in the 2027 Hajj. Both nations emphasized prioritizing pilgrim well-being and maintaining high standards of quality, safety, and efficiency.

Why it mattersGuinean Muslims planning to perform the Hajj in 2027 will benefit from improved organizational and safety standards.
