Cameroon rights commission urges equal treatment for election candidates

The commission cited document delays, refusals and fees as candidates prepared for legislative and municipal elections on 07 February 2027.

Sources: Journal du Cameroun and Actu Cameroun · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Cameroon's human rights commission, the CDHC, urged public services to treat candidates and political parties equally as they prepared for legislative and municipal elections on 07 February 2027. In a declaration dated 21 November 2026, it said candidates had faced obstacles while assembling nomination documents.

Citizens from the Movement for the Rebirth of Cameroon (MRC), the Cameroon People's Party (PCRN), the Social Democratic Front (SDF), the Cameroon Democratic Union (FDC) and the governing Cameroon People's Democratic Movement (RDPC) reported delays in birth certificates, refusals to certify documents and demands for residence proof such as utility bills, leases or land titles. The commission also cited fees for signatures or documents, and delays involving tax and police services. It said unjustified refusals and delays could prevent candidates from filing on time.

The commission recommended uniform handling of applications, timely document issuance by mayors and administrative officials, free birth certificates, and urgent, impartial processing by tax and police services. Opposition candidates were still reporting blockages on Monday 23 November, the day before candidacy filings closed.

Why it mattersCandidates and parties needed public services to issue and certify their documents before candidacy filings closed on Monday 23 November.
