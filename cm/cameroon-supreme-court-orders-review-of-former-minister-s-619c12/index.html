Cameroon supreme court orders review of former minister's graft case

The court unexpectedly postponed the appeal hearing for ex-defence minister Edgar Alain Mebe Ngo'o on October 21, 2026.

Source: Journal du Cameroun · 2026-10-23 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Cameroon's Supreme Court on October 21, 2026, postponed the appeal hearing for former Defence Minister Edgar Alain Mebe Ngo'o. The court ordered a review of the case, which had been scheduled for that day. Ngo'o was initially sentenced to 30 years in prison on January 31, 2023, by the Special Criminal Court for embezzling public funds, corruption, and money laundering.

The decision came as a surprise to Ngo'o's legal team, who were prepared to present their defense. The Supreme Court's move to re-examine the case means a return to the procedure and substance of the charges that led to his conviction. His wife, Bernadette Minla'a Nkoulou, and several associates also received prison sentences ranging from 9 to 25 years.

This review opens a new phase in the legal proceedings, potentially altering the outcomes for the former minister and his co-accused. The court's decision, which was not immediately explained, aims to clarify ambiguities surrounding the process that resulted in the initial convictions. Cameroon is also intensifying its fight against money laundering and terrorism financing, having completed 19 actions by October 22, 2026.
