World Bank adds CFA36 billion for Cameroon rice irrigation

The extra $60 million will fund unfinished works in the Benoue Valley and extend the project deadline to June 30, 2029.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Cameroon will receive an extra $60 million, about CFA36 billion, from the World Bank to finish irrigation works in the Benoue Valley, in the North Region. Officials want the money to lift domestic rice output, boost farm incomes and cut the country's dependence on imported rice.

Money from the World Bank will cover unfinished infrastructure, shifts in construction costs and quantities, and safety upgrades to the Lagdo Dam, the Ministry of Economy, Planning and Regional Development said. The project now runs two years longer, to June 30, 2029, so works on 7,131 hectares of farmland can be completed.

The project began with a World Bank credit of roughly CFA117 billion. The extension was agreed on November 24 at steering committee meetings in Yaounde.

Why it mattersRice farmers in the Benoue Valley have until June 30, 2029, for irrigation works covering 7,131 hectares to be completed.
