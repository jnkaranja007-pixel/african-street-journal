Cameroon's chamber seeks processing partnerships and more reliable power

At a Yaounde forum, the chamber also urged clearer approval timelines and regulatory rules to help firms assess investment risks.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Partnerships to expand local processing of raw materials, along with steadier electricity and simpler administrative steps, were the demands Cameroon's Chamber of Commerce put to an investment forum in Yaounde on November 18. Speaking for the chamber, Senator Nfon Mukete IV Ekoko said local firms wanted deals that would generate jobs and keep more value inside the country.

Many companies still run generators to keep working, Mukete said. The World Bank's 2024 Enterprise Survey recorded 93.3% of surveyed Cameroonian firms facing outages, averaging 10.4 a month against 6.9 across sub-Saharan Africa. Manufacturers that depend on electric machinery see production halted and costs rise each time they switch to generators.

The chamber also asked for better roads, digital services, predictable regulation and firm timelines for approvals. Mukete called for more support for small and medium-sized enterprises so they can compete in African and international markets.

Why it mattersManufacturers using electric machinery face interrupted production and generator costs; the survey recorded an average of 10.4 outages a month at surveyed firms.
