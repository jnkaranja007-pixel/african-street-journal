Institutional investors hold 38.3% of Cameroon’s Treasury bonds

Their bond holdings fell by CFA19.23 billion in October, while Treasury bill holdings rose 33.6% to CFA68.89 billion.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Institutional investors held CFA500.54 billion of Cameroon’s Treasury bonds at October 31, equal to 38.3% of outstanding bonds on the regional government securities market. Including Treasury bills, their holdings of Cameroonian government securities totalled CFA569.42 billion, according to the Bank of Central African States’ latest monthly statistics.

Investors’ share of all outstanding Cameroonian securities rose from 26.16% in September to 27.65% in October, even as their holdings slipped by CFA1.94 billion. The country’s total outstanding securities fell by nearly CFA125 billion over the same month.

Institutional investors’ Treasury bill holdings grew 33.6%, from CFA51.58 billion to CFA68.89 billion, while their bond holdings declined by CFA19.23 billion. Bonds still made up nearly 88% of their government securities portfolio. Primary dealers remained the largest holders, with CFA1,299.83 billion, or 63.12% of outstanding securities.

Why it mattersPension funds, insurers and other institutions are moving money from long-dated bonds into short-term bills, shortening the duration of their Cameroonian government holdings.
