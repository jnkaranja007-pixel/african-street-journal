Cameroon ships first 17 tons of coffee to China duty-free

The Ministry of Finance says the shipment qualifies under Beijing's zero-tariff policy, which gives Cameroonian exporters duty-free entry to the Chinese market.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-11 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Cameroon has shipped its first 17 tons of coffee to China under Beijing's zero-tariff policy, which lets Cameroonian exporters enter the Chinese market without import duties on qualifying shipments, according to the Ministry of Finance. The ministry announced the shipment after a November 10 meeting in Yaoundé between Finance Minister Louis Paul Motaze and Du Xiaohui, director-general of African Affairs at China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

The ministry said Cameroonian exports to China rose 41.8% year on year, with cocoa shipments up 272%. Discussions covered investment, ongoing projects and ways for Cameroonian businesses to benefit from China's tariff preferences, and both sides agreed to continue economic negotiations. Trade had already been growing before the policy took effect: Chinese Ambassador Xu Yong told the press in July that exports rose 23.5% year on year in the first four months of 2026.

China's Customs Tariff Commission of the State Council introduced the preferential rates on May 1, 2026, under the country's Tariff Law. The measure covers Cameroon and 19 other eligible African countries.

Why it mattersCameroonian coffee and cocoa exporters pay no Chinese import duties on qualifying shipments under the preferential rates in force since May 1, 2026.
