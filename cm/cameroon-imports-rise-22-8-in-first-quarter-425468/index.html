Cameroon imports rise 22.8% in first quarter

Goods imports climbed 25.8% as the trade gap widened, according to the National Institute of Statistics.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-12 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Cameroon's imports of goods and services surged by 22.8% in the first quarter of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025. This increase, driven primarily by a 25.8% rise in goods imports, occurred despite government initiatives aimed at reducing reliance on foreign products. The National Institute of Statistics (INS) reported these figures on November 5, 2026.

The country's external trade gap widened as exports grew by only 3.8% during the same period. The INS stated that the deficit in trade for goods and services reached 5.7% of GDP for the quarter. Imports of machinery and furniture saw a significant increase, while purchases of some manufactured and food products decreased.

Compared to the final quarter of 2025, imports rose 10.2% and exports increased by 0.6%. The INS noted that demand for nearly all imported goods increased significantly. This import surge coincided with a 12.3% year-over-year increase in private investment.

Why it mattersCameroon's trade deficit widened, impacting the national economy due to increased spending on foreign goods.
