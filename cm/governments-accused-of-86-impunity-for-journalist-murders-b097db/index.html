Governments accused of 86% impunity for journalist murders

UNESCO reports global impunity rate for journalist killings rose to 86% in 2026, a reversal of a downward trend since 2012.

Source: Journal du Cameroun · 2026-11-05 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The global impunity rate for journalist murders has climbed to 86% in 2026, according to UNESCO, meaning seven out of 10 killings go unpunished. This figure represents an increase of one point from previous years, reversing a decline that began in 2012 when the rate was 95%. Governments are accused of complicity and institutional failure in addressing these crimes.

The report criticizes governments for relying on excuses like conflict, terrorism, or insecurity to avoid prosecuting killers. It states that when journalists are killed, it silences whistleblowers and terrorizes newsrooms, ultimately blinding entire populations. The UNESCO report highlights that investigations are often dropped, judges are intimidated, or police are bribed, with powerful individuals like ministers or generals allegedly protecting perpetrators.

This high rate of impunity is described not as an accident but as a deliberate policy to send a message that speaking out carries fatal risks. The failure of justice systems and political courage is directly linked to the safety of all citizens, as the freedom of everyone is compromised when murderers of journalists are not brought to justice.

Why it mattersGovernments are accused of failing to prosecute those who murder journalists, impacting the public's right to information and the safety of reporters.
