Cameroon seeks German dairy technology

The nation aims to boost milk production and cut import costs with German expertise.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-13 · 1 min read

Cameroon's import bill for milk reached CFAF32.8 billion in 2025, and the Chamber of Agriculture, Fisheries, Livestock and Forestry (CAPEF) is now looking to Germany to shrink it. Its officials met a delegation of German experts on November 9 to explore technology transfers, equipment and training for raising, processing and storing milk.

Taking part in the talks are Dresden-based Elektrobildungs- und Technologiezentrum (EBZ), the Mbouo Multipurpose Training Centre and BERCO-INSTITUTE. CAPEF said the discussions covered weak points in herd breeding, livestock feed, animal health and the conditions farmers work under, with renewable energy and support for women and young people in agribusiness also on the table.

No investment commitments, equipment orders or timetable for putting anything in place were announced. Cameroon produced 183,286 tonnes of milk in 2025, up 1.5% on the year before, yet local output still falls short of demand.

Why it mattersDairy farmers and processors would gain new training and equipment if the German talks become concrete, but no timetable has been set.
