Cameroon opens coffee organisation to consumer countries

A revised framework lets African coffee-consuming countries seek membership and join producers in discussions on processing, marketing and sales.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Cameroon signed an agreement in Yaounde opening the Inter-African Coffee Organization to African coffee-consuming countries, giving them a route to seek membership alongside producers. Trade Minister Luc Magloire Mbarga Atangana signed the deal at the opening of the organisation’s 66th annual meetings.

The 2020 framework replaces the organisation’s 1998 rules and creates a platform for producers and consumers to discuss coffee processing, marketing and sales within Africa. Mbarga Atangana, the organisation’s outgoing president, said the continent’s 1.3 billion consumers offered producers and processors more buyers. He said stronger intra-African trade could reduce reliance on volatile international markets.

Cameroon’s local roasters processed about 750 tonnes in the 2024-25 season, nearly 7% more than the previous season, after a 44% decline, according to the National Cocoa and Coffee Board. The board recorded robusta prices of CFA1,300 to CFA2,500 per kilogram and arabica prices of CFA2,500 to CFA3,500.

Why it mattersCoffee growers are being urged to seek fairer, more transparent payments, while Cameroon recorded robusta prices of CFA1,300 to CFA2,500 per kilogram in 2024-25.
