Lukoil asset sale uncertainty deepens financing problems for Cameroon's Etinde gas project

British partner Bowleven says no further funding is available while one of the project's partners remains under international sanctions.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Financing for Cameroon's Etinde offshore gas project has been further complicated by uncertainty over the future of Russian oil company Lukoil's international assets. Bowleven, the British partner, told shareholders on November 13 that additional funding is currently unavailable while one partner remains subject to international sanctions. Lukoil holds a 37.5% interest in Etinde, and attempts to sell its overseas assets have yet to produce a completed transaction.

The financing problem adds to years of delays at the project, where operations remain largely suspended and several steps needed before development can proceed are still unresolved. Etinde is split between operator New Age and Lukoil, each with 37.5%.

Bowleven has identified a risk that the project's exploitation licence may be withdrawn or made subject to tougher conditions, either because of the sanctions or because partners have failed to meet obligations, including deadlines under the original work programme. The company presents this as a potential risk, not a withdrawal process begun by Cameroonian authorities.

Why it mattersOperator New Age and Lukoil, which each hold 37.5% of Etinde, must resolve the sale of Lukoil's assets before any new funding can flow to the project.
