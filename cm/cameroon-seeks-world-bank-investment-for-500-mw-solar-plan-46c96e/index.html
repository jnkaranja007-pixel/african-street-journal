Cameroon seeks World Bank investment for 500 MW solar plan

The proposal was discussed in Yaoundé as government projections put the power shortfall at 800 MW by 2030 without new investment.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

At a meeting in Yaoundé on November 19, Cameroon put forward an emergency solar plan of 500 MW to the World Bank, hoping to draw investment that could prevent a projected 800 MW power shortfall by 2030.

Water and Energy Minister Gaston Eloundou Essomba held talks with Ousmane Diagana, the Bank's vice president for Western and Central Africa. Government figures shown there forecast a deficit of 140 MW in 2027 and 470 MW in 2028. Business in Cameroon, citing government data, put installed capacity at 2,280 MW at the end of 2025, while the ministry is aiming for 3,000 MW by 2030.

Officials also discussed expanding the Kribi gas-fired plant to 600 MW, building smaller plants through the state electricity company SOCADEL, and investing in transmission lines to supply homes and factories.

Why it mattersThe power ministry must attract investment to close a projected gap that reaches 800 MW by 2030.
