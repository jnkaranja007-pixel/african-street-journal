Cameroon's minister orders mayors to issue birth certificates to election candidates

Georges Elanga Obam says refusing the documents or charging fees for them taints the electoral contest, and the rule is that they are free.

Sources: Journal du Cameroun and Actu Cameroun · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Georges Elanga Obam, Cameroon's minister of decentralisation and local development, has told town mayors to make it easier for election candidates to obtain birth certificate extracts. In a letter to municipal magistrates dated 18 November 2026, he said refusing to issue documents the law requires, or charging for them when the law provides for free issue, is a reprehensible practice that taints the electoral contest.

The minister said mayors are legally bound to carry out the service as civil registry officers and called on them to take useful measures to issue the extracts in line with the regulations. The intervention follows complaints from candidates who have struggled to assemble their candidacy files, and from parties including the MRC and MP3 and some branches of the ruling RDPC, which have taken up their cases.

The minister acknowledged that his office had not verified the complaints before issuing the warning. Reports say his intervention would be more effective if it were backed by specific, verified cases, and the nomination deadline for the municipal and legislative elections is six days away.
