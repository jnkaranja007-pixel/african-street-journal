Cameroon signs coffee pact opening African body to consuming countries

The agreement gives coffee-producing and consuming governments a forum to discuss processing, marketing and sales within Africa.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Cameroon signed an agreement on November 24 in Yaounde that opens the Inter-African Coffee Organization to African coffee-consuming countries. Trade Minister Luc Magloire Mbarga Atangana signed it at the opening of the organization’s 66th annual meetings.

The 2020 agreement replaces the organization’s 1998 framework and lets consuming countries seek membership alongside producers. It creates a forum for governments on both sides of the coffee trade to discuss processing, marketing and sales within Africa.

Mbarga Atangana, the organization’s outgoing president, said the African Continental Free Trade Area could help producers and processors reach more buyers. He estimated the potential market at 1.3 billion consumers and said African sales could reduce reliance on volatile international markets. The pact aligns with Cameroon’s policy of processing more raw materials locally and promoting locally made products.

Why it mattersCoffee-producing governments and processors can discuss how to reach a market the minister estimates at 1.3 billion consumers.
