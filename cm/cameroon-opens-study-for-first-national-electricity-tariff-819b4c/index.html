Cameroon opens study for first national electricity tariff policy

Consultations began in Yaoundé on November 25 to examine pricing principles, social tariffs, targeted subsidies and equalization.

Source: Business in Cameroon · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Cameroon’s Ministry of Water and Energy began work on the country’s first national electricity tariff policy, opening consultations in Yaoundé on November 25, 2026. The consultations will review the scope of a study to guide the proposed framework, which is intended to set clearer rules for electricity prices.

A ministry representative speaking for the water and energy minister said the lack of a national policy had contributed to inconsistent contractual practices and unpredictable pricing decisions. The study will assess social tariffs for eligible households, subsidies for specific consumer groups and equalization to address different prices paid by comparable consumers across geographic areas.

The framework is intended to balance affordability for consumers with the power sector’s financial needs. No new rates, subsidy amounts or eligibility criteria have been announced.

Why it mattersEligible households and other electricity consumers are the focus of a study examining lower social rates and targeted subsidies, though no amounts or criteria are set.
