Cameroon plans CFA50 billion more in annual beverage taxes

The 2027 finance bill would raise excise duties and add a CFA5 stamp duty to each bottled drink.

Sources: Journal du Cameroun and Actu Cameroun · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Cameroon’s 2027 finance bill proposes raising excise duties on bottled drinks and adding a CFA5 stamp duty to each bottle, with the government expecting CFA50 billion in extra tax revenue a year. The proposal covers brewers and makers of soft drinks and other bottled beverages.

The measures under discussion would increase costs by CFA100 to CFA150 per bottle, a rise of 10% to 20% depending on the brand, Journal du Cameroun reported. Producers say the state already receives 55% of a bottle’s price through taxes. The producers’ association says the industry provides 10 000 direct jobs and supports about 6 000 farmers and nearly 2 900 partner small businesses.

The proposal also comes after repeated increases in beverage duties since 2019 and rising energy, raw-material and freight costs, the report said. The government has previously blocked operators from raising prices, with the Commerce Ministry setting a CFA50 limit in 2019.

Why it mattersThe producers’ association says 6 000 farmers and nearly 2 900 partner small businesses depend on an industry facing the proposed tax increase.
