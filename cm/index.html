Cameroon refugee artisans seek wider markets

Over 135 refugees in East Region receive vocational training and access to sales outlets.

More than 135 Central African refugees in Cameroon's East Region have received or are undergoing vocational and business training. The programme, a partnership between Cameroon's Ministry of Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises and the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) from 2024–2026, offers skills in areas like cassava processing, tailoring, and business management. 10 refugees currently use a permanent exhibition and sales outlet in Bertoua.

The initiative, implemented through the Bertoua Regional Craft Village (VARB), aims to help beneficiaries organise production and reach wider commercial markets. Participating artisans have attended the Cameroon International Handicrafts Exhibition in 2024 and 2026. A refugee craftswoman won the national Art and Decoration award at the 2026 exhibition.

VARB coordinator Alexis Carole Ngassomo Emama Miasse proposed organising artisans by trade to boost production capacity and fulfil orders. UNHCR's Bertoua office and the University of Bertoua organised a dialogue on October 26 and 27 where e-commerce training was also recommended.

Why it mattersRefugees in Cameroon's East Region are gaining new skills and market access for their crafts.

Cameroon to raise 2027 public investment budget

The government aims for a significant rise in public investment next year but may miss its 40% budget target.

Cameroon's government plans to raise its public investment budget in 2027, aiming for a significant increase from the current year. However, this rise is still expected to fall short of the National Development Strategy's goal of allocating 40% of the general budget to investment.

The Cabinet acknowledged this gap after a meeting on October 27, 2026, in Yaoundé, chaired by Prime Minister Joseph Dion Ngute. While promising a substantial boost in investment, the statement did not provide specific figures for the proposed allocation or its percentage of the overall budget. This announcement follows a presidential circular from October 12, which set a minimum investment target of 30% for 2027.

In 2026, Cameroon's Public Investment Budget (PIB) is CFA2,031.15 billion, representing 23.4% of the CFA8,683.9 billion general budget. This is 6.6 percentage points below the 2027 minimum target. These figures reflect budgeted amounts, not actual spending, with unfinished projects and electricity prioritized for 2027.

Why it mattersCameroonian businesses and the economy will be affected by the planned increase in public investment, though it may not reach the government's long-term target.

Agriculture · 2026-11-01

Chinese firm eyes $63.0M sugar, rice investment in Cameroon

Nanning Agricultural Development Group is considering large-scale plantations and a processing complex in Batouri.

Chinese state-owned company Nanning Agricultural Development Group is considering investing approximately CFA88 billion (US$63.03M) in sugar and rice production in Batouri, Cameroon’s East Region. The proposed project includes sugarcane and rice cultivation alongside a factory designed to produce 60,000 tonnes of sugar annually.

The investment remains at the feasibility assessment stage, with the company seeking a specialised consultancy to evaluate the project’s technical and financial viability. Bids for the feasibility study, for which Nanning has allocated up to CFA78 million (US$55.87M), are due by November 5, 2026.

This proposed investment revives plans for a sugar complex in Batouri, where previous initiatives by Justin Sugar Mills and Cosumar failed to reach implementation. Orient’s Sugar and Industry SA also explored developing a sugar complex in the East Region in October 2017.

Why it mattersCameroon's East Region may see agricultural development and job creation if the Chinese firm's investment proceeds.

Business · 2026-11-01

Cameroon bauxite project awaits CFA36.5 billion loan

AFG Bank Cameroon will inspect the Minim-Martap bauxite site in November before deciding on CFA36.5 billion loan release.

Canyon Resources has not yet secured CFA36.5 billion in bank financing for its Minim-Martap bauxite project in Cameroon's Adamawa region. AFG Bank Cameroon scheduled a site inspection for November 2026 before deciding whether to resume disbursements under the syndicated loan. The bank suspended further withdrawals more than two months after the inspection was originally due.

The bank is awaiting the site visit and a review of the project's development schedule and financial model. Canyon submitted requested documents in the third quarter, but the review is ongoing. Lenders must still approve the project's requirements before releasing additional funds. As of September 30, 2026, Canyon's Cameroon subsidiary, Camalco, had drawn CFA45.5 billion of the CFA82 billion loan.

The syndicated loan was for a total of CFA82 billion. Camalco had drawn 55.5% of the loan as of September 30, 2026. The bank's decision on resuming disbursements is pending the site inspection and review outcomes.

Education · 2026-11-01

UNESCO gives Cameroon computers for education monitoring

The organization provided 25 laptops and technical equipment to improve learning assessments.

The UNESCO office in Cameroon officially handed over 25 laptop computers and technical equipment to the Permanent Operational Team and the Pilot Unit for School Achievements on Friday, October 30, 2026. This donation aims to strengthen the monitoring, evaluation, and quality of education across the country.

The equipment, including printing and projection devices, specialized software, and collaborative tools, was provided under the Support Program for the Professionalization of the Teaching Profession in Cameroon (PAPMEC). The Minister of Basic Education (MINEDUB) signed a property transfer agreement during the ceremony, marking a step in enhancing national capacity for educational system coordination and assessment.

This initiative, funded by the Global Partnership for Education (GPE) and implemented by UNESCO and UNICEF, will enable teams to better manage program activities, analyze school achievement data, and inform decisions for improved learning quality. The UNESCO Director emphasized the organization's commitment to supporting member states' efforts in providing inclusive and quality education.

Why it mattersCameroonian education officials received equipment to better track and improve student learning outcomes.
