Cameroon proposes SME financing plan in Qatar

Minister Achille Bassilekin III presented three proposals to boost small businesses at the World Investment Forum.

Cameroon presented three proposals to attract international investment and improve financing for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) at the World Investment Forum in Doha, Qatar, from October 25 to 27, 2026. Minister Achille Bassilekin III of the Ministry of SMEs, Social Economy and Handicrafts (MINPMEESA) put forward the plans on October 30.

The proposals aim to expand the Empretec entrepreneurship programme to all 10 regions of Cameroon, incorporating digital technologies and artificial intelligence. They also seek to connect Cameroonian entrepreneurs with international mentoring and investment platforms, such as the Global Empretec Fellowship, and promote tailored guarantee, refinancing, and co-financing mechanisms for SMEs in key sectors.

These initiatives address the ongoing constraint of limited access to financing for Cameroonian enterprises. The Minister highlighted that over 99% of Cameroon's economic fabric consists of SMEs, which generate more than 74% of decent jobs.

Why it mattersCameroonian SMEs, which form the backbone of the national economy and employment, will benefit from improved access to financing and global networks.

Metafrique Steel explosion kills four in Douala

16 employees were burned and taken to Douala General Hospital following a blast at the steel plant.

An explosion at the Metafrique Steel factory in Douala on November 2, 2026, killed four people and injured 16 others. The blast occurred around 7:30 AM in the Douala-Bassa industrial zone, triggering a significant fire and causing extensive material damage.

Firefighters recovered the four bodies, while the 16 injured employees were transported to the Hôpital Général de Douala. Governor Samuel Dieudonné Ivaha Diboua visited the hospital to check on the victims, some of whom are in serious condition. Metafrique Steel stated the incident's origin was external and that causes are still being determined.

This is not the first incident at the plant, which specializes in rebar and recycled steel production and has been in Cameroon for about 16 years. An earlier fire on May 1, 2026, injured at least 10 people, leading to a production suspension. Authorities have announced an investigation and potential conservatory measures, including a possible suspension of the company's activities.

Why it mattersFour people died and 16 employees were injured in an explosion and fire at the Metafrique Steel factory in Douala.

Cameroon agency finds 3,914 fintech security flaws

National Agency for Information and Communication Technologies identified weaknesses in policies, staff expertise, and data protection.

Cameroon's National Agency for Information and Communication Technologies (ANTIC) identified 3,914 security vulnerabilities in 108 fintech operator audits. The findings, presented by Director General Prof. Ebot Ebot Enaw on October 28, 2026, at the Digitise Cameroon Forum in Mbankomo, cover security policies, staff expertise, access controls, data protection, and business continuity. This follows earlier audits in November 2025 that flagged over 6,500 vulnerabilities.

The agency has issued recommendations to the fintech operators, which include banks, microfinance institutions, and payment providers. ANTIC did not disclose how many of the identified vulnerabilities were critical or how many have been corrected. Mobile Money generated FCFA135.8 billion in revenue in 2024 with 11.4 million active subscriptions.

Cameroon's new personal data protection law took effect in December 2024, with an 18-month compliance period ending in June 2026. The previous audits in November 2025 covered 119 financial operators.

Why it mattersFintech operators in Cameroon face scrutiny over security vulnerabilities affecting data protection and business continuity.

Cameroon and Türkiye chambers sign investment deal

A new forum will promote joint investments and strengthen business ties between the two nations.

Cameroonian and Turkish business chambers signed a memorandum of understanding on November 1 in Istanbul to promote joint investments and strengthen business ties. Christophe Eken, President of Cameroon's Chamber of Commerce, Industry, Mines and Crafts (CCIMA), signed the document with representatives of the Union of Chambers and Commodity Exchanges of Türkiye (TOBB).

The MoU establishes a Türkiye-Cameroon Chamber of Commerce and Industry Forum intended to facilitate joint investments in Cameroon and encourage long-term cooperation. This initiative supports Cameroon's goal to develop its manufacturing sector and attract foreign investment.

Bilateral merchandise trade reached approximately $349.2 million in 2025, with Cameroon exporting $159.8 million worth of goods, including cocoa and wood products. Türkiye supplies Cameroon with machinery and manufactured goods.

Why it mattersCameroon's business chambers and the country's manufacturing sector will benefit from strengthened ties and potential joint investments with Türkiye.

UDC to hold 8th congress in December

The Union démocratique du Cameroun will elect a new national president at its December 4-5 meeting.

The Union démocratique du Cameroun (UDC) will hold its 8th national congress on December 4 and 5, 2026, in Yaoundé. The congress will elect a new national president and a new executive committee. The party's supreme body will also set political and organizational directions.

UDC national president Patricia Tomaïno Ndam Njoya, who is also the mayor of Foumban, called for member mobilization. The congress is taking place as the party prepares for the February 2026 legislative and municipal elections. The UDC currently has four deputies in the National Assembly and manages six communes.

The UDC was founded on April 26, 1991, by Dr. Adamou Ndam Njoya, who led the party until his death in 2020. Patricia Tomaïno Ndam Njoya has led the party since 2020 and was confirmed in 2021. The newly elected president will be legally empowered to approve candidate nominations for the February 2027 elections.

Why it mattersThe UDC's 8th congress will elect a new national president and set the party's strategy for upcoming legislative and municipal elections.
