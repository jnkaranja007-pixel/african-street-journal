US Court Jails Three Cameroonian-Americans for Funding Separatist Attacks

A federal court in Missouri sentenced three men of Cameroonian origin to prison for providing financial and logistical support to separatist fighters in Cameroon.

A federal court in Kansas City, Missouri, has sentenced three American citizens of Cameroonian descent to prison terms ranging from over five to fifteen years for their roles in supporting separatist fighters in Cameroon. Francis Chenyi, 52, and Lah Nestor Langmi, 49, each received 15-year sentences, while Claude Ngenevu Chi, 43, was sentenced to 63 months, or just over five years. All three will also serve three years of supervised release after completing their sentences.

The men were convicted of conspiring to provide financial and logistical support for the creation and use of improvised explosive devices (IEDs) by separatist combatants operating in Cameroon's Northwest and Southwest regions. Prosecutors stated that they coordinated the manufacturing of explosives, trained individuals in their use, and facilitated money transfers from the United States to Cameroon to fund these attacks, which targeted several cities and areas near stadiums for the 2021 Africa Cup of Nations.

The U.S. Department of Justice indicated that Langmi was involved in coordinating the kidnapping of a traditional leader and Cardinal Christian Tumi. Chenyi allegedly provided questions for the interrogation of the captives and approved fund transfers to support the kidnapping. Chenyi also reportedly approved funds for IED construction and coordinated training for separatist fighters in their use.

Why it mattersThree Cameroonian-Americans were sentenced to prison for funding and facilitating separatist attacks in Cameroon, including kidnappings and the use of explosives.

Cameroon Pharmaceutical Production Units Grow, Local Output Remains Low

The number of pharmaceutical production units in Cameroon has risen to 19, yet local manufacturing still supplies less than 5% of the nation's medicine needs.

Cameroon has significantly expanded its pharmaceutical manufacturing capacity, increasing production units from seven in 2020 to 19 by 2026. Despite this growth, local output continues to fall far short of national demand, meeting less than 5% of consumption needs. Public Health Minister Dr. Manaouda Malachie presented these figures, noting that the country's 239 approved pharmaceutical products cover only about 15% of the National Essential Medicines List.

These statistics were disclosed following a ministerial dialogue in Addis Ababa on August 24, held during the World Health Organization Regional Committee for Africa's 76th session. African health ministers and stakeholders convened to discuss strengthening pharmaceutical regulation and boosting regional production. Minister Malachie highlighted ongoing challenges for Cameroonian manufacturers, including limited access to finance, unreliable energy supply, difficulties in sourcing raw materials, and competition from imported goods.

The government has implemented measures such as customs and tax exemptions for specific inputs and equipment, and has introduced preferential treatment for local products. However, manufacturers continue to face delays in payments for public orders, impacting their operational capacity and ability to scale up production to meet the substantial demand for medicines across the country.

Why it mattersLocal pharmaceutical production is insufficient to meet national demand, affecting the availability and accessibility of medicines for the Cameroonian population.

US Companies Pursue CFAF 4trn Business Pipeline in Cameroon

American firms are exploring nearly $7 billion in trade and investment opportunities across multiple sectors, following bilateral economic talks.

American companies are seeking nearly $7 billion, or CFAF 4 trillion, in trade and investment opportunities within Cameroon, according to the US Embassy in Yaounde. These ventures span energy, mining, transport infrastructure, technology, forestry, and logistics. The potential investments were a key topic during the inaugural US-Cameroon Bilateral Economic and Commercial Dialogue held in Yaounde on August 27.

The pipeline includes projects at various stages of development, such as a proposed $3 billion hydropower plant, a $2 billion airport project, and mining ventures valued at a minimum of $585 million. The dialogue established a formal platform for both countries to address investment obstacles and pursue shared economic priorities. Joint working groups will be formed to maintain momentum, with follow-up discussions scheduled for the United States in 2027.

Cameroon's Minister of the Economy, Planning and Regional Development, Alamine Ousmane Mey, led the Cameroonian delegation, while Deputy Assistant Secretary of State Sarah Troutman headed the American contingent. The US Embassy stated that the dialogue aims to facilitate these significant business opportunities and strengthen economic ties between the two nations.

Why it mattersAmerican companies are pursuing nearly $7 billion in trade and investment opportunities in Cameroon, spanning energy, mining, transport infrastructure, technology, forestry, and logistics.

Cameroon and UNFPA Approve 50 Million USD Cooperation Program

The UN Population Fund's Board approved the ninth Country Cooperation Program for Cameroon, focusing on population well-being and demographic dividends.

The United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) Board officially validated Cameroon's ninth Country Cooperation Program (CPD9) on August 27, 2026, in New York. This program, running from 2027 to 2031, is budgeted at 50 million USD and aligns with Cameroon's National Development Strategy (SND30) and the UN Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF). The initiative aims to enhance population well-being and leverage demographic dividends, continuing a partnership that began in 1969.

The CPD9 is structured around three strategic pillars: improving reproductive healthcare and maternal health outcomes, promoting gender equality and ending gender-based violence, and utilizing demographic data for informed public policy. Cameroon's Permanent Mission in New York expressed strong government support, noting the program's alignment with national development priorities. Several ministries, including Health, Women's Empowerment, Youth Affairs, Finance, and Planning, are involved in its implementation.

To facilitate the expanded cooperation, Cameroon will provide a new, modern headquarters for the UNFPA in Yaoundé. This move signifies Cameroon's commitment to its role as a key partner in advancing population-related goals. The program's validation marks a significant step in the ongoing collaboration between the Cameroonian government and the UNFPA, building on decades of partnership.

Why it mattersThe program will affect the well-being of Cameroonian populations by improving healthcare, promoting gender equality, and informing public policy with demographic data.

Cameroonian Parties Demand Action on Rising Feminicides

Multiple political parties are uniting to demand exemplary sanctions against perpetrators of femicides, as over sixty women have been killed in eight months.

Political parties in Cameroon are forming a united front to address the escalating issue of femicides, calling for severe penalties for those responsible. The Mouvement patriotiques pour la prospérité du peuple (MP3), Mouvement pour la renaissance du Cameroun (MRC), Parti camerounais pour la réconciliation nationale (PCRN), and Social Democratic Front (SDF) are among those expressing deep concern over the trend. They argue that silence and inaction are no longer acceptable responses to the loss of lives, shattered families, and orphaned children.

The MP3 has proposed measures including protection for women at risk, harsh punishments for aggressors, and educational initiatives to prevent violence. The MRC condemned the "silent hemorrhage" of domestic femicides and criticized authorities for their perceived indifference and the trivialization of these crimes, advocating for stricter penalties. The PCRN emphasized the need for "repression that instills fear" to deter such acts, stating that no grievance justifies violence against women.

The SDF highlighted that they have submitted legislative proposals to the National Assembly aimed at combating femicides and violence against women and children. However, they noted that the ruling party, the Rassemblement démocratique du peuple camerounais, has remained silent on these proposals. The SDF, lacking a majority to pass legislation independently, stated that their proposals depend on the will of those in power.

Why it mattersPolitical parties and their members are affected by the rising femicides and are advocating for stricter laws and enforcement to protect women.
