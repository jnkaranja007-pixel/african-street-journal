Léonide Mfoum resigns from UNDP after 12 years of activism

Léonide Mfoum, a national communication cell member, cited a lack of debate and party direction as reasons for his departure from the Union Nationale pour la Démocratie et le.

Léonide Mfoum has resigned from the Union Nationale pour la Démocratie et le Progrès (UNDP) after 12 years, effective August twenty-fifth, 2026. He stated his refusal to be led by “incompetents” and described the party as having “no horizon, no perspective.” Mfoum also criticized the party's inability to engage in debate, which he believes hinders progress and future planning.

Mfoum attributed his resignation to the party's failure in the 2025 presidential election, where candidate Bello Bouba Maigari placed fourth with 2.45% of the votes. He blamed party leaders for refusing self-reflection and criticism, which he called the “cancer” of the party. Mfoum specifically pointed to the campaign director, suggesting a lack of understanding of the role.

Despite the electoral setback, Mfoum claims no lessons were learned within the UNDP, and the same leadership continued. He denounced party leaders as having “adulterated depth” and being willing to sideline bright minds who could pose a challenge. His departure comes ahead of the 2027 municipal and legislative elections, though he did not indicate his next political move.

Why it mattersThis story directly affects citizens and political actors by detailing a high-profile resignation from a major political party, signaling internal strife and potential shifts in the political landscape ahead of future elections.

Canyon Resources postpones bauxite production amid financial dispute

Canyon Resources has indefinitely delayed its Minim-Martap bauxite project in Cameroon, citing a frozen credit line and a hostile takeover bid.

Canyon Resources announced on Monday, August twenty-fourth, 2026, that it is postponing the start of bauxite production at its Minim-Martap project, which was initially scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2026. The company's CEO, Peter Secker, will depart by the end of August, indicating internal governance issues.

The delay stems from AFG Bank Cameroon freezing an 82 billion FCFA ($140 million) credit line essential for operations. This financial blockage coincides with a hostile takeover attempt by A2MP Investments, led by Gagan Gupta, which already holds 55.56% of Canyon Resources' capital. A2MP Investments has launched a bid to acquire full control at a discounted price of 0.05 Australian dollars per share, arguing that rising logistical costs make the project unprofitable.

Despite these financial and corporate challenges, the physical infrastructure for the project is largely complete. The mining road is finished, and 60 wagons ordered from China are expected to arrive in Douala in October. The Minim-Martap deposit, containing 1.1 billion tons of bauxite, is ready for extraction, leaving the Cameroonian state, which anticipated bauxite exports to offset declining oil revenues, in a prolonged wait for the project's commencement.

Why it mattersThe delay in the Minim-Martap bauxite project directly affects the Cameroonian state's anticipated revenue from mineral exports and impacts the local economy in the Adamaoua region.

Minesup restricts professional license admissions to private higher education institutions

Minister of Higher Education Jacques Fame Ndongo has reinforced the requirement for a two-year post-Baccalaureate qualification for professional license programs, targeting.

The Ministry of Higher Education (Minesup) has tightened admission rules for professional licenses, prohibiting private higher education institutions (IPES) from admitting students without a Bac+2 level qualification. This directive, issued on August twenty-first, two thousand twenty-six, aims to uphold the LMD system standard, which mandates six semesters of study for a license.

Minister Jacques Fame Ndongo's communication specifies that professional licenses are accessible only to candidates holding a valid two-year post-secondary diploma, such as a DEUG, BTS, HND, or DUT. The minister noted that some IPES have been admitting Baccalaureate holders and granting them a license after just one year of study, which contradicts the established regulations.

This measure comes weeks before the two thousand twenty-six/two thousand twenty-seven academic year and warns IPES promoters to strictly adhere to the rule to avoid sanctions. The ministry emphasizes that this enforcement is crucial for maintaining the quality of education, preserving student standards, and ensuring their competitiveness in the job market, addressing concerns about declining academic levels.

Why it mattersStudents seeking professional licenses will need to ensure they meet the Bac+2 qualification, impacting their educational path and career prospects.

SCDP inaugurates three new infrastructures to boost energy supply

The Société Camerounaise des Dépôts Pétroliers (SCDP) launched a gas sphere, a pipeline, and a new headquarters in Douala to enhance national energy security.

The Société Camerounaise des Dépôts Pétroliers (SCDP) inaugurated three major infrastructures on Friday, August twenty-first, 2026, in Douala. These include a new domestic gas sphere in Bonabéri, a pipeline connecting the Port of Douala to the Bessengué depot, and a new company headquarters. The Minister of Water and Energy, Gaston Eloundou Essomba, presided over the ceremony alongside SCDP Director General Véronique Manzoua épouse Moampea Mbio.

The new gas sphere in Bonabéri increases Cameroon's national gas storage capacity by forty percent, aiming to prevent shortages of cooking gas for households. The pipeline from the Port of Douala to Bessengué is designed to accelerate the unloading of oil tankers, streamline the logistics chain, and reduce fuel supply pressures across the country.

Minister Eloundou Essomba stated that these investments mark a shift from reactive management to an approach based on anticipation, resilience, and energy sovereignty. The new headquarters symbolizes the public company's transformation, reinforcing efforts to secure daily life for Cameroonians by improving fuel and gas availability.

Why it mattersCameroonian households will experience more consistent access to cooking gas and fuel, reducing disruptions in their daily lives.
