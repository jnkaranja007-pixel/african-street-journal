Cameroon Ministry Invites SMEs to Indonesia Trade Expo

The Ministry of Small and Medium-sized Enterprises is seeking Cameroonian businesses to explore opportunities at the 41st Trade Expo Indonesia.

Cameroonian businesses are invited to participate in the 41st Trade Expo Indonesia 2026, scheduled for October 14-18 in Tangerang, near Jakarta. The Ministry of Small and Medium-sized Enterprises, Social Economy and Handicrafts (MINPMEESA) issued the invitation on August 31, targeting SME promoters, professional associations, and corporations interested in the Indonesian market. The event aims to connect international buyers with Indonesian exporters and suppliers.

Organized by Indonesia’s Ministry of Trade, the annual expo features exhibitions, business-matching sessions, trade missions, seminars, and official meetings. This year's exhibition will showcase five product zones: food, beverages and agriculture; manufactured goods; industrial estates; fashion and crafts; and furniture and home decor. Interested Cameroonian economic operators can register via the official Trade Expo Indonesia platform or contact the Indonesian Embassy in Yaounde.

The expo provides a platform for importers, exporters, manufacturers, investors, and trade institutions to engage. MINPMEESA's announcement encourages businesses to leverage this opportunity to explore potential trade and investment ventures in Indonesia. The event is designed to foster international business relationships and facilitate market access for participating companies.

Why it mattersCameroonian small and medium-sized enterprises are affected by this invitation to explore new markets and potential business partnerships in Indonesia.

Minister Orders Immediate Land Reform Implementation

Cameroon's Minister of State Property, Land Survey, and Land Affairs announced new directives for land governance, impacting consultative commissions and future legislation.

Yaoundé, Cameroon – Minister Henri Eyebe Ayissi on September 1, 2026, launched special sessions on land reform, mandating the immediate application of circular letters 2024-2026 and a new directive on consultative commissions. The minister cited demographic pressure, urbanization, and disputes as reasons for reform, aligning with national development strategies and FAO guidelines. He emphasized the need for updated records of traditional chiefdom attestations and adherence to new commission guidelines.

The reform aims to address the limitations of the 1974 ordinance, which has struggled to cope with current societal changes. Key announcements included the signing of Circular Letter No. 0005, detailing how notables will be appointed to consultative commissions. A rigorous parliamentary review of a future land law project was also promised. The minister stressed strict and immediate application of these new rules, including the submission of all ongoing cases to the updated regulations.

Administrative authorities, traditional leaders, and land sector stakeholders attended the meeting at the Regional Council of Centre. The minister's directives require an annual updated file of third-degree chiefdom attestations and updated commission descent scales. All current land dossiers must be processed under the new rules, with non-compliance potentially leading to sanctions.

Why it mattersLand owners and traditional authorities in Cameroon are directly affected by new regulations governing land ownership, commission appointments, and the processing of land disputes.

Cameroon Treasury Debt Reaches CFA2 Trillion as Growth Slows

Cameroon's outstanding government securities on the CEMAC market reached CFA2.061 trillion by July 2026, marking a significant slowdown in annual growth to 4.1%.

Cameroon's total outstanding Treasury securities on the CEMAC public debt market surpassed CFA2 trillion by the end of July 2026. The stock reached CFA2.061 trillion, an increase of CFA80.4 billion, or 4.1%, from the previous year, according to Bank of Central African States statistics. This represents a sharp deceleration in growth compared to the preceding two years, which saw increases of 23.7% and 29.6% respectively.

The nation held the third-largest stock of government securities within the six-member CEMAC region, accounting for 19.5% of the total CFA10.561 trillion in outstanding securities. Gabon led the regional market with CFA3.424 trillion, followed by Congo with CFA3.041 trillion. Cameroon's debt stock grew by CFA293 billion between July 2023 and July 2024, and by CFA452.2 billion in the year prior to that.

The slowdown in the annual growth rate of Cameroon's outstanding Treasury securities indicates a shift from the rapid expansion observed in recent years. The figures, published by the Bank of Central African States, detail the country's position within the regional debt market and the recent trajectory of its government borrowing.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the slowing growth of Cameroon's treasury debt may signal changes in government borrowing patterns and fiscal policy within the CEMAC region.

Cameroon Launches New Debt Audit Amid CFA288.8 Billion Unpaid Arrears

Finance Minister Louis Paul Motazé has directed government agencies to submit claims for an audit of unstructured debt accumulated between 2020 and 2025.

Cameroon has initiated a fresh audit to identify unpaid government obligations accumulated between 2020 and 2025. This new exercise proceeds despite CFA288.8 billion from a prior review remaining unsettled. The audit aims to quantify the extent of new debt accrued across various government entities over the specified six-year period.

In a directive dated August 19, 2026, Finance Minister Louis Paul Motazé mandated that all government agencies, public bodies, ministries, state universities, public institutions, companies, and local authorities submit their claims for the 2020-2025 period to the Directorate General of Budget by October 31. The review focuses on determining the volume of unstructured debt claimed against these entities since 2020.

The Finance Ministry defines floating debt as unstructured claims presented by third parties against the central government and its associated entities. The total amount can only be definitively established after all claims are identified, supporting documentation is scrutinized, and the figures are validated through a comprehensive audit process.

Why it mattersThis audit affects government agencies and public bodies by requiring them to submit claims for unstructured debt, impacting their financial reporting and potential liabilities.

New EU Ambassador Presents Credentials in Yaoundé

Veronika Boskovic Pohar met with Felix Mbayu to discuss bilateral cooperation and Cameroon's socio-political situation.

Veronika Boskovic Pohar officially began her tenure as the new head of the European Union delegation to Cameroon on September 2, 2026, by presenting her letters of credence to the Ministry of External Relations in Yaoundé. She met with Felix Mbayu, the Minister Delegate for Cooperation with the Commonwealth, to review existing cooperation between Cameroon and the EU. The meeting also provided a platform to discuss the current socio-political climate within Cameroon.

Boskovic Pohar, a career diplomat, succeeds Jean Marc Châtaigner, whose mission concluded at the end of August. She expressed her commitment to working with Cameroonian authorities to achieve her mandate. Her extensive background includes twenty years of experience in security policy, multilateral relations, and EU foreign policy. Prior to her appointment in Cameroon, she served as the head of the EU delegation in Afghanistan from September 1, 2024.

Before her assignment in Afghanistan, Boskovic Pohar held significant roles within the EU's diplomatic corps. Between September 2020 and August 2024, she represented the Political and Security Committee at the EU's permanent representation in Slovenia. She also served as deputy head of the Slovenian mission in France and Morocco. A lawyer by training, the Slovenian diplomat was designated to lead the EU delegation in Cameroon three months prior to presenting her credentials.

Why it mattersThe new EU ambassador's arrival and initial discussions signal a continuation and potential evolution of diplomatic and economic relations between the European Union and Cameroon.
