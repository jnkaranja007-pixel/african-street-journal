Cameroon suspends political party ahead of elections

Minister Paul Atanga Nji suspended the Front for National Salvation of Cameroon for three months due to pre-election unrest.

Cameroon's Minister of Territorial Administration, Paul Atanga Nji, suspended the Front for National Salvation of Cameroon (FSNC) for three months on October 27, 2026. The decision targets the party for actions before, during, and after the October 12, 2026 presidential election, including alleged incitement to violence and public disorder.

The suspension prohibits the FSNC from any national activity, with violations leading to prosecution. Minister Atanga Nji cited the party's alleged call to insurrection, attacks on territorial integrity, and incitement to violence causing property destruction as reasons for the ban. The FSNC's leader, who placed second in the recent presidential election, had previously called for boycotts of upcoming elections.

This move comes as Cameroon prepares for legislative and municipal elections, with the FSNC's suspension potentially hindering its participation. The party risks losing its elected officials and facing difficulties in future presidential bids if it misses these upcoming polls.

Why it mattersThe suspension of the FSNC affects its ability to participate in upcoming legislative and municipal elections.

SanlamAllianz wins CFA485.8 million in vehicle insurance contracts

The Telecommunications Regulatory Agency awarded SanlamAllianz Cameroon Assurances a three-year deal to cover its fleet and technical equipment vehicles.

SanlamAllianz Cameroon Assurances secured two contracts totaling CFA485.8 million to insure vehicles for the Telecommunications Regulatory Agency (ART) over three years. The tender results, announced on October 19, 2026, by ART Director General Philémon Zoo Zame, were awarded under an emergency procedure. The larger contract, worth CFA412.4 million, covers the agency's main vehicle fleet, while a second contract of CFA73.4 million is for vehicles with onboard technical equipment.

These new contracts will run for 36 months, averaging approximately CFA161.9 million annually. This award follows a tender from 10 years prior, where the ART budgeted CFA180 million for its vehicle fleet insurance between 2017 and 2019. An additional CFA30 million was allocated for mobile radio-frequency monitoring stations in that earlier budget.

The total value of the current contracts includes all taxes. The CFA412.4 million portion represents nearly 85% of the total award. The average annual cost is an estimate based on the contract duration and does not dictate payment schedules.

Why it mattersSanlamAllianz Cameroon Assurances will provide insurance for the Telecommunications Regulatory Agency's vehicles.

Cameroon and Nigeria customs officials share anti-smuggling tactics

A five-day program in Yaounde aims to improve border management and combat revenue losses.

Cameroon and Nigeria have started a five-day customs cooperation program in Yaounde to fight cross-border smuggling, financial crime, and revenue losses. The initiative, which began on Monday, October 26, involves officials from Cameroon's Customs Directorate General and a delegation from the Nigeria Customs Service Command and Staff College. The program focuses on sharing operational experience in customs surveillance, cargo inspection, financial intelligence, coordinated border management, and revenue collection.

The training, running until October 30, also covers real-time data sharing between customs administrations, measures to combat money laundering, and efforts to counter terrorism financing. These activities are part of customs operations involving the movement of goods, the detection of illicit trade, the collection of import duties, and the enforcement of trade regulations. The Nigerian delegation includes 13 trainees and four administrators, led by Happiness Ebelle, Assistant Comptroller of the Nigeria Customs Service.

This cooperation aims to strengthen efforts to combat financial crime and revenue losses while improving border management and trade security between the two nations.

Why it mattersCameroon and Nigeria are cooperating to combat cross-border smuggling and financial crime, affecting revenue collection and trade security.

Barry Callebaut names new Cameroon CEO after cocoa purchases drop

Blaise Kengne takes over at SIC Cacaos following a 14% decrease in cocoa bean supplies for the 2025-2026 season.

Blaise Kengne has been appointed CEO of SIC Cacaos and Barry Callebaut Cameroon, the country's largest industrial cocoa processor. Kengne announced his return to Cameroon in October 2026 after leading Barry Callebaut's operations in Nigeria. His arrival coincides with a 14% drop in cocoa bean purchases by SIC Cacaos during the 2025–2026 season.

SIC Cacaos received 40,012 tons of cocoa beans, a decrease of 6,520 tons from the previous year, according to the National Cocoa and Coffee Board. This decline contributed to a 13% overall drop in beans handled by Cameroon's industrial and small-scale processors, who processed 95,946 tons in total. SIC Cacaos still processed 41.8% of the beans supplied to industrial plants.

The slowdown in local cocoa processing affected other companies as well, with Atlantic Cocoa recording a decrease of 12,878 tons in bean supplies. The National Cocoa and Coffee Board reported the figures for the 2025–2026 season.

Why it mattersFarmers in Cameroon are affected by a 14% drop in cocoa bean purchases by the country's largest industrial processor.

Cameroon prisoners praise Maurice Kamto's sacrifices

11 detained Mouvement pour la renaissance du Cameroun members thanked their former leader for his commitment to justice and democracy.

11 detained members of Cameroon's Mouvement pour la renaissance du Cameroun (MRC) have sent a letter from Kondengui central prison in Yaoundé to their former leader, Maurice Kamto. The prisoners, including Alain Fogue and Olivier Bibou Nissack, expressed gratitude for Kamto's continuous engagement and sacrifices for political prisoners since 2019. They also acknowledged his role in strengthening the party and promoting ideals of justice, democracy, and freedom in Cameroon.

The letter, dated October 26, 2026, was sent six weeks after Kamto resigned as party president and 10 days after Tiriane Balbine Noah was elected as his successor. The signatories stated that Kamto's departure is just one phase for the party and that his legacy of activism and citizen awareness must be preserved. They emphasized the need for the party to continue its political engagement with clarity, unity, and determination under new leadership.

Maurice Kamto responded on Facebook, calling the letter "inspired" and a testament to convictions that endure hardship and commitment that remains alive despite sacrifices. He expressed deep gratitude, renewed esteem, and solidarity with the prisoners, assuring them that their convictions and hope remain intact and their commitment to Cameroon continues. The prisoners also pledged to nominate Kamto for the Nobel Peace Prize.

Why it matters11 detained MRC members in Cameroon expressed gratitude to their former leader, Maurice Kamto, for his commitment to justice and democracy.
