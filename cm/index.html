Cameroon to issue CFAF 10.19 trillion in debt by 2031

Most of the borrowing will go toward repaying existing securities, leaving the government with limited room for new spending.

Cameroon plans to issue CFAF 10.19 trillion (about $18 billion) in Treasury bills and bonds between 2027 and 2031, according to projections in the International Monetary Fund's (IMF) 2026 Article IV consultation report, published at the end of March.

The IMF figures show Cameroon will repay CFAF 8.47 trillion in Treasury securities over the same period, equal to 83% of gross issuance by calculations based on the Fund's numbers. The IMF does not publish that ratio itself. After those repayments, the government will have CFAF 1.72 trillion left for new spending.

A decision on a new IMF programme has been pending for more than a year, leaving the government's financing outlook uncertain.

Why it mattersTraders and state budgets relying on regional bond markets will face a government with only CFAF 1.72 trillion for new projects over five years.

Cameroon raises local investment transfers by 10% in 2025

Transfers to regional and local authorities reached CFAF157.8 billion, even as public investment's share of the budget shrank.

Cameroon transferred CFAF157.8 billion in public investment funds to regional and local authorities in 2025, up 10% from the previous year. The rise came even as public investment fell to 24.2% of the total state budget, down from 25.7% a year earlier.

The figures appear in the explanatory statement for the government's 2025 settlement bill, submitted to the National Assembly on November 12, 2026. Transfers under the decentralisation framework rose by about CFAF14.3 billion from an estimated CFAF143.5 billion in 2024.

The increase came during strain on public finances. General budget revenue, excluding financing and cash-flow operations, totalled CFAF5,174.8 billion, CFAF176 billion short of projections. The budget deficit reached CFAF535.2 billion, or 1.6% of GDP, against a projected CFAF309.9 billion, or 0.9% of GDP. The physical execution rate of public investment projects improved to 72.73% in 2025.

Why it mattersLocal authorities receive the transferred funds to carry out projects under the decentralisation framework, while the government must finance a deficit wider than it had projected.

Parlym wins CFAF 11.14 billion LPG contract in Cameroon

The French engineering group's subsidiary will build a storage and distribution hub for the state petroleum company in Douala.

CFAF 11.14 billion is the value of the contract that Parlym Cameroun has won to build a liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) storage and distribution hub in Douala. Signed on October 29, 2026, it was awarded to the Cameroonian subsidiary of French engineering group Parlym, which will build the facility for the state-owned Société camerounaise des dépôts pétroliers (SCDP).

Records from the Public Contracts Regulatory Agency (ARMP) list the deal under reference 029/M/DG/DTEC/SDPEST/SST/CIPM-SCDP/2026. SCDP confirmed the selection at a project kickoff meeting in Douala on November 11, and the sum of CFAF 11,140,906,208, taxes included, covers construction only.

A separate technical assistance contract for overseeing the project, estimated at CFAF 417.375 million including taxes, is not part of that figure. Investir au Cameroun had put the two contracts together at an estimated CFAF 11.65 billion in September, before either was awarded.

Why it mattersThe state petroleum company has awarded the LPG hub construction to a single contractor, with work starting after the November 11 kickoff meeting.

Cameroon hosts African customs intelligence workshop

Yaoundé is hosting a week of geospatial intelligence training for 28 customs participants from 14 countries, opened by Cameroon's customs director general.

Yaoundé is hosting the third customs master-trainer workshop on geospatial intelligence (GEOINT) from 16 to 20 November 2026. Edwin Nuvaga, Cameroon's director general of customs, opened the sessions on Monday 16 November.

The workshop is run by the World Customs Organization and Japan's International Cooperation Agency (JICA), and brings 28 participants from 14 countries together with eight experts from the United States, France, Japan, Uzbekistan and the Netherlands. Cameroon is represented by Captain Hugues Amba Bontsebe and Warrant Officer Caeli Ami Ndula. Nuvaga told the gathering that the customs security mission is now as imperative as its fiscal mission, given terrorism and organised crime.

The sessions will end on Friday with a roadmap for embedding GEOINT in national border security strategies. Earlier editions were held in Nairobi in February and Dakar in May.

Why it mattersCustoms trainers from 14 countries will return home to teach satellite imagery and spatial analysis to border staff, with a roadmap due on Friday.
