Olam Agri to build 300,000-ton poultry feed plant in Cameroon

The agribusiness group confirmed the project on November 5, 2026, with operations set for early 2028.

Olam Agri plans to construct a 300,000-ton poultry feed facility in Cameroon, with operations expected to commence in early 2028. The Singapore-based agribusiness group confirmed this project on November 5, 2026. Details regarding the investment cost, specific plant location, and construction timeline have not yet been released.

This Cameroon project is part of Olam Agri's wider strategy to invest an additional $400 million in food processing and animal feed production across Africa by 2028. The plan includes new facilities in Nigeria and Côte d’Ivoire, alongside an expansion of pasta manufacturing in Ghana. The company states it has already invested approximately $115 million in its West African processing operations since 2024.

Saurabh Mehra, head of Olam Agri's food processing and animal feed businesses, confirmed the project in a Bloomberg interview. The company has not provided a country-by-country breakdown for the $400 million investment, leaving Cameroon's precise share undetermined. The exact meaning of the 300,000-ton figure, whether it represents annual capacity or total output, also remains unspecified.

Why it mattersCameroon's poultry sector will see a new 300,000-ton feed plant, potentially affecting local feed supply and costs.

Cameroon awards CFAF3.95 billion in electricity meter contracts to Chinese firms

Three no-bid contracts for electricity and public lighting metering equipment were signed on June 17 and 19, 2026.

Cameroon has signed four contracts totaling nearly CFAF4.5 billion for electricity meters and public lighting metering equipment. Three of these contracts, valued at CFAF3.95 billion combined, were awarded to Chinese manufacturers, with one working alongside a local partner. These agreements were finalized on June 17 and 19, 2026, under the Cameroon Power Sector Reform Programme (PRSEC) following approval for direct contracting.

The contracts were published on November 4 on the Public Contracts Regulatory Agency (ARMP) website. The largest of the three, worth CFAF2.20 billion, was awarded to a consortium of Wuhan Sanfran Electronics Co Ltd and Future Master Engineering SARL. This contract is for 4,720 smart metering kits intended for public lighting in Yaoundé, Douala, and Bafoussam, representing almost 56% of the total value of these three deals.

Zhejiang Goldcard Power-Tech Co Ltd, a meter manufacturer from Hangzhou, China, received the other two contracts. One of these, valued at CFAF1.04 billion, is for the supply of 13,500 meters.

Why it mattersCameroonian citizens will be affected by the procurement of electricity meters and public lighting equipment through these contracts.

Cameroon gas assets part of US-Russia Ukraine talks

Lukoil's 30% stake in Cameroon's Etinde gas field is a bargaining chip in negotiations to end the Ukraine war.

Cameroon's Etinde gas field has become a point of negotiation between the United States and Russia as part of broader discussions to resolve the conflict in Ukraine. Russian multinational Lukoil, facing U.S. sanctions imposed in October 2025, is reportedly discussing the divestment of its international assets, including its 30% share in the Cameroonian project.

According to a New York Times investigation, Russian President Vladimir Putin has directly raised the issue of these strategic assets, specifically the Etinde stake, in meetings with American envoys. This central African gas prospect is now being used as an economic lever to facilitate a global compromise between Moscow and Washington.

The inclusion of Cameroonian natural resources in the Ukraine crisis discussions raises questions about the nation's economic sovereignty, as operational slowdowns at the Etinde project are linked to international sanctions. Cameroonian authorities have not yet issued an official statement on the ongoing preparations for a deal between Lukoil and Washington, leaving the government with limited room to defend its industrial interests.

Why it mattersCameroon's economic sovereignty and its ability to choose energy partners are impacted by international negotiations over its gas assets.

Cameroon court cancels 800 land titles

The Supreme Court's administrative chamber invalidated a 2005 land title and subsequent derivatives in Japoma-Bakoko.

The Supreme Court of Cameroon has cancelled 800 land titles in the Wouri department, specifically in Japoma-Bakoko. This decision, stemming from ruling n°079/P/FD/2025 by the Administrative Chamber, invalidates a 2005 land title (n°34535/W) and all subsequent derivative titles. The court's action nullifies a prior ruling from the Littoral Administrative Tribunal that had attempted to validate the original title from December 2020.

The Conservation foncière du Wouri « B » stated that copies of the cancelled titles are null and void. This ruling effectively returns the land to state ownership or restores prior legitimate rights, dispossessing current occupants. The case involved the administration and the village community of Japoma-Bakoko, represented by M. Moundi Isaac and others, and had been ongoing for several years.

Hundreds of families and economic operators in Japoma-Bakoko are now facing legal uncertainty. The land registry has published an extensive list of affected title holders, including investors and individuals who believed their properties were secure. This situation highlights ongoing issues with land title security in Cameroon, particularly in the rapidly expanding peripheral areas of Douala.

Why it mattersHundreds of families and economic operators in Japoma-Bakoko are now facing legal uncertainty regarding their property ownership.
