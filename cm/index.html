Cameroon Tax Generates CFA15 Billion, Strains Company Cash Flow

The new General Synthetic Tax collected CFA15 billion for municipalities in the first half of 2026, but businesses face cash flow challenges due to payment rules.

Cameroon's General Synthetic Tax (IGS) has generated nearly CFA15 billion for municipalities in the first half of 2026, representing a 14% increase over the previous year. This figure was disclosed by Roger Athanase Meyong Abath, director general of taxation, during private sector consultations in Douala on August 25. The tax authority noted that businesses are contesting regulations that may delay the availability of some tax payments until the following year, impacting their immediate cash flow.

While the total collected for the 2025 tax year is still being finalized, the reported CFA15 billion for the first half of 2026 indicates a significant rise from the approximately CFA13.16 billion that would have been collected in 2024 based on a 14% increase. An earlier statement from the Ministry of Finance in May indicated that CFA10.35 billion had already been transferred to municipalities for the 2025 tax year, following a CFA6.93 billion payment.

The consultations with the private sector are part of the preparatory work for the 2027 Finance Law. The IGS aims to streamline tax collection, but its implementation has raised concerns among businesses regarding the timing of tax remittances and its effect on operational liquidity. The tax authority has not yet released the final comparable figures for 2024 or the complete amount for 2025.

Why it mattersBusinesses are affected by the new tax rules which generate revenue for municipalities but can tie up company cash flow until the following year.

Cameroon Launches Aviation Fuel Study

A feasibility study will assess the viability of a domestic sustainable aviation fuel industry, potentially creating jobs and utilizing local resources.

Cameroon has officially begun a study to explore the possibility of establishing a local industry for sustainable aviation fuel. The Cameroon Civil Aviation Authority (CCAA) launched the assessment on August 31, 2026, as part of the International Civil Aviation Organization’s ACT-SAF program, with financial backing from the European Union. This initiative aims to determine if Cameroon can produce and use its own aviation fuel, which could lead to job creation and add value to the nation's resources.

The study will thoroughly examine available raw materials within Cameroon, suitable production technologies, and the necessary infrastructure, regulatory frameworks, and investment conditions required for such an industry. The CCAA expects the findings to inform a roadmap that aligns with International Civil Aviation Organization standards. The primary goal is to ascertain the technical, economic, and environmental feasibility of domestic sustainable aviation fuel production.

The launch was marked by a two-day workshop chaired by CCAA Director General Paule Avomo Assoumou Koki. This assessment is funded by the European Union and supported by the International Civil Aviation Organization's Assistance, Capacity-building and Training for Sustainable Aviation Fuels program. The findings will guide the development of a national strategy for sustainable aviation fuel.

Why it mattersCameroonian businesses and the government are affected by the potential development of a new domestic industry for sustainable aviation fuel, which could create jobs and add value to local resources.

Lieutenant-Colonel Cerlin Belinga appointed vice-president of Maroua military court

Presidential decree on August 31, 2026, also names him investigating judge, increasing his responsibilities.

Lieutenant-Colonel Cerlin Belinga has been appointed vice-president and investigating judge of the Maroua military court by presidential decree on August 31, 2026. He was previously the government commissioner at the Yaoundé military court. His move to the Far North region follows his involvement in the investigation of the assassination of radio host Martinez Zogo. The decree also appointed other officials to various military courts across Cameroon, including a new government commissioner and substitute for the Yaoundé military court.

The appointment of Lieutenant-Colonel Belinga to the Maroua court, where he will serve as both vice-president and investigating judge, signifies an expansion of his judicial authority. This comes as the Martinez Zogo case in Yaoundé is at the witness hearing stage. His successor at the Yaoundé military court, Lieutenant-Colonel Éric Atemengue Omgba, will now oversee the ongoing proceedings in that high-profile case.

This presidential decree represents a significant shift within the military judicial system, with several other appointments made to courts in Bamenda, Bertoua, and Ebolowa. The frequent changes in key positions, particularly since the Martinez Zogo investigation began, have led to speculation about potential sanctions, though the administration has not provided specific reasons. The courts, however, continue to function under new leadership.

Why it mattersLieutenant-Colonel Cerlin Belinga is affected by this appointment as his role and jurisdiction have been expanded to the Maroua military court.

Cameroon's Borrowing Costs Reach Record High on CEMAC Debt Market

The nation paid an average interest rate of 6.97% on Treasury bills in July 2026, exceeding the regional average and marking its highest borrowing cost.

Cameroon's short-term borrowing costs on the CEMAC government securities market surged to a new peak in July 2026. The government paid an average interest rate of 6.97% on Treasury bills during the month, according to data from the Bank of Central African States (BEAC). This rate surpassed the regional average of 6.83% and represents the country's highest borrowing cost since the market's inception in 2011.

This increase underscores the rising expense for the Cameroonian government to secure funds through this market, which has been a significant financing source since 2019. BEAC's data indicates that across the CEMAC region, the average rate paid on Treasury bills slightly decreased from 6.99% in July 2025 to 6.83% in July 2026. Cameroon's rate was among the highest in the region, with Equatorial Guinea at 7.87% and Congo at 7.24% paying more.

Other CEMAC countries experienced varying borrowing costs. Chad paid an average rate of 6.91%, the Central African Republic paid 6.83%, and Gabon paid 6.48%. The trend highlights a divergence in borrowing expenses within the Central African Economic and Monetary Community, with Cameroon facing a notably more expensive environment for raising short-term debt.

Why it mattersCameroonian investors and the government face higher costs for short-term borrowing, impacting fiscal operations and potentially future investment.

Socadel Seeks CFA200 Billion to Ease Cash Strain

Cameroon's state power utility signed a mandate with local banks to structure credit facilities aimed at refinancing debt and improving cash flow.

Cameroon's state-owned power utility, Socadel, is seeking CFA200 billion from local banks to improve its cash position and refinance existing debt. This move is part of an effort to alleviate financial pressure impacting payments across the nation's electricity sector. Socadel signed a mandate on August 31 in Yaoundé with General Bank of Cameroon (GBC), Afriland First Bank, and BGFIBank Cameroon to arrange two credit facilities totaling CFA200 billion.

General Bank of Cameroon will lead the syndication process as the lead arranger, with Afriland First Bank and BGFIBank Cameroon serving as co-arrangers. The utility emphasized that the CFA200 billion has not yet been secured, and the mandate initiates the structuring and syndication process. Access to the funds is contingent upon credit approvals, finalization of contractual documents, and other standard conditions.

The proposed financing includes a CFA50 billion revolving credit facility intended to cover daily cash needs and establish an operating liquidity reserve. A second, CFA150 billion medium-term facility is planned to refinance existing debt. This initiative aims to address the financial strain that has been affecting payments within the country's electricity sector.

Why it mattersSocadel is seeking CFA200 billion from local banks to address cash flow issues and refinance debt, which could impact the stability of electricity payments.
