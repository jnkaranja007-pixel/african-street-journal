AfDB approves CFA26.2 billion facility for AFG Bank Cameroon

The African Development Bank will provide a credit line and guarantee facility to boost trade finance for local businesses.

The African Development Bank (AfDB) Group has approved a CFA26.2 billion financing facility for AFG Bank Cameroon. The approval, made on October 21, 2026, aims to increase lending to businesses engaged in international trade. The package includes a CFA19.7 billion credit line and a CFA6.6 billion guarantee facility.

The AfDB's statement on October 21 detailed that the credit line has a three-and-a-half-year term with a three-year grace period. The five-year guarantee facility will allow the AfDB to cover up to 100% of the risk for AFG Bank Cameroon's trade finance transactions. This is intended to encourage foreign banks to confirm letters of credit issued by AFG Bank Cameroon, facilitating payments to suppliers.

The Cameroon Business Forum 2026, which opened in Douala on October 22, 2026, is also focusing on productive transformation and the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA). Prime Minister Joseph Dion Ngute is presiding over the forum, which concludes on October 23, 2026, and aims to make Cameroon a major industrial and commercial hub in Africa.

Why it mattersCameroonian businesses involved in international trade will benefit from increased access to financing and easier supplier payments.

Cameroon launches AgriConnect to boost farming sector

The International Fund for Agricultural Development partnered with Cameroon and the World Bank to accelerate agropastoral transformation.

The International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) today launched the AgriConnect Country Compact with Cameroon and the World Bank Group. This initiative aims to speed up the transformation of the agropastoral and agrifood sectors, improve food security, and create jobs. The plan focuses on strengthening the sector's foundations, reforming policies, and increasing access to finance for producers and businesses.

AgriConnect will target value chains including rice, maize, cassava, oil palm, cocoa, soybean, dairy, and aquaculture. IFAD Country Director Eric Rwabidadi stated IFAD's commitment to supporting youth entrepreneurship, inclusive value chains, rural finance, and climate resilience. Cameroon's agrifood sector currently represents 17 per cent of GDP and employs 4.7 million workers, but faces constraints in access to inputs, infrastructure, technology, markets, and finance.

By 2030, AgriConnect aims to significantly increase production of maize to 6 million tonnes and palm oil to 800,000 tonnes, achieve 55 per cent rice self-sufficiency, meet 60 per cent of national milk demand domestically, and boost fisheries and aquaculture to 450,530 tonnes. The Compact also seeks to reduce the food import bill by 28 per cent, from US$1.6 billion to US$1.15 billion, requiring an estimated US$1.87 billion in investment over 2026–2030.

Why it mattersCameroon's agrifood sector, which accounts for 17 per cent of GDP and 4.7 million jobs, will be transformed to increase production and reduce import bills.

Cameroon supreme court orders review of former minister's graft case

The court unexpectedly postponed the appeal hearing for ex-defence minister Edgar Alain Mebe Ngo'o on October 21, 2026.

Cameroon's Supreme Court on October 21, 2026, postponed the appeal hearing for former Defence Minister Edgar Alain Mebe Ngo'o. The court ordered a review of the case, which had been scheduled for that day. Ngo'o was initially sentenced to 30 years in prison on January 31, 2023, by the Special Criminal Court for embezzling public funds, corruption, and money laundering.

The decision came as a surprise to Ngo'o's legal team, who were prepared to present their defense. The Supreme Court's move to re-examine the case means a return to the procedure and substance of the charges that led to his conviction. His wife, Bernadette Minla'a Nkoulou, and several associates also received prison sentences ranging from 9 to 25 years.

This review opens a new phase in the legal proceedings, potentially altering the outcomes for the former minister and his co-accused. The court's decision, which was not immediately explained, aims to clarify ambiguities surrounding the process that resulted in the initial convictions. Cameroon is also intensifying its fight against money laundering and terrorism financing, having completed 19 actions by October 22, 2026.

Why it mattersFormer minister Edgar Alain Mebe Ngo'o and his associates face a potential modification of their prison sentences following the Supreme Court's decision to review their graft case.

Tusker Minerals deal raises questions in Cameroon

Australian miner Tusker acquired two firms linked to its CEO for rutile exploration in Cameroon.

Australian mining company Tusker Minerals acquired two firms on June 13, 2025, gaining access to rutile exploration projects in Cameroon. The transaction was with Gondwana Capital Pty Ltd, a company where Tusker's CEO, Clifford Fitzhenry, also serves as a director. Fitzhenry was a technical consultant for the company, then known as DY6 Metals, before becoming CEO on July 14, 2025.

The deal involved Tusker acquiring 100% of Aardvark Minerals Pty Ltd and EKOM Metals Pty Ltd, which control Rhino Resources Ltd and Gorilla Mining Ltd in Cameroon respectively. Shareholders approved the acquisition on June 11, 2025. Through Rhino Resources, Tusker gained rights to the Douala Basin project, covering 2,580 square kilometers.

The Douala Basin project includes three granted exploration permits and three pending permit applications. Tusker Minerals was formerly known as DY6 Metals. The company's annual report for the financial year ended June 30, 2026, details Fitzhenry's directorship at Gondwana Capital.

Why it mattersThe acquisition of mining rights by Tusker Minerals, a company linked to its CEO, affects Cameroon's resource management and potential revenue.

CEMAC and Interpol agree border security cooperation

Cameroon hosts talks where CEMAC and Interpol leaders discuss fighting cross-border crime and upgrading border posts.

The President of the CEMAC Commission, Baltasar Engonga Edjo'o, and Interpol President Lucas Philippe met in Yaoundé to strengthen cooperation against cross-border crime. The meeting, held during the 6th session of the Central African Police Chiefs Council, focused on enhancing border security and facilitating free movement within the CEMAC region.

Discussions included the strategic importance of free movement and the priority financing of infrastructure like juxtaposed border posts. Edjo'o specifically requested Interpol's support in equipping borders with biometric passport readers to balance fluidity with security, addressing concerns about trafficking, terrorism, and organized crime fueled by porous borders.

CEMAC requested Interpol's technical assistance, access to its databases, and capacity building for border police in its six member states. This cooperation aims to bolster security and ensure smoother intra-community exchanges.

Why it mattersCameroon is hosting discussions between CEMAC and Interpol to improve border security and combat cross-border crime.
