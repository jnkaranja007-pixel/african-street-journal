Cameroon governor calls for Metafrique Steel shutdown after fire kills four

Littoral Governor Samuel Dieudonné Ivaha Diboua cited safety failures following a November 2 explosion at the Douala plant.

A fire at Metafrique Steel's factory in Douala's Bassa industrial zone killed four people and seriously injured 16 on November 2, 2026. Littoral Governor Samuel Dieudonné Ivaha Diboua called for the company to halt operations, citing a failure to implement safety measures ordered after previous fires. An investigation into the incident has been announced.

The explosion preceded the fire, which broke out on the morning of November 2. Firefighters found four badly burned bodies inside the facility. Another 16 employees suffered severe burns and were taken to Douala General Hospital, where their conditions remain a concern. Metafrique Steel, a subsidiary of Indian group Metafrique, specializes in producing rebar and steel from recycled ferrous waste and employs around 500 people.

This is not the first incident at the plant; a fire on May 1, 2026, injured nine Cameroonians and one Indian, leading Metafrique Steel to suspend production. In April 2022, a partial fire damaged the raw materials stock. The company, present in Cameroon for about 16 years, stated the incident's origin was external and causes are yet to be determined.

Why it mattersFour people died and 16 were seriously injured in a fire at Metafrique Steel's factory in Douala.

Cameroonian investors boost government debt holdings

Local investors increased their stake in Cameroonian Treasury securities by CFA132.8 billion in September 2026.

Investors based in Cameroon increased their holdings of Cameroonian Treasury securities by CFA132.8 billion in September 2026. This rise brought their total holdings to CFA1,855.3 billion, up from CFA1,722.5 billion the previous month. This increase represented 94% of the total monthly growth in their holdings across the six CEMAC countries.

The Bank of Central African States (BEAC) released data on November 2, 2026, showing that Cameroonian investors now hold 84.9% of the country's Treasury securities. This share grew from 83.4% at the end of August. The total outstanding value of Cameroon's debt securities rose by CFA118.7 billion to CFA2,184.4 billion during the same period.

Holdings of Cameroonian securities by investors outside the country, including the BEAC, decreased by CFA14.1 billion. The growth in locally held securities surpassed the overall increase in Cameroon's total outstanding debt.

Why it mattersCameroonian investors now hold a larger portion of the nation's debt, increasing their stake by CFA132.8 billion in September 2026.

Cameroon Agribio partners Turkish firm for 4,000-hectare farm

The agro-industrial complex in Yoko will combine soybean cultivation with oil processing and poultry production.

Cameroon's Agribio has partnered with Turkey's NuNu Export to build an agro-industrial complex in Yoko, Central Region. The project will cultivate soybeans on 4,000 hectares and include a plant to process 60 tons of soybeans daily. A 1,000-hectare poultry farm with 48 houses for chicken and egg production is also planned.

This integrated complex aims to produce local vegetable oil and animal feed, supporting Cameroon's goal to reduce import reliance. Developers anticipate over 550 permanent jobs once operational. The project connects farming, industrial processing, and poultry within one operation.

A 2.5-megawatt-peak hybrid solar power plant will help supply electricity to the site. The processing plant will yield vegetable oil and soybean meal for animal feed, including for the complex's own poultry farms.

Why it mattersCameroonian farmers will benefit from a new integrated agricultural project that aims to boost local production and create jobs.

Journalists face 86% impunity rate

UNESCO reports global impunity for journalist murders rose to 86% in 2026, reversing a downward trend.

Globally, six out of seven murdered journalists see no justice, with the UNESCO reporting an 86% impunity rate in 2026. This figure marks an increase from previous years, reversing a decline that began in 2012 when the rate was 95%.

The report criticizes governments for inaction and complicity, stating that excuses like conflict or terrorism are used to avoid accountability. It highlights that when journalists are killed, it silences whistleblowers and terrorizes newsrooms, ultimately blinding entire populations.

The UNESCO data indicates that impunity for journalist murders is a deliberate policy, sending a message to others to remain silent. This failure of justice is presented as a direct reflection of political courage and the state of the rule of law.

Why it mattersJournalists in Cameroon and globally face a high risk of murder with no perpetrators brought to justice, impacting freedom of expression.

Politics · 2026-11-04

Cameroon party president stands by election boycott

Issa Tchiroma Bakary reaffirms National Salvation Front's decision not to contest polls despite party suspension.

Issa Tchiroma Bakary, president of Cameroon's National Salvation Front (FSNC), confirmed on November 2, 2026, that his party will not participate in the upcoming legislative and municipal elections. This decision was made despite a three-month suspension of the FSNC by Territorial Administration Minister Paul Atanga Nji.

The FSNC stated that the minimum conditions for democratic elections were not met, and participation would legitimize a government whose presidential election victory Tchiroma disputes. The party also cited the deaths during post-election unrest and the detention of individuals as reasons for their stance. Elh Moussa Firissou, acting secretary-general of the party's central committee, described the boycott as an "unchangeable political position."

The party's boycott was first announced on February 6, 2026, by the FSNC's central committee in a statement signed by Firissou. The FSNC argued that the minimum conditions for democratic, transparent, and peaceful elections were not in place.

Why it mattersCameroonian citizens who are members of the National Salvation Front will not have their party represented in the upcoming legislative and municipal elections.
