Neptune Group opens CFA6 billion Maithé Mall in Obala

Cameroon’s Neptune Group has entered the large-scale retail market with its first Maithé Mall, a CFA6 billion investment in Obala, near Yaoundé.

The Neptune Group, known for fuel distribution, diversified its operations by opening the Maithé Mall on August twenty-sixth. Trade Minister Luc Magloire Mbarga Atangana inaugurated the shopping center, which represents a CFA6 billion investment by the conglomerate founded by Cameroonian businessman Antoine Ndzengué.

Minister Mbarga Atangana stated that the new mall reflects the dynamism of Cameroon’s economy and retail sector, highlighting the increasing role of domestic operators in large-scale distribution. He also praised the decision to locate the project in Obala, noting it extends modern retail beyond major cities into secondary urban centers.

Antoine Ndzengué indicated the investment addresses a local need, as residents previously traveled long distances for certain products and services. The mall is the first large-scale distribution center in Obala, in the Lékié region.

Why it mattersResidents of Obala and surrounding areas now have access to a large-scale retail center, reducing the need to travel for products and services.

MRC faces administrative challenge ahead of 2027 elections

The Minister of Territorial Administration has questioned recent actions by the Movement for the Rebirth of Cameroon (MRC), including the return of Maurice Kamto as party leader.

The Movement for the Rebirth of Cameroon (MRC) is navigating a critical period as the Minister of Territorial Administration has challenged its recent decisions. This administrative scrutiny, reported on August twenty-seventh, two thousand twenty-six, comes as the party prepares for legislative and municipal elections scheduled for two thousand twenty-seven.

The party, which boycotted elections for about five years, aims to re-engage in national and local power struggles. However, the decision to return Maurice Kamto to the party's leadership, following an internal crisis and purges of dissenting members, is now under official review. This situation creates uncertainty about the party's stability and its ability to field candidates.

The MRC also faces difficulties in presenting a presidential candidate for two thousand twenty-five due to a lack of elected officials and the challenge of gathering three hundred signatures from elected officials, first-degree traditional chiefs, or members of consular chambers. The party claims these administrative actions are attempts to destabilize it ahead of upcoming elections, according to Actu Cameroun.

Why it mattersThe political party MRC and its members are directly affected by administrative challenges that could prevent their participation in upcoming elections.

Cameroon Revives Phone-Blocking Plan to Recover CFA25 Billion in Annual Customs Revenue

Cameroon will begin blocking undeclared mobile devices from local telecom networks on September 1, aiming to boost annual customs revenue by CFA25 billion.

Cameroon is set to implement a plan to block undeclared mobile phones, tablets, and other connected devices from its telecom networks starting September 1. This measure, announced by Finance Minister Louis Paul Motazé on August 20, comes three months after an initial enforcement deadline was missed.

The government expects this customs overhaul to generate CFA25 billion in annual revenue from mobile device imports. The restrictions will apply to devices that entered Cameroon without customs clearance after April 1, 2026, under a collection system established in the 2023 Finance Law and formally introduced in March 2026.

The blocking will affect undeclared devices connecting to a local telecom network for the first time, as well as phones activated after April 1 whose owners received warnings but did not comply. Devices activated in batches using the same SIM card after April 1 and no longer in continuous use are also included in the new policy.

Why it mattersConsumers who own mobile devices imported without proper customs clearance will find their devices blocked from local networks, impacting their ability to communicate.

UN Coordinator advocates pastoral mobility for climate adaptation in Cameroon

Issa Sanogo, the UN Resident Coordinator in Cameroon, urged for the preservation and facilitation of pastoral mobility as a critical climate adaptation strategy during the COP17.

During the COP17's Land and Peoples Day on August twenty-sixth, Issa Sanogo, the UN Resident Coordinator in Cameroon, highlighted pastoral mobility as a crucial climate adaptation strategy. He emphasized that this mobility should be facilitated, not restricted, to address desertification and strengthen community resilience.

Sanogo, speaking at a high-level roundtable on “Pastoralism for Resilience and Peace” organized by the IOM, called for a shift in perspective on pastoral mobility. He described pastoralism as a comprehensive socio-cultural and ecological system that secures sustainable livelihoods.

Drawing on Cameroon's experience with land pressure leading to tensions, Sanogo identified four key actions: securing access to pastoral lands and transhumance corridors, strengthening policy frameworks, improving data collection and early warning systems, and consolidating cross-border cooperation. These measures are deemed essential to prevent resource-related conflicts.

Why it mattersPastoral communities and those affected by land-use conflicts will see how international bodies are advocating for policy changes that directly impact their livelihoods and safety.

Cameroon boosts security for 2026 school and university re-entry

The Cameroonian government is increasing security measures nationwide ahead of the September 7, 2026 school re-entry and October university re-entry.

A special evaluation meeting on August 26, 2026, chaired by Minister Delegate to the Presidency in charge of Defense, Joseph Beti Assomo, addressed security concerns across the country. The meeting, held under high instructions from President Paul Biya, included key security and defense officials, such as the Minister of Territorial Administration and the Secretary of State for the National Gendarmerie.

While the overall security situation is deemed under control, specific areas of concern were highlighted. These include terrorist incursions in the Far North, persistent "dead city" orders in parts of the Northwest and Southwest, hostage-taking for ransom in the Septentrion region, and the security of borders, mining activities, and the maritime facade.

Authorities also discussed urban crime, specifically infanticides and femicides. In response to these challenges, the Minister of Defense ordered intensified security operations and improved coordination with administrative authorities to ensure a secure environment for students, teachers, and educational staff nationwide.

Why it mattersStudents, teachers, and educational staff across Cameroon will experience a heightened security presence during their return to school and university.
