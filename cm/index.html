Cameroon targets CFA1.12 trillion for farm investment

The AgriConnect pact aims to cut food imports by 28% and boost production of corn, palm oil, rice, milk, and fish by 2030.

Cameroon launched the AgriConnect pact in Yaoundé on October 21, 2026, to attract investment into eight agricultural sectors. The initiative requires nearly CFA1.12 trillion ($1.87 billion) for 24 interventions planned between 2026 and 2030. The goal is to reduce the country's food import bill by 28% by 2030, from approximately $1.6 billion to $1.15 billion.

The pact, supported by the World Bank Group and the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD), aims to increase domestic production and develop local food processing. It focuses on corn, rice, cassava, palm oil, cocoa, soybeans, milk, and fisheries. By 2030, Cameroon aims for 6 million tons of corn and 800,000 tons of palm oil annually, with domestic rice covering 55% of demand and milk meeting 60% of consumption.

IFAD and the World Bank are partners in the action framework designed to accelerate sector transformation and strengthen food security. The program also seeks to create jobs, particularly for young people and women, and mobilize public and private investment.

Why it mattersCameroonian farmers and consumers will be affected by increased domestic production and reduced food import costs.

Globeleq Cameroon power plants profit drops 24%

Combined profits fell to CFA15 billion in 2025 despite a 5.9% revenue increase to CFA82.6 billion.

Globeleq's two power plants in Cameroon reported combined profits of CFA15 billion in 2025, a 24.2% decrease from CFA19.8 billion the previous year.

Despite the profit drop, total revenue for the plants rose 5.9% to CFA82.6 billion in 2025, up from CFA78 billion in 2024. The Kribi gas plant saw profits fall by more than half to CFA6 billion, while Dibamba's thermal plant profit rose to nearly CFA9 billion.

Both plants continue to face cash flow issues, with unpaid electricity bills causing some generating units to go offline and reducing cash distributions.

Cameroon power line loses 44.7% of electricity

The Logbaba–Bassa 1 transmission line in Douala lost nearly nine times the acceptable electricity threshold in November 2025.

Cameroon's Electricity Sector Regulatory Agency (ARSEL) recorded a 44.7% active power loss rate on the 90-kilovolt Logbaba–Bassa 1 transmission line in Douala in November 2025. This amounted to 22,326 megawatt-hours (MWh) of lost electricity, far exceeding the regulator's 5% acceptable technical threshold.

ARSEL has recommended audits to investigate the high losses on the 4.131-kilometer line, suggesting potential issues with infrastructure or measurement accuracy. In May 2025, the same line had already reported 10,258 MWh in losses, a 14.7% loss rate.

Other transmission lines also show significant losses, with the Garoua–Guider line experiencing rates between 8.2% and 18.9% over six months. The National Electricity Transmission Company (Sonatrel) published an infrastructure inventory for the affected line in March 2025.

Why it mattersCameroon's electricity consumers and businesses are affected by significant power losses on transmission lines.

Cameroon nears 80% completion of anti-money laundering plan

The nation has completed 19 of 24 actions to exit GAFI's enhanced surveillance and restore investor trust.

Cameroon has completed 19 of 24 actions to combat money laundering and terrorism financing, raising its implementation rate from 46% to nearly 80%. The nation aims to finalize the remaining five measures to exit enhanced surveillance by the Financial Action Task Force (GAFI). This progress was reported at a meeting in Yaoundé on October 22, 2026, chaired by Minister Delegate Yaouba Abdoulaye.

The economic and reputational stakes are high, as being on GAFI's grey list increases financing costs and slows financial flows. To exit this list, administrations must complete the final actions and reinforce controls in the non-financial sector.

This acceleration of reforms is intended to secure the national financial system and enhance the country's attractiveness to economic partners. The government seeks to reassure investors and improve the country's financial reputation.

Why it mattersCameroon's efforts to exit GAFI's enhanced surveillance aim to restore investor confidence and improve the country's financial standing.

Citibank executive Ella Martin chairs AmCham Cameroon

The American Chamber of Commerce in Cameroon appointed Ella Martin, a Citibank executive, as its new board chair.

Citibank executive Ella Martin has been appointed the new board chair of the American Chamber of Commerce in Cameroon (AmCham Cameroon). She succeeds Laure E. Djoukam, head of Shekina Invest, who will continue as a director on the board. The chamber's new leadership team also includes Leah Malla Rackovsky as vice chair and Émilie Siewe as treasurer, with expertise in banking and financial technology.

The board features seven additional directors, including Hélène Dengoue, Richard Fowang, Laure E. Djoukam, Sarada Nya, Alain Nono, John Phipps, and Salamatou Bantse. These directors represent companies involved in sectors such as oil and gas, telecommunications, consumer goods, and legal services. AmCham stated its new leadership will focus on enhancing trade and investment between Cameroon and the United States.

The organization aims to improve services for its member companies. Rackovsky, founder of Azamra Financial Technologies, previously led AmCham's Emerging Technologies Committee. Siewe will now oversee the chamber's financial operations.

Why it mattersCameroon's trade and investment with the United States will be a focus for the new leadership of AmCham Cameroon.
