MRC asks Cameroon officials to clear election-candidate paperwork

The opposition party says thousands of its members face delays, refusals or demands for payment as they prepare for the 7 February 2027 vote.

Tiriane Balbine Noah, president of Cameroon’s Movement for the Renaissance of Cameroon (MRC), said on 17 November that thousands of its members seeking to contest the 7 February 2027 legislative and municipal elections face obstacles assembling their candidacy files. She said officials have refused or delayed some legally required documents, while some candidates have been asked to pay to legalise papers that should be free.

Noah accused administrative and municipal authorities and other officials of trying to discourage MRC candidates. The party called on Elections Cameroon (Elecam) and the ministers responsible for defence, territorial administration and decentralisation to ensure documents are issued promptly and extracts are provided free. It also asked authorities to extend the filing deadline for candidates refused documents.

The MRC urged affected candidates to document refusals for possible legal action. Noah said the party plans to field candidates nationwide and asked the ruling Cameroon People’s Democratic Movement to compete fairly. The filing deadline was approaching, the report said, without specifying its date.

Why it mattersCandidates refused required papers are being asked to document the denials for possible legal action, while the MRC wants the filing deadline extended for them.

COSUMAF reports Cameroon’s fund-investment share at 3.463%

The regulator’s June figure is down from 20.8% in February, but its reports contain inconsistencies and do not establish that funds sold Cameroonian debt.

Cameroonian government securities made up 3.463% of investments held by collective investment funds in the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC) on June 30, 2026, according to the Central African Financial Market Supervisory Commission (COSUMAF). Its February bulletin put the share at 20.8%.

The figures do not establish that funds reduced their holdings of Cameroonian debt, Business in Cameroon reported. COSUMAF’s February bulletin valued those holdings at CFA249.38 billion, but its first-half report, published November 14, gave no corresponding value for June.

A comparison of the regulator’s publications found a discrepancy of nearly CFA71 billion between two tables in the latest report. Gabon’s share rose from 39.21% to 39.719%, while the Republic of Congo’s increased from 19.32% to 19.930%.

Why it mattersFunds and holders of Cameroonian government debt are assessing conflicting regulator figures, including a nearly CFA71 billion discrepancy between tables in the latest report.

Cameroon allocates CFAF 550 million to 30 student businesses and 50 incubators

Of the 2026 grants, CFAF 350 million goes to ventures at higher education institutions, and nearly two-thirds of student funding backs food processing and agro-industry.

Two notices released on November 13 by the Ministry of Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises, Social Economy and Handicrafts (MINPMEESA) set out how Cameroon will spend CFAF 550 million in grants this year, split between 30 student-led businesses and 50 private business incubators. The money pays capital to chosen student ventures and operating support to accredited incubators.

A selection committee chose 30 projects from 64 applications reviewed between November 9 and 11, an approval rate of about 47%. Grants per project run from CFAF 2.5 million to CFAF 41.75 million, according to a joint notice from the SME ministry and the Ministry of Higher Education.

Each of the 50 accredited private incubators will receive CFAF 4 million from the remaining CFAF 200 million. Nearly two-thirds of student-venture funding goes to food processing and agro-industrial projects.

Why it mattersStudent founders whose projects are chosen receive between CFAF 2.5 million and CFAF 41.75 million each, while each of the 50 private incubators gets CFAF 4 million.

Business · 2026-11-18

Aeternum signs $1.91 million in funding deals for Cameroon project developer

The eight agreements relate to its planned acquisition of American Renaissance Minerals, which is involved in the Nkamouna project in eastern Cameroon.

U.S.-based Aeternum Resources signed eight financing agreements worth $1.91 million, about CFA1.14 billion, after September 30, as it continues efforts to acquire American Renaissance Minerals, a company involved in developing the Nkamouna nickel-cobalt-manganese project in eastern Cameroon. The acquisition remains unfinished.

Aeternum disclosed the agreements in quarterly financial statements filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on November 16. The company said it had received none of the money when it prepared the statements, and will record the financing only after funds arrive.

The agreements use Simple Agreements for Future Equity, which allow investors to receive shares later. Each has a $1.60-per-share conversion price cap following Aeternum’s October 1 reverse stock split; none carries interest or a maturity date. The $1.14 billion CFA figure is a contractual commitment, not cash available to Aeternum.

Why it mattersAeternum has yet to receive any of the committed financing, while its planned acquisition of the developer of Cameroon’s Nkamouna project remains unfinished.

Tech · 2026-11-18

Cameroonian engineer builds solar-powered clay cooler

Didier Dinamou’s prototype uses porous clay, water and a photovoltaic panel, and its designer says it can cool a room to 25 degrees in 48-degree heat.

Cameroonian engineer Didier Dinamou built a clay air conditioner as his final project at the National Advanced School of Engineering of the University of Maroua. The device uses porous clay, water and a photovoltaic panel to cool a room through evaporation.

A solar panel powers a pump that moves water through tubes, while a fan pushes room air across the wet surfaces. The machine works without a grid connection, compressor or refrigerant gas, and includes a USB charging port and a light. Dinamou told Sciences et Vie it can cool a room to 25 degrees when outdoor temperatures reach 48 degrees.

The system can cost 90 000 CFA francs and received a mark of 18,5/20 for Dinamou’s final project. It won first place among 427 entries in the EDF Pulse Africa competition in March 2024. The article says the device could be used in health centres and is suited to dry Sahelian areas.

Why it mattersHealth centres and residents in dry Sahelian areas are the stated users; the system can cost 90 000 CFA francs.
