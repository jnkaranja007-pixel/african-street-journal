Cameroon Pharmaceutical Production Rises, Still Fails to Meet Demand

The nation has nearly tripled its pharmaceutical production units since 2020, yet local output covers less than five percent of national consumption needs.

Cameroon has expanded its pharmaceutical manufacturing capacity significantly, growing from seven production units in 2020 to 19 by 2026. Despite this expansion, locally produced medicines fulfill under five percent of the country's demand, according to Public Health Minister Dr. Manaouda Malachie. The 239 approved pharmaceutical products available nationally represent only about fifteen percent of the Essential Medicines List.

These figures were presented following a ministerial dialogue in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, held on the sidelines of the World Health Organization Regional Committee for Africa meeting. African health ministers and stakeholders discussed strengthening pharmaceutical regulation and regional production. Minister Malachie highlighted ongoing challenges for Cameroonian manufacturers, including limited access to finance, inconsistent energy supply, difficulties in sourcing raw materials, and competition from imported goods.

To address these issues, the government has implemented measures such as customs and tax exemptions for specific inputs and equipment, and has introduced preferential treatment for local products. These initiatives aim to support the domestic pharmaceutical sector, though significant gaps remain in meeting the country's overall medicine requirements and covering a broader range of essential medications.

Why it mattersLocal pharmaceutical manufacturers are affected by the limited access to financing and raw materials, hindering their ability to meet national demand for medicines.

Cameroon Economy Grows 3.5% Amid CFAF2.1trn Trade Deficit

The nation's trade deficit widened as imports exceeded exports, despite a 3.8% rise in non-oil economic activities.

Cameroon's economy expanded by 3.5% in 2025, driven by a 4.3% growth in the services sector, according to a government economic report. Non-oil activities, the primary engine of this expansion, rose by 3.8%. The nation's trade deficit reached CFAF2.098 trillion as imports continued to outpace exports, signaling a challenging international economic environment.

The findings were presented in the nearly 200-page "Apport Annuel sur la Situation Économique du Cameroun (Édition 2025)" by Economy, Planning and Regional Development Minister Alamine Ousmane Mey. The report details the country's macroeconomic standing, competitiveness, investment climate, public policy execution, and vulnerability to external economic shocks.

Inflation eased to 3.4% in 2025 from 4.5% the previous year, indicating a slower general increase in consumer prices. However, the report noted a rise in food prices during the same period. The Ministry of Economy, Planning and Regional Development (MINEPAT) confirmed the 3.5% real gross domestic product growth for 2025.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the widening trade deficit and the resilience of non-oil economic growth, which indicates potential market stability.

UNFPA Approves 50 Million USD Program for Cameroon

The United Nations Population Fund's ninth country program, set to run from 2027 to 2031, focuses on population well-being and demographic dividends.

The United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) has approved its ninth Country Cooperation Program with Cameroon, allocating 50 million USD for the period 2027-2031. This program, validated on August 27, 2026, in New York, aligns with Cameroon's National Development Strategy (SND30) and the UN Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF). The initiative aims to enhance population well-being and leverage demographic dividends, building on a partnership that began in 1969.

The program's approval signifies official government endorsement, with Cameroon's Permanent Mission in New York expressing satisfaction with its alignment to national and UN frameworks. The initiative is structured around three strategic pillars: improving reproductive healthcare and reducing maternal deaths, promoting gender equality and eradicating gender-based violence, and utilizing demographic data for informed public policies. Several government ministries, including MINEPAT, MINREX, MINSANTE, MINPROFF, MINJEC, and MINFI, are involved in its implementation.

To support the program's expansion and the UNFPA's operations, the government of Cameroon is providing a new, modern headquarters in Yaoundé. This move underscores Cameroon's commitment to the partnership and its role as a key player in advancing the program's objectives. The joint mobilization of resources and expertise from various ministries highlights a coordinated national effort to achieve the program's goals by 2031.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the 50 million USD program which signifies a significant financial commitment and potential for development projects in Cameroon.

Cameroonian Parties Demand Action Against Rising Feminicides

Multiple political parties are uniting to demand stricter penalties and increased protection for women following a surge in killings.

Political parties in Cameroon are forming a united front to address the escalating issue of feminicides, with over sixty women reportedly killed in the past eight months. Groups including the Mouvement patriotiques pour la prospérité du peuple (MP3), Mouvement pour la renaissance du Cameroun (MRC), Parti camerounais pour la réconciliation nationale (PCRN), and the Social Democratic Front (SDF) are calling for exemplary sanctions against perpetrators and greater state protection for women.

The MP3 advocates for severe punishment for aggressors and educational initiatives to prevent future violence, criticizing the state's perceived inaction and the trivialization of violence against women. The MRC condemns the "silent hemorrhage" of domestic feminicides and urges authorities to prioritize the issue, while the PCRN emphasizes the need for "repression that instills fear" to deter such acts. Deputy Cabral Libii stressed that no grievance justifies violence or murder.

The SDF has highlighted its past submissions of draft laws to the National Assembly aimed at combating feminicides and violence against women and children. However, the party notes that the ruling Rassemblement démocratique du peuple camerounais, which holds a parliamentary majority, has remained silent on these proposals, hindering their potential implementation.

Why it mattersWomen are directly affected by the rising number of feminicides and the political parties' calls for action and protection.

US, Cameroon Advance CFAF 4trn Business Pipeline

American companies are pursuing nearly $7 billion in trade and investment opportunities across multiple sectors in Cameroon, according to the US Embassy.

American companies are pursuing nearly $7 billion, or about CFAF 4 trillion, in trade and investment opportunities in Cameroon, spanning energy, mining, transport infrastructure, technology, forestry and logistics. The portfolio was discussed during the inaugural US-Cameroon Bilateral Economic and Commercial Dialogue, held in Yaounde on August 27. The projects are at various stages of development and include a proposed hydropower plant valued at about $3 billion, a proposed $2 billion airport project and mining ventures worth at least $585 million.

The dialogue established a formal platform through which the two countries will address obstacles to investment and pursue shared economic priorities, according to the US Embassy in Yaounde. Joint working groups will be created to sustain the process, with follow-up talks expected in the United States in 2027. Cameroon’s Minister of the Economy, Planning and Regional Development, Alamine Ousmane Mey, led the Cameroonian side.

Deputy Assistant Secretary of State Sarah Troutman headed the American delegation, which also included US Chargé d’Affaires ad interim. The discussions aimed to foster greater economic ties and facilitate the realization of these significant investment projects.
