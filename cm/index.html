Chinese firm considers CFA88 billion sugar and rice investment

Nanning Agricultural Development Group seeks feasibility study for plantations and a processing complex in Cameroon's East Region.

Chinese state-owned company Nanning Agricultural Development Group is considering investing CFA88 billion in sugar and rice production in Batouri, Cameroon.

The proposed project includes large-scale plantations and a factory designed to produce 60,000 tonnes of sugar annually, but remains at the feasibility assessment stage.

The company has allocated CFA78 million for a feasibility study, with bids due by November 5, 2026, to assess land-use, investment needs, and administrative procedures.

Cameroon seeks markets for 135 refugee artisans

UNHCR and MINPMEESA partnership explores wider markets for refugee artisans in the East Region.

Over 135 Central African refugees in Cameroon's East Region have received or are undergoing vocational and business training. The Ministry of Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises, Social Economy and Handicrafts (MINPMEESA) reported this on October 29, under a 2024–2026 partnership with the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR). Training includes cassava processing, soap making, traditional dyeing, tailoring, beadwork, and business management, implemented through the Bertoua Regional Craft Village (VARB).

10 refugees have access to a permanent exhibition and sales outlet to market their products daily. Participating artisans attended the Cameroon International Handicrafts Exhibition (SIARC) in 2024 and 2026, with one craftswoman winning the national Art and Decoration award at the 2026 edition. UNHCR's Bertoua office and the University of Bertoua organised a dialogue on October 26 and 27.

VARB coordinator Alexis Carole Ngassomo Emama Miasse proposed organising artisans by trade to increase production capacity and fulfil orders. UNHCR is exploring ways to help beneficiaries organise production and reach wider commercial markets, including e-commerce training and digital marketing.

Why it mattersRefugee artisans in Cameroon's East Region are gaining vocational skills and access to markets for their crafts.

Cameroon secures €87 million for cocoa and coffee

The Green Climate Fund approved financing for a six-year program to boost agricultural output across five regions.

Cameroon will receive €87 million from the Green Climate Fund (GCF) to develop its cocoa and coffee sectors. The GCF Board approved the financing on October 27, 2026, as part of a €215.9 million program called Resilient Agriculture for Climate, Investment, Nature and Equity (RACINE). Cameroon's Ministry of Economy, Planning and Regional Development (MINEPAT) was accredited to oversee the project.

The RACINE program will run for six years and cover the Centre, South, East, Littoral, and South-West regions, with the Cocoa and Coffee Development Fund (FODECC) as the executing agency. Cameroon must contribute €88.5 million from export levies, and 90% of the GCF's €87 million, which is €78.3 million, will be provided as loans, with only €8.7 million as a grant.

The financing aims to significantly increase agricultural output, following a decline in cocoa sales. An additional €40.4 million in grants will come from the Central African Forest Initiative (CAFI) tied to results.

Why it mattersCameroonian farmers and the national economy will be affected by the loan and grant funding aimed at increasing cocoa and coffee production.

Sports betting models show varying predictions

Two models can offer different win probabilities for the same match, with differences explained by calculation methods and data used.

Sports betting models can present significantly different win probabilities for the same match, with one model giving a team 55% chance of victory while another shows only 42%. These discrepancies do not necessarily indicate a faulty model, but can stem from varied calculation methods, data inputs, and update timings.

The reliability of a prediction depends on the input data, ranging from simple past results to more complex metrics like shots, chance quality, team lineups, and expected goals (xG). The xG indicator, for example, measures the probability of a shot resulting in a goal based on the chance's characteristics, with Opta's model using nearly one million historical shots.

A trustworthy model's performance should be verifiable against past matches using pre-game probabilities, with evaluations gaining relevance over hundreds of games. The publication date of a probability is crucial, distinguishing pre-match estimates from those recalculated after the final whistle and definitive statistics.

Why it mattersCameroonian sports bettors may encounter differing odds for the same match due to variations in prediction models.

Cameroon plans 2027 public investment rise but misses budget target

The government acknowledges it will not meet its goal of allocating 40% of the general budget to investment next year.

Cameroon's public investment budget will increase in 2027, but it will not reach the government's target of 40% of the general budget. The Cabinet acknowledged this gap in a statement after the October 27, 2026 meeting in Yaoundé, chaired by Prime Minister Joseph Dion Ngute. The statement promised a "significant increase" in public investment for next year without providing specific figures.

This announcement follows an October 12 presidential circular that set a minimum investment allocation of 30% of the general budget for 2027. The Cabinet statement did not clarify if this lower threshold would be met. In 2026, Cameroon's Public Investment Budget (PIB) is CFA2,031.15 billion out of a CFA8,683.9 billion general budget, representing 23.4% of the budget.

The 2026 allocation is 6.6 percentage points below the minimum target set for 2027. These figures reflect budgeted amounts, not actual spending. The National Development Strategy 2020–2030 aims for the higher investment percentage.

Why it mattersCameroon's government plans to increase public investment in 2027, but it will fall short of the National Development Strategy's goal.
