Cameroon power link to Chad costs CFA145 billion

Revised estimate covers 426km of transmission lines and substations for electricity exports.

Cameroon's section of the electricity interconnection with Chad will cost an estimated CFA145.2 billion for its first phase. This revised figure follows an October 12-16, 2026, assessment mission by the African Development Bank (AfDB) to review the project's restructuring. The works include 426 kilometers of high-voltage transmission lines and substations.

The Cameroonian team for the Cameroon-Chad Power Interconnection Project (PIRECT) stated the estimate covers Option 2-bis, the first phase of the restructuring plan. This cross-border component aims to enable electricity exports of 100 megawatts (MW) to Chad by 2030. The infrastructure includes 225-kilovolt (kV) transmission lines along the Wouro-Soua–Garoua II and Maroua–Chad border routes.

This CFA145.2 billion is an estimated project cost and not a new financing package from the AfDB. The planned works are separate from the connection between Cameroon's Southern and Northern Interconnected Grids.

Why it mattersCameroon's infrastructure development and potential electricity exports to Chad are directly affected by this revised cost estimate.

Cameroon suspends opposition party FSNC for three months

Minister Paul Atanga Nji suspended the Front for National Salvation of Cameroon on October 27, citing past actions.

Cameroon's government suspended the opposition party Front for National Salvation of Cameroon (FSNC) for three months on October 27. Minister of Territorial Administration Paul Atanga Nji issued the order, which comes one year after the actions that led to the suspension and three months before legislative and municipal elections.

Several opposition parties, including UDC, PCRN, MANIDEM, and MP3, condemned the suspension. MP3 called the decision a serious hindrance to political freedoms and an attack on pluralism. MANIDEM described it as a political ambush by the ruling RDPC party, suggesting it aims to prevent opponents from competing in upcoming elections.

The UDC questioned whether the suspension is a precautionary measure or an additional sanction, and if it aims to block FSNC candidates. PCRN urged the minister to reconsider, stating the suspension could worsen existing national tensions and calling for appeasement instead. MP3 requested Prime Minister intervention.

Why it mattersOpposition parties in Cameroon condemn the three-month suspension of the FSNC, fearing it hinders political freedoms and aims to manipulate upcoming elections.

Cameroon secures €87 million for cocoa and coffee

The Green Climate Fund approved financing for a six-year program to boost agricultural output across five regions.

Cameroon will receive €87 million from the Green Climate Fund (GCF) to develop its cocoa and coffee sectors. The GCF Board approved the financing on October 27, 2026, as part of a €215.9 million program called Resilient Agriculture for Climate, Investment, Nature and Equity (RACINE). Cameroon's Ministry of Economy, Planning and Regional Development (MINEPAT) was accredited to oversee the project.

The RACINE program will run for six years and cover the Centre, South, East, Littoral, and South-West regions, with the Cocoa and Coffee Development Fund (FODECC) as the executing agency. Cameroon must contribute €88.5 million from export levies, and 90% of the GCF's €87 million, which is €78.3 million, will be provided as loans, with only €8.7 million as a grant.

The financing aims to significantly increase agricultural output, following a decline in cocoa sales. An additional €40.4 million in grants will come from the Central African Forest Initiative (CAFI) tied to results.

Why it mattersCameroonian farmers and the national economy will be affected by the loan and grant funding aimed at increasing cocoa and coffee production.

Blaise Kengne leads Barry Callebaut Cameroon

Kengne returns to Cameroon after leading Nigerian operations to head SIC Cacaos and Barry Callebaut Cameroon.

Blaise Kengne has been appointed chief executive officer of SIC Cacaos and Barry Callebaut Cameroon. Kengne returns to the country after leading Barry Callebaut Nigeria since October 2023.

Kengne brings over 18 years of experience, including previous roles at SIC Cacaos in Douala. His appointment occurs as SIC Cacaos received 40,012 tonnes of cocoa beans in the 2025–2026 season, a 14% decrease from the prior year.

Overall, Cameroon's industrial processors handled 95,946 tonnes of cocoa beans, down 13% from the previous season. SIC Cacaos, despite lower bean intake, remained the country's largest producer of processed cocoa products, making 29,245 tonnes.

Why it mattersBlaise Kengne's appointment affects Cameroon's cocoa processing sector, which saw a 13% decrease in handled cocoa beans.

MRC warns Cameroon faces implosion

The party calls for a presidential vacancy declaration and interim leadership amid economic and social crises.

The Mouvement pour la renaissance du Cameroun (MRC) warned on October 28, 2026, that Cameroon is on the verge of general implosion. The party cited a catastrophic business climate, with public investment spending down 74.4% in 2026, empty state coffers, and deep social unrest. They also pointed to political anger over Maurice Kamto's exclusion from the 2025 presidential election and a constitutional change on April 16, 2026.

According to the MRC, these factors indicate the country is a 'boiling volcano' that could erupt at any moment due to governmental democratic blockages, particularly concerning elections. The party's statement, signed by its new president Tiriane Balbine Noah, calls for competent authorities to declare a vacancy in the presidency based on the 1996 Constitution. They propose the Senate president takes interim charge and that a consensual electoral code be adopted before new elections.

The party's analysis projects an uncertain future for Cameroon, which is currently awaiting the convocation of the electoral body for legislative and municipal elections.

Why it mattersCameroonian citizens face potential chaos and instability due to the political situation and economic decline.
