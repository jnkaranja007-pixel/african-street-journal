Franklin Templeton funds hold CFAF 2 billion in Cameroon bonds

Two investment funds reported CFAF 2.04 billion in Cameroon sovereign bonds with a 5.95% coupon maturing in 2032.

Two investment funds managed by Franklin Templeton held Cameroon sovereign bonds worth CFAF 2.04 billion as of June 30, 2026. The bonds carry a 5.95% annual coupon and are set to mature on July 7, 2032. The Templeton Global Dynamic Income Fund reported the larger holding, with CFAF 1.90 billion in face value.

The second fund, formerly Putnam Master Intermediate Income Trust, held CFAF 144.3 million in face value. This fund changed its name to Franklin Master Intermediate Income Trust in July 2026. Its portfolio statement from June 30 still listed the former name and ticker symbol PIM on the New York Stock Exchange.

Holdings in Cameroon bonds by these funds have decreased from 2023 levels. The market value of the Templeton Global Dynamic Income Fund's Cameroon bond holding was $2.96 million, while the Putnam fund's was $225,216 on June 30, 2026.

IFC guarantees CFAF 2.87 billion loan for ACEP Cameroun

The International Finance Corporation will back a loan from BICEC to the microfinance lender ACEP Cameroun.

The International Finance Corporation (IFC) approved a CFAF 2.87 billion guarantee on October 9, 2026, for a loan from Banque Internationale du Cameroun pour l’Épargne et le Crédit (BICEC) to microfinance institution ACEP Cameroun. This transaction, updated on October 16, has not yet been signed, and no funds have been disbursed. The IFC's original plan in July 2025 was to lend up to $5 million directly to ACEP.

Under the new arrangement, BICEC will provide the loan, with the IFC guaranteeing repayment of up to CFAF 2.87 billion, covering the principal but not interest. If ACEP defaults, the IFC can seek reimbursement directly from the microfinance lender. BICEC, a subsidiary of Moroccan group BCP, already holds a 27% stake in ACEP Cameroun.

The IFC's guarantee aims to support ACEP Cameroun's lending activities. BICEC will act as the lender, with the IFC providing the financial backstop to facilitate the transaction.

Why it mattersCameroonian microfinance institution ACEP Cameroun will receive a guaranteed loan to support its lending activities.
