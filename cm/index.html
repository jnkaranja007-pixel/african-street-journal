Cameroon Treasury Debt Surpasses CFA2 Trillion as Growth Slows

The nation's outstanding securities reached CFA2.061 trillion by July 31, marking a significant deceleration in annual growth.

Cameroon's total outstanding Treasury securities on the CEMAC public debt market reached CFA2.061 trillion as of July 31, 2026. This figure represents an increase of CFA80.4 billion, or 4.1%, compared to the previous year. The Bank of Central African States (BEAC) reported that this growth rate is a sharp slowdown from the 23.7% and 29.6% increases observed in the two preceding years.

Within the regional market, Cameroon held the third-largest stock of outstanding government securities, trailing Gabon (CFA3.424 trillion) and Congo (CFA3.041 trillion). Cameroon's debt constitutes 19.5% of the total CFA10.561 trillion in government securities across the six CEMAC member countries. The deceleration in growth follows two years of substantially faster increases in the nation's debt.

The stock of outstanding securities grew by CFA293 billion, or 23.7%, between July 2023 and July 2024. This was followed by an increase of CFA452.2 billion, or 29.6%, between July 2024 and July 2025, highlighting the recent slowdown in debt accumulation.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the slowing growth of Cameroon's treasury debt indicates a potential shift in the country's borrowing patterns and fiscal expansion.

Cameroon Launches New Debt Audit Amid CFA288.8 Billion Unpaid Arrears

The Finance Ministry is seeking claims from government entities for unstructured debt accumulated between 2020 and 2025, while previous obligations remain unsettled.

Cameroon has initiated a fresh audit to tally unpaid government obligations from 2020 to 2025, even as CFA288.8 billion from a prior review remains outstanding. Finance Minister Louis Paul Motazé issued a circular on August 19, 2026, directing all government agencies, public institutions, state universities, companies, and local authorities to submit their outstanding claims by October 31. This exercise aims to quantify the extent of unstructured debt that has accumulated across these entities over the past six years.

The Finance Ministry defines floating debt as unstructured claims presented by third parties against the central government and its associated bodies. The total amount can only be determined after these claims are identified, their supporting documentation is reviewed, and the figures are officially validated through the audit process. This new review seeks to capture any additional debt that has built up since the last assessment.

This audit is crucial for understanding the full scope of the government's financial liabilities. By consolidating and verifying these outstanding claims, authorities aim to gain a clearer picture of the fiscal landscape and address the backlog of unpaid obligations. The process involves meticulous examination of submitted documentation to ensure accuracy before any validation occurs.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the government's significant unpaid obligations and ongoing audits indicate potential financial instability and impact on public contracts.

Chinese Supplier Claims CFA3.81 Billion in Sodinaf Palm Oil Equipment

A retention-of-title clause filed with the Douala-Bonanjo Court of First Instance asserts supplier ownership until payment for processing equipment.

Industrial equipment destined for palm oil processing and refining at Cameroon’s Société de distribution nouvelle d’Afrique (Sodinaf) is subject to a CFA3.81 billion ownership claim by Chinese supplier CNBM General Technology Co. Ltd. The claim is based on a retention-of-title clause, meaning the supplier retains ownership until the full obligation is met. This security arrangement was officially recorded with the Trade and Personal Property Credit Register (RCCM) on September 24, 2024, according to a recent registry certificate.

The value of the obligation secured by this clause is $6.47 million, equivalent to approximately CFA3.81 billion at the exchange rate on the registration date. Sodinaf originally acquired the processing and refining equipment under a sales contract signed with the Chinese supplier in Zhengzhou seven years prior, on September 20, 2017. The registration of the retention-of-title clause occurred seven years after the equipment purchase and nearly two years after Sodinaf publicly announced its palm oil refinery project.

This legal filing establishes a claim over the physical assets intended for Sodinaf's palm oil operations. The retention-of-title clause functions as a security interest for the supplier, CNBM General Technology Co. Ltd, ensuring their right to the equipment until the financial obligation from the 2017 sales contract is fully settled. The RCCM registration provides public notice of this claim.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because a significant claim has been lodged against industrial equipment essential for palm oil processing, potentially impacting asset ownership and financial arrangements.

Cameroon Launches Five-Year Health Research Plan

A new strategic plan aims to integrate research findings into public health policy and decision-making across the nation.

Cameroon has officially launched its National Strategic Plan for Health Research (Psnrs) for 2026-2030, a five-year initiative with an estimated budget of 2.533 billion FCFA. Presented in Yaoundé on September 1, 2026, the plan seeks to strengthen health research and ensure that its outcomes directly inform public health actions and policy formulation. Key focus areas include enhancing governance, research ethics, and the use of evidence-based data in decision-making processes.

The plan introduces innovations by expanding research beyond central administrations, universities, and specialized institutes to include regional health districts, health facilities, and communities. This decentralized approach will empower health districts to conduct observations, innovate, and engage in operational research. The Ministry of Public Health stated that the plan also aims to institutionalize a research culture within the health system and secure sustainable funding.

Coinciding with the plan's launch, Cameroon also inaugurated the Digital Documentation Center for the Health Sector. This virtual library will centralize, preserve, organize, and improve access to scientific reports, studies, technical documents, and other research outputs. Health professionals, researchers, and policymakers can access the center via cdnss-minsante.cm, facilitating the dissemination and application of health research findings.

Why it mattersThe new health research plan and digital documentation center will affect health professionals and researchers by providing tools and processes to improve health outcomes and decision-making.

NASLA, PROLOG Sign CFAF 844 Million Deal for Local Authority Management

A 12-month program will train 2,000 officials in administrative and financial management across six regions.

Cameroon’s National School of Local Administration (NASLA) and the Local Governance and Resilient Communities Project (PROLOG) have signed an agreement valued at CFAF 844 million to enhance administrative and financial management in 145 local authorities. This initiative, set to run for 12 months, aims to train approximately 2,000 local elected officials and staff. The training will be conducted in the Adamawa, East, Far North, North, North West, and South West regions of the country.

The memorandum of understanding was officially signed on August 31, 2026, at PROLOG’s headquarters in Yaounde. The ceremony was presided over by the Minister of Decentralization and Local Development, Georges Elanga Obam. Attendees included the World Bank’s PROLOG task team leader in Cameroon, the project’s national coordinator, representatives from partner government agencies, and officials from decentralized local authorities.

The program's curriculum will cover key areas such as administrative and financial management, human resources management, and civil status services. PROLOG has indicated that 1,937 participants are scheduled to complete the training in two sessions planned for October and November 2026. NASLA stated that the financial management component is specifically designed to improve how local authorities manage their funds.

Why it mattersLocal authorities in six regions will receive training to improve administrative and financial management, impacting their operational efficiency and fund handling.
