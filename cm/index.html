Neptune Group Invests CFA6 Billion in Cameroon Supermarket

The Neptune Group has launched its first Maithé Mall in Obala, near Yaoundé, marking a significant diversification into large-scale retail with a CFA6 billion investment.

Cameroon's Neptune Group has entered the large-scale retail market with the opening of its first Maithé Mall, a CFA6 billion investment located in Obala, near Yaoundé. Trade Minister Luc Magloire Mbarga Atangana inaugurated the shopping center on August 26. This move diversifies the group's operations beyond its established fuel distribution business.

Founded by Cameroonian businessman Antoine Ndzengué, Neptune Group's new mall aims to meet local demand, addressing observed needs for residents who previously traveled long distances for certain products. Minister Mbarga Atangana highlighted the mall's role in extending modern retail services beyond major cities into secondary urban centers, reflecting the dynamism of Cameroon's economy.

The Maithé Mall represents a significant expansion for Neptune Group into the country's retail sector. The investment underscores the growing presence of domestic operators in large-scale distribution and aims to improve access to goods and services for residents in Obala and surrounding areas.

Why it mattersThe opening of Maithé Mall by Neptune Group affects investors by demonstrating new retail market opportunities and diversification potential within Cameroon.

MRC Faces Internal Strife and Administrative Hurdles Ahead of 2027 Elections

The Mouvement pour la Renaissance du Cameroun (MRC) is grappling with leadership disputes and administrative challenges that threaten its preparation for the upcoming legislative.

The Mouvement pour la Renaissance du Cameroun (MRC) is experiencing a critical period marked by internal divisions and administrative scrutiny, potentially impacting its readiness for the 2027 legislative and municipal elections. After a five-year boycott of elections, the party aims to regain national and local power. However, its attempts to field candidates have been complicated by internal leadership struggles and administrative actions questioning recent party decisions, including the return of Maurice Kamto as leader.

The party's leadership crisis intensified with the contested return of Maurice Kamto, leading to the expulsion of internal opponents like Thierry Okala Ebode. This internal conflict is now before the courts for arbitration. Simultaneously, the Ministry of Territorial Administration has challenged the validity of the party's recent acts, aligning with dissenting members and raising concerns about the MRC's stability and its ability to ensure valid candidate nominations for the elections.

Despite these challenges, the MRC has not been formally prevented from participating in the 2027 elections. However, the party must address its internal issues and administrative compliance swiftly to prepare effectively. With opponents actively consolidating their positions, the MRC faces a tight timeline to reorganize, secure its electoral base, and regain voter confidence in the coming months.

Why it mattersThe internal conflicts and administrative challenges facing the MRC could affect its ability to present valid candidates and compete effectively in the 2027 elections, impacting the political landscape.

Cameroon Blocks Undeclared Phones to Boost Customs Revenue

Authorities will block mobile devices that have not cleared customs from connecting to local networks starting September 1, aiming to collect CFA25 billion annually.

Cameroon will begin blocking undeclared mobile phones and other connected devices from local telecom networks on September 1, three months after an initial deadline was missed. This measure is part of a customs overhaul intended to generate CFA25 billion in annual revenue from mobile device imports, according to Finance Minister Louis Paul Motazé.

The restrictions apply to devices that entered Cameroon without customs clearance after April 1, 2026, under a system established in the 2023 Finance Law. The blocking will affect devices connecting to a local network for the first time, as well as those first connected after April 1 whose owners ignored prior notices. Devices activated in batches using the same SIM card after April 1 and subsequently taken out of continuous use are also included.

This renewed enforcement follows a previous attempt to implement the policy, which was originally set to begin in June. The government expects the new system to significantly increase customs revenue by ensuring all imported devices are properly declared and taxed.

Why it mattersThe blocking of undeclared phones will affect individuals who have imported devices without paying customs duties, potentially impacting their ability to use their phones.

UN Resident Coordinator Advocates for Pastoral Mobility at COP17

Issa Sanogo urged preserving pastoral mobility as a climate adaptation strategy, not a restriction, highlighting its role in resilience and peace.

United Nations Resident Coordinator Issa Sanogo called for a shift in perspective on pastoral mobility, advocating for its preservation as a critical climate adaptation strategy during COP17's "People and Earth Day." Speaking at a high-level roundtable on August 26, 2026, organized by IOM, Sanogo emphasized that pastoralism is more than an economic activity; it is a comprehensive socio-cultural and ecological system that bolsters community resilience and secures sustainable livelihoods.

Sanogo pointed to Cameroon's experience, where land pressure fuels tensions, and outlined four key areas to support pastoral mobility: securing access to pastoral lands and transhumance corridors, strengthening policy frameworks, improving data collection and early warning systems, and consolidating cross-border cooperation. These actions are deemed essential for preventing resource-related conflicts and ensuring adaptive mobility remains an opportunity rather than a response to distress.

The UN reiterated its commitment to supporting inclusive solutions that leverage pastoralism as a driver of stability and peace. This stance comes as the world grapples with desertification and the need for effective climate adaptation measures, positioning pastoral mobility as a vital tool for both environmental sustainability and conflict prevention in affected regions.

Why it mattersPastoral communities face potential restrictions on their movement and access to land, impacting their livelihoods and cultural practices.

Cameroon State Acquires General Bank, Raising Fiscal Concerns

The African Development Bank warns of growing fiscal and governance risks as the state increases its stake in the banking sector, citing the recent acquisition of Société.

Cameroon's government has finalized its acquisition of Société Générale Cameroun, now renamed General Bank of Cameroon (GBC), increasing state control over the financial sector. The African Development Bank (AfDB) flagged this move in its 2026 Country Report, expressing concern over the expanding role of the state in banking. The AfDB noted that repeated public recapitalizations and acquisitions create additional fiscal and governance risks for the country.

The report specifically cites the May 12, 2026, acquisition of the 58.08% stake previously held by Société Générale Group as the latest instance of this trend. With the state already holding a 25.60% share, this transaction significantly deepens government ownership. The AfDB's warning, however, does not suggest that GBC itself is financially weak but rather focuses on the broader implications of accumulating government stakes in banks.

The central concern highlighted by the AfDB is the accumulation of government holdings in financial institutions, the associated governance risks, and the increasing interdependence between public finances and bank balance sheets. This trend raises questions about the long-term stability and independence of the banking sector within Cameroon as state influence grows.

Why it mattersInvestors face increased fiscal and governance risks due to the Cameroonian state's growing footprint in the banking sector, as highlighted by the African Development Bank's concerns.
