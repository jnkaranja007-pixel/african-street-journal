Cameroon Tax Collection Reaches CFA15 Billion, Businesses Cite Cash Flow Issues

The General Synthetic Tax for 2025 yielded CFA15 billion, a 14% increase from the previous year, but businesses are urging tax authorities to revise withholding rules.

Cameroon's General Synthetic Tax (IGS) generated nearly CFA15 billion in fiscal year 2025, with funds transferred to municipalities in early 2026. This figure represents a 14% increase compared to 2024 collections, according to Director General of Taxation Roger Athanase Meyong Abath. He announced the figures on August 25 in Douala during private sector consultations. The Directorate General of Taxation (DGI) has not yet released the final 2025 amount or the specific 2024 revenue figures.

Despite the reported increase in tax revenue, businesses are expressing concerns about the current withholding tax regulations. They argue that these rules are restricting their cash flow and are pressing the government for changes. The consultations with the private sector are intended to inform the upcoming 2027 Finance Law, suggesting ongoing dialogue about fiscal policies and their impact on commercial operations.

The CFA15 billion collected for 2025 surpasses an earlier Finance Ministry estimate from May 18, which reported CFA10.35 billion transferred to municipalities for the same fiscal year. This new figure indicates a stronger-than-anticipated performance for the IGS, though the exact final 2025 amount will only be known after all accounts are closed.

Why it mattersBusinesses are affected by the General Synthetic Tax and its withholding rules, which they claim are tying up cash flow.

Cameroon Treasury Bills See Reduced Bank Interest Amidst Higher Rival Rates

Participation in Cameroon's short-term government debt auctions fell below 20% in July 2026, reaching its lowest point since 2023.

Banks showed diminished interest in Cameroon’s short-term government debt during July 2026, with participation in Treasury bill auctions dropping to 18.98%. This decline, according to data from the Bank of Central African States (BEAC), occurred as competing issuers within the CEMAC region offered more attractive interest rates, weakening overall demand in the regional government securities market. Cameroon's participation rate was below the market-wide average of 20.1% for the month.

The July 2026 figure represents Cameroon’s lowest participation rate in Treasury bill issuances since at least 2023. In comparison, primary dealer participation stood at 23.8% in July 2023, rose to 26.3% in July 2024, and was 23.3% in July 2025. This trend places Cameroon with the second-lowest participation rate for Treasury bill issuances in the CEMAC zone, only ahead of Gabon’s 16.2%, despite Cameroon having the largest network of approved primary dealers in the region with 22.

This reduced appetite for Cameroon's short-term debt signals a shift in investment preferences within the regional financial market. The BEAC data highlights a competitive environment where banks are prioritizing higher yields offered by other CEMAC member states. The sustained decline in participation suggests a need for Cameroon to reassess its debt instruments or offer more competitive terms to attract domestic and regional investment.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because Cameroon's Treasury bills are less attractive due to lower rates compared to other CEMAC issuers, potentially impacting capital availability.

Police Disrupt Opposition Meeting, Detain Officials

Gendarmes dispersed a private gathering of Movement for the Rebirth of Cameroon militants in Bertoua on Sunday, detaining two party officials.

Gendarmes forcibly dispersed a private meeting of Movement for the Rebirth of Cameroon (MRC) militants in Bertoua on Sunday, August 30, detaining two regional officials. The party claims the intervention, ordered by the sub-prefect of Bertoua 2e, Orfeo Benasconi Nkomo Mimbo'o, involved a dozen gendarmes who arrested the regional secretary and the Lom et Djerem departmental secretary without a warrant. They were later released under guarantee.

The MRC denounces this action as an act of state barbarism orchestrated by the ruling Biya-RDPC regime, linking it to a series of perceived political maneuvers. These include the rejection of Maurice Kamto's presidential candidacy, the prohibition of the MRC's national convention, and the ministry of territorial administration's refusal to acknowledge resolutions from a December 2025 convention. The party also criticizes the judiciary for alleged complicity with the ruling party.

The party is also in dispute with the Ministry of Territorial Administration over a December 21, 2026, extraordinary convention held via videoconference. Minister Paul Atanga Nji refuses to acknowledge its resolutions, stating it was not declared to administrative authorities. The MRC contests this, questioning which authority is competent to receive declarations for virtual meetings and asserting that ministerial oversight is not applicable to political party operations according to law no. 90/056.

Why it mattersOpposition party militants and officials are directly affected by the disruption of their private meetings and the detention of party members.

Cameroon Invests CFAF 90 Million in Foumbot Youth Training Centre

The new facility, financed by the Ministry of the Economy, aims to boost employment and self-employment for young people.

Cameroon has allocated CFAF 90 million to establish a new Multifunctional Youth Promotion Centre (CMPJ) in Foumbot, West Region. This investment in public infrastructure is intended to enhance training and support services for young individuals, facilitating their entry into employment and self-employment. The facility was funded by the Ministry of the Economy, Planning and Regional Development (MINEPAT) and formally transferred to the center's director by the Noun Senior Divisional Officer, Chaïbou.

The new center will begin admitting its first learners at the commencement of the upcoming academic year. This development strengthens the existing network of CMPJs, which operate under the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Civic Education (MINJEC). These centers are designed to provide non-formal training, career guidance, and support to individuals aged 15 to 35, with a specific emphasis on their socio-economic integration into the community.

According to MINJEC, Cameroon currently operates 444 CMPJs across the country, including specialized reference and regional centers, alongside numerous divisional and sub-divisional facilities. In 2024, a total of 10,771 learners were registered in these centers, indicating a significant reach in youth development programs. The ministry has also committed CFAF 1.8 billion to these initiatives.

Why it mattersYoung people aged 15 to 35 in Foumbot will benefit from new training and support services aimed at improving their employment prospects.

Cameroon Proposes Grid Investment and Loss Reduction Methods

New proposals aim to improve electricity distribution by using data to plan investments and track losses across the network.

Cameroon's electricity sector can be improved through better investment planning and loss measurement, according to new proposals. The Ministry of Water and Energy (MINEE) is developing a geographic information system platform to manage its electrical and hydraulic infrastructure. This initiative, alongside the public transformation of ENEO and the creation of SOCADEL, aims to modernize the grid and reduce energy losses.

The first proposal suggests creating detailed electrical profiles for each load basin to guide investment decisions. These profiles would map the real state of the network, including equipment load, incidents, outages, losses, and connection requests, while also accounting for territorial changes that could affect demand. This data-driven approach would help identify constraints before they become critical issues.

The second proposal focuses on tracking electricity from injection into the network to its final destination, department by department. This method would allow for precise measurement of losses, enabling targeted corrections. By prioritizing investments based on criteria such as saturation levels, interruption frequency, expected demand, work costs, and the importance of served uses, a rolling three-to-five-year investment program can be established and revised annually.

Why it mattersThis plan aims to improve the reliability and efficiency of the national electricity grid, which affects all consumers and businesses.
