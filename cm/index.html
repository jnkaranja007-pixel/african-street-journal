CDEC records CFA61.2 billion in securities transactions

Cameroon's Deposits and Consignments Fund invested over 80% of its funds in government debt.

Cameroon's Deposits and Consignments Fund (CDEC) recorded CFA61.2 billion in securities transactions in the first nine months of 2026. Over 80% of these transactions involved securities issued by the Cameroonian government. CDEC aims to shift more than 20% of its investments outside government securities by the end of 2028.

The institution began investing in December 2024 with CFA9 billion in transactions, followed by CFA60 billion in 2025. These figures represent transaction volumes, not the value of securities CDEC currently holds. CDEC became a major institutional investor in government securities in 2025.

The CDEC's investment strategy is evolving as it becomes more active in the regional financial market. The fund's total transactions reached CFA61.2 billion between January and September 2026.

Why it mattersCameroon's Deposits and Consignments Fund is concentrating its investments in government debt, impacting the allocation of financial market funds.

Tusker Minerals gets nod for 21 Cameroon permits

Australian miner's applications recommended for approval but await final administrative steps.

Australian mining company Tusker Minerals received a favorable recommendation for 21 exploration permit applications in Cameroon on October 30, 2026. The interministerial committee recommended approval for permits under its Central Rutile project, adding to the previously granted Nsimbo permit. These applications, if finalized, will cover 9,152 square kilometers across 22 areas, including the Yaoundé West project.

The recommendation is subject to Tusker Minerals fulfilling several administrative and legal requirements, including updating tax, legal, and administrative documents. The company must also sign exploration agreements before ministerial orders can be issued. No confirmed timetable exists for the final issuance of these permits, which remain pending.

Laboratory analysis of collected samples is experiencing delays due to additional preparation, mineral separation, and quality control procedures. Tusker Minerals is processing samples from hand-auger drilling and soil samples at its specialized heavy-mineral laboratory in Yaoundé.

Why it mattersTusker Minerals' exploration permit applications in Cameroon are pending final approval, impacting its mineral exploration activities.

Cameroon governor calls for steel factory shutdown after fire kills four

Littoral Governor Samuel Dieudonné Ivaha Diboua cited safety failures following a deadly blaze at Metafrique Steel in Douala.

A fire at Metafrique Steel's factory in Douala's Bassa industrial zone killed four people and seriously injured 16 on November 2, 2026. Littoral Governor Samuel Dieudonné Ivaha Diboua called for the company to halt operations, citing a failure to implement safety measures after previous fires.

The governor criticized the plant's safety management, stating, "We truly believe it would be appropriate for this company to shut down." An investigation has been announced to determine the fire's circumstances, which initial reports suggest followed an explosion.

Four badly burned bodies were found inside the facility by firefighters, and 16 injured individuals were taken to Douala General Hospital. Governor Diboua warned that the death toll may rise as rescue efforts continue.

Why it mattersFour people died and 16 were seriously injured in a factory fire in Douala.

Governments accused of 86% impunity for journalist murders

UNESCO reports global impunity rate for journalist killings rose to 86% in 2026, a reversal of a downward trend since 2012.

The global impunity rate for journalist murders has climbed to 86% in 2026, according to UNESCO, meaning seven out of 10 killings go unpunished. This figure represents an increase of one point from previous years, reversing a decline that began in 2012 when the rate was 95%. Governments are accused of complicity and institutional failure in addressing these crimes.

The report criticizes governments for relying on excuses like conflict, terrorism, or insecurity to avoid prosecuting killers. It states that when journalists are killed, it silences whistleblowers and terrorizes newsrooms, ultimately blinding entire populations. The UNESCO report highlights that investigations are often dropped, judges are intimidated, or police are bribed, with powerful individuals like ministers or generals allegedly protecting perpetrators.

This high rate of impunity is described not as an accident but as a deliberate policy to send a message that speaking out carries fatal risks. The failure of justice systems and political courage is directly linked to the safety of all citizens, as the freedom of everyone is compromised when murderers of journalists are not brought to justice.

Why it mattersGovernments are accused of failing to prosecute those who murder journalists, impacting the public's right to information and the safety of reporters.

Cameroon and UK set London investment mission dates

The five-day mission from November 30 to December 4, 2026, aims to secure British financing and partnerships.

Cameroon and the United Kingdom have scheduled a trade and investment mission in London for November 30 to December 4, 2026. The mission aims to secure British financing, technology, and industrial partnerships for Cameroonian projects. Dates were confirmed on November 2 in Yaoundé during talks between Cameroon's Trade Minister Luc Magloire Mbarga Atangana and British High Commissioner Matthew David Woods.

The delegation will comprise representatives from Cameroonian government departments, public and private companies, business associations, and professional organizations. They will seek British partners for projects in sectors including energy, infrastructure, agriculture, agro-industry, consumer goods, and manufacturing. The Ministry of Trade reviewed preparations and the program for the mission during the November 2 meeting.

This mission was first publicly discussed in September, with the ministry identifying key sectors for potential cooperation. The November 2 meeting confirmed the five-day schedule and reviewed the preparations for the visit.

Why it mattersCameroonian businesses and government departments will seek British financing, technology, and industrial partnerships.
