Cameroon economy grows 1.6% despite investment drop

The country's GDP saw its strongest rise in six quarters in early 2026, driven by services and household spending.

Cameroon's economy grew by 1.6% in the first quarter of 2026, marking its strongest performance in six quarters. This growth occurred even as public investment fell by 27.7%. The national institute of statistics published these figures on November 5, 2026.

The overall real gross domestic product (GDP) increased by 3.4% compared to the first quarter of 2025. Services were the primary driver, with value added rising 2.2%, contributing 1.1 percentage points to the GDP growth. Transport services saw a significant 7.4% increase.

Household consumption rose by 2.6% in the same period, supporting the economic rebound. Imports also increased by 10.2% during the first quarter of 2026.

Why it mattersCameroonian households and businesses are affected by the 1.6% economic growth and the 2.6% rise in household consumption.

Cameroon considers reallocating unused fish import quotas

18 of 52 companies imported nothing by November 4, while Congelcam received 66% of the total allocation.

Cameroon's government is considering reallocating unused fish import quotas after 18 of 52 companies imported nothing by November 4. The Ministry of Livestock, Fisheries and Animal Industries (MINEPIA) reported that most other operators also imported negligible quantities. Trade Minister Luc Magloire Mbarga Atangana called for the redistribution during a November 4, 2026, meeting in Yaoundé.

MINEPIA allocated nearly 200,000 tons of fish for 2026. Congelcam, headed by Senator Sylvestre Ngouchinghe, received 130,000 tons, approximately 66% of the total allocation. Only Congelcam and Akain Nicolas Luis were among the few companies that had imported significant amounts, according to ministry figures presented at the meeting.

The concentration of import authorizations in Congelcam's hands highlights the issue, as many other importers have been unable to utilize their allocated quotas. The government is now exploring options to reallocate these unused portions to other operators.

Why it mattersCameroonian businesses that received fish import quotas are affected by the potential reallocation of unused portions.

Biya's 8th term pledges unmet one year later

Promises to prioritize youth and women remain unfulfilled, with corruption and insecurity persisting.

One year after President Paul Biya's oath for his eighth term, many commitments made on November 6, 2026, have not materialized. While the ruling CPDM party celebrated Biya's 44th anniversary in power, key pledges regarding youth and women, institutional reform, and government formation are still pending.

Despite promises to place young people and women at the heart of his mandate, these groups continue to face unemployment, inadequate training, high living costs, and limited access to decision-making roles. Public funds are reportedly being diverted, and various illicit trades are costing the nation billions. The establishment of a vice-president position, a result of the April 2026 constitutional revision, remains vacant, awaiting nomination.

The formation of a new government, announced eight months ago, has also been delayed, leaving some ministerial posts vacant or combined under existing ministers for nearly a year. Insecurity and armed violence persist across the country, despite assurances from the state that the situation is under control. The CPDM is encouraging youth and women to engage in upcoming legislative and municipal elections as a partial solution.

Why it mattersCameroon's citizens, particularly youth and women, continue to experience economic hardship and insecurity despite presidential pledges made a year ago.

Cameroon bars 65 firms from contracts over fraud

The number of companies banned from public procurement more than doubled in 2025 compared to the previous year.

Cameroon's Ministry of Public Contracts banned 65 companies from public procurement in 2025 due to forged documents and fraud. This figure represents a 132.1% increase from the 28 companies banned in 2024.

The ministry's 2025 report, published on November 2, 2026, also revealed a rise in contract terminations. 99 contractors had their contracts ended due to their own failures, up from 46 in 2024. Of these, 49 cases involved abandoned projects, according to 26 contracting authorities.

The budget execution reports for 2024 and 2025 provided the basis for these figures. The value of the terminated contracts and the extent of unfinished work were not specified in the reports.

Why it matters65 companies are banned from public contracts in Cameroon for fraud, impacting their ability to secure government work.

Cameroon grants CFA275,000 to 20,000 young informal workers

Applications open for the second phase of the Youth Economic Inclusion Program covering eight cities.

Cameroon will provide CFA275,000 grants to 20,000 young people working in the informal sector. Economy, Planning and Regional Development Minister Alamine Ousmane Mey announced applications are open for the second phase of the Youth Economic Inclusion Program (IEJ). This initiative targets self-employed individuals aged 18 to 35 who lack other opportunities or skills.

The grants, totaling CFA5.5 billion, will be disbursed electronically in two installments. Beneficiaries must reside in Maroua, Garoua, Ngaoundéré, Bertoua, Ebolowa, Bafoussam, Douala, or Yaoundé. The program is part of the Adaptive Social Safety Nets and Economic Inclusion Project (PFS-AIE).

Online applications for the program opened on November 4, 2026, and will run until December 9, 2026. The notice for the individual grant amount was confirmed on March 18, 2026.

Why it mattersYoung informal workers in eight Cameroonian cities will receive financial support to improve their economic standing.
