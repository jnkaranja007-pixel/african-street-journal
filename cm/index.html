Cameroon regulators fine telecoms firms 18.6 billion FCFA

Orange, MTN, and Camtel must fix 93 service and infrastructure issues within 15 days.

Cameroon's Telecommunications Regulation Agency (ART) has ordered Orange, MTN, and Camtel to correct 93 shortcomings in their service obligations within 15 days. These deficiencies, valued at 18.6 billion FCFA, include issues with equipment approval, frequency use, network coverage, and voice service quality in major cities and on key roads.

The ART's audit, conducted between July 13 and August 10, 2026, identified specific failures for each operator. Camtel faces additional scrutiny for degraded fiber optic infrastructure, marked by frequent cuts and civil engineering faults. These problems affect communication fluidity for users across the country, from urban centers to remote areas.

Previous audits and sanctions have not permanently resolved these telecommunication infrastructure issues. The ART is pushing for improved oversight and compliance from operators to address the ongoing problems that impact users and the cost of telecommunications services for Cameroonians.

Cameroon sets election dates

Legislative and municipal elections will take place on February 7, 2027.

Cameroon will hold legislative and municipal elections within 90 days, with the election day set for February 7, 2027. President Paul Biya convoked the electorate on November 9, 2026, initiating the electoral process. Candidates must submit their applications to ELECAM by November 24, 2026.

The official campaign period will begin on January 23, 2027, allowing candidates two weeks to engage with voters through meetings and on social media. Following the vote, results for municipal councilors are expected by February 10, 2027, and for national assembly deputies by February 22, 2027.

The newly elected municipal councils will convene by February 16, 2027, and the National Assembly will hold its first session on March 2, 2027. These elections will determine the composition of the 180-seat National Assembly and 360 communes across the country.

Why it mattersCameroonians will elect 180 deputies and thousands of municipal councilors.

Cameroon imports rise 22.8% in first quarter

Goods imports climbed 25.8% as the trade gap widened, according to the National Institute of Statistics.

Cameroon's imports of goods and services surged by 22.8% in the first quarter of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025. This increase, driven primarily by a 25.8% rise in goods imports, occurred despite government initiatives aimed at reducing reliance on foreign products. The National Institute of Statistics (INS) reported these figures on November 5, 2026.

The country's external trade gap widened as exports grew by only 3.8% during the same period. The INS stated that the deficit in trade for goods and services reached 5.7% of GDP for the quarter. Imports of machinery and furniture saw a significant increase, while purchases of some manufactured and food products decreased.

Compared to the final quarter of 2025, imports rose 10.2% and exports increased by 0.6%. The INS noted that demand for nearly all imported goods increased significantly. This import surge coincided with a 12.3% year-over-year increase in private investment.

Why it mattersCameroon's trade deficit widened, impacting the national economy due to increased spending on foreign goods.

Cameroon ships first 17 tons coffee to China duty-free

Cameroonian exporters now access China's market without import duties on qualifying shipments.

Cameroon has sent its first 17 tons of coffee to China under Beijing’s zero-tariff policy. This gives Cameroonian exporters duty-free access to the Chinese market for qualifying shipments. The Ministry of Finance announced the shipment on November 10 following a meeting in Yaoundé.

The meeting involved Finance Minister Louis Paul Motaze and Du Xiaohui, director-general of the Department of African Affairs at China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The ministry stated that Cameroonian exports to China increased 41.8% year on year, with cocoa shipments rising 272%. Discussions focused on investment and how Cameroonian businesses can benefit from China's tariff preferences.

China's Customs Tariff Commission introduced the preferential rates on May 1, 2026, under the country's Tariff Law. This measure covers Cameroon and 19 other eligible African countries. Both sides agreed to continue economic negotiations to further boost trade.

Why it mattersCameroonian coffee exporters will benefit from reduced costs when selling to China.

Cameroon lawmakers seek release of CFAF 5,000 billion in financing

National Assembly Speaker Théodore Datouo opened the budget session by urging faster delivery of stalled development projects.

National Assembly Speaker Théodore Datouo said on November 10 that Cameroon holds CFAF 5,000 billion in committed financing that has not yet been paid out, and warned that the delay is holding back development projects.

Speaking as parliament opened its November budget session at the Paul Biya Glass House in Yaoundé, Datouo asked the government to coordinate development projects better and to make fuller use of money secured from development partners. He also urged it to put emergency electricity projects first in the 2027 budget.

The session will mainly review and adopt the 2027 state budget. Datouo named the Douala-Bafoussam road and the Douala-Bangui corridor as transport priorities, calling for existing roads to be rehabilitated and for safety and traffic flow on major routes to improve.

Why it mattersProjects awaiting the CFAF 5,000 billion in committed financing, including emergency power and the named road corridors, depend on the government releasing those funds.
