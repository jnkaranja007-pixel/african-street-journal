US Court Jails Three Cameroonian-Americans for Funding Separatists

A federal court in Missouri sentenced three US citizens of Cameroonian origin to prison terms ranging from five to 15 years for providing financial and logistical support.

Three American citizens of Cameroonian descent have been sentenced to prison by a US federal court in Kansas City, Missouri, for their roles in supporting separatist fighters in Cameroon. Francis Chenyi, 52, and Lah Nestor Langmi, 49, received 15-year sentences, while Claude Ngenevu Chi, 43, was sentenced to 63 months, just over five years. All three will also face three years of supervised release after completing their sentences.

The court found the individuals guilty of providing financial and logistical support for the creation and use of improvised explosive devices in Cameroon. Prosecutors alleged they were part of a network that coordinated the manufacturing of explosives, trained individuals in their use, and facilitated fund transfers from the United States to Cameroon for these operations. These funds were reportedly used for attacks targeting cities in the Northwest and Southwest regions, including near a stadium intended for the 2021 Africa Cup of Nations.

The charges also include accusations that Langmi was involved in coordinating kidnappings, specifically mentioning the abductions of traditional leader Fonmbam Aforteka and Cardinal Christian Tumi. Chenyi is accused of providing financial contributions to these activities. These convictions add to a series of US legal actions against individuals suspected of financing or committing acts of terrorism in Cameroon, with the US committed to prosecuting such cases.

Why it mattersThree individuals of Cameroonian origin are facing significant prison sentences in the US for their alleged involvement in funding and supporting separatist activities in Cameroon, impacting cross-border financial flows and potentially deterring similar actions.

Cameroon's SCDP Seeks CFAF150 Billion for Kribi Fuel Terminal

The state-controlled petroleum depot company is in talks with financial institutions to fund a new import gateway aimed at diversifying fuel supply from Douala.

Cameroon's state-controlled petroleum depot company, SCDP, is pursuing up to CFAF150 billion in long-term financing for a new fuel storage terminal in Kribi. The project aims to establish a second major fuel import gateway for the country, complementing the existing infrastructure in Douala. SCDP presented the financing needs to banks and investors on August 26 in Douala, with plans to raise approximately CFAF100 billion in local currency and the remainder in euros.

The proposed Kribi terminal, known by its French acronym DPK, is being developed with advisory services from PG & Partners and Ivorian investment bank Matha Capital. The financing under discussion is expected to have a maturity of 10 to 15 years, including a principal grace period of three to five years. Project officials anticipate that the main financing parameters will be finalized within the next 60 days.

This new terminal is designed to enhance Cameroon's fuel import capacity by enabling the reception of larger tankers at Kribi's deep-sea port. The facility will increase the nation's overall fuel storage capabilities, reducing reliance on the current primary import hub in Douala and potentially improving supply chain resilience.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the potential for significant infrastructure investment and the development of a new energy import gateway.

Neptune Group Opens CFA6 Billion Maithé Mall in Cameroon

The conglomerate diversifies into large-scale retail with its first shopping center, aiming to serve local needs beyond its fuel distribution business.

Cameroon’s Neptune Group has launched its first Maithé Mall, a CFA6 billion investment marking its entry into the country’s large-scale retail sector. The shopping center, inaugurated on August 26 in Obala near Yaoundé by Trade Minister Luc Magloire Mbarga Atangana, signifies the group’s diversification beyond its established fuel distribution operations. Founded and led by Cameroonian businessman Antoine Ndzengué, Neptune Group developed the mall to address observed local demand for accessible goods and services.

Minister Mbarga Atangana highlighted the mall as a reflection of Cameroon's economic and retail dynamism, emphasizing the increasing role of domestic businesses in large-scale distribution. He also commended the project's location in Obala, noting its importance in extending modern retail infrastructure to secondary urban centers outside major cities. This expansion aligns with broader urbanization trends driving retail growth within the country.

The investment in Maithé Mall is intended to meet a specific local need, according to Ndzengué, who observed residents traveling significant distances for certain products. The opening represents a strategic move for Neptune Group, broadening its portfolio and contributing to the development of retail services in areas beyond the primary urban hubs.

Why it mattersThe opening of Maithé Mall provides a new retail destination for residents in Obala, potentially reducing travel time for essential goods and services.

Cameroon, UNFPA Sign 50 Million Dollar Development Program

The United Nations Population Fund's ninth country program, spanning 2027-2031, has been approved in New York, focusing on population well-being and demographic dividends.

The United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) Board approved Cameroon's ninth Country Cooperation Programme (CCP9) on August 27, 2026, in New York. This five-year program, running from 2027 to 2031, is budgeted at 50 million USD and aligns with Cameroon's National Development Strategy (SND30) and the UN Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF). The program aims to improve population well-being and leverage demographic dividends.

This initiative builds on a partnership between Cameroon and UNFPA that began in 1969. The program's approval signifies official government endorsement, with Cameroon's Permanent Mission in New York expressing satisfaction with its alignment to national and UN frameworks. The government has pledged firm support and invited the Board's approval, highlighting the collaborative effort involving multiple ministries including MINEPAT, MINREX, MINSANTE, MINPROFF, MINJEC, and MINFI.

CCP9 is structured around three strategic pillars: enhancing reproductive health and quality of care with universal access to family planning and reduced maternal deaths; promoting gender equality and empowerment by eradicating gender-based violence; and utilizing demographic data to inform public policy. To support these goals, Yaoundé will provide UNFPA with a new modern headquarters, reinforcing Cameroon's role as a key partner.

Why it mattersThe 50 million USD UNFPA program will fund initiatives in reproductive health, gender equality, and demographic data use, directly impacting public health services and policy development.

Cameroonian Parties Demand Action on Rising Feminicides

Multiple political parties are uniting to demand stricter penalties and preventative measures against the increasing number of women killed in Cameroon.

Cameroonian political parties are forming a united front to address the escalating issue of feminicides, with over 60 women reportedly killed in the past eight months. Groups including the Patriotic Movement for the Prosperity of the People (MP3), the Movement for the Rebirth of Cameroon (MRC), and the Cameroonian Party for National Reconciliation (PCRN) are calling for exemplary sanctions against perpetrators and greater state protection for women.

The MP3 advocates for enhanced protection for women at risk, severe penalties for assailants, and educational initiatives to prevent future violence. The MRC has condemned the "silent hemorrhage" of domestic feminicides and criticized the authorities' perceived indifference and inaction. Both parties, along with the Social Democratic Front (SDF), are urging for more robust legal deterrents and community vigilance.

The SDF has highlighted its previous submissions of draft laws to the National Assembly aimed at combating feminicides and other forms of violence against women and children. However, the party notes that progress on these proposals is stalled, attributing the lack of advancement to the ruling party's silence and the absence of a majority needed to pass such legislation.

Why it mattersPolitical parties are demanding action and stricter penalties for feminicides, affecting women and their families across Cameroon.
