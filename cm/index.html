Cameroon cancels CFA36.7 billion fuel tender for four power plants

The state electricity company SOCADEL scrapped the contract for heavy fuel oil, saying delivery and storage terms need to be revised.

SOCADEL, Cameroon's electricity company, has called off a CFA36.715 billion tender, taxes included, for heavy fuel oil for four thermal plants in Limbé, Dibamba, Oyomabang and Logbaba. The notice, signed by CEO Oumarou Hamandjoda, cancels both lots of the tender, which was meant to cover 2026 and 2027.

The emergency buying process was launched on October 13 and had already slipped once, when an amendment signed on November 2 moved bid submission and opening from November 3 to November 12. The cancellation is dated November 9, but it reached the Public Procurement Regulatory Agency (ARMP) platform only on November 12 at 6:58 p.m., after the scheduled opening, and the notice confirms no opening session took place.

The proposed contract was a 15-month framework agreement, and SOCADEL has not said when a new procurement will be launched.

Why it mattersFuel suppliers that bid for the contract have lost it, and the four plants have no set date for a replacement deal.

Cameroon seeks German dairy expertise to cut milk imports

Imports of milk and dairy products cost the country CFAF32.8 billion in 2025, and domestic output reached 183,286 tonnes.

Cameroon is looking to German expertise to expand domestic milk production and reduce its reliance on imports, which cost the Central African country CFAF32.8 billion in 2025. The Chamber of Agriculture, Fisheries, Livestock and Forestry (CAPEF) met a delegation of German experts on November 9 to discuss technology transfers, equipment and training aimed at improving milk production, processing and storage.

The talks involve Dresden-based Elektrobildungs- und Technologiezentrum (EBZ), the Mbouo Multipurpose Training Centre and BERCO-INSTITUTE. According to CAPEF, the discussions focused on constraints including cattle genetics, animal feed, livestock health and farming conditions. The partners also explored renewable energy use and support for women and young people in agribusiness.

No investment commitments, equipment orders or implementation timetable have been announced. The country produced 183,286 tonnes of milk in 2025, an increase of 1.5% from the previous year, and its dairy industry has struggled to meet domestic demand.

Why it mattersDairy farmers and cattle keepers stand to gain from better genetics, feed and training if the talks lead to equipment and programmes, but no timetable has been set.

Cameroon opens first international auto and transport expo in Douala

Cameroon Auto Expo 2026 runs in Douala until November 15, bringing carmakers, transport operators and public institutions together for business meetings.

Cameroon's first international automobile, transport and mobility exhibition opened in Douala on November 11. Transport Minister Jean Ernest Masséna Ngallè Bibéhè inaugurated the Cameroon Auto Expo 2026 at the Camtel Club in Bépanda, according to the Ministry of Transport.

Vehicle displays sit alongside business meetings on financing, insurance, customs and taxation, under a theme of infrastructure, innovation and regional integration. Automotive businesses, transport operators, logistics companies and public institutions are taking part. Cameroon imported CFAF375 billion worth of transport equipment in 2024, 76.8% more than in 2019, according to National Institute of Statistics figures reported by Investir au Cameroun.

Sessions on multimodal logistics and freight corridor performance cover how goods move through the country. The category of imports includes vehicles used to carry passengers and goods, among other equipment. The expo closes on November 15.

Why it mattersTransport firms and importers can meet customs, insurance and financing officials in one place in Douala until November 15.

Health · 2026-11-14

Yaoundé hospital to host African forum on hospital resilience

The Central Hospital of Yaoundé will hold a three-day forum in December, expecting nearly 300 health sector actors from Cameroon, Africa and France.

The Central Hospital of Yaoundé is organising the African Hospital Forum from December 8 to 10, 2026, at the Palais des Congrès in Yaoundé, to mark its 95th anniversary. Under the theme "Towards the resilience of the African hospital", the meeting aims to help modernise African hospitals facing economic, technological and health challenges.

Professor Pierre Ongolo Zogo, the hospital's director, presented the forum at a press conference on November 12, 2026. Professor Elie Claude Ndam Ndjitoyap, chair of the scientific committee, named inclusive and transparent governance, modernised infrastructure, digital transformation and better quality of care as the levers of resilience.

Professor Serge Billong, president of the Cameroonian Society of Public Health, said doctors, managers, researchers, decision-makers and partners are invited to propose concrete solutions to health crises, financial constraints and technological change. The hospital presents the anniversary as a step towards its centenary.

Why it mattersHospital managers, doctors and health partners are invited to Yaoundé from December 8 to 10 to propose solutions to financial and technological pressures.
