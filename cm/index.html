Cameroon imports jump 22.8% in first quarter despite curbs

Goods imports rose 25.8% while exports grew only 3.8%, pushing the trade deficit to 5.7% of GDP, the National Institute of Statistics said.

Cameroon's imports of goods and services rose 22.8% by volume in the first quarter of 2026 compared with a year earlier, even though the government has been trying to reduce dependence on foreign products. Goods imports climbed 25.8%, according to quarterly national accounts published on November 5, 2026 by the National Institute of Statistics (INS).

Exports grew just 3.8% over the same period, widening the external trade gap. The INS put the deficit in goods and services at 5.7% of GDP for the quarter. Compared with the last three months of 2025, imports rose 10.2% while exports rose 0.6%.

Among merchandise imports, purchases of machinery and furniture rose sharply, while some manufactured and food products fell. The institute said demand for nearly all imported goods increased significantly. The surge came alongside a 12.3% year-on-year rise in private investment, but the data do not show how much of the foreign purchasing was equipment for investment.

Why it mattersImporters and investors face a wider external gap, with the deficit now at 5.7% of GDP and exports lagging far behind purchases abroad.

Cameroon ships 17 tons of coffee to China duty-free

A first shipment of 17 metric tons of Cameroonian coffee is reportedly on its way to China under a zero-tariff deal that took effect on May 1, 2026.

Cameroon has reportedly sent its first 17-ton coffee shipment to China under a duty-free trade arrangement that began on May 1, 2026. Du Xiaohui, director-general of the Department of African Affairs at China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, announced the shipment on November 10 in Yaoundé, after a meeting with Finance Minister Louis Paul Motaze. Cameroon Tribune, reporting remarks on November 11, said it would be the first coffee shipment from Cameroon to benefit from the new tariff policy.

China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs issued a statement on November 12 that confirmed Du Xiaohui's visit and his talks with Cameroonian officials. The statement referred to both countries' plans to use zero-tariff trade but did not mention the coffee.

Several key details remain unknown more than six months after the measure took effect. Neither the exporter nor the Chinese buyer has been named, and no information has been given on the port of departure or the shipment's value.

Why it mattersCoffee exporters can now send shipments to China without tariffs, a change that took effect on May 1, 2026.

Cameroon seeks German dairy technology

The nation aims to boost milk production and cut import costs with German expertise.

Cameroon's import bill for milk reached CFAF32.8 billion in 2025, and the Chamber of Agriculture, Fisheries, Livestock and Forestry (CAPEF) is now looking to Germany to shrink it. Its officials met a delegation of German experts on November 9 to explore technology transfers, equipment and training for raising, processing and storing milk.

Taking part in the talks are Dresden-based Elektrobildungs- und Technologiezentrum (EBZ), the Mbouo Multipurpose Training Centre and BERCO-INSTITUTE. CAPEF said the discussions covered weak points in herd breeding, livestock feed, animal health and the conditions farmers work under, with renewable energy and support for women and young people in agribusiness also on the table.

No investment commitments, equipment orders or timetable for putting anything in place were announced. Cameroon produced 183,286 tonnes of milk in 2025, up 1.5% on the year before, yet local output still falls short of demand.

Why it mattersDairy farmers and processors would gain new training and equipment if the German talks become concrete, but no timetable has been set.

Pagou names Cameroon lineup without Baleba, with Angel Yondjo Matah starting

Cameroon's coach David Pagou picked his starting 11 for the qualifier against Namibia at Japoma Stadium in Douala.

Cameroon, known as the Indomitable Lions, were due to host Namibia on Thursday, 12 November 2026, at Japoma Stadium in Douala, in a qualifying match for which they wanted a win to keep their campaign on track.

Coach David Pagou was without Carlos Baleba, who is injured, and Aboubakar Nagida, who is suspended after picking up two yellow cards in earlier matches against the Comoros and the Congo. Angel Yondjo Matah is starting, and Simon Ngapandouetnbu will be in goal.

The Lions were scheduled to play the match with the aim of imposing their rhythm from the first minutes and taking control against their opponents.

Why it mattersCameroon's starting lineup is built without two key players, Baleba and Nagida, so the team's results in this match depend on the replacements Pagou has chosen.
