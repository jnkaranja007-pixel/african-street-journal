Cameroon's Socadel Seeks CFA52.6 Billion in Fuel for 33 Power Plants

The state-owned electricity company launched two tenders to secure heavy fuel oil and diesel for its thermal power stations.

Cameroon's state electricity company, Socadel, is seeking to purchase CFA52.622 billion worth of heavy fuel oil and diesel to supply 33 thermal power plants across the nation. The procurement aims to cover the operational needs of these plants, with funding allocated from the company's 2026 and 2027 budgets, depending on contract duration. The tenders, dated October 13, 2026, were published by the Public Procurement Regulatory Agency on October 19.

The larger of the two tenders, valued at CFA36.715 billion, is for the supply of HFO 3500 heavy fuel oil to the thermal power plants located in Limbé, Dibamba, Oyomabang, and Logbaba. This contract is further divided into two lots: one for the Limbé plant at CFA17.384 billion and another for the remaining three facilities at CFA19.331 billion. The combined minimum supply for these contracts is 35,327 metric tons, with a maximum indicative amount of 70,653 tons.

Deliveries will be managed through purchase orders issued by Socadel, contingent on the specific operating requirements of each power plant. The second tender, for diesel fuel, is also part of the overall effort to ensure consistent energy generation across the country. These purchases are crucial for maintaining the operational capacity of the country's thermal power infrastructure.

Why it mattersSocadel's fuel purchases are intended to meet the operating needs of 33 thermal power plants across Cameroon.

MP3 Congratulates Noah on MRC Presidency

The Mouvement patriotique pour la prospérité du peuple (MP3) has congratulated Tiriane Balbine Noah following her election as the new president of the Mouvement pour la.

The Mouvement patriotique pour la prospérité du peuple (MP3) sent congratulations on October 20 to Tiriane Balbine Noah, who was elected president of the Mouvement pour la renaissance du Cameroun (MRC) on October 17. Noah replaces Maurice Kamto and her election has prompted reactions from opposition parties. The MP3, led by Hiram Samuel Iyodi, expressed enthusiasm, calling Noah's election a lesson in democracy for other political parties.

The MP3's statement, released on October 20, also congratulated the new national bureau members elected during the October 17 convention. The party noted Noah's commitment to continuing the work of her predecessors and wished the new leadership success in strengthening democratic pluralism, idea debate, and citizen participation in Cameroon. The MP3 highlighted a shared responsibility with the MRC to work towards a more just, free, and prosperous Cameroon.

Both the MP3 and the MRC did not recognize the official results of the October 12, 2025 presidential election. This shared stance on electoral integrity suggests potential for strategic collaboration between the two parties in future legislative and municipal elections. The MP3 believes this proximity could lead to alliances aimed at shifting the current political landscape.

Why it mattersThe MP3 party is affected by the election of a new MRC president, as it shares political goals and non-recognition of past election results with the MRC.

Cameroon EDC Converts Government Debt to Capital

The state-owned Electricity Development Corporation has converted CFA10.289 billion in prior government funding into share capital, increasing its registered capital by 68.6%.

Cameroon's Electricity Development Corporation (EDC) has officially increased its share capital by 68.6%, from CFA15 billion to CFA25.289 billion. This adjustment was achieved through a debt-to-equity conversion, formalizing CFA10.289 billion in funds previously provided by the government. The transaction, approved at an extraordinary general meeting, does not represent new state financing, as the funds were already transferred to the company between 2008 and 2010.

The capital increase was registered with the Yaoundé-Centre Administratif Court of First Instance on October 7, 2026. Financial statements from EDC confirm the origin of these funds, noting that the Ministry of Finance began providing the money in 2008 and continued until 2010. Initially recorded as investment grants due to the unspecified nature of the funding, the classification was later flagged by the company's auditors.

The conversion of these past government disbursements into equity aims to reflect the actual financial structure of the state-owned enterprise. This move follows an audit recommendation and clarifies the company's capital base without introducing additional state expenditure at this time. The company's registration update is now being processed.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the company has restructured its capital, converting government debt into equity, which impacts its financial reporting and balance sheet.

Saconets Proposes CFA681 Million Capital Increase

Cameroonian telecommunications firm Saconets plans a debt-to-equity conversion reserved for Cenainvest, an Afriland-linked firm.

Satellite Communications and Network Services (Saconets) is proposing a CFA681.2 million capital increase through a debt-to-equity conversion. The move is reserved exclusively for Cenainvest, an investment firm with historical ties to Afriland First Bank. Shareholders will vote on the proposal, which includes removing existing shareholders' preferential subscription rights, on November 9, 2026, in Yaoundé. The transaction aims to reduce Saconets' liabilities and strengthen its equity without injecting new cash.

If approved, Cenainvest will receive newly issued Saconets shares in exchange for canceling a debt owed to it by the telecommunications company. This differs from a traditional capital increase where new funds are contributed. Other shareholders will not have the right to subscribe to this reserved share issue. The meeting notice was signed by board chair Ghislaine Kammogne Simo.

The proposed capital increase would convert an existing debt claim into an ownership stake, thereby strengthening Saconets' balance sheet. This financial maneuver is designed to improve the company's equity position by reducing its liabilities. The outcome hinges on shareholder approval during the upcoming meeting.

Why it mattersShareholders of Saconets will vote on a proposal that could dilute their ownership stake by converting a debt owed by the company to Cenainvest into equity.

Cameroon, France Review Rural Water Financing

Local councils may soon manage water infrastructure without full external aid after talks in Yaoundé.

Cameroon's Water and Energy Minister Gaston Eloundou Essomba met with French development partners on October 19 to discuss financing rural water services. The goal is to enable local councils to manage and maintain water infrastructure without complete reliance on external support. Representatives from Gescod and the Alsace-Moselle Water and Sanitation Syndicate (SDEA) attended the discussions in Yaoundé.

The meeting reviewed ongoing intercommunal water management projects, examining their financial and operational challenges and exploring avenues to enhance cooperation. A key focus was the capacity of local authorities to mobilize resources, maintain existing water infrastructure, and ensure consistent service delivery to communities. This initiative builds upon cooperation programs already in place in Cameroon's Centre Region.

The intercommunal management model involves neighboring councils pooling technical expertise, personnel, and financial resources. This approach aims to strengthen local management capabilities while distributing operational responsibilities and costs among participating councils. The ministry stated that this model is intended to improve the sustainability of rural water supply and sanitation services.

Why it mattersLocal councils are affected by discussions on how to finance and maintain water infrastructure, impacting their ability to provide services.
