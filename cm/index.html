Afriland First Bank raises share capital to CFAF 110 billion

The bank moved CFAF 60 billion from reserves to capital without new funding, keeping total equity the same.

Afriland First Bank increased its share capital from CFAF 50 billion to CFAF 110 billion by capitalizing CFAF 60 billion in reserves. The Central African Banking Commission (COBAC) approved the change, which was confirmed on November 5, 2026. This move did not involve any new cash injection into the bank.

Shareholders had agreed to the capital increase on April 28, 2026, but regulatory approval was necessary. The bank reclassified existing reserves as share capital, meaning its total shareholders' equity remains unchanged. Updated regulatory capital ratios were not provided by the bank.

The CFAF 60 billion now part of the share capital was previously held within CFAF 112.66 billion in combined reserves and retained earnings as of August 31, 2026. This reclassification represents 53.3% of those reserves.

Cameroon economy grows 1.6% despite public investment drop

Services, led by transport and financial services, drove growth in the first quarter of 2026.

Cameroon's economy expanded by 1.6% in the first quarter of 2026, recovering from a stagnant fourth quarter in 2025. This growth occurred even as public investment fell by 27.7% and imports rose by 10.2%. The National Institute of Statistics reported these figures on November 5, 2026.

The quarterly growth rate was the highest in six quarters, with real gross domestic product (GDP) increasing by 3.4% compared to the first quarter of 2025. Services contributed 1.1 percentage points to the overall GDP growth, with value added rising 2.2%. Transport services saw a 7.4% increase, followed by financial services at 4.3%.

Household consumption also rose by 2.6% in the same period. The primary sector grew by 1.3% and the secondary sector by 0.9%. Public administration services increased by 2.3%.

Business · 2026-11-08

Cameroon ministries partner to formalize informal economy

The Ministry of SMEs and GECAM agreed on a roadmap to integrate informal businesses into structured economic circuits by 2030.

Cameroon's Ministry of Small and Medium-sized Enterprises, Social Economy and Crafts (MINPMEESA) and the Group of Cameroonian Enterprises (GECAM) met on November 4, 2026, in Douala to discuss formalizing the informal economy. Minister Achille Bassilekin III led the MINPMEESA delegation in discussions with GECAM President Célestin Tawamba and other public and private sector representatives. The meeting aimed to find ways to support informal production units towards becoming more structured, productive, and job-creating.

The national consultation focused on three key areas: taxation, sector structuring, and financing. Following deliberations, joint recommendations were formulated and signed by the Minister and the GECAM President. These recommendations will form the basis of a 2027-2030 roadmap designed to accelerate the gradual transformation of the productive sector and ease the integration of economic actors into formal channels.

MINPMEESA stated its goal is to change perceptions of informal economy actors, highlight their entrepreneurial potential, and use their support as a tool for economic and social development.

Why it mattersCameroonian informal economy actors will be supported to integrate into formal economic structures, improving their productivity and access to finance.

Health · 2026-11-08

Cameroon kidney patients deny 30 deaths report

The Association of Cameroonian Kidney Insufficiency Patients disputes social media claims of 30 deaths in four months at Yaoundé General Hospital.

The Association of Cameroonian Kidney Insufficiency Patients-Transplanted (ACIRDT) has refuted claims of 30 deaths over four months at the Yaoundé General Hospital's hemodialysis center. The association stated that allegations, reportedly from a social media publication called "The Times of Cameroon", do not reflect the reality experienced by patients.

ACIRDT explained that some recorded deaths cannot be solely blamed on treatment delays, as some patients arrive at the hospital with advanced kidney failure and compromised health. The association acknowledged efforts to improve care, noting that extension work on the current center is complete and a new center with 30 additional machines is planned.

The association views these investments as an important advance to increase capacity and enhance treatment conditions for kidney patients. The 30 machines currently in use at the center are three years old.

Why it mattersKidney patients in Cameroon face potential treatment delays and compromised health conditions at the Yaoundé General Hospital's hemodialysis center.
