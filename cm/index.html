Cameroon fintechs found with 3,914 security flaws

National agency identifies weaknesses in policies, staff, and data protection after 108 audits.

Cameroon's National Agency for Information and Communication Technologies (ANTIC) identified 3,914 security vulnerabilities in 108 audits of fintech operators. Director General Prof. Ebot Ebot Enaw presented these findings at the Digitise Cameroon Forum on October 28, 2026, in Mbankomo, detailing weaknesses in security policies, staff expertise, access controls, data protection, and business continuity.

These new figures follow earlier audits in November 2025 that flagged over 6,500 vulnerabilities across banks, microfinance institutions, and payment providers. ANTIC has issued recommendations to the affected operators, but the agency has not disclosed how many vulnerabilities were critical or how many have been corrected.

Cameroon's new personal data protection law took effect in December 2024, with an 18-month compliance period ending in June 2026. Mobile Money generated FCFA135.8 billion in revenue in 2024, with 11.4 million active subscriptions.

Why it mattersFintech operators in Cameroon face security and data protection weaknesses that could affect customer data and financial operations.

Business · 2026-11-02

Cameroon certifies 43% of registered bakeries

Cameroon's standards agency found 200 bakeries lack certification, raising food safety questions.

Cameroon's National Agency for Standards and Quality (ANOR) has certified only 150 of the 350 registered bakeries, a rate of about 43%. The agency identified 200 bakeries operating without the required certification.

Acting Director-General Chantal Andely disclosed these figures on October 26, 2026, in an interview with Cameroon Tribune. These findings apply to bakeries registered with ANOR, not all industry operators, and highlight challenges in meeting food-safety standards where bread prices are regulated.

The certification rate indicates progress in meeting formal quality requirements, though it does not confirm product safety. ANOR has not released data on the production share from certified sites or the number of uncertified operators seeking approval.

Why it mattersConsumers in Cameroon face potential exposure to products from uncertified bakeries.
