Douala industries may get 170 MW more power by year-end

World Bank official announced the potential power boost for Cameroon's economic capital after meeting the finance minister.

Industries in Douala, Cameroon's economic capital, may gain access to an additional 170 MW of electricity by the end of 2026. Cheick Fantamady Kanté, the World Bank's division director for Cameroon and four other Central African countries, announced this outlook on October 30 in Yaoundé. This additional supply is for businesses and does not increase the Nachtigal hydropower plant's 420 MW generation capacity from its seven operational units.

The potential for 170 MW more electricity for Douala industries by year-end follows months of delays in building a second transmission corridor. Progress on this infrastructure is key to delivering more power from the Nachtigal plant to the Littoral region. Water and Energy Minister Gaston Eloundou Essomba inspected the site on October 7, 2026, and set November as a deadline for transmission infrastructure progress.

The availability of this extra power by year-end depends on progress with the transmission infrastructure. Kanté's announcement came after a meeting with Finance Minister Louis Paul Motaze.

Why it mattersIndustries in Douala may receive 170 MW more electricity by the end of 2026, potentially improving their operations.

Cameroon cocoa cooperative paid CFA1.03 billion in taxes

The South West Manyu Cocoa Farmers Exporters Cooperative paid CFA1.03 billion in taxes on exports to Nigeria in 2025.

Cameroon's South West Manyu Cocoa Farmers Exporters Cooperative paid CFA1.03 billion in customs duties and taxes in 2025 on cocoa exported to Nigeria. This amount is an increase from the more than CFA860 million paid in 2024. The cooperative exported approximately 2,200 tons of cocoa to Nigeria in 2025, up from 1,850 tons the previous year.

Chief Tabe Besong Oben Richard, the cooperative's board chairman, presented these figures at a meeting in Limbe on October 28, 2026. The Ministry of Finance provided the cooperative with a certificate acknowledging these tax payments. The National Cocoa and Coffee Board (NCCB) data supports the cooperative's reported export activity.

The cooperative's export volumes to Nigeria rose by 18.9% in 2025. This increase in exports was accompanied by higher reported customs and tax payments. The available information does not include a breakdown of the specific duties and taxes paid.

Cameroon secures CFA174.6 billion for Dibamba bridge

French Development Agency, EIB, and EU provide funding for road and bridge reconstruction.

Cameroon has secured CFA174.6 billion in financing for the reconstruction of the Edéa-Douala road and the construction of a second bridge over the Dibamba River. The funding package was presented to Public Works Minister Emmanuel Nganou Djoumessi on November 2, 2026, by a delegation from the French Development Agency (AFD), the European Investment Bank (EIB), and the European Union. This financing covers the road reconstruction, the new bridge, and the rehabilitation of the existing bridge.

The total proposed financing includes €125 million from AFD, €100 million from the EIB, and €39.2 million from the European Union. While the project aims to improve infrastructure, the role of Canadian contractor Magil Construction remains unclear. Magil had signed a contract in December 2019 for a project that included a second bridge, but budget issues later limited financing to road works. Magil submitted a new financing proposal for the bridge in February 2026, but the government has not confirmed if it is still being considered.

The funding package totals €264.2 million, with the remaining amount to be covered. The reconstruction of the Edéa-Douala section of National Highway N3 is a key component of the project. The government has not yet clarified Magil Construction's involvement in the bridge construction despite their previous contract and a recent financing proposal.

Why it mattersCameroonian infrastructure development and the role of international financial institutions are affected.

PlaYce Cameroun raises capital by CFA2.19 billion

The shopping mall operator increased its share capital by 14.5% to CFA17.285 billion after issuing new shares.

PlaYce Cameroun has increased its share capital by CFA2.19 billion, raising it from CFA15.095 billion to CFA17.285 billion. The company's board confirmed the transaction on September 28, 2026, following approval at a June 30 meeting. This capital increase represents 14.5% of the company's previous share capital and involved issuing 219,007 new shares at CFA10,000 each, all paid for in cash.

The legal notice, prepared by notary Gisèle Enguélé Dipanda, states that the shares were fully paid in cash. Article 7 of the company's bylaws concerning share capital was amended, and documents were filed with the Douala-Bonanjo Court of First Instance. The notice does not identify who subscribed to the new shares or the post-transaction ownership structure.

As of March 31, 2026, French holding company PlaYce SAS, part of the CFAO group, owned 99.99% of PlaYce Cameroun, according to its annual financial statements. This ownership figure predates the recent capital increase.

Cameroon police arrest fake presidential security agent

Authorities seized firearms, military uniforms, and fake presidential passes from two individuals in Yaoundé.

National gendarmerie in Yaoundé arrested two men in separate operations, seizing two firearms. Colonel Désiré Joseph Nguele, commander of the Centre Gendarmerie Legion, coordinated the arrests.

In Ebom, a 39-year-old man was arrested for posing as a Directorate of Presidential Security (DSP) agent to scam people with false recruitment promises. Investigators found a pistol, military uniforms, and fake presidential passes at his home.

A second suspect was apprehended in the Carrière neighborhood with a German-made revolver and six cartridges. He admitted to past robberies in the Moungo department. Both men are in custody as investigations continue.

Why it mattersTwo individuals were arrested in Yaoundé, one impersonating a presidential security agent to commit fraud, and the other found with a firearm and linked to past robberies.
