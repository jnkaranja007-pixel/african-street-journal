Cameroon Ministry Invites SMEs to Indonesian Trade Expo

The Ministry of Small and Medium-sized Enterprises is encouraging businesses to explore opportunities at the 41st Trade Expo Indonesia.

Cameroonian businesses are invited to participate in the 41st Trade Expo Indonesia 2026, scheduled from October 14 to 18 in Tangerang, near Jakarta. The Ministry of Small and Medium-sized Enterprises, Social Economy and Handicrafts (MINPMEESA) issued the invitation on August 31, targeting SME promoters, professional associations, and corporations interested in the Indonesian market.

The annual event, organized by Indonesia’s Ministry of Trade, aims to connect international buyers with Indonesian exporters and suppliers. It features exhibitions across five product zones: food, beverages and agriculture; manufactured goods; industrial estates; fashion and crafts; and furniture and home decor. The program also includes business-matching sessions, trade missions, seminars, and official delegation meetings.

Interested Cameroonian economic operators can register via the official Trade Expo Indonesia platform or contact the Economic Function of the Indonesian Embassy in Yaounde. The expo provides a platform for importers, manufacturers, investors, and trade institutions to engage in international commerce and explore new business ventures.

Why it mattersCameroonian SMEs are affected by the opportunity to explore new business and trade possibilities in Indonesia through participation in the Trade Expo.

Cameroon Agriculture Growth Slows to 1.1% as Export Sector Contracts

Industrial and export farming declined 3.1% in 2025, erasing previous momentum despite overall agricultural value reaching CFA3.97 trillion.

Cameroon's agricultural sector experienced a significant slowdown in 2025, with overall growth falling to 1.1% from 5.2% the previous year. This deceleration was primarily driven by a contraction in industrial and export agriculture, which declined by 3.1% after experiencing 9.5% growth in 2024. The National Institute of Statistics' 2025 National Accounts, released in August 2026, attribute this reversal to the performance of these specific sub-sectors.

Despite the slump in production activity for export crops, agricultural export earnings saw an increase of 8.8%. This rise is attributed to higher prices for several major crops. In contrast, food-crop agriculture demonstrated resilience, growing by 3.7% in 2025, an improvement from 3.2% in the prior year. The agricultural sector as a whole still generated CFA3.97 trillion in value added, representing approximately 11.5% of Cameroon’s nominal GDP.

The sharp deterioration in the growth rate of industrial and export agriculture significantly impacted the sector's overall momentum. This shift highlights a divergence between export-oriented farming and domestic food production. The data, compiled by the National Institute of Statistics, provides a detailed look at the economic performance of Cameroon's vital agricultural industry during 2025.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the contraction in industrial and export agriculture, which signals reduced output and potential challenges for related businesses and trade.

Yango Ride Trains Drivers, Partners with Cities on Safety

The ride-hailing service confirmed a new driver training and health check event for September 2026 in Cameroon.

Yango Ride, a service of Yango Group, has released its 2024-2025 Regional Safety Report, detailing efforts to enhance passenger and driver safety across Africa. In Cameroon, this includes ongoing partnerships with the municipalities of Douala and Yaoundé. In July 2025, over 100 drivers participated in joint safety training and free health check-ups, fostering dialogue between drivers, city officials, and Yango safety experts.

Building on these initiatives, Yango confirmed a similar event focused on driver safety and well-being will take place in September 2026. The report highlights a regional 38% decrease in dangerous driving incidents, from 13.05 to 8.08 per million trips, attributed to new driving style analysis and speed control technologies. Similar safety measures are now in place for Yango's operations in Cameroon.

The company also reported a decline in post-ride contact complaints across several African markets, with privacy protection technologies playing a role. Yango's 24/7 emergency assistance, available in Cameroon, utilizes trained specialists to respond to urgent requests, aiming for initial contact within five minutes in 90% of global urgent cases. These safety features are integrated before, during, and after every ride.

Why it mattersDrivers in Cameroon are affected by Yango's safety initiatives through training and health checks, aiming to reduce incidents and improve well-being.

Cameroon Diesel Import Costs Surge 31.5%, Widening Consumer Price Gap

The cost of importing diesel into Cameroon rose by 31.5% in two months, creating a wider disparity with the fixed retail price paid by consumers.

Cameroon's diesel import costs climbed to CFA860.10 per liter in September 2026, a significant increase from CFA653.85 in July. This rise has widened the gap between the actual cost of imported fuel and the regulated consumer price of CFA828 per liter in Douala. The escalating import expenses are directly impacting the state's subsidy budget, as indicated by the "state support/reimbursement" line in the official pricing structure.

The adjustment for diesel subsidies nearly tripled over two months, reaching CFA315.47 per liter in September, up from CFA109.22 in July. This surge is primarily attributed to upstream costs within the import supply chain. The average benchmark price, a key component in the pricing formula, increased from CFA487.08 per liter in July to CFA606 in September, contributing the most to the overall rise in import expenses.

Despite the increased import costs, retail prices for fuel products in Douala-Bonabéri remained stable. Gasoline is priced at CFA840 per liter, diesel at CFA828 per liter, and kerosene at CFA350 per liter. The Hydrocarbons Prices Stabilization Fund (CSPH) publishes the official price structure, which reflects the growing difference between the cost of acquiring fuel and the price consumers pay at the pump.

Why it mattersBusinesses and consumers are affected as the rising cost of imported diesel widens the gap with the fixed retail price, increasing state subsidies.

WHO, Global Fund Deepen Cameroon Health Partnership

Officials met September 4, 2026, to bolster collaboration against HIV, tuberculosis, and malaria.

The World Health Organization (WHO) and the Global Fund have strengthened their partnership in Cameroon, focusing on combating HIV, tuberculosis, and malaria. Dr. Magaran Monzon Bagayoko, WHO Representative in Cameroon, met with Osian Jones, Senior Portfolio Manager for the Global Fund in Cameroon and Equatorial Guinea, on September 4, 2026. The meeting aimed to enhance their joint efforts in public health.

Discussions included a review of the Global Fund's country team mission regarding Grant GC8, the WHO's support for the implementation of Grant GC7, and preparations for the upcoming GC8 grant. The Global Fund acknowledged the high quality of collaboration and the WHO's technical assistance to the Ministry of Public Health in managing these grants. The WHO advocated for increased support for Health System Strengthening (HSS) activities.

This reinforced collaboration signifies a robust commitment between the two institutions to accelerate the fight against major diseases and to durably consolidate Cameroon's health system. The partnership is designed to ensure sustained progress in public health initiatives across the nation.

Why it mattersThe WHO and Global Fund are strengthening their partnership to combat HIV, tuberculosis, and malaria, and to bolster the health system in Cameroon.
