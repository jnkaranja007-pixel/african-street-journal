SGS fires Cameroon manager who reported customs fraud

A Swiss inspection group says its internal investigation found employees who allegedly altered duty calculations and issued fake invoices to move money abroad.

SGS, the Swiss inspection and certification group, says an internal investigation at its Cameroon subsidiary uncovered customs fraud. Employees allegedly manipulated duty calculations and issued fake invoices to move funds abroad without importing any goods.

The case began in May 2023, when a whistleblower alerted the group to possible breaches of its Code of Integrity. SGS sent members of its corporate security team to Cameroon, who interviewed employees and reviewed documents, identifying several staff suspected of the misconduct.

The allegations are set out in a November 28, 2025, response to Switzerland's National Contact Point for Responsible Business Conduct, reproduced in a report dated May 5, 2026. The dispute involves a former manager at the subsidiary who was fired after reporting the suspected misconduct.

Why it mattersInvestors dealing with SGS Cameroon face a company under scrutiny over alleged fraud, with its handling of the whistleblower now under review.

Cameroon's debt payments to China Eximbank rise to CFAF 319.8 billion

Principal repayments drove most of the 2025 increase, as interest payments fell 14.8% to CFAF 61.6 billion.

Cameroon paid CFAF 319.8 billion in debt service to the Export-Import Bank of China (China Eximbank) in 2025, up 9.6% from CFAF 291.8 billion a year earlier. The figures come from reports by Cameroon's Autonomous Sinking Fund (CAA), published in September 2025 and March 2026.

Principal repayments rose 17.6% to CFAF 258.2 billion, an increase of CFAF 38.7 billion, while interest payments fell 14.8% to CFAF 61.6 billion. The fall in interest was CFAF 10.7 billion, which did not offset the rise in principal.

China Eximbank received 30.2% of the CFAF 1,060.6 billion Cameroon paid to foreign creditors in 2025, down from 31% of CFAF 942.6 billion in 2024. Overall external debt service rose 12.5%, faster than the growth in payments to the Chinese bank.

Why it mattersCameroon must now find larger principal repayments to the Chinese lender, though its share of foreign debt payments slipped slightly.

MTN Cameroon invests CFAF 12.7 billion

The operator reports completing 78.6% of its 2026 investment plan by November 10.

MTN Cameroon has spent approximately CFAF 12.7 billion on its 2026 investment plan, completing 78.6% of its targets by November 10. The Telecommunications Regulatory Board (ART) disclosed this figure on November 12. This spending is significantly less than the CFAF 64.4 billion in capital expenditure reported by MTN's South African parent company for the first half of 2026, excluding leases.

The discrepancy between the two reported figures may stem from differing scopes of investment plans submitted to ART. The regulator has been reviewing MTN Cameroon's investments since April 2026 to monitor service quality. Meetings in Kribi from July 21 to 23, reviewed on November 12, also addressed network coverage and subscriber services.

The CFAF 12.7 billion figure could represent investments tracked under specific service quality commitments rather than the company's total capital spending, although this explanation remains unconfirmed by ART. The regulator's statement on November 12 did not provide further details on the investment breakdown.

Why it mattersMTN Cameroon's investment in telecommunications infrastructure impacts service quality and network coverage for subscribers.

Parlym wins CFAF 11.14 billion LPG contract in Cameroon

The French engineering group's subsidiary will build a storage and distribution hub in Douala for the state-owned petroleum company.

Douala is set to get a new liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) storage and distribution hub, after Parlym Cameroun, a subsidiary of French engineering group Parlym, won a CFAF 11.14 billion construction contract. The facility will be built for Société camerounaise des dépôts pétroliers (SCDP), Cameroon's state-owned petroleum storage company, under a deal signed on October 29, 2026.

The Public Contracts Regulatory Agency (ARMP) lists the deal as No. 029/M/DG/DTEC/SDPEST/SST/CIPM-SCDP/2026. At a project kickoff meeting in Douala on November 11, SCDP confirmed it had chosen Parlym. The CFAF 11,140,906,208 figure, including taxes, excludes a separate technical assistance contract estimated at CFAF 417.375 million.

The construction award came in CFAF 93.1 million below the CFAF 11.234 billion budgeted in the international tender issued on August 17, 2026. Investir au Cameroun had estimated the two contracts together at CFAF 11.65 billion in September, before either was awarded.

Why it mattersTraders and gas distributors who depend on LPG supply in Douala will see the new hub's capacity and timing shaped by this contract.

Fecafoot absent from Jean Manga Onguéné's funeral in Yaoundé

The football federation did not attend the ceremonies held from Friday 13 to Saturday 14 November 2026 for one of the sport's pioneers.

The Cameroonian Football Federation (Fecafoot) was absent from the funeral of Jean Manga Onguéné, held in the capital, Yaoundé, from Friday 13 to Saturday 14 November 2026.

Manga Onguéné was one of the leading figures in the history of Cameroonian football, a member of the generation of pioneers that made the sport a source of national pride, according to the commentary. The article describes his career as having helped put Cameroon on the African football map.

The writer argues that a national federation should stand beside those who built the sport, not only appear when victories and cameras are present. The piece also says Samuel Eto'o's presidency of the federation gives the question an institutional dimension.

Why it mattersFormer players and their families now face a public record that the federation did not mark the funeral of a pioneer who represented the country.
