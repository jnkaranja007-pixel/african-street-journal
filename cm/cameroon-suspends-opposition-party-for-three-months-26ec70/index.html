Cameroon suspends opposition party for three months

Four opposition parties condemn the decision by the Minister of Territorial Administration, Paul Atanga Nji.

Source: Journal du Cameroun · 2026-10-30 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Cameroon's Minister of Territorial Administration, Paul Atanga Nji, suspended the Front for the National Salvation of Cameroon (FSNC) for three months on October 27. This decision, made one year after presidential elections and three months before legislative and municipal polls, has drawn condemnation from several opposition parties.

The UDC, PCRN, MANIDEM, and MP3 parties have voiced their disapproval, with the MP3 calling the suspension a serious hindrance to political freedoms and an attack on pluralism. MANIDEM suggests the ruling RDPC party uses authoritarian measures to secure electoral victories, while the UDC questions the timing and purpose of the suspension.

The PCRN urged the minister to reconsider the decision, stating that the country needs appeasement, not heightened tensions. The MP3 called for the Prime Minister to intervene in what they described as a political act, highlighting the FSNC's alleged violation of the republic's laws.

Why it mattersOpposition parties in Cameroon are concerned that the suspension of the FSNC will hinder political freedoms and affect upcoming elections.
