Supreme Court president denounces judicial arbitrary in Equatorial Guinea

Francisco Evuy Nguema highlights how court orders are ignored when they clash with the powerful.

Source: Diario Rombe · 2026-10-24 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

The president of Equatorial Guinea's Supreme Court of Justice, Francisco Evuy Nguema, has denounced the arbitrary application of judicial rulings within the country's penal system. He cited the case of Gregorio Mesian Nseme Ada, known as Goyo, whose judicial orders were disregarded at Black Beach prison. This situation illustrates how powerful individuals can override legal decisions.

Evuy Nguema stated that judicial sentences and release orders can become meaningless when they conflict with the will of those in power. The case of Goyo is presented as a severe example of this arbitrariness, revealing the vulnerability of the system when its own mechanisms are undermined. The report suggests this signals a potential unraveling of authority.

The denunciation by the Supreme Court president indicates a fracture within the country's power structures. The report implies that such disregard for legal processes by those in authority exposes their own weaknesses. This event highlights a struggle between judicial independence and executive power in Equatorial Guinea.

Why it mattersThe arbitrary application of judicial rulings by powerful individuals in Equatorial Guinea affects the citizens subjected to these rulings.
