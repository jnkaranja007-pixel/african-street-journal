Equatorial Guinea plans museums for archaeological finds

Government to establish sites in Malabo, Bata, and La Ciudad de la Paz to house artifacts dating from 50 BC to 1200 AD.

Source: Ahora EG · 2026-11-09 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Equatorial Guinea's government plans to build museums in Malabo, Bata, and La Ciudad de la Paz to preserve national archaeological heritage. The decision follows a meeting where officials presented findings from Corisco island, including 12 boxes of materials and certified pottery pieces. These artifacts, recovered from excavations that uncovered one of Central Africa's oldest necropolises, date between 50 BC and 1200 AD.

Miguel Mba Obiang, director general of Cultural Centers, Libraries, and Image, confirmed the artifacts are held at the Ecuatoguinean Cultural Center. The discoveries include iron tools, spears, jewelry, and ceramics, offering insights into daily life and metallurgy before colonization. Vice President Nguema Obiang Mangue emphasized the need for secure exhibition spaces to allow public access to the country's cultural patrimony.

The government also intends to tour the nation to identify and purchase antiquities from private owners, subject to expert evaluation. These initiatives aim to strengthen the recovery and conservation of Equatorial Guinea's historical and archaeological assets, preventing their loss from the country and promoting historical knowledge among younger generations.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guineans will gain access to historical artifacts and learn more about their ancestors' past through new museums.
