Malabo faces food inflation and power cuts as year-end demand rises

October inflation crossed the CEMAC threshold, while outages are cutting business income and making food and seasonings more expensive.

Source: Real Equatorial Guinea · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Equatorial Guinea is entering the year-end period with rising food prices and unstable electricity in Malabo, problems that could continue into early 2027. The country's October inflation rate crossed the threshold set by the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC), according to figures published by the national statistics body, which Real Equatorial Guinea identifies as the state statistics institute.

Power interruptions are affecting businesses and, in turn, family incomes, Real Equatorial Guinea reported. Vice-President Teodoro Nguema Obiang Mangué urged SEGESA, the state electricity company, to resolve the cuts or have its management resign. The report said it was not known when supply would stabilise. Food and seasonings have also become more expensive, making meals at home costlier.

The Government has tasked the Ministry of Trade with studying how to address the price increases, and its response is awaited. In previous years, the Government bought basic goods abroad for local wholesalers to sell at lower prices as demand rose in November and December.

Why it mattersMalabo businesses losing income during power interruptions affect family earnings, while households face rising food and seasoning costs.
