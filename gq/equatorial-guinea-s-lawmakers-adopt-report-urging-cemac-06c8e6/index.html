Equatorial Guinea's lawmakers adopt report urging CEMAC states to pay dues

The report says some members failed to pay the bloc's main revenue levy in 2025, hampering its institutions and community projects.

Source: Real Equatorial Guinea · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Equatorial Guinea's Chamber of Deputies adopted a report on the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC) on Wednesday, 25 November. The document reviews the regional parliament's ordinary session, held in Malabo from 2 February to 3 March 2026, and says funding shortfalls and unpaid member-state contributions obstruct the bloc's work.

Crispín Engono Nguema, the community deputy who presented the report, said some states did not pay the Community Integration Tax in 2025. The levy is CEMAC's main revenue source, and the shortfall hampers regional projects and institutional operations. Community representatives agreed to run a campaign urging member states to pay their contributions.

The report also cites incomplete reforms, reliance on oil and difficulties completing regional integration. The assembly unanimously backed a resolution on fully implementing decisions of the Central African Economic Union's Council of Ministers.

Why it mattersMember states that missed their 2025 contributions face a campaign urging payment, as the shortfall hampers CEMAC projects and institutions.
