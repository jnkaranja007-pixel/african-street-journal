Three children die in Basupú del Oeste house fire

A resident tried to rescue the minors but was prevented by flames.

A fire in Basupú del Oeste on Wednesday, November 11, killed three children in a semi-permanent house. The blaze started around seven in the evening and completely consumed the dwelling, which was divided into three sections each with a different owner. Residents could not save any belongings.

One resident told Ahora EG that she heard the children screaming as she escaped. She attempted to reach them but the intense flames blocked her path. The house lacked electricity, and residents were using a homemade lamp called a 'Bakoko', made from a bottle with kerosene and a wick, for light. This lamp reportedly fell onto a mattress, spilling kerosene and igniting the fire.

The youngest victim was a baby, and one of his siblings was six years old. The fire spread rapidly through the three sections of the house. All residents lost everything they owned in the fire.

Why it mattersThree children died in a house fire on November 11 after a homemade lamp ignited a mattress.

Equatorial Guinea trains survey agents to count informal border trade

Teams will collect data at land crossings with Cameroon and Gabon, where much trade is missing from customs records.

The National Statistics Institute (INEGE) has begun training field agents to measure informal cross-border trade in Equatorial Guinea. Much of this trade does not appear in the customs administration's records. The survey is part of a project running at the same time in all six members of the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC), funded by the World Bank and coordinated by the CEMAC Commission.

Ricardo Nsue Ndemesogo Obono, INEGE's director general, presented the project's objectives at the event and said reliable, complete data is needed to understand the country's foreign trade. INEGE leads the work with the Customs Directorate and the Directorate General of Foreign Trade. The training covers methods for fieldwork, identifying trade movements and ensuring data quality.

Once training ends, the teams will travel to the border posts to begin collecting information. Surveys will focus on the land borders with Cameroon and Gabon, especially at Ebibeyin, Micomiseng, Río Campo and Kogo. The government says the data will help it judge how large informal trade is in the national economy.

Why it mattersTraders and officials at Ebibeyin, Micomiseng, Río Campo and Kogo will be counted for the first time in national trade statistics.

Equatorial Guinea finance and tourism ministries review national lottery plan

A preliminary feasibility report on creating a national lottery was examined at a joint meeting of the two ministries on Wednesday, 11 November.

The ministries of Finance and Tourism in Equatorial Guinea held a working meeting to analyse a preliminary feasibility report on creating a National Lottery. The session was chaired by the Minister Delegate of Finance, Milagrosa Obono Angue, alongside the Secretary of State for Information, Press and Tourism, María Milagrosa Nchama, and the Vice Minister of Finance for Tax Administration, José Domingo Ndong Bakale.

Officials examined the legal framework, the economic implications and the tax obligations the system would bring. Obono Angue proposed a joint interdepartmental technical commission to study the project further, review its components and recommend changes to fit national priorities. Tax and state contracting directors also joined the discussion.

The technical commission will also examine and validate the final feasibility report. Whether the lottery goes ahead depends on that evaluation, which will determine if the proposal meets the legal, economic and fiscal conditions needed for implementation.

Why it mattersThe national lottery would bring new tax obligations and state revenue, and it will only proceed if the technical commission validates the final feasibility report.

Obiang asks Macron to help settle property dispute with France

Equatorial Guinea's president also proposed a three-way gas pipeline alliance with Nigeria and France.

President Obiang Nguema Mbasogo of Equatorial Guinea met French President Emmanuel Macron in Paris and asked for Macron's personal involvement in finding a solution to a dispute over a property that has concerned both governments. Obiang said he valued Macron's willingness to find a satisfactory outcome that would spare either side humiliation or loss of dignity.

Obiang also told Macron about a planned gas pipeline between Nigeria and Equatorial Guinea and proposed a three-way alliance with Nigeria and France to mobilise the financial, technical and business capacity needed. He also asked France for teaching equipment and materials for the Tika Naval School, and invited Macron to visit Equatorial Guinea.

The meeting was reported by Ahora EG and Real Equatorial Guinea, which said Obiang had met Richel Bassila, regional director for Central Africa of Sogea-Satom, in Paris a day earlier.

Why it mattersA French agreement to join the pipeline would bring Nigerian and French capital and engineering into a project that would link two Gulf of Guinea energy markets.
