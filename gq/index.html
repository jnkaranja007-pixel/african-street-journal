Equatorial Guinea approves first phase of Oyala prefab factory rehabilitation

The government will fund repairs to the plant so that national materials can supply 60 buildings planned for the Ciudad de la Paz from 2027.

The government of Equatorial Guinea has given the green light to the first phase of rehabilitating the Oyala prefab factory, after a meeting on Monday, November 9, at the People's Palace in Malabo. The meeting was chaired by Vice President Nguema Obiang Mangue, with the prime minister and officials from the public works, finance, treasury, electricity and mines ministries taking part.

The supervising company TEAM presented a report on the bids received and the work needed to restore the factory's operating capacity. The vice president authorised repairs to the concrete prefab facilities, the areas making blocks, tiles and balusters, the crusher and the quarry. A laboratory will also be set up to check material quality before it is used on sites, with the aim of cutting transport costs and securing supply for projects in the new administrative capital.

For the second phase, covering management and operation of the factory, Nguema Obiang Mangue has instructed the prime minister and the relevant ministries to renegotiate the economic terms proposed by the GICI group to secure a more favourable deal for the state. Once the contract is signed, the government plans to start building the first 60 buildings in the Ciudad de la Paz in 2027.

Why it mattersThe state will pay for repairs and cut transport costs, while construction firms bidding for the 60 Ciudad de la Paz buildings will depend on the factory's output.

Malian man dies after falling from Malabo Gendarmerie window

Mamadu Maiga, detained in the Bettomax betting probe, died after a fall from a window in the National Gendarmerie in Malabo on Saturday night.

Malian citizen Mamadu Maiga died after allegedly falling from a window at the National Gendarmerie headquarters in Malabo on the night of Saturday, November 7, at around 21:30. He had been detained in connection with an investigation into the sports betting company Bettomax, after being summoned for questioning on Friday, November 6.

Rufino Ndong Esono Nchama, a former minister of culture and tourism who was held in the same case, said he saw Maiga try to climb out of a window of their shared room and shouted for help before Maiga jumped. Ndong Esono Nchama said Maiga seemed very frightened and restless, and that he did not witness any physical violence against him. Officers searched the area near the port, where Maiga's body was later found.

Maiga's brother, Colaba, said he last saw him on Saturday when he brought him food. He said they spoke normally and he saw no signs of physical violence, adding that he asked about his health and his children during the visit.

Why it mattersMaiga's death places a detention case under scrutiny and leaves his family without the man who was being questioned in the probe.

Equatorial Guinea asks UNESCO prize winners to aid universities

The government wants laureates to share expertise to strengthen national research and innovation.

Equatorial Guinea's government has asked recipients of the UNESCO-Equatorial Guinea Prize for Research in Life Sciences to support the nation's universities. Minister of State for Education, Science, Youth and Sports, Clemente Engonga Nguema Onguene, made the appeal during the prize ceremony in Paris, France.

The request aims to leverage the experience of past laureates to bolster university research, innovation, and scientific development. Onguene specifically mentioned the National University of Equatorial Guinea (UNGE) and the African University of Central Africa (AAUCA) as institutions that could benefit from this shared expertise.

This initiative aligns with the Head of State's goal of promoting inclusive scientific research to reduce technological disparities between nations. The government believes the prize can help bridge this gap, ensuring scientific advancements benefit all peoples globally.

Why it mattersThe government seeks to enhance national scientific research and innovation by engaging international award winners.

Equatorial Guinea wins best natural tourism destination award

Bruselas awarded the country for its natural tourism potential at a global tourism forum.

Equatorial Guinea received the Best Natural Tourism Destination award in Brussels, Belgium, on November 9 or 10, 2026. Ambassador Miguel Oyono Ndong Mifumu accepted the prize from Balut Bagci, president of the World Tourism Forum Institute. The ceremony included diplomats and tourism agencies from various continents.

During the event, Ambassador Mifumu thanked organizers and highlighted Equatorial Guinea's natural resources and economic development opportunities in tourism. The international meeting, held November 9-10 in Brussels, aimed to promote tourism investment and sustainable development.

This award is expected to boost Equatorial Guinea's international profile as a tourist destination. It aims to increase visibility for its natural attractions among global tourism operators and investors.

Why it mattersThe award aims to increase international visibility for Equatorial Guinea's natural attractions among operators and investors.

Equatorial Guinea attends TRT World Forum in Turkey

Minister Juan José Ekuna Esono Mangué led the nation's delegation at the Istanbul event discussing global challenges.

Equatorial Guinea participated in the tenth TRT World Forum 2026, an international meeting in Istanbul, Turkey. The forum, organized by the Turkish Radio and Television Corporation (TRT), gathered government representatives, media leaders, academics, and experts to discuss global challenges.

The delegation was headed by Juan José Ekuna Esono Mangué, the Minister of Information, Press, Culture, and Tourism. His presence allowed for close monitoring of debates and exchange of perspectives with international communication professionals. The forum's theme was 'A world without a center. Who leads when the rules stop working?'.

Discussions over two days covered the transformation of the international order, geopolitics, energy crises, international law, the global economy, and artificial intelligence's influence. The event also addressed emerging powers, the humanitarian situation in Gaza, and technological challenges for states and citizens.

Why it mattersThe Minister of Information, Press, Culture, and Tourism attended an international forum discussing global issues, offering a chance for communication professionals to exchange perspectives.
