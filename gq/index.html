Health · 2026-11-17

Chinese hospital ship offers free medical care in Bata

The Jixiang Fangzhou, known as the Ark of Good Omen, will offer consultations, diagnostics and surgeries to about 600 people a day until November 26.

The Chinese hospital ship Jixiang Fangzhou, known as the Ark of Good Omen, will dock in Bata on Thursday, November 19, and stay until November 26. The visit is part of the Harmony Mission, a health cooperation initiative led by the People's Republic of China to give free medical services to the public.

Patient care starts on Saturday, November 21, with capacity for about 600 people a day in two shifts of 300, morning and afternoon. Chinese specialists will offer consultations, check-ups, clinical analyses, diagnostic tests and treatment for various illnesses. Surgery will depend on each patient's condition and an earlier medical assessment, and priority attention goes to people and families with limited means.

Visiting specialists will also meet medical staff for knowledge exchange and visit the Faculty of Medicine and Nursing Sciences of the National University of Equatorial Guinea (UNGE) in Bata, where students will receive training on sexually transmitted infections and new medical techniques.

Why it mattersPatients from families with limited means who struggle to pay for certain treatments can see specialists at no cost during the visit.

Sport · 2026-11-17

Nzalang Nacional loses to Zimbabwe, complicates CAN 2027 qualification

Equatorial Guinea's national team suffered a 3-0 defeat against Zimbabwe in Harare, impacting their chances for the Africa Cup of Nations.

The Equatorial Guinean national team, led by Italian coach Vincenzo Alberto Annese, lost 3-0 to Zimbabwe on Monday in Harare. This defeat in the fourth round of qualification matches for the Africa Cup of Nations (CAN) 2027 complicates their aspirations in Group E.

Zimbabwe took an early lead in the 4th minute through Thandolwenkosi Ngwenya. Despite efforts from Cristian Makaté, Santiago Eneme, and Rony Mbomio, the Nzalang Nacional failed to score. Zimbabwean goalkeeper Nelson Chadya made key saves in the first half.

Before this match, Equatorial Guinea and Zimbabwe shared four points in Group E, with the Democratic Republic of Congo leading with nine points. The Nzalang Nacional must now win their upcoming matches to keep their hopes of qualifying for the CAN 2027 finals alive.

CEMAC meets in Malabo to propose governance and funding reforms

Officials, experts and Technical Secretariat staff are reviewing how powers, programmes and resources are shared across the regional body.

Officials, experts and representatives of the Technical Secretariat of the Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC) are meeting in Malabo to examine problems in how powers are divided, how programmes are carried out and how much money is available. The meeting is chaired by Mbasogo Kung Nguidang, who also heads the Steering Committee.

In her opening speech, Mbasogo Kung Nguidang said the sessions should build on the issues examined from 26 to 28 October in Malabo and agree technical positions that can become concrete measures. The agenda covers four areas: governance, financial resources, human resources, and monitoring and evaluation. On finance, she stressed the need to guarantee the effective transfer of the Community Integration Fee (TCI), on which institutional budgets depend, and warned that uncertainty over funding requires collective responses.

The proposals draw on studies by the AUDITEC-FOIRIER Consulting and KPMG consortium and on the 2006-2010 Institutional Reform Programme. Conclusions must record agreed points, reservations and matters needing decisions from competent bodies, with recommendations ranked by priority, legal viability and financial impact. The distribution of posts subject to mandate has deadlines set for December.

Why it mattersInstitutions depend on the Community Integration Fee (TCI), so its effective transfer determines whether their budgets and activities can be carried out, and the reform file has deadlines set for December.

Education · 2026-11-17

Equatorial Guinea seeks training deal with Singapore Technological Institute

Its minister proposed a memorandum of understanding as the first step towards training professionals in oil, mining and digital skills.

Equatorial Guinea is seeking a professional training agreement with the Singapore Technological Institute during an official visit to Singapore by the vice president. Simeón Oyono Esono Angué, minister of state for foreign affairs, international cooperation and the diaspora, who led the delegation, proposed signing a memorandum of understanding as a first step, and the institute's officials said they were willing to share their educational and technological experience.

The minister set out the training the country needs, citing its natural resources and educational infrastructure. In hydrocarbons, the government is interested in oil production operations, the maintenance of oil facilities and the running of liquefied natural gas plants. The proposal also covers mining exploration and equipment maintenance, industrial refrigeration, electricity, industrial welding, and civil aviation skills such as piloting and aircraft maintenance.

On digital skills, the minister proposed training in cybersecurity, mobile application development and content creation, as well as technology laboratories and support for start-ups through incubators and accelerators. The institute said it works with about 35 countries and offers more than 40 courses across five faculties, including mechanical, civil and electrical engineering, robotics and applied artificial intelligence.

Why it mattersYoung Equatoguineans seeking skills in oil, gas and aviation would gain access to Singapore-trained instructors and courses if the memorandum is signed.

Politics · 2026-11-17

Equatorial Guinea to host 2027 assembly of CPLP prosecutors general

The Lusophone bloc's prosecutors voted nine to none to give the country the XXIV Assembly, which it will preside over and organise.

Equatorial Guinea will host the XXIV Assembly of Prosecutors General of the Community of Portuguese Language Countries (CPLP) and of Macao's prosecutor in 2027. The decision was taken on November 11 at the XXIII Assembly in Macao, held from November 9 to 14, 2026, after a process that began five years ago.

Anatolio Nzang Nguema, the country's Prosecutor General, presented the candidacy to the plenary. The prosecutors general voted by consensus, nine in favour and none against, after examining the bid dossier. The country will preside over and organise the next assembly.

Nzang Nguema returned home on Monday, November 16, after taking part in the Macao meeting. The outlet describes the win as consolidating the country's role in international legal cooperation.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guinea must now preside over and organise the 2027 assembly, a role that puts its prosecutors at the centre of the bloc's legal cooperation.
