GETESA faces $432,270 debt to MTN over international roaming

Diario Rombe reports that the state telecom operator faces roaming cuts and possible court seizures over unpaid debt to the South African company.

Equatorial Guinea’s state telecom operator GETESA owes MTN $432,270, according to Diario Rombe, which reported the debt on Tuesday. The outlet said the unpaid amount threatens international roaming services used by citizens abroad.

Citing sources close to GETESA’s new management, Diario Rombe reported that the company faces possible interruptions to roaming, inclusion on global debtors’ registers and threatened court seizures. MTN is a South African telecommunications company.

The report describes GETESA as facing a severe financial and operational crisis. It does not provide a date for any roaming cut or court action.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guineans abroad who rely on GETESA roaming face possible service interruptions, while the company also faces threatened court seizures over the $432,270 debt.

CEMAC reviews Malabo proposals to reform its institutions

The Central African bloc is examining governance, funding, staffing and oversight, with deadlines for a mandate-based post review set for December.

The Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC) is reviewing proposals in Malabo to improve governance and financing across its institutions. The sessions bring together institutional officials, experts and representatives of its Technical Secretariat to examine problems in the division of responsibilities, programme delivery and access to resources.

Mbasogo Kung Nguidang, the steering committee’s president, said the discussions should build on issues examined in Malabo from 26 to 28 October and produce practical measures for future reform decisions. The agenda covers governance, finances, human resources, and monitoring and evaluation. The minister said CEMAC institutions depend on effective transfers of the Community Integration Tax to fund budgets and activities, while uncertainty over financing requires a collective response.

A review of posts subject to mandates is to follow its scheduled stages, with deadlines in December. The conclusions will record agreements, reservations and matters needing decisions by competent bodies; recommendations are to be prioritised and assessed for legal feasibility and financial implications. They will inform the next steering committee session.

Why it mattersCEMAC institutions rely on transfers of the Community Integration Tax for their budgets and activities, while the review of mandate-based posts has deadlines in December.

Equatorial Guinea signs training memorandum with Singapore institute

The agreement sets a basis for future education cooperation after the country proposed training in energy, mining, aviation and digital skills.

Equatorial Guinea and the Singapore Institute of Technology signed a memorandum of understanding on Tuesday, 17 November, to lay the groundwork for future education cooperation. The signing came during an official visit to Singapore by the country's vice-president.

Simeón Oyono Esono Angué, minister of state for foreign affairs, cooperation and the diaspora, who led the delegation, had proposed the memorandum as a first step towards a fuller agreement. He outlined training needs in oil and gas production, plant maintenance, mining equipment, aircraft piloting and maintenance, and cybersecurity.

The institute said it was willing to share its education and technology experience. It works with about 35 countries and offers more than 40 programmes across five faculties.

Equatorial Guinea wins bid to host 2027 prosecutors' assembly

The Portuguese-speaking community's prosecutors approved the country's candidacy by consensus, with nine votes in favour and none against, on 11 November.

Equatorial Guinea will preside over and organise the twenty-fourth Assembly of Attorneys General of the Community of Portuguese Language Countries (CPLP) and the Prosecutor of the Macao Special Administrative Region (RAEM) in 2027, according to Ahora EG. Attorney General Anatolio Nzang Nguema presented the bid to the plenary at the twenty-third assembly in Macao, held from 9 to 14 November 2026.

The prosecutors and the RAEM prosecutor reached their decision on 11 November, after reviewing the country's candidacy memorandum. Real Equatorial Guinea reported that Nzang Nguema returned home on Monday 16 November.

The bid process began five years ago. Nzang Nguema stressed the country's growing role in the community's legal cooperation activities, the outlet reported. The 2027 meeting was originally scheduled to take place on the date set out in the assembly's programme.

Sport · 2026-11-18

Zimbabwe beats Equatorial Guinea 3-0 in Group E qualifier

The result leaves Nzalang Nacional under pressure in the race for places at the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations, after the teams had drawn 0-0.

Zimbabwe beat Equatorial Guinea 3-0 in Harare on Monday in the fourth round of Group E qualifying for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. The defeat leaves Equatorial Guinea needing results in its remaining matches to keep its qualification hopes alive.

Thandolwenkosi Ngwenya put the hosts ahead in the fourth minute at the National Sports Stadium. Equatorial Guinea created chances through Cristian Makaté, Santiago Eneme and Rony Mbomio, but Zimbabwe goalkeeper Nelson Chadya made important first-half saves, Ahora EG reported.

Before the match, Equatorial Guinea and Zimbabwe each had four points, while the Democratic Republic of Congo led the group with nine. The sides had drawn 0-0 on the 12th of the month; the tournament’s final stage is scheduled for Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guinea must respond in its remaining Group E matches to keep its place in contention for the 2027 tournament.
