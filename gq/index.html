News · 2026-10-24

fire kills six in malabo family

Salvador Nsue Nguema lost his wife, three children, and two grandchildren in the blaze.

A fire in the Semu-Salomé neighborhood of Malabo on Wednesday evening killed six members of Salvador Nsue Nguema's family. The victims included his wife, three of their children, and two grandchildren.

Nsue Nguema, the sole survivor, was hospitalized with burns. The family was using candles for light after a power outage, and investigators believe a candle may have started the fire. Security bars on windows and doors hindered rescue efforts.

The fire destroyed the family's belongings, and Nsue Nguema is seeking assistance. This tragedy echoes a previous incident in Malabo where Cosme Nguema Ondo lost his wife and six children to a collapsing wall.

Why it mattersSalvador Nsue Nguema and his family were directly affected by the fire.

Equatorial Guinea updates 2006 textbooks

A commission of over 90 sub-commissions will review and update educational materials to reflect the country's changes.

Equatorial Guinea's Ministry of Education is updating and reprinting teaching manuals last revised in 2006. Vice President Nguema Obiang Mangue presided over a meeting in Malabo where Minister of State Clemente Engonga Nguema Onguene explained the project's progress. A technical working commission with more than 90 sub-commissions is reviewing the books to correct errors and update content, incorporating teacher feedback.

The updated manuals will reflect changes in Equatorial Guinea over the past 20 years, including information on municipalities, urban districts, history, culture, and traditions. The Vice President authorized the editorial representatives to work with ministry technicians on revisions. He also instructed the Prime Minister to ensure other implicated ministries submit pending information to prevent further delays.

Two workshops will be held in Malabo and Bata to analyze commission reports and consolidate observations into a single document for the publisher. The goal is to ensure the new textbooks accurately represent the country's evolution.

Why it mattersStudents will receive updated textbooks reflecting changes in the country's history, culture, and administration.

Avante Talent program graduates 2026

The AVANTE agency in Malabo awarded certificates to students completing a six-month professional internship.

AVANTE's Malabo office celebrated the conclusion of its first AVANTE TALENTO internship program on October 19, 2026. The six-month program, a collaboration with BANGE Business School, provided students practical experience in communication, marketing, and media.

Participants worked with AVANTE teams, learning industry processes and demands to supplement their academic studies. AVANTE General Director Sergio Fernando Sehe Orichi praised the interns' commitment and evolution, emphasizing the value of such opportunities for young professionals.

The program aimed to bridge the gap between education and business needs, fostering talent development in Equatorial Guinea. AVANTE plans to continue the AVANTE TALENTO initiative, strengthening its ties with educational institutions.

Why it mattersYoung people in Equatorial Guinea gain practical skills and professional experience through the AVANTE TALENTO program.

Supreme Court president denounces judicial arbitrary in Equatorial Guinea

Francisco Evuy Nguema highlights how court orders are ignored when they clash with the powerful.

The president of Equatorial Guinea's Supreme Court of Justice, Francisco Evuy Nguema, has denounced the arbitrary application of judicial rulings within the country's penal system. He cited the case of Gregorio Mesian Nseme Ada, known as Goyo, whose judicial orders were disregarded at Black Beach prison. This situation illustrates how powerful individuals can override legal decisions.

Evuy Nguema stated that judicial sentences and release orders can become meaningless when they conflict with the will of those in power. The case of Goyo is presented as a severe example of this arbitrariness, revealing the vulnerability of the system when its own mechanisms are undermined. The report suggests this signals a potential unraveling of authority.

The denunciation by the Supreme Court president indicates a fracture within the country's power structures. The report implies that such disregard for legal processes by those in authority exposes their own weaknesses. This event highlights a struggle between judicial independence and executive power in Equatorial Guinea.

Why it mattersThe arbitrary application of judicial rulings by powerful individuals in Equatorial Guinea affects the citizens subjected to these rulings.

Health · 2026-10-24

Bata hosts free eye surgery campaign

The Ministry of Health and Health Infrastructure is organizing a second free eye surgery campaign in Bata from November 21 to December 3.

Equatorial Guinea's Ministry of Health and Health Infrastructure announced a second free eye surgery campaign in Bata. The initiative, in partnership with the AL-BASAR International Foundation, will run from November 21 to December 3, 2026, at the Doctor Damián Roku Epitie Monanga University Regional Hospital.

During the campaign, residents can receive free eye consultations, evaluations, treatments, surgeries, and follow-up care for conditions like cataracts. Interested individuals can register at ODEPAC services in Malabo's General Hospital of Sampaka and Bata's University Regional Hospital.

The ministry provided the phone number 222 233 844 for more information. This second edition, under the slogan "Recovering sight, renewing hope: a commitment that continues," aims to continue providing free ophthalmological services to the population.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guineans in Bata will receive free eye surgeries and treatments for conditions like cataracts.
