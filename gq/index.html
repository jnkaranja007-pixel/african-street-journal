Malabo drivers face fuel queues again

Long lines of vehicles at TotalEnergies in Semu signal potential new fuel shortages.

Drivers in Malabo are again lining up at petrol stations, raising concerns about a potential new fuel crisis. Restrictions on how much fuel can be purchased are also in place. At the TotalEnergies station in Semu, vehicle queues have caused traffic jams, with drivers and traffic police working to manage the flow.

This situation mirrors fuel supply issues seen in July and October of this year, which led to long waits and difficulties obtaining fuel. Drivers are calling for better coordination and communication from authorities to announce which stations will have fuel, when, and in what quantities. "We are going from station to station trying our luck, and that also consumes what little we have," said taxi driver Cornelio Rondo.

Amancio Mba Ndong, a civil servant, added that better communication is important during such times, allowing people to plan their days instead of relying on luck and rumors. The current situation is causing drivers to visit multiple stations to find fuel, consuming valuable time and existing fuel reserves.

Why it mattersDrivers in Malabo are experiencing long queues and fuel purchase limits, impacting their daily routines and finances.

Equatorial Guinea trains border staff for African trade zone

A workshop in Malabo aimed to equip customs and trade officials with skills to boost cross-border commerce.

A training seminar for border personnel on the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) concluded in Malabo on October 31. The workshop, supported by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) and the AfCFTA Secretariat, focused on improving the technical abilities of officials involved in trade procedures.

Vice Minister of Trade Avelino Mocache stated that Equatorial Guinea ratified the AfCFTA agreement in 2019 and must now implement it effectively. He emphasized that national institutions and actors need the capacity to understand and apply the agreement's provisions to benefit from African trade integration. Mocache noted this was the first national workshop specifically for border staff, including customs and trade inspectors.

UNDP Resident Representative Betty Wabunoha highlighted the need for efficient customs procedures and trained professionals to ensure products from Equatorial Guinea can reach other African markets. She stressed that the AfCFTA's success depends on effective and barrier-free trade operations, not just the signing of an agreement.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guinea's border officials are being trained to facilitate trade and improve access for national products to other African markets under the AfCFTA.

The telecommunications firm shared insights on artificial intelligence in human resources and business efficiency.

Equatorial Guinea Telecommunications S.A. (GETESA) participated in the "AI Business Malabo 2026" international conference held in Malabo from October 28 to 30. The company's administrative section head, Carolina Obama Elonga, spoke as a panelist on artificial intelligence's role in human resources.

Elonga shared GETESA's views on how AI can boost efficiency, optimize processes, and enhance customer experience and service quality. The conference, organized by the IDENTIC Foundation at KOIA Innovation, aimed to bring practical AI applications to businesses and professionals. GETESA's participation as a sponsor reaffirmed its commitment to the responsible use of technology and digital transformation for improved daily operations.

BEAC denies issuing new CFA franc banknotes

The Bank of Central African States confirmed that images of new banknotes circulating on social media are false.

The Bank of Central African States (BEAC) has denied issuing new 25 to 400 CFA franc banknotes, images of which have been circulating on social media. The institution stated on October 21 that the information was false, according to AhoraEG. The images displayed designs mimicking the security features and appearance of official banknotes.

This false information follows an agreement on October 7 by the Ministerial Committee of the Central African Monetary Union (UMAC) to withdraw old coins. This measure, effective November 1, affects coins issued between 1958 and 1985 across the six CEMAC countries. The BEAC clarified that the coin withdrawal does not involve the issuance of new banknotes.

AhoraEG advises the public to verify such information through official sources before sharing, due to an increase in manipulated images and falsified documents online. The withdrawal of old coins is a separate measure from any potential new banknote issuance.

Why it mattersResidents of Equatorial Guinea could be misled by false information about currency, potentially causing confusion or financial concern.

MYCHIC Project boosts entrepreneurs in Malabo

The initiative focuses on entrepreneurial mindset, financial education, and digital marketing for business growth.

MYCHIC Project held an event in Malabo on October 30, 2026, bringing together entrepreneurs and professionals to discuss business development. The gathering at the Hotel Bisila Palace focused on entrepreneurial mindset, financial education, and digital marketing, offering participants a platform to present projects and network.

The association, driven by Jennifer, aims to create a supportive space for individuals seeking to enhance their capabilities and launch new ventures. Jennifer explained that MYCHIC Project stems from her personal experiences, offering guidance, mutual support, and personal growth as tools to overcome challenges.

MYCHIC Project has members in Equatorial Guinea, Spain, and London, with plans to expand connections among those interested in learning, entrepreneurship, and growth. The event also emphasized self-love and valuing personal capabilities as key to achieving goals.
