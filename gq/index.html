Equatorial Guinea Council Approves Economic and Employment Reforms

The government has advanced new legislation on state budgets, tax identification, and employment programs, alongside international investment agreements.

The 4th Council of Ministers meeting in Equatorial Guinea, led by the Head of State, prioritized national economic and employment agendas. Key decisions included the presentation of two draft laws by the Ministry of Finance and Budget: the State Budgets Settlement for 2025 and draft laws for 2027. The council also discussed establishing a National College of Accounting Experts and a new tax identification number system for individuals and entities.

Further economic measures approved involve the adoption of a National Employment and Training Program and the creation of a National Institute for Employment Promotion. A decree was also debated to foster formal employment and reform social security contracting rules for new hires. In the energy sector, the pricing structure for refined products was reviewed. Additionally, agreements were signed with Zimbabwe for investment promotion, reciprocal visa exemptions for diplomatic passport holders, and economic and trade cooperation.

The Ministry of Health and Social Welfare reported on the status of Universal Medical Insurance. International relations were further addressed through the reform of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' organic regulations and the ratification of global agreements on marine biodiversity and biotechnology safety. The Prime Minister will present a synthesis report on previous inter-ministerial councils and review the execution of prior decisions.

Why it mattersThe council's decisions on economic policy, employment programs, and international investment agreements directly impact businesses and the national economy.

Equatorial Guinea Law Guarantees Teleworker Right to Disconnect

New labor law amendments establish telework and mandate employers create policies to ensure workers are not contacted during non-work hours.

The government of Equatorial Guinea has revised its General Labor Law to incorporate teleworking and address modern labor realities. A key change, introduced in Article 9 bis, defines telework as work performed using Information and Communication Technologies outside the usual workplace. This reform aims to align the legal framework with recent transformations in the world of work, ensuring that telework respects rights related to working time, health and safety, and digital disconnection.

A significant aspect of the telework regulation is the guaranteed right to disconnect. Employers are now obligated to establish internal policies outlining guidelines for this right. These policies must ensure teleworkers are not required to respond to communications or orders from employers during their designated rest periods. The law also protects workers from discrimination, whether they choose to telework or revert to in-office roles.

The amendments seek to adapt labor legislation to new work organization methods while safeguarding the rights of both employees and employers. The flexibility introduced allows for agreements on daily rest, weekly rest, and annual leave, provided business productivity is maintained. This update is intended to provide a specific legal framework for telework, which previously lacked clear regulation.

Why it mattersTeleworkers in Equatorial Guinea are now legally protected from employer communications outside of work hours, ensuring a clearer separation between professional and personal life.

Information Ministry Gives Hotels Two Weeks to Pay Tax Debts

Hotels and cable TV providers face a two-week deadline to settle outstanding tax obligations following a government review.

The Ministry of Information has issued a two-week ultimatum to hotels and cable television operators to clear their tax debts. The decision follows a review of ministry records that revealed significant delays in payments owed to the Treasury. Minister Juan José Ndong Nsue Mangue met with representatives from the tourism and cable television sectors to discuss regularizing their tax situations.

The new tax law, which came into effect in December 2025, mandates that all affected entities must pay their sector-specific fees. Some business representatives raised concerns during the meeting, which the minister addressed, offering further consultation at the ministry for any queries regarding their tax obligations.

This move by the Ministry of Information aims to ensure compliance with the tax law and improve revenue collection. The deadline is set to prompt immediate action from businesses that have accumulated overdue payments, thereby reinforcing the government's fiscal regulations.

Why it mattersHotels and cable TV operators are directly affected by the two-week deadline to pay outstanding tax debts to the Treasury.

Health Ministry Reinstates Suspended Managers

Officials return to posts at health centers in Evinayong, Djibloho, Mongomo, Ebibeyin, and Bata.

The Ministry of Health has reinstated suspended managers at several health facilities, including the Evinayong, Djibloho, Mongomo, and Ebibeyin health centers, as well as the Bata Hospital Emergency Unit. This action aims to ensure more efficient health management, service continuity, and adherence to administrative legality. The reinstated managers formally received their positions, superseding those appointed during an interim period.

Manuel Eribó Elobo, the Inspector General of Health Services for the Continental Region, presided over the official presentation and handover. He assured a formal administrative delivery and stated that the government is committed to strengthening the national health system for the benefit of the population. The Ministry of Health and Health Infrastructure reaffirms its commitment to following directives and ensuring transparency and efficiency in public health services.

This move signifies the government's dedication to improving healthcare delivery and administrative oversight. The reinstatement is expected to bring stability and renewed focus on operational effectiveness within the affected health centers. The Ministry emphasizes its role in upholding standards and ensuring public health services meet the needs of citizens across the region.

Why it mattersSuspended health managers have been reinstated to strengthen health management and ensure service continuity for the population.

Prosecutors and Anti-Corruption Commission Plan New Corruption Prevention Mechanism

Equatorial Guinea's public prosecutor and the new anti-corruption commission met to forge stronger inter-institutional cooperation against graft.

The Public Prosecutor's Office and the National Anti-Corruption Prevention and Fight Commission have initiated collaboration to enhance efforts against corruption. This marks the first meeting between the Prosecutor General's Office and the commission's new leadership, who presented their work plan. A key focus was strengthening inter-institutional cooperation while respecting each body's autonomy.

Discussions covered criminal law and criminal policy, clarifying the roles of each institution to prevent confusion in their competencies. The commission requested support from the Prosecutor General's Office to establish working meetings for more effective prevention of offenses. The Prosecutor's Office proposed creating a permanent coordination mechanism with institutional liaisons and a cooperation protocol for information exchange.

Both institutions aim to improve corruption prevention and control mechanisms, avoid duplication of efforts, and bolster good governance and institutional trust. They also proposed regular follow-up meetings and joint training programs for public officials and others involved in anti-corruption efforts. The Prosecutor's Office affirmed its commitment to strengthening this cooperation.

Why it mattersThis initiative aims to improve the prevention and prosecution of corruption, potentially leading to better governance and increased trust in public institutions.
