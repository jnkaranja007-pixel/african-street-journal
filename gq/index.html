Equatorial Guinea rehabilitates Oyala factory

Government approves first phase to restart precast concrete production for 60 new buildings.

The government of Equatorial Guinea approved the first phase of rehabilitating the Oyala precast factory on Monday, November 9. Vice President Nguema Obiang Mangue led the meeting in Malabo, attended by the prime minister and ministers of Public Works, Finance, Treasury, Electricity, and Mines. The supervising company TEAM presented a report on bids and necessary interventions to restore the factory's operational capacity.

Vice President Nguema Obiang Mangue authorized repairs to the concrete prefabrication facilities, areas for block, tile, and baluster manufacturing, as well as the crusher and quarry. The project includes a laboratory to test material quality before use in construction. The government aims to boost national construction material production, cut transport costs, and ensure supply for projects in the new administrative capital.

For the second phase, focused on factory management, Nguema Obiang Mangue tasked the prime minister and relevant ministries to renegotiate economic terms with the GICI group for a more favorable state agreement. Once negotiations conclude and contracts are formalized, the government plans to begin constructing the first 60 buildings in the City of Peace starting in 2027.

Why it mattersThe rehabilitation of the Oyala precast factory will enable the construction of 60 new buildings in the City of Peace starting in 2027.

Malian man dies after falling from Gendarmerie window in Malabo

Mamadu Maiga, held in a betting-company probe, died after jumping from a window where he was detained with a former minister.

Malian citizen Mamadu Maiga died after apparently jumping from a window at the National Gendarmerie in Malabo, where he was held in an investigation into sports betting companies. The incident happened on the night of Saturday, November 7, at about 21:30, according to witness accounts.

Maiga was summoned on Friday, November 6, to give a statement to investigators and was then kept in custody alongside former Minister of Culture, Tourism and Crafts Promotion Rufino Ndong Esono Nchama, who is also linked to the same case. Ndong Esono Nchama said he saw Maiga try to climb out of a window of the room they shared and shouted for security officers. Officers searched the area near the port, where Maiga's body was later found.

Ndong Esono Nchama described Maiga as very frightened and restless, but said he saw no physical attacks on him. Maiga's brother Colaba said he last saw him on Saturday, when he brought him food, and reported no visible signs of violence.

Why it mattersMaiga's death leaves his brother and family without the man they were waiting to see, and puts the Gendarmerie's custody conditions under question.

Equatorial Guinea asks UNESCO prize winners to aid national universities

Laureates of every edition of the UNESCO-Guinea Ecuatorial life sciences research prize are asked to share their research practice with two universities.

Equatorial Guinea's government has asked all past winners of the UNESCO-Guinea Ecuatorial International Prize for Research in Life Sciences to support the country's universities. Minister of State for Education, Science, Youth and Sports Clemente Engonga Nguema Onguene read a message from the head of state at the ceremony in Paris, France, for the prize's tenth edition.

The laureates, from the first edition to the tenth, are asked to share their experience in advanced research practice, innovation and local scientific development with the National University of Equatorial Guinea (UNGE) and the African American University of Central Africa (AAUCA). The minister said the request is meant to strengthen university research and reduce technological inequalities between nations.

Engonga Nguema Onguene said advances in technology are creating new opportunities and challenges, and that knowledge, infrastructure and investment in young people must be shared so that the benefits are not monopolised by certain nations.

Why it mattersUniversities in Equatorial Guinea would gain access to the experience of prize-winning researchers from every edition so far.

Equatorial Guinea joins TRT World Forum on digital challenges

The nation's delegation attended the 10th edition in Istanbul, discussing AI and media transformation.

Equatorial Guinea participated in the 10th TRT World Forum 2026 in Istanbul, an event organized by the Turkish Radio and Television Corporation (TRT). The forum focused on the theme “A world without a center. Who leads when the rules stop working?” to analyze changes in the international order and global challenges.

The Equatorial Guinean delegation included the director general of Production and Programming of the national television, José Mañana Aba Ayang, alongside the minister. Discussions covered artificial intelligence, digital transformation, international relations, energy crises, the global economy, and international law, as well as the role of media in technological shifts.

For Equatorial Guinea, the forum offered a chance to learn from international experiences in modernizing media, audiovisual production, and digital platforms. The country's participation aims to integrate national audiovisual sector challenges into global discussions on new information technologies.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guinea's participation in the TRT World Forum 2026 provides insights into modernizing its national media and audiovisual sector.

Equatorial Guinea denounces UN veto use

A UN peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara has ended after Russia vetoed a US proposal.

Equatorial Guinea on Tuesday, November 10, told the UN General Assembly in New York that the use of the veto power by permanent members has ended the UN peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara. The mission's mandate expired on October 31, 2026, after Russia blocked a US-backed resolution to renew it. A counter-proposal by Moscow also failed to gain enough support.

The veto created a legal and operational vacuum, affecting both the local population and contributing countries, according to Ndong Mba, Equatorial Guinea's representative. He stated that it was a historical anomaly for two non-African permanent members to decide the fate of a mission deployed in Africa on an African issue. Mba argued that veto use should protect international peace and security for all 193 UN members, not serve particular interests.

Equatorial Guinea supports the African Common Position on Security Council reform, including two permanent seats for Africa with veto power. The country backs UN envoy Staffan de Mistura's diplomatic efforts and views Morocco's autonomy proposal as a basis for a peaceful political solution. Algeria insists the conflict remains a decolonization issue, while Namibia and South Africa expressed regret over the mission's end and solidarity with the Sahrawi people.

Why it mattersThe end of the UN peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara creates a legal and operational vacuum affecting the region's stability.
