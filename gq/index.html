Turkey embassy marks republic anniversary in Malabo

Ambassador Ahmet Ergin expressed a desire to expand trade, investment, education, and cultural ties.

The Turkish Embassy in Malabo celebrated the 103rd anniversary of the Republic of Turkey on October 29. The event commemorated the founding of the republic in 1923 by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, following the Turkish War of Independence. The celebration included the national anthems of both Turkey and Equatorial Guinea, performed by the Maarif School choir.

Ambassador Ahmet Ergin addressed attendees, recalling Atatürk's legacy and Turkey's foreign policy principle of "Peace at home, peace in the world." He highlighted the existing friendship between Turkey and Equatorial Guinea and stated Turkey's commitment to strengthening bilateral relations. Ergin specifically mentioned Turkey's interest in increasing cooperation in trade, investment, education, and culture.

Mari Cruz Evuna Andeme, Secretary of State from Equatorial Guinea's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, attended the ceremony and congratulated the Turkish people. She emphasized the importance of bilateral relations and expressed hope for continued cooperation to foster development and mutual benefits in strategic sectors.

Why it mattersThe Turkish ambassador stated a desire to expand cooperation with Equatorial Guinea in trade, investment, education, and culture.

GETESA sponsors AI Business Malabo 2026 event

The event on October 28 and 30 explored artificial intelligence opportunities for businesses.

GETESA, the national telecommunications company of Equatorial Guinea, sponsored the AI Business Malabo 2026 event held on October 28 and 30. The event, hosted at KOIA Innovation Hub, brought together institutional representatives, business leaders, entrepreneurs, and professionals to discuss artificial intelligence's potential to optimize work processes and enhance competitiveness. GETESA's participation focused on integrating new technological tools into the business environment.

During the event, local business representatives shared their experiences using AI to streamline tasks and improve efficiency. Carolina Obama Elonga represented GETESA on a panel discussing AI's business applications. Jorge Figueredo, a specialist in digital transformation and generative AI, also participated, leading a workshop on creating AI assistants for professional use.

Reginaldo Boriko Okomo, director general of New Technologies and Civil Aviation, emphasized the need to extend AI tool benefits across all economic sectors. Figueredo highlighted AI's capacity to boost productivity while stressing that human judgment remains essential in business operations. GETESA's sponsorship aligns with Equatorial Guinea's growing focus on digital transformation and technological development.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guinean businesses and professionals can leverage AI for improved efficiency and competitiveness.

Equatorial Guinea to launch cocoa processing factory in six months

The government aims to transform raw cocoa into paste for chocolate production, boosting national resources.

The Equatorial Guinean government has given the Ministry of Agriculture six months to establish a cocoa processing factory in the country. This initiative, discussed in Malabo, aims to move beyond selling raw cocoa beans and begin producing cocoa paste, a key ingredient for chocolate.

The decision was made during a meeting attended by the prime minister and ministers of Agriculture, Finance, and Treasury, alongside representatives from the National Institute for Agricultural Promotion and selected companies. Negotiations with suppliers ANONEY SERVICE and GETRANSCAO resulted in a price reduction of up to 10% for supplies like quicklime and copper sulfate.

The Ministry of Agriculture has a maximum of six months to launch the factory. This local processing aims to generate new employment opportunities and add greater value to national production, reducing reliance on imported goods.

Why it mattersFarmers and the nation will benefit from processing cocoa locally, creating jobs and adding value to agricultural output.

Equatorial Guinea treasury staff to sign confidentiality pacts

New deputy minister Montserrat Mbasogo Edu Angue prioritizes information security and open communication with staff.

Equatorial Guinea's new deputy minister of the Treasury, Montserrat Mbasogo Edu Angue, met with department staff to outline her priorities. She emphasized working together with officials, fostering open communication, and valuing technical staff's contributions. The minister stressed the importance of transparency and collaboration in achieving institutional goals.

A key focus of the meeting was the protection of institutional information. Angue announced that all staff will be required to sign a confidentiality agreement to formalize their commitment to safeguarding internal documents and data. She warned that unauthorized disclosure of information could lead to disciplinary measures.

The deputy minister also addressed punctuality and labor discipline, reminding staff of the importance of respecting work hours and fulfilling their duties. She encouraged staff to face challenges with dedication and a spirit of service, noting that institutional results depend on employee involvement and responsibility.

Why it mattersStaff at Equatorial Guinea's Treasury will sign mandatory confidentiality agreements, with potential disciplinary action for breaches, affecting their daily work and information access.

Equatorial Guinea president urges Africa to transform raw materials locally

Obiang Nguema Mbasogo stated at the Russia-Africa summit that Africa must move beyond exporting raw materials and develop its own industries.

President Obiang Nguema Mbasogo of Equatorial Guinea stated at the Third Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow that African nations should not be content with merely supplying raw materials to other continents. He emphasized that Africa can and should transform these resources locally to build its own economies.

Speaking at the Kremlin, Mbasogo argued that true economic independence for Africa lies in responsible exploitation, local transformation, and the creation of value chains within the continent. He presented Equatorial Guinea's National Development Agenda Horizon 2035 as an example of diversifying growth beyond natural resources.

The president called for concrete, viable, and sustainable projects with Russian partners, combining human capital, technology, and local business participation. This initiative aims to industrialize Africa using its own resources and capabilities.

Why it mattersPresident Obiang Nguema Mbasogo's call for economic transformation directly affects Equatorial Guinea's economic strategy and its role in international trade.
