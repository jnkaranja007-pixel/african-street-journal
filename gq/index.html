Equatorial Guinea opens 24-block oil and gas licensing round to fight output slide

Equatorial Guinea's Ministry of Hydrocarbons launched a new licensing round in April 2026, offering 24 exploration blocks, two onshore and the rest offshore, running until November. National crude production has fallen from 241,000 barrels a day at its 2010 peak to 55,000 barrels a day in 2023, according to OPEC data. Hydrocarbons still account for 42% of GDP, 95% of exports and 90% of public revenue, the African Development Bank estimates.

Why it mattersNew exploration acreage is the government's main lever to slow a 15-year revenue decline that funds most of the state budget.

IMF projects Equatorial Guinea growth of just 0.9% a year through 2030

The International Monetary Fund expects Equatorial Guinea's oil- and gas-dependent economy to expand by an average of only 0.9% annually between 2025 and 2030 because of declining hydrocarbon production. The forecast underscores the country's struggle to diversify away from crude, which has powered the bulk of state revenue since the 1990s discoveries.

Why it mattersSub-1% growth leaves little room for new public spending as older oil fields keep depleting.

Chevron agrees terms on $690 million Aseng gas project with Equatorial Guinea

Chevron's Noble Energy subsidiary has agreed terms with Equatorial Guinea on developing the Aseng gas project, moving the venture toward a final investment decision, according to Reuters. The project in the Douala Basin carries an initial investment of about $690 million and is meant to sustain liquefied natural gas supplies. Malabo is also reviving the $4.5 billion EG-27 gas project, backed by Afreximbank, which could produce 2.4 million tons of LNG a year over two decades.

Why it mattersThe deals represent rare fresh foreign investment as legacy oil operators such as ExxonMobil have exited the country.

Equatorial Guinea confirms capital shift from Malabo to Ciudad de la Paz

Equatorial Guinea officially announced on 3 January that its capital will move from Malabo, on Bioko island, to the mainland city of Ciudad de la Paz. The government has cited strategic reasons for relocating administrative functions away from the island seat that has hosted the capital for decades.

Why it mattersShifting the seat of government to the mainland will redirect infrastructure spending, land deals and logistics contracts.

Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea sign deal to jointly develop Yoyo-Yolanda gas field

Cameroon and Equatorial Guinea signed an agreement on 3 February to jointly extract natural gas from the cross-border Yoyo-Yolanda field. The pact adds to a series of gas partnerships Malabo has pursued as it tries to offset falling crude output with LNG production.

Why it mattersA shared gas field with Cameroon could add a new production stream as Equatorial Guinea's own reserves mature.

Nzalang Nacional exit AFCON group stage after Algeria hand third straight loss

Algeria beat Equatorial Guinea to finish Group E of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations with a perfect record, eliminating the Nzalang Nacional after three consecutive defeats, according to Outlook India. The team had been drawn against Burkina Faso, Sudan and Algeria in the group phase.

Pope Leo XIV's April visit to Malabo spotlights poverty despite oil wealth

Pope Leo XIV arrived in Malabo on 21 April for the final leg of his four-nation Africa trip, the first papal visit to Equatorial Guinea since 1982, according to Vatican News. Despite the country's oil wealth, it ranks 133rd out of 193 nations on the UN's Human Development Index, with wealth distributed unevenly, the outlet reported.

Why it mattersThe visit drew global attention to the gap between Equatorial Guinea's hydrocarbon riches and living standards for most citizens.
