Justice Ministry Probes Low Revenue Collection

The Ministry of Justice is launching an investigation into its own revenue collection mechanisms following a review by the Prime Minister.

The Ministry of Justice has initiated an inquiry to pinpoint areas experiencing revenue collection difficulties. This action follows a recent accountability session with the Prime Minister, which highlighted a concerningly low level of income for the department. The investigation is being conducted in collaboration with the Ministry of Finance, which oversees public financial management.

The primary goal is to diagnose the current financial situation and understand the underlying causes for the registered income levels. Data presented during the revenue review indicated that the Justice Ministry's collection rate was insufficient. The Ministry of Finance intends to scrutinize figures from recent fiscal periods to identify the main obstacles.

Oluy Mba Edjidjiba, head of Disclosure and Taxpayer Services, stated the process is diagnostic and not punitive, aiming to assess the effectiveness of collection systems and identify any shortcomings. The findings will inform recommendations to enhance the Justice Ministry's revenue-generating capacity.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Justice's revenue collection issues could impact public services and financial oversight, affecting the government's ability to fund operations.

GETESA brings services to Malabo youth with fair

The telecommunications company hosted an event in the Los Ángeles neighborhood offering information and direct service.

GETESA launched an initiative in Malabo's Los Ángeles neighborhood to connect with residents, especially youth, through a day of recreation and education. The company set up a space at the neighborhood field to inform the public about its services and facilitate transactions like new SIM cards, line activations, and ownership changes. This event aims to bring GETESA closer to young people and promote activities related to education, sports, and entertainment.

The initiative included educational games and contests on Sunday, August 30, covering general knowledge, math, geography, and GETESA-specific information. Winners received GETESA-branded school supplies such as backpacks, notebooks, and pens. The company invited young people from Los Ángeles and surrounding areas to participate and compete for prizes.

This event is part of a traveling program GETESA plans to take to various Malabo neighborhoods. Following Los Ángeles, the program is scheduled for Ela Nguema, San Valentín, and Buena Esperanza. GETESA seeks to strengthen its engagement with youth and provide direct access to its services and customer support.

Why it mattersGETESA is engaging with young people in Malabo by offering educational and recreational activities alongside direct access to its telecommunications services.
