Turkish Airlines considers Equatorial Guinea flights

The airline evaluated Bata, Mongomoyen, and Malabo airports for potential service resumption.

Turkish Airlines is assessing Equatorial Guinea's airport infrastructure for a possible return of its flights. The evaluation follows a meeting in Turkey between the Minister of State for Transport, Honorato Evita Oma, and Turkish Airlines' CEO, Ahmet Olmuşur. The airline previously operated a route to Malabo, inaugurated in February 2020.

Delegations visited the international airports of Bata, Mongomoyen, and Malabo, examining terminals, check-in, security, baggage handling, and boarding facilities. At Mongomoyen, they toured operational areas and sites for a future Aeronautical Special Economic Zone. The visit also included the Aeronautical School and an aircraft repair hangar, which the government aims to use for regional pilot training and maintenance services.

The government seeks to leverage its airport investments to create an aviation hub in Central Africa. This strategy includes passenger and cargo transport, aeronautical training, and maintenance. A resumption of Turkish Airlines flights would enhance international connectivity, open new markets via Istanbul, and boost tourism, trade, and investment.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guinea's government aims to boost its economy by developing its airport infrastructure into a regional aviation hub.

Equatorial Guinea validates census tools

Experts reviewed strategies and questionnaires for population and agricultural data collection.

The National Institute of Statistics of Equatorial Guinea (INEGE) closed a technical validation workshop in Malabo on November 6. The workshop, held from October 26 to November 6, reviewed strategies and instruments for upcoming population and agricultural censuses. Experts from various ministries, civil society, and UN agencies participated.

Ricardo Nsue Ndemesogo Obono, INEGE's Director General, presided over the closing ceremony. The technical team presented a report with conclusions and improvement proposals. The workshop focused on the methodological coherence and operational viability of census tools, including cartography, household questionnaires, and agricultural modules.

Recommendations included verifying administrative boundaries and local names, testing access procedures to difficult areas, and preserving the independence of the post-census survey. Specialists also proposed applying additional modules for detailed agricultural production information.

Why it mattersThe validation of census instruments will lead to more precise and reliable data on the country's population and agricultural activities.

Health · 2026-11-08

Equatorial Guinea president visits medical center

President Obiang Nguema Mbasogo urged healthcare workers to treat patients with love and care.

President Obiang Nguema Mbasogo visited the La Paz de Malabo Medical Center in Sipopo on Friday, November 6. He toured the facilities and met with staff, including the center's director, Irvin Simbarashe, and the chairwoman of its board, Margarita Roka Elobo.

During his visit, the president emphasized the importance of human connection in healthcare. He asked medical professionals to treat patients with affection and to ensure they felt heard, respected, and supported during their recovery, in addition to receiving medical assistance.

The La Paz de Malabo Medical Center offers various specialties, including internal medicine, surgery, neurosurgery, gynecology, cardiology, and pediatrics, along with laboratory, radiology, and pharmacy services.

Equatorial Guinea joins women's autonomy forum closing

Burundi's First Lady highlighted women's role in development at the seventh forum.

Equatorial Guinea participated in the closing ceremony of the seventh forum on the autonomy of women and young people in Burundi. The forum's final declaration focused on strengthening female autonomy through education, health, economic empowerment, and preventing gender-based violence.

The closing session featured representatives from the United Nations and OPDAD, alongside speeches from participating delegations and Burundi's First Lady, Angeline Ndayishimiye. She acknowledged the attendees' involvement and emphasized women's role in societal development. The official closing speech was delivered by Burundi's Vice President, Prosper Bazombanza.

Following the ceremony, Angeline Ndayishimiye invited delegations to commemorative photo sessions. The Equatorial Guinean delegation then began their journey home, with a representative joining a traditional farewell dance. The forum aimed to renew commitments to women's autonomy, sustainable development, and continental resilience.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Equatorial Guinea appoints first resident ambassador to Botswana

Domingo Mituy Edjang presented credentials in Gaborone on November 3, aiming to boost bilateral ties.

Equatorial Guinea has established its first resident ambassador in Botswana, with Domingo Mituy Edjang presenting his credentials to Botswana's President on November 3. This appointment aims to strengthen diplomatic relations between Ciudad de la Paz and Gaborone.

Ambassador Mituy Edjang also met with Botswana's Ministry of International Affairs to review bilateral cooperation and identify priority sectors. The following day, he met with the Botswana Investment and Trade Centre (BITC) to promote economic diplomacy, trade, and investment opportunities.

During the BITC meeting, Mituy Edjang highlighted Equatorial Guinea's economic potential and invited Botswanan investors to explore business prospects. The ambassador also presented the country's tourism and cultural attractions.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guinea's appointment of its first resident ambassador to Botswana aims to foster stronger diplomatic and economic ties between the two nations.
