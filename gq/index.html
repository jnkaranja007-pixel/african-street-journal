Turkish Airlines considers Equatorial Guinea flights

The airline evaluated Bata, Mongomoyen, and Malabo airports for potential service resumption.

Turkish Airlines is assessing Equatorial Guinea's airport infrastructure for a possible return of its flights. The evaluation follows a meeting in Turkey between the Minister of State for Transport, Honorato Evita Oma, and Turkish Airlines' CEO, Ahmet Olmuşur. The airline previously operated a route to Malabo, inaugurated in February 2020.

Delegations visited the international airports of Bata, Mongomoyen, and Malabo, examining terminals, check-in, security, baggage handling, and boarding facilities. At Mongomoyen, they toured operational areas and sites for a future Aeronautical Special Economic Zone. The visit also included the Aeronautical School and an aircraft repair hangar, which the government aims to use for regional pilot training and maintenance services.

The government seeks to leverage its airport investments to create an aviation hub in Central Africa. This strategy includes passenger and cargo transport, aeronautical training, and maintenance. A resumption of Turkish Airlines flights would enhance international connectivity, open new markets via Istanbul, and boost tourism, trade, and investment.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guinea's government aims to boost its economy by developing its airport infrastructure into a regional aviation hub.

Equatorial Guinea validates census tools

Experts reviewed strategies and questionnaires for population and agricultural data collection.

The National Institute of Statistics of Equatorial Guinea (INEGE) closed a technical validation workshop in Malabo on November 6. The workshop, held from October 26 to November 6, reviewed strategies and instruments for upcoming population and agricultural censuses. Experts from various ministries, civil society, and UN agencies participated.

Ricardo Nsue Ndemesogo Obono, INEGE's Director General, presided over the closing ceremony. The technical team presented a report with conclusions and improvement proposals. The workshop focused on the methodological coherence and operational viability of census tools, including cartography, household questionnaires, and agricultural modules.

Recommendations included verifying administrative boundaries and local names, testing access procedures to difficult areas, and preserving the independence of the post-census survey. Specialists also proposed applying additional modules for detailed agricultural production information.

Why it mattersThe validation of census instruments will lead to more precise and reliable data on the country's population and agricultural activities.

Equatorial Guinea appoints first resident ambassador to Botswana

Domingo Mituy Edjang presented credentials to Botswana's president on November 3, strengthening bilateral ties.

Domingo Mituy Edjang presented his credentials to the President of Botswana on November 3, becoming Equatorial Guinea's first resident ambassador in Gaborone. This appointment aims to strengthen diplomatic relations between the two nations, which were established in 2006.

Following the presentation, Ambassador Mituy Edjang attended a technical meeting with Botswana's Ministry of International Affairs to review bilateral cooperation and identify priority sectors. He also met with the Botswana Investment and Trade Centre (BITC) on November 4 to promote economic diplomacy, trade, and investment opportunities.

During the BITC meeting, Mituy Edjang highlighted Equatorial Guinea's economic potential and legal framework for investors, inviting Botswana businesses to explore opportunities. The ambassador also presented Equatorial Guinea's tourism and cultural attractions.

Why it mattersThe appointment of a resident ambassador and subsequent meetings aim to foster stronger diplomatic and economic ties between Equatorial Guinea and Botswana.

Equatorial Guinea delegation closes women's forum in Burundi

The seventh forum on women's and young women's autonomy concluded with a final declaration and recommendations.

A delegation from Equatorial Guinea attended the closing ceremony of the seventh forum on women's and young women's autonomy in Burundi. The event gathered representatives from African nations to renew commitments to women's autonomy, sustainable development, and continental resilience. The closing session included speeches from United Nations and OPDAD representatives, and Burundi's First Lady Angeline Ndayishimiye, who highlighted women's role in societal development.

The forum's final declaration and recommendations were read, focusing on strengthening female autonomy through education, health, economic empowerment, preventing gender-based violence, and increasing women's participation in decision-making. Burundi's Vice President Prosper Bazombanza delivered the official closing speech. First Lady Ndayishimiye later invited delegations to photo sessions.

The Equatorial Guinean delegation began their journey home after the forum. Before boarding their aircraft, a representative of the First Lady joined a traditional farewell dance. The forum concluded Equatorial Guinea's participation in discussions on advancing women's autonomy and sustainable development in Africa.

Why it mattersWomen in Equatorial Guinea are affected by the forum's recommendations on education, health, economic empowerment, and decision-making participation.
