Judiciary inflates labor compensation claims for companies

A reported mechanism within the justice administration allegedly inflates labor indemnities, forcing companies to deposit higher amounts in legal proceedings.

A mechanism in the justice administration is allegedly inflating labor indemnities, according to Ahora EG. This practice reportedly requires companies to deposit significantly higher amounts in judicial proceedings, benefiting the courts regardless of the case outcome.

The report highlights a case involving Hermenegildo Esono Anguezomo, a former Government Relations Manager at Marathon. The Malabo Labor Court No. 2 ruled his dismissal improper and ordered Marathon to pay 1,520,154,184 CFA francs, plus 45,604,625 CFA francs in legal costs. Marathon appealed to the Supreme Court, which required a deposit of 1,878,910,572 CFA francs, including an additional 20% of the judgment.

The core of the complaint centers on the calculation of salary differences. Esono Anguezomo's contract specified a monthly salary of 3,267,111 CFA francs, but the court used a reference salary of 17,335,285 CFA francs from another position, creating a difference of 14,068,174 CFA francs. Marathon's argument that the positions were not comparable was not accepted in the final ruling, raising concerns about judicial corruption and risks for companies operating in Equatorial Guinea.

Why it mattersCompanies operating in Equatorial Guinea, especially those in the oil sector like Marathon, face increased financial risk and potential legal challenges due to allegedly inflated labor compensation claims.

DRC Opposition Urges President Tshisekedi to Halt Constitutional Changes

The C64 opposition coalition is pressuring President Tshisekedi to abandon constitutional reforms that could extend his term beyond 2028, citing ongoing conflict and economic.

Delly Sesanga, president of the ENVOL party and a member of the C64 opposition coalition, stated that the opposition's main goal is to ensure President Tshisekedi leaves office when his second and final term concludes in 2028. Tshisekedi, in power since 2019, secured a second five-year term in the 2023 elections. Under the current constitution, he cannot seek another term.

The debate over constitutional reform emerges as the country remains embroiled in conflicts, particularly in the east. The M23 armed group, reportedly backed by Rwanda, controls significant areas of North and South Kivu. Sesanga argues that neither the government nor the armed group can achieve a decisive military victory.

Sesanga emphasized the need to end hostilities and alleviate the suffering of the people, stating that a military victory is out of reach for both the AFC-M23 and the government. With elections scheduled for 2028, the opposition seeks to increase pressure against constitutional changes and demand an inclusive political process to address the nation's security and economic crises.

Why it mattersThis story directly affects citizens of the Democratic Republic of Congo by influencing the future of their political leadership and the potential for prolonged conflict and economic instability.

ANIF receives training from Guatemala's IVE to combat financial crimes

Equatorial Guinea's Financial Intelligence Unit (ANIF) is undergoing training from Guatemalan specialists to enhance its capabilities against money laundering and terrorism.

The training, which runs until August twenty-seventh, addresses recommendations from the Mutual Evaluation Report of the Central African Anti-Money Laundering Group (GABAC) and the National Action Plan. This initiative aims to improve the national system for preventing and combating money laundering, terrorism financing, and the financing of weapons proliferation.

Zenón Obiang Obiang Avomo, ANIF's national director, emphasized the importance of cooperation between similar institutions and the exchange of experiences within the Egmont Group. He noted that evolving financial crime risks require trained personnel, effective coordination mechanisms, and responsiveness to new scenarios. He thanked the Guatemalan IVE specialists for their collaboration.

Milagrosa Obono Angüe, Minister Delegate of Finance, Budgets, and National Planning, highlighted the training's alignment with national priorities and GABAC's mutual evaluation process. She stressed the need for a cooperative structure beyond ANIF itself, involving public institutions, financial entities, and other actors to ensure the system's effectiveness.

Why it mattersThis training directly affects financial institutions and businesses in Equatorial Guinea by strengthening the regulatory framework against financial crimes, potentially impacting their operations and compliance requirements.

Education Ministry intensifies school rehabilitation inspections in Malabo

The Ministry of Education, through its Delegate Minister, Constantino Obiang Esono Ayingono, continued inspections of school rehabilitation projects in Malabo ahead of the.

Delegate Minister of Education, Constantino Obiang Esono Ayingono, inspected several educational centers in Malabo undergoing rehabilitation on Monday. Accompanied by inspectors from GE Proyectos and regional education officials, the minister verified the progress of works and adherence to government standards at sites including Colegio Nacional Cuartel Mañe Ela, Colegio Nacional Siale Bileka, Colegio Nacional Bioko Norte, Colegio Nacional Cuartel Sipopo de Marina, and Colegio 3 de Agosto de la Presidencia.

The inspections are part of a government plan to improve national educational infrastructure, aiming to rehabilitate 46 schools in Malabo and Bata with the involvement of 19 companies. The minister urged contractors, including Lea Multiservicios, MS, and VIKO S.L., to maintain quality and responsibility, emphasizing that some students might be temporarily relocated to facilitate construction.

Obiang Esono Ayingono stressed that the rehabilitation project is a national strategy to strengthen the education system and called on school administrators to ensure proper maintenance of facilities after completion. Future inspections are planned for the Continental Region to oversee other schools in the national program.

Why it mattersStudents and educators in Malabo are directly affected by the ongoing rehabilitation of 46 schools, which aims to provide better learning environments and may involve temporary student relocations.

Education · 2026-08-25

Grand Hotel Djibloho certifies ANAMOA students in hospitality training

The Grand Hotel Djibloho awarded certificates to students from the ANAMOA center, marking the completion of their practical training in hotel operations.

The Grand Hotel Djibloho recently concluded a practical training program for students from the ANAMOA center, issuing certificates to participants. Senior hotel management, including the Director of Operations and the Director of Human Resources, presided over the ceremony. This initiative aimed to enhance professional skills and knowledge in various operational areas within the hotel sector.

Students gained hands-on experience across different departments, such as Kitchen and Pastry, Food & Beverage, and Finance. This on-site professional exposure allowed them to apply theoretical knowledge in a real-world hotel environment, familiarizing them with industry practices in Equatorial Guinea.

The hotel stated that this practical training is a fundamental tool for helping young people transition into the workforce. The Grand Hotel Djibloho affirmed its commitment to fostering professional development, nurturing young talent, and strengthening competencies within Equatorial Guinea's hospitality and tourism sector through such collaborations with educational institutions.

Why it mattersStudents from the ANAMOA center received certificates for completing practical training at the Grand Hotel Djibloho, directly impacting their professional development and entry into the workforce.
