ANIF Concludes Cybersecurity Training, Bolstering Anti-Money Laundering Efforts

Equatorial Guinea's financial intelligence agency, ANIF, has completed technical training with Guatemalan experts, focusing on cybersecurity and anti-money laundering measures.

The National Agency for Financial Intelligence (ANIF) has concluded a technical training program delivered by experts from Guatemala's IVE. This initiative directly addresses recommendations from the Central African Action Group against Money Laundering (GABAC) and supports Equatorial Guinea's National Action Plan against money laundering.

During the final session, ANIF Director Zenón Obiang Obiang Avomo and the Guatemalan experts highlighted cybersecurity threats, including electronic scams, identity theft, and information theft. Obiang Avomo warned attendees about fraudulent messages on WhatsApp and social media, as well as deception tactics used to solicit money or access contacts. The training emphasized the need for collaboration among institutions, users, and financial institutions to prevent and respond to these risks.

The closing ceremony was attended by Milagrosa Obono Angüe, Minister Delegate of Finance, Budget, and National Planning. She stressed that acquired knowledge must translate into improved work practices and reiterated that combating money laundering is a shared responsibility requiring effective coordination among authorities, financial institutions, and all stakeholders. ANIF committed to enhancing institutional capacities, improving suspicious transaction reports, and increasing technological resilience in line with international standards.

Why it mattersThis training directly impacts investors by strengthening the nation's financial intelligence capabilities and its fight against financial crimes, which can affect the stability and security of financial markets.

UEFA Pursues Criminal Charges Against FIFA President Infantino

European football's governing body seeks evidence for Swiss legal proceedings, accusing Infantino of criminal mismanagement and a self-enrichment scheme.

The Union of European Football Associations (UEFA) has initiated legal action, filing a request for evidence in Switzerland against FIFA President Gianni Infantino. UEFA accuses Infantino of criminal mismanagement related to a proposed plan to sell shares in FIFA competitions to private investors through a new entity, FiFA Forward Enterprise. This plan was reportedly abandoned after causing significant discontent.

UEFA alleges that Infantino devised the scheme to benefit himself and potential investors, a move that could have negatively impacted its 55 member nations. Key UEFA officials met recently in Monaco to discuss Infantino's situation, resolving to pursue all necessary institutional, political, and legal avenues for fundamental reform and to re-establish limits on presidential authority.

The European football body stated that FIFA, headquartered in Zurich, Switzerland, would be subject to Swiss law in these proceedings. Infantino, who is seeking a fourth term as FIFA president in March, has been accused by UEFA, CONCACAF, and the Asian Football Confederation of betraying trust through deception in an open letter.

Why it mattersInvestors could be affected by potential legal actions and reforms within FIFA that might alter investment opportunities in football competitions.

US Consulates Halt Immigrant Visa Processing for Public Charge Training

The U.S. State Department has paused all immigrant visa interviews and processing worldwide to retrain staff on identifying applicants who may rely on public assistance.

The U.S. Department of State has suspended all immigrant visa processing at its consulates globally, a move aimed at retraining staff to identify visa applicants who might utilize public assistance programs upon arrival in the United States. This pause, reported by the Financial Times, is intended to ensure that prosperous U.S. immigration does not risk burdening public resources.

A State Department spokesperson stated that the goal is to prevent visa applicants from becoming a public charge or depending on U.S. aid reserved for citizens. Immigrant visas are pathways to legal permanent residency, often obtained through family ties or the visa lottery. This measure does not impact non-immigrant visas for tourism or business, nor specific skilled worker visas like the H1-B, which continue processing as usual.

The duration of this pause for immigrant visa applications has not been specified by the Department of State. This action follows a recent announcement where the department revoked over 175,000 foreign national visas, citing violations of visa terms, criminal activity, incitement of violence, fraud, immigration system abuse, or national security risks.

Why it mattersThis pause affects Equatorial Guineans seeking to immigrate to the U.S. through family ties or the visa lottery, delaying their ability to obtain permanent residency.

Equatorial Guinea Government Analyzes Ministerial Revenue Reports

Prime Minister Manuel Osa Nsue Nsua met with Finance Ministry officials to review non-oil revenue collection and implement reforms to improve tax collection across departments.

Prime Minister Manuel Osa Nsue Nsua convened a meeting in Malabo II with Finance Ministry officials to scrutinize non-oil revenue reports from ministerial departments. The objective was to identify areas for improvement in tax collection and establish uniform procedures between ministries, finance, and the treasury.

The Prime Minister emphasized the urgent need for structural reforms, including expanding and strengthening key ministerial departments, particularly economic sections. This initiative aims to address deficiencies in ministerial accounts and rectify irregularities in revenue collection processes. Officials were instructed to draft a ministerial order detailing these measures.

The government plans to conduct a tripartite operation to identify anomalous or unrealistic charges, detect funds collected without clear purpose, and verify document authenticity. These actions are intended to boost revenue collection beyond previous years and foster a modern, transparent, and self-sufficient public administration model.

Why it mattersThe government's focus on improving tax collection and administrative transparency directly affects how public funds are managed and potentially impacts the efficiency of services and economic stability.
