Equatorial Guinea government to cut subsidies for public entities with debts

Prime Minister warns that entities failing to pay SEGESA will face direct subsidy deductions starting January 1, 2027.

The Equatorial Guinean government will directly deduct subsidies from public entities that fail to pay their debts to SEGESA, the national electricity company. This measure follows a meeting where the accumulated debt was discussed, with some entities having settled their accounts while others remain in arrears. The government has given parties a week and a half to reach specific payment agreements.

Prime Minister Manuel Osa Nsue Nsua warned that starting January 1, 2027, payments will not be made to entities that have not regularized their accounting, tax obligations, and legal documentation. The Ministry of Finance reported that only a minority of entities are paying their taxes regularly. The government also reviewed social obligations, asking INSESO to list entities with unpaid employee contributions.

The government is also pushing for the restructuring of public entities to ensure fiscal sanitation and sustainability. A third meeting will be held to evaluate the results of the payment agreements and make final decisions on unresolved cases. SEGESA has also been asked to consider compensation mechanisms for electricity supply interruptions.

Why it mattersPublic entities in Equatorial Guinea face direct subsidy cuts and payment freezes if they do not settle debts and regularize their financial and tax obligations.

Equatorial Guinea president attends Moscow gas summit

President Obiang Nguema Mbasogo joined leaders from gas-producing nations to discuss global market stability.

Equatorial Guinea's President attended a summit in Moscow focused on ensuring global gas market stability. The meeting at the International Trade Center brought together leaders and representatives from major gas producing and exporting countries. Russian President Vladimir Putin addressed the plenary session, highlighting natural gas's strategic importance.

Putin stated that natural gas currently accounts for about a quarter of global energy consumption. He presented forecasts suggesting that worldwide demand could reach five and a half trillion cubic meters annually by mid-century. The summit included Iran's President Masud Pezeshkian and Qatar's Emir Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani.

The summit aimed to foster cooperation among key players in the international gas sector. Discussions focused on strategies to maintain predictable supply and pricing in the global market.

Why it mattersPresident Obiang Nguema Mbasogo participated in a summit discussing global gas market stability.

African journalists explore China cooperation in energy and industry

A delegation including a journalist from Equatorial Guinea visited Hunan province to identify opportunities in clean energy, sustainable industrialization, and cultural tourism.

A delegation of African journalists, including Quisito Santos Bacale Asumu Abeso from Real Equatorial Guinea, visited China's Hunan province from October 26 to explore cooperation in clean energy, industrial modernization, infrastructure, and cultural exchange. The visit aims to identify solutions for expanding electricity access, strengthening productive capacities, and diversifying economic alliances for African nations.

In Hengyang, journalists saw energy storage solutions for renewable energy with potential applications in markets like South Africa and Zambia, which could improve electricity supply in areas with limited grid access. They also visited metallurgical companies that have improved processes through waste reuse and energy saving, offering models for African countries promoting sustainable industrialization and production efficiency.

The exchanges also covered raw material supply and industrial chain development, relevant for exploring new collaboration avenues between Chinese and African businesses. In the cultural sphere, journalists learned about initiatives combining tradition with gastronomy, crafts, and tourism, providing references for African countries seeking to protect heritage and boost local economies.

Why it mattersJournalists from Equatorial Guinea and other African nations are exploring opportunities for cooperation with China in energy, industry, and culture.
