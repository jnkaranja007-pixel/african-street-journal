Equatorial Guinea joins Russia-Africa summit action plan

Leaders agreed a joint action plan for 2027-2029 and discussed economic cooperation.

Equatorial Guinea's President Obiang Nguema Mbasogo participated in the final day of the III Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow on October 29, 2026. The summit's activities took place in the Grand Kremlin Palace, featuring plenary sessions chaired by Russian President Vladimir Putin and African Union Chair Évariste Ndayishimiye of Burundi. Discussions covered Russia-Africa relations, Africa's international role, and expanding economic, political, technological, and humanitarian cooperation.

A key outcome was the planned approval of a final declaration on Russian-African relations, a declaration on artificial intelligence cooperation, and a joint action plan for 2027-2029. Several cooperation agreements and memorandums between Russia and African nations were also set to be signed. President Mbasogo's attendance continued his Moscow agenda, which included participation in the Gas Exporting Countries Forum and a bilateral meeting with Vladimir Putin.

The summit concluded with a joint press conference by the Presidents of Russia and Burundi, detailing the meeting's main results. Part of the declarations focused on Africa's economic transformation, industrial development, and establishing result-oriented alliances.

Why it mattersPresident Obiang Nguema Mbasogo attended the summit where Equatorial Guinea was involved in agreeing a joint action plan and discussing economic cooperation with Russia.

Malabo conference promotes disaster prevention knowledge

Government, business, and university representatives met in Malabo to discuss disaster preparedness.

Malabo hosted a conference on October 28 to promote a culture of disaster prevention. The event, held at the Centro Cultural de España en Malabo, brought together government officials, national companies, and students and professors from the National University of Equatorial Guinea. Ángela Mbang Ncogo Nsa, a professor at the university and promoter of the initiative through the AFODECA association, organized the conference to raise societal awareness about risk prevention and emergency preparedness.

The conference aimed to involve institutions, the private sector, and academia in building a more prepared society. Participants included Maximiliano Ndong Nzo Obono, Professor Lucrecia Nchama Ncogo, Miguel Martín Ondo Sima Mangue from the Ministry of Finance, and Miguel Ángel Anusogo from FUNDACIÓN IDENTIC. They engaged in discussions on strengthening risk knowledge and prevention within the country.

Future plans include awareness campaigns, educational activities, and community actions, such as the 'Yo estoy preparado/a' campaign to foster preventive habits. AFODECA seeks to embed prevention as a daily practice for citizens, moving beyond institutional responsibility. This initiative marks the beginning of a project to enhance Equatorial Guinea's alliances and response capacity to disaster risks.

Why it mattersGovernment, business, and university sectors in Malabo are affected by the initiative to build a more disaster-prepared society.

Equatorial Guinea Senate approves press freedom law

The new regulation aims to protect journalists, establish clear duties, and promote rigorous reporting.

The Senate in Equatorial Guinea has approved a new law regulating press freedom, recognizing journalism as a pillar of the state and a public service. The law focuses on three key areas: protecting journalists' security, rights, and dignity; regulating professional duties and ethics; and demanding rigor and continuous training.

The Secretary of State for Information, Press, Culture, and Tourism expressed gratitude for the law, stating it resulted from dialogue with journalists, media directors, civil society, and students. The Democratic Coalition Group voted in favor, emphasizing the need for editorial independence, fair labor practices, media funding transparency, and institutional guarantees.

The PDGE Parliamentary Group also supported the bill, with its spokesperson warning about the dangers of fake news and hate speech on social media. The Senate president highlighted the importance of good journalistic practices, adherence to truth, and social responsibility to foster national development and cohesion.

Why it mattersThe new press freedom law aims to protect journalists and ensure responsible reporting, impacting the daily work and professional standing of media practitioners in Equatorial Guinea.

BEAC sets deadline for old CFA franc coins

Central African states have one year to exchange old 1 to 500 CFA franc coins for new currency.

The Bank of Central African States (BEAC) has set a one-year deadline for exchanging old 1 to 500 CFA franc coins across the CEMAC region. This decision, made by the UMAC Ministerial Committee, aims to streamline currency circulation. The exchange period begins on November 1, 2026, and concludes on October 31, 2027.

During the first phase, from November 1, 2026, to July 31, 2027, individuals can exchange the old coins at BEAC branches, commercial banks, and microfinance institutions. The second phase, from August 1 to October 31, 2027, limits exchanges to BEAC offices only, with no limit on the amount for individuals and businesses. Commercial banks and microfinance institutions are obligated to accept these coins from the public, regardless of customer status.

After October 31, 2027, the old coins will no longer be redeemable. The BEAC has urged financial intermediaries to establish necessary mechanisms to facilitate the exchange process and prevent citizens from losing the value of their old currency due to lack of information or procedural difficulties. Coins from the 2006 and 2024 series remain legal tender.

Why it mattersCitizens in Equatorial Guinea and other CEMAC countries must exchange old CFA franc coins within a year to avoid losing their value.

Cronos Airlines cancels Malabo flights

Passengers stranded at Malabo airport face uncertainty as technical issues ground flights to Bata.

Cronos Airlines cancelled flights from Malabo to Bata on Thursday, leaving passengers stranded at the airport. Passengers were informed on Tuesday to stop checking in and go to company offices, later receiving confirmation of cancellations. Some passengers who bought tickets on Tuesday were still sold tickets despite ongoing cancellations.

A passenger showed a message received on Thursday, October 29, stating flight 901 to Bata was rescheduled due to a technical problem, with no new departure time given. Passengers who bought tickets directly from Cronos agencies were told they would receive a message, while those who used third-party agencies had their phone numbers taken for updates.

Passengers expressed concern about when they would be able to travel and some are considering seeking refunds to travel by boat. The suspension of Ceiba Intercontinental flights, due to aircraft reviews, further limits air transport options between Malabo and Bata. Cronos Airlines passengers were still awaiting confirmed travel dates and clear information on flight resumption.

Why it mattersPassengers in Equatorial Guinea are stranded and facing uncertainty due to flight cancellations by Cronos Airlines.
