Equatorial Guinea Senate passes energy law reform

The new law modernizes the energy sector, enhances environmental protection, and boosts local content.

Equatorial Guinea's Senate approved a reform of the Hydrocarbons Law on October 28, 2026, to modernize the national energy sector and enhance environmental protection. This reform, alongside the recent Mining Law, updates the regulatory framework for extractive industries, aligning with the PNDES-Horizonte 2035 economic diversification plan.

Minister Delegate Domingo Mba Esono outlined five strategic directives: flexible contracting models, industrial natural gas use, legal stability for foreign investors, modulated royalties based on field specifics, and reinforced national content requirements for local employment and technology transfer. A National Environmental Monitoring Fund will oversee ecological impact and field abandonment financing.

The reform received support from the PDGE parliamentary group, which highlighted the hydrocarbon sector's role in national development, and the Coalition group, which urged balanced fiscal competitiveness and local technical training. The government committed to submitting model contracts to the Senate for review and holding voluntary periodic appearances to report on investments, contracts, and national employment.

Why it mattersThe reform aims to attract foreign investment and ensure local participation in the energy sector.

BEAC sets deadline for old CFA franc coin exchange

Central bank gives until October 31, 2027, to swap older coins for new currency.

The Bank of Central African States (BEAC) has set a one-year deadline for exchanging old 1 to 500 CFA franc coins across the CEMAC region. This decision, made by the UMAC Ministerial Committee, aims to streamline currency circulation. The exchange period begins on November 1, 2026, and concludes on October 31, 2027.

During the first phase, from November 1, 2026, to July 31, 2027, individuals can exchange the old coins at BEAC branches, commercial banks, and microfinance institutions. The second phase, from August 1 to October 31, 2027, restricts exchanges to BEAC offices only, with no limit on the amount for individuals and legal entities.

After October 31, 2027, the older coins will no longer be accepted. The BEAC has reminded financial intermediaries of their obligation to accept these coins from the public, regardless of customer status, to ensure a smooth transition and prevent value loss for citizens. Coins from the 2006 and 2024 series remain legal tender.

Why it mattersCitizens holding older CFA franc coins must exchange them by October 31, 2027, to avoid losing their value.

Equatorial Guinean journalists see Russian diversity

Journalists from Real Equatorial Guinea and Guinea Ecuatorial Press attended an exhibition on Russia's geography, culture, industry, and technology.

Juan Ronaldo Oluy Nká Mbengono, a journalist from Real Equatorial Guinea, and María Jesús Nzang visited an exhibition showcasing Russia's diverse regions during the third Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow.

The exhibition detailed Russia's natural resources, infrastructure, technological advancements, and cultural traditions, including shipbuilding at the Zvezda shipyard in Vladivostok and references to the Russian space program.

Participants learned about the history of navigation through Captain Anna Shchetinina, saw the Superjet 100 and MC-21 aircraft, and explored the natural beauty of Kamchatka, including its volcanic landscapes and wildlife.

Why it mattersJournalists from Equatorial Guinea gained exposure to Russia's geography, culture, industry, and technology.

Cronos Airlines cancels Malabo flights

Passengers stranded at Malabo airport face uncertainty as technical issues ground flights to Bata.

Cronos Airlines cancelled flights from Malabo to Bata on Thursday, leaving passengers stranded at the airport. Passengers were informed on Tuesday to stop checking in and go to company offices, later receiving confirmation of cancellations. Some passengers who bought tickets on Tuesday were still sold tickets despite ongoing cancellations.

A passenger showed a message received on Thursday, October 29, stating flight 901 to Bata was rescheduled due to a technical problem, with no new departure time given. Passengers who bought tickets directly from Cronos agencies were told they would receive a message, while those who used third-party agencies had their phone numbers taken for updates.

Passengers expressed concern about when they would be able to travel and some are considering seeking refunds to travel by boat. The suspension of Ceiba Intercontinental flights, due to aircraft reviews, further limits air transport options between Malabo and Bata. Cronos Airlines passengers were still awaiting confirmed travel dates and clear information on flight resumption.

Why it mattersPassengers in Equatorial Guinea are stranded and facing uncertainty due to flight cancellations by Cronos Airlines.

Sport · 2026-10-29

Equatorial Guinea football federation seeks vice president's help for match payments

FEGUIFUT asks Nguema Obiang Mangue to speed up budget releases for upcoming CAN 2027 qualifiers.

The Equatorial Guinean Football Federation (FEGUIFUT) has requested Vice President Nguema Obiang Mangue's intervention to expedite payments for the national football team, Nzalang Nacional. The federation needs budget releases for logistics, accommodation, and general expenses for upcoming matches in the CAN 2027 qualifiers. These matches are scheduled for November, including a game against Zimbabwe.

FEGUIFUT, led by Clemente Engonga Nguema Onguene, met with the Vice President and the Ministry of Sports to discuss the financial arrangements. Nguema Obiang Mangue has instructed the Treasury to review the files for an equitable budget balance before proceeding. He also directed the Ministry of Sports and FEGUIFUT to review invoices to cut unnecessary state spending.

The national team has played two matches so far, with one win and one loss. The Democratic Republic of Congo leads Group E with six points, while Zimbabwe and Equatorial Guinea each have three points. The Vice President's aim is to prevent excessive spending on international competitions for the men's national team, ensuring timely payments and proper management.

Why it mattersThe national football team's upcoming matches and their financial logistics are directly affected by the Vice President's intervention.
