Judiciary accused of inflating labor indemnities in Equatorial Guinea

A report alleges a judicial mechanism inflates labor indemnities, potentially benefiting courts and increasing costs for companies like Marathon Oil.

A judicial mechanism is allegedly inflating labor indemnities in Equatorial Guinea, according to a publication. The claim centers on a case involving Hermenegildo Esono Anguezomo, a former Government Relations Manager for Marathon, whose dismissal led to a court order for the company to pay 1,520,154,184 CFA francs in compensation, plus 45,604,625 CFA francs in legal costs.

Marathon appealed the ruling to the Supreme Court of Justice, citing an error of law. To appeal, the company had to deposit 1,878,910,572 CFA francs, which included the original judgment plus an additional 20%. The core of the complaint is that the Malabo Labor Court No. 2 calculated salary differences using a higher salary from a different position within the company, inflating the amount by 14,068,174 CFA francs.

This alleged mechanism is not an isolated incident, but a sustained practice to inflate amounts companies must deposit in judicial proceedings, according to a vice president. If confirmed, this scheme could allow judicial bodies to benefit financially regardless of the appeal's outcome, posing a risk to companies operating in Equatorial Guinea, particularly those in the oil sector.

Why it mattersCompanies operating in Equatorial Guinea, especially those in the oil sector, face increased financial risk and potential legal challenges due to alleged inflated labor indemnities.

DRC Opposition Urges President Tshisekedi to Halt Constitutional Changes

The C64 opposition coalition in the Democratic Republic of Congo is pressing President Tshisekedi to abandon constitutional reforms that could extend his term beyond 2028.

Delly Sesanga, president of the ENVOL party and a member of the C64 opposition coalition, stated that the opposition's priority is to ensure President Tshisekedi leaves office at the end of his second and final term in 2028. Tshisekedi, who has been in power since 2019, secured a second five-year term in the 2023 elections, and the current constitution prohibits him from seeking another term.

The debate over constitutional reform unfolds as the country continues to face conflicts, particularly in the east. The M23 armed group, reportedly backed by Rwanda, controls significant areas of North and South Kivu, contributing to ongoing instability. Sesanga claims that neither the government nor the armed group can achieve a decisive military victory.

Sesanga emphasized the need for an inclusive political process to address the country's security and economic crises, alongside calling for an end to hostilities. With elections scheduled for 2028, the opposition aims to increase pressure against any constitutional changes that would allow the president to prolong his stay in power.

Why it mattersThe political stability of the Democratic Republic of Congo is at stake as the opposition challenges potential constitutional changes that could prolong the president's term, impacting governance and conflict resolution.

ANIF receives training from Guatemala's IVE to improve financial crime prevention

Equatorial Guinea's National Financial Intelligence Agency (ANIF) is undergoing training from Guatemala's IVE to strengthen its capabilities against money laundering and.

The ANIF is receiving training from specialists of Guatemala's IVE, which began today and will continue until August twenty-seventh. This initiative aims to enhance the national system for preventing and combating money laundering, terrorism financing, and the financing of weapons of mass destruction proliferation, addressing recommendations from the Mutual Evaluation Report of the Central African Anti-Money Laundering Group (GABAC) and the National Action Plan.

Zenón Obiang Obiang Avomo, ANIF's national director, highlighted the importance of cooperation between similar institutions and the exchange of experiences within the Egmont Group. He emphasized that the evolving financial crime risks necessitate well-trained personnel, effective coordination mechanisms, and the ability to respond to new scenarios. The director also stressed the need to apply acquired knowledge to identify areas for improvement and implement concrete measures.

Milagrosa Obono Angüe, Minister Delegate of Finance, Budgets, and National Planning, underscored that this training is a priority within the National Action Plan and the GABAC mutual evaluation process. She emphasized the need for a cooperative structure extending beyond the Financial Intelligence Unit, involving public institutions, financial entities, and other actors to ensure the system's effectiveness in detecting financial crimes.

Why it mattersThis training directly affects financial institutions and businesses by improving the national framework for detecting and preventing financial crimes, potentially impacting their compliance requirements and operational security.

Education Ministry intensifies inspections of Malabo school renovations

The Ministry of Education is increasing oversight of rehabilitation projects at 46 schools in Malabo and Bata to ensure quality standards are met before the 2026-2027 school year.

Constantino Obiang Esono Ayingono, the Delegate Minister of Education, responsible for School and Sports Infrastructure, continued inspections of educational centers undergoing rehabilitation in Malabo. Accompanied by inspectors from GE Proyectos and regional education officials, the minister verified the progress and adherence to government standards at several schools.

During the inspections, Obiang Esono Ayingono visited the Cuartel Mañe Ela National School and Siale Bileka National School, where Lea Multiservicios is performing work. He also supervised projects at Bioko Norte National School by MS, and Cuartel Sipopo de Marina National School and 3 de Agosto de la Presidencia School, managed by VIKO S.L. Companies presented their advancements at each site.

The minister emphasized the need for serious, responsible work that meets established quality standards. This initiative is part of a national plan to improve educational infrastructure and provide suitable learning environments for students. Inspections will extend to the Continental Region for other schools in the program.

Why it mattersStudents and educators in 46 schools in Malabo and Bata are affected by these renovations, which aim to improve their learning and working conditions.

Grand Hotel Djibloho certifies students in hospitality training

The Grand Hotel Djibloho awarded certificates to students from the ANAMOA center, marking the completion of their professional training in hotel operations.

The Grand Hotel Djibloho recently certified students who completed practical training at the hotel, according to Ahora EG. The ceremony was presided over by senior management, including the Director of Operations and the Director of Human Resources. This initiative aims to strengthen professional knowledge and skills in various operational areas of the hotel sector.

Students gained practical experience across different departments, including Kitchen and Pastry, Food & Beverage, and Finance. This hands-on training provided familiarization with the professional environment of the hotel industry, bridging theoretical knowledge with direct workplace experience.

The hotel stated its commitment to empowering training, developing young talent, and strengthening professional competencies within Equatorial Guinea's hospitality and tourism sector. The Grand Hotel Djibloho plans to continue supporting such training initiatives and collaborating with educational centers to develop professionals for the industry.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects students by providing them with certified professional experience, enhancing their employability in the hospitality sector.
