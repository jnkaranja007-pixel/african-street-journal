Equatorial Guinea inflation hits 3.0% in September

Consumer prices rose across the country, with food, alcohol, transport, and health services seeing significant increases.

Equatorial Guinea recorded a 3.0% inflation rate in September 2026, surpassing the CEMAC threshold. The rise was driven by increases in several key sectors. Food and non-alcoholic beverages saw a 1.8% inflation, while alcoholic beverages and tobacco increased by 5.4%. Transport prices rose 3.6%, and health services experienced a 4.6% inflation rate. Restaurants and hotels saw a 2.7% price increase.

Within the food sector, significant price hikes were observed in coffee, tea, and cocoa (11.0%), fish (9.4%), zebu meat (11.0%), pastries (8.8%), and dairy products (14.1%). Alcoholic beverages and tobacco prices were pushed up by a 16.1% increase in spirits and a 5.9% rise in wines. Transport costs were affected by a 15.4% increase in car prices and a 5.7% rise in motorcycle prices.

Cities of Malabo and Ebibeyin registered the highest inflation rates at 4.9% each, both exceeding the community threshold. Evinayong recorded 2.7% inflation, Mongomo 1.6%, and Bata 1.1%. Overall, the general price level increased by seven tenths of a percent compared to the previous month.

Why it mattersConsumers across Equatorial Guinea face higher prices for essential goods and services, impacting household budgets.

Equatorial Guinea VP demands fuel distribution plan

The vice president has ordered Gepetrol to create a master plan within 48 hours to end the country's fuel crisis.

Equatorial Guinea's vice president has demanded a comprehensive master plan from state oil company Gepetrol to resolve the ongoing fuel crisis. He described the current situation, with multiple distributors simultaneously lacking fuel, as an unacceptable systemic failure. The vice president has given Gepetrol 48 hours to present a definitive plan aimed at preventing future shortages.

The proposed strategy includes increasing fuel purchase volumes in the short term, reclaiming leased storage tanks, and constructing new, larger facilities to expand national reserves. In the long term, the government plans to build a national refinery to ensure domestic supply and serve partner countries in the subregion. The goal is to establish Gepetrol as the central hub for national fuel distribution, allowing both domestic companies and neighboring countries to purchase fuel directly.

To inform these decisions, Gepetrol and the Ministry of Mines will analyze five years of national fuel consumption data. The vice president also proposed exporting small quantities of crude oil for refining in neighboring countries to minimize costs and increase revenue. These measures aim to strengthen Gepetrol's capacity to manage crises and supply both domestic and regional partners.

Why it mattersThe vice president's actions directly affect the government's ability to manage national resources and ensure the stable distribution of fuel to its citizens.

Military personnel tortured and jailed amid leak accusations

201 soldiers face detention and mistreatment in Black Beach prison following allegations of leaking sensitive information.

Sources within the Ministry of Defense have confirmed the detention of 201 military personnel accused of leaking sensitive information. Testimonies suggest these detentions involve coercive interrogations, leading to widespread unease within the armed forces. This operation is reportedly targeting soldiers from various branches, raising concerns about internal stability.

The accusations of leaking information are reportedly false, fueling fears that such claims are being used to target individuals who question the Ministry of Defense's operations and alleged corruption. The situation has created significant anxiety among military ranks, potentially fracturing the unity of the Equatorial Guinean Armed Forces.

The detained soldiers have been transferred to Black Beach prison, a facility known for its harsh conditions. The scale of the detentions and the alleged mistreatment highlight a severe internal security issue within the nation's military apparatus, with implications for its operational readiness and public trust.

Why it matters201 military personnel are affected by torture and imprisonment due to accusations of leaking information.

Atalia Modesta Baha Ondo appointed director general of the Vice Presidency's office

Atalia Modesta Baha Ondo, formerly head of the press and image cabinet, takes on a new leadership role within the Vice Presidency.

Atalia Modesta Baha Ondo has been appointed the new director general of the Cabinet of the Vice Presidency of the Republic. She brings a wealth of experience from her previous roles in media and institutional communication.

Baha Ondo previously served as deputy minister of Information, Press, and Radio from August 2020 to February 2023. Her career began at Televisión Guinea Ecuatorial and Asonga, where she worked as a presenter and reporter before becoming press attaché at the Equatorial Guinean Embassy in China.

Before her latest appointment, she directed the Press and Image Cabinet of the Vice Presidency since 2018, where she spearheaded institutional communication initiatives. She studied Political Science at the National University of Equatorial Guinea and has undertaken courses in image, presentation, and business management.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guinean citizens are affected by changes in government appointments that shape institutional communication.

Equatorial Guinea reshuffles economic council members

Presidential decrees dismiss and appoint officials to the National Council for Economic and Social Development.

Equatorial Guinea's President has issued decrees to change leadership within the National Council for Economic and Social Development (CNDES). Decree 126/2026 details the dismissal of several key figures, including members Bindang Ndong Okiri, Eduardo Orobiyi Lajay, and Teodora Ronda Rubio. The decree also removed Paulino Bololo Ekobo from his role as Counselor and Anacleto Nsue as Secretary General.

Following these dismissals, Decree 127/2026 announces the appointment of new officials to the CNDES. Seven new members have been named, including Anacleto Nsue Meñan Abegue and Paulino Bololo Ekobo, who were previously dismissed from different positions. Additionally, three new counselors were appointed, and 10 high-ranking officials, including a new Secretary General, Ismael Mercader Okenve, were designated to various technical and administrative roles.

The changes affect multiple levels of the CNDES, impacting its membership, advisory roles, and senior administrative functions. The newly appointed officials will oversee areas such as international relations, development agendas, social policies, industrial and environmental commissions, legal services, and budget execution. The specific reasons for the dismissals and appointments were not provided in the decrees.

Why it mattersGovernment officials and their staff are affected by these changes in leadership and roles within a key economic development council.
