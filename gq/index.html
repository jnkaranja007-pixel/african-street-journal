Equatorial Guinea minister addresses UNESCO science prize event

Minister Clemente Engonga Nguema Onguene proposed collaboration between award winners and national universities.

Equatorial Guinea's Minister of State for Education, Science, Youth and Sports, Clemente Engonga Nguema Onguene, attended a UNESCO event in Paris on November 9, 2026, to mark 10 years of the Life Sciences Research Prize. He spoke on behalf of President Obiang Nguema Mbasogo, reaffirming the nation's commitment to inclusive scientific research and reducing technological disparities.

During the event, the minister proposed creating collaboration mechanisms between the prize recipients and Equatorial Guinean universities to enhance national scientific training and research. UNESCO Director-General Khaled El-Enany highlighted the importance of opportunities for scientific talent and recognized the contributions of five researchers, including Dr. Nancy Ip from China for Alzheimer's research and Professor Carlos Martín Montañés from Spain for a new tuberculosis vaccine.

The UNESCO-Guinea Ecuatorial International Prize also awarded Professor Adnane Remmal of Morocco for antibiotic resistance studies. The second edition of the African Women Scientists Scholarship Programme honored Dr. Aude Christelle Ka’e of Cameroon for pediatric HIV research and Dr. Azizath Moussiliou of Benin for malaria prevention studies, underscoring female contributions to public health research.

Why it mattersThe event directly involved Equatorial Guinea's Minister of State for Education, Science, Youth and Sports, who proposed strengthening national scientific research and training.

Equatorial Guinea plans museums for archaeological finds

Government will establish sites in Malabo, Bata, and Ciudad de la Paz to house artifacts dating from 50 BC to 1200 AD.

The government of Equatorial Guinea plans to create museums in Malabo, Bata, and Ciudad de la Paz to preserve national archaeological heritage. This decision follows a meeting where officials presented findings from Corisco island to the vice president. 12 boxes of materials, including certified pottery pieces from 50 BC to 1200 AD, are currently held at the Ecuatoguinean Cultural Centre.

These artifacts originate from excavations on Corisco island, a project involving the government and Spanish researchers from the CSIC. The digs uncovered one of Central Africa's oldest necropolises, yielding iron tools, weapons, jewelry, and ceramics. These items offer insights into ancient daily life, cultural practices, and metallurgical skills predating colonization.

Vice President Nguema Obiang Mangue ordered the creation of suitable facilities for the protection and public display of these discoveries. He also mandated a national tour to identify and acquire antiquities from private owners, subject to expert evaluation. The initiative aims to enhance the recovery, conservation, and dissemination of the nation's historical and archaeological patrimony.

Why it mattersEquatorial Guineans will gain access to their country's historical and archaeological heritage through new museums and public displays.

Equatorial Guinea officials keep posts despite audit findings

State Secretary Jesús Namabal and Vice Minister Rimme Bosio remain in their justice ministry roles following an audit revealing irregularities.

State Secretary Jesús Namabal and Vice Minister Rimme Bosio continue in their positions at the Ministry of Justice in Equatorial Guinea, even after an official audit uncovered serious irregularities. The audit's findings, which included documentation of fraudulent contracts, have not led to their dismissal or any judicial investigations.

Diario Rombe reported that institutional corruption in Equatorial Guinea rarely results in real consequences such as investigations or dismissals. The cases of Jesús Namabal Bilingung Ngung and Rimme Bosio Riocalo exemplify this, as they retain their posts despite the ministry's audit and banking documentation revealing significant issues.

The audit, supported by official findings and banking records, highlighted irregularities linked to companies associated with high-ranking officials. This situation reflects a pattern where individuals implicated in corruption scandals often avoid accountability and remain in their government roles.

Why it mattersHigh-ranking officials in Equatorial Guinea's Ministry of Justice are not facing consequences for irregularities uncovered by an audit, indicating a lack of accountability.

Russia offers 75 scholarships to Equatorial Guinea students

Alexander Kulikov highlights university training, Russian language learning, and cultural exchange projects.

Alexander Kulikov, an African studies expert and literary translator, has proposed new avenues for educational and cultural cooperation between Equatorial Guinea and Russia. He emphasized opportunities in university training, scholarships, Russian language learning, and cultural and literary exchange projects that could foster closer ties between the two nations.

Kulikov, who holds a doctorate from St. Petersburg State University, noted that Russia annually offers 75 scholarships to citizens of Equatorial Guinea. He recalled a June 2026 visit by a delegation from Moscow Technical University of Communications and Informatics to explore collaboration in telecommunications, cybersecurity, and artificial intelligence.

The expert believes studying in Russia can equip young Equatoguineans with professional skills and international connections. Russian President Vladimir Putin also mentioned the training of Equatoguinean officials at St. Petersburg State University and the Russian Presidential Academy during an October 27 meeting in Moscow.

Why it mattersYoung Equatoguineans can access higher education and professional development through Russian scholarships and exchange programs.

Equatorial Guinea education ministry seeks stronger school oversight

New director general aims to improve evaluation and supervision of national schools.

Equatorial Guinea's Ministry of Education held a meeting at Modesto Gené Roig Polytechnic Institute to address challenges in educational inspection. Eusebio Seme Abaga Nfumu met with district inspectors to discuss their responsibilities and the difficulties they face in supervising schools.

The officials focused on establishing common evaluation criteria and control mechanisms to gather precise information on school operations. These measures are intended to help identify deficiencies and enhance educational management. The meeting also aimed to improve coordination between the General Directorate of Evaluation, Control and Monitoring of the Educational System and district inspection services.

This meeting marks the start of the new director general's tenure, as he seeks closer collaboration with inspectors. His goal is to strengthen the evaluation and supervision procedures for the national education system.

Why it mattersStudents and teachers in Equatorial Guinea will be affected by new measures to improve school oversight and management.
