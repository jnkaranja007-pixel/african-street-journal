Government Finalizes Special Economic Zone Law Implementation

A newly sanctioned law aims to boost national growth by establishing special economic zones with fiscal incentives and streamlined procedures.

Equatorial Guinea's government has convened to strategize the implementation of the recently sanctioned Law No. 8/2026, General Law of Special Economic Zones. This legislation is designed to align national development with the Sustainable National Development Strategy (Equatorial Guinea 2035) and the African Union's Agenda 2063. Key aspects include the creation of tax and customs incentives, land leasing schemes, legal guarantees against arbitrary actions, expedited administrative processes, and a consolidated Foreign and Maritime Trade One-Stop Shop.

The government has instructed its technical teams to draft the regulatory framework within an immediate, non-extendable 48-hour deadline. This working group, comprising ministers from Hydrocarbons, Transport, Labor, Finance, Treasury, Justice, Agriculture, Public Works, Land Management, and Economy and Commerce, must present the institutional structure, including statutes and the creation decree for the National Authority of Special Economic Zones. They also need to finalize territorial delimitations for industrial, agro-industrial, logistical, and technological complexes and prepare draft decrees for each priority area.

This accelerated deployment of the foundational law signals the Executive's commitment to fostering a competitive and transparent regulatory environment attractive to global capital. Equatorial Guinea seeks to invigorate key production sectors and position itself as a leading investment destination in the region through modern development instruments. The government has committed to establishing these zones as hubs for investment, production, innovation, and national economic development.

Why it mattersThe government's push to implement special economic zones with incentives and streamlined procedures directly affects investors seeking to establish businesses and access capital in Equatorial Guinea.

Students Take Extra Exams for University Access

An extraordinary examination session is scheduled for September 23 and 24, offering a chance for students to qualify for university.

Equatorial Guinea's examination commission has set September 23 and 24 for an extraordinary university entrance examination, known as Selectividad. This session is designed for students who did not pass previous tests or have outstanding phases required for university admission. The matriculation process for eligible students began on September 1 and will continue for several days, following the commission's calendar.

This examination is a crucial step for second-year Baccalaureate students aiming to pursue higher education. Meanwhile, secondary schools across the country are currently conducting recovery exams. Students who successfully pass these recovery tests will be eligible to register for the extraordinary Selectividad.

Those who do not succeed in the September extraordinary session will have another opportunity during the ordinary examination period scheduled for June of the following year. The Selectividad tests are a mandatory requirement for entry into university-level studies in the country.

Why it mattersSecond-year Baccalaureate students are affected as they must pass these exams to gain access to university education.

GETESA clarifies account warnings do not mean service suspension

The telecommunications company stated that a system error caused erroneous notifications about account blockages, assuring users their service remains active.

GETESA has assured customers that alerts warning of account blockages are not indicative of actual service suspensions. Several users reported receiving messages stating their accounts would be blocked at 23:59:59 on September 3rd, prompting concern about potential service interruptions. The company clarified that these notifications stem from a temporary issue within the notification system, which is currently being addressed by technical teams.

The erroneous messages, which instructed users to recharge their accounts to avoid blocking, have caused confusion. GETESA emphasized that receiving this alert does not obligate users to make a payment before the specified time to prevent service suspension. The company is actively working on revising and normalizing its notification system to rectify the ongoing incorrect alerts.

GETESA is urging its users to remain calm as the situation is under active management and normalization is in progress. The company's statement aims to alleviate the anxiety caused by the system malfunction and reaffirm the continuity of its services for all affected customers.

Why it mattersUsers of GETESA's services are affected by erroneous notifications that could lead them to believe their accounts are at risk of suspension.

GETESA Opens AfriMall Store to Improve Customer Access

The telecommunications company expands its physical presence to offer a wider range of services and better customer support.

GETESA has inaugurated a new store at AfriMall, aiming to bring its services closer to the population. The location will allow customers to manage their mobile lines, including SIM card activation, renewals, replacements, and ownership changes. Additionally, customers can inquire about and subscribe to various internet services, such as TRINET and 4G mobile internet, for both personal and professional use.

The new outlet also stocks essential equipment for these services, including mobile phones from brands like ZTE, Nubia, and Redmi, as well as modems and routers. Standard customer service operations, such as top-ups and service inquiries, will also be available. This opening follows GETESA's recent expansion, including a new store in Malabo in July.

This initiative aligns with the new management's strategy to enhance accessibility and improve the user experience by increasing face-to-face service points. The company views this as part of its ongoing modernization and transformation process, aiming to facilitate customer interactions and streamline service administration.

Why it mattersThe new GETESA store in AfriMall provides easier access to mobile line management, internet services, and necessary equipment for residents.

Grand Hotel Djibloho Offers Tourism Package

The hotel's World Tourism Day promotion combines accommodation, dining, leisure, and excursions for groups.

El Grand Hotel Djibloho has launched a special weekend package to promote national tourism, coinciding with World Tourism Day. The initiative offers a comprehensive experience including round-trip transport from Bata, two nights' accommodation with breakfast, and a special "Taste of the World" dinner. This package aims to showcase Djibloho's attractions by integrating various elements into a single offering.

The program also features an outdoor Afro Fitness session and an excursion to Ndong Mba and Malamba, designed to immerse participants in the local culture and natural environment. Entertainment is provided through a White Party at Ozean Pool Bar, complete with live music and a DJ. This multi-faceted approach seeks to provide a memorable national tourism experience.

This special offer is available for groups of at least 10 people. Pricing is set at 205,000 XAF per person for single occupancy and 290,000 XAF for double occupancy. Reservations and further information can be obtained via WhatsApp at +240 555 502 359. The hotel is promoting domestic travel through this curated weekend getaway.

Why it mattersThe Grand Hotel Djibloho's tourism package affects investors by offering a new business venture and potential revenue stream through group bookings and package sales.
