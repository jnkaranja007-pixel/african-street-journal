Equatorial Guinea Businesses Lose Value Due to Poor Public Perception

Many companies in Equatorial Guinea, despite years of operation and significant contributions, remain largely unknown outside their immediate circles, hindering their market.

Businesses in Equatorial Guinea are failing to translate their internal value into public recognition, according to a report from Ahora EG. The publication highlights that even companies with two decades of history and substantial contributions to economic activities often struggle with a weak or non-existent public presence.

This issue is not exclusive to large markets; it affects local businesses that are well-known by clients, suppliers, and employees but not by the broader market. The report suggests that while these companies may be extraordinary in their operations, their inability to effectively communicate their value prevents them from building a strong market position.

The problem is not a lack of activity or value creation, but rather a failure to make existing achievements visible. Effective business communication, the report notes, should consistently answer who a company is, what it does well, why it matters, and why it should be remembered, rather than just demonstrating activity.

Why it mattersBusinesses in Equatorial Guinea are directly affected by losing potential market value and growth opportunities due to their inability to communicate their existing value to the wider market.

Equatorial Guinea Government Removes Provincial Court President Over Abuse Case

The government dismissed Lucas Ela Meñe Bilingun from his recent appointment as president of the Provincial Courts of Bioko Norte, Bioko Sur, and Annobón due to a prior sexual.

The decision was made during a meeting at the People's Palace in Malabo, chaired by Vice President Teodoro Nguema Obiang. The government reviewed Bilingun's file, which revealed he had previously been prosecuted for sexually abusing a thirteen-year-old minor. Despite this record, he had been reinstated to positions of responsibility within the Justice Administration.

This action is part of a broader government initiative to tighten screening processes for public appointments. The executive noted that some individuals previously convicted or jailed for fraud, or linked to corruption cases, had been proposed by ministers and appointed to other administrative roles. The government stated that individuals with backgrounds compromising responsibility, trust, and suitability for certain functions should not hold positions of special responsibility.

The new criteria for public office candidates now include a more thorough review of their backgrounds and suitability, beyond just professional experience. This aims to prevent those previously convicted of crimes or involved in serious administrative misconduct from returning to positions of responsibility through new appointments, establishing higher standards for each designation.

Why it mattersCitizens of Equatorial Guinea are directly affected as the government implements stricter vetting for public officials, aiming to ensure integrity in leadership and prevent individuals with criminal records from holding positions of power.

Vincenzo Alberto Annese signs two-year contract as Nzalang Nacional coach

The Italian coach Vincenzo Alberto Annese officially became the new manager of Equatorial Guinea's national football team, signing a two-year contract on Wednesday, August.

Annese takes over from Juan Micha Obiang, who led the team through a period considered its best in history, including three consecutive Africa Cup of Nations (CAN) qualifications. The new coach faces an immediate challenge to qualify for next year's CAN, with competitive matches beginning next month.

His appointment comes at a demanding time for the Nzalang Nacional, which failed to advance past the group stage in the last CAN. Additionally, the team recently saw the retirement of Emilio Nsue, the all-time leading scorer and captain, and midfielder Pedro Obiang, creating significant gaps in the squad.

Annese expressed satisfaction and commitment during his official presentation at FEGUIFUT in Malabo, acknowledging the scale of the challenge. He emphasized the need for effort to achieve positive results and maintain the Nzalang Nacional's standing in African football amidst these player departures.

Why it mattersFootball fans in Equatorial Guinea are affected by this change as it signals a new era for their national team, impacting their hopes for future tournament qualifications and performance.

Trump administration halts immigrant visa processing globally

The Trump administration has paused interviews and other processing for immigrant visas at US consulates worldwide to train staff on detecting applicants who might use social.

The US State Department announced a global pause on immigrant visa processing, impacting those seeking legal permanent residency through family ties or the visa lottery. This measure does not affect non-immigrant visas for tourism, business, or H1-B skilled workers, which will continue as usual. The department did not specify the duration of this pause.

This suspension is in place to allow for "in-depth training" of diplomatic staff to identify visa applicants who might become a "public charge" or rely on US public assistance reserved for eligible American citizens, according to a State Department spokesperson. The Financial Times reported this information.

Earlier this month, the State Department revoked over 175,000 visas from foreign nationals for violating terms, committing crimes, inciting violence, defrauding Americans, abusing the immigration system, or endangering national security. This new pause aims to ensure visa applicants do not risk becoming a burden on the public system.

Why it mattersIndividuals seeking to immigrate to the US for family reunification or through the visa lottery are directly affected by the indefinite suspension of visa processing.

Government reforms non-oil revenue collection procedures

The Prime Minister of Equatorial Guinea met with Treasury officials to address inconsistencies and improve the collection of non-oil tax revenues across ministerial departments.

Prime Minister Manuel Osa Nsue Nsua met with Treasury officials in Malabo II to review non-oil revenue reports from ministerial portfolios. The meeting aimed to refine actions for improving tax collection and establish uniform procedures among ministerial departments, the Treasury, and the General Treasury.

The Prime Minister proposed structural reforms, including expanding and strengthening key dependencies within ministries, particularly Economic Section Heads. These actions are considered urgent to rectify ministerial accounts. The official in charge of Administrative Coordination noted that a lack of understanding of the tax law has led to arbitrary issuance of income notes, often not by economic departments, causing chaos in fee amortization.

Treasury officials received direct instructions to implement immediate actions, including drafting a ministerial order detailing measures for economic leadership in ministerial departments. This order will address organizational deficiencies in economic sections, which are identified as the source of many irregularities. Another key aspect will be the purification of accounting entries to identify anomalous or unreal collections and verify document authenticity, aiming to surpass previous collection levels and foster a modern, transparent, and self-sufficient public administration model.

Why it mattersThe government's push for transparent and efficient revenue collection directly affects businesses and individuals through changes in tax procedures and enforcement.
