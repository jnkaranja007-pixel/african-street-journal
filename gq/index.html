Court Convicts Man in Tupac Shakur Murder Case

A jury found Duane Davis guilty of murder and conspiracy for his role in the 1996 shooting death of the influential rapper.

Thirty years after the event, a court has convicted Duane Davis in connection with the murder of Tupac Shakur. Shakur died at age 25 from gunshot wounds sustained while a passenger in a BMW. The attack occurred on September 7, 1996, following a Mike Tyson boxing match in Las Vegas. Prosecutors stated Davis did not fire the shots but was central to planning the assault, including procuring the weapon and coordinating individuals in a Cadillac from which the shots were fired.

The shooting followed a physical altercation at the MGM Grand between Shakur's associates and Orlando "Baby Lane" Anderson, Davis's nephew and a member of the South Side Compton Crips. Investigators identified this confrontation as the trigger for the fatal retaliation. Davis's own public statements over the years, including interviews and his book "Compton Street Legend," became key evidence for the prosecution, despite his defense arguing they were self-serving and lacked physical corroboration.

After several weeks of trial, the jury deliberated for less than three hours before reaching its verdict. Davis, who remains in custody, faces a potential life sentence, with sentencing scheduled for October 13. This conviction marks a significant turning point in a case that had become a major mystery in American popular culture, fueled by decades of speculation and theories surrounding the hip-hop rivalry of the era.

Why it mattersDuane Davis has been convicted of murder and conspiracy for his role in the 1996 shooting death of rapper Tupac Shakur, resolving a decades-old mystery.

DRC Ebola Outbreak Exceeds 6,000 Cases

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola outbreak has surpassed 6,000 confirmed cases and nearly 3,000 deaths as it spreads rapidly across six provinces.

Health authorities in the Democratic Republic of Congo reported 6,041 confirmed Ebola cases and 2,911 deaths as of August 31. The outbreak, declared on May 15, has now reached six provinces and dozens of health zones in the country's north and east. This epidemic has already surpassed the previous record of 3,317 cases recorded between 2018 and 2020, making it the largest and fastest-spreading Ebola outbreak ever reported in the nation.

The World Health Organization has identified the Bundibugyo virus, a rare strain of Ebola for which no approved vaccine or specific treatment exists, as the cause of the current outbreak. This has significantly complicated efforts to control its transmission. Ituri province remains the epicenter, with cases also confirmed in North Kivu, South Kivu, Haut-Uélé, Tshopo, and Bas-Uélé provinces. By August 26, the outbreak had extended to 60 health zones.

The World Health Organization's latest data indicates a fatality rate of approximately 48%, highlighting the severity of the disease and the challenges in early detection and treatment. Despite these grim statistics, over 1,300 individuals have recovered, offering a glimmer of hope to health officials. The WHO has cautioned that this outbreak could potentially exceed the 2014-2016 West Africa Ebola epidemic, which caused over 11,000 deaths and remains the deadliest on record.

Why it mattersThe rapidly spreading Ebola outbreak in the DRC, characterized by a high fatality rate and a rare virus strain, poses a significant public health concern for the region.

Malabo students register for September university entrance exams

Registration for the extraordinary September 2026 university entrance exams opens today in Malabo for students seeking a second chance at higher education.

Students in Malabo can now register for the extraordinary September university entrance examinations, beginning today, September 1. The registration process will be open in the morning from nine to twelve and again in the afternoon from two to four, with specific time slots allocated for different educational centers. This opportunity is for students who did not pass the initial examinations and wish to gain access to university studies.

To complete registration, students must provide a national identity document or passport, or a literal birth certificate for those under eighteen. A passport-sized photograph is also required. Students from Bata will need a photocopy of their matriculation proof, and all applicants must submit stamped effects worth two thousand five hundred CFA francs and four thousand CFA francs in cash for the registration fee.

Following the national recovery tests, which also commence today, registration details for official students who have successfully completed second-year baccalaureate studies in September will be announced. This extraordinary session aims to provide a pathway for students to overcome pending tests and secure their university admission.

Why it mattersStudents who did not pass previous university entrance exams can register to retake them in September.

Equatorial Guinea Sets 2026-2027 Agricultural Prices

New minimum prices for cocoa, coffee, and coconut are established, alongside fixed input costs for the upcoming agricultural season.

The agricultural campaign for cocoa, coffee, and coconut in Equatorial Guinea has officially opened for the 2026-2027 season. The government has set new minimum internal marketing prices for these key export products, acknowledging the volatility of international markets. These prices are crucial for producers who face fluctuating global demand and the rising costs of essential agricultural inputs.

Producers will see revised prices for various grades of dried cocoa, ranging from 1,250 CFA francs per kilogram for Grade 2 to 450 CFA francs for Grade 3, with specific rates for sun-dried and damaged cocoa. Coffee prices are set at 550 CFA francs per kilogram for clean coffee and lower rates for other grades. Coconuts are priced at 100 CFA francs for peeled and 50 CFA francs for unpeeled units.

To support farmers, the government will subsidize agricultural inputs, including a 50 kg bag of copper sulfate for 15,000 CFA francs and a 200 kg drum of quicklime for 15,000 CFA francs. Subsidies for cocoa will also be adjusted based on international market price fluctuations, aiming to stimulate farmer efforts and stabilize production.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected by the new minimum prices for cocoa, coffee, and coconuts, as well as the subsidized costs of essential inputs like copper sulfate and quicklime.

Ministry and Gepetrol Seguros Discuss Insurance Strategies

Officials from the Ministry of Mines, Hydrocarbons and Electricity met with Gepetrol Seguros to explore new insurance solutions for the energy sector and national projects.

The Ministry of Mines, Hydrocarbons and Electricity and Gepetrol Seguros are analyzing collaboration strategies, focusing on the insurance company's expansion plans and its role in supporting strategic national projects. Discussions included developing insurance solutions specifically for the electrical sector and other key areas of the country's development.

Gepetrol Seguros also expressed interest in participating in the African Energy Week, aiming to strengthen its continental presence and expand its network within the African energy sector. This engagement is part of the company's broader strategy for international growth and projection.

The meeting fostered dialogue and collaboration between the two entities, highlighting the importance of institutional cooperation in identifying new opportunities and initiatives for the development of Equatorial Guinea's strategic sectors. Both parties agreed to follow up on discussed topics to advance potential collaborations.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the potential for new insurance solutions linked to the electrical sector and strategic projects, which could support business operations and development.
