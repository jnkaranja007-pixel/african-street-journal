News · 2026-10-23

fire kills six in malabo family home

Salvador Nsue Nguema lost his wife, three children, and two grandchildren in the blaze.

A fire in the Semu-Salomé neighborhood of Malabo killed six members of Salvador Nsue Nguema's family on October 22, 2026. The victims included Nguema's wife, three of his children, and two grandchildren. Nguema himself survived but was hospitalized with burns.

The family had been using candles for light after an electricity outage, and investigators believe a candle may have started the fire. Nguema stated that security bars on the windows and doors hindered rescue efforts. The blaze also destroyed all the family's belongings, prompting Nguema to seek assistance.

This tragedy echoes a similar incident months prior in Malabo, where Cosme Nguema Ondo lost his pregnant wife and six children when a retaining wall collapsed on their home. Both Nguema and Ondo originate from Nkahale Yebiveñ, a village in the Añisok district, linking these two family disasters.

Why it mattersSalvador Nsue Nguema and his surviving family members lost loved ones and property in a fire.

Equatorial Guinea updates 2006 textbooks

A technical commission of over 90 sub-commissions will review and update educational materials.

Equatorial Guinea's Ministry of Education is updating and reprinting teaching manuals last revised in 2006. Vice President of the Republic presided over a meeting in Malabo yesterday to review progress on this project. The updated books will reflect changes in the country over the past 20 years, including information on municipalities, districts, history, culture, and traditions.

Minister of State for Education, Clemente Engonga Nguema Onguene, explained that a technical working commission with over 90 sub-commissions has been formed to revise the books. Teachers' contributions are being considered, and the goal is to incorporate recent national developments. The Vice President authorized the editorial representatives to work with ministry technicians on the revisions.

Two workshops will be held in Malabo and Bata to analyze reports from the working commissions and consolidate observations into a single document for the publisher. The Prime Minister has been asked to ensure other involved ministries submit pending information to prevent further delays.

Why it mattersStudents and teachers in Equatorial Guinea will use updated textbooks reflecting current national information.

Avante Talent program graduates its first class

The AVANTE agency in Malabo awarded certificates to students completing a six-month professional internship.

The AVANTE agency in Malabo held its first AVANTE TALENTO closing ceremony on October 19. The program, a professional internship initiative, was a collaboration between AVANTE and BANGE Business School. Students completed six months of practical training in communication, marketing, and media.

During the internship, participants worked with AVANTE teams to learn industry processes and demands. The ceremony recognized their dedication and growth, with AVANTE General Director Sergio Fernando Sehe Orichi highlighting the importance of such opportunities for young people. He expressed satisfaction with the students' progress and AVANTE's commitment to sharing expertise.

This initiative aims to bridge the gap between education and business needs, allowing students to apply academic knowledge in a real professional setting. AVANTE, an agency with offices in Equatorial Guinea, Spain, and Miami, reaffirmed its commitment to local talent development and creating opportunities for the next generation.

Why it mattersYoung people in Equatorial Guinea gain practical skills and professional experience through the AVANTE TALENTO program.

Equatorial Guinea inflation hits 3.0% in September

Food, drink, and transport prices drove the monthly inflation rate to 3.0%, exceeding the CEMAC threshold.

Equatorial Guinea recorded a 3.0% inflation rate in September 2026, surpassing the CEMAC threshold. This rise was largely fueled by increases in food and non-alcoholic beverages, which saw a 1.8% inflation, and alcoholic beverages and tobacco at 5.4%. Transport prices also climbed by 3.6%, and health services by 4.6%.

Within the food category, significant price hikes were observed in coffee, tea, and cocoa (11.0%), fish (9.4%), and zebu meat (11.0%). Dairy products increased by 14.1%, and poultry by 6.9%. Alcoholic beverages saw spirits rise by 16.1%.

Malabo and Ebibeyin cities both registered a 4.9% inflation rate, exceeding the community threshold. Evinayong recorded 2.7%, Mongomo 1.6%, and Bata 1.1%. Overall prices increased by seven-tenths of a percent compared to the previous month.

Why it mattersConsumers in Equatorial Guinea face higher prices for essential goods and services, impacting household budgets.

Equatorial Guinea president pardons 70 prisoners

President Obiang Nguema Mbasogo grants clemency to 70 individuals, facilitating social reintegration and family reunification.

President Obiang Nguema Mbasogo of Equatorial Guinea has pardoned 70 individuals, aiming to aid their social reintegration and family reunification. Those granted clemency must avoid reoffending; repeat offenders will lose their pardon benefits. Foreign nationals who receive this pardon will be repatriated to their home countries immediately after their release.

The presidential pardon includes a list of 70 names, such as Joel Nguema Mba, Virgilio Emuara Esono, and Collins Wandun. This measure does not affect civil liabilities stemming from the convictions, unless explicitly stated otherwise by law. The beneficiaries are expected to comply with the terms to retain the pardon's benefits.

The presidential decree aims to facilitate the return of individuals to society and their families. The list of beneficiaries is extensive, covering a range of individuals whose sentences have been commuted by the head of state. This action is a direct presidential intervention in the judicial process.

Why it matters70 prisoners in Equatorial Guinea have been pardoned by the president, impacting their freedom and family lives.
