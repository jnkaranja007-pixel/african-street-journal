CEMAC meets in Malabo to propose governance and funding reforms

Officials, experts and Technical Secretariat staff are reviewing how powers, programmes and resources are shared across the regional body.

Sources: Ahora EG and Real Equatorial Guinea · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Officials, experts and representatives of the Technical Secretariat of the Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC) are meeting in Malabo to examine problems in how powers are divided, how programmes are carried out and how much money is available. The meeting is chaired by Mbasogo Kung Nguidang, who also heads the Steering Committee.

In her opening speech, Mbasogo Kung Nguidang said the sessions should build on the issues examined from 26 to 28 October in Malabo and agree technical positions that can become concrete measures. The agenda covers four areas: governance, financial resources, human resources, and monitoring and evaluation. On finance, she stressed the need to guarantee the effective transfer of the Community Integration Fee (TCI), on which institutional budgets depend, and warned that uncertainty over funding requires collective responses.

The proposals draw on studies by the AUDITEC-FOIRIER Consulting and KPMG consortium and on the 2006-2010 Institutional Reform Programme. Conclusions must record agreed points, reservations and matters needing decisions from competent bodies, with recommendations ranked by priority, legal viability and financial impact. The distribution of posts subject to mandate has deadlines set for December.

Why it mattersInstitutions depend on the Community Integration Fee (TCI), so its effective transfer determines whether their budgets and activities can be carried out, and the reform file has deadlines set for December.
