CEMAC completes review of institutional reform proposals in Malabo

The six member states’ ministerial committee will assess recommendations on administrative and financial changes before leaders decide what to adopt.

Sources: Ahora EG and Real Equatorial Guinea · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

The Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC) completed a two-day review of proposed administrative and financial reforms in Malabo, Equatorial Guinea, on 18 November. The talks examined weaknesses in the regional organisation’s institutions and measures intended to improve their functioning.

Ramón Castro Nvomo Bibangá, who leads the programme’s monitoring cell, said the review consolidated recommendations from a preliminary diagnosis, after member states submitted comments on the report. The proposals will go to a steering committee made up of ministerial representatives from the six CEMAC states.

The committee will select proposals for possible submission to the Conference of Heads of State and Government, which has authority to decide on reforms. The review follows an initial examination of the diagnosis from 26 to 28 October.

Why it mattersMinisterial representatives from all six CEMAC states must assess the recommendations before the heads of state and government decide on reforms.
