Equatorial Guinea lawmakers demand explanation of monthly social-housing charge

The government's public works minister told parliament the levy is a hard social problem, while deputies pressed for an account of where the money goes.

Sources: Ahora EG and Real Equatorial Guinea · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Equatorial Guinea's Chamber of Deputies summoned government ministers to explain the monthly deduction of CFA10,000 from residents of social housing across the country, a complaint that has caused widespread unrest. Deputies want to know where the money goes and how it is used.

Justino Nchama Ondo, the delegate minister for public works, territorial planning and housing, appeared on behalf of the government and said the issue was a social problem that was hard for the government to solve. He asked parliament for support in finding a lasting solution. Deputies said the housing developments were not receiving maintenance and that the current balance of the account had not been justified.

Deputies recommended that each resident's deduction be credited to their monthly housing repayment and that neighbourhood communities organise cleaning of their estates. Salvador Nguema Mangue, spokesman for the Democratic Coalition, demanded an official government explanation, while José Pablo Nvo Owono Aviri, deputy spokesman for the ruling Democratic Party of Equatorial Guinea (PDGE), also backed using the money to pay off the housing.

Why it mattersSocial-housing residents are paying CFA10,000 each month, and lawmakers are asking the government to account for the fund and consider crediting it to housing repayments.
