CEMAC reviews Malabo proposals to reform its institutions

The Central African bloc is examining governance, funding, staffing and oversight, with deadlines for a mandate-based post review set for December.

Sources: Ahora EG and Real Equatorial Guinea · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

The Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC) is reviewing proposals in Malabo to improve governance and financing across its institutions. The sessions bring together institutional officials, experts and representatives of its Technical Secretariat to examine problems in the division of responsibilities, programme delivery and access to resources.

Mbasogo Kung Nguidang, the steering committee’s president, said the discussions should build on issues examined in Malabo from 26 to 28 October and produce practical measures for future reform decisions. The agenda covers governance, finances, human resources, and monitoring and evaluation. The minister said CEMAC institutions depend on effective transfers of the Community Integration Tax to fund budgets and activities, while uncertainty over financing requires a collective response.

A review of posts subject to mandates is to follow its scheduled stages, with deadlines in December. The conclusions will record agreements, reservations and matters needing decisions by competent bodies; recommendations are to be prioritised and assessed for legal feasibility and financial implications. They will inform the next steering committee session.

Why it mattersCEMAC institutions rely on transfers of the Community Integration Tax for their budgets and activities, while the review of mandate-based posts has deadlines in December.
