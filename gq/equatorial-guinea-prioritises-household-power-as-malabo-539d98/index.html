Equatorial Guinea prioritises household power as Malabo turbine repair drags on

The vice-president ordered the state electricity company, Sociedad de Electricidad de Guinea Ecuatorial (SEGESA), to favour homes over street lighting while a damaged turbine is.

Sources: Ahora EG and Real Equatorial Guinea · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Equatorial Guinea's Vice-President Nguema Obiang Mangue has ordered the state electricity company, the Sociedad de Electricidad de Guinea Ecuatorial (SEGESA), to give household use priority over street lighting as it manages available power amid outages in several Malabo neighbourhoods. The order followed a meeting on service recovery and maintenance of electricity infrastructure.

A representative of CMEC, the company responsible for operating the Turbogas plant, said one turbine failed on 12 November and specialists estimate repairs will take three to four months. Obiang Mangue also ordered the appointment of an operations director to oversee street-light maintenance and respond to faults.

SEGESA is to prepare a technical study with CMEC and SINOHYDRO on expanding the street-lighting network. A feasibility analysis will also examine remote controls for lighting in the country's main cities; any system remains subject to the study's findings.

Why it mattersHouseholds in Malabo are to receive priority over street lighting while SEGESA manages available power during repairs estimated at three to four months.
