Sudan plans to mobilise 6,000 fighters on Ethiopian border

A Sudanese Special Forces official announced the plan in Gedaref State, accusing Ethiopia of assembling troops near the frontier.

Source: Radio Tamazuj · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Sudanese military officials have announced plans to mobilise 6,000 fighters along the country's border with Ethiopia in Gedaref State. Major General Ammar Sayed Ahmed, head of the mobilisation and support committee of the Sudanese army's Special Forces, made the announcement at a press conference on Monday.

Ahmed accused Ethiopia of assembling troops and military formations near the Sudanese border, describing the build-up as a growing security threat that would not deter Sudan from defending its territory. Major General Amer Hassan Abbas, a Sudanese army security expert, accused Ethiopia of supporting armed groups fighting the Sudanese army.

Abbas alleged that the Rapid Support Forces and the Sudan People's Liberation Movement-North, led by Abdelaziz Adam al-Hilu, were seeking to open a new front from Ethiopian territory. He warned that further escalation along the border could have serious consequences for both countries and their populations.

Why it mattersTraders and families along the Gedaref frontier face a heightened risk of cross-border fighting, with Abbas warning of serious consequences for both countries' populations.
