African Union and China train officials in AI-based disaster warnings

A five-day Addis Ababa workshop taught participants to use AI, satellite data and geographic information systems to improve hazard forecasting.

Source: Capital Ethiopia · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

The African Union Commission and China trained officials in Addis Ababa from November 23 to 27 on using artificial intelligence, geographic information systems and other tools in multi-hazard early warning systems. The five-day workshop at African Union headquarters brought together participants from member states, regional economic communities and continental institutions.

The training covered satellite observation, remote sensing and data analysis for monitoring hazards, assessing vulnerability and forecasting impacts. Participants were expected to apply products from the Africa Multi-Hazard Early Warning and Early Action System in national disaster planning. African Union Commissioner Moses Vilakati said the 2026/2027 El Nino threatens up to 50 million people with hunger.

The African Union says floods, droughts, tropical cyclones and heatwaves cause human, environmental and economic losses, while limited institutional capacity and warning coverage constrain preparedness. China's deputy mission head Zhang Wei said only about 40% of African countries have established multi-hazard warning systems.

Why it mattersDisaster-risk officials from African countries are expected to use the system's products in national planning, while only about 40% of countries have established multi-hazard warnings.
