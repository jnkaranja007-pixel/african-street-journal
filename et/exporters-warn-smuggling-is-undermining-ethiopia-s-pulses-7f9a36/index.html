Exporters warn smuggling is undermining Ethiopia’s pulses and oilseeds trade

The exporters’ association says illegal channels are weakening formal sales as Ethiopia seeks to expand processing, quality and overseas markets.

Source: Capital Ethiopia · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

The Ethiopian Pulses and Oilseeds Exporters Association warned that cross-border smuggling is undermining Ethiopia’s formal exports of pulses and oilseeds. Association President Idao Abdi said agricultural commodities continue to leave through illegal channels despite government efforts to curb the trade.

Trade and Regional Integration Minister Yasmin Wahabrebi said action against illegal trade requires coordination across government institutions. She said foreign-exchange reforms had reduced some incentives for smuggling, especially those linked to black-market currency trading. Neither speaker estimated how much product or export value was lost.

The association said Ethiopia exported 555,000 metric tonnes of pulses and oilseeds last year, earning more than US$571 million. Abdi said post-harvest losses could reach 25% of harvested crops, while exporters also need investment in processing, quality and access to markets.

Why it mattersPulse and oilseed producers and exporters face crop losses of up to 25% after harvest, while illegal trade undermines formal exports.
