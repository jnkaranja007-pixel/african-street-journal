El Niño threatens Ethiopia’s 2027 growth with harvest losses

An IFPRI analysis projects up to 1.1% slower growth as poor rains put crops, household incomes and food supplies at risk.

Source: Capital Ethiopia · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Ethiopia’s economy could grow 0.4% to 1.1% below its baseline projection in 2027 as El Niño-driven rainfall shortages threaten farm output, according to a November 17 analysis by the International Food Policy Research Institute (IFPRI). The average projected reduction is about 0.8%.

In a moderate-risk scenario, weak rainfall and high global fuel prices could push 905,000 more people into poverty and 2.8 million into undernourishment, researchers estimate. An IFPRI rapid assessment found maize and sorghum planting down an estimated 6% in parts of North Shewa and North Wollo; a full national crop assessment is pending.

The meher harvest, gathered from October through December, accounts for more than 95% of Ethiopia’s annual agricultural output. Local fuel prices were about 40% higher in August than at the start of 2026, but researchers said El Niño poses a greater threat to economic output.

Why it mattersFarmers in North Shewa and North Wollo have an estimated 6% less land planted with maize and sorghum, while the national crop assessment is still pending.
