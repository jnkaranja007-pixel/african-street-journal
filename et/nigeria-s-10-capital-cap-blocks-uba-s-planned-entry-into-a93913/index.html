Nigeria's 10% capital cap blocks UBA's planned entry into Ethiopia

UBA Chairman Tony Elumelu said the bank's proposed investment exceeded the limit on Nigerian banks' capital in foreign subsidiaries.

Source: Capital Ethiopia · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

United Bank for Africa (UBA), one of Nigeria's largest financial institutions, has halted plans to enter Ethiopia after Nigeria's banking investment rules blocked the proposed expansion. The bank had sought to become the first international bank to establish operations in Ethiopia after the country opened its banking sector to foreign investors.

UBA Chairman Tony Elumelu said the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) limits banks' investment in foreign subsidiaries to 10% of their capital. He said UBA approached the regulator but was told its proposed investment exceeded the cap. Elumelu said the bank accepted the decision.

Ethiopia's National Bank is finalising rules for foreign participation, including ownership limits, acquisitions and regulatory approvals. A draft directive on foreign ownership and equity investment was released this month.

Why it mattersThe National Bank of Ethiopia is finalising ownership and approval rules for foreign banks, while UBA cannot proceed under Nigeria's 10% capital cap.
