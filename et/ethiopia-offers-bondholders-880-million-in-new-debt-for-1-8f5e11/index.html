Ethiopia offers bondholders $880 million in new debt for $1 billion

The exchange cuts principal by 12%, pays missed interest and offers investors a $5 fee for valid instructions before December 11.

Source: Capital Ethiopia · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Ethiopia launched an exchange offer on November 25 for its defaulted $1 billion international bond, proposing $880 million in new securities and cash for missed interest. The Ministry of Finance is inviting institutional investors holding notes due in 2024 to exchange their bonds for new amortising notes carrying a 6.15% annual coupon and maturing on July 15, 2029.

The offer includes $99.38 million for three missed interest payments. After estimated committee expenses of about $9.61 million, investors are expected to receive roughly $89.77 per $1,000 of existing principal. Bondholders who submit valid instructions before December 11 will also receive a $5 consent fee per $1,000 and detachable warrants to subscribe to a future Ethiopian bond issue of up to $1 billion.

A written resolution needs approval from holders of at least 75% of the outstanding principal to make the exchange binding on all bondholders. The International Monetary Fund confirmed the terms align with Ethiopia’s debt sustainability targets, and official creditor committee co-chairs did not object.
