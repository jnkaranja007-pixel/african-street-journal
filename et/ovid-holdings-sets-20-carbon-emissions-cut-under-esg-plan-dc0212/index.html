OVID Holdings sets 20% carbon emissions cut under ESG plan

The real estate arm will change how it designs, builds and sources materials for its projects to meet the target.

Source: Capital Ethiopia · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Under its wider Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) commitments, OVID Holdings has committed to cutting its carbon dioxide (CO₂) emissions by 20 percent, with the pledge sitting inside its "North Star" strategy. The building and construction industry accounts for roughly 37 to 39 percent of global energy-related emissions, which the company cites as the reason its real estate work is in scope.

Engineer Muluken Mitiku, chief operating officer of OVID's real estate business unit, said the target will be met through changes to construction methods, material choices and how projects are carried out. Practical steps include avoiding unnecessary tree felling and cutting the use of plywood for scaffolding and formwork, which the company says would limit deforestation and related emissions.

Hayat Mohammed, OVID Construction Design's chief engineer, named three stages where the company can act: design, construction, and monitoring and accountability. At design stage, she said building orientation, natural light and ventilation, structural systems and the volume of concrete and steel can all be adjusted, while construction-stage plans include reducing material waste and monitoring fuel and electricity use on site.

Why it mattersClients and contractors working with OVID will see changes in how its projects are designed, what materials they use and how waste and fuel use are tracked on site.
