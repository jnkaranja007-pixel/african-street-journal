48 killed in Southeast Oromia armed attack

Ethiopian Human Rights Commission confirms 20 injured, 247 households displaced and 21 houses destroyed.

48 people died and 20 were injured in an armed attack on October 24, 2026, in Ethiopia's Southeast Oromia Zone. The Ethiopian Human Rights Commission (EHRC) reported that the OLF-Shene group attacked Birbona Chole Kebele, Sirka Woreda, destroying 21 houses and displacing 247 households. An undetermined number of cattle and sheep were also looted.

The EHRC investigation found the victims included 23 kebele security personnel, five militia members, and 20 civilians. The Commission gathered information from victims, families, eyewitnesses, and officials, verifying the killings through field and digital methods. EHRC Chief Commissioner Berhanu Adelo called the incident extremely serious and urged the government to strengthen civilian protection and improve early warning systems.

The Commission has repeatedly urged the government to prevent recurring attacks in the former Arsi Zone and bring perpetrators to justice. The EHRC is continuing investigations into human rights violations in Southeast Oromia and monitoring other regions including Gambella, the Oromia-Somali border, Amhara, and Tigray.

Why it mattersCivilians in Southeast Oromia are directly affected by an armed attack that resulted in deaths, injuries, displacement, and property destruction.

China grants Ethiopia $15 million for railway parts

The funds will support spare parts and technical assistance for the Ethio-Djibouti railway.

China has provided Ethiopia with a $15 million grant to supply spare parts and maintenance equipment for the Ethio-Djibouti railway. The agreement, signed in Addis Ababa on November 4, 2026, aims to enhance the reliability of this transport link. The grant will cover mechanical and electrical components for locomotives, track parts, and station accessories, along with machinery for inspection and maintenance.

Ethiopia’s State Minister of Finance, Tiguist Fisseha, and China’s Minister Counsellor Liu Xiaoguang signed the deal. The funding also includes technical assistance from Chinese experts to train railway staff and build local capacity. This support is intended to improve the railway's operational reliability and maintenance, particularly since Ethiopia and Djibouti took over management in May 2024.

The Ethio-Djibouti railway, which began operations in January 2018, is Ethiopia's main gateway to the sea. This grant builds on previous cooperation, including a five-year technical consultancy agreement signed in December 2025. Both nations pledged to coordinate the project's implementation for sustainable and efficient corridor operation.

Why it mattersEthiopia's primary maritime gateway, the Ethio-Djibouti railway, will receive critical spare parts and technical training to improve its operational reliability.

Ethiopian farmers learn soil and fertilizer innovations

Field days in Southern Ethiopia from November 4 to 6, 2026, showcase digital tools and science-based practices.

The International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics (ICRISAT) and partners are holding farmer field days in Southern Ethiopia from November 4 to 6, 2026. These events promote cost-effective ways to manage soil health and apply fertilizers, aiming to boost agricultural productivity.

Development organizations including Precision Development, People In Need, and Farm Africa are participating to inform farming communities about practical solutions. Farmers are experiencing science-based soil fertility management and learning about fertilizer advisory recommendations, including the AFER_LERSHA Telegram Bot.

The AFER_LERSHA (Soil for Farm) Telegram Bot offers farmers digital advice on fertilizer use tailored to their needs. This innovation helps farmers make informed decisions, optimize resource use, and achieve better yields at reduced costs.

Why it mattersFarmers in Southern Ethiopia are learning new methods to improve crop yields and lower production costs.

Ethiopia clashes push 50,000 into Kenya

Fighting between Somali and Oromo communities over Moyale town has displaced tens of thousands seeking safety.

Deadly clashes between Ethiopian Somalis and Oromos over the town of Moyale have driven an estimated 50,000 people into neighbouring Kenya since October 18. The fighting, which has been ongoing daily, has made return home unsafe for the displaced.

The United Nations refugee agency (UNHCR) reports that at least 11,000 displaced individuals, primarily women and children, are now sheltered in temporary sites like schools. Others are being hosted by local communities in Kenya's border county, where two-thirds of residents live below the poverty line, straining local resources. Beshir Dadie Tufa, a father of three, told AFP the fighting continues daily and people are dying.

Conflict monitor ACLED has documented several deaths in the clashes, though an exact toll is difficult to confirm. Moyale itself is a town that straddles the border between Ethiopia and Kenya.

Politics · 2026-11-06

AU steps up diplomacy to ease Ethiopia-Eritrea tensions

Ambassador Bankole Adeoye calls for implementation of peace agreements and warns against escalation.

The African Union (AU) has intensified diplomatic efforts to reduce tensions between Ethiopia and Eritrea. AU Commissioner Bankole Adeoye stated the continental body is engaging leaders in both nations to prevent a full-blown crisis in the Horn of Africa. The AU is working to re-energize the Algiers and Pretoria Agreements, which address relations between Ethiopia and Eritrea and the conflict in Tigray, respectively.

Former Kenyan President Uhuru Kenyatta, the AU Chairperson’s High Representative for the Horn of Africa, is conducting consultations and shuttle diplomacy. Adeoye emphasized the AU's commitment to peace and collective security, condemning external interference that prolongs African conflicts. The AU Commission will continue engaging all parties to prevent the situation from developing into a full-scale crisis.

The AU is also strengthening its continent-wide approach to peacebuilding, focusing on conflict prevention. Leaders adopted new mechanisms in Luanda in August 2026 to identify emerging threats and address underlying causes, including climate shocks and geopolitical rivalries. The AU aims to advance Agenda 2063, envisioning a peaceful and prosperous continent.

Why it mattersEthiopia's relations with Eritrea and the internal Tigray conflict are being addressed through AU diplomacy.
