Sudanese Army Shoots Down Drone Near Ethiopian Border

The Sudanese army reported shooting down an “enemy strategic drone” that crossed into the country from the direction of Ethiopia on Thursday morning.

The Sudanese army’s General Command announced it shot down an unmanned aerial vehicle north of Sarkam in Sudan’s Blue Nile state. This incident follows a previous report earlier this month where the army claimed to have downed three FH-95 drones in North Kordofan state.

The army described the drone as a “strategic” unmanned aircraft but did not provide further details regarding its origin or purpose. Blue Nile State, which borders Ethiopia, is considered strategically important amidst the ongoing conflict between Sudan’s army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF).

Sudan has been engaged in a war between the army and the RSF since April 2023, a conflict that has resulted in tens of thousands of deaths and displaced approximately 13 million people, according to U.N. and international estimates. Sudanese authorities and human rights organizations have accused the RSF of targeting civilian infrastructure during this conflict.

Why it mattersThis event affects Sudanese citizens living near the border by increasing tensions and potential for conflict in an already war-torn region.

Minibus-Excavator Crash Kills Six in Addis Ababa

A traffic accident involving a minibus taxi and an excavator in Addis Ababa's Bole Subcity claimed the lives of at least six people on Monday, according to police.

Six people died in a traffic accident in Addis Ababa on Monday when an excavator's bucket fell onto a moving minibus taxi, the Addis Ababa Police reported. The incident, which occurred in the morning of August 24, also left the minibus driver and several other passengers with serious or minor injuries, requiring medical treatment at nearby health facilities.

The police have launched an investigation to determine the exact cause of the accident. This incident is part of a series of deadly traffic accidents in Ethiopia, which authorities often link to factors such as reckless driving, weak law enforcement, and poor vehicle conditions.

Experts are calling for stricter safety enforcement, thorough vehicle inspections, and more rigorous licensing systems to prevent future tragedies. These calls come as vehicle numbers rise in the East African nation, alongside its growing economy and expanding middle class.

Why it mattersSix people died and others were injured, directly affecting their families and communities in Addis Ababa.

Enat Bank Registers 6.8 Million Shares with Regulators

Enat Bank Share Company secured official approval today for the registration of nearly 6.8 million existing shares with the Ethiopian Capital Market Authority (ECMA).

The Ethiopian Capital Market Authority (ECMA) announced its approval of Enat Bank's securities registration statement on August 27, 2026. This registration covers 6,785,310 existing shares held by shareholders, formalizing them under Public Offer and Trading of Securities Directive No. 1030/2024.

The directive mandates that all securities offered, sold, or previously issued to the public must be registered with the Authority, unless specifically exempted. This requirement aims to ensure transparency and regulatory oversight as Ethiopia's capital market continues to develop.

ECMA clarified that the public notice regarding Enat Bank's shares is solely for notification and should not be interpreted as an endorsement to sell or a solicitation to buy any securities. This move aligns with the broader regulatory framework for the nation's capital market.

Why it mattersShareholders of Enat Bank are directly affected as their existing shares are now officially registered with the capital market regulator, ensuring transparency and regulatory oversight.

WHO, AU Leaders Call for African Health Sovereignty

Leaders from the World Health Organization and African Union convened in Addis Ababa to advocate for Africa-led health sovereignty, aiming to reduce reliance on external.

The 76th Session of the WHO Regional Committee for Africa, held in Addis Ababa, brought together African health ministers and leaders to discuss the continent's health future. Mohamed Yakub Janabi, WHO regional director for Africa, stated that the session will review Africa’s determination to shape its own health future and make decisions to improve health services across the continent.

The initiative seeks to reduce the nearly seven billion U.S. dollars Africa spends annually on referring patients abroad. Janabi emphasized strengthening Africa’s centers of excellence and connecting African patients to African expertise to keep investments working for the continent. AU Commission Chairperson Mahmoud Ali Youssouf highlighted Africa's efforts to achieve health sovereignty through vaccine production and increased budget allocations.

Ethiopian President Taye Atske Selassie, the guest of honor, noted that climate change and conflict are exerting pressure on African communities, disrupting health services and displacing populations. He suggested these challenges present an opportunity for Africa to strengthen its collective voice, deepen regional cooperation, and assume full ownership of its health architecture. The session is set to endorse regional health policies and strategies to improve health outcomes.

Why it mattersThis event affects African citizens by aiming to improve local health services and reduce the need for costly medical travel abroad, making healthcare more accessible and affordable.

Education Authorities Release Grade 12 Exam Results

Ethiopian education authorities have made public the 2025–2026 Grade 12 national university entrance exam results today.

The Educational Assessment and Examinations Service (EAES) announced the release of the Grade 12 national university entrance exam results for the 2025–2026 academic year. Students can now access their scores through web, Telegram, and short text messages. This follows the Ministry of Education's statement last Saturday regarding the overall performance.

Only 12.8% of the 563,510 examinees achieved the passing mark required for higher education qualification, according to the Ministry of Education. This figure represents the proportion of students who met the criteria to advance to university-level studies.

Students who wish to dispute their results have until noon on September 1st to lodge a complaint via a dedicated online link. The EAES confirmed that responses to these complaints will be provided through the same platform, ensuring a clear channel for appeals.

Why it mattersThe release of these exam results directly affects over half a million students who took the exam, determining their eligibility for higher education and future career paths.
