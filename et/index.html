48 killed in Oromia armed attack, rights commission confirms

The Ethiopian Human Rights Commission reported 20 injured, 247 households displaced, and 21 houses destroyed in Southeast Oromia.

The Ethiopian Human Rights Commission (EHRC) confirmed 48 people died and 20 were injured in an armed attack on October 24, 2026, in Southeast Oromia Zone. The Oromo Liberation Front, known as OLF-Shene, reportedly attacked Birbona Chole Kebele between 6:30 a.m. and 8:30 p.m., destroying 21 houses and displacing 247 households.

Victims included 23 security personnel, five militia members, and 20 civilians, according to the EHRC report which gathered information from witnesses and officials. EHRC Chief Commissioner Berhanu Adelo called the incident extremely serious and urged the government to strengthen civilian protection and improve early warning systems.

The commission has repeatedly called for government action to prevent similar attacks in the former Arsi Zone and stressed the state's constitutional duty to protect citizens' rights. The EHRC is continuing investigations into other human rights violations across the Southeast Oromia Zone and other regions.

Why it mattersCivilians in Southeast Oromia are directly affected by armed attacks, leading to deaths, injuries, and displacement.

AU renews Ethiopia-Eritrea peace talks

Ambassador Bankole Adeoye calls for implementation of Algiers and Pretoria agreements to prevent crisis.

The African Union has restarted diplomatic efforts to ease tensions between Ethiopia and Eritrea. Ambassador Bankole Adeoye, AU Commissioner for Political Affairs, Peace and Security, stated the AU is engaging leaders in both nations to prevent a full-blown crisis in the Horn of Africa.

Adeoye confirmed the AU is working to re-energize the Algiers Agreement between Ethiopia and Eritrea, and the Pretoria Agreement that ended the Tigray conflict. He said former Kenyan President Uhuru Kenyatta is conducting consultations as the AU Chairperson’s High Representative for the Horn of Africa.

The AU Commission will continue engaging all parties to prevent the situation from escalating, Adeoye said, emphasizing collective security and condemning external interference. He noted that African leaders adopted new mechanisms in Luanda in August 2026 to improve conflict prevention and identify emerging threats.

Why it mattersEthiopia is directly involved in renewed AU diplomatic efforts aimed at easing tensions with Eritrea and preventing a regional crisis.

Somaliland envoy visits Ethiopian ambassador in Hargeisa

Sharmarke Gelle met Teshome Shunde ahead of his new assignment in Addis Ababa.

Somaliland's envoy-designate to Ethiopia, Sharmarke Gelle, visited Ethiopian Ambassador Teshome Shunde in Hargeisa on Wednesday. Gelle was preparing to start his new role at the Ethiopian mission in Addis Ababa.

Gelle stated on the social media platform X that he thanked Ambassador Shunde for the reception he received. The meeting took place at the Ethiopian mission located in Somaliland.

The envoy's visit signals ongoing diplomatic engagement between Somaliland and Ethiopia as Gelle prepares for his upcoming duties in the Ethiopian capital.

Ethiopia and DHL boost coffee export capacity

A new deal will train exporters in international trade, logistics, and customs procedures.

The Ethiopian Coffee and Tea Authority (ECTA) and DHL Express Ethiopia signed a deal on November 3 to improve coffee export capacity. The partnership, part of DHL’s GoTrade programme, aims to enhance knowledge of international trade, logistics, and customs for Ethiopian coffee businesses.

ECTA Director Office head Sefissa Ababu stated the agreement will equip enterprises with skills for international markets, improving competitiveness and value addition. DHL Express Ethiopia Commercial Manager Yonas Sbhat noted that while demand for Ethiopian coffee is strong, customs and logistics can be barriers, especially for growing businesses.

The cooperation has already supported a nine-month training program for about 30 participants from private coffee firms and ECTA. The United Nations Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO) facilitated the MoU through its ACT Coffee Programme.

Why it mattersEthiopian coffee exporters will gain practical skills to access global markets and increase their competitiveness.

Tigray rebels claim heavy army losses

The Tigray People's Liberation Front said it killed 500 soldiers and wounded 800 more in clashes near Aksum.

Ethiopia's Tigray rebels claimed on Monday to have killed 500 soldiers and wounded 800 others in clashes with government forces and allied militias. The Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF) said the fighting occurred on Saturday near Aksum, a historic town in the northern region.

According to TPLF-affiliated Dimtsi Weyane television, the rebels also captured territory and seized weapons, ammunition, food, and medical supplies. The group accused federal forces of using artillery and drones, and reported disruptions to telephone and internet services in the area. Ethiopia's government has not commented on the casualty figures, which could not be independently verified.

These clashes follow weeks of escalating fighting in northern Ethiopia. Government troops had captured several towns, including Mekelle, the Tigray regional capital, in early October, but TPLF fighters have continued counterattacks.

Why it mattersFighting in the northern region of Ethiopia has resulted in claimed heavy casualties for the army and disruption of services.
