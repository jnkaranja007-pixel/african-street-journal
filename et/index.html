FAO urges Ethiopia to shift from aid to early action

The UN agency wants Ethiopia to invest in forecasts and warnings to protect livelihoods before climate disasters strike.

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) is urging Ethiopia to move away from emergency aid towards anticipatory action for recurring climate disasters. FAO Assistant Director-General Abebe Haile-Gabriel stated that Ethiopia needs to invest in early warning systems and scientific forecasting to protect communities before crises escalate. He noted that repeated responses after livestock and crops are lost offer no sustainable solution to the country's climate vulnerability.

Instead of reacting to disasters, the FAO advocates for a system where authorities anticipate climate threats and act on scientific forecasts. This approach involves identifying high-risk communities and deploying targeted support, such as pre-positioning livestock feed, strengthening veterinary services, and distributing drought-resistant seeds. The goal is to protect productive assets before households suffer losses.

This call comes as Ethiopia faces climate concerns linked to El Niño and the Indian Ocean Dipole, which affect rainfall patterns. While drought is a priority, excessive rainfall and floods are also a concern for the October-December season. Ethiopia has already activated anticipatory action in four districts across the Somali and Oromia regions, with approximately 9.3 million people residing in districts identified for early intervention.

Why it mattersEthiopian farmers and pastoralists face repeated losses of livestock and crops due to climate shocks, and the FAO proposes a shift to anticipatory action to protect their livelihoods.

Jinhua trade with Africa reaches 161.41 billion yuan

Chinese city's exports to Africa, led by new energy products, grew 29.6% in nine months.

Jinhua, a city in China's Zhejiang Province, saw its trade with Africa reach 161.41 billion yuan from January to September, a 35.3% increase from the previous year. Exports to the continent totaled 144.51 billion yuan, marking a 29.6% rise and representing 10.3% of China's total exports to Africa. The city is seeing strong sales of lithium batteries, photovoltaic products, and electric passenger vehicles in African markets.

Lin Wancheng, director of international sales and marketing at Zhejiang Jujiang New Energy Technology Co., Ltd., stated that their energy storage products are well-received in Africa due to limited grid coverage and high diesel costs. The Jinyi Comprehensive Bonded Zone imported 2.81 billion yuan worth of goods from Africa between January and September, a 253.4% year-over-year increase, benefiting from zero-tariff treatment extended to 53 African nations.

Jiang Zhenlei, president of the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade Jinhua Sub-Council, noted that cooperation is expanding beyond goods trade into green industries, the digital economy, agricultural technology, and industrial park development. Mao Keqiang, president of the Machinery and Equipment Chamber of Commerce at China Commodity City, highlighted Africa's demand for agricultural machinery and Jinhua's role in developing agricultural technology parks and smart agriculture joint laboratories.

Why it mattersEthiopian businesses and consumers may benefit from increased availability and potentially lower costs of Chinese manufactured goods, particularly in green energy and agricultural technology.

Business · 2026-11-03

Renewed conflict threatens ethiopia's economic growth

The IMF forecasts 9.2% growth for 2025-26, but fighting could deter investment and raise living costs.

Renewed fighting in northern Ethiopia threatens the country's strong economic growth, which the IMF forecasts at 9.2% for the 2025-26 fiscal year. The conflict could deter investment, strain public finances, and increase the cost of living.

Financial analyst Abdulmenan Mohammed warned that a major conflict would make current economic growth unsustainable. The 2020-2022 war caused extensive damage, with a World Bank assessment putting damage between November 2020 and December 2021 at over $22.6 billion.

A reconstruction plan from 2023 to 2028 faces funding uncertainty, and the economy is still recovering from the previous war's impact, including lost output and foreign currency shortages. Ethiopia also lost duty-free access to the US market under the AGOA in 2022.

Why it mattersRenewed conflict in Ethiopia could deter investment, strain public finances, and push up the cost of living, impacting the country's economic recovery.

Politics · 2026-11-03

TPLF loses support after Tigray offensive falters

The Tigray People's Liberation Front has retreated to hills after its latest offensive against federal forces was repelled.

The Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF) is facing backlash from former supporters in northern Ethiopia following its recent offensive. The TPLF's attempt to overthrow federal leaders in late September was quickly repelled by pro-government forces using drones. The party has since retreated to the hills of the Tigray region, occasionally attacking federal troops.

Tsegay Girmay, a 32-year-old former TPLF supporter from Adigrat, expressed satisfaction that the movement is "on the verge of disappearing." Girmay joined the TPLF in 2020, angered by atrocities during the Tigray war. Federal authorities took formal control of Tigray in October, establishing a six-month interim administration and promising elections.

Girmay stated that the TPLF has "not the slightest chance of returning to power," citing the current situation on the ground. Eritrea, which had previously fought alongside Ethiopia against the TPLF, has since fallen out with its neighbour. The TPLF is now confined to occasional attacks from the Tigrayan hills.

Why it mattersTigrayan residents like Tsegay Girmay are losing faith in the TPLF's ability to regain power after their recent military setbacks.
