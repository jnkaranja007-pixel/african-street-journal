Excavator bucket kills six in Addis Ababa minibus crash

A traffic accident in Addis Ababa on Monday, August 24, claimed the lives of at least six people when an excavator's bucket fell onto a moving minibus taxi.

At least six people died in Ethiopia's capital, Addis Ababa, on Monday, August 24, after an excavator bucket struck a minibus taxi in the Bole Subcity. The Addis Ababa Police reported that the incident occurred in the morning, resulting in the immediate deaths of six passengers.

The driver and several other passengers aboard the minibus sustained injuries, ranging from serious to minor, and were transported to nearby health facilities for treatment. Police have initiated an investigation to determine the precise cause of the accident, according to a statement issued Monday.

This incident adds to a series of fatal traffic accidents in the East African nation, which authorities often link to factors such as reckless driving, inadequate law enforcement, and poor vehicle conditions. Experts are calling for stricter safety enforcement, thorough vehicle inspections, and more rigorous licensing systems to prevent future tragedies as vehicle numbers increase with economic growth and an expanding middle class.

Why it mattersResidents of Addis Ababa are directly affected by the safety of public transportation and the enforcement of traffic regulations, impacting their daily commutes and personal safety.

Ethiopia bids to host 2028 Africa Cup of Nations

Ethiopia has submitted an official bid to host the 2028 Africa Cup of Nations (Afcon), aiming to bring the tournament back for the first time in over 50 years.

Ethiopia's football federation (EFF) has submitted a government-backed proposal to host the 2028 Africa Cup of Nations, affirming the nation's readiness for Africa's largest football event. The country last hosted Afcon in 1976 and has only qualified three times since then, most recently for the delayed 2021 edition.

The bid comes despite Ethiopia's current football infrastructure lagging behind other African nations. Last season, only one stadium was approved by the Confederation of African Football (Caf) for use. However, a new international stadium and sports complex in Addis Ababa is being inaugurated, which could serve as a venue.

A key challenge for Ethiopia will be providing enough compliant venues for a 24-team Afcon across multiple locations, as many existing regional facilities do not meet Fifa or Caf standards for infrastructure and match-day requirements. Ethiopia played its home qualifiers for the 2026 Fifa World Cup at neutral venues in Morocco and Rwanda, though it did host matches in Dire Dawa earlier this year, according to BBC Africa and Africanews reports.

Why it mattersEthiopian citizens, particularly football fans, are affected by the potential for their country to host a major international tournament and the associated infrastructure development.

Health · 08/26/2026

WHO, AU Leaders Urge African Health Sovereignty

Leaders from the World Health Organization and African Union called for Africa to achieve health self-reliance and reduce dependence on external financing.

The 76th Session of the WHO Regional Committee for Africa, held in Addis Ababa, brought together African health ministers and leaders of regional and international health institutions. Mohamed Yakub Janabi, WHO regional director for Africa, stated that the session will review Africa’s determination to shape its own health future and make decisions and recommendations to improve health services across the continent.

This initiative aims to reduce the nearly 7 billion U.S. dollars the continent spends annually on referring patients abroad. Janabi emphasized strengthening Africa’s centers of excellence, connecting African patients to African expertise, and keeping investments working for Africa. He noted that African countries have improved surveillance systems, expanded access to essential health services, and responded to outbreaks with growing speed.

AU Commission Chairperson Mahmoud Ali Youssouf highlighted Africa's efforts to create conditions for health sovereignty through vaccine production and increased budgets to strengthen collective capacity against health threats. Ethiopian President Taye Atske Selassie, the guest of honor, added that climate change and conflict exert pressure on communities, offering an opportunity for Africa to deepen regional cooperation and assume full ownership of its health architecture.

Why it mattersAfrican citizens will benefit from improved health services, reduced need for overseas medical travel, and stronger local healthcare infrastructure.

Ethiopian Consumers Face 15.3% Annual Price Increase in July

Ethiopia's headline inflation reached 15.3% in July, driven by rising fuel and food costs, marking the highest rate since January 2025.

Ethiopia's Consumer Price Index (CPI) climbed 2.6% in July, pushing the annual inflation rate to 15.3%, according to data from the Ethiopian Statistical Service (ESS). This increase, which is the highest since January 2025, follows a 13.9% gain in June and aligns with the National Bank of Ethiopia's projection that inflation will remain in double-digits.

Non-food inflation accelerated by 3.0% for the month and 14.8% annually, matching the highest rate recorded in September 2025. Education services saw a 25.3% annual surge, while transport costs rose 15.6% year-on-year, indicating that higher fuel prices are affecting other sectors. Housing, water, electricity, gas, and other fuels, the largest component of non-food CPI, increased by 3.3% monthly and 13.3% annually.

Food inflation also contributed significantly, reaching an annual rate of 15.7%, the highest since January 2025, with a monthly increase of 2.3%. Sugar, jam, and honey prices surged 39.4% annually, while non-alcoholic beverages and coffee rose 33%. Meat prices saw the largest monthly gain at 4.4%, and bread and cereals, the largest food CPI component, increased 1.4% monthly and 8.0% annually.

Why it mattersConsumers in Ethiopia are directly affected by the rising cost of essential goods and services, including food, transport, and education, reducing their purchasing power.

Education · 08/26/2026

Ethiopian Authorities Release Grade 12 Exam Results

Ethiopian education authorities today published the 2025–2026 Grade 12 national university entrance exam results, with 12.8% of examinees passing.

The Educational Assessment and Examinations Service (EAES) today made the 2025–2026 Grade 12 national university entrance exam results available for students. This follows the Ministry of Education's announcement last Saturday that 12.8% of the 563,510 examinees achieved the passing mark for higher education qualification.

Students can access their results through various platforms, including the EAES website, Telegram, and short text messages. This digital distribution aims to provide broad access for all examinees across the country.

For students wishing to dispute their results, a dedicated online link is provided for lodging complaints. The deadline for submitting these complaints is noon on September 1st, with responses to be issued through the same online portal, according to the exam agency.

Why it mattersOver half a million students and their families are directly affected by these results, which determine their eligibility for higher education and future career paths.
