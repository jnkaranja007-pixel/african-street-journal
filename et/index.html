Sudanese Army Downs Drone Near Ethiopian Border

The Sudanese army announced Thursday it shot down an "enemy strategic drone" that had crossed into Blue Nile state from the direction of Ethiopia.

The Sudanese army announced Thursday it shot down an "enemy strategic drone" that crossed into the country from the direction of Ethiopia. The unmanned aerial vehicle was downed in the morning north of Sarkam in Sudan’s Blue Nile state, according to a statement from the army’s General Command. This incident follows an earlier report this month of the Sudanese army shooting down three FH-95 drones in North Kordofan state.

The army described the downed aircraft as a "strategic" unmanned vehicle but did not offer further specifics. Blue Nile State, which shares a border with Ethiopia, holds strategic significance amid the ongoing conflict between Sudan’s army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF).

Sudan has been embroiled in a war between its army and the RSF since April 2023, resulting in tens of thousands of deaths and displacing approximately 13 million people, according to United Nations and international estimates. Sudanese authorities and human rights groups have accused the RSF of targeting civilian infrastructure.

Why it mattersThe drone incident near the Ethiopian border affects regional security and could impact cross-border trade and movement for people living in the affected areas.

Enat Bank Shares Registered with Ethiopian Capital Market Authority

The Ethiopian Capital Market Authority has officially approved the registration of nearly 6.8 million existing shares for Enat Bank Share Company, enhancing transparency.

Enat Bank Share Company has received official approval from the Ethiopian Capital Market Authority (ECMA) for the registration of close to 6.8 million existing shares. The capital market regulator issued a public notice on August 27, 2026, confirming the approval of Enat’s securities registration statement.

The registration, formalized under the Public Offer and Trading of Securities Directive No. 1030/2024, encompasses a total of 6,785,310 shares currently held by shareholders. ECMA stated that the notice serves solely to inform the public and does not constitute an endorsement or solicitation for buying or selling securities.

This action aligns with the directive's requirement for all publicly offered, sold, or previously issued securities to be registered with the Authority, unless specifically exempted. The move aims to ensure transparency and regulatory oversight as Ethiopia's capital market continues its development.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the registration of existing shares with the capital market authority increases transparency and regulatory oversight for Enat Bank's securities.

WHO, AU Leaders Seek Africa Health Self-Reliance

African leaders and WHO officials met in Addis Ababa to discuss ending external financing dependence and strengthening local health systems, including vaccine production.

African leaders and World Health Organization officials have called for health sovereignty to reduce the continent's reliance on foreign funding. The 76th Session of the WHO Regional Committee for Africa, held in Addis Ababa, focused on empowering Africa to shape its own health future and improve services. WHO regional director Mohamed Yakub Janabi stated the goal is to decrease the approximately 7 billion U.S. dollars spent annually on referring patients abroad, by strengthening local centers of excellence and expertise.

The African Union Commission Chairperson Mahmoud Ali Youssouf emphasized the need for self-reliance through local vaccine production, increased budget allocation for health threats, and research and innovation. Ethiopian President Taye Atske Selassie highlighted that climate change and conflict create opportunities for Africa to enhance its collective voice, regional cooperation, and ownership of its health architecture. These challenges are pushing vulnerable populations across borders, triggering humanitarian crises.

The ongoing session aims to endorse regional health policies, strategies, and plans to improve health outcomes across Africa. African countries have reportedly strengthened surveillance systems, expanded access to essential health services, advanced universal health coverage, and improved regulatory capacity in recent years. This push for self-sufficiency comes as the continent faces significant health pressures from environmental and security issues.

Why it mattersThis initiative affects farmers by potentially impacting the availability and cost of agricultural inputs if health crises disrupt supply chains, and indirectly through the economic stability of rural communities.

Ethiopian Education Authority Releases Grade 12 Exam Results

The Educational Assessment and Examinations Service has made the 2025-2026 national university entrance exam results available to students through multiple online platforms.

Education authorities in Ethiopia have released the Grade 12 national university entrance exam results for the 2025-2026 academic year. The Educational Assessment and Examinations Service (EAES) announced that students can now access their scores via the official EAES website, a dedicated Telegram channel, or through short text messages.

This release follows an earlier assessment by the Ministry of Education last Saturday, which indicated that only 12.8% of the total 563,510 examinees achieved the passing mark required for higher education. The EAES has also provided a specific link for students who wish to lodge complaints regarding their results.

Students have until noon on September 1st to submit any grievances through the provided complaint link. The EAES will then respond to these complaints via the same online portal. The results are crucial for students seeking to enroll in higher education institutions across the country.

Why it mattersStudents who took the Grade 12 national exam are directly affected as they can now check their results and submit complaints if necessary.
