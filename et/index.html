Ethiopia backs UAE diplomat for UN chief role

Addis Ababa welcomes Lana Nusseibeh's candidacy, citing her experience and commitment to global cooperation.

Ethiopia has endorsed Emirati diplomat Lana Nusseibeh for the position of United Nations secretary-general. The Ethiopian foreign ministry announced its support, highlighting Nusseibeh's diplomatic experience and dedication to multilateral cooperation. Ethiopia is a founding member of the UN.

The ministry stated its position in a message posted on X. Ethiopia views Nusseibeh's candidacy as important for the United Nations. The announcement came from Addis Ababa, the country's capital.

Nusseibeh's bid aims to make her the first woman to hold the UN's top job. Ethiopia's support adds to her campaign as the international community considers potential candidates for the role.

Why it mattersEthiopia's government has publicly supported a candidate for a top international position.

Ethiopians flee border gunfire into Kenya

Over 36,000 people, mostly women and children, have crossed into Kenya seeking refuge from fighting.

Gunfire was heard from the Ethiopian side of the border in Kenya’s Moyale town on Friday morning as more people fled violence. An Associated Press journalist recorded the sounds on October 30.

The United Nations reported that 36,000 people have crossed into Kenya due to ongoing fighting near Moyale, with most fleeing civilians being women and children. Benedict Munywoki, deputy county commissioner for Moyale Sub-County, stated that more people are arriving daily.

The fighting stems from a territorial dispute between the Oromia and Somali regional administrations, reportedly aggravated by the killing of a 13-year-old girl. Local leaders in Kenya’s Marsabit County have requested support, concerned about overwhelming existing food and water shortages.

Why it mattersLocal leaders in Kenya's Marsabit County are concerned that the influx of refugees could overwhelm communities already struggling with food and water shortages.

Ethiopia acquires Russian fighter jets amid Tigray war

Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's military purchases could transform air power as fighting continues in the Tigray region.

Ethiopia has acquired Russian fighter jets and air defence systems, according to reports on October 29, 2026. The new warplanes could significantly alter the country's air capabilities.

The acquisition comes as fighting continues in the northern Tigray region, which began last month. Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's military purchases raise questions about how the debt-strapped nation is funding these acquisitions.

Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed also met with Russian President Vladimir Putin on October 29, 2026, to discuss cooperation in nuclear energy, health, and trade during the third Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow.

Why it mattersEthiopia's military spending on fighter jets and air defence systems is increasing while conflict persists in the Tigray region.
