Allied Gold pours first gold at Ethiopia's Kurmuk mine

The Canadian miner now expects the western Ethiopian site to start commercial operations by the end of 2026.

Canadian miner Allied Gold Corporation announced on November 9, 2026, that it had poured the first gold at its Kurmuk Gold Mine in western Ethiopia. The milestone moves the site from a development project into an operating mine.

The company says Kurmuk is among the first large-scale mechanised mines in the country, and a key test of Ethiopia's plan to build a modern mining industry. The mine sits in the Benishangul-Gumuz region near the Sudanese border. Allied Gold holds 93 percent of the project, and its subsidiary signed a 20-year power purchase agreement with Ethiopian Electric Power in August 2024.

Allied Gold expects to finish final power installations in November 2026 and fully deploy its haulage fleet. Commercial production is targeted by the end of 2026. The mine is targeting 25,000 to 35,000 ounces of gold this year, rising to 240,000 to 270,000 ounces for 2027 and 300,000 ounces by 2028.

Why it mattersAllied Gold's Kurmuk mine is set to reach 240,000 to 270,000 ounces of gold in 2027, with power supplied under a 20-year agreement with Ethiopian Electric Power.

Ethiopia's dollar demand exceeds supply by $16.73 million

Commercial banks bid $156.73 million for foreign currency at the National Bank of Ethiopia's latest auction.

Commercial banks submitted bids totaling USD 156.73 million in Foreign Exchange Auction No. 32, held by the National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE) on November 10, 2026. The central bank had allocated USD 140 million, leaving unmet demand of USD 16.73 million. 31 commercial banks took part, and 26 of them secured foreign currency.

The marginal rate, which the central bank calls the cut-off rate, settled at 159.1876 Birr per US dollar. Successful bids were weighted at an average of 159.7091 Birr per dollar. The NBE said the excess demand continues a pattern seen in earlier auctions.

In Auction No. 30, held on October 13, 2026, bids reached USD 152.09 million against the same USD 140 million allocation, with a marginal rate of 158.1724 Birr per dollar.

Why it mattersEthiopian businesses that need foreign currency to import goods or services are competing for a supply that falls short of bids.

Ethiopian women sew reusable sanitary pads

Hanan Ahmed's company produces 1,000 washable pads daily to help girls stay in school.

In Addis Ababa, Hanan Ahmed's workshop produces reusable sanitary pads from cotton fabric. Her company, Han's With Care, established in 2021, aims to provide an affordable alternative to disposable products, which Ethiopia imports entirely. The high cost of imported pads prevents many Ethiopian women and girls from affording them, leading to missed school days and potential use of unsafe alternatives.

Ahmed, 41, was inspired by her own experiences with heavy menstrual flow that caused her to miss school. She left the fashion industry to start Han's With Care, which now makes washable pads reusable for up to two years. The company produces about 1,000 pads daily, distributing them free to girls and women across Ethiopia through sponsors like banks.

At Nigat Kokeb School, 15-year-old student Betselot Mahareg reported that the reusable pads prevent leaks and embarrassment compared to disposable ones. Ahmed also aims to change attitudes towards menstruation, particularly among younger generations in Ethiopia.

Why it mattersEthiopian girls can now attend school without interruption due to menstruation, and women have a dignified and affordable option for menstrual hygiene.

Zemen Bank seeks cash equipment bids

The Addis Ababa bank is procuring note detectors, safes, generators, and TVs.

Zemen Bank S.C. is seeking bids for various cash-related equipment and branch opening items. The procurement includes multi-currency detecting machines, note counters, fire-resistant safes of different sizes, generators, and LED TVs with receivers. A one-year contract for the supply and installation of electrical and data cables is also included.

Interested bidders can obtain bid documents from Zemen Bank's headquarters in Addis Ababa. The non-refundable fee for a complete set of documents is Birr 230.00, payable in cash. Bid proposals must be submitted by November 24, 2026, at 5:00 PM, accompanied by a bid security ranging from ETB 150,000.00 to ETB 700,000.00 per lot.

Bid opening sessions are scheduled for November 25, 2026, starting at 9:00 AM. Zemen Bank reserves the right to reject any or all bids submitted.

Why it mattersBusinesses that supply cash handling equipment, safes, generators, or electrical and data cabling services can bid for contracts with Zemen Bank.

Ethiopian Jews mark Sigd holy day in Jerusalem

Thousands gathered in Jerusalem on Monday for the Sigd observance, which Kessim religious leaders led as a day of prayer and reflection.

Thousands of Ethiopian Jews gathered in Jerusalem on Monday to observe the holy day of Sigd. Kessim, the community's religious leaders, led the observance, which is meant as a day of introspection and prayer seeking a return to Israel.

Most Ethiopian Jews now live in Israel, and the community comes together in Jerusalem each year to mark the day and celebrate its culture. Religious leader Michael Maharat said the community has integrated well and made progress this generation, and he voiced hope that the Ethiopian Jews who have not yet immigrated will be able to do so.

In 1991, while Ethiopia was in civil war, Israel carried out Operation Solomon, airlifting some 14,500 Ethiopian Jews out in less than two days. The Ethiopian Jewish community in Israel now numbers 180,000.

Why it mattersEthiopian Jews living in Israel, and those still waiting to immigrate, are the people directly affected by the observance and its emphasis on return to Israel.
