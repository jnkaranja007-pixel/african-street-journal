Tigray rebels claim heavy losses for Ethiopian army

The TPLF reported killing 500 soldiers and wounding 800 more during clashes near Aksum on Saturday.

Ethiopia's rebel Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF) claimed on Monday to have inflicted heavy losses on the country’s armed forces. The group stated it killed 500 soldiers and wounded 800 others during clashes with federal troops and allied militias in the northern region. These confrontations occurred on Saturday near the town of Aksum, according to TPLF-affiliated Dimtsi Weyane television.

The TPLF also reported capturing territory and seizing weapons, ammunition, food supplies, and medical equipment. The rebels accused federal forces of using artillery and drones and noted disruptions to telephone and internet services. Ethiopia’s government has not commented on the casualty figures, which could not be independently verified.

The clashes follow weeks of escalating fighting in northern Ethiopia. Government troops had captured several towns, including Tigray's regional capital, Mekelle, in early October, but TPLF fighters have continued mounting counterattacks.

Why it mattersFighting in the northern region has led to rebel claims of significant casualties among Ethiopian soldiers.

Ethiopia central bank holds policy rate at 16 percent

The National Bank of Ethiopia maintained its benchmark rate due to persistent double-digit inflation and moderate looseness in financial conditions.

The National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE) has kept its benchmark policy rate at 16 percent, continuing a tight monetary policy. This decision was made at the Monetary Policy Committee meeting on November 3, 2026, after reviewing economic developments and inflation trends. The central bank also kept its interest rate corridor at plus or minus 300 basis points around the policy rate.

Maintaining tight monetary conditions is essential for reducing inflation to single digits, according to the NBE. Recent conditions indicate moderate looseness, with short-term inflation forecasts remaining in double digits, justifying the unchanged policy. The committee will monitor commercial banks’ loan-to-deposit ratios and may introduce targeted reserve requirements if rapid credit expansion threatens the inflation outlook.

The NBE will use indirect tools like open market operations to absorb excess liquidity and restrain money supply and credit growth. The current 16 percent policy rate was set in July 2026 when it was raised by one percentage point. The MPC is scheduled to meet again at the end of January 2027, but could convene earlier if economic conditions necessitate.

Why it mattersEthiopian businesses and consumers face continued high borrowing costs as the central bank prioritizes fighting inflation.

Ethiopia and DHL boost coffee export capacity

A new deal will train exporters in international trade, logistics, and customs procedures.

The Ethiopian Coffee and Tea Authority (ECTA) and DHL Express Ethiopia signed an agreement on November 3 to improve the export capacity of the country's coffee sector. The partnership, part of DHL’s GoTrade programme, aims to enhance knowledge and skills in international trade, logistics, and customs for Ethiopian coffee businesses.

The deal, facilitated by the United Nations Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO), will see ECTA and DHL conduct training for coffee exporters and professionals. DHL will offer expertise in shipping and cross-border trade, while ECTA will identify participants and sector priorities. ECTA Director Office head Sefissa Ababu stated the partnership equips businesses with practical skills for international markets.

DHL Express Ethiopia Commercial Manager Yonas Sbhat noted that while Ethiopian coffee has strong demand, customs and logistics challenges can hinder market access. A recent nine-month capacity-building programme under this cooperation already trained about 30 participants from private coffee enterprises and ECTA.

Why it mattersEthiopian coffee exporters and businesses will gain practical knowledge and skills to access global markets more effectively.

US contractor admits passing secrets to Ethiopia

Abraham Teklu Lemma shared classified intelligence with Ethiopian officials for over two years.

An Ethiopian-born American citizen, Abraham Teklu Lemma, has pleaded guilty to providing classified intelligence to Ethiopia, including information intended to help target fighters in the country's civil war. The former US contractor admitted to sharing dozens of classified documents and photographs with senior Ethiopian officials between 2021 and August 2023.

US Attorney Jeanine Ferris Pirro stated that Lemma betrayed his oath, colleagues, and country, jeopardizing national security. The 54-year-old worked for agencies including the State Department and the National Reconnaissance Office, extracting electronic intelligence files and copying sensitive information into handwritten notes. He faces up to 22 years in prison.

Lemma's plea agreement includes a sentencing scheduled for next April. The civil war, which concluded prior to his arrest, reportedly killed hundreds of thousands and displaced millions.

Why it mattersAn Ethiopian-born American citizen pleaded guilty to passing classified intelligence to Ethiopia, jeopardizing national security.

Business · 2026-11-04

Jinhua trade with Africa jumps 35.3%

China's Zhejiang Province city's trade with Africa reached 161.41 billion yuan from January to September.

Jinhua, a city in China's Zhejiang Province, is increasing its trade and economic ties with Africa. From January to September, Jinhua's trade with the continent reached 161.41 billion yuan, a 35.3% increase compared to the previous year. The city's exports to Africa grew by 29.6% to 144.51 billion yuan, representing 10.3% of China's total exports to Africa.

The city is seeing strong demand for its "new trio" export categories: lithium batteries, photovoltaic products, and electric passenger vehicles. Zhejiang Jujiang New Energy Technology Co., Ltd. reported that its energy storage products are well-received in Africa due to limited grid coverage and high power generation costs. China extended zero-tariff treatment to 53 African countries on May 1, 2026, leading to a significant drop in import costs for raw metals.

Imports from Africa into Jinhua's Jinyi Comprehensive Bonded Zone increased by 253.4% year over year to 2.81 billion yuan from January to September. Cooperation is expanding beyond goods trade into green industries, the digital economy, agricultural technology, and industrial park development. Jinhua is also developing agricultural technology through the China-Africa (Jinhua) Agricultural Science and Technology Innovation Park and a joint laboratory on smart agriculture.

Why it mattersEthiopian businesses and consumers can benefit from increased trade and access to new technologies and products from Jinhua.
