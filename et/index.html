Ethiopia submits official bid to host Afcon 2028

Ethiopia's football federation, with government backing, has submitted an official bid to host the 2028 Africa Cup of Nations, aiming to bring the tournament back after 52 years.

Ethiopia has officially submitted a bid to host the 2028 Africa Cup of Nations (Afcon), according to the country's football federation (EFF). The government-backed proposal affirms the nation's readiness to host Africa's largest football event, which would mark the first time Ethiopia has staged the tournament since 1976.

Despite having hosted Afcon three times previously in 1962, 1968, and 1976, Ethiopia's current football infrastructure lags behind other African nations. Last season, only one stadium was approved by the Confederation of African Football (Caf) for use, though a new international stadium and sports complex is being inaugurated in Addis Ababa.

This new venue in the capital could serve as one location for the tournament. However, questions remain regarding whether Ethiopia possesses enough compliant facilities to host a 24-team Afcon across multiple sites, as many existing regional facilities do not meet Fifa or Caf standards for infrastructure and match-day requirements.

Why it mattersThe bid to host Afcon 2028 directly affects Ethiopian football fans and the national sports infrastructure, potentially bringing a major international event to the country.

Ethiopia sees 15.3% annual inflation in July, driven by food and fuel costs

Ethiopia's Consumer Price Index rose 2.6% in July, pushing the annual inflation rate to 15.3%, the highest since January 2025, according to the Ethiopian Statistical Service.

Headline inflation in Ethiopia climbed above 15% in July, reaching 15.3% annually, as reported by the Ethiopian Statistical Service (ESS). This increase follows a 2.6% rise in the Consumer Price Index (CPI) for the month, marking the highest annual rate since January 2025 and exceeding June's 13.9% gain. The National Bank of Ethiopia had previously projected that inflation would likely remain in double-digits before moderating by December 2026.

Rising fuel prices contributed significantly to the overall inflation, with non-food items accelerating 3.0% for the month and 14.8% annually. Transport costs increased 15.6% year-on-year, and education services surged 25.3%. Housing, water, electricity, gas, and other fuels, the largest component of non-food CPI, rose 13.3% from a year earlier.

Food inflation also played a major role, reaching 15.7% annually, the highest since January 2025, with a monthly rate of 2.3%. Sugar, jam, and honey prices saw the biggest annual surge at 39.4%, followed by non-alcoholic beverages and coffee at 33%. Meat prices increased 21.6% year-on-year, while bread and cereals, the largest component of food CPI, rose 8.0% annually.

Why it mattersConsumers in Ethiopia are directly affected by the rising cost of essential goods and services, making daily life more expensive.

Ethiopia concludes 40-day National Dialogue, producing 400 recommendations

Ethiopia's National Dialogue Conference concluded Saturday, with nearly 4,000 participants generating approximately 400 recommendations to address national challenges.

Ethiopia concluded its National Dialogue Conference on Saturday, following 40 days of deliberations by nearly 4,000 Ethiopians from various backgrounds. The discussions focused on the country’s most significant national questions, aiming to resolve long-standing political, social, and institutional issues.

The conference, as reported by Horn Diplomat on August 24, produced around 400 recommendations. These proposals are intended to lay the groundwork for addressing the identified challenges and shaping the country's future direction.

Participants engaged in extensive discussions to formulate solutions for complex national issues. The outcome reflects a broad effort to tackle deep-rooted problems affecting the nation, according to Mohamed Duale of Horn Diplomat.

Why it mattersEthiopian citizens are affected by the potential changes to political, social, and institutional structures that these recommendations may bring.

Sudanese Opposition Rejects Burhan's National Dialogue Proposal

A coalition of Sudanese political forces and civil society groups meeting in Addis Ababa has rejected General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan’s national dialogue initiative.

A major gathering of Sudanese political forces and civil society groups in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, concluded with a rejection of General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan’s proposed national dialogue. Participants stated they would not take part in or recognize the conclusions of the initiative, arguing it cannot bring peace while fighting continues and the Rapid Support Forces are excluded.

The groups, including members of the National Congress Party, the Umma movement, the peace coalition Summud, and various civil society organizations, accused Burhan of using the dialogue to legitimize his authority following the 2021 military coup. They warned that the initiative could deepen Sudan’s division.

Former Prime Minister Abdalla Hamdok also emphasized that a credible peace process is not possible without an end to the ongoing conflict. The opposition's stance highlights a significant hurdle for any peace efforts in Sudan, as a broad consensus remains elusive.

Why it mattersThis rejection affects Sudanese citizens by prolonging political instability and conflict, delaying a credible peace process, and potentially deepening national divisions.
