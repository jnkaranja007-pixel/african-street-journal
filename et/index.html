IGAD brings 2023 treaty into force, replacing its 1996 agreement

The eight-member bloc's new treaty covers 18 areas of cooperation, including trade, free movement of people and agriculture.

The Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD), a regional organisation of eight states, has brought its 2023 treaty into force. The new treaty replaces the 1996 agreement that gave the bloc its legal framework for nearly three decades. Ethiopia is one of five member states that had ratified it by September 2026.

The IGAD Council of Ministers confirmed on September 15 that the required threshold of two-thirds ratification had been met. IGAD Executive Secretary Workneh Gebeyehu said the agreement would strengthen the region's collective capacity to respond to crises. The treaty covers 18 areas under Article 24, including trade liberalisation, free movement of people and labour, renewable energy, agriculture and food security.

The treaty also clarifies the responsibilities of IGAD institutions, sets more structured mechanisms for resolving disputes and reinforces members' duty to implement decisions. The revision targets gaps in enforcement and dispute procedures under the old agreement. The announcement coincides with IGAD's 40th anniversary; the body was founded in 1986 to coordinate action on drought and food insecurity.

Why it mattersTraders and workers moving across the bloc's borders will now fall under rules on trade liberalisation and free movement of people and labour.

Ethiopia opens central bank sandbox for financial firms

The framework lets banks, fintechs and insurers test products under National Bank supervision before seeking full approval or wider market entry.

Ethiopia’s National Bank opened a regulatory sandbox on November 17, allowing financial firms to test products, services and technologies in a live, controlled setting before full approval or wider market entry. The framework covers fintechs, commercial banks, insurers and other financial institutions.

Applicants must show that a proposal is innovative, offers consumer benefits and cannot be tested under existing rules. The bank will issue calls for applications at least twice a year, and fees range from 35,000 birr for general applicants to 100,000 birr for commercial banks.

Approved trials can run for up to 12 months, with one extension of another year subject to progress and central bank approval. Participants must follow anti-money laundering, cybersecurity and data-protection laws, and obtain customers’ written consent before enrolling them.

Why it mattersApplicants pay fees of 35,000 to 100,000 birr and must secure written customer consent before trials begin.

Business · 2026-11-18

Dodai closes $16 million Series A with Tokyo Gas-backed extension

The Ethiopian electric-mobility company secured a further $3 million from Tokyo Gas and Singapore-based Kaneto Capital Management.

Ethiopian electric-mobility startup Dodai closed its Series A at $16 million after securing a $3 million extension from Tokyo Gas and Singapore-based family office Kaneto Capital Management. The extension adds to a $13 million round completed earlier this year, comprising $8 million in equity and $5 million in debt.

Tokyo Gas invested through its corporate venture capital subsidiary, Acario, while Kaneto joined as a new institutional backer. Founded in 2021 by Japanese entrepreneur Yuma Sasaki, Dodai designs and assembles electric motorbikes and operates battery-swapping stations for delivery and logistics workers. It has deployed more than 2,000 bikes in Ethiopia, employs more than 150 people, about 98 percent of them Ethiopian, and runs about 10 swapping stations in Addis Ababa.

The company says its active fleet is estimated at about 2,500 bikes. Ethiopia exempts completely knocked-down electric-vehicle kits assembled locally from customs duties, excise taxes and value-added tax; semi-knocked-down kits face a 5 percent tariff. The government has set a target of 500,000 electric vehicles on the road by 2030. Tokyo Gas is expected to advise Dodai on power management and its battery-swapping network.

Why it mattersDodai employs more than 150 people in Ethiopia, about 98 percent of them Ethiopian, and Tokyo Gas is expected to advise on its power and swapping network.
