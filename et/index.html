EPSS Wins Gold Award for Supply Chain Excellence

The Ethiopian Pharmaceutical Supply Service received a top honor for its reforms in medicine procurement and supply chain management.

The Ethiopian Pharmaceutical Supply Service (EPSS) has been awarded the Gold Award at the fifth Africa Supply Chain Excellence Awards in Pretoria, South Africa. Director General Dr. Abdulkedir Gelgelo stated the award recognizes EPSS's improved performance in pharmaceutical and medical supply procurement and its broader supply-chain management reforms. Digital transformation, enterprise resource planning, and data-driven decision-making were cited as key factors in achieving this recognition, moving EPSS closer to its 2030 vision of becoming a leading African supply-chain institution.

This award highlights EPSS's commitment to quality and encourages pooled procurement for medicines. The state-owned enterprise is responsible for supplying essential medicines to over 420 hospitals, 4,100 health centers, and 18,500 health posts nationwide. Historically, EPSS faced challenges including lengthy procurement cycles that could take up to 400 days, fragmented records, and outdated manual tracking, which limited inventory visibility and disrupted medicine supply.

EPSS has undergone significant public-sector transformation, addressing systemic and operational weaknesses that previously impacted health service delivery. Reforms have focused on legislative changes and digitization to overcome administrative bottlenecks and information shortages. These efforts aim to ensure a more stable, accessible, and reliable supply of pharmaceuticals and medical equipment across the country, improving the efficiency of warehousing and last-mile delivery networks.

Why it mattersEthiopian farmers are indirectly affected by improved pharmaceutical supply chains that ensure the availability of veterinary medicines and other health-related inputs for livestock and agricultural operations.

Deloitte Gains Investment Bank License in Ethiopia

The Ethiopian Capital Market Authority has granted Deloitte a license to operate as an investment banking service provider, making it the second foreign firm to receive the permit.

Deloitte has secured an investment banking license from the Ethiopian Capital Market Authority (ECMA), allowing it to offer non-brokerage services such as facilitating mergers and corporate organizations. This marks a significant step for the firm, which previously operated as a securities investment advisor after re-entering the Ethiopian market. The license was granted after a rigorous assessment process, according to the regulator.

The granting of this license makes Deloitte the second foreign entity to be authorized for investment banking services in Ethiopia, bringing the total number of licensed investment banks to nine. United Capital Financial Services Plc, a subsidiary of Nigeria's United Capital Group, was the first foreign firm to receive a similar license in June 2026. ECMA views these developments as a demonstration of confidence in Ethiopia's emerging financial ecosystem.

This upgrade from a Securities Investment Advisory role to an Investment Bank signifies growth and trust in the Ethiopian capital market, according to ECMA. The new license specifically permits Deloitte Ethiopia to engage in investment banking activities but does not allow it to act as a securities broker or dealer. The authority highlighted this transition as a positive indicator of the market's potential.

Why it mattersDeloitte's new investment banking license allows it to facilitate mergers and corporate organizations for clients in Ethiopia.

National Bank Seeks Financial Tool Developer

The National Bank of Ethiopia is inviting bids for the creation of a digital platform to compare financial product prices.

The National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE) has issued an invitation for bids to develop a Financial Services Price Comparator Tool. This initiative aims to enhance transparency, protect consumers, and support informed financial decision-making within Ethiopia's expanding financial sector. The tool will be a regulator-led, non-commercial digital platform designed to allow consumers to easily access, compare, and understand the pricing and service features of various financial products.

The NBE is seeking a qualified consulting firm or consortium to design, develop, test, and implement the comparator tool. This project is supported by the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) through its Rural Financial Intermediation Programme (RUFIP). The initiative aligns with national objectives to improve financial capability and inclusion, particularly for rural and underserved populations, by ensuring access to transparent and affordable financial services.

The bidding process is underway, with the bid closing date set for September 24, 2026, at 10:00 A.M., followed by the bid opening at 10:30 A.M. on the same day. The objective is to create a user-friendly and scalable digital platform that enables consumers to compare standardized financial products and pricing information across different institutions, thereby empowering them to make better financial choices.

Why it mattersThe National Bank of Ethiopia is developing a tool to help consumers compare financial product prices, aiming to improve transparency and financial inclusion.

Ethiopia Delegates Back Executive Presidency Plan

A national dialogue has endorsed constitutional changes that would merge the roles of prime minister and president into a powerful executive presidency.

Ethiopia's national dialogue has endorsed constitutional changes that would merge the roles of prime minister and president into a powerful executive presidency, a move critics warn could allow Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed to further consolidate his control. More than 4,000 delegates participated in the weeks-long dialogue, which concluded on August 22. The delegates overwhelmingly backed a proposal to replace the current system with an executive presidency combining the powers of the prime minister and the largely ceremonial president.

The recommendations could be presented to parliament when it reconvenes in October. Although Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed was absent from the dialogue, many observers expect him to be the leading candidate for the new position if the changes are adopted. The president would be chosen by a parliament dominated by members of his ruling party.

Supporters argue the proposal could create a more unifying national leadership, with the president representing all ethnic groups rather than being elected as a parliamentary representative. Opponents, however, fear the proposed changes could transform the political system to serve one individual, with political analyst Befekadu Hailu stating the reforms could effectively allow Abiy to assume the executive presidency while concentrating power in a single office.

Why it mattersThe proposed executive presidency could concentrate power in the hands of Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed, altering the nation's political structure.
