Ethiopian PM trains ministers in military drills

Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed said armed forces discipline can help build stronger leaders and advance the country's development.

Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed has put his cabinet ministers and other senior government officials through military training at the Hurso military training centre on October 31.

Photos shared by the prime minister show him taking part in exercises alongside ministers, deputy ministers and heads of government institutions, including Speaker of the House of Federation Muferiat Kamil, Finance Minister Fitsum Assefa and Health Minister Mekdes Daba.

Abiy stated that Ethiopia's ambition for universal prosperity requires leadership to be physically fit, morally strong and intellectually capable, adding that military institutions offer valuable lessons in discipline, patriotism, teamwork, accountability and leadership.

Why it mattersSenior government officials, including ministers, are undergoing military training to foster discipline and leadership skills.

Drone strikes kill 25 civilians in Ethiopia

Witnesses report multiple drone attacks in Amhara and Tigray regions since Thursday, hitting a camp for displaced people.

At least 25 civilians, including children, died in drone strikes across Ethiopia's northern regions starting Thursday. Witnesses and a medical source reported the deaths on Friday. The Ethiopian government has been using drones in its conflict with rebels in Amhara and Tigray states.

In Amhara, four drone strikes hit a camp for displaced people on Thursday, according to one witness. Yasin, who requested anonymity, stated that 14 people were buried at the camp and two more died during medical treatment, with children and pregnant women among the victims. Other residents reported 14 and 15 deaths, including several children.

The strikes occurred near Addis Ababa, approximately 400 kilometers northeast of the capital, as students were returning from school. One witness described the dismemberment of his sister-in-law's body. At least 22 others were injured in repeated strikes on shelters housing families displaced by the conflict.

Why it mattersCivilians, including children and displaced families, are directly affected by drone strikes in conflict zones.

Ethiopia backs UAE diplomat for UN chief role

Addis Ababa welcomes Lana Nusseibeh's candidacy, citing her experience and commitment to global cooperation.

Ethiopia has announced its support for Emirati diplomat Lana Nusseibeh's bid to become the next United Nations secretary-general. The foreign ministry stated on X that the country values Nusseibeh's extensive diplomatic experience and dedication to multilateral cooperation.

The ministry's statement highlighted Ethiopia's position as a founding member of the United Nations and its strong belief in the importance of international collaboration. This endorsement comes as the global body prepares to select its next leader.

Ethiopia's support signals its engagement in international diplomacy and its willingness to back candidates it believes can strengthen global governance. The UN is expected to announce further details regarding the selection process in the coming months.

Why it mattersEthiopia's government is actively participating in the selection of a new UN secretary-general, influencing international leadership.

Ethiopian gunfire heard as thousands flee into Kenya

Over 36,000 people have crossed into Kenya from Ethiopia due to ongoing fighting.

Gunfire was heard from Ethiopia into Kenya's town of Moyale on Friday morning as civilians fled violence. An Associated Press journalist recorded the sounds on October 30. The United Nations reported that 36,000 people had crossed into Kenya by Tuesday.

The influx of refugees, mostly women and children, is due to fighting near Moyale, according to the UN. Benedict Munywoki, deputy county commissioner for Moyale Sub-County, stated that more people are fleeing Ethiopia into Kenya. Aid agencies including the UN refugee agency, International Organization for Migration, and Kenya Red Cross are providing medical care, food, and shelter.

An Ethiopian doctor, Seifu Negash, who fled to Kenya, is volunteering with the Kenya Red Cross to treat refugees. The conflict on the Ethiopian side involves a territorial dispute between Oromia and Somali regional administrations, exacerbated by the killing of a 13-year-old girl. Local leaders in Kenya's Marsabit County are seeking support, concerned that the arrivals could strain resources amid existing food and water shortages.

Why it mattersEthiopians fleeing violence are seeking refuge and aid in Kenya, straining local resources.
