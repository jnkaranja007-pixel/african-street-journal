Sudanese Army Downs Drone Near Ethiopian Border

The Sudanese army announced Thursday it shot down an "enemy strategic drone" that had crossed into Blue Nile state from the direction of Ethiopia.

The Sudanese army announced Thursday it shot down an "enemy strategic drone" that crossed into the country from the direction of Ethiopia. The unmanned aerial vehicle was downed in the morning north of Sarkam in Sudan’s Blue Nile state, according to a statement from the army’s General Command. This incident follows an earlier report this month of the Sudanese army shooting down three FH-95 drones in North Kordofan state.

The army described the downed aircraft as a "strategic" unmanned vehicle but did not offer further specifics. Blue Nile State, which shares a border with Ethiopia, holds strategic significance amid the ongoing conflict between Sudan’s army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF).

Sudan has been embroiled in a war between its army and the RSF since April 2023, resulting in tens of thousands of deaths and displacing approximately 13 million people, according to United Nations and international estimates. Sudanese authorities and human rights groups have accused the RSF of targeting civilian infrastructure.

Why it mattersThe drone incident near the Ethiopian border affects regional security and could impact cross-border trade and movement for people living in the affected areas.

Enat Bank Shares Registered with Ethiopian Capital Market Authority

The Ethiopian Capital Market Authority has officially approved the registration of nearly 6.8 million existing shares for Enat Bank Share Company, enhancing transparency.

Enat Bank Share Company has received official approval from the Ethiopian Capital Market Authority (ECMA) for the registration of close to 6.8 million existing shares. The capital market regulator issued a public notice on August 27, 2026, confirming the approval of Enat’s securities registration statement.

The registration, formalized under the Public Offer and Trading of Securities Directive No. 1030/2024, encompasses a total of 6,785,310 shares currently held by shareholders. ECMA stated that the notice serves solely to inform the public and does not constitute an endorsement or solicitation for buying or selling securities.

This action aligns with the directive's requirement for all publicly offered, sold, or previously issued securities to be registered with the Authority, unless specifically exempted. The move aims to ensure transparency and regulatory oversight as Ethiopia's capital market continues its development.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the registration of existing shares with the capital market authority increases transparency and regulatory oversight for Enat Bank's securities.

25 Partners Warn Ethiopia on Renewed Tigray Conflict

International coalition urges adherence to peace deal amid stalled talks and troop build-ups, highlighting catastrophic humanitarian consequences.

A coalition of 25 international partners has issued a joint warning regarding the escalating tensions in northern Ethiopia, urging all parties to constructively engage in implementing the 2022 Pretoria peace agreement. The statement, backed by the European Union Delegation and African Union, expresses deep concern over developments and welcomes renewed mediation efforts, including recent visits by AU High Representative Olusegun Obasanjo.

The warning comes as both sides have been massing troops along the northern border, with accusations of drone strikes and military preparations exchanged between the Tigray People’s Liberation Front (TPLF) and the federal army. The joint statement specifically highlights that a renewed military confrontation in the drought-stricken Tigray region, which faces significant humanitarian needs, would be devastating for its people.

The Tigrayan government confirmed Obasanjo's visit to Mekelle but noted a lack of meaningful follow-up on critical unresolved issues, including lifting an economic embargo, ending military encirclement, restoring occupied territories, facilitating the return of displaced persons, and restoring the TPLF's legal status. The government expressed frustration with a process characterized by repeated talks and unfulfilled assurances.

Why it mattersA renewed conflict in Tigray would be devastating for the people of northern Ethiopia, who are already facing drought and staggering humanitarian needs.

Argentina Reiterates Malvinas Sovereignty Claim at International Forums

International bodies including the OAS and UN C24 have again called for negotiations between Argentina and the United Kingdom to resolve the Malvinas Islands dispute.

Argentina's Ministry of Foreign Affairs has reaffirmed its commitment to resolving the Malvinas Islands sovereignty dispute through sustained diplomatic efforts. Recent pronouncements at the Organization of American States (OAS) and the United Nations Special Committee on Decolonization (C24) reiterated calls for negotiations between Argentina and the United Kingdom to settle the matter peacefully and in accordance with international law.

These international forums have clearly reaffirmed the existence of a sovereignty dispute over the Malvinas, South Georgias, and South Sandwich Islands and their surrounding maritime areas. While resolutions do not resolve the dispute themselves, they serve to maintain the obligation to negotiate, a mandate established by the United Nations, and highlight the United Kingdom's continued refusal to engage in such talks.

The Argentine government is actively engaged in explaining the legal basis of its claim, presenting arguments against the applicability of self-determination in this case, and building a network of shared positions. This sustained effort aims to keep alive the essential truth that Malvinas is integral to Argentina's territorial integrity, oceanic projection, bicontinental identity, and strategic depth in the South Atlantic.

Why it mattersArgentine foreign policy and national identity are shaped by the ongoing Malvinas sovereignty dispute, impacting its territorial claims and strategic interests in the South Atlantic.
