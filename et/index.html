Compensation Disputes Halt Ethiopian Electric Power Projects

Billions of birr are tied up in legal battles over compensation, delaying national grid expansion and new electrification initiatives.

Ethiopian Electric Power (EEP) faces significant operational bottlenecks, with ongoing and planned energy projects stalled by complex compensation disputes. The state-owned utility reports that systemic legal and procedural gaps have led to billions of birr being locked in litigation, threatening national grid expansion. EEP CEO Ashebir Balcha stated that the crisis affects both existing infrastructure and new developments, with over seven billion birr currently involved in active compensation-related disputes.

These financial drains stem from procedural loopholes, fraudulent claims, and third-party interference, forcing unjustified payouts. A revised Expropriation Proclamation in July 2024 shifted property valuation and compensation responsibilities to regional administrations to streamline processes. However, a lack of clear implementation directives and insufficient regional capacity have created new administrative hurdles, slowing down electrification projects while legacy disputes remain unresolved.

EEP acknowledges that energy infrastructure projects face unique social pressures, as transmission lines often cross residential and agricultural lands without direct community power access, unlike roads. Discrepancies in valuing communal lands and traditional holdings have historically triggered friction and legal challenges. EEP has completed an internal assessment with recommendations to be presented to its board and relevant government bodies for structural policy and legislative refinements.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because billions of birr are tied up in compensation disputes, creating financial bottlenecks and delaying crucial infrastructure projects.

Thailand Backs Ethiopia's Sea Access Bid

Thailand's Deputy Prime Minister stated Ethiopia's pursuit of maritime access is legitimate and should be resolved through dialogue.

Thailand's Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sihasak Phuangketkeow has voiced support for Ethiopia's ambition to gain access to the sea. Phuangketkeow stated that Ethiopia's quest is legitimate and should be pursued through peaceful dialogue and cooperation with its neighbors. These remarks were made during an official visit to Ethiopia, where discussions also covered the country's ongoing efforts to secure maritime routes.

The Thai minister's comments signal international acknowledgment of Ethiopia's strategic needs. Ethiopia, a landlocked nation, has been actively seeking ways to establish direct access to the sea for trade and economic purposes. Phuangketkeow's endorsement suggests a willingness from Thailand to support diplomatic solutions to this long-standing issue.

This diplomatic engagement highlights the importance of regional cooperation in addressing landlocked countries' economic challenges. Ethiopia's push for sea access is seen as crucial for its economic development and integration into global trade networks. The dialogue initiated with Thailand could pave the way for further international support and collaborative efforts.

Why it mattersEthiopia's pursuit of sea access is supported by Thailand, which views it as a legitimate goal to be achieved through dialogue and cooperation.

Addisu Gobena Breaks Sydney Marathon Course Record

The Ethiopian runner set a new fastest time of 2:04:42, surpassing the previous record and finishing ahead of his compatriot.

Addisu Gobena shattered the Sydney Marathon course record on Sunday, clocking a winning time of 2:04:42 at the World Athletics Platinum Label event. He broke away from a leading pack of seven runners in the final stages to secure his second career marathon victory, narrowly beating fellow Ethiopian Chimdessa Debele by three seconds.

The performance saw the first seven male finishers all record times faster than the previous course record of 2:06:06, set by Hailemaryam Kiros. Gobena's previous personal best was 2:05:01, achieved during his marathon debut in Dubai in 2024.

In the women's race, Kenya's Peres Jepchirchir won in 2:18:31, narrowly missing the course record. Ethiopia's Shure Demise finished third in the women's event with a time of 2:22:33, followed by compatriots Haven Hailu in fourth and Meseret Abebayahu in fifth.

Why it mattersEthiopian athletes achieved significant success in a major international marathon, showcasing national talent on a global stage.
