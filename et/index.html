Ethiopia launches national cybersecurity awareness month

The Seventh National Cybersecurity Awareness Month focuses on making citizens vigilant for a secure nation.

Ethiopia is reinforcing digital security through its Seventh National Cybersecurity Awareness Month, themed “An Alert Citizen for a Secure Nation.” This initiative encourages collective vigilance to protect citizens, businesses, and public institutions as they increasingly use mobile financial services, e-commerce, and online platforms. The campaign aims to build trust in the digital economy by safeguarding personal information and transactions.

The "Secure by Habit" call to action urges everyone to integrate cybersecurity into daily behavior, moving beyond an annual focus. This push comes as digital transformation creates new opportunities alongside sophisticated cyber threats like phishing, ransomware, and AI-driven scams. International findings from the World Economic Forum and Verizon highlight the growing risks from third parties and software vulnerabilities, underscoring the need for strong systems and informed decisions.

A secure digital environment is presented as essential for technology adoption, inclusion, innovation, business continuity, and national development. The campaign emphasizes that consistent safer choices, not just awareness, are the true measure of success in protecting against cyber intrusions. The WannaCry ransomware attack in 2017, which affected over 200,000 computers globally, serves as a reminder of how a single security weakness can disrupt essential services and undermine public trust.

Why it mattersEthiopian citizens are directly affected by the need to make safer choices online to protect their personal information and transactions in the growing digital economy.

Ethiopia coffee exports earn $878.5M

Revenue increased 15% in three months as China became the third-largest buyer of Ethiopian beans.

Ethiopia generated US$878.52 million from coffee exports in the first three months of its fiscal year, a 15% rise from the same period last year. During this period, the country shipped 131,069.51 tonnes of coffee, meeting 91% of its export volume target. China has emerged as the third-largest buyer, purchasing 15,125.54 tonnes worth US$103 million.

Saudi Arabia remained the top destination, buying 28,362.77 tonnes for US$196.85 million, while Germany was second with 22,924.18 tonnes valued at US$136.69 million. The Ethiopian Coffee and Tea Authority stated that the improved performance is due to better coffee quality, increased technology adoption, and coordinated stakeholder efforts.

The Authority plans to intensify efforts to boost coffee export volumes and earnings and access more international markets. Coffee is a major pillar of Ethiopia's agriculture-based economy, supporting export earnings and livelihoods across the sector.

Why it mattersEthiopian farmers and the agriculture sector benefit from increased export earnings and efforts to expand market access.

Ethiopian women make reusable sanitary pads

Hanan Ahmed's company produces 1,000 washable pads daily to help girls stay in school.

In Addis Ababa, women are sewing reusable sanitary pads to address the high cost of imported products. Hanan Ahmed founded Han's With Care in 2021 to provide an affordable alternative, aiming to restore dignity and keep girls in school.

The lack of affordable sanitary pads in Ethiopia means many women and girls miss school or resort to unsafe methods. Ahmed, inspired by her own experiences with heavy flow and embarrassment, left the fashion industry to start the business. Her company now produces washable cotton pads that can be reused for up to two years.

Sponsors, including banks, fund the production of around 1,000 pads daily, which are distributed free to girls and women nationwide. At Nigat Kokeb School, 15-year-old Betselot Mahareg finds the reusable pads a practical and less embarrassing solution than disposable ones.

Why it mattersGirls in Ethiopia can now attend school without interruption due to menstruation thanks to affordable, reusable sanitary pads.

Ethiopian Jews mark Sigd holy day in Jerusalem

Thousands gathered in Jerusalem to mark the holy day of Sigd, a tradition of introspection and prayer.

Thousands of Ethiopian Jews gathered in Jerusalem on Monday to mark the holy day of Sigd. Religious leaders, known as Kessim, led the community in the day of introspection and prayer. The holiday traditionally pleaded for a return to Israel.

Now that most Ethiopian Jews live in Israel, the community gathers in Jerusalem to commemorate the day and celebrate their culture. "Our people are successful this generation made progress," said religious leader Michael Maharat. He expressed hope that Ethiopian Jews still in Ethiopia would be able to immigrate.

In 1991, Israel airlifted approximately 14,500 Ethiopian Jews to Israel during Operation Solomon. The Ethiopian Jewish community in Israel now numbers 180,000 people. Ethiopian Jews were historically called "Falashas," a derogatory term meaning "strangers."

Business · 2026-11-10

Business coalition urges COP31 to close electrification gap

We Mean Business Coalition wants the climate summit to focus on turning electrification ambition into actual deployment.

The We Mean Business Coalition has urged the COP31 Presidency to prioritize closing the electrification implementation gap at the upcoming climate summit in Antalya. The coalition, a non-profit focused on business action for climate, wants to translate the ambition of raising electricity's share of global energy demand to 35% by 2035 into concrete deployment. This call comes one week before governments convene for the summit.

Recent research by WMBC has repeatedly identified an implementation gap, where policy frameworks are not translating into on-the-ground progress. This gap leaves countries vulnerable to energy insecurity and price volatility, while also hindering industrial competitiveness and investment. Businesses are experiencing increased costs and delayed projects as a result.

The Electrification Competitiveness Index shows that while China leads in actual electrification, nations like the US are at risk of losing momentum. Major European economies, including Germany, the UK, Italy, and Poland, are also struggling to match their strong policy frameworks with comparable progress. Without faster deployment, businesses face tougher decisions about capital investment.

Why it mattersBusinesses face higher costs, delayed projects, and tougher decisions about where to put capital due to the electrification implementation gap.
