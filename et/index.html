Ethio Telecom deploys AI network technology

The new system increases 4G capacity by 11 percent and user throughput by 40 percent.

Ethio Telecom partnered with Chinese manufacturer ZTE Corporation to implement Ethiopia’s first pre-commercial Artificial Intelligence Radio Access Network (AIR RAN) on November 4, 2026. This technology uses AI to improve mobile network capacity and data speeds.

The deployment has increased 4G network capacity by 11 percent and improved peak-hour user throughput by 40 percent compared with conventional solutions, according to the companies. The system uses ZTE’s AIREngine and AI-driven scheduling to allocate radio resources more intelligently during high traffic.

This initiative supports Ethio Telecom’s “Next Horizon: Digital & Beyond 2028” strategy to transform into an AI-driven digital enterprise. The company serves over 90 million subscribers and aims to improve network efficiency without solely adding physical infrastructure.

Why it mattersEthiopians using mobile data services will experience improved network speeds and capacity.

ICRISAT promotes soil health solutions in Southern Ethiopia

Farmer field days from November 4 to 6, 2026, showcase digital tools and science-based practices.

The International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics (ICRISAT) and Ethiopia’s Ministry of Agriculture are holding farmer field days in Southern Ethiopia from November 4 to 6, 2026. These events aim to promote innovative and cost-effective soil health and fertilizer management solutions to farming communities.

Development partners including Precision Development, People In Need, and Self Help Africa are collaborating to raise awareness about practical methods to improve agricultural productivity and lower production costs. Farmers are learning about science-based soil fertility management, appropriate fertilizer application, and improved crop production through demonstrations and knowledge sharing.

A key innovation featured is the AFER_LERSHA (Soil for Farm) Telegram Bot, a digital advisory tool offering tailored fertilizer recommendations. This digital service bridges research and practice, enabling farmers to make informed decisions, optimize fertilizer use, and achieve better yields at reduced costs.

Why it mattersEthiopian farmers in Southern Ethiopia are learning about new methods to improve their soil and fertilizer use, increasing their yields and reducing costs.

China grants Ethiopia $15 million for railway parts

The funds will support spare parts, maintenance equipment, and technical assistance for the Ethio-Djibouti railway.

China has provided Ethiopia with a $15 million grant to secure spare parts, maintenance equipment, and technical assistance for the Ethio-Djibouti railway. The agreement, signed in Addis Ababa on November 4, 2026, aims to enhance the reliability of this transport corridor.

The grant will supply mechanical and electrical spare parts for electric locomotives, specialized track components, and station-yard accessories. It also includes machinery for routine railway inspection and maintenance, along with technical training from Chinese experts to build local capacity. Ethiopia’s State Minister of Finance, Tiguist Fisseha, and China’s Minister Counsellor Liu Xiaoguang signed the deal.

This support is designed to improve the railway's operational reliability and maintenance, ensuring the availability of essential components. The Ethio-Djibouti railway, which began operations in January 2018, is Ethiopia's main maritime gateway and a key part of its freight transport strategy.

Why it mattersEthiopian businesses and the national economy rely on the Ethio-Djibouti railway for freight transport and access to maritime trade.

Ethiopia clashes push 50,000 into Kenya

Fighting between Somali and Oromo communities over Moyale town has displaced tens of thousands seeking safety.

Deadly clashes between Ethiopian Somalis and Oromos over the town of Moyale have driven an estimated 50,000 people into neighbouring Kenya since October 18. The fighting, which has been ongoing daily, has made return home unsafe for the displaced.

The United Nations refugee agency (UNHCR) reports that at least 11,000 displaced individuals, primarily women and children, are now sheltered in temporary sites like schools. Others are being hosted by local communities in Kenya's border county, where two-thirds of residents live below the poverty line, straining local resources. Beshir Dadie Tufa, a father of three, told AFP the fighting continues daily and people are dying.

Conflict monitor ACLED has documented several deaths in the clashes, though an exact toll is difficult to confirm. Moyale itself is a town that straddles the border between Ethiopia and Kenya.

Ethiopia plans quarterly GDP reporting

The move aims to modernize economic statistics and improve fiscal transparency.

Ethiopia is preparing to report its Gross Domestic Product (GDP) quarterly as part of a plan to update its national economic statistics system. This transition from annual to quarterly reporting will improve fiscal transparency and support evidence-based policymaking.

Fitsum Assefa, Minister of Finance, stated that the government will build on recent GDP rebasing reforms to enhance economic and public finance information. The initiative includes modernizing Government Finance Statistics and aligning national accounting with international standards.

The reform involves improving the classification of public revenues, expenditures, assets, and liabilities. Ethiopia's GDP was estimated at 24.03 trillion birr ($201.42 billion) in the 2024/25 fiscal year, expanding to 31.86 trillion birr ($210.70 billion) in 2025/26.

Why it mattersEthiopian businesses and investors will have more frequent data on the country's economic performance.
