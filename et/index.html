Allied Gold begins wet commissioning at Kurmuk mine

The Canadian company expects to pour its first gold within days from the Benishangul-Gumuz Regional State operation.

Allied Gold Corporation has started wet commissioning at its Kurmuk Gold Mine in western Ethiopia, moving the project towards production. Water is now flowing to the processing plant, allowing individual circuits to be tested before ore is introduced. The company expects to begin processing ore within days, targeting the first gold pour shortly after.

The mine, located in the Benishangul-Gumuz Regional State, has a crushing circuit already operational and a stockpile of crushed ore ready for the ramp-up. Allied Gold, which also operates in Côte d’Ivoire and Mali, views Kurmuk as a key project to strengthen its international standing. The operation uses truck-and-shovel methods for an open-pit mine supplying a carbon-in-leach processing plant.

Kurmuk currently holds Proven and Probable Mineral Reserves of 2.7 million ounces of gold. The mine is projected to produce 25,000 to 35,000 ounces in November and December 2026, with commercial production targeted within this period. Production is forecast to reach 55,000 to 65,000 ounces in the first quarter of 2027, with an annual average of nearly 300,000 ounces expected over the first four years.

Why it mattersEthiopia's Benishangul-Gumuz Regional State will see a new gold mine begin production.

Ethiopian PM trains cabinet in military drills

Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed says armed forces discipline builds stronger leaders and advances national development.

Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed has put his cabinet ministers and senior government officials through military training at the Hurso military training centre. Photos show the prime minister participating in exercises alongside ministers, deputy ministers, and heads of government institutions on October 31.

Abiy stated that military institutions offer valuable lessons in discipline, patriotism, teamwork, accountability, and leadership, which are necessary for Ethiopia's ambition to achieve universal prosperity. This training is unusual for senior civilian officials in Ethiopia.

The training aims to make leadership physically fit, morally strong, and intellectually capable. It is unclear when the training began or its expected duration, as Ethiopia faces continued fighting in parts of the northern Tigray region.

Why it mattersEthiopian cabinet ministers and senior government officials are undergoing military training to foster discipline and leadership skills.

Drone strikes kill 25 civilians in Ethiopia

Witnesses report multiple strikes hit northern regions since Thursday, with children among the victims in Amhara.

At least 25 civilians, including children, died in multiple drone strikes in Ethiopia's northern regions since Thursday, according to witnesses and a medical source.

In Amhara, a witness described four drone strikes hitting a camp for displaced people on Thursday, killing 14 people at the center with two more dying later. Two other residents reported 14 and 15 deaths respectively, with children among them.

The strikes occurred about 400 kilometers northeast of Addis Ababa, as students were returning from school, one witness said, adding his sister-in-law's body was dismembered.

Why it mattersCivilians in Ethiopia's Amhara and Tigray regions are directly affected by drone strikes during ongoing conflict.
