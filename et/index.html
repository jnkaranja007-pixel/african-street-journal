Ethiopian Airlines Adds Direct Freighter Route to Chengdu, China

The airline's cargo division launched a new direct freighter service connecting Addis Ababa with Chengdu, China, enhancing its Asian network.

Ethiopian Airlines has initiated a new direct freighter service between Addis Ababa and Chengdu, China, expanding its cargo operations in Asia. The airline's cargo and logistics business announced the service on Friday, establishing a direct cargo link between Africa and China. The inaugural flight was met with a ceremony and water cannon salute upon arrival in Chengdu, the capital of Sichuan province.

The new air link is expected to significantly boost trade and economic exchanges between western China and Africa, according to Ethiopian Cargo and Logistics services. China is a crucial market for Ethiopian, which operates the largest cargo network in Africa. This Chengdu service follows the addition of Urumqi to the airline's Chinese cargo network last year.

Ethiopian Airlines currently operates over 190 weekly flights to 27 Asian destinations, including 83 weekly flights to 11 cities in China. With a fleet of more than 145 modern aircraft and advanced cargo infrastructure, the airline transports nearly one million tons of cargo annually to over 145 global destinations.

Why it mattersEthiopian Airlines' new direct freighter service to Chengdu will facilitate increased trade and economic exchanges between western China and Africa.

Sudanese Army Shoots Down Drone Near Ethiopian Border

The Sudanese army reported downing an "enemy strategic drone" that entered its airspace from the direction of Ethiopia.

The Sudanese army announced on Thursday that it had shot down an "enemy strategic drone" that crossed into the country from the direction of Ethiopia. The unmanned aerial vehicle was downed in the morning north of Sarkam in Sudan’s Blue Nile state, according to a statement from the army’s General Command.

This incident follows an earlier report this month where the Sudanese army claimed to have shot down three FH-95 drones in North Kordofan state. The army described the latest downed aircraft as "strategic" but provided no further details about its origin or purpose.

Blue Nile State, which shares a border with Ethiopia, remains a strategically significant area amidst the ongoing conflict between the Sudanese army and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF). Sudanese authorities and human rights groups have accused the RSF of targeting civilian infrastructure in the region.

Why it mattersThe downing of a drone near the Ethiopian border affects investors by highlighting regional instability and potential risks to cross-border trade and infrastructure.

Excavator Bucket Kills Six in Addis Ababa Minibus Crash

A police report states that a falling excavator bucket crushed a minibus taxi in Bole Subcity, killing six passengers and injuring others.

At least six people died in Addis Ababa on Monday when a falling excavator bucket struck a moving minibus taxi. The accident occurred in the morning in Bole Subcity, according to a police report. Six passengers were killed instantly, and the driver and several other passengers sustained injuries. They were transported to nearby medical facilities for treatment.

The Addis Ababa Police have initiated an investigation to determine the precise cause of the incident. This event is part of a pattern of fatal traffic accidents in Ethiopia, which are frequently attributed to factors such as reckless driving, insufficient law enforcement, and poor vehicle maintenance.

As the nation's economy grows and its middle class expands, vehicle numbers are increasing. Experts are calling for enhanced safety enforcement, rigorous vehicle inspections, and a more stringent licensing system to help prevent future tragedies on the roads.

Why it mattersSix passengers were killed and others injured when an excavator bucket fell on their minibus, highlighting road safety concerns.

Thai Foreign Minister Arrives in Ethiopia for Bilateral Talks

Sihasak Phuangketkeow landed in Addis Ababa Friday to bolster diplomatic ties between Thailand and Ethiopia, according to the Ethiopian Foreign Ministry.

Thailand’s Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs, Sihasak Phuangketkeow, arrived in Addis Ababa on Friday for an official visit. The purpose of the visit is to strengthen bilateral relations between the two nations, as stated by Ethiopia’s Foreign Ministry.

Upon arrival at Addis Ababa Bole International Airport, Mr. Phuangketkeow was received by Ethiopia’s State Minister of Foreign Affairs. The visit signifies an effort to enhance diplomatic and potentially economic cooperation between Thailand and Ethiopia.

This engagement underscores Ethiopia's ongoing efforts to foster international partnerships and attract foreign interest. The specific outcomes of the bilateral discussions were not immediately detailed, but the visit itself marks a step in diplomatic engagement.

Why it mattersEthiopian officials are engaging with a foreign dignitary to strengthen bilateral relations, potentially impacting future diplomatic and economic cooperation.

25 Partners Warn Ethiopia on Renewed Tigray Conflict

International coalition urges adherence to peace deal amid stalled talks and troop build-ups along the border.

A coalition of 25 international partners has issued a joint statement expressing deep concern over the stalled peace talks in northern Ethiopia and warning of catastrophic consequences should conflict resume. The statement, backed by the European Union and African Union diplomatic missions, along with several EU member states, Canada, Norway, and the United Kingdom, underscores the fragility of the 2022 Pretoria peace agreement.

The international partners are urging all parties to engage constructively and in good faith to implement the Permanent Cessation of Hostilities Agreement. This comes as both sides have been massing troops along the northern border, with Tigrayan forces accusing the federal army of drone strikes and Addis Ababa alleging military preparations by Tigrayan forces. The statement specifically highlights that renewed military confrontation in the drought-stricken Tigray region, which faces significant humanitarian needs, would be devastating.

The Government of the National Regional State of Tigray confirmed a recent visit by AU High Representative Olusegun Obasanjo to Mekelle, noting a lack of meaningful follow-up on critical unresolved issues since June. These include lifting the economic embargo, ending military encirclement, restoring occupied territories, facilitating the return of displaced persons, and restoring the TPLF's legal and political status. The Tigrayan government expressed frustration with a process characterized by repeated talks and unfulfilled assurances.

Why it mattersThe people of northern Ethiopia are at risk of devastating consequences from a renewed military confrontation due to stalled peace talks and ongoing troop build-ups.
