Ethiopian PM urges African parties to build consensus on growth

Abiy Ahmed called for unity, accountability, and inclusive governance to improve lives.

Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed urged African political parties to agree on long-term national priorities, saying unity, accountability, and inclusive governance were essential to improving people’s lives. Abiy delivered the remarks at the second African Political Parties Summit in Addis Ababa on November 6.

The Prime Minister stated that political parties should look beyond electoral competition to build consensus on growth. The summit brought together political leaders from across the continent.

The second African Political Parties Summit aimed to foster dialogue on national priorities.

Why it mattersEthiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed spoke at a summit in Addis Ababa about improving citizens' lives through political consensus.

Ethiopia pushes biotech research commercialization

Minister Frehiwot Tamru calls for public-private partnerships to speed up market access for new technologies.

Ethiopia's Minister of Innovation and Technology, Frehiwot Tamru, urged greater public-private collaboration to commercialize research findings. Discussions at the Bio and Emerging Technology Institute (BETIn) also focused on establishing a national shared laboratory to support institutions nationwide.

The proposed laboratory aims to prevent resource duplication and enhance the use of existing capacities, thereby boosting the biotechnology sector's competitiveness, according to the Ministry of Innovation and Technology. BETIn has transferred technologies in crop development, livestock genetics, vaccines, and industrial processing over the past five years.

Achievements include improved maize and rice varieties, enset-processing technologies, Spirulina cultivation, Boran cattle genetic improvement, and poultry vaccines. These advancements have been transferred to youth enterprises, commercial producers, and investors, contributing to food security and industrial development.

Why it mattersEthiopian citizens will benefit from improved crop varieties, livestock genetics, and new processing technologies that enhance food security and create jobs.

Business coalition urges COP31 to close electrification gap

We Mean Business Coalition wants the climate summit to focus on turning ambition into actual electricity deployment.

The We Mean Business Coalition (WMBC) has urged the COP31 Presidency to prioritize closing the electrification implementation gap at the upcoming climate summit in Antalya. The coalition wants the summit to focus on turning the 35x35 ambition, aiming to increase electricity's share of global final energy demand to 35% by 2035, into tangible deployment.

WMBC research has repeatedly identified an implementation gap where policy and infrastructure outpace on-the-ground progress. This gap leaves countries vulnerable to energy insecurity and price volatility, while businesses face increased costs and delayed projects, according to the coalition. The Electrification Competitiveness Index shows China leading in actual electrification, while major European economies struggle to translate policy into progress.

The coalition's call comes one week before governments gather for COP31. Businesses are concerned about competitiveness and investment decisions being impacted by the slow pace of electrification. The Electrification Competitiveness Index also noted that the US risks losing momentum in this area.

Why it mattersBusinesses face higher costs, delayed projects, and tougher decisions about where to invest capital due to the slow pace of electrification.

Business · 2026-11-08

AfDB chief urges African finance sector to aid businesses

African Development Bank Group President Sidi Ould Tah called for financial institutions to align with business cycles.

African Development Bank Group President Sidi Ould Tah urged Africa’s financial institutions to match the pace of businesses they serve to translate financial strength into economic power. He spoke at the Africa Financial Summit (AFIS) in Luanda on 3 November, presenting the New African Financial Architecture for Development (NAFAD) framework.

NAFAD aims to strengthen collaboration and rethink capital mobilisation and risk sharing within Africa’s financial ecosystem. President Ould Tah highlighted a disconnect between the reporting cycles and processing times of financial institutions and the business cycles, order fulfilment, and talent retention needs of companies. He stated this gap causes lost economic growth.

The framework is designed to coordinate initiatives within the African financial system. The summit opened on 3 November in Luanda.

Why it mattersAfrican businesses face lost economic growth due to a disconnect with financial institutions' operational timelines.
