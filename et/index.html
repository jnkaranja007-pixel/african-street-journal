EPSS Wins Gold Award for Supply Chain Excellence

The Ethiopian Pharmaceutical Supply Service received the top honor at the Fifth Africa Supply Chain Excellence Awards for significant improvements in procurement and management.

The Ethiopian Pharmaceutical Supply Service (EPSS) has secured the Gold Award at the Fifth Africa Supply Chain Excellence Awards (ASCEA 2026) in Pretoria, South Africa. EPSS Director General Dr. Abdulkedir Gelgelo stated the award recognizes the service's enhanced performance in pharmaceutical and medical supply procurement, alongside broader supply-chain management reforms. Key factors contributing to this achievement included digital transformation, enterprise resource planning (ERP) implementation, data-driven decision-making, and streamlined procurement processes.

Dr. Abdulkedir highlighted that this recognition propels EPSS closer to its 2030 vision of becoming a leading African supply-chain institution. He attributed the success to the collective dedication of EPSS leadership and staff, emphasizing the organization's continued commitment to ensuring a stable, accessible, and reliable supply of medicines and medical equipment. The award also signals EPSS's commitment to quality and encourages pooled procurement with fewer suppliers.

Historically, EPSS faced systemic weaknesses, including lengthy procurement cycles that could take up to 400 days and limited inventory visibility due to fragmented records and manual tracking. Previous regulations treated critical medicines similarly to everyday items, overlooking specialized needs like long lead times and import dependency. These challenges impacted the delivery of essential medicines to over 420 hospitals, 4,100 health centers, and 18,500 health posts across Ethiopia.

Why it mattersThe Ethiopian Pharmaceutical Supply Service (EPSS) won a gold award for improving its supply chain, which affects the reliable delivery of medicines to public health facilities across the country.

Deloitte Gains Investment Bank License in Ethiopia

The Ethiopian Capital Market Authority has granted Deloitte a license to operate as an investment banking service provider, marking the second foreign firm to receive such.

Deloitte has secured an investment bank license from the Ethiopian Capital Market Authority (ECMA), enabling it to offer non-brokerage investment banking services. This license, granted after a rigorous assessment, allows the subsidiary, D and T Ethiopia Management Consulting Plc, to facilitate mergers and corporate organizations. The move signifies growth in Ethiopia's capital market, with Deloitte transitioning from its previous role as a securities investment advisor.

ECMA stated that the new license does not permit Deloitte to act as a securities broker or dealer. This development makes Deloitte the second foreign entity to be licensed for investment banking services in Ethiopia, bringing the total number of licensed investment banks to nine. The first foreign investment bank to receive a license was United Capital Financial Services Plc, a subsidiary of Nigeria's United Capital Group, in June 2026.

The Ethiopian capital market regulator views Deloitte's license upgrade as a demonstration of market trust and capacity. ECMA directors previously described the entry of United Capital as a historic milestone, reflecting strong confidence in the credibility of Ethiopia's emerging financial ecosystem. Deloitte had reentered the Ethiopian market two years prior as a securities investment advisor.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as Deloitte can now offer expanded financial services like facilitating mergers and corporate organizations, increasing options in the capital market.

Ethiopia Delegates Back Executive Presidency Plan

Constitutional changes endorsed by over 4,000 delegates could merge prime minister and president roles, raising concerns about power consolidation.

Ethiopia's national dialogue has concluded with delegates overwhelmingly endorsing constitutional changes that would create a powerful executive presidency, merging the current roles of prime minister and president. The proposal, backed by more than 4,000 participants in the weeks-long dialogue, aims to address political tensions and ethnic divisions. Critics, however, warn that this move could allow Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed to further consolidate his control following his party's landslide election victory.

The recommendations from the national dialogue, which concluded on August 22, are expected to be presented to parliament when it reconvenes in October. While Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed was not present at the dialogue, observers anticipate he would be a leading candidate for the new executive position if the changes are adopted. The president would be selected by a parliament currently dominated by members of his ruling party.

Supporters of the proposed executive presidency argue it could foster a more unifying national leadership, with the president representing all ethnic groups rather than being elected as a parliamentary representative from a specific region. Conversely, opponents express fears that the reforms could fundamentally alter the political system to concentrate power within a single office and individual, potentially serving one person's interests.

Why it mattersThe proposed executive presidency could concentrate power, potentially affecting the stability of the political system and the direction of governance for all citizens.

National Bank Seeks Price Comparator Tool Developer

The central bank is initiating a tender for a digital platform to help consumers compare financial product pricing and features.

The National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE) has issued an invitation for bids to develop a Financial Services Price Comparator Tool. This initiative aims to enhance transparency, strengthen consumer protection, and support informed financial decision-making within Ethiopia's expanding financial sector. The tool will be a regulator-led, non-commercial digital platform designed to allow consumers to easily access, compare, and understand the pricing and service features of various financial products.

This project is being financed with support from the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) through its Rural Financial Intermediation Programme (RUFIP). The NBE seeks a qualified consulting firm or consortium to design, develop, test, and support the implementation of this user-friendly and scalable platform. The bid closing and opening dates are both set for September 24, 2026.

The development of the comparator tool aligns with national objectives to improve financial capability and inclusion, particularly for rural and underserved populations. By standardizing financial product information, the NBE intends to empower consumers to make more appropriate financial choices and promote fair competition among financial institutions. The initiative addresses the growing complexity of financial products and non-standardized pricing structures that often make comparison difficult for consumers.

Why it mattersThe National Bank of Ethiopia is seeking bids for a tool that will allow consumers to compare financial product prices, aiming to improve transparency and consumer decision-making.

Thailand Backs Ethiopia's Sea Access Bid

Thailand's Deputy Prime Minister stated Ethiopia's pursuit of sea access is legitimate and should be resolved through dialogue and cooperation.

Thailand's Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sihasak Phuangketkeow has affirmed Ethiopia's quest for sea access as legitimate. He stated that this pursuit should be addressed through dialogue and cooperation with neighboring countries, during an official visit to Ethiopia.

Phuangketkeow's remarks came as Ethiopia continues its efforts to secure access to the sea. The visit provided a platform for discussions on this critical issue, highlighting international perspectives on Ethiopia's regional ambitions and economic development goals.

The Thai minister's statement offers diplomatic backing for Ethiopia's long-standing aspiration. This support emphasizes a peaceful and collaborative approach to resolving territorial and trade access challenges in the Horn of Africa region.

Why it mattersEthiopia's pursuit of sea access is supported by Thailand, which could influence regional cooperation and trade dynamics.
