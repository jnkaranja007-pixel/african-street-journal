Security · 2026-11-13

Ethiopian army takes control of Tigray capital Mekelle

Hundreds of people are reported dead after the renewed fighting, which began in late September.

Federal forces entered Mekelle, the capital of Ethiopia's northern Tigray region, less than two weeks after the fighting began in late September. The Tigray People's Liberation Front, which had run the region for decades, made what it called a "strategic withdrawal" from the city and other main towns, so the army met little resistance.

Hundreds of people are reported to have died in the conflict, including dozens killed in drone strikes and bombing raids by the air force. The fighting was a return to hostilities between the TPLF and the national army, nearly four years after the two-year civil war officially ended in 2022.

Life in Mekelle remains far from normal. Firut Me'ar, who runs a hair salon in the city, told the BBC that people have no money and no morale, and that business is almost non-existent. She said residents are uncertain about what comes next.

Why it mattersMekelle residents are struggling to feed their families, and business has nearly stopped as they wait to learn what comes next.

Business · 2026-11-13

Ethiopia taxes digital earnings

New rules require influencers and online freelancers to declare income and pay taxes.

Ethiopia has introduced new income tax rules for digital content creators, including social media influencers and online freelancers. The Council of Ministers issued Regulation No. 597/2026, which requires these creators to declare their earnings and pay taxes on income generated through digital platforms. Income from online content creation will be subject to business income tax or a 15 percent tax rate on other income, depending on the circumstances.

Creators earning over 50,000 birr annually from digital content are required to pay a 15 percent tax and declare their income quarterly. They must maintain accurate financial records of all income, including advertisements, sponsorships, and digital gifts, from both domestic and foreign sources. Online platforms and organizations facilitating payments to Ethiopian-resident creators must also submit quarterly reports to the Tax Authority detailing amounts paid.

The new regulation clarifies that Ethiopian residents cannot avoid tax obligations by registering accounts abroad or receiving payments outside the country. It also extends tax withholding to income earned through digital freelance services and other remote working arrangements. The Tax Authority can monitor creators' activities and cooperate with international platforms to gather earnings information.

Why it mattersDigital content creators earning over 50,000 birr annually must now pay a 15 percent tax and declare income quarterly.

Ethiopia and Britain discuss climate ties and COP32 preparations

Talks in Antalya covered the COP32 summit that Addis Ababa hosts next year, with Ethiopian and British ministers at the table.

Ethiopia and Britain discussed deepening climate cooperation and preparations for next year's COP32 summit in Addis Ababa, during talks held on Thursday in Antalya, Türkiye, Ethiopia's foreign ministry said.

Gedion Timothewos, Ethiopia's foreign minister and the COP32 president-designate, held the talks with British Foreign Secretary [name not provided in source]. Fitsum Assefa, the finance minister and the summit's chief negotiator, also took part.

The ministry's statement gave no further details of the outcome of the talks.

Why it mattersEthiopia is preparing to host COP32 next year, and the talks set out how the country and Britain will work together on climate policy before the summit.
