Ethiopia's ride-hailing sector pays 340 million birr in income tax

More than 30 platforms compete in Addis Ababa on fares and vehicle types, while 33,000 drivers, most of them self-employed, carry the costs of fuel and rent.

Source: Capital Ethiopia · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

More than 30 ride-hailing platforms operate in Addis Ababa, where passengers compare fares, choose vehicle types and see the price before booking. Options range from economy cars to motorcycles for short trips. A nurse in Bahir Dar said she books a ride after her 11:00 pm shift, reaching home about 20 minutes sooner than by public bus.

Over 33,000 drivers in Addis Ababa have generated more than 340 million birr in income tax for the sector. Self-employed drivers, many of whom rent their cars, work without guaranteed earnings or social protection. A driver in Adama said fuel, maintenance and taxes take a large share of his income, leaving what remains to cover rent, school fees, food and car repayments.

In Dire Dawa, about 6,000 Bajaj make up most of the public transport network. Researchers want rural Ethiopia to promote motorcycle-based transport, and they urge the government to ease the import barriers that keep those vehicles expensive.

Why it mattersDrivers in Addis Ababa pay the income tax and the fuel, maintenance and rent costs, so what they keep after expenses is what covers their households.
