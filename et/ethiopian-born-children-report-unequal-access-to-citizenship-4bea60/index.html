Ethiopian-born children report unequal access to citizenship

Some children of Ethiopian mothers and foreign fathers say they struggle to secure nationality, while another man obtained an identity card and passport without difficulty.

Source: Capital Ethiopia · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Children born and raised in Ethiopia to Ethiopian mothers and foreign fathers describe sharply different paths to citizenship. Monica Marcel, born in Addis Ababa to an Ethiopian mother and a Congolese father who arrived as a refugee, said she and her siblings have struggled to obtain Ethiopian nationality and still receive services under refugee status.

Marcel said her family repeatedly sought answers from authorities about their applications. She said the lack of citizenship exposed her to discrimination at school; her child’s future also worries her. Wude Kassa, 64, said her six children with her Congolese husband have not obtained citizenship and face difficulties finding employment despite growing up and studying in Ethiopia.

Louis Bauna, 54, said he obtained citizenship without difficulty. His mother’s nationality, birth certificate and other documents enabled him to get an Ethiopian identity card and passport.

Why it mattersMarcel and Kassa’s children face barriers to employment and services, while authorities have yet to provide Marcel’s family with answers on their applications.
