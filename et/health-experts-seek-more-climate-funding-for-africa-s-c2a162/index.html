Health experts seek more climate funding for Africa’s malaria response

The Roll Back Malaria Partnership says health gets only 6% of climate finance, despite rising pressure on health systems and medicine delivery.

Source: Capital Ethiopia · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

Health experts at the International Conference on Public Health in Africa in Addis Ababa called for more investment in climate-resilient healthcare, warning that climate change is extending malaria transmission into previously unaffected areas and disrupting medicine delivery.

Dr. Michael Adekunle-Charles, chief executive of the Roll Back Malaria Partnership to End Malaria, said malaria kills about 620,000 people worldwide each year, with roughly 95% of those deaths in Africa, mostly among children under five. He said only 6% of climate financing goes to health, and urged donors, African governments and private businesses to increase funding.

Dr. Elizabeth Juma, who leads the World Health Organization’s Africa office programme for eliminating neglected tropical diseases, said climate risks should be built into national health strategies. She warned that extreme weather damages health infrastructure and disrupts essential services.

Why it mattersAfrican health authorities must plan for climate-driven shifts in malaria and service disruption while health receives only 6% of climate finance.
