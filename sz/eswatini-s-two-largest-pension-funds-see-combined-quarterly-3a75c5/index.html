Eswatini's two largest pension funds see combined quarterly surplus fall to E59.2 million

The Public Enterprises Unit, which supervises state entities for the Finance Ministry, blamed the drop from about E800 million on the US-Israel war against Iran.

Source: Times of Eswatini · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Eswatini's two largest pension funds recorded a combined quarterly surplus of E59.2 million, down from about E800 million in the previous quarter, according to a report by the Public Enterprises Unit (PEU), which supervises state entities for the Ministry of Finance. The PEU attributed the fall to the US-Israel war against Iran.

The Public Service Pensions Fund (PSPF), which holds public servants' retirement savings, reported a net surplus of E4.86 million, compared with E1.52 billion in the prior quarter. Its equity revaluation shifted from gains of E840.71 million to losses of E680.60 million. The Eswatini National Provident Fund (ENPF), which reports to the PEU, saw its surplus fall to E54.3 million from E175.6 million. The PEU said volatility in oil, energy shares and defence stocks hurt investment performance.

The PEU said both funds remained financially sound. The PSPF held accumulated funds of E43.001 billion, while the ENPF's funding level, its measure of ability to meet long-term commitments to members, was 1.07 times, unchanged from the previous quarter.

Why it mattersMembers of both funds face much weaker quarterly results, though the PEU says both remain financially sound and puts the ENPF's funding level at 1.07 times.
