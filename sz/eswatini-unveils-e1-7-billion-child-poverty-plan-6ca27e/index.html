Eswatini unveils E1.7 billion child poverty plan

The plan targets a reduction in child poverty from 56.5% to 40% by 2028, with funding for eight deprivation dimensions.

Source: Times of Eswatini · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Eswatini’s Ministry of Economic Planning and Development has unveiled a E1.7 billion action plan to reduce child poverty from 56.5% to 40% by 2028. Under Secretary Siboniso Masilela presented it in Mbabane on Friday alongside the country’s second Multi-Dimensional Child Poverty Analysis report.

The report found that 46.6% of children face deprivation in three or more non-income dimensions, including education, health, water and nutrition. Rural deprivation stands at 51.8%, compared with 23.1% in urban centres. Masilela said the plan funds interventions across eight dimensions, with the largest allocations for vaccines, clean energy, water, sanitation and ICT infrastructure.

Masilela urged ministries to include interventions in sector plans and budgets, prioritise deprived rural communities in Lubombo and Shiselweni, coordinate across government and strengthen monitoring. Lubombo had the highest regional child deprivation rate, at 55.4%; Hhohho had the lowest, at 39.5%.

Why it mattersMinistries must build the interventions into sector plans and budgets to meet the 2028 child poverty target.
