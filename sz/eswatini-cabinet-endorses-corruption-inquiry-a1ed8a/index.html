Eswatini Cabinet endorses corruption inquiry

The government says the judicial investigation follows a parliamentary motion, as reported monthly losses from corruption rise to E90 million.

Source: Times of Eswatini · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Eswatini’s Cabinet has endorsed a judicial inquiry into corruption, following a motion passed recently in Parliament, Agriculture Minister Mandla Tshawuka said at a media engagement in Mbabane. The endorsement advances a call for an investigation, but no start date was given.

Finance Minister Neal Rijkenberg said estimated losses to corruption had increased to E90 million a month, from E80 million in earlier years. He acknowledged that the Anti-Corruption Commission had secured no convictions in recent years and said ministers responsible for foreign affairs and justice had worked to resolve institutional issues affecting the commission.

Rijkenberg said he hoped changes to the institutional arrangements would help the relevant bodies carry out their responsibilities more effectively.

Why it mattersThe Cabinet must now advance the inquiry, while Rijkenberg says estimated corruption losses have reached E90 million a month.
