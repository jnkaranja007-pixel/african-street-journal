Eswatini survey finds 82.8% of rural pregnancies unintended

A baseline assessment of 260 young women and girls also found that 72.2% of pregnancies across the surveyed group were unplanned.

Source: Eswatini Positive News · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

A February 2026 assessment of 260 young women and girls in Bhunya, Ngonini and Matsapha found that 82.8% of pregnancies among rural participants were unintended, compared with 69.6% among urban participants. The findings were presented on Sunday at the SADC Parliamentary Forum in Ezulwini.

Colani Hlatjwako, One Billion Rising Africa’s regional coordinator, said the assessment linked 20.7% of unintended pregnancies among rural participants to rape or violence. It also found that 93.3% of pregnancies among girls below the age of 18 were unplanned, and that 21% of all recorded pregnancies involved girls under 18.

Hlatjwako called on the government to declare gender-based violence a national disaster or equivalent emergency, finalise the Marriage Bill and provide sustainable funding for community prevention. Eswatini’s Children’s Protection and Welfare Act of 2012 sets 18 as the minimum marriage age, but enforcement and awareness remain challenges, particularly in rural communities.

Why it mattersRural communities face documented barriers to enforcing the 18-year marriage-age law, and Hlatjwako urged government funding for community prevention.
