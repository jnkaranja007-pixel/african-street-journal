dagga trade fuels sex work in Madlangampisi

Residents report increased visibility of sex workers, some from South Africa, drawn by money from illegal cannabis sales.

Source: Times of Eswatini · 2026-10-24 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Madlangampisi residents are concerned about a rise in sex work in their town, which they link to money from the illegal dagga trade. Sex workers, some reportedly from South Africa, have become more visible, particularly during the dagga harvesting season when traders are believed to have more cash.

Alex Tsabedze, the Indvuna Yenkhundla for Madlangampisi, confirmed the presence of sex workers, noting a change in the town's social environment and increased visibility of young women after dark. Residents also expressed worry about young girls allegedly forming relationships with dagga farmers and older men attracted by the trade's profits.

Police have been targeting illegal dagga cultivation and trafficking around Madlangampisi, destroying hectares of plants and arresting individuals for smuggling. Some seized drug consignments have been valued at millions of Emalangeni, reinforcing the perception of a significant illegal industry.

Why it mattersThe illegal dagga trade in Madlangampisi is reportedly changing the town's social environment, leading to an increase in sex work.
