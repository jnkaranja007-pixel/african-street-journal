Eswatini tightens checks on hazardous chemical imports

Authorities are compiling an inventory of persistent organic pollutants to decide which substances to eliminate, restrict or manage better.

Source: Eswatini Positive News · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Eswatini is increasing checks on prohibited chemicals at its borders, regulating licences for restricted substances and tracking how toxic substances are disposed of. The measures target persistent organic pollutants (POPs), a group of hazardous chemicals that includes polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) and can remain in the environment for decades.

Zandisile Howe, an environmental information officer at the Eswatini Environment Authority, said a national inventory is being compiled to establish which POPs are present, their quantities and the sectors where they are found. The assessment covers agriculture, healthcare, waste management and electronic waste, with consultations across the country's four regions.

The findings are expected to help authorities identify priority chemicals and decide which need complete elimination and where restrictions or better management are required. Dr Gcina Mavimbela, director of the University of Eswatini's Centre for Sustainable Energy Research, said phasing out some chemicals could be difficult for industries, particularly agriculture, that depend on them to protect crops and livestock.

Why it mattersAgricultural users who depend on restricted chemicals to protect crops and livestock will need safer, effective alternatives as the inventory guides which substances are phased out.
