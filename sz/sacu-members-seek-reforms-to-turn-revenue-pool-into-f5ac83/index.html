SACU members seek reforms to turn revenue pool into industrial driver

An African Development Bank-backed study will examine changes to the 116-year-old Southern African Customs Union, whose transfers help fund Eswatini's public spending.

Source: Times of Eswatini · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Eswatini and four other members of the Southern African Customs Union (SACU) are seeking to shift the 116-year-old bloc from sharing customs revenue towards industrialisation, investment and economic integration. An initiative supported by the African Development Bank is backing a comprehensive study to identify the reforms needed.

The bank's May 2026 project appraisal report says the study will examine obstacles including weak industrial growth, infrastructure gaps, skills shortages and limited financing. SACU collects duties on imports from outside the bloc into a common pool and distributes them among Eswatini, Botswana, Lesotho, Namibia and South Africa.

The report says the pool is particularly important to the smaller members' fiscal positions, and that Eswatini's transfers affect its ability to finance public expenditure. SACU's 2022–2027 strategic plan already calls for industrialisation, regional value chains and investment facilitation.

Why it mattersEswatini's government relies on SACU transfers as an important revenue source for financing public expenditure, and the study will identify reforms to the pool.
