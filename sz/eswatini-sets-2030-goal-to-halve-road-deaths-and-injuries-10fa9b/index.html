Eswatini sets 2030 goal to halve road deaths and injuries

Police and transport officials say the target rests on tougher enforcement, safer roads and public education, after 108 road deaths were recorded this year.

Source: Times of Eswatini · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Eswatini's Ministry of Public Works and Transport has reaffirmed its aim of halving road deaths and injuries by 2030, a target tied to the Eswatini National Road Safety Strategy (2023–2030) and the United Nations Decade of Action. Between January and October 2026, the kingdom logged 108 road fatalities and 309 serious injuries.

Under Secretary Richard Phungwayo, speaking at Lobamba Christian Revival for Africa Road Safety Day and the United Nations World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims, said the ministry will work with the Royal Eswatini Police Service. A joint enforcement drive run by the Road Transport Inspectorate since August 2026 will continue through the coming Festive Season. Matsebula, representing the National Commissioner of Police, warned that falling accident numbers must not breed complacency, adding that more than 90 per cent of crashes are preventable.

Officials said the plan also depends on coordination among the Road Safety Council, the Sincephetelo Motor Vehicle Accident Fund and emergency first responders. Safer road design, clear signage, pedestrian facilities and public education with churches, traditional leaders and civil society are also being funded, while the National Road Transportation Council is introducing cashless fares and the Bika customer service app.

Why it mattersMotorists, pedestrians and families of crash victims face stricter roadside enforcement through the Festive Season, with the ministry tracking progress towards the 2030 target.
