Eswatini Cabinet backs judicial inquiry into corruption

Finance minister Neal Rijkenberg said estimated monthly losses to corruption have risen from E80 million to E90 million.

Source: Times of Eswatini · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Eswatini's Cabinet has approved a judicial inquiry into corruption, Agriculture Minister Mandla Tshawuka said at an editors' forum in Mbabane. Parliament recently passed the motion calling for the investigation.

Finance Minister Neal Rijkenberg said estimated losses to corruption had risen from E80 million a month in earlier years to E90 million. He acknowledged that the Anti-Corruption Commission had secured no convictions in recent years and said concerns about its effectiveness were valid.

Rijkenberg said the foreign affairs and justice ministers had worked to resolve constitutional and institutional issues affecting the commission, and that he understood the outstanding issues had been addressed.

Why it mattersSuppliers already waiting for payment would see the inquiry as the first concrete test of whether government can curb losses it now estimates at E90 million a month.
