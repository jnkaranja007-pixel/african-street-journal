Eswatini pension funds’ combined quarterly surplus falls to E59.2 million

The Public Enterprises Unit links the decline from about E800 million to oil, energy and defence-stock volatility during the quarter.

Source: Times of Eswatini · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Eswatini’s two biggest pension funds, the Public Service Pensions Fund (PSPF) and the Eswatini National Provident Fund (ENPF), reported a combined quarterly surplus of E59.2 million, down from about E800 million. The Public Enterprises Unit (PEU), which supervises parastatals for the Finance Ministry, attributed the fall to the US/Israel war against Iran.

The PEU said PSPF’s net surplus dropped to E4.86 million from E1.52 billion in the previous quarter, alongside equity revaluation losses of E680.60 million, compared with gains of E840.71 million. ENPF recorded a surplus of E54.3 million, down from E175.6 million. The unit said the conflict amplified volatility in oil, energy equities and defence-related stocks, affecting investment performance.

The PEU said both funds remained financially sound: PSPF’s accumulated funds rose slightly to E43.001 billion, while ENPF’s funding level remained 1.07 times. Combined investment income rose to E950.22 million from E803.35 million; total expenditure increased from E557.76 million to E664.69 million.

Why it mattersThe two funds’ reported quarterly surpluses fell to a combined E59.2 million, and the PEU attributes the decline to investment volatility linked to the conflict.
