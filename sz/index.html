Sugar surge lifts Eswatini manufacturing growth

Food processing rose 27 per cent in the second quarter of 2026, driven by a 53.5 per cent increase in sugar production.

Eswatini's manufacturing sector grew 14 per cent in the second quarter of 2026, with food processing up 27 per cent, the Central Bank of Eswatini reports. The Central Bank attributed most of that gain to a 53.5 per cent jump in sugar production, which was supported by a larger area of harvested sugar cane and better sucrose and sugar yields.

Across the economy, output expanded by 6.5 per cent year-on-year in the quarter ended June 2026, when measured by Gross Domestic Product (GDP), after 5.9 per cent growth in the first quarter. Quarter-on-quarter growth, however, eased to 0.7 per cent from a revised 2.6 per cent in March. Primary, secondary and tertiary sectors all grew from a year earlier.

Non-food manufacturing expanded 11.1 per cent, held up by chemical products and cement, though both grew more slowly than in the first quarter. Textile and wearing apparel makers contracted 3.4 per cent amid uncertain conditions in their key export markets.

Why it mattersSugar millers and cane growers are the clearest winners, while textile and apparel firms that export face a contraction tied to weaker demand abroad.

Eswatini coaches face decade-long CAF A licence gap

Eswatini has not offered the CAF A coaching licence since 2017, leaving local coaches unable to meet new continental standards.

Eswatini has gone nearly a decade without offering the CAF A coaching licence, stranding local coaches as continental deadlines approach. The country has been unable to run a CAF A course since 2017, when the Confederation of African Football (CAF) suspended the programme continent-wide for updates. This near 10-year blackout affects coaches holding only the CAF B licence, preventing their progression.

New CAF coaching standards, approved in December 2024, will be fully enforced by the end of the current season, requiring head coaches in major men’s competitions to hold a CAF A or Pro licence. While coaches on contracts signed before the new rules have a grace period, new appointments must meet requirements immediately or risk being barred from technical areas. The Eswatini Football Association (EFA) has appointed interim leaders Dominic Kunene and Caleb Ngwenya to ensure compliance for current roles.

The Eswatini Football Coaches Association (EFCA) secretary, James Kunene, acknowledged the severe frustration caused by the prolonged drought, noting ongoing talks with the EFA offer hope for a breakthrough. He stated that a lack of a dedicated education officer at the EFA is the primary obstacle to relaunching the curriculum. The national pool of CAF A licence holders has dwindled from 22 to around 18 since 2017.

Why it mattersCoaches in Eswatini risk being barred from technical areas and official CAF competitions if they cannot obtain a CAF A licence by the end of the season.

Eswatini weighs wildlife partnership with Tusk

Foreign Affairs Minister Pholile Shakantu met Tusk Chairman Alexander Rhodes OBE in London to discuss ranger welfare and expanded conservation work at home.

Eswatini is exploring a new conservation partnership with the international wildlife organisation Tusk, aimed at strengthening wildlife protection and improving the welfare of rangers. Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation Senator Pholile Shakantu held talks with Tusk Chairman Alexander Rhodes OBE in London, on the sidelines of the 14th Tusk Conservation Awards.

Shakantu represented Eswatini at the ceremony, which is held under the patronage of Prince William, the Prince of Wales, and recognises African conservationists for courage and leadership in protecting endangered wildlife. Speaking after the talks, she said, "I am looking forward to welcoming the Tusk Conservation team to the Kingdom of Eswatini in early 2027."

Talks centred on bringing Tusk's programmes into Eswatini, with ranger welfare a priority. The team's visit next year is set to open further discussions on where the two sides could cooperate.

Sport · 2026-11-15

Eswatini hold Malta 1-1 in first match on European soil

Captain Philani Mkhontfo scored Eswatini's first-ever goal in Europe in first-half stoppage time, even after Gift Gamedze was sent off in the 29th minute.

Eswatini drew 1-1 with Malta in an international friendly at Ta' Qali National Stadium on Thursday, the nations' first-ever meeting and Eswatini's first match on European soil. Gift 'Jobe' Gamedze was sent off in the 29th minute for a foul on Keyon Ewurum, but captain Philani 'Mkasabuli' Mkhontfo struck in first-half stoppage time to give the visitors a 1-0 half-time lead.

Malta coach Emilio De Leo had rested several regulars, but substitute Basil Tuma headed in from Joseph Mbong's delivery two minutes after the restart to level the score. Eswatini then held on for the remaining 43 minutes, despite only four places separating the sides in the FIFA rankings before the tie.

Malta were crowned UEFA Nations League Group D1 winners on Friday night after Andorra beat Gibraltar 2-0 in Encamp, leaving them mathematically out of reach at the top with a match to spare. It was the first time Malta have topped their group in the competition.

Why it mattersEswatini's first goal in Europe and the 1-1 draw give the national team a concrete result to build on, with Malta's next Nations League fixture due on Monday.

Agriculture · 2026-11-15

Enactus Luyengo team receives E50 000 for World Cup trip

The University of Eswatini team will present two agricultural innovations at the Enactus World Cup in São Paulo, Brazil, from November 16 to 19.

Montigny Investments has given E50 000 to the University of Eswatini (UNESWA) Luyengo Campus Enactus team to support its journey to the 2026 Enactus World Cup in São Paulo, Brazil. Five students will travel to the competition, where they will represent Eswatini against teams from 35 countries.

The sponsorship was handed over at Coffee Culture in Mhlabanyatsi by Montigny Investments Chief Executive Officer Andrew Le Roux, who urged the students to represent Eswatini with pride. The team won the 2026 Enactus Eswatini National Champion title at the national finals at The George Hotel in Manzini on August 21, its third consecutive national championship.

The team will present Nutri-Plume, an organic fertiliser made by recycling discarded broiler chicken feathers into a nitrogen-rich input, and Green Guard, an organic pesticide offering an alternative to chemical crop protection. Le Roux said Eswatini's poultry industry made Nutri-Plume a candidate for a business with prospects beyond the country's borders.

Why it mattersFive students and their two farm-input innovations will compete in Brazil from November 16 to 19, with the E50 000 covering their travel.
