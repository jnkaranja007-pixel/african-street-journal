South Africa backs Eswatini president's role in ASA overhaul

Eswatini Athletics president Zakhele Dlamini appointed independent observer for Athletics South Africa's governance reforms.

South Africa's Sport, Arts and Culture Minister Gayton McKenzie has fully endorsed a roadmap to reform Athletics South Africa (ASA), appointing Eswatini's Athletics Eswatini President Zakhele Dlamini as an independent observer. Minister McKenzie confirmed his support in a letter dated October 19, 2026, to World Athletics CEO Jon Ridgeon, welcoming the joint correspondence that appointed Dlamini. Restoring sound governance at ASA is a priority for the South African government, McKenzie stated, noting that ASA's issues are structural, not personal.

McKenzie pledged full cooperation with Dlamini, including making senior officials available, and expects ASA's progress to be reflected in its reporting to the department. The minister also supports the findings of ASA's independent disciplinary panel, which found ASA President James Moloi guilty of misusing funds and recommended his removal. The department will facilitate engagement with provincial sports authorities and work with the South African Sports Confederation and Olympic Committee to ensure system-wide support.

The minister's overriding concern is for South African athletes, who should not suffer from administrative failures. He expressed a clear preference for reform over sanctions and pledged to help ASA meet its obligations within a six-month period. Dlamini's mandate as independent observer will continue until a new ASA constitution is ratified and fresh elections are held under the revised framework.

Why it mattersEswatini's Athletics Eswatini President Zakhele Dlamini has been appointed as an independent observer to oversee governance reforms within Athletics South Africa.

Eswatini men netball team heads to South Africa for tournament

A 15-player squad will enter a full-time training camp on November 2 for the Males Super Power Week tournament in Johannesburg.

Eswatini's men's netball team will begin intensive preparations for the Males Super Power Week tournament in Johannesburg, South Africa. Netball Eswatini (NESWA) confirmed a full-time training camp starting November 2, ahead of the November 11-15 event at the Wembley Indoor Arena. Head Coach Jabulani Vilane has selected a 15-player squad aiming to build on the team's recent international successes.

The team seeks to improve on their silver medal finish at the Africa Netball Cup in Nairobi, where they lost 49-45 to South Africa in the final. This tournament features 11 teams, including regional rivals and South African national and departmental sides. Coach Vilane is supported by assistants Vimbumkhosi Ndlangamandla and Wandile Sithebe, with Dr Bongani Magagula as medical practitioner and Nhlanhla Ngcamphalala as team manager.

Eswatini's squad includes players like Brandon Dlamini, Thabo Maphosa, and Aidan Van Reenen. The team aims to challenge regional rivals, drawing on experience from previous encounters, including a group-stage loss to South Africa in Nairobi and a friendly defeat earlier in the month.

Why it mattersThe Eswatini men's netball team is preparing to compete in an international tournament in South Africa.

King urges action on late business payments

Eswatini's King Mswati III launched a new SEDCO reporting line for entrepreneurs facing overdue government and corporate bills.

King Mswati III stated that late invoice payments are severely impacting small businesses and jobs in Eswatini. He announced that entrepreneurs experiencing overdue payments from government, public enterprises, and corporations can now report these issues to the Small Enterprises Development Company (SEDCO). The King highlighted that unpaid invoices prevent businesses from paying workers and can force viable ventures to close.

Speaking at the 20th Entrepreneur of the Year Awards in Ezulwini, His Majesty emphasized the need for practical solutions to support entrepreneurs. He noted the Citizen Economic Empowerment Act and the National MSME Policy 2024–2029 provide a framework for improving market access, financing, and digital readiness. The King also encouraged large companies to integrate indigenous businesses into their supply chains.

The King challenged FNB Eswatini and other partners to increase their support for the Entrepreneur of the Year Awards, aiming for larger prizes in future editions. Businesses recognized through the awards have achieved a combined annual turnover exceeding E167 million. He urged entrepreneurs who did not win awards to seek support from SEDCO for business scaling and networking.

Why it mattersSmall businesses and their employees in Eswatini face closure and job losses due to late payments from government and corporations.

The George Hotel earns three-star rating

The Eswatini Tourism Authority awarded the Manzini hotel its official three-star accommodation plaque and certificate.

The George Hotel in Manzini has officially received its three-star accommodation grading from the Eswatini Tourism Authority (ETA). The hotel was presented with a plaque and certificate following a successful assessment under the ETA’s accommodation grading programme. This recognition highlights the hotel's commitment to maintaining industry standards and improving guest services.

General Manager Kumi Bright described the three-star recognition as an important milestone for The George Hotel. The ETA's programme aims to encourage tourism establishments to uphold recognised standards and enhance service quality. The official certificate provides guests with added confidence in the establishment's accommodation and dining offerings.

The George Hotel offers guests fine dining, a health spa, and a pool bar with cocktails. Visitors can choose the hotel for business stays, weekend escapes, spa experiences, or meals with friends. The hotel invites guests to experience its hospitality in Manzini.

Eswatini minister urges voters to register in home chiefdoms

Public Service Minister Mabulala Maseko warned that voting elsewhere could lead to poor representation and service delivery.

Public Service Minister Mabulala Maseko urged emaSwati to register to vote in their home chiefdoms for community development. Maseko, who is also the Maphalaleni Member of Parliament, stated yesterday in Mbabane that electing unknown leaders could undermine community and national development. He warned against registering and voting in constituencies where people do not belong, as this could result in representatives not understanding local needs.

The minister spoke at the Ministry of Public Service offices where nearly 50 staff registered for the 2028 National Elections. This exercise is part of a nationwide initiative by the Elections and Boundaries Commission (EBC) to strengthen the electoral system and encourage civic participation. Maseko commended the EBC for bringing registration services closer to public servants and reiterated the importance of voting in one's designated constituency.

Maseko demonstrated his message by registering at Maphalaleni, his home constituency, where he intends to continue voting. He stressed that participating truthfully ensures citizens elect leaders familiar with community challenges, which impacts development and essential service delivery. The minister said elections offer an opportunity for emaSwati to contribute to building their country by choosing capable leaders.

Why it mattersEmaSwati voters are directly affected by the minister's call to register in their home chiefdoms, as it influences who represents them and impacts service delivery.
