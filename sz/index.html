runners fuel illegal dontonto trade in mbabane market

non-verbal signals and discreet handovers are used to sell the home-brewed drink for E10 per half cup.

An investigation uncovered a network of runners and hidden selling points for illegal dontonto, a home-brewed alcoholic drink, at Mbabane Market. Half a cup of dontonto, also known as mankanjane, sells for E10. Runners stationed near market entrances direct customers to concealed sellers, using non-verbal signals to avoid detection by inspectors.

The trade is sustained by low-income workers who find commercial beer too expensive, costing E25 to E30 per bottle. Informal traders and consumers said the trade grew during COVID-19 lockdowns in 2020 and continued due to unemployment and rising living costs. Buyers seeking larger volumes of two litres or more are directed to brewing locations in the Skom area near Msunduza.

Sellers package the drink in small plastic sachets, similar to those for ice blocks, for discreet handovers. Customers include young men earning E20 or E30 for casual jobs and informal mechanics. Some workers use part of their daily E20 earnings to purchase the drink.

Why it mattersLow-income workers and casual labourers in Mbabane are buying cheaper, illegal alcoholic drinks because commercial options are unaffordable.

Eswatini U-15 boys advance in FIFA World Cup festival

The Mandla Sigudla-led team secured a 3-0 win against the Maldives to move into the Tier E cross-elimination bracket.

The Eswatini Under-15 boys' national team has advanced in the Tier E cross-elimination bracket at the FIFA Under-15 World Cup and Festival 2026 in Azerbaijan. The team defeated the Maldives 3-0 early on Wednesday. Samkelo Tsabedze scored in the third minute, Mnotfo Simelane doubled the lead four minutes later, and Junior Shabangu added a third goal five minutes before the end.

Eswatini finished fifth in Group M with three points after beating Samoa 3-0. The tournament features an eight-a-side format on smaller pitches with 20-minute halves. No teams are eliminated after the group stage; instead, nations enter classification tiers based on their group finish. Eswatini will compete for a global ranking between 129th and 160th.

The FIFA festival aims to provide young talent with international exposure and diverse playing styles. Eswatini advances on the winners' path in the Tier E bracket, guaranteeing them four to five matches in the second stage. Victorious teams play fellow winners, while losing sides meet in lower-tier placement ties.

Why it mattersEswatini's Under-15 boys' national football team is competing in an international tournament, advancing in a cross-elimination bracket.

bedridden woman appeals for house

The 95-year-old woman shares a single room with her family and needs a private space for care.

A 95-year-old bedridden woman in Eswatini is appealing for help to build a house.

Gogo Makomane Mawelela, paralysed after a car accident, currently shares a single room with her son, daughter-in-law, and two children. She depends on caregivers for daily needs and to treat painful skin sores from prolonged lying. The cramped conditions make private care difficult, prompting her appeal for a house of her own. The Nkwene Youth Charity Organisation sometimes provides food but lacks resources for her broader needs.

Mbuso Sibandze of the Nkwene Youth Charity Organisation stated that washing and caring for a sick grandmother in a small room with children is extremely difficult. He appealed to Good Samaritans for assistance, noting the organisation's limited capacity to meet Mawelela's needs.

Why it mattersA bedridden 95-year-old woman in Eswatini needs a house for private and dignified care.

Eswatini government arrears fall to E2.7 billion

The African Development Bank reports a reduction in outstanding government payments due to reforms.

Eswatini government arrears have fallen to E2.7 billion, representing 3.2 per cent of GDP, down from 5.2 per cent in the 2022/23 financial year. The African Development Bank (AfDB) noted this progress in its September 7, 2026, report, attributing the decline to ongoing reconciliation and settlement efforts. Despite this improvement, the outstanding payments remain above the programme's target of two per cent of GDP.

The AfDB's Enhancing Economic Resilience and Competitiveness Programme (Phase I) has been rated highly satisfactory for implementation progress. Reforms aimed at strengthening fiscal discipline and improving public financial management, including the implementation of the Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS), have contributed to the reduction. Cabinet also approved the write-off of commitments that do not qualify as arrears.

Eswatini is undertaking these reforms with support from an AfDB loan of US$47.5 million, approved in June 2025. The report highlights improvements in public financial management, such as the establishment of the Revenue Stabilisation Fund in 2023 to manage SACU revenue fluctuations. This fund has helped cushion the economy from the recent global energy crisis.

Why it mattersBusinesses supplying goods and services to the Eswatini government face reduced pressure from falling arrears.

Business · 2026-10-28

EswatiniBank trains agents on ShareSha digital wallet

The bank's digital wallet allows users to send money to any mobile number, even if the recipient is not registered.

EswatiniBank trained agents and residents in Shiselweni on its ShareSha digital wallet on October 27, 2026. The digital wallet allows users to send money to any mobile number, even if the recipient is not registered on the platform. This training took place at Bethesda Church in Nhlangano.

Senior Manager Channels Product Simanga Motsa told agents they were now colleagues of the bank and should work together to ensure customers received assistance. He urged agents to prioritise customer service, stating their relationship with customers was important to the bank’s operations and their own businesses. Eswatini Bank is the only institution offering a digital wallet that can operate independently of a traditional bank account.

Bank representative Thembisa Maseko demonstrated how the public can register for ShareSha using a mobile phone and a national identity document via the Nomakuphi mobile application, USSD code *656#, or a bank branch. Customers can also buy airtime, data bundles, and pay electricity and water bills using the wallet.

Why it mattersEswatini residents can now send money to any mobile number using EswatiniBank's ShareSha digital wallet, improving access to financial services.
