Ntshangase scores first Eswatini goal

The former Kaizer Chiefs midfielder's lone strike secured Amawele a 1-0 victory in the MTN Premier League.

Siphelele Ntshangase scored his first goal since arriving in Eswatini today, netting the only goal in a match against Madlenya at Killarney Sports Ground. The former Kaizer Chiefs player's strike secured a 1-0 win for his club, Amawele, in the fifth week of the MTN Premier League season.

This victory places Amawele, owned by South African businessman Chief Ally Kgomongwe, on nine points with their third win of the campaign. Ntshangase's goal marks his return to professional football after a three-year break, having joined Amawele from SAFA Limpopo ABC Motsepe League side Mohlabile United.

Amawele's win follows a 1-0 victory over Pigg’s Peak Professionals last week. The league's top goalscorer this season is guaranteed a revised E15 000 prize.

Eswatini football team faces E1 million travel bill for Malta match

Sihlangu Semnikati will make history on November 12, 2026, playing their first international friendly against a European nation.

Eswatini's senior men's national football team, Sihlangu Semnikati, faces an estimated travel bill exceeding E1 million for their upcoming friendly match against Malta. The team is scheduled to play the European side on Thursday, November 12, 2026, at the Ta’ Qali National Stadium. Round-trip flights for a delegation of 30 individuals are projected to cost over E1 million.

Eswatini Football Association (EFA) Marketing and Communications Officer Muzi ‘Rhoo’ Radebe confirmed a signed agreement between the associations but declined to share specific details, stating that logistics will be guided by the contract. Typically, the visiting national association covers its own international airfare and transit costs, while the host nation provides accommodation, meals, and ground transport.

This fixture marks the first time Eswatini will play an international friendly match against a European nation. The match is crucial preparation for Malta, currently ranked 160th in FIFA, ahead of their UEFA Nations League clash against Gibraltar on November 16. Malta leads their group with seven points from three matches and is on a four-match unbeaten streak.

Why it mattersThe national football team's travel expenses will cost over E1 million.

Melisizwe Zwane named best conductor

His Majesty’s Correctional Services Choir conductor Melisizwe Zwane received E5 000 from Eswatini Med for his award.

Melisizwe Zwane, conductor for His Majesty’s Correctional Services Choir, was awarded E5 000 by Eswatini Med as the Best Conductor at the 2026 Ntjilo-Ntjilo Choral Competition. The prize was presented on Friday, October 23, at the Manzini Arts and Culture offices by Eswatini Med’s Principal Officer, Sicelo Mkhonta.

Zwane expressed gratitude, calling the award a reflection of the choir's collective effort and his leadership. The award came as a surprise, as his name was not announced during the main competition held late last month. Eswatini Med commended local conductors for raising artistic standards and cultivating homegrown talent.

The Ntjilo-Ntjilo competition, founded by Mathokoza Sibiya, received over E500 000 in total sponsorship from corporate sponsors, including Tibiyo Insurance group. Stanley Dlamini, CEO of the Eswatini National Council of Arts and Culture, congratulated the choirs representing Eswatini in regional eliminations this weekend.

Why it mattersMelisizwe Zwane and other local choral groups are preparing for upcoming trips to South Africa for regional competitions.

Eswatini minister highlights vast CEO to worker pay gaps

Minister Manqoba Khumalo stated some CEOs earn E280,000 while lowest paid workers take home E3,500.

Minister for Commerce, Industry and Trade Manqoba Khumalo explained that Eswatini's economic growth does not benefit ordinary workers due to vast salary disparities in some companies. He noted the absence of formal salary structures means the gap between chief executive officers and the lowest-paid employees is determined solely by management. Khumalo cited an example where a CEO earns E280,000 while a worker receives E3,500, meaning the boss earns 80 times more.

Speaking at a breakfast meeting in Ezulwini, Khumalo stated that in companies with such gaps, economic growth benefits only high earners, leaving those at the bottom unaffected. The World Bank projects Eswatini's economy to grow by five percent, its strongest since 2019, yet over half of emaSwati live below the poverty line. Khumalo previously stated in August that income inequality remains a significant challenge, with growth benefits not always distributed equally.

Khumalo pointed out that pay gaps have been closed in developed countries. He previously served as Coca-Cola's general manager in Ireland before becoming a senator and minister in Eswatini. Economists are observing Eswatini's growth but questioning its distribution. The minister stressed that continued attention is needed to achieve more inclusive economic growth.

Why it mattersOrdinary workers in Eswatini are not feeling the benefits of economic growth due to large salary gaps between CEOs and the lowest-paid employees.

dagga trade fuels sex work in Madlangampisi

Residents report increased visibility of sex workers, some from South Africa, drawn by money from illegal cannabis sales.

Madlangampisi residents are concerned about a rise in sex work in their town, which they link to money from the illegal dagga trade. Sex workers, some reportedly from South Africa, have become more visible, particularly during the dagga harvesting season when traders are believed to have more cash.

Alex Tsabedze, the Indvuna Yenkhundla for Madlangampisi, confirmed the presence of sex workers, noting a change in the town's social environment and increased visibility of young women after dark. Residents also expressed worry about young girls allegedly forming relationships with dagga farmers and older men attracted by the trade's profits.

Police have been targeting illegal dagga cultivation and trafficking around Madlangampisi, destroying hectares of plants and arresting individuals for smuggling. Some seized drug consignments have been valued at millions of Emalangeni, reinforcing the perception of a significant illegal industry.

Why it mattersThe illegal dagga trade in Madlangampisi is reportedly changing the town's social environment, leading to an increase in sex work.
