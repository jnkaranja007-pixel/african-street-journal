FNB Eswatini offers repayment breaks to new clients

The bank introduced a campaign offering qualifying customers three-month repayment holidays on home loans and vehicle finance.

FNB Eswatini launched a new campaign on November 8, 2026, offering qualifying customers repayment holidays, cash-back incentives, and funeral cover to attract new clients. The 'Switch to FNB' campaign aims to draw emaSwati to transfer their banking and salary accounts by providing financial relief on home loans, vehicle finance, and personal loans.

Customers who switch their home loans to FNB may receive a three-month repayment holiday and have qualifying bond costs covered. Those transferring vehicle finance to WesBank, FNB’s division, can also get a three-month instalment break. Additionally, customers opening accounts and transferring salaries will receive cash back equivalent to their first three months’ account fees.

FNB Eswatini Executive Head of Retail Dr Mncedzi Ngomane stated the campaign addresses financial pressures and rising living costs faced by customers. Qualifying customers transferring salaries will also receive E5 000 funeral cover for the first three months with waived premiums.

Why it mattersEmaSwati customers facing rising living costs can receive immediate financial relief through repayment breaks and cash back incentives.

Eswatini food prices steady amid rising production costs

The Eswatini Competition Commission reports modest food price movements in October, but warns of future affordability risks.

Food prices in Eswatini remained relatively stable in October, with most essential commodities showing only small price changes. The Eswatini Competition Commission's Monthly Price Tracker noted increases in rice, sugar, maize meal, cooking oil, and milk, with a 10kg bag of rice rising four percent to E117.13.

However, the commission warns that rising agricultural production costs and regional supply issues could impact future food affordability. Factors like high fertiliser prices in East and Southern Africa, increased fuel costs, and potential adverse weather are cited as risks that could raise expenses for Eswatini, which imports fertiliser from South Africa.

These production cost pressures could lead to reduced agricultural output, threaten food security, and ultimately push consumer food prices higher. While some items like cabbage and spinach saw price drops in October, the overall outlook for sustained food affordability is uncertain due to these emerging cost pressures.

Why it mattersEswatini consumers face potential future increases in food prices due to rising agricultural production costs and supply chain constraints.

Matsapha opens Eswatini’s first silent eco park

The 15-hectare sanctuary combines recreation with conservation and includes an inclusive outdoor gym.

Matsapha has opened Eswatini’s first Silent Eco Park, a 15-hectare urban sanctuary combining recreation with conservation. The park offers scenic paths, jogging routes, an outdoor gym for all abilities, a plant nursery, and a wetland area supporting wildlife.

Minister for Housing and Urban Development Appolo Maphalala, speaking through Principal Secretary Dr Simon Zwane, described the park as a practical outcome of the Eswatini Urban Forum. He stated that urban resilience requires recognising and protecting natural systems, not just infrastructure development.

The park supports biodiversity through the protection of wetlands, indigenous trees, and diverse plant species, providing habitats for birds and other wildlife. Additional facilities, including walking and cycling trails, are expected as development progresses, supported by CONCO, YKK, AG Thomas, and MBF.

Why it mattersEswatini residents now have a new place to unwind, exercise, and reconnect with nature.

MP urges Eswatini to tap E136bn local funds

Hhukwini MP Alec Lushaba proposed using local pension and insurance funds instead of foreign loans.

Hhukwini Member of Parliament Alec Lushaba urged the Eswatini government to stop borrowing from international institutions and instead use E136 billion held by local non-bank financial institutions. Lushaba questioned why the government sought external loans when substantial funds belonging to emaSwati are held by local pension funds and insurance companies. He suggested the Ministry of Finance engage the Financial Services Regulatory Authority (FSRA) to explore borrowing arrangements.

Lushaba posed the question to Finance Minister Neal Rijkenberg during a presentation of three loan Bills totalling over E3 billion for infrastructure projects. The MP specifically mentioned pension entities like MOPADO as potential lenders. He argued that using domestic funding could benefit the government and provide investment opportunities for local institutions.

Minister Rijkenberg acknowledged the suggestion's merit but cautioned that the E136 billion is not readily available for borrowing. He explained that a significant portion consists of insurance funds, which require geographical diversification to protect policyholders from local catastrophes. This diversification poses challenges for imposing local investment requirements on these funds.

Why it mattersEmaSwati's money held in local pension and insurance funds is being considered for government borrowing, potentially affecting investment opportunities and government debt.
