Eswatini seeks E3.79 billion in new loans

Government plans to borrow up to E1.6 billion from Standard Bank for infrastructure and social projects.

The Eswatini government is seeking E3.79 billion in new loans, with E1.6 billion earmarked from Standard Bank of South Africa. Minister for Finance Neal Rijkenberg tabled three loan Bills in the House of Assembly on Thursday to secure the funds. The proposed borrowing from Standard Bank is intended to finance a portfolio of infrastructure and social development projects.

The loan from Standard Bank, which will act as the sole mandated lead arranger, will carry a margin of 4.95 per cent per annum over the ZARONIA reference rate. Government will also pay an arranging fee of 1.30 per cent, potentially E20.8 million based on the maximum facility. An annual management fee of E380,000, exclusive of VAT, will also be charged, alongside costs capped at E1.2 million for negotiating finance documents.

As of September 29, 2026, Eswatini's total public debt stood at E43.04 billion, representing 41.63 per cent of GDP. This debt comprises E22.82 billion domestic and E20.22 billion external debt. The new loan aims to fund agreed-upon infrastructure and social expenditure.

Why it mattersThe Eswatini government is seeking new loans to fund infrastructure and social development projects, increasing the nation's public debt.

High Court allows widow to lead church

Sibongile Dlamini will temporarily remain leader of Faith Christian Fellowship Church after a court ruling.

The High Court has temporarily allowed Sibongile Dlamini, widow of the late Apostle Jeremiah Dlamini, to continue leading the Faith Christian Fellowship Church. This decision follows an urgent application challenging earlier magistrate court rulings concerning the church's leadership. Judge Mzwandile Fakudze granted an interim order reviewing and setting aside the magistrate’s decisions from September 29 and October 27.

The applicants, the church, Sibongile Dlamini, and Senior Pastor Muzi Myeni, sought to have Sibongile remain the overall leader and overseer. This is in line with a resolution allegedly made by the church’s pastors on September 12. The court also ordered Magistrate Thamsanqa Ndlovu to transmit the record of proceedings from Manzini Magistrates Court Case 347/2026.

The dispute arose after Apostle Jeremiah Dlamini's death on July 10, 2026. The church, founded around 1983, has approximately 14 branches in Eswatini and South Africa. A meeting on September 12 reportedly resolved that Sibongile, known as Mum-Apostle, would assume leadership, a decision later challenged by congregants Nsizwa Mhlanga and Absalom Dlamini.

Why it mattersSibongile Dlamini, widow of the late church founder, will continue to lead the Faith Christian Fellowship Church while a court reviews leadership decisions.

Eswatini forum to rethink urban finance

Municipalities will explore bonds and public-private partnerships to fund development beyond property rates.

Eswatini's urban development faces financial and infrastructural challenges as towns grow. The 2026 Eswatini Urban Forum aims to find new ways for municipalities to finance their expansion, moving beyond traditional revenue sources and government aid. The forum will gather financiers, development partners, and government officials to discuss smart solutions, resilient infrastructure, and housing.

Housing and Urban Development Minister Appolo Maphalala stated the forum will focus on improving service delivery by finding additional financing for local authorities, rather than relying solely on property rates. He suggested that urban local authorities could issue municipal bonds, similar to the national government, to raise development funds. Public-private partnerships, such as the Build, Operate and Transfer model, were also highlighted as ways to leverage assets like vacant land for development.

The forum, first held in 2024, seeks practical solutions to urban challenges. Diversifying municipal financing requires exploring instruments beyond property rates to attract private investment and meet the growing demands of urban populations for roads, water, sanitation, and housing.

Why it mattersEswatini's urban local authorities are seeking new ways to finance development and improve services due to limitations in their current revenue bases.

Dominic Kunene to coach Sihlangu again

The Eswatini Football Association appointed Kunene as interim head coach for a friendly against Malta.

Dominic Kunene will lead Sihlangu as interim head coach for the November international friendly against Malta, the Eswatini Football Association (EFA) confirmed yesterday. The EFA is finalising compensation for a substantive head coach who is expected to join the team during the match week. Kunene previously managed Sihlangu from 2020 to 2024, overseeing 42 matches with 12 wins, 13 draws, and 17 defeats.

The interim technical team, including assistant coach Caleb Ngwenya, fitness coach Siza Dlamini, and goalkeeper coach Thokozani Mamba, will prepare the squad for the trip to Malta. This arrangement is necessary due to the upcoming friendly and logistical needs. The substantive coach will later select his preferred technical bench. Kunene's notable achievements include reaching the 2021 COSAFA Cup semi-finals and guiding the team to two victories over Somalia in the 2023 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers.

The Sihlangu squad for the Malta match will be announced on Tuesday, November 3. The international friendly is scheduled for November 12 at the Ta’ Qali National Stadium, kicking off at 6:30pm. This will be Eswatini's first international friendly against a European nation. Reports had suggested a Moroccan coach might be considered for the permanent role under a football cooperation agreement.

Why it mattersEswatini's national football team, Sihlangu, will play an international friendly against Malta on November 12.

Man sentenced for four murders

Vusi Mncina received multiple sentences for murder and attempted murder in Ezulwini.

Former deputy sheriff Vusi Mncina was sentenced on Wednesday by Judge Titus Mlangeni for the murders of four women and the attempted murder of another in Ezulwini. Mncina received 14 years for the attempted murder of Thuli Mkhwanazi. He was sentenced to 29 years each for the murders of Nonhlanhla Thwala, Lungile Shongwe, and Fikile Shongwe. An additional 23 years were given for the murder of his girlfriend, Cynthia Khulile Shongwe.

Lawrence Mavuso, Mncina's stepfather, stated that even 300 years would not have shocked him regarding the sentence. Mavuso, who lives in Maduma, said he had anticipated a lengthy prison term since the shootings on October 28, 2023. He has visited his stepson in prison but has not received a clear explanation for the crimes, only claims of being influenced by demons.

The case has drawn national attention due to the severity of the crimes and the lengthy sentence. Judge Mlangeni described Mncina as a dangerous person unable to control his anger. Mavuso, who raised Mncina, described their relationship as close and met with his stepson after the sentencing. He noted that Mncina had never shown violent behaviour before.

Why it mattersFour women and one woman were killed or injured in Ezulwini, affecting their families.
