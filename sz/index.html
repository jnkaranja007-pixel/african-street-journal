Fans Trek 3 500km Overland to Support Eswatini Football Club

Green Mamba supporters face a more than two-day journey across three countries to reach Angola for a CAF Confederation Cup match.

Green Mamba supporters are preparing for an extensive overland journey of over 3 500km to Angola for the second leg of their CAF Confederation Cup preliminary tie. The team will first host Clube Desportivo Primeiro de Agosto at Somhlolo National Stadium on September 4, 2026, before the challenging away fixture on September 12, 2026.

The club has invited interested fans to register for the road trip, which costs US$200 (E3 200). This journey from Mbabane to Luanda is expected to take more than two days, traversing through Botswana and Namibia via the Trans-Kalahari route. The route involves crossing borders at Oshoek/Ngwenya, Pioneer Gate/Lobatse, and Mamuno/Trans-Kalahari, before entering Angola via Oshikango/Santa Clara or Ruacana.

Supporters will then travel north through Namibia, passing through Windhoek and Oshakati, before crossing into southern Angola. From the Angolan border, the journey continues north through Lubango and Huambo to reach the final destination, Luanda. An alternative route through Zimbabwe and Zambia is considered less viable due to road conditions and safety concerns.

Why it mattersFans undertaking this significant overland journey are directly affected by the travel costs and logistics involved in supporting their team abroad.

Eswatini Under-25 darts duo advances to regional final

Sandziso Nhleko and Mbali Sihlongonyane secured a spot in the final with a 3-0 victory over Zambia in the AUSC Region V games.

Eswatini's Under-25 darts duo, Sandziso Nhleko and Mbali Sihlongonyane, are set to compete for gold after a decisive 3-0 win against Zambia in the semi-finals of the AUSC Region V games. The tournament is being held at the Aquamarine Tide Hotel in Gaborone. While the final match schedule is yet to be confirmed, this advancement marks a significant achievement for the youth national team.

The competition, which officially began with an opening ceremony on Tuesday, includes participants from Eswatini, Zimbabwe, Botswana, South Africa, and Namibia. Eswatini's senior men's team experienced mixed results on August 26, defeating Namibia 23-13 after an earlier loss to Botswana. However, they later faced a substantial 30-6 defeat against South Africa on August 27, an improvement from their 31-1 loss to the same opponent in 2024.

Other Eswatini teams also competed, with the Under-25s losing to Botswana (5-11) and Namibia (7-9), and the ladies' team narrowly losing to Botswana (8-9) and Zimbabwe (6-10), but securing a win against Namibia (10-6). The senior men's team is scheduled to play Zimbabwe to conclude their group stage matches.

Why it mattersEswatini's youth darts players are competing for regional gold, potentially bringing national pride and recognition.

Eswatini Fashion Showcases Global Market Potential

The IP Runway event highlighted Eswatini's creative economy, bridging artistic talent with intellectual property protection to prepare designers for international markets.

Eswatini models displayed a fusion of tradition and modern design at The Royal Villas during the IP Runway: The Business of Fashion, Furniture and Arts event. Hosted by the Eswatini Intellectual Property Office, the showcase aimed to demonstrate the commercial viability of legally protected designs and prepare local talent for global markets.

The event featured distinct categories including Roots and Heritage, Contemporary and Streetwear, Formal and Evening Wear, NextGen, Upcycle and Innovation, and Fashion Complementaries. It served as a platform for local design houses like Kotini, Guzzani, Designs by Yanda, and Goodness Fashion, underscoring the kingdom's ready fashion ecosystem for investment and licensing.

Her Royal Highness Princess Sakhizwe emphasized the importance of domestic support for Eswatini brands, encouraging citizens to champion their own design heritage. The event underscored the need for structural investment and international scale to elevate Eswatini's fashion industry on the world stage.

Why it mattersThis event is important for Eswatini designers and businesses as it prepares them for international markets and highlights the need for structural investment and cross-border licensing.

Central Bank Introduces E10 Coin for Independence Anniversary

Eswatini's Central Bank is launching a limited-edition E10 coin in 2028 to commemorate the nation's 60th independence anniversary and test public acceptance of higher.

The Central Bank of Eswatini (CBE) announced plans to introduce a limited-edition E10 circulating commemorative coin in 2028, coinciding with the nation's 60th independence celebrations on September 6, 2028. CBE Governor Dr. Phil Mnisi launched a design competition yesterday, inviting artists and the public to submit concepts that reflect six decades of independence. The competition, open for eight weeks until October 26, 2026, offers both physical and online submission channels to encourage broad participation.

Dr. Mnisi stated the coin will serve as a lasting symbol of national pride and allow public contribution to a generational keepsake. This initiative aligns with the CBE's mandate to issue currency and is part of its approved operational projects for the 2026/27 financial year. Beyond marking the anniversary, the project aims to gauge public reception to higher denomination coins within Eswatini's currency system.

The E10 denomination was chosen due to the high usage and frequent wear and tear of the E10 banknote, which requires regular replacement. Coins typically last 20 to 50 years, significantly longer than banknotes which last one to five years. Transitioning to an E10 coin could enhance currency system efficiency by reducing replacement frequency due to deterioration, thereby maintaining the quality and reliability of currency for the public.

Why it mattersThe introduction of a new E10 coin affects investors by potentially improving currency system efficiency and offering a new denomination for circulation, while farmers and the diaspora have limited direct impact.

ANOCA Zone VI SGs Experience Eswatini Culture and Sport

Delegates attending the Association of National Olympic Committees of Africa (ANOCA) Zone VI Secretary Generals Seminar will engage in cultural festivities and sporting events.

Delegates arriving in Eswatini for the ANOCA Zone VI Secretary Generals Seminar will experience the kingdom's annual Reed Dance Ceremony and other cultural attractions. The seminar, running from August 28 to August 30, coincides with the historic Reed Dance, where maidens showcase their chastity before King Mswati III and the Queen Mother. All ten member nations within the Southern African zone have confirmed their participation in this inaugural meeting.

Chief Executive Officer Maxwell Jele confirmed that representatives from National Olympic Committees will arrive on Friday and extend their stays to partake in the festivities. The itinerary includes a visit to the main day of the Reed Dance Ceremony on Monday, a tour of the Mantenga Cultural Village, and a visit to Malandelas in Malkerns. The delegates will also witness the Swazi Rally motorcycling spectacle on Saturday.

Beyond the cultural and sporting excursions, the delegates will address sports development issues, focusing on implementation strategies for each National Olympic Committee and their integration for regional benefit. A key discussion point will be outlining strategic pathways toward the Los Angeles 2028 Olympic Games, encouraging collaboration among Southern African nations to achieve global sporting excellence.

Why it mattersInvestors may benefit from enhanced regional cooperation in sports development and potential infrastructure improvements discussed during the ANOCA Zone VI meeting.
