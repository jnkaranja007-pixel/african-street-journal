Eswatini farmers urged to sell livestock as drought kills over 50 cattle

Shiselweni Region faces severe water and pasture shortages, impacting livestock health and productivity.

Farmers in Eswatini's Shiselweni Region are being urged to sell their livestock as over 50 cattle have died due to drought conditions. The figure was revealed at an emergency stakeholder meeting in Nhlangano yesterday. Severe shortages of grazing pasture and drinking water have led to poor cattle body conditions, increased drought-related abortions, and reduced productivity.

Shiselweni Regional Veterinary Officer Dr. Allen Simbai Maruve stated that Hluti reported 10 to 20 cattle deaths in the worst-affected communities, with Lavumisa experiencing more than 20 deaths. Dip tanks in Hluti, such as Tsekwane and Qomintaba, face critical stress, while Lavumisa faces acute livestock water shortages impacting multiple dip tanks. Common challenges include pasture depletion, drying water sources, and increased disease susceptibility.

Dr. Maruve called for immediate emergency assistance over the next three months, including feed support like hay bales and mineral licks, and water support such as tankers and rehabilitation of water points. Medium-term priorities include water infrastructure development, borehole drilling, and solar-powered pumping systems.

Sport · 2026-11-05

Eswatini coach attends ICC Level 2 course in Kenya

Mancoba Jele aims to train local cricket instructors after completing a four-day programme in Nairobi.

Eswatini Cricket Association (ECA) sent former national player Mancoba Jele to Nairobi, Kenya, for an ICC Level 2 Coaching Course from November 5-8, 2026. Jele, who holds an ICC Level 1 Coaching Certificate, will gain a qualification that brings him closer to training other local instructors. He currently coaches Eswatini's Under-19 and Senior Women's teams.

The four-day training programme in Nairobi is facilitated by ICC Master Educators Christopher Cardoso and Jolene Campher. Jele's participation is seen by the ECA as a crucial step towards establishing a sustainable coaches’ education pathway. The Eswatini Sport and Recreation Council (ESRC) provided financial support for Jele's travel and participation.

The ECA stated that Jele's new qualification will help elevate the standard of cricket across Eswatini. The association praised the ESRC for its commitment to sports development in the country. Jele previously represented Eswatini on the pitch for six years before moving into coaching roles.

Why it mattersMancoba Jele, a coach for Eswatini's national youth and women's cricket teams, is attending a course to improve local coaching standards.

Eswatini court hears explosive found in South Africa

Assistant Superintendent Bhekani Shiba testified that a device was collected from Bonginkhosi IB Dlamini’s home in South Africa.

An explosive device allegedly found with businessman Zweli Dlamini was collected from Bonginkhosi IB Dlamini’s home in South Africa, Assistant Superintendent Bhekani Shiba testified yesterday. Shiba stated that Zweli, a member of PUDEMO, communicated with activist Samora Ginindza before collecting the device. Zweli faces charges under the Suppression of Terrorism Act and Explosives Act before Judge Sabelo Masuku.

Shiba, Head of the Counter-Terrorism and Organised Crime Unit, told the court that police investigations established Zweli travelled to South Africa on December 19, 2025, and returned the next day. Zweli allegedly explained to police that he was instructed to transport the explosive device from South Africa into Eswatini. Two names, Samora Ginindza and Bonginkhosi IB Dlamini, emerged during the accused’s interview as comrades with knowledge of the items.

The investigation into Zweli Dlamini began on December 22, 2025, after Superintendent Derrick Makhanya informed Shiba of the arrest. Intelligence suggested the explosives were intended to disrupt the Incwala Ceremony. Shiba has nearly 20 years of experience in terrorism investigations and has handled over 500 such cases.

Why it mattersBusinessman Zweli Dlamini is accused of plotting to destabilise the Incwala Ceremony using an explosive device allegedly sourced from South Africa.

Sport · 2026-11-05

Eswatini football squad announcement delayed

The Eswatini Football Association has failed to name the Sihlangu Semnikati team for a friendly against Malta.

The Eswatini Football Association (EFA) has not yet announced the Sihlangu Semnikati squad for an upcoming international friendly against Malta, despite promising to do so by Tuesday. The team was scheduled to depart for the match against the European nation, set for November 12, 2026, in Ta’ Qali. This fixture marks the first time Eswatini will play a friendly against a European country.

EFA Marketing and Communications Manager Muzi ‘Rhoo’ Radebe stated he was awaiting the executive committee's decision on the squad. He admitted on Tuesday that he had received no update and remained unaware of any new timeline for the announcement. The EFA had previously confirmed concluding engagements with the Malta Football Association (MFA) for the match.

Malta, ranked 160th by FIFA, is using the friendly as preparation for their UEFA Nations League match against Gibraltar on November 16, 2026. The EFA also recently appointed an interim technical bench led by Dominic Kunene for this FIFA international window, after failing for a year to name a permanent head coach.

Why it mattersEswatini football fans are affected by the delayed announcement of the national team squad for a historic international friendly.

Health · 2026-11-05

MP fears drought will leave patients hungry

Shiselweni I MP Shawnette Henwood questioned hospital preparedness for a potential El Niño drought and its impact on patient nutrition.

Shiselweni I MP Shawnette Henwood questioned whether hospitals can support patients facing food shortages during a possible drought. Henwood raised the concern during debate on the Ministry of Health’s second-quarter performance report, asking if the ministry had prepared for a possible El Niño-related drought and its effects on patients who depend on medication. She recalled that the ministry previously provided food to some patients and asked why the programme had ended, wanting to know what assistance would be available if drought conditions made it difficult for patients to obtain food.

Legislators also called for dialysis services at more hospitals to reduce patients’ journeys to Mbabane Government Hospital, citing non-operational dialysis machines at some public facilities. Sithobelweni MP Vincent Sihlongonyane questioned why antivenom appeared to be available at certain hospitals but not others, noting that high temperatures in his constituency increased concerns about snakebites. Nkomiyahlaba MP Mantunta Dlamini and LaMgabhi MP Sicelo Jele also called for wider availability of antivenom as temperatures rose.

Health Minister Mduduzi Matsebula responded to questions about dialysis, stating that machines would begin operating later in the month. Henwood also raised concerns about access to cancer treatment, pointing to a backlog of cases involving patients awaiting support from the Phalala Fund and calling for intervention. She also asked when bagcugcuteli would receive their proposed allowance increase.

Why it mattersEmaSwati patients face potential food shortages during drought and delayed medical treatment due to funding backlogs and limited services.
