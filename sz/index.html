Lower Maguduza Hydropower Project adds 13.5 megawatts to Eswatini's grid

The Lower Maguduza Hydropower Project is set to become fully operational in January, providing an additional 13.5 megawatts of locally generated electricity to Eswatini.

Eswatini will begin receiving an additional 13.5 megawatts of locally generated electricity in January when the Lower Maguduza Hydropower Project becomes fully operational. This development aims to reduce the country’s reliance on imported power and increase domestic generation capacity. The project, located along the Usuthu River near Sidvokodvo, features two generating units of 6.75MW each, contributing to Eswatini's goal of greater energy self-sufficiency by 2030.

The new hydropower station is expected to generate approximately 67 gigawatt-hours of electricity annually, increasing Eswatini’s local power generation by about 20 per cent. All electricity produced will be supplied to the national grid through the Eswatini Electricity Company. Inyatsi Construction, with Anthem, is constructing the project, which is part of a broader effort including solar, biomass, and other planned developments to boost domestic generation.

Minister for Natural Resources and Energy Prince Lonkhokhela highlighted the project's importance during a tour, stating that such initiatives are critical for strengthening domestic generation and improving the reliability of the national electricity system. He noted that reliable and affordable electricity is fundamental for economic development, industrialisation, job creation, and improving the quality of life for emaSwati, while acknowledging the high cost of electricity in the country.

Why it mattersEmaSwati will benefit from increased energy security and potentially more stable electricity supply, which the minister stated is fundamental to job creation and improving quality of life.

Namibia pledges support for Eswatini's national research council

Namibia committed to assisting Eswatini in establishing its National Research Council and strengthening its research, science, technology, and innovation system.

Namibia has pledged continued support to Eswatini as the kingdom works to operationalize its National Research Council (NRC) and enhance its national research, science, technology, and innovation system. Dr. Nhlanhla Lupahla, General Manager for Research, Science, Technology and Innovation Coordination and Support at Namibia’s National Commission on Research, Science and Technology, stated that Namibia is ready to share its experiences and lessons with Eswatini.

Lupahla emphasized that Namibia views the establishment of the council as a significant investment in Eswatini's capacity to use research, science, technology, and innovation to address national development priorities. He noted that the two countries share historical and regional ties, along with similar institutional environments, opportunities, and challenges in developing national research and innovation systems.

Namibia's support will also extend to the continental science and research community, with Namibia planning to host the Science Granting Councils Initiative (SGCI) All Partners Meeting in Windhoek from September 27 to October 3, 2026. During this meeting, Namibia intends to support Eswatini's admission into the SGCI network, which would strengthen Eswatini's research ecosystem and contribute to the collective voice of science granting councils across Africa.

Why it mattersEswatini's researchers and innovators will benefit from Namibia's experience in establishing and regulating a national research council, potentially leading to new opportunities.

Two emaSwati professionals named among Africa's infrastructure elite

Zizwe Vilane and Nosipho Simelane received nominations for the inaugural Powered By Africa Top 100 Most Influential Africans in Infrastructure.

Two emaSwati professionals, Zizwe Vilane and Nosipho Simelane, have been nominated among the Top 100 Most Influential Africans in Infrastructure. This recognition highlights their contributions to investment, energy, and development across the continent. The nominations are part of the first Powered By Africa Top 100 program, which celebrates individuals shaping Africa's infrastructure development through various fields.

Zizwe Vilane, an Economic Development Consultant and Director of Mpowered Eswatini, was recognized for his work in economic development, investment policy, and private-sector engagement. Nosipho Simelane, a Research and Renewables Engineer at the Eswatini Electricity Company (EEC), also received a nomination for her contributions to the energy sector.

The final Top 100 list will be announced in September 2026. Those selected will participate in a high-level international delegation to Accra, Ghana, from November 17 to 21, 2026, which includes the Infrastructure and Investment Conference and an invitation-only gala.

Why it mattersThis recognition highlights the expertise of emaSwati professionals in continental infrastructure development, potentially opening doors for further investment and collaboration for Eswatini.

CAF moves Nsingizini match to Harare National Sports Stadium

The Confederation of African Football approved a request to relocate Nsingizini Hotspurs' CAF Champions League preliminary round first leg from Francistown, Botswana, to Harare.

Nsingizini Hotspurs' CAF Champions League preliminary round first leg against Scottland FC will now take place at Harare’s National Sports Stadium on September 6, following a venue change approved by the Confederation of African Football. Zimbabwean champions Scottland FC confirmed the move on their official Facebook page, stating the match was relocated from Francistown, Botswana. The National Sports Stadium recently received CAF approval.

The venue change significantly alters logistical plans for the Eswatini team. Nsingizini Hotspurs had already arranged transport for supporters to Botswana, with fees ranging between E900 and 1 300, which will now require revision. The match date coincides with Eswatini’s Independence Day, adding another layer to the travel recalibration for the Eswatini outfit.

For Scottland FC, the fixture marks a celebrated homecoming to the 60 000-capacity National Sports Stadium, Zimbabwe's largest stadium. Scottland FC stated, “We now look forward to a grand re-opening of the venue and we bank on Zimbabweans’ support to make it a memorable occasion.” Nsingizini Hotspurs previously faced Zimbabwean opposition in Simba Bhora in Botswana last year.

Why it mattersEswatini football fans who planned to travel to Botswana for the match will now need to revise their travel arrangements and budgets due to the venue change to Harare.

Manzini festival concludes regional creative competitions

The final regional National Arts and Culture Awards festival in Manzini showcased local talent and marked the end of the regional circuit before national competitions begin.

Manzini hosted the last regional National Arts and Culture Awards (NACAs) festival at Millennium Park on Friday, concluding the regional phase of the competition. Creatives from various sectors, including poetry, drama, Ingadla, Gospel music, DJs, live bands, comedy, and theatre, presented their work to judges and audiences, aiming for regional recognition.

This event followed similar festivals in Lubombo, Shiselweni, and Hhohho, completing the regional circuit. Successful nominees from these events will now advance to the national stage to compete for national titles. The initiative also focuses on strengthening professional skills within the creative sector.

The Eswatini National Council of Arts and Culture Chief Executive Officer, Stanley Dlamini, stated that the National Music Development Programme would support this aspect by providing training in areas like sound engineering and music business. South African music industry expert Vusi Leeuw is expected to oversee intensive workshops for participating creatives after the national awards.

Why it mattersLocal creatives are affected by gaining opportunities for recognition, skill development, and career advancement within the arts and culture industry.
