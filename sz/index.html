Low Wages Force Textile Workers into Transactional Sex for E20

A new study reveals that some textile workers in Eswatini engage in transactional sex for as little as E20 due to insufficient wages to cover basic monthly expenses.

A study presented by the National Emergency Response Council on HIV and AIDS (NERCHA) at the Emafini Conference Centre found that low wages compel some textile workers to offer sex for E20. This finding emerged from the HIV Prevention Indaba study, which investigated factors influencing HIV prevention among various population groups across the country.

Textile workers interviewed for the study reported that their remuneration is too low to meet their needs, leading some to engage in transactional sex for money to buy necessities like bread or pay for electricity. One worker suggested government intervention to ensure textile firms pay employees better, highlighting the choice between dignity and survival when faced with immediate financial offers.

The study emphasizes that these economic pressures increase vulnerability to HIV, particularly among young women in the textile sector. It points to the need for interventions that address the economic circumstances driving these behaviors, rather than focusing solely on health messaging, to improve HIV prevention efforts in the country.

Why it mattersTextile workers are directly affected by low wages, which force some into risky behaviors to meet basic needs, impacting their financial stability and health.

Eswatini Government Plans E1.5 Billion Debt Amid Inequality Concerns

The Eswatini government intends to raise an additional E1.5 billion this financial year to fund capital projects, even as economic growth disproportionately benefits the wealthy.

Finance Minister Neal Rijkenberg announced the government's plan to secure E1.5 billion to address financial pressures and support capital projects. This comes as the country's total public debt decreased by E652.48 million between March and June, settling at E41.84 billion, or 40.47 per cent of GDP, by the end of June.

Despite an expected economic growth of 5.6 per cent in 2025, Minister for Economic Planning and Development Dr Thambo Gina acknowledged that this growth is not translating into improved living standards for ordinary citizens. He attributed this to high income and wealth inequality, where benefits are concentrated among a small segment of society.

Gina highlighted that major public and private investments, including renewable energy, the Mkhondvo-Ngwavuma Water Augmentation Project, new Central Bank facilities, and textile and agribusiness investments, are driving the growth. However, he stressed the need for more inclusive economic growth to ensure benefits reach a broader range of households, as many emaSwati remain below the poverty line.

Why it mattersThe Eswatini government's borrowing plans and the acknowledged issue of wealth inequality directly affect the financial stability and economic opportunities for all citizens.

Namibia pledges support for Eswatini's research council

Namibia will provide continued assistance to Eswatini as the kingdom works to establish its National Research Council and strengthen its national research, science, technology.

Namibia has committed to supporting Eswatini's efforts to operationalize its National Research Council (NRC) and enhance its research, science, technology, and innovation system. Dr. Nhlanhla Lupahla, General Manager for Research, Science, Technology and Innovation Coordination and Support at Namibia’s National Commission on Research, Science and Technology, stated that Namibia is prepared to share its experiences and lessons with Eswatini during a capacity strengthening workshop.

Lupahla emphasized that Namibia views the establishment of Eswatini's council as a significant investment in the kingdom's capacity to utilize research, science, technology, and innovation to address national development priorities. He noted that the two countries share historical and regional ties, along with similar institutional environments, opportunities, and challenges in developing national research and innovation systems.

Namibia's support will also extend to the continental science and research community, with Namibia hosting the Science Granting Councils Initiative (SGCI) All Partners Meeting in Windhoek from September 27 to October 3, 2026. During this meeting, Namibia plans to support Eswatini's admission into the SGCI network, which is expected to strengthen Eswatini's research ecosystem and contribute to the collective voice of science granting councils across Africa.

Why it mattersEswatini's researchers, scientists, and innovators will benefit from Namibia's experience and support in establishing a national research council and integrating into a continental network.

PLE schedules 8Bet Trade Fair Cup during CAF matches

The Premier League of Eswatini (PLE) has created a fixture conflict by scheduling its 8Bet Trade Fair Cup during the CAF Champions League and CAF Confederation Cup preliminary.

The Premier League of Eswatini (PLE) has scheduled the 8Bet Trade Fair Cup to coincide with the first preliminary rounds of the CAF Champions League and CAF Confederation Cup, according to the Times of Eswatini. This decision appears to contradict CAF Inter-club Competitions Regulations, which state that CAF matches take priority over other fixtures.

PLE chairman Polycarp Dlamini acknowledged the clash involving Eswatini’s CAF envoys, Nsingizini Hotspurs and Green Mamba, but stated that clubs must plan to participate in both competitions. Green Mamba, representing Eswatini in the CAF Confederation Cup, is scheduled to play a domestic quarter-final on September 3 and a semi-final on September 5, the same day as their CAF Confederation Cup first-leg match against 1º de Agosto.

Green Mamba Chief Executive Officer Ntuthuko Dlamini confirmed the club is aware of CAF regulations but intends to participate in both, awaiting guidance from the PLE if a clash materializes. The 8Bet Trade Fair Cup is aligned with the Eswatini International Trade Fair calendar and has a new three-year agreement through 2031.

Why it mattersFootball clubs and fans in Eswatini are directly affected by the scheduling conflict, as it creates uncertainty for teams participating in both domestic and continental competitions.

Gin Gardens 2026 Expands Entertainment and Lifestyle Offerings in Ezulwini

The Gin Gardens event on October 3, 2026, will feature a diverse lineup of international and local artists, food, and art experiences for over 1,500 guests at Sharma House.

Gin Gardens 2026 is set to offer a broader lifestyle experience beyond music, according to organizers So Fresh, in partnership with Shelela Gin, Schweppes, and Polished Events. The event, scheduled for October 3, 2026, at Sharma House in Ezulwini, aims to host more than 1,500 guests for a curated daytime event that combines premium beverages, food, music, culture, and social experiences.

The entertainment program will include South African artists Sino Msolo, Nia Pearl, Touchline, and Dusse Wavy, alongside Eswatini talent such as Velemseni, DJ Tap, and MBO. Mdura Dlamini will serve as one of the official Masters of Ceremony. Organizer Welile Mthethwa stated that the goal is to establish Gin Gardens as Eswatini’s premier premium lifestyle event, reflecting the evolving tastes of the local audience.

Beyond music, the event will feature a wide selection of local and international gin brands, including Eswatini's Explorers Gin and Imbali Gin, a beer garden, and various food options. A partnership with Phillz Art will introduce live painting and art giveaways for VIP guests, creating an interactive and creative dimension to the overall experience.

Why it mattersResidents and visitors to Eswatini are affected by the expansion of entertainment and lifestyle options, offering a new cultural event that combines music, food, and art.
