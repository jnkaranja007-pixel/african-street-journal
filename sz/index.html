Eswatini SMEs face extinction threat

Economist warns local businesses struggle against foreign competition and rising costs.

Local small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in Eswatini are under threat from foreign competition, rising operating costs, and limited finance, according to Economist Dr George H. Choongwa.

Dr Choongwa, Coordinator at SARFED, stated in a policy brief that indigenous businesses risk losing ground to foreign-owned firms with stronger models and market access.

He noted that SMEs are the backbone of economic growth but face external and internal pressures, including long payment delays from larger companies, which create working capital challenges.

Why it mattersLocal small businesses in Eswatini are at risk of failing due to competition and financial pressures.

SA coaches clash in Eswatini Charity Cup semi-finals

Moneni Pirates coach Selaotse Mosala faces Mandla Qhogi of Nsingizini Hotspurs for a chance at the E400 000 prize.

South African coaches Selaotse ‘Sly’ Mosala and Mandla Qhogi will compete in the PLE Charity Cup semi-finals on Sunday at Somhlolo National Stadium. Mosala, coaching Moneni Pirates, recently won the 8Bet Trade Fair Cup. He will face Qhogi, who coaches Nsingizini Hotspurs and previously managed Milford FC. Nsingizini Hotspurs are seeking revenge against Moneni Pirates after a recent league loss.

Mandla Qhogi took charge of Nsingizini Hotspurs in May 2025 and led them to a domestic treble last season, winning the MTN Premier League, Ingwenyama Cup, and Trade Fair Cup. Despite Qhogi’s past success, Moneni Pirates enter the match with momentum from a 1-0 league victory over Nsingizini Hotspurs. The winner of this match will proceed to the final to compete for the E400 000 prize money.

The other semi-final will feature Shauwn ‘MaMkhize’ Mkhize’s Mbabane Highlanders against Ubombo Sugar. The draw sets up a rematch between Nsingizini Hotspurs and Moneni Pirates, with Mosala aiming for another trophy with the 'Buccaneers'.

Why it mattersFootball fans in Eswatini will watch South African coaches lead their teams in a high-stakes cup competition with a significant cash prize.

Eswatini rights body flags police recruitment issues

Hundreds of job seekers queue in harsh weather for limited police positions.

The Eswatini Commission on Human Rights and Public Administration Integrity has raised nine concerns about the ongoing Royal Eswatini Police Service (REPS) recruitment. The commission observed hundreds of young people queuing for prolonged periods in exposed conditions at regional police offices in Hhohho, Manzini, Shiselweni, and Lubombo. The recruitment exercise, which began on Monday, November 2, requires in-person application submissions by today, November 4.

Hundreds of applicants gathered along public roads, enduring harsh weather while waiting to submit documents for limited Constable II positions. In Mbabane, a queue stretched 500 metres, with some applicants arriving at 4am or spending the night to secure a spot. In Nhlangano, applicants faced rain and thunderstorms, with one 28-year-old applicant arriving at 7pm Sunday for a 3:30am queue position.

The commission proposed allowing electronic submissions, or applications via tinkhundla centres and police stations, to make the process more accessible. It stated that reliance on physical submissions places an unnecessary burden on applicants, particularly those from remote areas or with limited funds. The current process also pressures the police service's limited resources.

Why it mattersYoung Emaswati seeking jobs as police officers are enduring difficult conditions and long waits due to the current recruitment process.

Court dismisses ESRIC officer's disciplinary challenge

Siphiwe Mdluli argued the HR manager's involvement in initiating and sanctioning disciplinary charges was unlawful.

The Industrial Court in Mbabane dismissed an application by Eswatini Royal Insurance Corporation (ESRIC) senior compliance officer Siphiwe Mdluli. Mdluli sought to declare unlawful the human resources manager's involvement in initiating disciplinary charges against her, fearing prejudice at the sanction stage. Judge Manene Thwala delivered the judgment yesterday, with nominated members M.T.E. Mtetwa and A.M. Nkambule, dismissing Mdluli and the Swaziland Union of Financial Institutions and Allied Workers (SUFIAW) case against ESRIC and chairperson Sithembiso Dlamini.

Mdluli faced two charges of gross neglect of duty and gross insubordination, with her disciplinary hearing suspended due to the court proceedings. Her challenge focused on the HR manager's role in initiating charges and potentially determining sanctions, as outlined in ESRIC's November 28, 2017, Disciplinary Code and Procedure. The code's Clause 8.3 states the presiding officer submits recommendations on dismissal or acquittal to the HR Manager.

The court examined the disciplinary code's procedures, including Clause 3.1.7 requiring a manager's decision within five working days of an employee's explanation, and Clause 3.1.8 for written communication to the HR manager within 10 working days if the matter proceeds. Mdluli argued the HR manager's dual involvement in initiation and sanctioning was undesirable.

Why it mattersThe Industrial Court's decision affects Siphiwe Mdluli's employment status at ESRIC and the interpretation of disciplinary procedures within the company.

Simiso Simelane releases seven-track gospel album

The independent artist's new project, 'Springs of Life 2', aims to direct listeners to Christ as the ultimate source of spiritual fulfillment.

Local independent Gospel music minister Simiso Simelane released his seven-track album, 'Springs of Life 2', in Mbabane. This project is a continuation of his music ministry after his 2022 debut, 'Emtfonjeni Wakhe'.

The album's central theme, inspired by John 7:37-38, points listeners to Jesus Christ as the ultimate source of spiritual fulfillment, according to Simelane. He stated his desire for the music to go beyond entertainment and convey the truth of the Gospel.

Simelane hopes 'Springs of Life 2' will expand his reach beyond Eswatini's borders and contribute to the growth of Gospel music in the country. He described the release as evidence of God's faithfulness and the support received.

Why it mattersThe release of the album 'Springs of Life 2' by Eswatini artist Simiso Simelane aims to spread a spiritual message and foster the growth of Gospel music within the country.
