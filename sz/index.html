SEL gives E600 000 to Eswatini charities

The investment holding company distributed funds to education, children's welfare, and cancer care initiatives.

Swazi Empowerment Limited (SEL) has donated E600 000 to three Eswatini institutions supporting education infrastructure, vulnerable children, and cancer care. The investment holding company, listed on the Eswatini Stock Exchange, provided the financial support through its Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) programme. Beneficiary institutions included the Ministry of Education and Training, SOS Children’s Village, and Eswatini Cancer Network.

SEL CSR Chairperson Shaka Ndlangamandla stated that the funding comes from dividends generated by the company’s investments. He explained that this demonstrates how corporate returns can address social development needs. Representatives from the recipient organisations expressed gratitude, noting the timely assistance amid financial difficulties faced by NGOs.

The Ministry of Education and Training highlighted the donation's importance for addressing school infrastructure challenges, such as rehabilitating dilapidated facilities. Principal Economist Nkululeko Gwebu noted SEL's long-standing partnership with the ministry, which has previously funded school construction and water provision.

Why it mattersEswatini charities and government ministries received financial support from SEL for essential social services.

Nearly half of police homes unfit

Royal Eswatini Police Service reports deteriorating housing and accommodation shortages affect officers.

Almost half of police residential properties in Eswatini have been declared unfit for human occupation, according to the Royal Eswatini Police Service (REPS). Health and environmental officials made the declaration during the second quarter of the 2026/27 financial year, which ran from July to September 2026.

REPS stated that deteriorating housing and accommodation shortages have led to officers sharing facilities, causing disputes and health risks. The service acknowledges that the affected buildings require urgent repairs or replacement, impacting officer welfare and operations. Planned repairs include work at the Police Academy kitchen and bat waste removal from ceilings at facilities in Tshaneni, Big Bend, Lavumisa, Simunye, Lomahasha, and Lubulini.

The government allocated E15.75 million for rehabilitation and security fencing, but REPS considers this insufficient. Additional funding of E13.125 million was received for institutional housing projects in Mbabane, Lubulini, and Hlane, with some units completed but awaiting handover. Financial and administrative challenges, including potential contractor payment delays, could affect planned policing programmes.

Why it mattersEswatini police officers face substandard living conditions due to nearly half of their housing being declared unfit for occupation.

South African DJs bring album tour to Eswatini

SculpturedMusic and DJ Nana will headline the Playhouse x Beyond Audio Album Tour at 23:28 Lounge on November 20, 2026.

House music fans in Eswatini will experience the Playhouse x Beyond Audio Album Tour on Friday, November 20, 2026. The event at 23:28 Lounge in Mbabane features South African producer and DJ SculpturedMusic and DJ Nana. They will showcase their unique styles and impact on the South African House music scene.

NewLife Entertainment is presenting the tour, which brings together the Playhouse and Beyond Audio musical projects. Local talent including DJ Cup, Ace4Nic, Mafaka, Cnatra, Vasco, De Dang, Slotta, and Sedikins will support the headline acts. The line-up promises Deep House, Soulful House, and Afro-inspired sounds.

The event aims to bring quality musical experiences to Eswatini and provide local DJs a platform with regional performers. This tour follows SculpturedMusic and DJ Nana's performances across South Africa. November 20 is set to be a memorable night for House music lovers in the Kingdom.

Why it mattersHouse music enthusiasts in Eswatini will have the opportunity to experience a live music event featuring prominent South African DJs.

MP says pastors' divorces fuel rising divorce cases

Eswatini MP Tanele Magagula links church leaders' marital issues to family breakdown and questions their guidance.

Hhohho Region Member of Parliament Tanele Magagula stated in Parliament that pastors losing their mandate may be contributing to rising divorce cases in Eswatini. She expressed concern that some pastors are themselves divorcing, questioning who will guide couples if religious leaders fail to uphold marriage values. Magagula believes this failure means couples are not adequately guided on resolving marital disagreements.

During a debate on the Ministry of Home Affairs’ performance report, Magagula noted that pastors' personal divorces raise questions about their ability to teach couples how to handle marriages. She argued that some pastors no longer preach marriage fundamentals or the importance of strong family structures. This lack of guidance leads to disputes being taken to court instead of being resolved within families.

The MP also criticized the erosion of family involvement in marriage arrangements, citing modern practices where only pastors and a few church members are involved. Magagula questioned how church members, unfamiliar with couples' backgrounds, could offer adequate guidance. She also warned about mushrooming churches that allegedly preach against families and discourage involvement with relatives in marriage matters.

Why it mattersEswatini citizens are affected as MP Tanele Magagula suggests that the marital failures of pastors and the diminished role of families in marriage arrangements are contributing to a rise in divorces.
