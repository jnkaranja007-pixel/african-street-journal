Mbabane council plans E191.4 million growth budget

The Mbabane Municipal Council has drafted a 2027/28 budget focused on service delivery and infrastructure, with property taxes forming 91 percent of projected income.

The Municipal Council of Mbabane has proposed a E191.4 million budget for the 2027/28 fiscal year, prioritizing service delivery and infrastructure development. Residents can review draft budget documents at the Mbabane Municipal Council offices and provide input. Property taxes are expected to generate 91 percent, or E173,665,000, of the council's operating income.

To maintain service levels, the council plans a modest 3 percent increase in property taxes, intended as an inflation adjustment. Other anticipated income includes E4.2 million from Refuse Removal Fees, E5.3 million from User Fees, and E6.5 million from Finance Sundries, which covers income from leased land and facilities. Director of Technical Services Muziwendoda Masina encouraged public participation in the budget review process.

Expenditure includes E13.5 million for Mantsholo Road rehabilitation, E10 million for storm water deviation at Eveni, and E42 million for a new landfill cell. Additionally, E5 million is allocated for stormwater drainage at Elusentseni Road to alleviate flooding in Eveni. The total estimated income for 2027/28 is projected to increase by 5 percent from the previous year.

Why it mattersMbabane residents will see a 3 percent property tax increase to fund infrastructure projects and maintain services, with E5 million allocated to fix flooding in Eveni.

Eswatini names Cebisile Ngwenya to lead co-operatives department

The magistrate and former High Court official replaces the late Russell Nxumalo, who died in October 2025, as the sector faces financial and governance strain.

Cebisile Ngwenya, a magistrate who specialises in corporate law, has been named Commissioner of Co-operatives in Eswatini. She takes over from Russell Nxumalo, who died in October 2025, and will head the Department of Co-operative Development in the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Trade.

The appointment comes as some co-operatives face serious financial and governance problems. Ngwenya has sat as a magistrate at the Pigg's Peak Magistrates Court since August 2019, and before that was Assistant Master of the High Court from May 2013 to August 2019, where she administered deceased estates and supervised company and trust liquidations. Her legal background is expected to help strengthen governance and financial accountability in the department.

Ngwenya holds an LLB from the University of Pretoria and a Master of Laws in Corporate Law from the University of South Africa. She has also chaired the Eswatini Football Association's Disciplinary Committee for Lubombo and Hhohho from 2019 to 2025 and sits on its Club Licensing Board.

Why it mattersCo-operative members and the department's staff now depend on a new commissioner to oversee the finances and governance of the sector's struggling co-operatives.

Police probe death of missing guard found in Mfabantfu forest

Preliminary findings suggest the 40-year-old from Ekudzeni may have swallowed a poisonous substance, but the cause of death is not yet confirmed.

Police have opened an inquiry after the body of a 40-year-old security guard from Ekudzeni was found in a Mfabantfu forest, ending a two-day search by family and community members. The man had gone missing on Tuesday after an alleged domestic disagreement with his partner, and had reportedly sent his mother a message saying he would be found dead in a forest.

A police officer at the scene said preliminary information indicated the man had ingested a poisonous substance. The death is being treated as unnatural, and the body has been taken to the mortuary while authorities wait for post-mortem results. Chief Police Information and Communications Officer Senior Superintendent Phindile Zwane said two other people also died in separate incidents reported on Wednesday, 11 November 2026.

In one case, a 33-year-old man from Ngwane Park was found hanging in his bathroom and certified dead at Manzini Clinic. In the other, a 37-year-old woman from Lawuba was found hanging from the rafters of her bedroom and certified dead at Hlatikhulu Hospital. Figures from the Royal Eswatini Police Service showed 486 suicide deaths between 2022 and 2024, a steady rise over the three years.

Why it mattersFamily members of the dead man and the wider community now await post-mortem results that will determine how he died.

SADC and Germany launch wildlife crime project covering Eswatini

The Law Enforcement and Anti-Poaching project will train national authorities and involve communities in protecting elephants, rhinoceroses, lions and pangolins.

Eswatini is among 16 Southern African countries set to benefit from a new regional initiative against wildlife crime, which the Southern African Development Community (SADC) and Germany launched in Skukuza, South Africa, on Thursday, November 12, 2026. The Law Enforcement and Anti-Poaching (LEAP) Project is fully funded by the German government and implemented by the German development agency GIZ with the SADC Secretariat. It was launched to target cross-border poaching and wildlife trafficking networks.

Professor Kula Ishmael Theletsane, director of the Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Affairs at the SADC Secretariat, said the region no longer lacks strategies but needs them turned into coordinated action and measurable results. He said wildlife populations and the criminal networks that exploit them do not recognise political boundaries. The project supports the SADC Law Enforcement and Anti-Poaching Strategy for 2022 to 2032.

The project will focus on regional coordination, national law enforcement capacity and community-based prevention. National authorities, wildlife agencies and security institutions will be trained to detect, investigate and prosecute wildlife offences. The community component will address human-wildlife conflict around selected Transfrontier Conservation Areas, with communities encouraged to take an active part in conservation.

Why it mattersEswatini's wildlife agencies and security institutions will receive training to detect, investigate and prosecute wildlife offences under the project.

Eswatini U-23 team beats Algeria

The national Under-23 football team's victory over Algeria has boosted national pride after a series of poor senior team performances.

Eswatini's national Under-23 football team secured a significant 2-1 victory against Algeria on Wednesday in a Confederation of African Football Africa Cup of Nations qualifier. This win follows their earlier 2-1 aggregate triumph over Malawi, advancing them to the second preliminary round. The team's impressive performance has resonated with local fans, offering a morale boost after the senior national team's recent struggles.

Approximately 500 spectators at Somhlolo National Stadium expressed delight, praising the team's fearless fighting spirit and seeing their victory as a sign of a bright future for Eswatini football. Fans on Facebook suggested integrating the Under-23 squad with the senior team, emphasizing the need to nurture this talent for future generations. One supporter noted the technical team's effectiveness in building a strong foundation.

Fans also commented on the potential for players to earn a living from football if well-supported, with some suggesting the team deserved significant bonuses. Despite one fan's claim that Algeria was 'robbed', others acknowledged the Under-23 team's quality, with one user hoping for at least a point in Algeria even if they didn't win.

Why it mattersThe national Under-23 football team's recent success has restored national pride and sparked discussions about the future of Eswatini football.
