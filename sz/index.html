Eclipse Renews Mbabane Swallows Deal Amidst Investment Search

Mbabane Swallows secures continued partnership with Eclipse, providing stability as the club seeks new investment after a previous deal collapsed.

Mbabane Swallows has renewed its sponsorship deal with Eclipse, a move that offers the Eswatini football club crucial stability during a period of rebuilding. The renewal comes as the club continues its search for new investment following the failure of a potential deal with an investor from the Democratic Republic of Congo. Eclipse's continued commitment will provide daily training and matchday meals, as well as playing and training kits, supporting the team through internal challenges and infighting.

The partnership renewal is significant for Mbabane Swallows, one of Eswatini's most prominent football clubs with seven league championships and a history of continental competition. This continuity from Eclipse is vital as the club prepares for the 8Bet Trade Fair Cup and the upcoming domestic season. The club is also urging its supporters to patronize Eclipse's businesses to strengthen the commercial relationship and contribute to the team's resources, with a supporter competition planned to reward those who support the sponsor.

Discussions regarding new merchandise and playing kits are now a priority for Mbabane Swallows following the Eclipse renewal, with agreements typically finalized in the off-season. The club has apologized for any delay and promised further updates. While Eclipse's decision may not resolve all the club's challenges, it provides the essential continuity the team needs to move forward and rebuild its stability.

Why it mattersThe renewal of the Eclipse sponsorship provides Mbabane Swallows with essential resources and continuity, enabling them to continue operations and seek further investment.

South African stars ignite Eswatini International Trade Fair opening

Mduduzi Ncube and Simmy performed for King Mswati III and the Queen Mother at the 58th EITF.

The 58th Eswatini International Trade Fair officially opened with vibrant musical performances at the Mavuso Trade Centre Sports Ground. South African artists Mduduzi Ncube and Simmy headlined the event, delivering high-energy sets that captivated the audience. Ncube, known for his Afro-pop and Afro-soul style, performed hits like ‘Ngeke Ngimyeke’ and ‘Isigici’, engaging directly with the crowd. He later performed for His Majesty King Mswati III and the Queen Mother, expressing that Eswatini felt like home.

Simmy, a South African singer and songwriter blending neo-soul and electronic music, followed with a performance of tracks including ‘Ubala’ and ‘Ngiyesaba’. Despite initially performing in high heels, she removed them to finish her set barefoot, maintaining the energetic atmosphere. Both artists mingled with fans, posing for photos and videos, and expressed their appreciation for the warm reception in Eswatini.

The opening ceremony also featured a spiritually charged performance by gospel artist Linda Dlamini, who delivered gospel dances backed by the Tende sound. The event celebrated music and culture, providing attendees with a memorable experience that highlighted both local and international talent.

Why it mattersThe opening of the 58th Eswatini International Trade Fair was marked by musical performances from South African artists, attracting attendees and showcasing cultural exchange.

Kellogg Tolaram factory expansion tender issued

Government has issued a tender for the expansion of the Kellogg Tolaram factory shell, advancing a pledge made at last year's investment conference.

The Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Trade has released a tender for the expansion of the Kellogg Tolaram factory shell, including the construction of a canteen, ablution facilities, and an access road. This move signifies a concrete step towards realizing investment commitments made by the joint venture at the 2025 inaugural Investment Conference. The tender, issued on July 28, 2026, with a bid deadline of September 1, 2026, marks the transition from pledged investment to a formal procurement process for construction.

Kellogg Tolaram Eswatini, a subsidiary of the joint venture between Kellogg Company USA and Tolaram Group of Singapore, aims to develop a value-added manufacturing facility for cereals and snacks for local and export markets. The company previously invested E210 million in its existing factory, while the government contributed E200 million for the factory shell. The company plans to introduce additional production lines for breakfast cereals and snacks.

This development is part of a broader investment drive that saw over E37 billion in pledges at the 2025 conference. Another factory shell development at Ngwenya, also linked to the same investors, is expected to produce bleach products and create approximately 700 jobs, with the government committing E78 million towards this project.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the tender signals progress in converting investment pledges into tangible construction and expanded productive capacity.

Reed Dance crowds boost small business sales

Thousands of maidens and attendees at the annual cultural event create economic opportunities for local traders.

The annual Reed Dance in Eswatini is generating significant economic activity as thousands of small business owners capitalize on the large crowds. Traders selling various goods and services are experiencing increased sales, providing a crucial income boost during the cultural gathering. This economic impact highlights the event's broader reach beyond its traditional and educational functions.

Organizers and educators are using the Reed Dance to impart messages on personal values, health, and education, alongside cultural preservation. Celiwe Mohammed from the Ministry of Education and Training emphasized correct SiSwati language use, underscoring the importance of indigenous language preservation. These educational components are delivered to thousands of young people gathered for the event.

The Reed Dance, also known as Umkhosi weMhlanga, serves as a major national gathering that blends cultural celebration with educational outreach and economic stimulation. While maidens showcase traditional heritage through song, dance, and regalia, the event's scale allows for direct engagement with young attendees on vital life issues and fosters a vibrant marketplace for local entrepreneurs.

Why it mattersSmall business owners are directly benefiting from increased sales and income generation due to the large crowds attending the Reed Dance.

Gama, Dlamini Win Ngwenya Mountain Trail Run

Runners raised funds for Cheshire Homes' hydrotherapy pool project for people with disabilities.

Nkululeko Gama and Nosi Dlamini secured the 21km titles at the Ngwenya Mountain Trail Challenge yesterday. The event, which began and ended at Ngwenya Glass, saw approximately 300 participants across various distances, including 12.5km and 7km races. Top finishers in each category received gift boxes of glassware.

The challenge aimed to raise awareness and funds for Cheshire Homes' hydrotherapy pool, a facility needed for patients with disabilities. Ngwenya Glass Managing Director Chas Prettejohn expressed delight in hosting the event, noting that patients currently rely on a facility in Ezulwini and need a dedicated space. Plans are underway to host the event again next year, with hopes to attract over 500 runners.

Cheshire Homes Board Chairman Themba Dlamini, speaking for patron Inkhosikati Lantentesa, thanked the nation and corporate partners for their support. The hydrotherapy pool project requires E180,000, with E70,000 already raised. Cheshire Homes CEO Dumsani Msibi highlighted the need for ongoing funding for this and other initiatives.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the event's success in raising funds for a community facility and the stated intention to repeat the event, potentially indicating future sponsorship or partnership opportunities.
