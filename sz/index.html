Eswatini PM says no one is above the law, foreign firms included

Prime Minister Russell Mmiso Dlamini says businesses operating illegally on Swazi Nation Land must obey the law or face consequences.

Prime Minister Russell Mmiso Dlamini has said no individual, institution or business, local or foreign, is above the law, weighing in on foreign-owned businesses allegedly operating illegally on Swazi Nation Land (SNL). His remarks follow a recommendation adopted by Members of Parliament (MPs) giving non-compliant foreign-owned businesses 12 months to regularise their operations or close.

Dlamini said the rule of law was non-negotiable and that the government has a constitutional duty to enforce it without fear, favour or prejudice. He warned that allowing people or businesses to operate outside the law could lead to lawlessness and erode national sovereignty. He also called on emaSwati to take primary responsibility for building their country, saying foreign investment should complement local participation rather than displace it.

He said allegations of bribery, improper inducements and corrupt influence should be thoroughly investigated, and that anyone found to have engaged in wrongdoing must be held accountable through lawful processes. He added that no amount of money could justify compromising the economic future of the nation.

Why it mattersForeign firms on Swazi Nation Land that are found non-compliant have 12 months to regularise their operations or close, and any bribery found will be investigated.

Soldiers accused of aiding cigarette smugglers

Vendors claim some Eswatini soldiers accept bribes to allow illegal tobacco into the country from Mozambique.

Cigarette smugglers allege that some soldiers accept bribes to allow illegal tobacco consignments into Eswatini from Mozambique. An investigation found street vendors selling smuggled GT cigarettes for E25 a packet or E1.50 loose, who claimed certain members of the Umbutfo Eswatini Defence Force (UEDF) facilitate their movement across borders. The Lomahasha and Ngwenya border areas are allegedly used by smugglers, though some soldiers reportedly confiscate cigarettes instead of accepting bribes.

UEDF Public Relations Officer Major Sandile Gwebu denied the allegations, stating soldiers operate under strict supervision and report hourly. He asserted that soldiers arrest smugglers when intercepted and are motivated enough by their pay not to take bribes, warning that bribery attempts are an offense. The army maintains a zero-tolerance policy for bribery, with guilty members facing serious consequences.

Ministry of Health Non-Communicable Diseases Coordinator Vusi Shabangu alleged that some law enforcement officers sell confiscated cigarettes via social media instead of destroying them, citing an army officer seeking buyers for GT cigarettes. Shabangu stated this conduct undermines tobacco control efforts and called for stricter enforcement and proper destruction of confiscated items, warning that the proposed Tobacco Control Bill of 2026 would be ineffective if illegal practices persist.

Why it mattersStreet vendors selling smuggled cigarettes face arrest if caught, and the illegal trade undermines public health efforts in Eswatini.

Eswatini minister urges chiefs to turn indigenous economies into jobs

S'khumbuzo Dlamini told the African Traditional Leaders Forum in Addis Ababa that informal traditional activities should become formal industries.

Eswatini's Minister for Tinkhundla Administration and Development, S'khumbuzo Dlamini, has called on traditional leaders to help turn indigenous economic activities into businesses that create jobs and reduce poverty. He made the call at the African Traditional Leaders Forum 2026, held at the African Union headquarters in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia.

Dlamini said agriculture, livestock production, traditional crafts, indigenous medicine and cultural tourism could all drive growth, but much of that potential stays untapped because the activities operate informally. He urged investment to formalise, modernise and commercialise them while protecting the traditional knowledge behind them, with priority for youth employment and women's participation.

In Eswatini, traditional leadership forms part of the governance structure through the Tinkhundla System, which gives communities a way to set their development priorities. Chiefs, Dlamini said, play an important role in connecting that leadership with community development.

Eswatini healer survives KZN hijacking

Two armed men shot at a traditional healer and stole his friend's car in South Africa's KwaZulu-Natal province.

A traditional healer from Eswatini escaped unharmed after two armed men shot at him and hijacked a vehicle in Tongaat, KwaZulu-Natal, on Sunday afternoon, November 15, 2026. The incident occurred along the R102 near Gopallal Road.

The healer had travelled from Eswatini for his mother’s funeral and agreed to help two men with a Zulu traditional healing matter. After providing assistance, he offered them a ride back in his friend's white Toyota Yaris, registration NDOSI 9 ZN. The suspects, seated in the back, produced firearms and fired two shots at the healer before stealing the car, four cellphones, and a blank-firing firearm.

Reaction Unit South Africa (RUSA) responded to the call for assistance at 4:22pm but were unsuccessful in tracing the stolen vehicle. The suspects fled towards Gopallal Road and Noodsberg Road, and remain at large.

Why it mattersA traditional healer from Eswatini was targeted in a hijacking and shooting in South Africa, with his vehicle and cellphones stolen.

EYEOP gets siSwati name Litsafa Lensha

The Eswatini Youth Employment Opportunities Project adopted a new name and logo to boost youth economic opportunities.

The Eswatini Youth Employment Opportunities Project (EYEOP) has officially adopted the siSwati name Litsafa Lensha. The name, submitted by competition winner Bongekile Zelda Dlamini, was unveiled alongside the project’s official logo during a ceremony at The George Hotel in Manzini on Thursday, November 12, 2026. Memoranda of understanding were signed with partner institutions, and working tools were handed over.

Minister for Economic Planning and Development Dr Thambo Gina described the unveiling as representing national ownership of an initiative designed around the aspirations of young emaSwati. He noted that approximately 25,000 young people enter the labour market annually, with only about 1,000 formal jobs created. The five-year government initiative, in partnership with the World Bank, has financing of US$27 million through an International Development Association (IDA) credit.

Litsafa Lensha aims to create income-earning opportunities for up to 30,000 young people across Eswatini’s four regions. It will focus on demand-driven skills development, apprenticeships, entrepreneurship support, and improved access to resources and markets. Particular attention will be given to young women, persons with disabilities, and young people in rural communities.

Why it mattersThe Litsafa Lensha project aims to create income-earning opportunities for up to 30,000 young people across Eswatini's four regions.
