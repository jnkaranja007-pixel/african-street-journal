Nsingizini Hotspurs Signs Eight Players for Continental Push

The Eswatini club has bolstered its squad with new signings, though they will miss the continental competition's registration deadline.

Nsingizini Hotspurs, Eswatini's representative in the CAF Champions League, has announced the signing of eight new players. Among the notable additions is South African forward Senzo Ndlovu, who joins permanently after a successful loan spell, and Ghanaian striker Emmanuel Nana Sarpong. Domestically, the team has acquired Njongo Mazibuko, Bongi Magagula, and Mbongeni Magagula from rival clubs, along with Muzi Mkhonta and Ncamiso Nkwanyana. Lwandisa Dlamini has been promoted from the development squad. These new recruits are expected to strengthen the team for their domestic title defense.

Despite the significant player acquisitions, the new signings are unlikely to feature in Nsingizini Hotspurs' upcoming CAF Champions League preliminary round match against Scottland FC in Zimbabwe. This is because the registration deadline for the first window of CAF inter-club competitions has already passed. The team will instead focus on integrating these players into the squad for their 8Bet Trade Fair Cup title defense, which begins today against Madlenya.

This marks Nsingizini Hotspurs' second appearance in Africa's premier club competition. Last season, they advanced past Simba Bhora in the first round before being eliminated by Tanzanian giants Simba SC. Their opponents in Zimbabwe, Scottland FC, are making their debut in the tournament. Nsingizini Hotspurs will have to divide their squad as their domestic season opener coincides with their continental preparations.

Why it mattersFootball investors and fans in Eswatini will be interested in how these new signings impact the team's performance in domestic and continental competitions.

Moneni Pirates Defeat Ex-Coach Caleb Ngwenya in Debut

Andile Mamba's 71st-minute goal secured a 1-0 victory for Moneni Pirates against Ezulwini United, spoiling Caleb Ngwenya's first match as coach.

Moneni Pirates secured a 1-0 win over Ezulwini United at the Mavuso Sports Centre yesterday, handing former Sihlangu Head Coach Caleb Ngwenya a disappointing debut. The victory came in the final match of the 8Bet Trade Fair Cup grand opening, with the win setting up a tie against the winner of Royal Leopard versus Tabankulu Celtics. The decisive goal arrived in the 71st minute when substitute Andile Mamba headed home Sandziso Vilakati’s corner.

The match also marked the first game for Pirates' new South Africa-born coach, Salaotse ‘Sly’ Mosala, who recently switched from Mbabane Highlanders. Both teams started with high intensity, but Moneni Pirates created the clearer chances in a goalless first half. Pirates continued their pressure after the break, and Sananelo Nkambule had a goal disallowed for offside in the 62nd minute.

Ezulwini United's best opportunity came eight minutes before the late goal, when Boniswa Gama set up Banele Nkambule, whose shot was saved by Pirates goalkeeper Bernard Ayikwe. Mamba's header shortly after proved to be the match-winner, sealing the victory for the 'Sea Robbers' and marking a tough start for Ngwenya's tenure with Ezulwini United.

Eswatini League Revival Secures E400,000 Funding

The Premier League of Eswatini has allocated E400,000 to revive the country's development league after it was sidelined last season.

The Premier League of Eswatini (PLE) is set to reintroduce its development league this season with a dedicated budget of E400,000. This funding aims to revive the grassroots competition, which was absent last term. The development league, traditionally contested by junior sides of MTN Premier League clubs, is a requirement for club licensing by the Confederation of African Football (CAF).

Last season, the competition, formerly known as the Under-19 League, failed to commence despite assurances from the PLE. The organization cited a shortage of playing venues as the reason for the delay. Previous discussions about restructuring the league into an Under-17 national competition or converting the MulaSport National First Division into an Under-23 league have stalled.

Chronic underfunding has been identified as the primary obstacle to the league's operation, despite E400,000 being budgeted last season without materializing. The same amount has been allocated for the upcoming campaign. Young Buffaloes are the current champions, having won the 2024/25 title.

Why it mattersThe revival of the development league will provide a platform for young footballers to develop their skills and potentially advance to higher levels of the sport.

Maidens Deliver Reed for Umhlanga Ceremony

Thousands of emaSwati and international visitors gathered at Ludzidzini Royal Residence for the annual Umhlanga Ceremony's reed delivery.

Thousands of young women, led by Princess Sakhizwe and Indvuna Bongiwe Vivane Hlatjwako, delivered reeds to Ludzidzini Royal Residence on August 30, 2026. The reed, a plant from swamps, is used to cover enclosures at royal residences and is exclusively gathered by virgin girls to signify their commitment to chastity. This event marks a significant milestone for the participants, who came from across the country and beyond.

Following the reed delivery, the maidens proceeded to the cultural stadium for demonstrations planned for the next day, in preparation for the main Umhlanga Ceremony. The ceremony aims to allow maidens to showcase their chastity before Their Majesties and spectators, promoting the protection of young girls from sexually transmitted diseases. The buildup to the event has attracted thousands of local residents and international tourists.

Several tourists expressed that they visited Eswatini specifically to witness the Umhlanga Ceremony, appreciating a culture that values and encourages young girls for preserving their virginity. The arena is open to all nations interested in sharing their cultural heritage with the emaSwati people, fostering an exchange of traditions and values.

Why it mattersThe Umhlanga Ceremony's reed delivery and subsequent events attract thousands of international visitors, boosting tourism and cultural exchange.

EFA President Simelane: Clubs can sell businesses if licensing rules met

Eswatini Football Association president Peter Simelane stated clubs have the right to buy or sell their businesses, provided licensing rules are followed.

Eswatini Football Association president Peter Simelane confirmed that clubs can buy or sell their businesses as long as they adhere to licensing regulations. Simelane made these remarks during the EFA Annual General Meeting, addressing inquiries about club ownership. He emphasized that the EFA's role is to ensure compliance with club licensing requirements, not to interfere in private transactions between individuals.

His comments come as the Manzini Wanderers club is reportedly entering a new ownership phase. While the sale is understood to be finalized, the club missed the deadline for submitting a new name. Consequently, the team is expected to compete as Red Rhinos, with official records showing Wanderers (Rhinos), when they face Green Mamba on Tuesday.

Red Rhinos director Ngudze Mamba expressed readiness for the upcoming match, stating all requirements have been met pending EFA approval. Wanderers Communications Manager Sandile ‘Ntofo’ Dlamini also conveyed confidence in the team taking the field, awaiting final confirmation from EFA and the Premier League of Eswatini. These administrative processes highlight the EFA's club licensing framework.

Why it mattersThe Eswatini Football Association's clarification on club ownership rules impacts potential investors and business entities looking to acquire or sell football clubs.
