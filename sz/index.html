Eswatini League Teams Adopt Animal Monikers

A 16-team football league in Eswatini is embracing animal names and imagery, with one team undergoing a rebrand following its acquisition by a South African businessman.

Eswatini's top-tier football league, the Premier League, features 16 teams that have adopted animal names and imagery for their identities. This trend sees clubs drawing inspiration from the natural world, with names reflecting the characteristics of various creatures.

Notable examples include Nsingizini Hotspurs, named after the southern ground hornbill, and Mbabane Swallows, inspired by agile birds. Security forces teams also feature animal themes, such as Royal Leopard, Green Mamba, and Young Buffaloes, evoking their perceived attributes. Mbabane Highlanders are known as the 'Black Bull'.

The team Amawele, based in Msunduza, is currently seeking approval to rebrand as Mbabane Black Eagles after being acquired by South African businessman Ally Kgomongwe. Other teams in the league include Manzini Sea Birds and Red Rhinos, further diversifying the animal-themed identities.

Why it mattersThe acquisition and rebranding of a football team by a South African businessman indicates potential investment and business activity within Eswatini's sports sector.

Eswatini Raids Uncover Expired Food, Illegal Traders

A two-day crackdown in Nhlangano inspected 94 businesses, leading to 37 arrests for offenses including expired goods, unlicensed trading, and immigration violations.

Authorities in Nhlangano have charged 37 traders and inspected 94 businesses during a two-day compliance operation that uncovered expired food, unlabelled stock, and immigration violations. The crackdown, part of a 60-day nationwide campaign, saw inspectors from multiple government bodies examine retail establishments, with a focus on foreign-owned businesses. Officials reported finding expired and unlabelled goods, some of which were confiscated and handed to the town council for destruction.

The operation, led by the Ministry of Labour and Social Security in conjunction with Commerce, Health, the Eswatini Revenue Service, Nhlangano Town Council, and the Royal Eswatini Police Service, identified several breaches. Initial inspections on Tuesday revealed three immigration breaches, five public health violations, and five businesses operating without valid licenses. Subsequent focus on retail shops led to charges against operators for lacking residence permits or allegedly using fraudulent ones.

Task team Manager Gcwalisile Mngometulu stated that offenders were charged and ordered to pay fines at the police station. The nationwide compliance campaign is set to continue for another two to three months, aiming to enforce laws governing businesses and employment across Eswatini. The inspections covered a range of issues including food safety standards and trading licenses.

Why it mattersThe raids and arrests of 37 traders for offenses like expired food and unlicensed trading directly impact business operations and regulatory compliance.

Moneni Pirates puts national team goalkeeper Nyawo on transfer list

The Eswatini national team's first-choice goalkeeper, Qiniso Nyawo, has been placed on the transfer list by his club, Moneni Pirates, alongside two other players.

Moneni Pirates FC announced on Thursday, August 27, 2026, that goalkeeper Qiniso Nyawo, defender Vukile Khumalo, and player Phila Dlamini have been placed on the transfer list with immediate effect. Nyawo, who was the team's first-choice goalkeeper last season and a key player for the Eswatini national team during recent CAF Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers, is now available for transfer. Khumalo, a regular starter at left-back known for his attacking contributions and penalty-taking ability, also faces an uncertain future with the club.

Phila Dlamini had already departed Moneni Pirates mid-season to join Ezulwini United. These three players join four others who have officially left the club: Mthobisi Zwane and Innocent Pakule, both to Nsingizini Hotspurs; Reagan Kangumbu, who returned to Manzini Wanderers; and Ivorian striker Bi Zahi Herve. The club has also been active in recruitment, signing midfielder Ncomo Gina from Mbabane Swallows.

Nyawo's tenure at Moneni Pirates was his first season with the club, where he was initially the first-choice goalkeeper. However, for the final matches of the season after the March FIFA International Break, Ghanaian goalkeeper Bernard Ayikwei took over the starting position. Nyawo has previously played for three of Eswatini's major clubs: Mbabane Highlanders, Mbabane Swallows, and Manzini Wanderers.

Author Sue Nyathi to Discuss Book-to-Screen Adaptation in Eswatini

Award-winning author and screenwriter Sue Nyathi will share insights on adapting local stories for global audiences at the Eswatini International Trade Fair.

Award-winning author and screenwriter Sue Nyathi will lead a creative session in Manzini on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, focusing on the journey of a manuscript to a successful screen adaptation. Nyathi's novel, ‘The Polygamist,’ was adapted into a Netflix series that achieved the number one spot in 16 countries. The session, titled 'From Page to Screen,' is scheduled to take place from 1pm to 3:30pm in the Green Chamber of the Mavuso Trade and Exhibition Centre.

The event aims to demystify the process of transforming local narratives into content that can reach diverse markets and audiences across different formats. Organizers have targeted writers, filmmakers, storytellers, aspiring screenwriters, and creative entrepreneurs who are seeking practical advice on the business of adaptation. The format will be conversational, allowing Nyathi to detail the decisions, compromises, and opportunities involved in adapting a book for the screen.

Discussions will cover storytelling techniques, the practicalities of screenwriting, and the commercial aspects of pitching and producing for television and film. Nyathi will analyze shifts in narrative structure between novels and series, methods for maintaining an author's voice during collaboration, and emerging funding and distribution channels for African creatives. This event signals a growing ambition within Eswatini's creative sector to expand beyond domestic reach, using Nyathi's success as a case study.

Why it mattersCreative professionals in Eswatini can gain practical insights into adapting local stories for international markets, potentially opening new revenue streams and audiences.

Deputy PM Challenges SADC Public Service Commissions on Action

Eswatini's Deputy Prime Minister Thulisile Dladla urged public service leaders to deliver tangible improvements rather than just words, emphasizing the need to combat corruption.

Deputy Prime Minister Thulisile Dladla has urged public service commissions across the SADC region to move beyond talk and deliver concrete results for citizens. Speaking at the 3rd Conference and Annual General Meeting of the SADC Public Service Commissions Forum in Ezulwini, Dladla stated that the effectiveness of these bodies will be measured by their ability to create tangible improvements in people's lives.

The three-day meeting, themed 'Accelerating transformation through resilient, responsive, citizen-centric, innovative, and incorruptible public service institutions,' brings together senior officials to discuss regional development commitments. Dladla highlighted that ambitious goals, such as increasing agricultural trade and improving cross-border logistics, will only succeed if public institutions can translate them into practical actions.

Dladla emphasized that public service commissions are crucial for ensuring governments have the necessary human capital and integrity to implement regional commitments. She also stressed that fighting corruption and ensuring accountability are central to rebuilding public trust and safeguarding resources, aligning with King Mswati III's agenda for Eswatini where every Lilangeni should benefit the people.

Why it mattersPublic service leaders are challenged to implement tangible changes and combat corruption, directly affecting how regional development commitments translate into citizen benefits.
