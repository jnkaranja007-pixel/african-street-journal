Eswatini Trade Fair and Rally Draw International and Local Talent

The Eswatini International Trade Fair and Swazi Rally are set to host a diverse range of performers and participants, attracting both regional stars and dedicated enthusiasts.

The Eswatini International Trade Fair will open with performances by South African artists Simmy and Mduduzi Ncube, blending Afro-Soul with folk and acoustic narratives. Local hip hop and contemporary Swati artists, including Young Zesh, Sancheziey, Mco Jamrock, and DJ Bandora, will also showcase indigenous lyrical flows and modern street culture. The event continues with a Gospel Sunday featuring South African headliner Jumbo alongside Eswatini's Phathwakahle, Ndawonye Christ Worshippers, and Shongwe and Khuphuka Saved Group.

Further entertainment includes a pop set by South African duo Q Twins and DJ Tira, complemented by Eswatini's saxophonist ZwiLe, who will introduce jazz-influenced textures. This cultural showcase aligns with national Umhlanga holiday celebrations. Separately, the 33rd edition of the Swazi Rally, an annual bikers' event established in 1993, will take place at Riders Ranch in Sidvokodvo. Organizers have expanded camping facilities due to increasing attendance.

The Swazi Rally, founded by Carlos Paiva and his wife Sonia, has a long-standing tradition, with some attendees participating since its inception. The event is described as the ultimate biker experience in Southern Africa, featuring roaring engines and nights under the stars. The rally's continuous development is attributed to the ownership of the site, offering picturesque scenery along its winding roads.

Why it mattersThe Eswatini International Trade Fair and Swazi Rally are expected to attract significant numbers of both local and international visitors, boosting economic activity through tourism and commerce.

Green Chert Mine Halts Operations, Leaving Workers Unemployed

The mine's sudden closure has left between 80 and 100 workers jobless and cast doubt on community development projects.

Green Chert Mine, a project once heralded for its potential to create jobs and stimulate economic growth in northern Hhohho, has ceased operations. The mine's premises in Nkomazi were found deserted, with heavy machinery stationary since July 23, the day workers were informed of the halt. Former employees reported being verbally told operations were suspended 'until further notice,' a surprise announcement that left various categories of workers, from blasters to drivers, without income.

The mine's closure raises concerns about the future of promised community development initiatives, including two mini-shops and a spaza shop, as well as planned housing for displaced residents. These projects had generated optimism for long-term economic transformation in the traditionally rural area, with expectations of increased economic activity and improved infrastructure.

The halt affects workers across different roles, including stone parkers, sorters, blasters, drivers, and boilermakers, at both the Nkomazi headquarters and the extraction site near kaNdeva. The mine's development had previously attracted significant investment, with millions of Emalangeni reportedly invested, fueling hopes for a more urbanized community and enhanced livelihoods.

Why it mattersBetween 80 and 100 mine workers are directly affected by the halt in operations, losing their income and facing uncertainty.

Eswatini Captain Dlamini Pursues Global Cricket Coaching Certification

Mbali Dlamini aims to become the first Eswatini Cricket Association coach to achieve ICC Level 2 certification at a program in Tanzania.

Eswatini national women's cricket captain Mbali Dlamini is attending the ICC Future Leadership Programme for Female Coaches in Tanzania from August 29 to September 6. Dlamini, who has captained the national team since 2020, was selected for the program based on her progress through the ICC coaching development pathway. The initiative offers aspiring female coaches access to international expertise and mentorship, aiming to strengthen their coaching and leadership skills in an international setting.

The program presents an opportunity for Dlamini to potentially earn the ICC Level 2 Coaching Certificate, a qualification that would make her the first coach, male or female, in Eswatini Cricket Association history to achieve it. This milestone is significant for the country's efforts to grow its cricket infrastructure and develop a more robust pipeline of coaches, particularly in the women's game. The knowledge gained is expected to benefit players and future coaches back home.

Dlamini's participation is supported by the Eswatini Sport and Recreation Council and the Eswatini Olympics and Commonwealth Games Association. Her journey represents a significant step for Eswatini within an international coaching development program focused on the future of women's cricket. The immediate goal is for Dlamini to maximize her learning experience in Tanzania, which could mark the beginning of a new era for women's coaching in Eswatini.

Why it mattersEswatini cricket stakeholders, including administrators and aspiring coaches, are affected by the potential for a historic coaching qualification that could bolster the sport's development.

Pam Golding Properties Relaunches in Eswatini

The international real estate brand marks a new chapter in the Kingdom with trusted local leadership and a commitment to personalized client experiences.

Pam Golding Properties has relaunched in Eswatini, establishing a new chapter built on heritage and continuity. The company, founded in South Africa in 1976, has evolved into a respected real estate brand with an international presence. This relaunch signifies confidence in Eswatini's property market potential and a commitment to partnering with experienced local professionals who align with the brand's values.

The relaunch is led by Musawenkosi C. Nhleko, principal and franchise holder of Pam Golding Properties Eswatini, along with his team of specialist real estate professionals. Nhleko and his wife, Nomcebo Nhleko, attended the 2026 GoldClub Gala Awards in Johannesburg, celebrating the company's 50th anniversary and its achievements.

Pam Golding Properties first entered Eswatini in 2003, opening an office in Ezulwini. The brand's return aims to build on its legacy in the Kingdom by combining deep local knowledge with the strength of its regional and international network. The company's ethos emphasizes trusted relationships, local expertise, market insight, and a dedication to delivering personalized client experiences.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the relaunch of Pam Golding Properties, which signals confidence in Eswatini's property market potential and offers a platform for future growth through local expertise and an international network.

Thousands of Maidens Complete Four-Day Umhlanga Reed March

Older Imbali, led by Princess Sakhizwe, concluded their return journey from Mpisi Farm after a physically demanding reed-cutting exercise.

Thousands of older Imbali yesterday began their return journey from Mpisi Farm, marking the fourth day of the Umhlanga ceremony. Led by Imbali Regiment Leader Princess Sakhizwe, the maidens marched towards Mafutseni, arriving at the police station after covering 5.9 kilometres. The procession was filled with singing and dancing, showcasing the endurance and energy of the participants who had gathered from across the kingdom.

The annual national ceremony began on Tuesday with the arrival and registration of thousands of maidens at Ludzidzini. The second day saw the maidens commissioned for the reed-cutting exercise, with King Mswati III directing older Imbali to Mpisi Farm and younger ones to Masundvwini. The third day was the most physically demanding, as older Imbali travelled to Mpisi Farm for the reed-cutting, officially opened by Princess Sakhizwe.

Participants from various chiefdoms, including contingents from the Royal Eswatini Police Service, Eswatini Defence Force, and Eswatini National Fire, Rescue and Emergency Services, joined the reed-cutting. Maidens sang and danced as they gathered reeds, assisted by Emazinyane and tindvuna, preparing them for the return journey to the royal residence.

Why it mattersThe Umhlanga ceremony involves thousands of young women from across Eswatini in traditional activities and physical tasks, reinforcing cultural practices.
