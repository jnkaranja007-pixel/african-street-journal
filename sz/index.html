Eswatini MPs seek easier travel with Lesotho, Botswana

Eswatini parliamentarians met in Lesotho to discuss removing travel barriers with neighbouring countries.

Parliamentarians from Eswatini and Lesotho met on Saturday at Thaba-Bosiu Cultural Village in Lesotho to push for greater freedom of movement between their nations and Botswana. Legislators proposed exploring ways to ease travel restrictions for citizens of the three countries. The engagement aimed to strengthen regional cooperation and foster friendships.

Marwick Khumalo, Chairperson of the Commonwealth Parliamentary Association (CPA) Eswatini Branch, highlighted the importance of neighbourliness and solidarity. He stated that exchange programmes like this help build relationships that can lead to stronger cooperation. Khumalo supported considering proposals for easier travel if they offer tangible benefits.

The visit provided opportunities for parliamentarians to experience new places and learn from each other. Khumalo recalled similar successful exchanges with South African provinces in the past. The discussions align with previous calls for stronger regional ties and practical measures to facilitate citizen interaction.

Why it mattersEswatini parliamentarians are seeking to make it easier for citizens to travel to Lesotho and Botswana.

Vusi Nova applauds Eswatini musician Young Zesh

South African artist Vusi Nova praised Young Zesh's song 'KaKhoza' at the Malkerns Country Club Kota Festival.

The Malkerns Country Club hosted the Kota Festival on Saturday, a celebration of food, culture, and music. The event featured local and South African artists, including Vusi Nova and Young Stunna, who performed for attendees dressed in yellow and white. Local companies Berachah Kitchen and Embiveni Meat Industries partnered with the festival, providing food.

South African artist Vusi Nova performed popular tracks like ‘Thandiwe’ and ‘As’phelelanga’ with a live band. He asked the crowd to light up their phones and sing in memory of the late Zahara. Following his performance, Amapiano sensation Young Stunna energized the crowd with his music. Local artist Young Zesh also performed Swati hits like ‘Senibeketele’.

After his set, Young Stunna spoke to the Times of Eswatini and praised local musician Young Zesh. Vusi Nova also expressed admiration for Young Zesh’s song ‘KaKhoza’, encouraging Eswatini residents to support local talent. Hip Hop star Emtee and Morda Bongz also performed, alongside Eswatini artist Gifted Kosie.

Why it mattersEswatini residents attended the Kota Festival for food, culture, and music performances.

DHL, EIPA train Eswatini MSMEs for export

A new partnership aims to equip local businesses with skills for international markets and customs.

DHL and the Eswatini Investment Promotion Authority (EIPA) partnered last week to train micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) in export skills. The collaboration combines EIPA’s Export Readiness Programme with DHL’s GoTrade Initiative to help businesses identify global markets, understand customs, and manage cross-border logistics. Training is set to begin next month, with EIPA inviting businesses to register.

EIPA Chief Executive Officer Sibani Mngomezulu stated the partnership bridges the gap for businesses wanting to export but lacking the necessary skills. He explained that while Eswatini produces goods with international potential, many firms struggle with market identification, export procedures, and efficient product movement. The programme will focus on practical capabilities, using DHL’s Trade and Grow Beyond Borders curriculum and the GoTrade Portal.

EIPA will contribute by assessing export preparedness, analysing markets, and identifying product opportunities. The authority will help businesses understand demand, market prospects, and competitive requirements. This combined approach addresses challenges from finding buyers to fulfilling orders, helping firms overcome gaps in market access and practical trade requirements.

Why it mattersEswatini's micro, small, and medium enterprises will gain practical skills to access international markets and increase export sales.

Eswatini votes on Africa Cup of Nations schedule

The Confederation of African Football is consulting its 54 member associations on tournament frequency.

Eswatini is among the 54 member associations consulted by the Confederation of African Football (CAF) regarding the Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) schedule. CAF has formally requested member federations' official stances on returning the tournament to a two-year format. National federations must submit their positions by Wednesday, October 28.

CAF cited financial concerns as a reason for potentially shifting AFCON to a four-year cycle, which would necessitate another revenue-generating competition like the proposed Africa Nations League. CAF President Patrice Motsepe had previously indicated a move to a four-year cycle starting in 2028, but the debate has been reopened for direct member input.

A biannual AFCON schedule historically provides regular competitive action and player exposure, while a four-year cycle aligns with the global calendar and may reduce friction with European clubs. However, retaining the two-year cycle ensures consistent television rights, sponsorship revenue, and financial distributions for member associations.

Why it mattersEswatini's football association is participating in a CAF vote that will determine the financial and competitive future of the Africa Cup of Nations.

34 Eswatini police graduate with police science diplomas

The officers gained skills to tackle emerging crimes like cybercrime through a UNESWA and police academy partnership.

34 police officers in Eswatini have graduated with diplomas in Police Science from the University of Eswatini (UNESWA) and the Eswatini Police Academy. The graduation ceremony took place at Kwaluseni Campus on Saturday.

National Commissioner of Police Vusi Masango stated that the joint training equips officers to address evolving criminal activities driven by technological advancements. He thanked UNESWA for the partnership, calling it a step towards a more professionally equipped police service.

The police service aims to expand this partnership with UNESWA to include more academic programmes addressing other crime and policing areas. Further training opportunities are also planned to support officers' personal development and career advancement.

Why it matters34 police officers in Eswatini received diplomas in Police Science, enhancing their skills to combat modern crime.
