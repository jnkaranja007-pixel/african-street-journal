Drought kills 99 cattle in Lavumisa, Eswatini

Farmers in Maplotini say the death toll has almost doubled since the previous week as water sources run dry.

Drought has killed 99 cattle across 13 dip tanks at Maplotini in Lavumisa, and another 26 cattle were slaughtered because of their deteriorating condition. Maplotini Bucopho Sabelo Zulu said veterinary services had confirmed the deaths. The toll has almost doubled from the approximately 50 deaths reported at Maplotini, under Somntongo Inkhundla, the previous week.

Zulu said farmers had reported previously unrecorded deaths between October and November, and more cattle had died in the past week. He warned the toll could rise further after unverified reports of missing cattle suspected to have died in nearby forests. Grazing remains dry, water sources are depleted and earth dams hold little more than mud, so starving cattle walk more than eight kilometres towards Jozini in search of water.

Some animals reach dams only to become trapped in the mud and die, Zulu said. He called on government to urgently desilt, rehabilitate and repair earth dams around Maplotini and Lavumisa, saying buying water is too expensive for farmers to sustain their herds.

Why it mattersFarmers at Maplotini are losing cattle to drought and say buying water is too costly to keep their herds alive.

Eswatini businesses flout laws

Nearly seven in 10 inspected firms failed to meet legal or regulatory standards.

Government inspectors visited 3226 businesses across Eswatini and found 69.4 per cent non-compliant with laws. This means approximately 2239 businesses failed to meet legal or regulatory requirements. The inspections, which began on May 5, 2026, focused heavily on the retail sector, which accounts for 68.9 per cent of all businesses checked.

Minister of Commerce, Industry and Trade Senator Manqoba Khumalo stated on Thursday, November 12, 2026, that the exercise was not targeted at any specific group. He acknowledged that some foreign business operators may need more time to comply with Eswatini's laws, rather than facing immediate closure.

The inspections cover laws including the Trading Licences Order of 1975, the Immigration Act of 1982, and the Income Tax Order of 1975. The Ministry has not yet released a breakdown of specific offences or the number of businesses that have been closed as a result of the inspections.

Eswatini PM orders assessment of E406m electricity loss

Prime Minister Russell Mmiso Dlamini directed the Eswatini Electricity Company board to assess financial sustainability.

Prime Minister Russell Mmiso Dlamini has ordered the Eswatini Electricity Company (EEC) Board to conduct a comprehensive assessment of the utility’s financial sustainability and operational efficiency. This follows an operating loss of approximately E406 million for the financial year ended March 31, 2026. The assessment aims to find the causes of financial difficulties and identify ways to reduce costs for consumers.

Dlamini confirmed the losses in Parliament, stating that EEC recorded an operating loss of E406 million and a net loss of about E231 million. He cited high imported electricity costs, tariff under-recoveries, inflation, and other operating expenses as contributing factors. The country relies on imported electricity for about 70 per cent of its consumption, creating a significant financial burden.

The prime minister explained that EEC faced a mismatch between rising import costs and granted tariff increases. While EEC received an average tariff increase of eight per cent in 2025/26, its main supplier raised tariffs by approximately 13.24 per cent. This under-recovery contributed to the company’s financial problems, necessitating government subsidies to prevent further electricity price hikes.

Why it mattersEmaSwati face rising electricity costs due to the Eswatini Electricity Company's financial difficulties and reliance on expensive imports.

Eswatini youth delegate highlights child hunger

A 15-year-old delegate described schoolchildren fighting over food at a school visit.

Eswatini's 15-year-old child delegate, Hlelolwenkosi Nsibandze, witnessed schoolchildren fighting over a plate of rice and beans due to insufficient food at a school in a poor community. The official delegate, supported by World Vision, shared this experience to urge global governments to increase efforts against child hunger and malnutrition.

Nsibandze stated that the scene deeply affected him, making him reflect on the struggles of children with little food at home and their reliance on school meals. He noted that inadequate food impacts children's health, growth, concentration, and ability to learn, warning that child hunger remains a significant challenge in Africa and globally.

He also pointed out that only 23 African countries have policies addressing child hunger, affecting an estimated 73 million children on the continent. Nsibandze welcomed Eswatini's initiative to extend school feeding programmes to weekends, acknowledging that more action is needed to ensure children receive adequate nutrition.

Why it mattersThe event highlights the critical issue of child hunger and malnutrition affecting children's well-being and future leadership potential in Eswatini and across Africa.
