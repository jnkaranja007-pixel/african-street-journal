Eswatini finance minister admits 30,000 jobs not enough

Neal Rijkenberg said about 190,000 people are seeking work, a gap his government's current job creation plans will not close.

Eswatini's Minister of Finance, Neal Rijkenberg, told the House of Assembly on Monday that government efforts to create 30,000 jobs will not be enough to address the country's unemployment crisis. Speaking in the midterm budget report debate, he said about 190,000 people had been identified as needing employment.

Rijkenberg told MPs that unemployment and poverty are the country's two main problems, and that solving unemployment would largely take care of poverty. He said the latest employment figures showed an improvement of about two percentage points, but conceded that progress remained inadequate.

He said government is pursuing short-term projects, such as factory shells, and longer-term infrastructure projects that will take more time to deliver full employment benefits. He also said addressing unemployment would require contributions from different sectors of the economy, not only government programmes.

Why it mattersWorkers without jobs now face a longer wait, because the larger infrastructure projects that the minister expects to create better-paying work will take more time to deliver.

Eswatini sets 2030 goal to halve road deaths and injuries

Police and transport officials say the target rests on tougher enforcement, safer roads and public education, after 108 road deaths were recorded this year.

Eswatini's Ministry of Public Works and Transport has reaffirmed its aim of halving road deaths and injuries by 2030, a target tied to the Eswatini National Road Safety Strategy (2023–2030) and the United Nations Decade of Action. Between January and October 2026, the kingdom logged 108 road fatalities and 309 serious injuries.

Under Secretary Richard Phungwayo, speaking at Lobamba Christian Revival for Africa Road Safety Day and the United Nations World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims, said the ministry will work with the Royal Eswatini Police Service. A joint enforcement drive run by the Road Transport Inspectorate since August 2026 will continue through the coming Festive Season. Matsebula, representing the National Commissioner of Police, warned that falling accident numbers must not breed complacency, adding that more than 90 per cent of crashes are preventable.

Officials said the plan also depends on coordination among the Road Safety Council, the Sincephetelo Motor Vehicle Accident Fund and emergency first responders. Safer road design, clear signage, pedestrian facilities and public education with churches, traditional leaders and civil society are also being funded, while the National Road Transportation Council is introducing cashless fares and the Bika customer service app.

Why it mattersMotorists, pedestrians and families of crash victims face stricter roadside enforcement through the Festive Season, with the ministry tracking progress towards the 2030 target.

Eswatini plans more local electricity generation

Government is working on hydropower, solar and geothermal projects to cut reliance on power imported from neighbouring countries, and says regional cooperation remains essential.

Government is working on plans to produce more electricity locally and improve the country's power supply, as Eswatini seeks to reduce its dependence on electricity imported from neighbouring countries. The plans include the proposed Lower Maguduza Hydropower Station, solar power projects and the exploration of geothermal energy.

Under Secretary in the Ministry of Natural Resources and Energy Gabisile Mabuza, who represented Minister HRH Prince Lonkhokhela, outlined the plans at the 61st Southern African Power Pool (SAPP) Executive Committee Meeting in Mbabane on Tuesday, November 17, 2026. She said the Eswatini Electricity Company (EEC) had completed major substations at Nhlangano 2, Hluti, Matsanjeni and Lavumisa, along with associated 132kV transmission infrastructure under the Network Reinforcement and Access Project.

Mabuza said Eswatini cannot address its electricity challenges alone, because demand is rising across Southern Africa and mining, manufacturing and agriculture need reliable power. Electricity trading through the SAPP Competitive Market rose to approach 2000 gigawatt-hours in the financial year ending March 2026, up from less than 1000 gigawatt-hours the year before.

Why it mattersHouseholds, businesses and industries across the country depend on the new substations and planned generation to keep reliable power flowing.

Pupil, 14, drowns in Mbabane River after leaving school to swim

Police and fire rescue divers recovered the body of the Form I pupil from Hermann Gmeiner (SOS) High School several hours after the drowning was reported.

A 14-year-old pupil at Hermann Gmeiner (SOS) High School drowned in the Mbabane River at Manzana, behind the Sidvwashini Correctional Facility, at about 12:45pm after an examination, police said. The deceased, identified as Ryan, was a Form I pupil from Ngwenya, and police say he had left school with other pupils to go swimming.

Chief Police Information and Communication Officer Senior Superintendent Phindile Vilakati confirmed the incident. Community member Banele Vilakati said he and a colleague found the pupil's school uniform and shoes at the scene and entered the river to search, but failed to find him. Police divers and fire rescue personnel began the search between 4:40pm and 5pm, and recovered the body after relatives were brought from Ngwenya.

Community members questioned the delay, which police attributed to divers attending another drowning at Gebeni. Vilakati urged parents and communities to protect children from swimming in rivers, particularly during hot weather.

Why it mattersParents of pupils at the school now face a direct risk, as a teacher said pupils had previously escaped through a hole in the fence to swim in the river.

Eswatini U-23 AFCON dream ends in Algeria

Algeria defeated Eswatini 3-1 in the second leg, securing a 4-3 aggregate victory and ending Eswatini's hopes of reaching the continental finals.

Eswatini's Under-23 Africa Cup of Nations campaign concluded in Algeria after a 3-1 loss at the Stade du 5 Juillet 1962, failing to advance after holding a first-leg advantage. The team, known as young Sihlangu, had won the initial match 2-1 at Somhlolo National Stadium but could not maintain their lead.

Algeria's Akhrib scored in the eighth minute, leveling the aggregate score, before Eswatini's Sibonelo ‘Mzala’ Vilakati equalized with a free-kick in the 27th minute. However, Algeria quickly regained the lead with two goals from Akhrib in the 30th minute and Benchik five minutes later, establishing a 4-3 aggregate lead.

The defeat means Eswatini will not compete for a place in the 2028 Olympic Games in Los Angeles, nor for the tournament's reported E5 million prize. Algeria advances to the final qualifying round, with seven teams from that stage joining hosts Egypt for the 2027 AFCON tournament.

Why it mattersEswatini's U-23 football team will not compete in the Africa Cup of Nations or the 2028 Olympic Games after losing their qualifying tie to Algeria.
