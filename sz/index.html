Eswatini leaves 56 per cent of capital budget unspent at mid-year

Government spent E3.4 billion of its E7.8 billion capital allocation by the end of September, with project execution at 44 per cent.

Eswatini's government had E4.4 billion left from its capital budget at mid-year, according to the Mid-Year Budget Review. Capital expenditure stood at E3.4 billion against an approved annual allocation of E7.8 billion, leaving 56 per cent of the budget unexecuted by the end of September.

The Ministry of Finance acknowledged the need to turn allocations into timely projects and value for money. The review says the 44 per cent execution rate is a slight improvement on the same period last year, which government credits to faster project implementation and stronger monitoring. Tight cash flow conditions are also cited as a constraint across line ministries.

The ministry expects spending to pick up in the third and fourth quarters, as it usually does in the second half of the financial year. It projects capital spending to fall by an average of 5.2 per cent over the medium term, as major projects including the Mkhondvo-Ngwavuma Water Augmentation Project and the MR14 road project reach completion.

Why it mattersContractors and ministries delivering infrastructure projects face a deadline to spend the remaining E4.4 billion before the financial year closes.

Eswatini farmers test new methods to grow more food

Farmers in Shiselweni demonstrated techniques to improve production and protect crops and livestock as weather patterns change.

Farmers in Eswatini showed new farming methods and technologies at Maseyisini Inkhundla in the Shiselweni Region during the 2026 World Food Day commemorations. The field day was organised by the Government of Eswatini, the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) and development partners under the theme "Innovate today. Nourish tomorrow."

FAO Representative Rosanne Marchesich said farmers also need the knowledge and freedom to decide what works best for their communities, not only new technology. Minister of Agriculture Mandla Tshawuka said government wants more Emaswati to earn a sustainable income from farming.

The demonstrations gave participants a chance to discuss how to maintain production and protect their crops and livestock as weather patterns change. Organisers stressed cooperation among government, development partners, businesses and farming communities to help more farmers benefit.

Why it mattersSmallholder farmers, including women and young people, gain access to practical farming knowledge that can improve household food supplies and income.

Eswatini minister says local banks hold E21 billion in government bonds

Finance Minister Neal Rijkenberg says the E1.6 billion Standard Bank loan is part of a wider borrowing mix, not a sign of local refusals.

Local banks have not refused to lend to the Eswatini government, Finance Minister Neal Rijkenberg has said. Asked whether the government took the E1.6 billion Standard Bank loan because local banks had turned it down, he said local banks hold and participate in about E21 billion in Government Treasury Bill Bonds on the domestic market.

Rijkenberg said external and syndicated loans, including the Standard Bank facility, form part of a financing mix used to fund the budget deficit. He said the government is working towards reducing public debt to 45% and is cleaning up and paying off all outstanding debts.

Senators had questioned how often the government borrows, foreign currency risks, and value for money on projects such as the Sikhuphe highway. Rijkenberg acknowledged concerns about spending and corruption, saying corruption can be eliminated if proof is forwarded. He also pointed to a USD 48.44 million AfDB budget support loan and a USD 60 million BADEA loan for the Mpakeni Dam.

Why it mattersGovernment borrowing relies on a mix of domestic bonds and external loans, which affects investors holding Eswatini debt.

Algeria coach skips Eswatini media briefing before CAF qualifier

Rafik Saïfi was at the stadium for training but did not meet reporters, and CAF rules set a USD 5 000 fine for a first offence.

Algerian coach Rafik Saïfi did not attend a media briefing scheduled for Tuesday at 3pm at Somhlolo National Stadium in Lobamba. The briefing was meant to update reporters on Algeria's preparations for Wednesday's TotalEnergies CAF Africa Cup of Nations qualifier against Eswatini, a clash between the two nations' Under-23 squads.

Eswatini Football Association marketing and communications manager Muzi 'Rhoo' Radebe said an Algerian official told them Saïfi could not attend because he and the team were tired after an 11-hour journey. Saïfi was at the stadium overseeing a training session but still did not speak to the media.

CAF's Media and TV Handbook makes the pre-match press conference mandatory and requires the head coach to attend unless CAF is told in advance. A head coach who misses it faces disciplinary proceedings and a USD 5 000 fine for a first offence, rising to USD 10 000 for a second in one season. The amount is set by CAF's Disciplinary Board.

Why it mattersThe USD 5 000 fine under CAF rules applies to the Algerian Football Association, which must pay it if the Disciplinary Board imposes it.

Eswatini actor Luyanda Mali nominated for UK Handprint award

Mali, who grew up in Mpaka, also earned three nominations at Ghana's Great Excellence Movie Awards 2026 this year.

Luyanda Mali, an actor, filmmaker, director and editor originally from Mpaka in Eswatini, has been nominated for The Young Changemaker of the Year Award at the UK Handprint Awards 2026. Based in South Africa, where he studied acting at the Market Theatre Laboratory in Johannesburg, Mali runs Perfomix Media Production, an independent company that aims to produce original African stories and mentor emerging talent.

Mali recently received three nominations at the Great Excellence Movie Awards 2026 in Ghana, for Best Rising Star, Best Male Discovery and Most Promising Actor. He said the UK nomination recognises young people who challenge limitations and create opportunities through their work, not only entertainers.

He said he sees these honours from Ghana and the United Kingdom as momentum for future projects rather than a final goal. His stated ambition is to become an internationally recognised storyteller and build sustainable platforms for the next generation of talent.

Why it mattersEswatini-born creative Luyanda Mali is nominated in the UK and has earned three nominations in Ghana, according to his packet's account of his work.
