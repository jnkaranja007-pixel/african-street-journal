Home Affairs Official Found Guilty in ID Fraud Case

A Civil Service Commission ruling found an official guilty of illegally issuing an Eswatini ID and birth certificate to a South African businessman.

The Civil Service Commission (CSC) has found a Home Affairs official guilty of unlawfully processing an Eswatini birth certificate and national identity card for South African businessman Vusi ‘Cat’ Matlala. The CSC described the actions of suspended Assistant Regional Civil Registrar Nonhlanhla Malambe as a serious breach that compromised national security and border integrity.

Malambe was charged with dishonesty for registering Matlala’s birth on February 12, 2024, under the name Vusimuzi Dlamini, falsely listing Thembi Christinah Zulu as the mother. She then issued an identity card on February 20, 2024, without requiring necessary source documents. Malambe admitted to bypassing protocols, claiming she was misled by an individual she trusted.

The commission stressed the registration was a deliberate violation, not an error, noting that standard late registration procedures, including a letter from the relevant umphakatsi and verification of the informant’s relationship to the registrant, were entirely bypassed. The ruling raises concerns about the integrity of the country’s civil registration system and the potential for other fraudulent documents.

Why it mattersA Home Affairs official's conviction for issuing fraudulent identity documents to a foreign national compromises Eswatini's national security and civil registration system.

Police Probe Maize Smuggling Syndicate

Authorities have intercepted two truckloads of white maize suspected of being illegally imported from South Africa.

The Royal Eswatini Police Service, in collaboration with the National Maize Corporation, is investigating a suspected syndicate involved in smuggling tonnes of white maize into Eswatini from South Africa. Two consignments were intercepted at the Ngwenya Border Gate, with trucks detained as investigations into their origin, ownership, and documentation continue. The National Maize Corporation has intensified its monitoring and scrutiny of maize imports to protect the regulated supply chain.

Regulations under the National Agricultural Marketing Board Act and the Control of Cereals Act require permits for importing maize, with only the National Maize Corporation currently authorized to do so. Bongani Mdluli, NAMBoard Head of Legal Trade and Regulation, confirmed the interceptions, expressing concern over broken laws and stating that one case is now in court. He noted that one intercepted entity lacked a permit and failed to declare the consignment.

Mdluli suggested the two interceptions within two weeks could indicate a larger problem, potentially the "tip of the iceberg" of a smuggling syndicate. NAMBoard is reportedly tightening its regulatory measures, though specific actions will not be publicly disclosed. The illegal importation of maize can lead to fines, penalties, and seizure of goods.

Why it mattersThe investigation into suspected maize smuggling affects investors by potentially disrupting the regulated supply chain and raising concerns about illegal trade practices.

Muzi brace sinks Ally’s rebranded Amawele

Manzini Sea Birds launched their new season with a 2-0 victory over Ally Kgomongwe’s Amawele in the 8Bet Trade Fair Cup.

Manzini Sea Birds began their new season with a 2-0 win against Ally Kgomongwe’s Amawele in the 8Bet Trade Fair Cup at Mavuso Sports Centre. The victory propels Sea Birds into the quarter-finals, where they will face Green Mamba. Amawele, who are awaiting approval to change their name to Mbabane Black Eagles for the 2027/28 season, were unable to score despite creating chances.

Veteran forward Muzi Tsabedze scored both goals for Sea Birds, opening the scoring three minutes after halftime and sealing the win with his second goal 11 minutes from full time. Amawele’s Wandile Ntsakala forced a save from Sea Birds goalkeeper Ntokozo Shabangu with a long-range effort. Sea Birds’ new recruits, including Mncedisi ‘Mpotho’ Dlamini, featured in the match.

The win marks a strong start for Manzini Sea Birds, who finished as league runners-up last season. Amawele, under new ownership by Kgomongwe, showed potential but lacked the finishing touch. The match saw Sea Birds advance to the next stage of the cup competition, while Amawele were eliminated.

Why it mattersAmawele's elimination from the 8Bet Trade Fair Cup affects their chances of winning the competition this season.

Two new radio stations receive operating licences

Eswatini Communications Commission awarded free-to-air commercial radio broadcasting service licences to Sakha Live FM and Icon Connect Radio.

The Eswatini broadcasting sector is set to expand with the official handover of operating licences to Sakha Live FM and Icon Connect Radio. The Eswatini Communications Commission (ESCCOM) presented the two stations with their free-to-air commercial radio broadcasting service licences yesterday. These licences were issued following an invitation to apply published by ESCCOM in December 2025, marking a significant step in the liberalisation of the sector.

ESCCOM Acting Chief Executive Officer Ozzie Thakatha stated that the new broadcasters are expected to introduce greater content diversity, innovative approaches, and new technologies, fostering increased audience engagement and a wider range of opinions. Thakatha also highlighted the potential for economic opportunities through employment in various roles, benefiting the wider creative industry including independent producers, musicians, and advertising agencies. The stations have 12 months from the licence award date to commence operations.

Sakha Live FM Managing Director Melusi ‘Zox’ Dlamini expressed excitement about the licence, emphasizing the station's aim to be more than an entertainment platform. He stated Sakha Live will connect listeners, businesses, and young people to opportunities, while also providing a platform for diverse views within legal boundaries. Dlamini also indicated the station's intention to hold government accountable by reporting on community service delivery.

Why it mattersNew commercial radio stations are expected to create jobs and foster economic opportunities within the creative industry.

Seven Friends Place Bed in Traffic Circle, Face Court

Seven friends pleaded guilty to endangering traffic by placing a bed, pillows, and alcohol in a Mbabane traffic circle for a music video.

Seven friends appeared in Mbabane Magistrate's Court after placing a bed, pillows, and alcohol in the middle of a traffic circle. The group, charged with contravening the Road Traffic Act, admitted to unlawfully endangering themselves and other road users on August 30, 2026, along Somhlolo public road. They were found at the traffic circle with the furniture and drinks, an act that reportedly caused motorists to hoot and attracted police attention.

The accused, Samkeliso Shabangu, Bandile Gama, Siyabonga Ginindza, Lungelo Dlamini, Delisile Shongwe, Lwazi Kunene, and Owethu Lukhozi, stated they were attempting to film a music video to promote their singing careers. During mitigation, they expressed remorse, with some citing a lack of awareness regarding the need for permission and the potential dangers of their actions. Gama mentioned being an unemployed student, while Dlamini explained they were initially waiting for transport for a friend's relocation.

Magistrate Chazile Thwala cautioned the group to research such activities before undertaking them, questioning their judgment in using a bed on a public road. The magistrate remarked on the perceived recklessness of the younger generation. All seven pleaded guilty to the charge, and their apologies and explanations were heard before sentencing.

Why it mattersSeven individuals are affected by facing court charges and potential sentencing for their actions in a public road.
