Eswatini plans more local electricity generation

Government is working on hydropower, solar and geothermal projects to cut reliance on power imported from neighbouring countries, and says regional cooperation remains essential.

Source: Eswatini Positive News · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Government is working on plans to produce more electricity locally and improve the country's power supply, as Eswatini seeks to reduce its dependence on electricity imported from neighbouring countries. The plans include the proposed Lower Maguduza Hydropower Station, solar power projects and the exploration of geothermal energy.

Under Secretary in the Ministry of Natural Resources and Energy Gabisile Mabuza, who represented Minister HRH Prince Lonkhokhela, outlined the plans at the 61st Southern African Power Pool (SAPP) Executive Committee Meeting in Mbabane on Tuesday, November 17, 2026. She said the Eswatini Electricity Company (EEC) had completed major substations at Nhlangano 2, Hluti, Matsanjeni and Lavumisa, along with associated 132kV transmission infrastructure under the Network Reinforcement and Access Project.

Mabuza said Eswatini cannot address its electricity challenges alone, because demand is rising across Southern Africa and mining, manufacturing and agriculture need reliable power. Electricity trading through the SAPP Competitive Market rose to approach 2000 gigawatt-hours in the financial year ending March 2026, up from less than 1000 gigawatt-hours the year before.

Why it mattersHouseholds, businesses and industries across the country depend on the new substations and planned generation to keep reliable power flowing.
