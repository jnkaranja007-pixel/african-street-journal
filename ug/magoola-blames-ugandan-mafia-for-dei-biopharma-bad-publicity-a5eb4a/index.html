Magoola blames Ugandan ‘mafia’ for Dei BioPharma bad publicity

The company's managing director alleges unnamed interests blocked a US$430 million funding package and spread claims about its government-backed investment.

Source: The Independent Uganda · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Dei BioPharma's managing director, Dr. Mathias Magoola, told journalists at the firm's Matugga plant on Monday that unnamed international interests had blocked a planned US$430 million funding package. He said they were now spreading lies about the company's government-backed investment, which he framed as an attempt to stop Uganda making its own medicines.

Opposition leader Joel Ssenyonyi questioned the government's investment in Parliament on November 10, asking why there was no valuation report or share certificates for the company. Board chair Patrick Wakida, who said he signed the agreement, put the facility's value, including its intellectual property, at US$2.1 billion, and said the USh723 billion investment bought a 9.4% stake.

Ssenyonyi has called for an audit and a suspension of further funding until the companies account for past allocations. The Matugga facility now employs about 500 people and is projected to reach around 40,000 jobs once fully operational.
