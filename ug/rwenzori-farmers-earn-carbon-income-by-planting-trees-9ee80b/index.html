Rwenzori farmers earn carbon income by planting trees

A Kasese farmer has received USh880,000 since 2021, while agroforestry is also supporting crops and livestock on his land.

Source: The Observer Uganda · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Farmers on the slopes of Uganda’s Rwenzori Mountains are earning carbon-credit payments by planting and maintaining trees. William Ndeleghana, a farmer in Karambi village, Kasese district, has received USh880,000 since joining a tree-growing project in 2021, for trees on his 4.3 acres.

Ndeleghana says the payments have helped him pay school fees and improve his household income. Philimon Baluku Saalongo, another participant, received three payments of USh370,000 after tree assessments. He now grows coffee, bananas and cassava and keeps goats, chickens and pigs; he says trees have reduced erosion and water runoff on his land.

The Rwenzori Slopes Tree Farmers Cooperative Society has more than 2,500 members across five sub-counties and supports agroforestry, beekeeping, livestock projects and access to seedlings. It also runs a mobile money service.

Why it mattersFarmers such as Ndeleghana use the payments to meet household costs, including school fees; he has received USh880,000 since 2021.
