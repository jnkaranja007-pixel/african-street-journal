Uganda wildlife board to review tourism revenue-sharing

Chairman James Kalema says the board will assess whether communities near protected areas benefit effectively from tourism income.

Sources: Softpower News and Nile Post · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Uganda’s 11th Wildlife Authority Board of Trustees will review how tourism revenue-sharing benefits communities near protected areas, its chairman, Prof. James Kalema, said at its inauguration on Thursday, November 26. The board is also prioritising solutions to human-wildlife conflict and stronger action against poaching as it takes over oversight of the country’s wildlife resources.

Tourism, Wildlife and Antiquities Minister Col. Tom Butime inaugurated the board and urged it to ensure timely compensation for communities affected by wildlife. He said more than half of Uganda’s wildlife lives outside gazetted protected areas, making cooperation with communities important to conservation.

The board will also prepare the authority to manage additional protected areas and improve staff welfare. Kalema said visitor numbers had risen during the outgoing board’s tenure, but travel advisories linked to an Ebola outbreak in neighbouring Democratic Republic of Congo affected tourism as the peak season began.

Why it mattersCommunities near protected areas are due for a review of how tourism revenue is shared, while those affected by wildlife are owed timely compensation.
