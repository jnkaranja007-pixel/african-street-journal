Rebels from Zaire strike Kasese border towns, killing about 50

Roughly 600 fighters of the Allied Democratic Forces crossed the Mpondwe-Lhubiriha river on November 13, 1996, in the group's first major offensive against Uganda.

Source: Nile Post · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

On November 13, 1996, at about 6am, around 600 heavily armed fighters from the Allied Democratic Forces (ADF) crossed the Mpondwe-Lhubiriha river from Kasindi in eastern Zaire, now the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Within the same hour they hit Mpondwe Customs Post, Bwera township and nearby Karambi in Kasese District, swamping local defences.

The attack reportedly killed about 50 people and drove an estimated 25,000 residents out of the border corridor. As they pulled back towards Zaire after Ugandan military reinforcements arrived, the fighters carried off supplies, wrecked infrastructure and seized civilians to serve as porters and recruits.

Uganda's army, the Uganda People's Defence Force (UPDF), first sent Colonel Peter Kerim to retake Mpondwe and Bwera, with Colonel Katumba Wamala taking over soon after. Within days the UPDF crossed into Zaire, took the border town of Kasindi and struck ADF rear camps, pulling Uganda into the wider conflict later called the First Congo War.

Why it mattersResidents of the Kasese border corridor were forced to flee their homes, and civilians seized by the fighters were taken across the border as porters and recruits.
