Uganda launches $17.7 billion energy compact

The government targets 85% electricity access and 50% clean cooking by 2030 under a new energy plan.

Sources: The Independent Uganda and The Observer Uganda · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Uganda and the African Development Bank Group launched a National Energy Compact in Kampala requiring US$17.7 billion in investments over five years. The plan aims to extend modern electricity services to more than 13 million additional people by 2030. National electricity access stood at approximately 60% in 2025, with rural access at 43% and urban access at 80%.

The government plans to reach 75% rural electricity access and 95% urban access by 2030 through grid expansion, about 2,712 mini-grids, and six million solar home systems. The document notes that operational installed capacity must rise from about 2,099 megawatts in 2025 to 2,500 megawatts by the end of the decade. An additional 5,800 megawatts of generation projects are scheduled to reach financial close within the same five-year period.

The Compact seeks to mobilize approximately US$9.4 billion in private capital and forms part of Mission 300, a joint initiative with the World Bank Group. Uganda also targets 50% access to modern cooking solutions by 2030. Hydrological variability, the performance of the Karuma hydropower plant, and transmission bottlenecks are identified as constraints on reliable supply.

Why it mattersInvestors and developers must commit to a US$17.7 billion program over five years to expand electricity and clean cooking infrastructure.
