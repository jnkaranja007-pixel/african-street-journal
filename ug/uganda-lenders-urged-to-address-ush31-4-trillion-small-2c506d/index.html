Uganda lenders urged to address USh31.4 trillion small-business gap

At a Kampala meeting, financial institutions were asked to connect available investment capital with businesses that struggle to qualify for formal loans.

Source: The Independent Uganda · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Uganda's financial institutions were urged to develop new ways to finance micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs), which face an estimated USh31.4 trillion credit gap. The call came at the sixth Economic Mkutano, held in Kampala on November 17, where central bankers, pension fund managers, banks and regulators discussed how to connect capital with small businesses.

Bank of Uganda Governor Michael Atingi-Ego spoke at the event. Figures presented there showed that only 9% to 10% of micro and small enterprises have formal loans, while about 70% report unmet credit needs. MSMEs make up 90% to 95% of Uganda's businesses, employ more than 2.5 million people and contribute about 75% of gross domestic product.

Kenneth Owera, chief investment officer at the National Social Security Fund, said its USh36.7 trillion portfolio was projected to grow to USh80 trillion by 2035, and that the fund was looking beyond government securities, real estate and listed shares. Pension funds face limits on backing businesses whose accounts, governance and repayment capacity cannot be established; many small firms lack audited statements, credit histories or conventional collateral.

Why it mattersSmall businesses seeking formal credit face unmet needs, while about 70% of MSMEs report a shortfall against the USh31.4 trillion financing gap.
