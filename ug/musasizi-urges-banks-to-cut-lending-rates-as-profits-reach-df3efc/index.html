Musasizi urges banks to cut lending rates as profits reach USh2.17 trillion

The finance minister says private-sector credit must rise from 14% of GDP to 25% by 2030 to support Uganda’s US$500 billion economy ambition.

Sources: The Independent Uganda and Nile Post · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Finance Minister Henry Musasizi urged commercial banks in Kampala to lower lending rates and expand credit to businesses, after sector net profits reached USh2.17 trillion. He said rates were around 18% and called for more financing for productive sectors.

Musasizi said private-sector credit was about 14% of Uganda’s gross domestic product, below the government’s 25% target for 2030. He questioned stringent borrowing requirements despite banks’ improved performance and urged them to lend more to manufacturers, commercial farmers and technology innovators.

Bank of Uganda director Bosco Bainamazima said industry assets rose by USh9.25 trillion, or 16.7%, in the year to June 2026. Lending expanded by USh3.2 trillion, or 14%, while deposits grew by USh6.35 trillion, or 15.6%. Musasizi said digital innovation and shared infrastructure could help banks reduce operating costs.

Why it mattersBusinesses and commercial farmers borrowing at rates around 18% are being pressed to seek more affordable credit as banks reconsider lending practices.
