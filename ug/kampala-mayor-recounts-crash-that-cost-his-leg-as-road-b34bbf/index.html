Kampala Mayor recounts crash that cost his leg as road deaths rise

Uganda recorded over 5,300 traffic deaths in 2025 as officials push for automated enforcement.

Sources: The Independent Uganda and Nile Post · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Kampala Lord Mayor Ronald Balimwezo recounted losing a leg in a motorcycle crash while addressing the World Day of Remembrance for Road Crash Victims at Bwaise Transport Terminal on Sunday, November 15. Speaking alongside the Uganda Professional Drivers Network and the Road Safety Coalition Uganda, he said the tragedy pushed him into politics after residents blamed ghosts for accidents caused by potholes.

Police recorded 26,044 road crashes in 2025, according to the Uganda Police Annual Crime Report, up from 25,107 in 2024. Fatal crashes rose from 4,434 to 4,602, and deaths climbed from 5,144 to more than 5,300, averaging at least 14 a day. People under 35 accounted for 56.2% of fatalities, and males made up 83% of those killed.

The rising toll has strengthened support for the government's Intelligent Transport Monitoring System, which features automated enforcement through surveillance cameras. Robert Kisakye, a Senior Licensing Officer at the Ministry of Works and Transport, noted that Uganda has no more than 2,000 traffic officers to enforce regulations nationwide.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Works and Transport is pressing for automated camera enforcement after traffic deaths rose past 5,300 in 2025.
