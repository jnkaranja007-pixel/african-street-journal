ACCA Uganda urges accountants to tackle stigma and prepare sustainability data

At its Kampala convention, the association called for earlier environmental data collection as Uganda plans a phased rollout of international reporting standards.

Sources: Softpower News and Nile Post · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

The Association of Chartered Certified Accountants (ACCA) Uganda urged accounting professionals to confront mental-health stigma and prepare for sustainability reporting at its two-day 2026 Members Convention in Kampala, which ended at Speke Resort Munyonyo. The convention brought finance professionals, business leaders and industry experts together to discuss changing demands in the profession.

Dr Noeline Nakasujja, a psychiatry professor at Makerere University’s College of Health Sciences, said pressure to appear competent can stop professionals from acknowledging psychological difficulties. Joseph Owolabi, chief executive of Rubicola Consulting and a former ACCA president, told organisations to start collecting environmental data before reporting becomes mandatory, so they can respond to regulators, investors and customers.

Lydia Nankabirwa of the Institute of Certified Public Accountants of Uganda said a committee would act as a resource hub and develop guidance for a phased introduction of international sustainability reporting standards. Convention discussions also covered green finance, carbon markets and how financial institutions can include sustainability in lending and risk management.

Why it mattersUgandan organisations are being urged to build environmental reporting data early so they can answer regulators, investors and customers when requirements take effect.
