Judge Orders DPP to Disclose Exhibits in Shs60 Billion Payment Fraud Case

The Director of Public Prosecutions must reveal evidence against nine officials accused of manipulating payment systems, following defense arguments of trial delays.

The Director of Public Prosecutions has been ordered to disclose exhibits in the trial of nine Ministry of Finance and Bank of Uganda officials accused of causing Shs60 billion in losses by manipulating government payment systems. Anti-Corruption Division Judge David Makumbi issued the order after the prosecution requested more time to share an Auditor General’s report and two statements with the defense.

The defense, led by former Deputy Attorney General Mwesigwa Rukutana, opposed the delay, arguing that the prosecution had already missed a July 13, 2026, deadline for full disclosure. Rukutana stated that his clients, who are interdicted and earning half pay, are prejudiced by the continued partial disclosure and referred to alleged directives from the "Supreme Authority" to halt the trial and reinstate the accused.

Chief State Attorney Richard Birivimbuka stated the prosecution is committed to a speedy trial and is working to complete disclosure, while denying knowledge of any "Supreme Authority" pronouncements or the accused's interdiction. Justice Makumbi noted the defense's concerns, emphasizing that piecemeal disclosure hinders adequate preparation, risks trial by ambush, and violates the right to a fair hearing.

Why it mattersNine Ministry of Finance and Bank of Uganda officials face trial for alleged Shs60 billion fraud, with their defense arguing for a speedy resolution due to interdiction and reduced pay.

Uganda Court Orders Deportation of Three Kenyans Linked to Doctor's Murder

Three Kenyan nationals convicted of illegal entry and stay in Uganda are to be deported to face murder charges in Nairobi.

Kampala's City Hall Magistrate’s court has ordered the deportation of three Kenyan citizens who illegally entered and resided in Uganda. The individuals, identified as police constable Bett Kiplangat Collins, businessman Pius Muiru Mbugua, and Samuel Karanja Gathoni, pleaded guilty to immigration offenses on Monday. Senior principal magistrate Nicholas Aisu sentenced them to a caution and ordered their immediate return to Kenya, where they are wanted for investigations into the murder of Dr. Victoria Nthunya Mutiso.

The trio entered Uganda through the Busia border in August and were apprehended at Kyanja Heights Apartments in Wakiso district without valid immigration documents. Their arrests followed an investigation by Kenya’s Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) into the July 29 shooting of Dr. Mutiso in Nairobi. Kenyan authorities allege a Toyota Land Cruiser Prado was used to intercept the doctor's Uber before she was shot, and forensic analysis led them to the Kampala apartment.

During the court proceedings, Kiplangat and Mbugua sought leniency, citing their status as first-time offenders and frequent visitors, respectively. Karanja, however, requested deportation to face the charges in Kenya. State Attorney Mercy Yamangusho highlighted the growing concern over illegal entries into Uganda and urged a deterrent sentence, emphasizing the need for deportation to facilitate the murder investigation.

Why it mattersThree Kenyan nationals are deported from Uganda to face murder charges, impacting cross-border law enforcement cooperation.

Ugandan Entrepreneur Wins International Award for Renewable Energy Advocacy

Samuel Nabeeta received an honorary award in Spain for his work in renewable energy, innovation, and youth empowerment in Africa.

Ugandan entrepreneur Samuel Nabeeta Mulowooza has been honored with an international award for his contributions to renewable energy advocacy, innovation, and youth empowerment in Uganda and across Africa. The award was presented on Sunday, August 30, at the conclusion of the Clevenard Open Project 2026 gathering in Alicante, Spain. This event brought together global leaders, entrepreneurs, and innovators.

Nabeeta's recognition stems from his achievements in renewable energy development, entrepreneurship, financial inclusion, and economic transformation. He also served as a keynote speaker at the concurrent Global Economy, Trade, Agriculture, and Solid Minerals & Investment Summit on Friday, August 28. His presentation focused on empowering Africa's future through renewable energy and youth innovation.

During his address, Nabeeta highlighted the systemic, relational, and political gaps hindering progress, emphasizing that they are closeable. He argued that the renewable energy transition in Africa requires specific interventions, including currency risk de-risking, harmonized regional regulations, and mandatory local ownership in concessions. He noted that while Africa has high entrepreneurial activity, many small and medium enterprises fail within five years due to systemic issues rather than a lack of ambition or skill.

Why it mattersUgandan entrepreneurs and innovators are affected by the systemic, relational, and political barriers Nabeeta identified, which hinder economic growth and the success of small businesses.

Four Police Officers Dismissed Over $4 Million Gold Fraud Scam

Four police constables were dismissed from the Uganda Police Force for their alleged role in a $4 million gold fraud scheme targeting a Dubai-based businessman.

Four police officers have been dismissed from the Uganda Police Force following their alleged involvement in a $4 million gold fraud scam. Police constables Ayub Kurut, Emorat Raazak, Benon Waiswa, and Waiswa Siraje were arraigned before the Police Disciplinary Court and subsequently dismissed. They are also set to face criminal prosecution for their alleged roles in the scheme.

The scam came to light when a Dubai-based businessman reported the matter to the Minerals Police Unit. He alleged that Moses Asiimwe and associates, operating as Real Sunex Limited, defrauded him of $4 million through a bogus gold transaction. Police investigations revealed that the suspects were allegedly preparing to solicit another $4 million from the same businessman using a similar fraudulent method.

A joint intelligence operation involving the police and army led to the arrest of 16 individuals suspected of being part of the gold fraud syndicate in Kampala. Exhibits recovered include suspected fake gold, scam-related documents, and eight vehicles. Police spokesperson Kituuma Rusoke stated the dismissed officers had abandoned their posts to provide security at an illegal refinery for the syndicate, a serious breach of conduct.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the scam highlights risks in the precious minerals trade and potential for fraud, impacting confidence in transactions.

AI Will Transform Health, Warfare, and Income Growth

Artificial intelligence promises to revolutionize human existence by reducing costs and increasing productivity across key sectors.

Artificial intelligence is poised to fundamentally alter human existence by transforming how we combat disease, wage war, and stimulate income growth. AI's ability to reduce costs and boost the availability and productivity of goods and services will have a profound impact on these three historical drivers of human transformation.

In healthcare, AI could accelerate progress by improving both the quality and quantity of human life through preventive diagnostics, faster drug discovery, and personalized medical care. This could reduce the human and economic costs of non-communicable diseases like cancer and Alzheimer's, and enable faster responses to outbreaks of communicable diseases by predicting viral spread and mutations. AI is also already being used to combat antimicrobial resistance and could drastically reduce healthcare costs.

AI is also ushering in a new era of technology-driven conflict, with countries increasingly relying on drones and autonomous weapons. In Ukraine, drones account for seventy to eighty percent of battlefield casualties, and AI-powered systems are playing a growing role in conflicts elsewhere, from accelerating mine clearing to tracking submarines and gathering intelligence.

Why it mattersAI's potential to reduce healthcare costs and stimulate income growth could significantly impact Ugandan businesses and the national economy.
