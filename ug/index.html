Uganda drivers urge return of speed cameras as road deaths rise

Uganda recorded 5,383 road crash deaths in 2025, up from 5,144 the year before, and the government plans to bring back a revised automated enforcement system.

Uganda's professional drivers have called on the government to restore automated speed enforcement, arguing that traffic cameras could help cut the country's rising road death toll. The Uganda Professional Drivers Network (UPDN) and Road Safety Coalition Uganda (ROSACU) made the appeal on Sunday, November 15, at the World Day of Remembrance for Road Crash Victims at Bwaise Transport Terminal in Kampala. They asked for a credible, transparent and accountable system.

Apollo Kasule, chairperson of the Uganda Drivers' Board, said road safety should not be left to one group of people. Police recorded 5,383 road crash deaths in 2025 and 18,444 injuries. SP Michael Kananura, spokesperson for the Directorate of Traffic and Road Safety, said many crashes were preventable, citing speeding, reckless overtaking, drink-driving and distracted driving.

Apollo Kashanku, Assistant Commissioner for Road Transport Regulation and Safety at the Ministry of Works and Transport, said the government plans to reintroduce a revised Advanced Traffic Management System and reinstate the Express Penalty Scheme. The system uses cameras and number plate recognition to detect speeding and red-light running, and is meant to cover gaps left by a limited number of traffic officers, which a senior licensing officer put at no more than 2,000.

Why it mattersMotorists and enforcement will change if the revised camera system returns, with the Express Penalty Scheme reinstated to issue fines for offences the cameras detect.

Kampala traders face rent hike threats for joining URA protest shutdown

A warning circulating among traders says shops that open during a planned shutdown against the Uganda Revenue Authority will pay an extra 10 per cent in rent.

Kampala traders have been warned that anyone who opens their business during a planned shutdown on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, will have 10 per cent added to their rent. The demonstration against the Uganda Revenue Authority (URA) is being organised under KACITA Uganda and targets URA's tax administration, EFRIS implementation and customs valuation.

A message circulated among traders also thanked landlords who had reportedly agreed to back the closure. Traders who depend on daily sales to pay rent, workers and family costs say losing a day's income, plus a higher rent bill, would be a heavy burden. A woman was reportedly distributing flyers at Pentagon City urging traders to close.

Several leaders in countrywide traders' associations say they were never consulted before the demonstration was organised. The National Entrepreneurs and Traders Association (NETA Uganda) said in a statement on November 14, 2026, that it supports traders' concerns but was neither consulted nor formally invited to plan the shutdown, and has made no institutional decision to join it.

Why it mattersShop owners who keep trading on the day must weigh lost sales against a reported 10 per cent rent increase demanded by their landlords.

Six armed men rob Ugandan petrol station of Shs 14m

The robbers used a chemical substance to incapacitate four workers before stealing cash, a laptop, and a CCTV device from the Stabex station in Lamwo.

Six armed men robbed the Stabex Petrol Station in Pobel Central Cell, Lukung Town Council, Lamwo district, on Friday morning, making off with Shs 13.9 million. The assailants attacked four petrol station workers around 3:30am, tying them up after using a chemical substance to render them unconscious.

The robbers, armed with pangas, broke into the manager's office, destroyed a safe, and stole the cash, an office laptop, and a CCTV storage device. The victims, aged between 16 and 25, are Simon Peter Okema, Geoffrey Akena, Ronald Obama, and Denis Odyeki. Phillip Kipkoech, the station manager, reported the incident to Lamwo Central Police Station.

North Kyoga police spokesperson Patrick Jimmy Okema stated that investigations are underway, with officers documenting the crime scene and deploying a canine unit. No suspects or stolen property have been recovered. Police are appealing to the public for any information that could aid their investigation.

Why it mattersFour petrol station workers were tied up and rendered unconscious during the robbery, which resulted in the theft of Shs 13.9 million.

Uganda's Abuja mission asks diaspora in Nigeria to register

The High Commission wants a database of Ugandans' skills and interests to turn growing trade into investment at home.

Ugandans living in Nigeria have been asked to register with the Uganda High Commission in Abuja. Ambassador Philip Odida, Uganda's chargé d'affaires in Nigeria, made the appeal on Saturday at a celebration of the country's 64th independence anniversary, held at the mission's Chancery Gardens. It was the first community-wide gathering of Ugandans in the city in several years.

Odida said the community includes professionals, entrepreneurs, business people, academics, students and skilled workers, and pledged to listen to their concerns and help solve problems they face. The mission will use the register to record where nationals live, their skills, and their interests, identify Ugandan-owned businesses and investors in Nigeria, and link them with partners at home.

The appeal follows a Nigeria-Uganda Business Forum and Exhibition held in Kampala from September 9 to 11. Trade between the two countries rose from about $4m in 2020 to about $34m in the most recent year, according to Ambassador Charles Sentongo, head of protocol at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The mission plans regular community meetings, networking events and cultural activities.

Why it mattersUgandans in Nigeria who register can be matched with partners in Uganda and get better consular help.

Uganda army passes out platoon commanders

Officers completed intensive leadership and command training at two military schools.

The Uganda People’s Defence Forces (UPDF) has passed out officers who completed the Platoon Commanders’ Course at the School of Defence Intelligence and Security (SODIS) in Migyera and the Military Police Training School in Masindi. The ceremonies marked the completion of intensive military training aimed at strengthening leadership, command, discipline, and operational capabilities.

At Migyera, Deputy Commander Land Force Maj Gen Felix Busizoori told Intake 18/26 that training scenarios reflected field realities and stressed teamwork. He urged officers to remain vigilant, ready, and flexible, stating, “If we do not adapt, we are doomed.” Brig Gen John Patrick Otongo challenged the officers to uphold discipline, loyalty, and integrity, noting that a disciplined soldier is dependable.

Col Mike Mukula commended the UPDF leadership for investing in training infrastructure and support. Maj DL Onyadi, representing the SODIS Commandant, said the course equipped officers with professional military knowledge and skills to lead effectively in peace and war. Maj Gen William Beinomugisha presided over the pass-out in Masindi, commending officers for their resilience and commitment.

Why it mattersOfficers who completed the Platoon Commanders’ Course will now lead soldiers in military operations.
