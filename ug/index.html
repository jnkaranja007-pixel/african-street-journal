Uganda DPP disputes Shs129 million robbery claim

The Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions says the reported amount stolen from Assistant Director Alex Bagada is exaggerated.

The Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP) in Uganda has refuted media reports claiming Assistant Director Alex Bagada was robbed of Shs129 million. ODPP spokesperson Jacquelyn Okui confirmed a suspected robbery occurred on October 8, 2026, but stated the reported sum was greatly exaggerated. The incident reportedly took place at Alchemist Restaurant and Bar in Kampala, not a sauna as some outlets suggested.

Okui clarified that the actual amount of money involved is significantly less than reported and will be confirmed after the ongoing criminal investigations conclude. The ODPP also disputed claims that Bagada had a bank balance of Shs570 million, calling those figures inaccurate. The matter was officially reported to the Uganda Police Force on October 10, 2026, and remains under police investigation.

The clarification follows a report by ChimpReports alleging Bagada was drugged and robbed by an unidentified woman after an encounter. The ODPP expressed concern that media coverage has shifted focus from the criminal act to speculation. They urged the media and public to avoid comments that could prejudice the investigation, emphasizing the need for accuracy and professionalism in reporting.

Why it mattersAssistant Director of Public Prosecutions Alex Bagada was the victim of a suspected robbery on October 8, 2026.

Uganda Airlines launches direct Entebbe-Accra flights

The new route aims to boost trade, tourism, and investment between East and West Africa.

Uganda Airlines has begun direct flights between Entebbe and Accra, Ghana, creating a new air link between East and West Africa. The service operates four times weekly, aiming to facilitate trade, tourism, and investment.

Minister of Works and Transport Fred Byamukama stated the route will reduce travel time from up to 10 hours to a maximum of five hours. He highlighted the service's role in strengthening people-to-people relations and supporting continental initiatives like the Single African Air Transport Market.

State Minister for Planning Amos Lugoloobi identified potential Ugandan exports for West Africa, including coffee, tea, and pharmaceuticals. He also noted the route's potential to attract Ghanaian investment in Uganda's manufacturing and ICT sectors.

Why it mattersUgandan businesses and investors can now access West African markets more easily, and Ghanaian investors can explore opportunities in Uganda.

Uganda army chief links Rwanda exile group to ex-spy chief's arrest

General Muhoozi Kainerugaba suggests Rwanda National Congress may be retaliating for Major General Abel Kandiho's September detention.

Uganda’s army chief, General Muhoozi Kainerugaba, has publicly linked the September arrest of former spy chief Major General Abel Kandiho to exiled Rwandan General Kayumba Nyamwasa’s Rwanda National Congress (RNC). The connection was made following the beheading of former Rwandan soldier Robert Kabera in Mpigi on October 17.

General Muhoozi posted on X that he suspects RNC elements retaliated for Kandiho’s arrest, stating, “We shall get to the truth.” This marks the first time a top UPDF commander has publicly connected Kandiho and Nyamwasa in the same security matter. Police investigations into Kabera’s murder are ongoing, with no suspects named.

Exiled General Nyamwasa, living in South Africa, responded to Muhoozi’s claims, issuing a challenge to a potential conflict. General Kandiho, formerly head of military intelligence, was arrested under unclear circumstances and has been accused by Kigali of arming dissidents, allegations Kampala has denied.

Why it mattersUganda's army chief has publicly accused a Rwandan exile group of involvement in a murder, potentially escalating regional security tensions.

uganda launches culinary training to boost tourism

A three-day program in Mukono aims to transform traditional dishes into marketable visitor experiences.

Uganda's Ministry of Tourism, Wildlife and Antiquities has launched a culinary tourism training program in Mukono District. The three-day initiative brings together 50 chefs, hospitality students, food vendors, and tour operators. It aims to equip participants with skills in food preparation, presentation, hygiene, and sustainable cooking methods to package traditional dishes for visitors.

David Harongo, Curator of Educational Research at the Ministry, stated that Uganda has not fully exploited its rich and diverse local cuisine as a tourism product, focusing instead on nature-based attractions like wildlife and gorillas. The training seeks to change this by learning from countries like Egypt and Nigeria, where traditional cuisine is a key part of the tourism value chain.

The program, implemented with support from the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (COMCEC), is a pilot expected to be rolled out to other tourism hubs. Facilitators emphasize that culinary tourism involves understanding the story behind the food, from ingredient sourcing to cultural significance. This initiative comes as Uganda prepares to host more international visitors, including for AFCON 2027.

Why it mattersUganda is developing its local cuisine as a tourism product to attract visitors, create jobs, and boost local businesses.

Museveni urges Iran to build Ugandan agro-processing factories

President Museveni asked Iranian investors to process local crops like cocoa and coffee instead of buying farmland.

President Yoweri Museveni urged Iranian investors on Monday, October 26, 2026, to build agro-processing factories in Uganda. He stated that processing local agricultural commodities like cocoa, coffee, and pineapples would create jobs and boost export earnings more than acquiring farmland.

Meeting outgoing Iranian Ambassador Majid Saffar at State House, Entebbe, Museveni proposed a model where Ugandan farmers supply raw materials for foreign-owned factories. Ambassador Saffar expressed readiness to support Ugandan cooperatives and agricultural unions in processing local products to promote trade.

The discussions also covered cooperation in healthcare and education. Museveni advised Middle Eastern countries to move away from identity-based politics to solve problems.

Why it mattersUgandan farmers and agricultural cooperatives stand to gain additional markets and investment in processing facilities.
