Museveni Covers King Oyo's Medical, Repatriation Costs

President Museveni has funded the medical expenses and transport of the late King Oyo's remains from the United States to Uganda for burial.

President Yoweri Museveni has paid for the medical costs and repatriation of the late Omukama of Tooro, Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV, from the United States to Uganda. Minister for the Presidency Milly Babalanda informed Parliament that the President provided funds for medical expenses and the transportation of the remains from Dallas, Texas, via Nairobi to Entebbe. The government, in conjunction with the royal family and Tooro Kingdom, is coordinating comprehensive burial arrangements.

The late king's remains are scheduled to arrive at Entebbe International Airport on Friday, September 4, at 10 a.m., where President Museveni will receive them before they are flown to Kyegegwa. From Kyegegwa, the procession will travel by road to Fort Portal, allowing subjects to pay their respects. The remains will lie in state at Karuzika Royal Palace from September 4 until September 12, with public viewing and daily evening services.

The official burial is set for September 12, coinciding with the late king's 31st coronation anniversary. Government is providing extensive logistical, security, and protocol support for the funeral, including facilities at five key locations. Funeral management expenses, covering transportation, caskets, and the construction of a royal mausoleum, are being handled by the government through A-Plus Funeral Home.

Why it mattersThe government's financial support for the burial of King Oyo demonstrates state involvement in traditional leadership and national mourning rituals.

UNICEF Warns El Niño Rains Threaten Ugandan Children

The agency states that over two-thirds of children in the region live in areas exposed to the strengthening El Niño, with Uganda among 13 countries at risk.

UNICEF has issued a warning that the approaching El Niño rains could pose significant risks to children across Uganda. The agency highlights that over 162 million children in Eastern and Southern Africa, more than two-thirds of the total, reside in areas vulnerable to the climate pattern. Uganda is identified as one of thirteen nations where drought, extreme heat, and flooding may endanger children's nutrition, health, access to clean water, education, and overall safety.

El Niño, a natural climate pattern driven by warm sea-surface temperatures in the Pacific Ocean, is expected to disrupt rainfall and temperatures globally. In East Africa, UNICEF anticipates an increased risk of flooding during the short-rains season, with potential impacts on children continuing into 2027. These shifts can rapidly escalate into economic and health crises for families, as floods can destroy infrastructure and contaminate water, while poor rainfall can lead to reduced harvests and heightened food insecurity, particularly affecting young children's nutrition.

The agency is seeking one hundred seventy-three million dollars under its existing humanitarian appeal to support thirteen priority countries, including Uganda. UNICEF advocates for early action, such as pre-positioning nutrition supplies, strengthening disease surveillance, securing water systems, and protecting schools, noting that every one dollar invested in disaster-risk reduction can save up to fifteen dollars in future recovery costs.

Why it mattersUgandan children face risks to their nutrition, health, education, and safety due to potential drought, heat, and flooding exacerbated by El Niño.

Uber Exits Uganda After 12 Years Amidst Market Competition

The global ride-hailing company has ceased operations in Uganda and Nigeria after a thorough review of its business, impacting drivers and riders.

Uber has ended its 12-year presence in Uganda, effective Wednesday, September 2, 2026, following a review of its business. The company stated this decision is specific to Uganda and Nigeria and will not impact its operations elsewhere in Africa. Uber's immediate priority is supporting drivers, riders, and local team members through a 21-day transition period. The company launched in Uganda as the first major app-based ride-hailing service, initially connecting passengers with special-hire taxis and relying on cash payments.

The ride-hailing market in Uganda has become increasingly competitive since Uber's arrival. In March 2018, Uber introduced UberBoda, its first motorcycle ride-hailing service, directly competing with established players like SafeBoda. The market further intensified with the entry of Taxify, later rebranded as Bolt, which expanded into boda boda and delivery services. SafeBoda also entered the car-hailing market in 2022, leading to direct competition across both vehicle and motorcycle transport.

While Uber did not cite competition as a reason for its withdrawal, analysts point to market pressures, driver retention issues, and operating costs. Drivers have previously raised concerns about Uber's commission structure, with the Smart Online Drivers Association petitioning parliament in 2019 over alleged exploitative practices. Drivers also reported negotiating fares directly with passengers, bypassing the application, and some have moved to competing platforms.

Why it mattersDrivers and riders in Uganda are affected by Uber's exit, losing a platform for transport services and facing a transition period for support.

Starlink enters Uganda business market via Roke, Paratus deal

The satellite internet service will now be available to Ugandan companies through a new partnership aimed at improving connectivity in underserved areas.

Starlink connectivity services have arrived in Uganda's enterprise market through a new partnership between Roke Telkom and Paratus Uganda. This collaboration introduces an additional option for businesses seeking reliable, high-speed internet, particularly in locations where traditional fiber and wireless networks are insufficient or absent. The service leverages Starlink's Low Earth Orbit satellite technology to serve companies with dispersed operations or those aiming to bolster their existing internet infrastructure.

Edwin Kyambadde, Country Manager for Paratus Uganda, stated that the launch addresses persistent connectivity challenges faced by businesses increasingly reliant on stable internet access. He emphasized that the partnership will focus on understanding specific customer operational needs to tailor connectivity solutions, rather than merely selling internet packages. The enterprise offering is expected to benefit sectors including agriculture, tourism, mining, education, healthcare, and government agencies.

Roke Telkom CEO Peter Muhumuza noted that the partnership aligns with evolving business demands for integrated digital solutions. He explained that businesses now require connectivity that complements their wider digital infrastructure, including network integration, cybersecurity, and cloud services. Under this agreement, Roke and Paratus will combine Starlink's satellite internet with these advanced enterprise services to offer comprehensive connectivity solutions.

Why it mattersUgandan businesses now have a new high-speed internet option, especially those in remote areas or with multi-location operations, to improve their digital infrastructure and services.

Vipers SC adds new players for CAF Champions League campaign

The Ugandan club has signed new goalkeepers, defenders, and midfielders to strengthen their squad for the upcoming continental tournament.

Vipers SC is set to begin their Caf Champions League campaign on Saturday, September 5, against Mauritanian side Nouadhibou FC. Following a recent league loss, the club has been active in the transfer window, aiming to build a competitive squad under French coach Denis Lavagne. Key acquisitions include Gabonese international goalkeeper Loyce Mbaba, Nigerian defender Princewill Biweribo, and midfielder Joel Sserunjogi.

The club's recruitment drive addresses perceived weaknesses, particularly in goalkeeping following the departure of Alfred Mudekereza and defensive lapses in previous matches. The signing of Mbaba is intended to provide reliable protection, while Biweribo bolsters the backline alongside Kevin Dasylva. The team also anticipates contributions from forwards Farouk Miya and the returning Caesar Manzoki, though Manzoki's current fitness is a concern.

These strategic signings aim to improve Vipers SC's performance in the lucrative group stages of the Caf Champions League. The club hopes to replicate past successes, such as their 2022 run that saw them eliminate TP Mazembe. The new additions are expected to bring depth and quality across various positions, from midfield control to attacking prowess, as the team prepares for the challenges of continental competition.

Why it mattersVipers SC players and staff are affected by the new signings which aim to improve team performance in the CAF Champions League.
