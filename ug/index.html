Uganda minister urges patience as DRC border stays shut

Dr Charles Ayume said border closure is vital to protect Ugandans from Ebola, despite calls for reopening.

Uganda's Minister for Primary Health Care, Dr Charles Ayume, asked for public patience as the country maintains its border closure with the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC). The border was shut on May 27, 2026, due to an escalating Ebola outbreak in eastern DRC. Security forces have reinforced the border, but some Congolese nationals continue to enter Uganda through unofficial routes, seeking work, trade, or services. On November 6, 10 Congolese nationals were arrested inside Echuya National Park for illegal entry.

Ayume stated on Thursday at Katuna Border that the Ministry of Health's priority is safeguarding Ugandans from public health threats, not inconveniencing travellers. He warned that reopening the border while Ebola remains active in the DRC could trigger a new outbreak in Uganda and strain its health system. The minister also called for greater investment in thermal imaging cameras at major border crossings to detect suspected cases.

Uganda was declared Ebola-free in July after an outbreak that began in May and resulted in 20 confirmed cases and two deaths, with 15 linked to DRC imports. The DRC has reported over 10,600 confirmed cases and 5,100 deaths by November 3, according to the World Health Organisation (WHO). Uganda and Rwanda recently conducted joint cross-border health preparedness exercises to strengthen their response to public health emergencies.

Why it mattersThe border closure and health screening measures directly affect travellers and traders seeking to cross between Uganda and the DRC.

EACOP outlines community investments as Uganda nears first oil

The East African Crude Oil Pipeline company says it is funding water, schools and health projects along the pipeline corridor, while it still struggles to restore land cleared.

The East African Crude Oil Pipeline (EACOP) says its work now centres on lasting community benefits and environmental responsibility as Uganda moves closer to first oil. Deputy Managing Director John Bosco Habumugisha made the case at the 4th Annual Joint Civil Society Conference organised by the Petroleum Authority of Uganda, held under the theme "Advancing Sustainability in Uganda's Oil and Gas Sector".

Habumugisha said the company is funding water solutions, solar energy for schools, support for girls' education and tree planting, alongside school Green Clubs. Among ongoing projects, a Health Centre II in Bunyoro is being upgraded to Health Centre III to improve maternal and child health services. EACOP is also training engineers with the China University of Petroleum, the Petroleum Authority of Uganda and Mbarara University.

EACOP Environmental Engineer Charity Tushemereirwe named the reinstatement of the pipeline right-of-way and wetlands among the project's most complex outstanding challenges. She said temporary access routes built during construction are now used by communities, which makes closing them difficult.

Why it mattersFamilies along the pipeline corridor are left with construction access routes they now rely on, and the company must restore that land while keeping those routes open.

Dfcu Bank offers up to Shs40,000 cashback on VISA spending

Cardholders can earn up to 8% back on eligible purchases, and discounts of up to 20% apply to flights and online shopping until December 31, 2026.

Up to Shs40,000 is available to dfcu VISA cardholders for every complete Shs500,000 they spend, through a festive campaign that runs until December 31, 2026. Eligible spending at any merchant point of sale that accepts dfcu cards earns the cashback, which works out to as much as eight per cent back on purchases.

Isaac Bigirwa, dfcu Bank's Head of Cards and Payments, said the end of the year is when households and travellers spend most, so customers can earn value on purchases they were already planning. Cardholders can also get up to 20 per cent off online shopping and on flights with Emirates, Qatar Airways and Ethiopian Airlines.

Groceries, fuel, dining, retail, hospitality, travel and online purchases are all covered. The campaign, called "Pay. Save. Get More.", is aimed mainly at existing cardholders who use their cards only occasionally.

Why it mattersHolders of dfcu VISA cards who spend through the end of the year can recover part of their outlay, and travellers booking with three named airlines can cut their fares.

Politics · 2026-11-14

Muhoozi promotes CID Director Magambo to Acting Lt Col

The promotion lifts Tom Magambo, who was appointed to lead the police's Criminal Investigations Directorate in 2022, into an army rank.

Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba, the Chief of Defence Forces, has elevated Criminal Investigations Directorate (CID) Director AIGP Maj Tom Magambo to acting lieutenant colonel. Col Chris Magezi, acting director of Defence Public Information at the Ministry of Defence and Veteran Affairs, made the announcement.

Before leading CID, Magambo spent two decades at the Internal Security Organisation (ISO) from 2002, reaching chief intelligence officer and director of analysis. He later coordinated the Eastern Africa Fusion and Liaison Unit, which links Uganda with Ethiopia, Kenya, Sudan, South Sudan, Somalia, Tanzania, Djibouti, Burundi and Rwanda.

President Yoweri Museveni named him CID director on January 25, 2022, succeeding AIGP Grace Akullo, who had held the post for nearly a decade. Magambo had never been a police officer before that appointment, and Museveni regularised him as an AIGP in April 2022.

Why it mattersMagambo now holds both a police command and an army rank, a combination that ties the country's top detective office to the military leadership.

Security · 2026-11-14

ADF stages first major attack on Uganda from Zaire

Around 600 fighters crossed from eastern Zaire at dawn and struck Kasese District, killing about 50 people in the first assault and driving an estimated 25,000 residents.

On November 13, 1996, about 600 heavily armed fighters from the Allied Democratic Forces (ADF) crossed the Mpondwe-Lhubiriha river from Kasindi in eastern Zaire, now the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and attacked communities in Kasese District from around 6am. The rebels hit the Mpondwe Customs Post, Bwera township and nearby Karambi at the same time, overwhelming local defences in one of the most serious breaches of Uganda's western border at the time.

Around 50 people were reportedly killed in the first assault, and an estimated 25,000 residents abandoned the border corridor. Fighters carried off supplies, damaged infrastructure and seized civilians to serve as porters and recruits. They pulled back towards Zaire once Ugandan military reinforcements arrived.

The ADF had been formed in late 1995 by merging anti-government groups, including former members of the National Army for the Liberation of Uganda and radical Tabligh Islamist factions, and was led politically by Jamil Mukulu, a former Catholic turned Tabligh cleric. Within days the Uganda People's Defence Force crossed into Zaire, took the border town of Kasindi and attacked ADF rear camps.

Why it mattersResidents of Kasese District's border towns were forced from their homes, and the Ugandan army moved across the border to strike the rebels' bases.
