King Oyo's Fast Sparked Museveni's Weight Loss Remark

The Tooro King's 2014 protest fast over territory division drew a blunt response from President Museveni, highlighting enduring tensions.

In July 2014, Omukama Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV of Tooro Kingdom announced a six-day fast to protest the creation of new cultural institutions in Kasese and Bundibugyo, which he viewed as a division of his ancestral territory. The move followed the formal recognition of Obusinga Bwa Rwenzururu and Obudhingiya Bwa Bwamba, areas historically under Tooro. King Oyo's officials stated the fast would express displeasure over the lack of consultation and the delayed return of kingdom assets.

President Yoweri Museveni, who had served as one of King Oyo's guardians since 1995, publicly commented on the planned fast, remarking that it would help the king "shape up and be healthy." This statement provoked anger in Fort Portal, leading to demonstrations and arrests. Tooro Kingdom officials demanded an apology, asserting that the President's words undermined the monarchy.

The six-day fast concluded without the new institutions being dissolved, though demands for the return of kingdom assets persisted, eventually leading to a 2019 Memorandum of Understanding. Despite the public exchange, Museveni maintained his role as guardian to King Oyo. The 2014 fasting incident remains a notable moment of tension between the Tooro throne and the central government during the king's reign.

Why it mattersThe event highlights a past conflict between the Tooro Kingdom and the central government over territorial integrity and cultural institutions, affecting the kingdom's autonomy.

Stanbic Bank Funds Marathon for Maternal Health Services

The second annual Independence Marathon, themed "Save a Mother," will raise funds for maternal health at Mengo Hospital.

Stanbic Bank Uganda is sponsoring the upcoming Independence Marathon, an event aimed at reducing maternal mortality. The October 4 race, themed "Save a Mother," will direct its proceeds to enhancing maternal health services at Mengo Hospital. Diana Ondoga, manager for Corporate and Social Investment at Stanbic Bank Uganda, stated that the partnership highlights sport's capacity to unite communities for national causes. The marathon is expected to draw participants from various groups, including professional and amateur runners, corporate teams, and the general public.

The race will commence and conclude at Mandela National Stadium, utilizing the Northern Bypass for a professionally managed and timed course. Uganda's maternal mortality ratio has decreased to 207 deaths per 100,000 live births, according to the 2024 National Population and Housing Census, a significant drop from 336 deaths per 100,000 in 2016. This initiative seeks to further improve these statistics by strengthening healthcare services.

Health professionals and advocates are urging public support for the cause. Dr. Frank Kakuba of Mengo Hospital encouraged voluntary blood donations, a sentiment echoed by safe-motherhood advocate Daniel Lubwama Kigozi, also known as Navio. Both emphasized the critical need for blood donations to assist mothers experiencing severe bleeding during childbirth, a common cause of maternal mortality.

Why it mattersUgandan mothers facing childbirth complications are affected by the initiative to strengthen maternal health services and increase blood donations.

Attorney General Warns Against Expressway Contract Termination

Uganda risks costly international arbitration if the contract for the Kampala-Entebbe Expressway operator is terminated without following proper procedures, the Attorney General.

The Attorney General has cautioned against the immediate termination of the private operator's contract for the Kampala-Entebbe Expressway, warning that Uganda could face expensive international arbitration if contractual procedures are not followed. Deputy Attorney General Jackson Karugaba Kafuuzi presented this warning to Parliament as legislators reviewed recommendations from an inquiry into the expressway's management and toll collection.

Parliament's Committee on Physical Infrastructure called for the termination of French company EGIS Road Operation S.A.'s contract, which began on May 24, 2021, and is valued at Shs122.86 billion. The committee cited incomplete installations of traffic monitoring systems, inadequate lighting, and shortcomings in weigh-in-motion systems as justification for transferring operations to the Ministry of Works and Transport and toll collection to the Uganda Revenue Authority.

However, Kafuuzi advised the Ministry of Works and Transport to conduct a thorough assessment to determine if EGIS's alleged failures meet the contractual threshold for termination. He emphasized that the operator must first be formally notified of any breaches and given 14 days to rectify them, with liquidated damages and payment reductions applicable for delayed works before termination can be considered.

Why it mattersInvestors and government officials are affected by the potential for costly international arbitration and the need for a thorough assessment before terminating the expressway operator's contract.

Court Clears Extradition of Ugandan Soldier to US for Drug Charges

A former UPDF soldier will face trial in the United States for cocaine trafficking, illegal firearms, and supporting a cartel.

Michael Katungi Mpeirwe, a former soldier in the Uganda People's Defence Force, has been committed to Luzira prison by the Buganda Road Chief Magistrates Court. This action awaits a decision from the Minister of Justice and Constitutional Affairs regarding his extradition to the United States. Katungi is wanted in the US to stand trial on charges including cocaine trafficking, illegal firearms possession, and providing material support to a Mexican drug cartel designated as a foreign terrorist organization.

Chief Magistrate Ritah Kidasa Neumbe dismissed all preliminary objections raised by Katungi, ruling that the Ugandan government met the legal threshold for committal under the Extradition Act. The US seeks Katungi for an alleged international drug-trafficking and weapons conspiracy, facing three counts in the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia. These charges involve conspiring to distribute cocaine, possess firearms for drug trafficking, and provide support to the Cartel de Jalisco Nueva Generación.

Katungi was initially brought before the court on June 29, 2026, following an arrest warrant requested by the US government. His legal team challenged the extradition process, citing procedural issues, the authority of officials, diplomatic immunity claims, and the admissibility of US evidence. The court, however, rejected these objections, paving the way for the extradition process to proceed.

Why it mattersA former Ugandan soldier is to be extradited to the US to face serious drug and terrorism-related charges.

Uganda Youth Face Jobless Future Despite Education Investments

Millions of young Ugandans struggle to find meaningful work due to skills mismatches and weak school-to-work transitions, despite significant national and international investment.

Uganda's large youth population, with nearly 78 percent under 30, presents a significant demographic dividend, yet economic participation remains constrained. Each year, approximately 700,000 young Ugandans enter the labor market, but only about 200,000 secure formal employment. Young women, refugees, and rural youth face the most substantial barriers to finding work.

Despite extensive investments in education, entrepreneurship, and digital skills development, these efforts alone are insufficient to unlock the country's demographic potential or foster inclusive growth. Experts suggest that developing skills must be coupled with creating environments that integrate new ideas with institutional knowledge and shared leadership.

The challenge lies not in a lack of aspiration among young people, who seek quality education and decent work, but in economies failing to connect generations and transform potential into productivity. This disconnect poses a growing economic risk for the nation.

Why it mattersYoung Ugandans are affected by a failure of the economy to create sufficient jobs and integrate their potential into productive work, leading to widespread unemployment.
