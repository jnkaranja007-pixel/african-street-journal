Four pupils die in suspected poisoning at Kampala school

Police are investigating the deaths of four girls aged nine to 11 after they fell ill with stomach complications.

Police in Kampala are investigating the deaths of four pupils from Ta-Alim Primary School and Quran Memorisation Centre in Kisasi, who fell ill with stomach complications and were found to have suspected poisoning. The dead are 11-year-olds Mariam Nakitende, Rwahim Kiruta and Nasuna Nusufa, and nine-year-old Nabeta Failuzi. Police have closed the school and declared the premises a crime scene.

Kampala Metropolitan Police spokesperson Rachel Kawala said detectives would visit the village where oranges the pupils reportedly ate had come from. Police medical teams are conducting postmortem examinations at Mulago City Mortuary to establish the exact cause of death. The school director, Musa Suna, told local leaders the pupils had eaten oranges shared by a 12-year-old P.4 pupil from Wakiso district.

The illness began after one pupil complained of stomach pain on Monday night and was given paracetamol, and her condition worsened as other pupils showed similar symptoms. The school director and dormitory matron first took the affected pupils to a nearby clinic before parents began collecting their children. Some parents said doctors suspected food poisoning, but the family of one child said the school administration had not explained what happened.

Why it mattersParents of the affected pupils are waiting for the postmortem results and an explanation from the school administration about what their children ate.

Health · 2026-11-13

UAE reopens visa applications for Ugandans after Ebola curbs

Ugandans may apply for UAE visas again, provided they have spent at least 21 days outside Uganda before travelling, the emirate's authorities announced on Wednesday.

Ugandans can apply for United Arab Emirates visas again. The UAE announced on Wednesday, November 11, that it has lifted the suspension it imposed in June 2026, when Ebola concerns led it to halt new visas for Ugandan nationals. Applicants must have spent at least 21 days outside Uganda before travelling to the Emirates.

The revised measures were announced by the National Emergency Crisis and Disaster Management Authority, working with the Ministry of Health and Prevention and the Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security. Ugandan students who enrolled in UAE institutions before June 2026, along with their families, may return under approved health procedures, as may administrative staff at the Ugandan Embassy and their dependants.

Uganda declared the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak over on July 28, 2026, after health authorities stopped local transmission, and the Ministry of Health says the country remains Ebola-free. Humanitarian and medical cases will still be assessed one by one, and patients travelling for treatment must supply a diagnosis, the name of the treating facility and a start date. Ebola continues to spread in the Democratic Republic of Congo, which had recorded 11,042 confirmed cases and 5,387 deaths by November 10.

Why it mattersUgandan students who enrolled in UAE institutions before June and their families can return, and medical travellers need approved reports before departure.

Ruparelia seeks Shs889bn from dfcu and backers in London court

The claimants say the 2016 Crane Bank sale was tainted by an unvalued transaction and an alleged $27.5 million bribe.

Ugandan billionaire Sudhir Ruparelia and other former Crane Bank shareholders are seeking at least €200 million, about Shs889 billion, in damages from dfcu Bank and its backers in London's Commercial Court. The trial opened on 5 October 2026 and is expected to run for 12 to 16 weeks.

The claimants allege the Bank of Uganda's 2016 seizure of Crane Bank and the January 2017 transfer of its business to dfcu, for Shs200 billion, was part of an unlawful, corrupt scheme. They say the deal had no independent valuation and that an internal late-2016 email shows $27.5 million from a loan portfolio sale going to the central bank unrecorded. dfcu rejects all allegations, saying the money repaid emergency support.

The London case follows a string of Ruparelia victories against the Bank of Uganda in Ugandan courts. A 2019 parliamentary inquiry chaired by Abdu Katuntu criticised the central bank for breaking the law on several points, and a parallel dispute is now before Parliament.

Uganda ranks 23rd of 56 Commonwealth countries on youth AI readiness

A Commonwealth study says Uganda scores 47.0 out of 100, below its readiness benchmark of 53, and that AI could add US$899 million to its GDP over a decade.

Uganda ranks 23rd out of 56 Commonwealth countries in the inaugural 2026 Commonwealth Youth AI Readiness Index, scoring 47.0 out of 100. The index, produced by the Ayre Think Tank, was unveiled on the sidelines of the Commonwealth leaders' summit in St John's, Antigua and Barbuda, from 1 to 4 November. It found Uganda is marginally behind Kenya, which ranks 22nd with 48.7 points, and ahead of Tanzania at 25th with 45.5.

The study estimates artificial intelligence could increase Uganda's GDP by US$899 million over the next decade, provided it creates conditions for young people to benefit. Uganda has 13.4 million people aged 15 to 29. The Uganda Bureau of Statistics' 2025 Labour Market Survey puts youth unemployment among people aged 15 to 24 at 17.9%, with young women at 21% and young men at 15.2%.

UBOS estimates that 42.6% of people aged 15 to 24, about four million people, are outside employment, education or training. Among those aged 18 to 30 the proportion rises to 51%.

Why it mattersYoung women in the 15 to 24 age group face a 21% unemployment rate, and about four million young people are outside work, education or training.

Politics · 2026-11-13

Museveni challenges African creators on historical missions

Ugandan President Museveni met content creators from five African nations to discuss promoting Africa's history and identity.

President Yoweri Museveni has urged African content creators to champion five historical missions for the continent's transformation: liberation, democracy, economic prosperity, strategic security, and African unity. He met a delegation of creators from Ghana, Zambia, Tanzania, Kenya, and Uganda at State House, Entebbe, on Thursday. Museveni stated that understanding societal challenges is key to finding solutions, drawing parallels between patients and doctors.

The President explained that Africa possesses strong foundations for wealth creation, including agriculture, craftsmanship, and indigenous knowledge, alongside a rich cultural heritage and linguistic diversity. He noted that historical divisions, poor governance, and conflicts weakened the continent, offering lessons for present-day leaders and the youth on unity and cooperation. The first mission discussed was liberation from colonial rule, with the formation of movements like the ANC in 1912 cited as a milestone.

Museveni also highlighted democracy as the second historical mission, emphasizing the importance of governance systems reflecting the political will and interests of African people. He invited the creators after watching their discussions on African history, culture, and languages, seeing an opportunity to share historical knowledge to enrich their storytelling about the continent. The delegation included creators such as Berthold Kobina Ackon from Ghana and Daniel Wambua Ndambuki from Kenya.

Why it mattersPresident Museveni has tasked African content creators with promoting the continent's history and identity through their platforms, affecting how Africa's narrative is shaped.
