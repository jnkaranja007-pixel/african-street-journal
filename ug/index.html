Uganda needs Shs118 trillion for climate action

Bank of Uganda partners with European Investment Bank to manage climate risks and attract investment.

Uganda requires approximately Shs118.22 trillion for climate action by 2030, according to figures released on Wednesday. The Bank of Uganda (BoU) and the European Investment Bank (EIB) signed a cooperation agreement in Kampala to strengthen the financial sector's capacity to handle climate-related risks and draw in investment.

The deal will provide technical assistance to the central bank to create a roadmap for greening the financial sector, improve climate-risk disclosure, and enhance reporting by financial institutions. BoU Deputy Governor Prof. Augustus Nuwagaba stated the country must build resilience to climate risks without limiting access to finance.

The partnership will also support the practical use of Uganda’s National Green Taxonomy, launched in September 2025, which defines sustainable economic activities. EIB Vice-President Marko Primorac said the agreement aims to turn climate ambitions into practical tools and boost market confidence.

Why it mattersUganda needs Shs118.22 trillion by 2030 for climate action, and the Bank of Uganda is partnering with the European Investment Bank to attract necessary investment.

Museveni asks EU investors to boost Uganda manufacturing

President Museveni urged European investors to expand in manufacturing, energy, and infrastructure to create jobs and grow markets.

President Yoweri Museveni has called on European investors to increase their presence in Uganda's manufacturing, energy, agriculture, and infrastructure sectors. He met with European Union ambassadors and investors at State House Entebbe, emphasizing that productive investment is key to job creation and economic growth. Museveni highlighted Africa's large and growing population as a significant opportunity for European businesses seeking new markets.

The President stressed Uganda's need for investments that boost domestic production and strengthen trade ties with Europe. He welcomed infrastructure projects that connect Uganda to wider markets, stating they benefit both investors and the nation. Museveni also identified the cost of electricity as a barrier for Ugandan manufacturers and called for attention to electricity tariffs, railway transport, and access to financing.

Museveni urged the European Investment Bank to increase funding for strategic infrastructure, including railways and electricity. He also pointed to the Uganda Development Bank as a resource for businesses focused on expanding production and creating jobs. The government reaffirmed its commitment to tax incentives aimed at attracting investment, particularly in manufacturing.

Why it mattersUgandan President Museveni is seeking European investment to grow manufacturing, energy, and infrastructure, aiming to create jobs and expand markets.

Uganda MPs query Shs41.6 billion diverted by justice ministry

Parliament's Public Accounts Committee found Shs41.6 billion spent without authorisation, contravening the Public Finance Management Act.

Uganda's Parliament has questioned the Ministry of Justice and Constitutional Affairs and the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions (ODPP) for diverting Shs41.6 billion. The funds were reportedly used for activities outside approved work plans without authorisation from the Finance Minister, Henry Musasizi. The Public Accounts Committee, chaired by Hon. Patrick Nsamba, stated this expenditure violated the Public Finance Management Act.

Committee findings, presented on Wednesday, 21 October 2026, revealed the ODPP spent Shs900 million on constructing offices in Katakwi and Yumbe, while the Ministry of Justice diverted Shs40.7 billion. Nsamba noted that savings do not grant authority for unapproved spending, with much of the money allocated to court awards and compensation. The ODPP also faces scrutiny for paying Shs2.95 billion to Graphics Systems for system maintenance without key deliverables being met.

The report highlighted that the ODPP has filled only 600 of its 1,704 approved positions, leading to heavy workloads for prosecutors. The committee recommended enforcing contract liability provisions, recovering funds, and auditing the Prosecution Case Management Information System (PROCAMIS), which has seen Shs42 billion spent since 2017 with reported malfunctions. The Ministry of Justice reported contingent liabilities of Shs3.492 trillion, largely from lawsuits.

Why it mattersUgandan taxpayers' money was spent without proper authorisation by government entities responsible for justice.

Dettol Hygiene Quest expands to 750 Ugandan schools

Over 650,000 pupils will receive hygiene education and handwashing skills in the programme's third phase.

The Dettol Hygiene Quest initiative will reach an additional 650,000 pupils in 750 Ugandan schools through its third phase, launched in 2026. The programme, a partnership between Dettol, the ministry of Education and Sports, and CHIL Hygiene Centre, aims to teach proper hygiene practices to prevent infections.

Organisers stated that the initiative has already reached over one million children in 1,500 schools across Uganda, including in Gulu, Kampala, and Isingiro. Reckitt's country manager for Uganda, Bornface Shaka Onyari, said the new phase will help schools sustain the hygiene practices introduced.

The programme uses an AI-powered chatbot called Keti to support teachers and assess learning outcomes. Aisha Sekindi, representing the state minister for Water, commended the effort but stressed that success should be measured by behavioural change and facility sustainability, not just the number of schools reached.

Why it mattersOver 650,000 Ugandan pupils will benefit from improved hygiene education and practical skills to prevent infections.

Museveni commissions $53 million Russian military repair facility

The new facility in Jinja aims to boost Uganda's capacity to maintain military equipment locally.

President Yoweri Museveni commissioned a $53 million military repair facility at Magamaga Cantonment in Jinja District on October 21, 2026. The facility, along with training infrastructure and marine security equipment, was provided through Uganda's defence cooperation with Russia. This development is intended to enhance the Uganda People’s Defence Forces' (UPDF) ability to service military vehicles and equipment within the country.

During the commissioning, President Museveni thanked Russian President Vladimir Putin and a Russian delegation led by Lt Gen Aleksandr Kshimovskii for the military assistance. He expressed a desire for Uganda to develop domestic manufacturing capacity, asking, “What if we made them here with the Russians?” The President stressed the importance of learning from defence partners to reduce reliance on imported equipment and foreign technical expertise.

The two-day engagement, which concluded on October 22, 2026, also included the launch of a digital multimedia training classroom for the BRDM-2MS armoured reconnaissance vehicle at the Mechanised Warfare College in Kalama. In Entebbe, marine operational assets and supporting infrastructure were commissioned, including surveillance radar and eight boats, to strengthen the UPDF’s marine operational capacity.

Why it mattersUganda's military will now have enhanced local capacity to maintain and repair its equipment, reducing reliance on foreign services.
