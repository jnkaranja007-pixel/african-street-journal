EACOP opens latrine and gives sports gear to Kabonera

The pipeline company also donated footballs, jerseys, and netball posts to the community.

The East African Crude Oil Pipeline (EACOP) Ltd. has opened a new four-stance drainable pit latrine block in Kabonera Village, Kyotera District. The facility includes a rainwater harvesting tank and aims to improve public health and sanitation for communities and schools.

EACOP also handed over sports equipment for football, netball, and volleyball, including goalposts, nets, jerseys, and balls. Matred Murungi, EACOP’s Social Economic Investment Manager, stated the initiative supports the company's Social Economic Investment programme under the Community Health and Safety pillar.

The handover ceremony in Kabonera village was attended by local leaders, including Resident District Commissioner Mr. Apollo Mugume. Mugume praised the project for nurturing young talent and promoting unity, commending EACOP for its corporate citizenship.

Why it mattersThe Kabonera community in Uganda received new sanitation facilities and sports equipment from EACOP, improving hygiene and recreation.

Health · 2026-11-02

C-Care and DFCU Bank expand health insurance

The partnership aims to make comprehensive medical cover more accessible to individuals and families across Uganda.

C-Care Uganda and DFCU Bank have partnered to increase access to quality healthcare using Blue Care Insurance. This initiative combines DFCU Bank's financial network with C-Care's 21 healthcare facilities to reach more Ugandans. The partnership was announced at C-Care's Customer Appreciation Dinner in Kampala on October 30.

Blue Care Insurance, launched this year, offers comprehensive medical cover for individuals aged 18 to 70, with annual premiums starting at UGX 1 million. Arthur Kintu, Head of Bancassurance at DFCU Bank, stated that the bank will leverage C-Care's community presence to distribute the medical product. C-Care serves over 700,000 patients annually.

Sheila Aboth, Head of Brand and Client Experience at C-Care, noted that patient feedback from the dinner would guide improvements in healthcare services. Azhar Sundhoo, CEO of C-Care East Africa, emphasized that patient input should lead to tangible enhancements in care delivery.

Why it mattersUgandans seeking medical treatment will have greater financial protection against healthcare costs through this new insurance offering.

Politics · 2026-11-02

Museveni warns MPs on digital number plate report accuracy

President cautions legislators against inaccurate findings in the controversial digital number plates project.

President Yoweri Museveni has cautioned members of parliament investigating Uganda’s controversial digital number plates project against producing inaccurate reports. The warning, disclosed by the permanent secretary in the Office of the President, Yunus Kakande, follows a State House meeting last Thursday where legislators raised concerns about the project’s rising costs and contractual arrangements with Russian firm Joint Stock Company Global Security (JSCGS).

Kakande stated that Museveni recognised parliament’s oversight role but advised legislators against reaching conclusions without adequately engaging the technical teams implementing the project. The parliamentary investigation, led by the Physical Infrastructure Committee chaired by Mbarara City South MP Mwine Mpaka, has examined allegations of shortcomings in the vehicle-tracking technology.

The digital number plates initiative followed Museveni’s 2018 directive to strengthen vehicle identification and tracking after a series of high-profile assassinations. The government entered into an agreement with JSCGS in 2021 to implement the Intelligent Transport Monitoring System (ITMS), with motorists paying Shs 714,300 for new digital plates.

Why it mattersMembers of Parliament are investigating the digital number plates project, which has escalating costs and contractual issues.

Health · 2026-11-02

Uganda launches e-waste collection drive

Health Minister Chris Baryomunsi announced plans for city cleanliness competitions and waste-to-value initiatives.

Uganda's government has started a campaign to collect electronic waste and plans cleanliness competitions for cities and districts. The initiatives aim to improve waste management and sanitation across the country. Health Minister Dr. Chris Baryomunsi announced these plans on Saturday, October 31, 2026, during the National Cleaning Day in Lira City.

The campaign encourages households to separate e-waste from other garbage for proper handling and recycling. Baryomunsi stated that the government is developing arrangements to convert waste into useful products, such as turning organic waste into fertilizer and recycling plastics. He also revealed plans for a competition among Ugandan cities, with awards for the cleanest areas, to be extended later to districts and communities.

Baryomunsi urged citizens to integrate cleanliness into their daily routines, not just rely on monthly national exercises. Government Chief Whip Dr. Jane Ruth Aceng also encouraged residents to sustain cleaning efforts beyond the national campaign, emphasizing that making every day a cleaning day contributes to a healthier nation.

Why it mattersUgandan citizens are directly affected by the government's new waste management and sanitation initiatives, including e-waste collection and cleanliness competitions.

Ugandan comedian Kapere dies in Buddo

Popular comedian Yokana Mbuuse, known as Kapere Lufotose, died on Saturday, November 1, 2026, at a medical facility in Buddo.

Popular Ugandan comedian Yokana Mbuuse, known as Kapere Lufotose, died on Saturday, November 1, 2026, at a medical facility in Buddo, Wakiso District. News of his death emerged on social media and was confirmed by entertainment journalist Kasuku and former presidential candidate Robert Kyagulanyi Ssentamu, also known as Bobi Wine.

Kapere rose to national prominence with the Amarula Family comedy group, known for his self-deprecating humour and distinctive stage presence. He also featured in Bobi Wine's music video for the hit song 'Mr. Money'. Bobi Wine described Kapere as a man who brought happiness during difficult moments and loved his country.

Kapere, a father of three, had spoken about his difficult childhood and a childhood hernia condition. In recent years, he advocated for Ugandan entertainers facing economic challenges, highlighting financial instability and limited booking opportunities in the comedy industry.

Why it mattersThe death of comedian Yokana Mbuuse, known as Kapere, affects fans and fellow entertainers who found happiness in his performances and who heard his advocacy for artists' economic challenges.
