Uganda AI growth risks leaving youth behind

A new study shows artificial intelligence could boost Uganda's GDP by $899 million, but access to technology remains a barrier for young entrepreneurs.

Uganda's economy could grow by an additional US$899 million over 10 years through artificial intelligence, according to a new study. The Ayre Think Tank's Ayre Index, released on November 5, 2026, found that Uganda's youthful population could drive this growth. However, millions of young people risk being excluded due to unaffordable internet access, electricity, and necessary technology.

The study ranks Uganda 23rd out of 56 Commonwealth countries for youth AI readiness with a score of 47 out of 100, below the benchmark of 53. Researchers estimate AI could increase Uganda's GDP by US$899 million after 10 years under a moderate scenario. This potential economic gain depends on expanding access to technology and equipping the population with the skills to use it.

Currently, only 55.3 per cent of Uganda's population has electricity access, and fewer than half of young people report using the internet. High mobile data costs further restrict access, particularly for poorer households. This disparity risks benefiting businesses already connected to infrastructure while leaving smaller enterprises in less-connected areas behind.

Why it mattersYoung Ugandans who could drive economic growth through AI risk being excluded due to a lack of affordable internet, electricity, and technology.

Stanbic Uganda wins Titans award for reporting

The company received a Gold award in the Titans Category at the 2026 Financial Reporting Awards for sustained excellence.

Stanbic Uganda Holdings Limited (SUHL) received a Gold award in the Titans Category at the 2026 Financial Reporting (FiRe) Awards in Kampala. The awards, organised by the Institute of Certified Public Accountants of Uganda (ICPAU) and its partners, promote excellence in financial reporting, corporate governance, and sustainability disclosure. This year's ceremony focused on "Driving Transparent Sustainability Reporting through Technology and Innovation."

The Titans category honours institutions with a legacy of sustained excellence in corporate reporting. SUHL earned this distinction by accumulating eight Gold Awards across various categories since the FiRe Awards began in 2011. Mark Ocitti Ongom, Chief Executive of SUHL, stated that strong reporting builds stakeholder trust and confidence in a listed entity.

Ronald Makata, Chief Finance and Value Management Officer at SUHL, highlighted that credible reporting is central to maintaining confidence among shareholders, regulators, and investors. The FiRe Awards Committee noted that organisations recognised as Titans have demonstrated the ability to compete regionally and won at least three Gold accolades. The awards ceremony was held on November 4, 2026.

Why it mattersStanbic Uganda Holdings Limited's recognition as a Titan at the FiRe Awards highlights its consistent high standards in financial reporting, impacting stakeholder trust and confidence.

Wendi rewards digital transactions with Shs10,000 minimum

Pearl Bank's mobile wallet Wendi launched a campaign offering fuel vouchers, smartphones, and cash prizes for transactions.

Pearl Bank's mobile wallet, Wendi, has started a weekly rewards campaign for customers making digital transactions of at least Shs10,000. The campaign, named Wendi Ku Win, began on November 6, 2026, at Kampala’s Old Taxi Park. Prizes include fuel vouchers, smartphones, household appliances, and cash.

The initiative, launched in partnership with the Uganda Taxi Operators Federation (UTOF), aims to encourage digital financial services adoption. Andrew Kabeera, Pearl Bank’s Executive Director, stated the campaign rewards both new and existing users. Customers can use Wendi for payments, transfers, savings, and government collections.

Uganda's active mobile banking users grew by 16 per cent to 2.5 million between March 2024 and March 2025, according to a Bank of Uganda report. Transaction values increased by 42.4 per cent to Shs14.2 trillion in the same period. Wendi also offers a savings product with a 12.5 per cent annual interest rate.

West Nile hosts 800km cycling race

The Race The Nile Uganda Ultra from November 23 to 27, 2026, will traverse 13 districts.

An 800-kilometre cycling race, the Race The Nile Uganda Ultra, will take place across 13 districts in West Nile from November 23 to 27, 2026. The five-day event, starting and ending in Arua City, aims to boost tourism and create business opportunities for local communities. Organisers expect 50 participants, including 14 international cyclists who have already registered.

Arua City Senior Tourism Officer Wathum Fred stated the race will showcase West Nile’s tourism potential as cyclists pass through various communities. Route architect Michael Adriko explained the race is self-supported, requiring participants to source food, accommodation, and other necessities from local vendors and mechanics along the route. The winner will have the unique opportunity to name a white rhino at Ajai Wildlife Reserve, an incentive coordinated by the Uganda Wildlife Authority.

The race is incorporated into the national tourism calendar and is planned to become an annual event. Cyclists will travel from Arua towards Koboko, then Yumbe, Moyo, Obongi, Adjumani, Madi-Okollo, Pakwach, Nebbi, and Zombo before returning to Arua. Organisers anticipate the event will also contribute to ecosystem restoration and promote the cultural diversity of West Nile.

Why it mattersLocal communities in 13 West Nile districts will benefit from increased business and tourism opportunities during the five-day cycling race.

Mwanyi Terimba seeks UGX 5 billion for coffee expansion

The Ugandan coffee processor plans to use the funds for purchasing, exports, and new processing facilities.

Uganda’s Mwanyi Terimba Limited is seeking UGX 5 billion (US$1.24M) to expand its coffee purchasing and export operations. The company also plans to invest in processing facilities as it moves further into value addition. The funding would finance purchases, exports, and processing infrastructure.

Godfrey Ssebaggala Lusiba Sebaka, the company’s Vice-Chairperson, stated that the capital is needed to strengthen operations and support expansion. He noted that purchasing enough coffee for one 20-foot container costs about UGX 300 million (US$74.15K), straining the company’s finances. Sebaka added that financing from banking institutions often comes with high interest rates that the company cannot easily afford.

Mwanyi Terimba is working with Uganda Development Bank and exploring partnerships to secure affordable working capital. Buganda Prime Minister Charles Peter Mayiga urged the company to promote Buganda-grown coffee domestically before expanding international sales. Uganda earned US$2.4 billion from coffee exports between December 2024 and December 2025, with total exports reaching 8.7 million 60-kilogramme bags.

Why it mattersMwanyi Terimba Limited requires UGX 5 billion to expand its coffee purchasing, export, and processing operations.
