NSSF, businessman Nzeyi settle 18-year land dispute in Wakiso

The National Social Security Fund and Amos Nzeyi have agreed to a settlement regarding 55 acres of land in Temangalo, Wakiso District, after nearly two decades of disagreement.

The National Social Security Fund (NSSF) and businessman Amos Nzeyi reached a settlement on Monday, August 24, 2026, to resolve an 18-year land dispute in Temangalo, Wakiso District. The agreement, mediated by Parliament’s Committee on Commissions, Statutory Authorities and State Enterprises (COSASE), involves Nzeyi transferring 10 acres of alternative land to NSSF and purchasing the remaining 45 acres at current market value.

Under the terms, Nzeyi will retain his country home, paddocks, and pasture on the 45 acres, with the title reverting to him after the purchase is complete. The 10 acres of alternative land have been assessed as suitable for NSSF. The 45 acres will be valued by both private valuers and the Chief Government Valuer, and the transaction is subject to NSSF’s internal approval processes and other statutory requirements.

The dispute originated in March 2008 when NSSF purchased approximately 463.87 acres from Nzeyi and Arma Limited. A Memorandum of Understanding at the time stipulated Nzeyi would retain about 104.88 acres, including his farmhouse, in exchange for providing alternative land to NSSF. This land swap was never fully completed, leading to years of arbitration and negotiations that narrowed the dispute to 55 acres.

Why it mattersThis settlement affects NSSF members by potentially recovering the value of disputed land, and businessman Amos Nzeyi by allowing him to retain his home and surrounding property.

Museveni forgives former speaker Among, orders release from house arrest

President Yoweri Museveni has reportedly directed the release of former speaker of parliament Anita Annet Among from house arrest and will not prosecute her.

President Museveni's son and Chief of Defence Forces (CDF) Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba claimed today that Museveni instructed him to release Among and that the State would not prosecute her. This decision marks a dramatic turn for Among, who faced investigations into alleged corruption, illicit enrichment, abuse of office, and money laundering.

Among was placed under house arrest in May after joint security agencies searched properties linked to her in Nakasero, Kigo, and Bukedea. The investigations also extended to entities associated with her, including a radio station and school in Bukedea, and she was forced to step aside from the race for the speakership of the 12th Parliament.

The investigations centered on allegations of corruption, illicit enrichment, money laundering, and abuse of office, examining her wealth, property acquisitions, and financial dealings. Among was linked to “service awards” reportedly paid to commissioners in 2022 and 2023, with individual payments ranging between Shs1.4 billion and Shs1.7 billion.

Why it mattersThis decision directly affects Anita Among, who will no longer face prosecution or house arrest, and impacts the public perception of accountability for high-ranking officials.

Uganda completes 42-day Ebola monitoring, awaits travel restriction changes

Uganda reached the end of the World Health Organization's enhanced monitoring period for Ebola on August 27, following no new locally transmitted cases since July.

Uganda officially completed its 42-day enhanced monitoring period for Ebola on Thursday, August 27, as confirmed by the World Health Organization. This milestone follows the discharge of the last imported Bundibugyo virus disease case on July 16 and the country's declaration of the outbreak over on July 28 after no new cases were reported during the required monitoring period. The outbreak recorded 20 confirmed cases and two deaths, with 18 patients recovering.

The completion of this monitoring period does not automatically lift the various travel restrictions, entry measures, and enhanced screenings imposed by several countries since May. Countries like the United States, Canada, the Bahamas, Jordan, and Bahrain introduced measures affecting travelers from Uganda, some of which also targeted individuals from the Democratic Republic of Congo and South Sudan. By May 28, Africa CDC Director-General Dr. Jean Kaseya reported that 15 countries had implemented some form of travel restriction.

While the WHO provides public health guidance, individual governments ultimately decide their travel conditions. The economic impact of these restrictions is significant, affecting airlines, hotels, tour operators, and businesses reliant on international visitors. Uganda's continued vulnerability to reintroduction due to the ongoing outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, which has recorded 4,665 confirmed cases and 2,184 deaths, remains a key consideration for countries reviewing their measures.

Why it mattersAirlines, hotels, tour operators, and businesses that depend on international visitors in Uganda are directly affected by the potential lifting or continuation of travel restrictions.

Kampala taxis evade crackdown, return to roads at night

Dilapidated commuter taxis, locally known as Gganyegenya, reappeared on Kampala roads late in the evening after largely avoiding a daytime police operation targeting unfit.

Commuter taxis in dangerous mechanical condition returned to Kampala roads by 8pm on Monday, following a daytime crackdown by the Directorate of Traffic Police. Drivers of these old and worn-out taxis largely stayed off main routes during the day, leaving vehicles in parking lots or using shortcut routes to evade the police operation, which focused on major roads within the Kampala Metropolitan Area.

The operation, directed by Transport Minister Fred Byamukama, lost momentum by 3pm, with deployed traffic police winding down. By 8pm, city roads were again filled with the prohibited taxis, many in worse condition than those impounded earlier. Taxi conductors openly loaded passengers with reduced fear due to the decreased police presence.

Dan Ngabonziza, a taxi driver, stated he began working around 7pm after keeping his vehicle parked all day, noting that most vehicles are acquired on loans, making it difficult to keep them off the road. He appealed to the government to reconsider the drive against old taxis, warning it risks leaving families without income, as transportation is their only source of income.

Why it mattersTaxi drivers and their families are directly affected by the crackdown, as it impacts their ability to earn income from their primary source of livelihood.

Uganda Cabinet approves Shs 139bn French loan for Mbarara cancer centre

The decision to borrow €31.98 million from the French government aims to decentralize cancer services and ease pressure on the Uganda Cancer Institute in Kampala.

Uganda's Cabinet has approved plans to borrow up to €31.98 million, approximately Shs 139 billion, from the French government. This financing is designated for the construction and equipping of a regional oncology centre in Mbarara, western Uganda, as part of efforts to bring specialized cancer care closer to patients.

The new Mbarara Regional Oncology Centre is expected to alleviate the burden on the Uganda Cancer Institute (UCI) in Kampala, which currently handles most specialized cancer diagnosis and treatment. This move aims to reduce the need for long-distance travel, prolonged stays, and additional costs for patients from western, northern, and other parts of the country.

According to ICT and National Guidance minister Justine Kasule Lumumba, the project is also intended to expand access to cancer diagnosis, strengthen referral systems, and support prevention, community sensitization, and early detection in the region. This initiative is part of Uganda's broader efforts to enhance health infrastructure and achieve universal health coverage.

Why it mattersPatients in western Uganda will gain closer access to specialized cancer diagnosis and treatment, potentially reducing travel and financial burdens.
