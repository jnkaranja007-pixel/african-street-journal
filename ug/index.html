Uganda Urges Refugees to Respect Laws Amidst Unrest

Authorities call for refugee compliance with Ugandan laws and public order following riots and property destruction in West Nile.

Refugees and asylum seekers in Uganda's West Nile region are being urged to understand and adhere to Ugandan laws to foster peaceful coexistence and prevent conflicts. This initiative follows recent riots at Imvepi Refugee Settlement, which resulted in fatalities and significant property damage. West Nile hosts over 1.7 million refugees, primarily from South Sudan and the Democratic Republic of Congo, spread across several districts and urban centers.

Francis Ayume, Executive Director of Refugee Network-RedNet, stated that many refugees are unaware of their rights and obligations, making them susceptible to legal violations. He emphasized that refugees are entitled to documentation, fair treatment, social services, and the ability to open financial accounts, provided they meet legal requirements. Sustained awareness campaigns are deemed crucial for refugees to grasp the laws governing their stay and their duties to the government and host communities.

Moses Batali, chairperson of the South Sudanese Refugees Association in West Nile, highlighted that refugees must register with the Office of the Prime Minister, undergo security screening, and report life changes. He cautioned against activities threatening national security or public order, noting that those employed must pay taxes. Urban refugees have also raised concerns about higher school fees compared to local learners.

Why it mattersRefugees are affected by needing to understand and follow Ugandan laws to avoid conflict and legal issues.

Uganda Coffee Seedlings Survive Drought

Sixty percent of coffee seedlings distributed in Agago District have survived nearly four months of dry weather, defying earlier fears of total crop loss.

About six in every 10 coffee seedlings distributed to farmers in drought-hit Agago District have survived, despite nearly four months of dry weather that threatened the new coffee crop. The survival rate comes after intense sunshine killed many of the 290,000 seedlings distributed, with some farmers struggling to keep the young plants alive. Nicholas Okino, the regional coffee extension coordinator at the Ministry of Agriculture, stated that recent monitoring visits showed reports of entire crop loss were inaccurate, with an average of 60 percent surviving.

Survival rates varied significantly based on garden preparation and seedling management during the drought. Some farmers kept seedlings under trees, while others planted them before the dry spell intensified. Okino attributed better survival in some gardens to proper preparation, including digging planting holes in advance and caring for seedlings during the dry spell. He urged farmers to prepare gardens before collecting seedlings for next year.

The survival of thousands of seedlings offers a rare positive development in a district badly affected by the prolonged dry spell, which destroyed food crops and affected livestock. District Chairperson Wilson Otto highlighted the vulnerability of farmers dependent on rainfall and called for investment in irrigation systems to allow continuous food and livestock feed production during dry periods.

Why it mattersFarmers in Agago District are affected by the survival rate of coffee seedlings, which impacts their potential income and recovery from drought.

African delegates clash over migrant worker protections in Gulf

Disagreements over safeguards and accountability marked the second Doha Dialogue on Labour Mobility in Dar-es-Salaam.

Delegates from African nations and the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) convened in Dar-es-Salaam for the second Doha Dialogue on Labour Mobility, aiming to translate principles into concrete protections for African migrant workers. Discussions centered on recruitment transparency, wage security, skill recognition, and safer migration corridors. However, African representatives sought stronger safeguards, leading to disagreements over the extent of protections and the wording of the final declaration, with some accusing Tanzania of undermining the African Union's position by pursuing bilateral agreements with Qatar.

The negotiations highlighted the persistent challenges of worker mistreatment, opaque contracts, and exploitative recruitment practices, often involving collusion between employers and agencies in origin countries. The bargaining power imbalance between African nations seeking jobs and remittances and Gulf economies needing labor was evident. One delegate expressed frustration, stating, "The Doha Dialogue is dead," reflecting a sentiment of stalled progress despite commitments from bodies like the African Union and Tanzanian officials.

Human trafficking, particularly of women, was identified as a significant challenge by Tanzanian officials. The dialogue underscored the complex dynamic where the pursuit of employment opportunities can leave workers vulnerable to abuse and exploitation. The African Union Commission reiterated its commitment to dialogue, while Tanzanian officials emphasized the need for practical steps and skills development for workers migrating to the Gulf.

Why it mattersAfrican nations and their citizens are affected by the difficulty in achieving concrete protections for migrant workers seeking employment in Gulf countries.

Ugandan Businessman Urges Italian Investment in Local Processing

Louis Kimuli called for Italian industrialists to establish coffee, milk, and mineral processing facilities in Uganda to boost the economy.

Ugandan businessman Louis Kimuli is urging the government to attract Italian investors to develop local processing capabilities for coffee, milk, and minerals. Kimuli stated that Uganda should move beyond exporting raw materials and instead capture more value by processing and packaging products domestically. He believes leveraging strong trade links with Italy can bring the necessary industrial expertise, technology, and capital to achieve this.

Speaking at the Uganda–Italy Business and Investment Forum in Rome, Kimuli emphasized that bringing processing capacity to Uganda would create jobs for youth and increase economic returns. The forum, themed ‘Unlocking Shared Prosperity,’ brought together officials and private sector representatives to explore partnerships. Key requirements for attracting investors, as noted at the forum, include reliable electricity, affordable financing, suitable infrastructure, and predictable regulations, alongside meeting European market standards.

Uganda exported 8.8 million 60-kilogramme bags of coffee, earning $2.4 billion in the year ending April 2026. Italy is Uganda’s largest coffee buyer, accounting for approximately a quarter of these exports. The Uganda Free Zones and Export Promotion Authority presented a 1,031-square-metre production facility in Entebbe to Italian investors for value addition targeting European Union markets.

Why it mattersUgandan youth and the national economy are affected by the potential for new jobs and higher returns from domestic processing of export products.

Ugandan Entrepreneur Builds Tech Business Solo

Andrew Kasi, known as 256 Genuine Gadgets Kampala, established a successful electronics business in Uganda through self-reliance.

Andrew Kasi, 31, has built a reputation for authentic tech sales in Uganda, operating independently from his base in Pioneer Mall. Known online as 256 Genuine Gadgets Kampala, Kasi chose a path of solitary effort in a market where partnerships are common. His business philosophy is rooted in the integrity and reputation emphasized by his father, Andrew Mubiru, during his upbringing in Naguru.

Kasi’s journey began with a deep curiosity about how machines function, leading him to pursue a Diploma in Electrical Engineering. This technical background provided him with expertise, differentiating him from merchants who simply buy and sell electronics. He aimed to be a master of his trade, understanding the intricacies of the technology he deals with.

After gaining experience in international trading hubs like the United Arab Emirates, Kasi returned to Uganda with a mission to redefine authenticity in the local tech market. His success, evidenced by a significant online following, demonstrates the viability of his independent approach and commitment to genuine products.

Why it mattersUgandan entrepreneurs are affected by the success of individuals like Andrew Kasi who build businesses independently, offering a model for navigating competitive markets.
