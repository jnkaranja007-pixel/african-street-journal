URA operation bullet kills 13-year-old boy in Busia

A UPDF soldier attached to the Uganda Revenue Authority enforcement team fired a shot that killed Dominic Wejuli, aged 13, inside his home.

A 13-year-old boy, Dominic Wejuli, died in Busia district on April 11, 2026, when a stray bullet struck him during a Uganda Revenue Authority (URA) operation. URA enforcement officers were attempting to seize suspected smuggled goods from Kenya stored at a shop in Buhobe trading centre, Bulumbi Sub-county.

Residents reportedly became rowdy and confronted the URA team, attempting to prevent the confiscation of goods. Moses Johnson Mugwe, Bukedi South Police spokesperson, stated that a UPDF soldier fired to disperse the crowd, and a bullet hit Wejuli inside his house. Eyewitness Muhammed Ntalo accused URA of using excessive force and endangering residents.

Harriet Nekesa, the deceased's sister, confirmed Wejuli was a primary four pupil. Resident David Wandera urged URA to review its procedures and prioritize civilian safety, calling for government intervention. Teacher David Ouma also appealed for justice for the bereaved family.

Why it mattersA 13-year-old boy was killed by a stray bullet during a URA operation to impound smuggled goods.

Roke Telkom marks 20 years of Ugandan ICT services

The telecommunications company celebrated its anniversary with the Uganda Communications Commission chief in attendance.

Roke Telkom launched its Roke@20 celebrations in Bugolobi, Kampala, marking two decades of operation in Uganda's telecommunications and ICT sector. The event, held at Roke House, was attended by Uganda Communications Commission (UCC) chief Nyombi Thembo and other industry stakeholders.

Founded by Ugandans, Roke Telkom began with a goal to make internet access more available to underserved communities and businesses. Chief Executive Officer Peter Muhumuza stated that the company has grown from internet connectivity to providing comprehensive ICT solutions that meet evolving needs.

Nyombi Thembo commended Roke Telkom for its contributions to Uganda's digital transformation and ICT landscape. He highlighted the importance of homegrown companies in expanding connectivity and supporting businesses within the country's digital ecosystem.

Why it mattersThe company's 20-year journey in providing ICT solutions contributes to Uganda's digital development.

Uganda launches Shs694 billion water project for Greater Kampala

The project will draw water from Lake Victoria to supply over one million people in Wakiso and Mpigi districts.

The Ugandan government has launched a Shs694 billion water and sanitation project to provide safe drinking water to over one million people in Wakiso and Mpigi districts. The Wakiso West Water and Sanitation Project, managed by the National Water and Sewerage Corporation (NWSC), will draw water from Lake Victoria at Bwerenga. It aims to supply areas including Kyengera, Nsangi, Kajjansi, Kasanje, Kiringente, Wakiso Town Council, and Mpigi Town Council.

NWSC Managing Director Dr. Silver Mugisha stated the project addresses the growing demand for water in the Greater Kampala Metropolitan Area, which has seen persistent supply challenges due to rapid population growth. The project will increase water production to 62.5 million litres daily and includes 94.2 kilometres of transmission pipelines and 78.5 kilometres of secondary pipelines. Consultants NIRAS A/S and Fichtner GmbH & Co. KG, along with Ugandan firms M&E Associates Ltd and Alliance Consultants Ltd, will handle design and supervision.

The project will also install 941 community water dispensers and yard taps with prepaid meters in informal settlements, benefiting approximately 160,000 residents. This initiative comes as communities like Kyengera have faced intermittent supply, with some households resorting to buying water at Shs1,000 to Shs2,000 per jerrycan.

Why it mattersOver one million residents in Wakiso and Mpigi districts will gain reliable access to safe drinking water, improving public health and reducing reliance on expensive alternatives.

Agriculture · 2026-11-05

Uganda agricultural body seeks private partnerships

NARO plans to commercialise research facilities like processing plants and mills to boost revenue.

The National Agricultural Research Organisation (NARO) in Uganda is exploring public-private partnerships to generate revenue and ensure financial sustainability for agricultural research. The organisation held a top management meeting on November 4, 2026, at Speke Resort Munyonyo to review proposals for commercialising its assets and facilities. These partnerships aim to attract private sector investment, expertise, and technology to maximise the use of NARO’s existing infrastructure.

NARO Director General Dr. Yona Baguma stated that the organisation needs to harness the capacity of its facilities to generate additional resources for research and contribute to national revenue. Identified facilities for commercialisation include a dairy processing plant and livestock feed mill at NaLIRRI, a fish feed mill at NaFIRRI, and a feed sorting facility at Mbarara ZARDI. Hostel facilities at NaFIRRI and MuZARDI are also part of the plan.

Dr. Baguma noted that while government funding remains primary, NARO must diversify its sources as development partner support declines. He highlighted that NARO's annual budget has increased from UGX 61 billion to UGX 223 billion over the past decade, but additional financing is needed. An existing initiative, licensing improved crop varieties, already generates over UGX 1 billion annually.

Why it mattersUgandan farmers and the agricultural sector will benefit from increased investment in research and development of new crop varieties and processing technologies.

Climate · 2026-11-05

Uganda Development Bank commits UGX 200 million to COP31

The bank will sponsor the Ugandan Pavilion for the second year, aiming to boost climate action and attract investment.

Uganda Development Bank (UDB) announced its commitment to climate action by sponsoring the Ugandan Pavilion at the upcoming Conference of the Parties (COP31) in Antalya, Türkiye, with UGX 200 million. This marks the bank's fourth consecutive participation in the annual UN Climate Conference. The announcement came during a Pre-COP31 National Forum held in Kampala.

Minister of State for Environment Beatrice Anywar stated that the forum was key to consolidating Uganda's priorities in seven negotiation areas, including adaptation, mitigation, and climate finance. She emphasized the need for reliable water sources and predictable weather forecasts for Uganda's agricultural sector. UDB's Director of Economic Research and Knowledge Management, Dr. Francis Mwesigye, highlighted the sponsorship as an investment in showcasing Uganda's climate investment potential.

UDB has established a dedicated Climate Finance Facility (CFF) and Climate Finance Unit, mobilizing over USD 200 million to date. Launched in 2023 with UGX 50 billion in seed capital, the CFF aims to finance bankable climate investments, assess risks, and support projects contributing to emission reduction and economic resilience.

Why it mattersUganda's farmers will benefit from efforts to secure reliable water sources and predictable weather forecasts amidst climate change threats.
