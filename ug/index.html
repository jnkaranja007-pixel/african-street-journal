US delivers experimental Ebola treatment to Uganda

The United States has provided Uganda with an experimental antibody treatment for Ebola, bolstering the nation's preparedness for potential cross-border outbreaks.

The U.S. Embassy in Uganda has delivered an experimental antibody treatment for Ebola, known as MBP134, to the Ministry of Health. The consignment, received on August 27, is available for immediate use should any new cases be detected. This donation strengthens Uganda's preparedness and response capabilities, particularly in light of the ongoing Bundibugyo virus disease outbreak in the neighboring Democratic Republic of Congo.

Dr. Charles Ayume, Minister of State for Health, received the treatment, describing it as a critical safeguard for Ugandan families. The delivery follows a U.S. delegation's visit from July 11-15, which assessed Uganda's Ebola response, regional health security, and humanitarian needs. The delegation toured border areas, Entebbe International Airport, and the Uganda Virus Research Institute to evaluate disease detection and surveillance systems.

The U.S. government emphasized the importance of coordination between Uganda and the DRC in managing cross-border health threats. The donation supports U.S.-backed efforts to enhance Uganda's capacity to prevent and respond to infectious disease outbreaks, especially given the proximity of communities along the shared border.

Why it mattersUgandan families are directly affected by the immediate availability of experimental Ebola treatment to safeguard against potential cross-border outbreaks.

Tyre Burst Kills Five in Napak Truck Crash

A truck carrying passengers and cows overturned on the Moroto-Soroti highway after a front tyre burst, killing five occupants.

Five people died and several others were injured Saturday evening when a truck overturned on the Moroto-Soroti highway in Napak district. The crash occurred around 5:30 pm in Lodoon village, Lorengechora sub-county, when the vehicle, registration number UBH 543U, travelling from Moroto to Soroti, experienced a front tyre blowout.

Police reported that the sudden tyre failure caused the driver to lose control, leading the truck to veer off the road and overturn multiple times. The five occupants who died were pronounced dead at the scene. Others sustained injuries and were transported for medical treatment. The truck was reportedly carrying both passengers and cattle.

The deceased's bodies were taken to a mortuary for postmortem examinations. Police have initiated an investigation into the precise cause of the accident. The incident highlights the risks associated with transporting passengers and livestock in a single vehicle on rural highways.

Business · 2026-08-31

Thieves Steal Livestock and Crops, Threatening Ugandan Wealth Creation

Criminals are targeting farms and herds across Uganda, leading citizens to abandon livestock and crops due to mounting losses.

Widespread theft of livestock and harvested crops is undermining national wealth creation efforts in Uganda. Criminals are stealing cows, goats, and produce from farms, forcing some citizens to abandon animal husbandry and other agricultural ventures. The problem is so severe that some farmers are now hiring armed private security guards to protect their properties at night, while those unable to afford such measures are losing their livelihoods.

In a recent operation in Mbarara, police apprehended three individuals found with meat from 39 stolen cows and 10 goats. The suspects were intercepted while loading the carcasses into vehicles in Kageeti, Sanga Town Council. The audacity of the thieves is highlighted by the alleged theft of cows from President Museveni's own ranches, with some slaughtered animals bearing his initials, YKM. This trend is prevalent in Uganda's cattle-keeping regions, where thieves use small vehicles to bypass roadblocks.

The escalating crime is attributed to lenient laws that treat such offenses as minor. This environment allows robbery to thrive, directly impacting the economic progress championed by various government programs aimed at poverty reduction. The theft of livestock and crops is a significant existential threat to the economic status of Ugandans, who are increasingly discouraged from investing in agriculture and animal rearing.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected as their livestock and crops are stolen, leading to financial losses and discouraging them from continuing their agricultural activities.

Education · 2026-08-31

Ministry Clears Masaka SS Headteacher Nabukeera of Misappropriation Allegations

The Inspectorate of Government found insufficient evidence to sustain charges against Zulaika Nabukeera, who has now been reinstated.

The Ministry of Education and Sports has rescinded the interdiction of Masaka Secondary School headteacher Zulaika Nabukeera, allowing her to resume her duties. The Inspectorate of Government (IGG) found insufficient evidence to support allegations of misappropriation of funds, irregular recruitment, and abuse of school resources against her. A letter dated August 27, 2026, from the permanent secretary formally reinstated Nabukeera.

The investigation followed accusations that Nabukeera procured a digital notice board, constructed toilets, and was purchasing a school bus without proper procedures. However, the IGG's report dated July 8, 2026, indicated that some procurement irregularities occurred during the tenure of the former acting headteacher, Ahmed Mukiibi. Mukiibi had overseen the construction of toilets for Shs 60 million without board approval or adherence to procurement processes.

While Nabukeera oversaw the procurement of a digital notice board and two toilet structures, which were approved by the school board, they were also undertaken without following required procedures. Nabukeera cited weaknesses in the school's procurement unit. Corrective measures have since been implemented, including hiring a procurement officer and strengthening the contracts committee.

Why it mattersHeadteacher Zulaika Nabukeera was cleared of allegations, allowing her to return to her administrative role at Masaka Secondary School.

News · 2026-08-31

Uganda Police Aid Kenya in Arresting Murder Suspects

Three individuals, including a police constable, were apprehended in Uganda in connection with the killing of Dr. Victoria Mutiso.

Detectives have arrested three more suspects linked to the killing of Dr. Victoria Nthunya Mutiso, including a serving police constable apprehended in Kampala. The Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) described the arrests as a significant breakthrough, made during a coordinated operation in Nairobi and Uganda's capital.

Acting on forensic analysis, detectives traced two suspects to Kyanja Heights flats in Kampala's Nakawa suburb, where they were arrested with the help of the Uganda Police Force. Police Constable Bett Kiplangat Collins and civilian Pius Muiru Mbugua, who allegedly entered Uganda illegally after the murder, are held at Jinja Road Police Station pending their return to Nairobi. A third suspect, Samuel Karanja Gathoni, was intercepted in Nairobi.

The DCI stated that PC Bett was tasked with surveillance and acted as co-driver for the operation's central organizer, who drove a vehicle used to block Dr. Mutiso's car before she was fatally shot on July 29, 2026. Mbugua allegedly served as the link between the execution team and the contractor, acting as paymaster. The National Police Service acknowledged the Uganda Police Force's crucial support in the operation.

Why it mattersThree suspects linked to a murder in Nairobi were arrested in Uganda, impacting cross-border law enforcement cooperation and the judicial process for those involved.
