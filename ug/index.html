Uganda urges predictable funding for African peace operations

Kampala calls for joint AU-UN frameworks and structured financial partnerships to ensure long-term stability across the continent.

Uganda has called for predictable funding and joint African Union-United Nations frameworks to secure long-term stability across Africa. Ambassador Rebecca Amuge Otengo, Uganda’s Permanent Representative to the AU, made the call during a strategic review on AU and UN Peace Operations in New York. She stressed the need for a balanced, predictable financial burden-sharing partnership beyond ad-hoc security cooperation.

Ambassador Otengo stated that UN Security Council Resolution 2719 (2023) provides a mechanism for a structured partnership, emphasizing institutionalized AU-UN cooperation throughout the planning cycle. She highlighted that future operations must have clear political strategies, achievable objectives, and measurable benchmarks, with mandates aligning to available resources. Lessons from African-led operations in Somalia informed the implementation of Resolution 2719, underscoring the value of African peace initiatives and the need for reliable financing.

Uganda stressed integrating vital capabilities like strategic mobility, logistics, aviation, and medical evacuation into early planning stages. The country also called for enhanced resource mobilization, diversified financing, and continued contributions to the AU Peace Fund to counter uncertainty from extra-budgetary contributions. Ambassador Otengo reminded global partners that maintaining continental peace is a shared global duty, with success measured by the ability to deploy adequately and sustainably financed peace operations.

Why it mattersUganda's representative called for predictable funding and joint frameworks for African peace operations to ensure long-term stability.

Uganda discharges 60 Ebola contacts

All individuals monitored for 21 days showed no symptoms after contact with a Kenyan traveller.

Uganda has released 60 people who were in contact with a Kenyan traveller diagnosed with Ebola. Health Minister Dr. Chris Baryomunsi announced Sunday that all contacts completed the mandatory 21-day monitoring period without developing symptoms. Uganda has not recorded any new Ebola cases since declaring its outbreak over in July.

The contacts included hotel staff, transport operators, and airport personnel who were monitored after investigations into the Kenyan traveller's movements through Uganda. Baryomunsi confirmed that none of the monitored individuals tested positive for the virus. Uganda declared its Bundibugyo virus disease outbreak ended on July 28, 2026.

Despite being Ebola-free, Uganda remains vigilant due to ongoing transmission in the Democratic Republic of Congo and cross-border travel. Health authorities are investigating a reported case in Nigeria involving a woman who travelled from Uganda. Screening at Entebbe International Airport and border crossings has been intensified.

Why it mattersUganda has successfully monitored and discharged 60 contacts of a Kenyan Ebola case, confirming the country's continued freedom from the disease.

Spiro Uganda to triple battery hubs by December

The electric mobility company plans to expand its network from four to 11 mega battery-swapping hubs to support growing demand for electric motorcycles.

Spiro Uganda plans to triple its mega battery-swapping hubs from four to 11 by December 2026, the company announced. This expansion aims to support the growing demand for electric motorcycles by improving battery availability for riders. Each hub can charge 200 batteries simultaneously, with the company integrating solar and grid power sources.

Bruce Mucunguzi, Spiro Uganda's Deputy Country Director, stated the expansion is critical for riders switching to electric two-wheelers, alongside maintenance and spare parts. He spoke at the Renewable Energy Conference and Expo 2026 in Kampala, organised by the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Development. Spiro Uganda has four existing hubs in Mukono, Soroti, Jinja, and Bukoto.

The planned 11 hubs by December will enhance Spiro's network of over 360 stations nationwide, allowing riders to swap depleted batteries for charged ones. Allan Muhumuza from the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation noted that Uganda's electric vehicle production capacity reached 79,000 units annually in 2025, with 25,000 electric vehicles manufactured locally. Officials highlighted the need for clear regulations governing electric vehicle infrastructure, including safety and charging facilities.

Why it mattersSpiro Uganda is expanding its electric motorcycle infrastructure, affecting riders and the growing electric vehicle sector in Uganda.

Mutebile's son sues stepmother over estate and confinement

Duncan Mutebile accuses administrators of hiding assets and arranging his psychiatric confinement.

Duncan Tumusiime Mutebile has sued his stepmother, Betty Tumusiime Mutebile, and co-administrator David Kyomukama Mutebile, in the High Court’s Family Division in Kampala. He accuses them of concealing estate assets, transferring property without consent, and arranging his involuntary confinement in a psychiatric facility. The late Emmanuel Tumusiime Mutebile, former Bank of Uganda Governor, left the estate now under dispute.

Duncan alleges the administrators hid commercial properties, shares in Garuga Properties Ltd, a matooke plantation, forestry land, and bank accounts. He claims estate assets were transferred to Betty Mutebile’s name without his consent. His attorneys also accuse the administrators of arranging his confinement at facilities like Hope and Beyond Rehabilitation Centre to prevent him from challenging estate decisions.

The administrators deny the claims, stating Duncan is not the biological son of the former governor and that he has bipolar affective disorder. Duncan’s attorneys dispute this, presenting a birth certificate and a psychiatric evaluation from Kira Health Centre IV dated September 24, 2026, which reportedly found his cognition intact. Police and the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions are reviewing the confinement allegations.

Why it mattersDuncan Mutebile is affected by alleged concealment of his father's estate assets and his own involuntary confinement.

Agriculture · 2026-10-26

Museveni urges Sheema farmers to adopt irrigation

President Museveni promoted commercial farming, pasture growing, and fertiliser use to combat poverty and drought.

President Yoweri Kaguta Museveni urged farmers in Sheema District to adopt irrigation, pasture growing, fertiliser use, and commercial agriculture to increase productivity and lift households out of poverty. He spoke today at a thanksgiving ceremony for Internal Affairs Minister Prof. Ephraim Kamuntu at Bugongi Town Council Playground.

The President stated his commitment to fighting poverty began in his school days, recalling visits to government stock farms where he saw improved livestock and agricultural infrastructure. He contrasted these with the subsistence farming many communities practiced for immediate needs.

Museveni highlighted the four-acre model, introduced in 1996, which advises dedicating land to coffee, fruit, pasture, and food crops, supplemented by poultry, piggery, or fish farming. He called on leaders to intensify efforts to reach households not yet engaged in commercial production.

Why it mattersUgandan farmers in Sheema District are directly affected by President Museveni's call to adopt commercial agriculture practices.
