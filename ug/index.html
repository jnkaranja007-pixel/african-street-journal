Health · 2026-11-01

Jubilee Insurance cleans Kampala streets

Company staff joined city officials in a cleanup exercise to prevent disease spread and flooding.

Jubilee Insurance Uganda employees participated in a city cleaning exercise on Saturday alongside Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA) officials. The cleanup covered streets including Kimathi Avenue, Parliament Avenue, and Kampala Road, aiming to improve sanitation and reduce disease risks.

Jubilee Insurance chief executive officer Eugine Mutekhele stated that regular cleaning and proper waste management are critical to reducing the spread of diseases like malaria, typhoid, and cholera, which disproportionately affect low-income communities. He emphasized that preventive care is better than curative, urging other corporations to support the government's monthly cleaning initiative.

KCCA officials noted that the monthly cleaning program has not yet attracted widespread participation and appealed for more organisations to provide equipment and mobilize residents. Sheila Komugisha, KCCA woman councillor, thanked Jubilee Insurance and called for sustained cooperation between authorities, businesses, and communities to improve sanitation.

Why it mattersKampala residents face increased risks of preventable diseases and flooding due to poor sanitation and blocked drainage channels.

Business · 2026-11-01

Uganda Tourism Board partners with CAF for AFCON 2027 promotion

The "Explore Uganda – The Pearl of Africa" brand will feature in tournament promotions to attract visitors.

Uganda has secured destination sponsorship rights for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) through an agreement between the Uganda Tourism Board (UTB) and the Confederation of African Football (CAF). This partnership will promote Uganda's tourism attractions to football supporters, teams, officials, and media attending the continental tournament. The TotalEnergies CAF AFCON PAMOJA 2027 will be co-hosted by Uganda, Kenya, and Tanzania from June 19 to July 17, 2027.

The deal was announced in Kampala, with CAF General Secretary Samson Adamu stating that Uganda can leverage football's popularity to position itself as a tourism destination. UTB Chief Executive Officer Juliana Kagwa called the event the biggest tourism marketing opportunity for Uganda in a generation, aiming to showcase wildlife, culture, and hospitality. The partnership was launched with the attendance of Tourism Minister Tom Butime and Minister of State for Sports Peter Ogwang.

The agreement aims to expose Uganda's attractions to over 3 billion global viewers of the AFCON. CAF is working with Uganda, Kenya, and Tanzania to build ties with the business community. The "Explore Uganda – The Pearl of Africa" brand will be visible through broadcast, digital, and in-stadium platforms, encouraging visitors to explore beyond matches.

Why it mattersUganda's tourism sector will be promoted to a global audience during the AFCON 2027 tournament, aiming to increase visitor arrivals and spending.

Politics · 2026-11-01

Austrian diplomat warns of trust erosion over Uganda court delays

Dr Katja Kerschbaumer stated that prolonged trials and torture risk undermining public confidence in Uganda's institutions.

Dr Katja Kerschbaumer, head of the Austrian Embassy, warned that lengthy court proceedings and torture are damaging public trust in Uganda's institutions. Speaking at a governance review meeting in Kampala, she emphasized that timely justice and adherence to constitutional rights are vital for national stability and public confidence. Kerschbaumer stated that justice delayed can become justice denied, affecting individuals, families, and businesses.

The diplomat expressed concern over suspects spending excessive time in custody awaiting trial, noting that fundamental rights, including protection from torture, cannot be suspended. She highlighted that while some rights can be limited for public interest, such as land acquisition for infrastructure, these must be lawful, transparent, and proportionate. Kerschbaumer praised Uganda's constitution for its protections but stressed that citizens must believe institutions are fair and impartial.

Kerschbaumer noted that when citizens trust institutions, social cohesion, cooperation, and stability are enhanced. She was speaking on behalf of the Justice Sector Development Partners Group during the Fifth Annual Governance and Security Programme Review meeting.

Why it mattersUgandans risk losing faith in their government institutions if court cases are not resolved promptly and if torture is not prevented.

Agriculture · 2026-11-01

Uganda targets higher coffee export earnings

A new African Union strategy aims to boost local processing and continental trade for coffee.

Uganda aims to increase coffee export earnings by focusing on local processing and expanded continental trade, reducing reliance on raw bean exports. This initiative follows figures showing African coffee producers earn less than US$3 billion annually from a global market exceeding US$465 billion. The African Union has designated coffee as a strategic commodity to help producers capture more value through processing, roasting, packaging, and direct market access.

Inter-African Coffee Organisation (IACO) Secretary-General Ambassador Solomon Rutega shared these figures with Uganda’s Chargé d’Affaires in Ethiopia, Margaret Kafeero, in Addis Ababa. Rutega stated that processing coffee locally could raise its value from US$2 per kilogram of raw beans to US$40 per kilogram of finished products. This transformation could improve the livelihoods of over 10 million households across Africa dependent on coffee production.

The strategy aligns with Uganda's efforts to boost domestic processing, create jobs, and increase earnings for farmers and exporters. Kafeero confirmed Uganda's support for expanding African coffee trade under the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA), leveraging its 1.5 billion consumer market. Ethiopia, which produces 39% of Africa's coffee, and Uganda, with 23%, are central to this plan.

Why it mattersUgandan farmers and exporters will benefit from increased earnings and improved livelihoods through local coffee processing and expanded trade.

Business · 2026-11-01

Uganda and EU target €2.2bn for green jobs

Trade between Uganda and the European Union has surged to €1.8 billion by 2025, with EU investments reaching €2.2 billion.

Uganda and the European Union are shifting focus to private capital, green manufacturing, and job creation, marking 50 years of diplomatic ties. The Uganda–European Union Business Forum in Kampala on October 20-21, 2026, alongside the Renewable Energy Conference and Expo, highlighted this new direction. Trade between Uganda and the bloc increased from roughly €420 million in 2015 to €1.8 billion by 2025.

European investments represented about 79 per cent of Uganda’s foreign direct investment inflows last year, totalling approximately €2.2 billion. Ambassador Jan Sadek stated the partnership uses Europe’s “Team Europe” and Global Gateway strategy to mobilize funding for sustainable projects. An upcoming €170-million overhaul of hydropower stations is backed by EU grants and development loans.

The Private Sector Foundation Uganda stressed that electricity must be reliable and affordable for manufacturers to benefit from expanded generation capacity. Minister of Energy Monica Musenero urged European investors to view renewable energy as a catalyst for industrial growth. Organizers will monitor if business matchmaking sessions yield financed projects translating into affordable power and employment.

Why it mattersUgandan citizens need stable employment and reliable livelihoods, while local factories require steady power for efficient operation.
