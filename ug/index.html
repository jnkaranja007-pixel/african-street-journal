Uganda's EAC trade deficit hits $809 million

Imports from Kenya and Tanzania far outstripped Uganda's exports in August 2026, widening the regional trade gap.

Uganda's trade deficit with the East African Community (EAC) reached $809 million in August 2026, driven by a surge in imports from Kenya and Tanzania. The country earned $241.29 million from exports to the bloc but spent $1.05 billion on imports, creating a significant imbalance.

Kenya alone accounted for $714.5 million in Ugandan imports, with only $41.05 million in Ugandan exports to Kenya, resulting in a $673.45 million deficit. Tanzania contributed another $189.8 million deficit, with imports at $203.6 million against exports of $13.8 million.

These deficits with Kenya and Tanzania outweighed surpluses with the Democratic Republic of Congo ($77.1 million), South Sudan ($56.71 million), and Rwanda ($21.89 million). The Bank of Uganda linked this widening deficit to pressure on the Uganda shilling and concerns about imported inflation.

Why it mattersUganda's trade balance with its East African neighbours has worsened, impacting the national currency and increasing the cost of imported goods.

Museveni urges church to champion wealth creation

President Museveni's message at the 141st Bishop Hannington commemoration stressed combining spiritual guidance with practical efforts to improve livelihoods.

President Yoweri Kaguta Museveni has urged the Church of Uganda and other religious institutions to promote socio-economic transformation and household wealth creation. The President's message was delivered by Minister for the Presidency, Hon. Babirye Milly Babalanda, at the 141st commemoration of Bishop James Hannington in Mayuge District.

The commemoration honoured the Anglican missionary martyred in Uganda in 1885. President Museveni thanked the Church of Uganda, the organising committee, and the people of Busoga for keeping Bishop Hannington’s legacy alive, noting his faith, courage, and sacrifice. Bishop Hannington first led missionaries to Uganda in 1882, facing numerous challenges.

He returned in 1885 with fellow missionaries like William Jones, but they were detained near Lake Victoria as their motives were misunderstood by the Kabaka. Bishop Hannington was killed on October 29, 1885, on the orders of Kabaka Mwanga II, through Busoga Chief Luba, at Kyando.

Why it mattersUgandan Christians are encouraged to integrate practical wealth creation with spiritual guidance.

Uganda screens over 30,000 buildings for earthquake safety

The National Building Review Board is updating seismic risk maps and construction standards to protect communities.

Uganda is assessing over 30,000 buildings nationwide to improve earthquake preparedness and construction safety. The National Building Review Board (NBRB) is conducting these assessments to identify structural weaknesses and guide necessary strengthening interventions. This work is part of the Serene initiative to update seismic risk mapping and improve building regulations.

Eng. Dr Maurice Oleng, head of research at the NBRB, stated that the building screenings provide evidence for revising construction standards and disaster preparedness plans. The NBRB is collaborating with the Global Earthquake Model Foundation on this project, which examines earthquake hazards, population exposure, and building vulnerability. Updated seismic hazard assessments now identify areas more susceptible to ground shaking, particularly around the East African Rift System.

Research also includes assessing 100 school buildings in Fort Portal, Hoima, and Mbarara for earthquake vulnerability, with findings expected to inform targeted improvements. The NBRB aims to update Uganda's seismic design code to ensure engineers have a foundational baseline for constructing resilient structures. These efforts follow a recent church collapse in Bwerenga that killed one person and injured 25, where preliminary assessments cited design weaknesses and poor workmanship.

Why it mattersUgandan communities are at risk from earthquakes due to the country's location in the East African Rift System, and the NBRB's work aims to make buildings safer.

Uganda: epilepsy drug mix-up kills child, hospitalises 16

Health workers in Butebo district administered carbamazepine instead of deworming medicine during a child outreach.

A two-year-old boy died and 16 children were hospitalised in Butebo district after health workers mistakenly gave them an epilepsy drug instead of deworming medicine. The incident occurred during a child health outreach in Kabwangasi town council on October 30, 2026. The children were given carbamazepine, an epilepsy drug, instead of mebendazole, a deworming medicine, reportedly because the tablets looked similar.

The affected children were rushed to Mbale Regional Referral Hospital, where they developed severe central nervous system depression. Dr Julian Abeso stated that one child died from respiratory depression, while 15 others began recovering from deep sedation. The hospital notified the ministry of Health, and the health workers involved have been suspended pending investigation.

Butebo district health officer Dr Lawrence Oowunye confirmed 23 children received the wrong medication, with 17 showing severe symptoms. The district has suspended all community health outreaches to audit its medicine supply systems. Police and the National Drug Authority are investigating how the mix-up occurred at both packing and administration stages.

Why it mattersChildren in Uganda's Butebo district are at risk of receiving incorrect medication due to errors in drug handling and distribution during community health outreach programs.

Jumia Uganda launches Black Friday deals up to 80% off

The online marketplace offers discounts on electronics, appliances, and fashion from October 30 to November 30.

Jumia Uganda has launched its annual Black Friday shopping campaign, offering discounts of up to 80% on selected items. The promotion, which runs from October 30 to November 30, aims to help consumers manage household spending by providing reduced prices on electronics, smartphones, home appliances, fashion, beauty products, and household essentials.

Steven Lamony, chief executive officer of Jumia Uganda, stated that the campaign, themed “Jumia Black Friday — Your dreams, now on deal!”, is designed to offer customers more value for their money. He acknowledged that many Ugandan households are being more deliberate with their spending and that Jumia wants to facilitate access to attractive offers on everyday products. The campaign officially began at 11:59 p.m. on October 29.

Customers can find specific deals such as an SPJ 15-kilogram twin-tub washing machine for Shs699,000 and a Pixel 65-inch QLED Android television for Shs999,000. A Pixel 280-litre double-door refrigerator with a water dispenser is also available for Shs399,000, subject to availability. Shoppers can access these offers through the Jumia website or mobile application.

Why it mattersUgandan consumers can access discounted prices on a wide range of products to help manage household budgets amid rising living costs.
