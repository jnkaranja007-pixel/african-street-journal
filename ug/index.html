Mortgage Finance Lags Uganda's Economic Growth Agenda

Uganda's housing finance sector, at 1 percent of GDP, is significantly underdeveloped compared to global benchmarks, hindering the nation's ambitious tenfold economic growth.

Uganda's goal to expand its economy tenfold to US$500 billion by 2040 hinges on strengthening financial systems, particularly mortgage financing, which remains critically underdeveloped. While successful economies leverage mortgage debt above 80 percent of GDP, Uganda's stands at approximately 1 percent. This low penetration is despite the housing sector contributing about 11 percent to GDP and facing a deficit of 2.4 million units against an annual construction rate of 60,000 units.

The limited access to affordable mortgage financing forces many Ugandans to rely on personal savings for home construction, often resulting in substandard housing and tied-up capital. This compromises construction quality, leaving homes unfinished and impacting economic activity. A robust mortgage system, however, could enable individuals with stable incomes to acquire homes, stimulating construction, creating jobs in sectors like manufacturing and trade, and facilitating asset accumulation.

The National Development Plan IV acknowledges affordable housing and sustainable urbanisation as crucial for Uganda's transformation. Yet, mortgage loans represent less than 1 percent of GDP, a stark contrast to emerging economies. This gap not only exacerbates the housing deficit but also limits the multiplier effect housing construction has on the broader economy, affecting demand for materials, services, and labor.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the underdeveloped mortgage market limits capital mobilization for the construction and manufacturing sectors, hindering Uganda's economic growth targets.

Zambian Golfer Dominates Uganda Open, Leads by Six Strokes

Dominic Musonda's near-flawless play puts him in a commanding position heading into the final day of the Johnnie Walker Uganda Open, with Ugandan golfers trailing significantly.

Zambian golfer Dominic Musonda is poised to win the Johnnie Walker Uganda Open after a dominant performance that saw him build a six-stroke lead. Musonda played 42 holes with only one bogey, showcasing exceptional accuracy and recovery skills. He came close to matching the course record of 63, set by Deo Akope in 2010, with his impressive play at Kitante.

Fellow Zambian Gilbert Chalwe holds second place after shooting a one-under 71, maintaining a five-stroke advantage over the leading Ugandan player, Joseph Kasozi. Ugandan golfers Juma Abiti, John Musiimenta, and Joseph Cwinya-ai are positioned within the top six, with Cwinya-ai notably scoring eight birdies in his round of 69.

The main tournament concludes on Friday, with the PRO-AM event scheduled for the following week. The Johnnie Walker Uganda Amateur Golf trophy is unlikely to remain in Uganda this year, given Musonda's substantial lead. Sponsors supporting the event include ABSA Bank, Aquafina, MTN, and Uganda Airlines, among others.

Why it mattersUgandan golfers are unlikely to win the Johnnie Walker Uganda Open, as Zambian Dominic Musonda holds a significant six-stroke lead heading into the final day.

Escaped Theft Suspect Beaten to Death by Mob in Kassanda

A 30-year-old man, Derrick Kasumba, died after being assaulted by villagers in Lwenyange village shortly after escaping police custody in Manyogaseka.

Derrick Kasumba, 30, died following a mob beating in Kassanda district after he escaped from police custody at Manyogaseka sub-county police station. Kasumba, identified as alias Kalevu, was being held for burglary and theft. He reportedly slipped away around 9 am while being escorted to the toilet by Police Constable Aaron Nalyanya. An hour later, police received word that Kasumba had been intercepted by a mob in Lwenyange village.

Officer in charge Emmanuel Ogwang dispatched Constable Nalyanya to Lwenyange village, where he found the mob assaulting Kasumba. The group dispersed upon the officer's arrival. Nalyanya retrieved the injured suspect and transported him back to the police station, where his condition worsened. He was taken to Kyansaswa health centre III but was pronounced dead.

Kasumba's body was moved to Mityana General Hospital for a postmortem. Police have launched an investigation into the escape, the assault, and the circumstances leading to his death. Authorities are working to identify and apprehend those involved in the mob attack. The investigation will also examine how the suspect managed to escape from custody.

Why it mattersThe mob assault and death of a theft suspect in Kassanda affects rural communities by demonstrating vigilante justice and the potential for violence when law enforcement is perceived as inadequate.

Movit Foundation Trains 78 in Brick Technology for School Construction

A new five-classroom block at Kadindimo Primary School in Bunyangabu District was built using local labor trained in interlocking brick technology, demonstrating a new model.

More than 78 residents of Bunyangabu District gained employment and new construction skills through a Movit Foundation project that delivered a five-classroom block at Kadindimo Primary School. The initiative combined infrastructure development with local economic participation and skills transfer, using interlocking stabilised bricks manufactured on-site with a Makiga machine provided by the foundation. This approach reduced transportation needs and ensured a consistent material supply during construction.

Movit Foundation Board Chairman Dr Bruce Mpamizo stated that businesses should evaluate the long-term value of their social investments, focusing on who benefits and what capabilities are created. He highlighted that the community's participation in building the facility and acquiring skills represents a significant return. The project proceeded despite challenges including heavy rainfall, supply chain issues, and rising construction input prices, which the team managed through schedule adjustments and advance procurement.

The completed facility meets specified quality standards and is ready for use. Guest of Honour, Minister Balaam Barugahara, emphasized the importance of private sector collaboration with the government for Uganda's development, viewing business investment in education and skills as a contribution to building productive capacity. He encouraged businesses to align their resources with government priorities and community needs.

Why it mattersThe project provided 78 residents with new skills in brick technology and employment, directly impacting local livelihoods and potentially future construction opportunities.

Security Withdrawn From Among Residence After Presidential Pardon

Security officers have been removed from former speaker Annet Anita Among's residence following President Museveni's decision to forgive her and halt prosecution.

Security personnel have been withdrawn from the Nakasero residence of former parliament speaker Annet Anita Among, hours after Chief of Defence Forces Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba announced her pardon by President Museveni. The withdrawal signifies a potential de-escalation of the restrictions imposed on Among, who had been under house arrest. The change in security detail saw Special Forces Command (SFC) personnel replaced by Counter Terrorism Police officers at the main gate, with some individuals in civilian attire observed nearby.

Among's house arrest followed investigations into allegations of corruption, abuse of office, money laundering, and illicit wealth accumulation, which reportedly began after a political disagreement over the speakership earlier this year. Her bank accounts were also reportedly frozen during this period. Despite the announcement of her release and pardon, Among remained largely inaccessible to visitors at her residence on Wednesday, with staff indicating she had instructed no visitors would be received.

In statements posted on X, Among expressed gratitude to President Museveni for what she termed "parental grace" and mercy, and thanked Gen Muhoozi for his professionalism. The pardon and subsequent security withdrawal suggest a resolution to the political and legal troubles that led to her house arrest and investigations into her properties across several locations.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects political figures and potentially alters the landscape of political investigations and prosecutions in Uganda.
