Deaf Link Uganda director shot dead in Kisoro lodge attack

Unidentified gunmen killed Barbra Nassozi Kiyaga, 58, during a suspected robbery attempt at Mucha Lodge.

The founding executive director of Deaf Link Uganda, Barbra Nassozi Kiyaga, was shot dead during a gun attack at Mucha Lodge in Kisoro district early on Thursday. Unidentified gunmen attacked the lodge near River Mucha at about 2 am, in what police suspect was an attempted robbery. The assailants exchanged fire with security guards, resulting in Kiyaga's death and injuring Police Constable Charles Mukesi.

Kigezi Region police spokesperson Nelson Tumushime stated that Kiyaga, a Kampala resident, had checked into the lodge on Wednesday for a four-day stay. She died at the scene and her body was taken to Kisoro Hospital for a postmortem. Constable Mukesi sustained a gunshot wound to his left leg and is receiving treatment in stable condition at Kisoro Hospital. Police have launched an operation to find the attackers and have increased security in the area.

This incident follows two other killings in Kisoro district within the past month. Prominent businessman Africano Nteziyaremye and his wife Peace Kyompeire were shot dead on October 17, 2026. A week prior, suspected gunmen attacked Bweza Lodge in an apparent robbery attempt. Police have not yet established a connection between these incidents or the motive for Kiyaga's killing.

Why it mattersThe killing of Barbra Nassozi Kiyaga and previous violent incidents in Kisoro district raise security concerns in an area popular with tourists.

Ugandan coffee firms debut at Swiss festival

Gorilla Conservation Coffee and Carico Coffee sold products directly to 5,500 visitors at Zurich's largest coffee trade fair.

Ugandan coffee companies Gorilla Conservation Coffee and Carico Coffee participated in the Swiss Coffee Festival in Zurich from October 31 to November 1. This marked Uganda’s first appearance at Switzerland’s largest coffee trade fair, which drew about 5,500 visitors and 78 exhibitors. The companies offered tastings, met potential buyers, and sold their products directly to consumers in an effort to capture more value from their crop.

Uganda’s Ambassador to Switzerland Marcel Tibaleka stated, “We are delighted to introduce more of Uganda’s exceptional coffee brands to Swiss consumers and look forward to building lasting partnerships.” The embassy in Switzerland secured the stand for the two companies, supporting efforts to expand coffee exports and promote value addition. Carico Coffee purchases directly from approximately 1,700 smallholder farmers in the Mount Elgon region.

Uganda shipped 8.8 million 60kg bags of coffee worth $2.4 billion in the 12 months to March 2026, though over 98 percent leaves the country as unroasted green beans. The government launched a national coffee brand, “Uganda Coffee: It’s in Our Nature,” in June and aims for production exceeding 20 million bags annually by 2030. About 1.7 million Ugandan households depend on coffee for their livelihoods.

Why it mattersUgandan coffee companies are seeking to increase their earnings by selling branded coffee directly to consumers in international markets like Switzerland.

Uganda court extends indecent assault protection to men

Constitutional Court rules existing laws are discriminatory and orders government to propose amendments.

Uganda's Constitutional Court has extended legal protection against indecent assault and acts insulting modesty to men, ruling that provisions of the Penal Code Act previously protecting only women and girls are discriminatory. The court, sitting in Kampala, also declared unconstitutional provisions of the Magistrates Courts Act that allow magistrates to investigate cases, formulate charges, and then preside over the trial.

A panel of five justices, led by Justice Cheborion Barishaki, unanimously ordered that the words “any woman or girl” in Section 115(1) and (3) of the Penal Code Act be replaced with “any person.” This ruling followed consolidated petitions filed by city lawyer Steven Kalali, Pader District Chief Magistrate Martins Kirya, and lawyer Michael Aboneka.

The petitioners argued that restricting protection against indecent assault and acts insulting modesty to women and girls discriminated against men, contravening Article 21 of the Constitution. The Attorney General defended the provisions as affirmative action for women, but the court rejected this, stating that while women need protection, the law cannot deny men equivalent protection against similar harm.

Why it mattersMen in Uganda now have the same legal protection against indecent assault and acts insulting modesty as women.

Uganda shilling hits record low against dollar

The Uganda shilling has weakened by about 12% against the US dollar, pushing up the cost of living.

The Uganda shilling is at a record low against the US dollar, depreciating by about 12% and increasing the cost of living. Imports are now more expensive, impacting industries reliant on foreign materials and driving up prices for essential goods like fuel. The dollar now costs over 4400 shillings, making a liter of gas approximately 7000 UGX.

This currency weakness benefits a minority of exporters but squeezes households and small businesses, which form the backbone of Uganda's economy. The Bank of Uganda raised interest rates to 12% in October to combat inflation, which stood at nearly 5% in September.

The price of a loaf of bread is set to rise from 5000 UGX to 8000 UGX due to increased wheat costs. Transport fares have also increased, with a 1000 UGX journey now costing 1500 UGX or more, affecting families on fixed incomes.

Why it mattersUgandan households and small businesses face rising costs for essential goods and transport due to the weakening shilling.

Uganda finance professionals to convene on tech and AI

The fifth ACCA Uganda Members Convention on November 19-20 will gather accountants and leaders to discuss profession-wide changes.

Finance and accounting professionals will gather in Kampala for the fifth ACCA Uganda Members Convention on November 19 and 20, 2026. The two-day event at Speke Resort and Convention Centre will focus on how technology, artificial intelligence, and evolving business demands are reshaping the profession. Organised by ACCA Uganda, the convention's theme is “Navigating Change: Flexible and Inclusive Career Pathways.”

The convention will convene accountants, business leaders, policymakers, ACCA members, affiliates, and students to discuss adapting to automation, AI, data-driven business, and global standards. Finance Minister Henry Musasizi is expected to officiate the opening, while National Planning Authority Executive Director Dr Joseph Muvawala will discuss accountants' role in Uganda’s growth. Speakers will also cover global opportunities and sustainability reporting.

Discussions will include AI in public financial management, Uganda’s sustainability roadmap, IFRS S1 and S2 adoption, green finance, and carbon markets. Topics also cover labour market changes, digital transformation, talent management, professional wellbeing, and mentorship. ACCA Uganda Country Manager Charlotte Kukunda stated the event will help professionals examine changes impacting their work and businesses.

Why it mattersFinance and accounting professionals in Uganda will examine changes impacting their roles and businesses, including new technologies and global standards.
