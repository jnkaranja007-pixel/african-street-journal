Deputy Field Force Commander Ecotu Agapitus Dies at Mulago Hospital

Commissioner of Police Ecotu Agapitus, who led public order and tactical operations, has passed away at Mulago Hospital with the cause of death still unknown.

The Deputy Commander of the Field Force Unit (FFU), Commissioner of Police Ecotu Agapitus, has died at Mulago National Referral Hospital. The cause of his death remains unknown. Agapitus was a senior officer in charge of public order, riot control, and tactical operations, serving under AIGP John Nuwagira. His career included roles in administration, field operations, and international peacekeeping.

Before his tenure with the FFU, Agapitus managed personnel and institutional matters in the Directorate of Human Resource Administration at Police Headquarters. He also served in the Counter-Terrorism Directorate, including the Oil and Gas Protection Division, and held command in the Rwenzori region, overseeing operations after the 2016 Kasese clashes. In June 2023, President Museveni promoted him to Commissioner of Police as part of a large group of 1,638 officers.

Agapitus's experience also led to his consideration for United Nations police roles in Somalia, where he was involved in training the Somali Police Force and advising on reforms. Colleagues described him as disciplined and dependable. Police Spokesperson ACP Kituuma Rusoke stated that an official announcement regarding the cause of death and burial arrangements will be made.

Why it mattersThe death of a high-ranking police official responsible for public order and tactical operations may affect the stability and security of the nation.

Socialite Bryan White granted bail after 293 days in custody

The High Court in Kampala released Bryan Kirumira, known as Bryan White, on mandatory bail after he spent over 180 days detained without trial.

Kampala socialite Bryan Kirumira, also known as Bryan White, has been granted mandatory bail by the High Court after spending 293 days in custody without being committed for trial. Justice Gadenya Paul Wolimbwa ruled that Kirumira had acquired a constitutional right to mandatory bail after exceeding the 180-day limit for offenses triable only by the High Court. Kirumira faces six charges including human sacrifice, manslaughter, and possession of narcotics, stemming from an alleged incident on October 22, 2025.

The prosecution had opposed bail, citing the seriousness of the human sacrifice charge and the potential for Kirumira to be a flight risk or interfere with investigations. However, Justice Gadenya stated that the constitutional right to bail, based on the passage of time, could not be overridden by the gravity of the charges. The judge noted that the 180-day period expired on May 12, 2026, and Kirumira had remained in custody for an additional 113 days.

Strict conditions were imposed on Kirumira's release, including a 10 million shilling deposit in court and two sureties each executing a 50 million shilling non-cash bond. He must also reside at a fixed address within the court's jurisdiction. The court dismissed prosecution arguments about potential witness interference due to a lack of substantiated evidence or specific witness identification.

Why it mattersBryan White, a socialite accused of serious crimes including human sacrifice, has been released on bail, affecting the legal process and public perception of justice.

King Oyo's death mourned by UNICEF for child advocacy

UNICEF Uganda Representative Dr Robin Nandy stated the late King Oyo's early assumption of responsibility shaped his advocacy for children.

UNICEF has remembered King Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV, who ascended the Tooro throne at three years old, for his lifelong advocacy for children's rights. The organization noted that his unusual childhood prepared him to understand children not as future citizens but as individuals with present needs. Throughout his reign, King Oyo championed education, health, and protection for young people, leveraging the trust cultural leaders often hold.

The late monarch's voice amplified UNICEF's efforts in Uganda, which include working with cultural institutions on issues such as keeping girls in school, protecting children from violence, and connecting families with health services. In Tooro, this partnership extended to basic services like immunization, nutrition, and sanitation. UNICEF also supported emergency preparedness along the border with the Democratic Republic of Congo, assisting refugee children and host communities.

Dr. Nandy highlighted that King Oyo's personal commitment to children's rights, such as a child's right to learn or a girl's right to complete education, resonated with families in a way that public campaigns could not replicate. This unique ability to bridge policy and families, rooted in local authority, made his advocacy particularly impactful in a country where community acceptance is crucial for the success of health, education, and child protection initiatives.

Why it mattersUNICEF mourns King Oyo's death, recognizing his unique ability to advocate for children's education, health, and protection due to his early assumption of royal duties.

C-Care Uganda Provides Free First Aid to Thousands at Rotary Cancer Run

Hundreds of runners received medical support, hydration, and wellness assistance from C-Care Uganda's teams at the 15th annual Rotary Cancer Run.

C-Care Uganda served as a key healthcare partner at the 15th Rotary Cancer Run, offering free first aid, emergency response, and hydration to thousands of participants at Kololo Independence Grounds. The organization's medical teams also provided post-run wellness support and emphasized the importance of regular health checks and seeking timely medical attention. This initiative aligns with C-Care's commitment to enhancing healthcare infrastructure and accessibility in Uganda.

The Rotary Cancer Run, held under the theme “Ggwanga Mujje,” aimed to raise UGX 5 billion to complete and equip the Cancer Treatment Centre at St. Francis Hospital Nsambya. This facility is crucial for strengthening Uganda's specialized cancer treatment capacity, particularly radiotherapy, addressing a significant shortage of such infrastructure. C-Care's partnership with Rotary extends to broader cancer awareness campaigns and community medical outreaches.

Dr. Miriam Mutero Musinga, General Manager of C-Care IHK, stated that the partnership reflects C-Care's dedication to strengthening Ugandan healthcare, viewing the run as a call to action that unites communities and supports vital cancer care infrastructure. Sheila Aboth, Head of Brand and Client Experience at C-Care Uganda, highlighted the collective action demonstrated by participants in addressing the nation's cancer burden.

Why it mattersUgandans are directly affected by the provision of free first aid and wellness support at a major health event, and indirectly by the funds raised for cancer treatment infrastructure.

Equity Bank Funds Dormitory Completion at Vurra Secondary School

A Shs120 million credit facility from Equity Bank enabled Vurra Secondary School to finish a 120-bed boys' dormitory, addressing rising student numbers.

Vurra Secondary School in Arua, Uganda, has completed a new 120-bed boys' dormitory, a project made possible by a Shs120 million credit facility from Equity Bank Uganda. The school, founded in 1981 and now government-aided, is experiencing rapid enrollment growth, with projections reaching 900 students next year and a long-term target of 1,200. The new dormitory eases pressure on existing accommodations and is part of the school's expansion efforts to avoid turning away qualified students.

The partnership with Equity Bank began in late 2024, with the credit facility secured by January to contribute to the dormitory's total cost of Shs280 million. Head Teacher David Dima stated that the bank's support was crucial, as public funding and the school's operating budget were insufficient for such infrastructure development. The school is now planning a similar dormitory for girls, aiming for completion by February next year to accommodate incoming Senior Five students in March 2027.

Equity Bank Uganda Managing Director Gift Shoko emphasized that educational institutions should not delay expansion due to financial constraints. He highlighted the bank's structured financing options that allow schools to commence projects immediately and manage repayments over time. Beyond accommodation, Vurra Secondary School is also seeking land for future development and exploring financing for a school bus to improve student transport and outreach.

Why it mattersVurra Secondary School students and staff are directly affected by the new dormitory, which provides essential accommodation and supports the school's capacity to admit more learners.
