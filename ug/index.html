Uganda trade deficit with EAC hits $809 million

Imports from Kenya and Tanzania surged in August 2026, widening the trade gap despite surpluses with other regional partners.

Uganda's trade deficit with the East African Community (EAC) reached $809 million in August 2026, as imports from Kenya and Tanzania significantly exceeded exports. The country imported $1.05 billion worth of goods while earning only $241.29 million from exports to the bloc. This imbalance contributed to a wider merchandise trade deficit, which grew by 263.5 per cent from July to $817.03 million in August.

Kenya alone accounted for $714.5 million in Ugandan imports, creating a bilateral deficit of $673.45 million, while Tanzania supplied goods worth $203.6 million, resulting in a $189.8 million deficit. These shortfalls overshadowed trade surpluses with the Democratic Republic of Congo ($77.1 million), South Sudan ($56.71 million), and Rwanda ($21.89 million). The Ministry of Finance cited non-tariff barriers as a cause for the deteriorating trade balance.

A proposed Kenyan plant-health inspection fee on agricultural goods could further increase Uganda's export costs and potentially violate EAC trade rules, according to Sheema Municipality MP Dicksons Kateshumbwa. The Uganda shilling has faced pressure, with the Bank of Uganda partly blaming external imbalances for its weakness, raising concerns about imported inflation.

Why it mattersUganda's trade deficit with the East African Community widened to $809 million in August 2026 due to increased imports from Kenya and Tanzania.

Uganda screens 30,000 buildings for earthquake safety

The National Building Review Board is updating seismic risk maps and construction standards to protect communities.

Uganda is assessing the safety of its buildings to prepare for earthquakes, screening over 30,000 structures nationwide. The National Building Review Board (NBRB) is identifying weaknesses and guiding improvements to construction standards.

Eng. Dr Maurice Oleng, head of research at the NBRB, stated that these assessments, combined with new scientific studies, will inform building regulations and disaster preparedness. The NBRB is collaborating with the Global Earthquake Model Foundation on this project, which examines earthquake hazards, exposure, and building vulnerability. A study assessed 100 school buildings in Fort Portal, Hoima, and Mbarara, identifying areas for targeted safety improvements.

Research is important for Uganda due to its location within the East African Rift System, with areas like Toro, Kigezi, Ankole, and Bunyoro identified as susceptible to earthquakes. The NBRB aims to update Uganda’s seismic design provisions to better withstand seismic forces, informed by new scientific evidence and modern engineering practices.

Why it mattersUgandan communities, particularly in areas prone to seismic activity, face risks from earthquake-vulnerable buildings.

Museveni asks church to champion wealth creation

President Museveni's message, delivered by Minister Babalanda, urged religious institutions to promote socio-economic transformation and household wealth.

President Yoweri Museveni has called on the Church of Uganda to actively promote socio-economic transformation and household wealth creation. The message was delivered by Minister for the Presidency, Hon. Babirye Milly Babalanda, at the 141st commemoration of Bishop James Hannington in Mayuge District.

The commemoration, held at Kyando Village, honoured the Anglican missionary martyred in Uganda in 1885. President Museveni thanked the Church of Uganda, the organising committee, and the people of Busoga for keeping Bishop Hannington’s legacy alive and for inviting him to the occasion.

Bishop Hannington first led missionaries to Uganda in 1882, facing numerous challenges before returning to England. He embarked on a second journey in 1885 with fellow missionaries like William Jones, but their mission was misunderstood near Lake Victoria, leading to their detention and Hannington's death on October 29, 1885.

Why it mattersUgandan Christians are urged by the President to combine spiritual guidance with practical efforts to improve livelihoods and create household wealth.

Uganda seeks pension funds for economic growth

Minister Simon Mulongo wants pension savings to become patient capital for productive investment.

Uganda's government aims to transform its economy from $50 billion to $500 billion by 2040, a goal requiring increased pension coverage and long-term savings to fund growth. Simon Mulongo, minister of state for Labour, Employment and Industrial Relations, stated at a Kampala conference that a strong savings culture is needed to provide patient capital for economic activities. He highlighted that a $500 billion economy cannot be financed with short-term money and low national savings.

Mulongo explained that long-term domestic savings are critical for financing enterprises, creating jobs, and boosting exports. The government targets increasing national savings from 20% of GDP to 40% by 2040, with the pensions sector converting long-term savings into productive investment. He cautioned that pension funds must be invested commercially, transparently, and deliver appropriate returns for beneficiaries, not just for national development.

The minister called for improved governance in the pensions sector, including competent trustees, transparency from fund managers, effective regulation, and timely contribution remittances. He also stressed the need for greater financial literacy among workers to build public confidence. Stanbic Uganda was commended for convening the inaugural SBG Securities Pensions Conference.

Why it mattersUganda's economic transformation plan requires significant domestic capital mobilisation, with pension funds expected to convert long-term savings into productive investments.

Agrosap wins PachiPanda innovation challenge

Ugandan innovators Agrosap, Lutre Plastics, and Reyre Blocks Ltd received cash prizes for environmental solutions.

MTN Uganda and WWF Uganda announced three Ugandan winners for the 2026 PachiPanda Innovation Challenge in Kampala on October 29, 2026. The competition recognized innovators developing environmental solutions with economic potential. Over 300 applications were received for the youth-focused challenge, themed “Nature Meets Innovation: Unlocking Africa’s Green Economy.”

Agrosap, led by Garvin Billy Ssemyalo, won first place and UGX 15 million for an innovation improving soil water retention to support crop growth. Lutre Plastics, led by Arther Lule and Sharma Kemigisha, received UGX 10 million as first runner-up for producing boards from recycled plastics. Reyre Blocks Ltd, led by Rhona Janet Atugonza, took third place with UGX 5 million for transforming discarded tyres into construction materials.

The three winning teams will represent Uganda at the Africa PachiPanda Challenge continental finale in South Africa in February 2027. MTN Uganda CEO Sylvia Mulinge stated that the initiative shows how innovation can address environmental challenges and create jobs. WWF Uganda Executive Director Ivan Tumuhimbise added that the focus will shift to helping these innovations grow and benefit communities and nature.

Why it mattersUgandan innovators are developing and winning prizes for solutions to environmental challenges like soil moisture, plastic waste, and tyre disposal.
