Uganda and Rwanda launch joint health exercise

Health officials from both nations will test emergency response coordination at the Katuna/Gatuna border post starting November 10.

Uganda and Rwanda will conduct a three-day joint cross-border health preparedness exercise starting November 10 at the Katuna/Gatuna One Stop Border Post. The simulation aims to strengthen the capacity of health officials from both nations to respond to public health emergencies. The exercise will involve testing coordination, information sharing, and referral arrangements between health teams and border agencies.

Uganda’s Ministry of Health stated that the simulation will use scenarios to assess preparedness and enhance cross-border cooperation. The initiative seeks to strengthen public health protection while ensuring the continued safety of cross-border travel and trade. Members of the public may observe some of the drills involving health workers and other border agencies.

This exercise follows concerns about potential cross-border disease spread, particularly from the ongoing Ebola outbreak in neighboring Democratic Republic of the Congo. Health officials have warned that porous borders increase the risk of transmission to neighboring countries.

Why it mattersUgandan and Rwandan health officials will test their coordination and information sharing to better respond to public health emergencies, including potential disease outbreaks.

Beatrice Lugalambi joins Pride Bank board

Lugalambi brings over 30 years of banking experience to her new role as Executive Director.

Beatrice Lugalambi has joined Pride Bank Limited’s executive leadership team as Executive Director. She brings more than 30 years of experience in banking and financial services to the Ugandan institution. Lugalambi’s appointment follows Pride Bank’s transition to a Tier II credit institution, which expanded its capacity to mobilize deposits and offer broader financial solutions.

Lugalambi has extensive knowledge of Uganda’s banking landscape, having previously held senior management positions at Centenary Bank, including General Manager for Corporate Communications and Marketing. She also held leadership roles at Barclays Bank Uganda and Bank of Africa. Pride Bank Managing Director Veronica Namagembe stated that Lugalambi’s market understanding will be valuable for the bank’s next phase of growth.

Lugalambi holds a Master of Business Administration from ESAMI, a Postgraduate Diploma in Management from Uganda Management Institute, and a Bachelor of Arts from Makerere University. She is also involved in community service and mentorship through Rotary. Her role supports Pride Bank’s mission of empowering people and communities for sustainable economic progress.

Why it mattersUgandans seeking financial services will benefit from the bank's expanded capacity and improved leadership.

East African states boost maritime intelligence sharing

16 nations met in Dar es Salaam to build a framework for sharing maritime information and coordinating responses to sea threats.

Governments across the Red Sea and Western Indian Ocean are enhancing maritime surveillance and intelligence sharing to counter rising threats. A meeting in Dar es Salaam from September 15-17, hosted by Tanzania and facilitated by the IMO, brought together representatives from 16 countries. They focused on developing a draft framework for cooperation between national maritime information-sharing centres and regional bodies like the RCOC in Seychelles and the RMIC in Madagascar.

The discussions aimed to create a connected maritime security architecture, addressing issues beyond piracy to include illicit trafficking and geopolitical competition. Delegates also identified gaps in current systems for exchanging maritime information. This initiative follows incidents like the hijacking of MT Honour 25 by Somali pirates in April, which resulted in five crew deaths, and the Kenyan Navy's interception of a dhow carrying US$63 million of methamphetamine in October 2025.

The Kenyan operation highlighted the importance of cross-border intelligence, with information shared through the Regional Narcotics Interagency Fusion Cell in Bahrain and surveillance assistance from Seychelles. The Kenyan Navy intercepted the dhow and escorted it to Mombasa, arresting six crew members.

Why it mattersEast African nations are strengthening maritime security cooperation to protect vital shipping routes, energy transport, and fishing activities from piracy and illicit trafficking.

Business · 2026-11-10

Nuwagaba urges Uganda to fund local research

Bank of Uganda deputy governor says importing solutions weakens the economy and pressures the currency.

Bank of Uganda Deputy Governor Professor Augustus Nuwagaba has urged the government to increase funding for local research and innovation. He warned that Uganda’s continued dependence on imported technologies and solutions is undermining efforts to strengthen the economy. Nuwagaba said universities should lead in developing homegrown solutions for agriculture, manufacturing, and healthcare to reduce the country’s import bill and improve its trade balance. He argued that reliance on imported products contributes to the current account deficit, which pressures the local currency.

Speaking at Uganda Christian University, Nuwagaba challenged higher education institutions to align academic research with national development priorities. He stated that research and innovation are central to achieving the National Development Plan IV and the 10-fold Growth Strategy. Nuwagaba urged universities to prioritize research addressing practical challenges for farmers, manufacturers, and healthcare providers, rather than solely academic publications.

He called for research into drought-resistant crops, improved farming methods, pest control, and affordable storage technologies for agriculture. Nuwagaba also advocated for stronger university-industry partnerships to develop production technologies using local raw materials. In healthcare, he stressed research into affordable diagnostic equipment and locally developed treatments. Nuwagaba warned that meaningful research requires increased government funding, academic freedom, and independence from external agendas.

Why it mattersUganda's continued reliance on imported technologies and solutions is weakening its economy and putting pressure on the local currency.
