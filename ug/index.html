Tooro Kingdom Mourns King Oyo's Death

Traditional mourning rituals have begun for Omukama Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV, who died in the United States.

The Tooro Kingdom has commenced a nine-day period of traditional mourning, known as okugaragara, following the death of Omukama Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV. The King passed away on Thursday evening in the United States while undergoing medical treatment. Cultural rites, including the symbolic turning upside down of the royal drum and the removal of royal regalia by the Basiita clan, are underway to signify the end of his reign and prepare for his burial.

Richard Rwabuhinga, chairperson of the King’s send-off organizing committee, stated that Friday marked the start of traditional norms and rites leading to the burial. During the mourning period, the Tooro Kingdom anthem will not be sung, and the kingdom flag will fly at half-mast. Lion sculptures, symbols of the reigning king's power, will be covered. Ecumenical prayers and services are scheduled daily at Karuzika Palace until the burial.

Custodian of the Kingdom, Omusuga Charles Kamurasi, confirmed that he holds custodianship until a new King is enthroned and that all official communications will come through him. He assured subjects that Karuzika Palace remains open for mourning. Traditional rites performer Zebidayo Mwesige explained that further rituals will unfold as the King's body is repatriated from the United States and taken to a royal palace for burial preparations.

Why it mattersThe Tooro Kingdom is observing a nine-day mourning period and specific cultural rites following the death of King Oyo, impacting traditional practices and royal symbols.

E-Bus Express Launches New Kira Routes, Plans Kampala Expansion

The urban electric bus operator is expanding its fleet to 50 buses and introducing cashless payment systems and free Wi-Fi on its new routes.

E-Bus Express has launched new routes in Kira Municipality, deploying 16 electric buses on three routes: Kisasi–Kyanja–Kungu, Ntinda–Najjera–Nakwero, and Ntinda–Naalya–Namugongo. The company plans to expand its fleet to 50 buses by year-end and extend services to Kampala Central. Passengers will receive free rides until September 6, after which adult fares will start at Shs800, with schoolchildren paying half price. The buses feature a cashless payment system via MTN MoMo, free Wi-Fi, phone charging, and enhanced safety features.

The initiative is seen as a step towards reducing Kampala's traffic congestion, caused by private cars, minibuses, and inadequate infrastructure. Minister Minsa Kabanda urged E-Bus Express and Kira Motors to consider adding washrooms to buses and assured support from the Kampala Capital City Authority. She also advised engagement with existing matatu operators to explain the electric bus model and prevent potential conflicts.

Eng. Jacob Byamukama noted that operating in central Kampala will be challenging due to the lack of dedicated bus lanes, forcing competition with other traffic. He acknowledged that previous bus operations faced similar infrastructure issues. By December, when services expand to Kampala Central, improvements in traffic management and road infrastructure are anticipated. E-Bus Express Chairman Emmanuel Mugunga requested additional routes, stating readiness to deploy 100 buses across the Greater Kampala Metropolitan Area.

Why it mattersThe expansion of E-Bus Express services and fleet directly impacts urban commuters by offering new transport options and improved amenities.

Boda boda rider shot dead during Uganda street cleaning enforcement

A 38-year-old motorcyclist was killed by police on Saturday morning while enforcing movement restrictions for a national cleaning day.

A 38-year-old boda boda rider, Ronald Kizito Jjuuko, was shot dead in Kanaaba, Ndejje, on Saturday morning as police enforced movement restrictions during the National Cleaning Day. The incident occurred shortly after 7 am when an officer attached to Ndejje police post reportedly fired his weapon. Residents stated Jjuuko was riding towards his home and did not immediately notice the officers enforcing the three-hour cleaning exercise, who were positioned in an area with poor visibility.

According to a resident, Hassan Ssezi, the officer shot Jjuuko from behind as he passed the officers' position, causing him to hit a road barricade. Ssezi added that Jjuuko appeared to have worked through the night and was heading home before the restrictions took effect. Some residents also alleged that officers from the Ndejje police post had a history of erratic behavior and drunkenness. Jjuuko's body was transported to Mulago City Mortuary for an autopsy.

Kampala Metropolitan Police spokesperson Rachel Kawala confirmed that an investigation has begun to determine the circumstances of the shooting, including the justification for force and adherence to police procedures. This incident follows police warnings of strict enforcement against movement restriction violators during the cleaning day. Police stated that officers are deployed to maintain order and protect property, but any use of force must be lawful, necessary, and proportionate.

Why it mattersA boda boda rider was killed by police during enforcement of movement restrictions for a national cleaning day.

Zambian golfer Musonda wins Uganda Open Amateur title

Dominic Musonda secured the Johnnie Walker Uganda Open Amateur title with a nine-under par performance, finishing three shots ahead of compatriot Gilbert Chalwe.

Dominic Musonda, an electrical engineering student from Zambia's Nkana Golf Club, claimed the Johnnie Walker Uganda Open Amateur title after finishing nine-under par. He held a significant lead throughout the final round, ensuring his victory even as fellow Zambian Gilbert Chalwe mounted a late challenge. Musonda's win marks a personal triumph, having lost to Uganda's Joseph Cwinya-ai in the previous year's tournament.

Joseph Cwinya-ai, the defending champion, finished in third place after a strong showing in the final two days, but his earlier rounds proved too costly for a repeat victory. His card of 70 secured him third place, one stroke ahead of Ugandan golfer Joseph Kasozi. Several other Ugandan players, including Lawrence Walakira, Joel Okoth, John Musiimenta, Godfrey Kambale, and Sande Lulet, also featured in the top 10.

The tournament also saw a notable moment when Ignatious Twesigye achieved his first career hole-in-one on the fourth hole with a 50-degree wedge shot. The competition, supported by sponsors such as ABSA Bank, MTN, and Uganda Airlines, will conclude with a prize-giving ceremony for Musonda and other notable golfers. The subsidiary round for group B golfers is scheduled for Saturday.

Why it mattersThe event is a golf tournament that directly affects professional golfers and enthusiasts, with potential implications for sports tourism and related business sponsorships.

Court of Appeal Halts Shs 1.27 Billion Debt Enforcement Against Businessman

The Court of Appeal has temporarily stopped the collection of over Shs 1.27 billion from businessman Abid Alam and his company, Casements (Africa) Limited.

The Court of Appeal has halted the enforcement of a High Court decree that ordered Casements (Africa) Limited and Abid Alam to pay Chinese glass manufacturer Weihai Rongsheng Glasswork Co. Limited approximately Shs 1.27 billion ($338,000). The stay of execution was granted on August 28, 2026, by Justice Florence Nakachwa, following an application by Alam and Casements who are challenging the High Court's decision.

Casements and Alam argued that enforcing the debt before their appeal is heard would cause irreparable loss and render their appeal pointless. The dispute arose from three contracts in June 2022 for glass supply totaling $338,800.52. Weihai claims the glass was delivered but the company failed to pay within 120 days, leading to a lawsuit and a default judgment after the company's former lawyers allegedly failed to attend a crucial hearing.

Weihai opposed the stay, asserting that Casements and Alam had acknowledged the debt and defaulted on payment arrangements, and that the intended appeal lacked merit. The Chinese company had initiated execution proceedings, including seeking attachment of Alam’s shares and bank accounts, and his arrest. However, Justice Nakachwa found that the applicants had met the legal threshold for a stay by demonstrating arguable questions for appeal.

Why it mattersBusinessman Abid Alam and his company Casements (Africa) Limited are affected by the halt on debt enforcement, which prevents the immediate collection of over Shs 1.27 billion.
