Museveni commissions $53 million repair facility from Russia

Uganda's President Museveni launched military infrastructure and training facilities as part of defence cooperation with Russia.

President Yoweri Museveni commissioned new military facilities and equipment in Uganda on October 21 and 22, 2026, as part of defence cooperation with Russia. The two-day event included military training, marine security, and modern warfare technology, aimed at boosting the Uganda People’s Defence Forces' (UPDF) capabilities.

Activities began at the Mechanised Warfare College in Kalama, Mubende District, with a digital training classroom for BRDM-2MS armoured vehicles. In Entebbe, marine operational assets including radar, eight boats, and workshops were launched. President Museveni urged the military to embrace AI and automation for responding to security threats.

The programme concluded at Magamaga Cantonment in Jinja District with the commissioning of a mechanised major repair facility valued at $53 million, donated by Russia. President Museveni thanked President Putin for the support, which also included training infrastructure at Kalama and marine facilities in Entebbe.

Why it mattersUgandan military personnel will receive training and equipment maintenance to improve operational capabilities.

Uganda seeks Shs674 billion loan for Bwindi roads

Finance Minister Henry Musasizi proposed borrowing from Citi Bank to pave two roads vital for tourism to Bwindi Impenetrable National Park.

Uganda's government is seeking to borrow up to 674.114 billion Ugandan shillings (approximately €142.69 million) from Citi Bank to upgrade two roads in the Kigezi sub-region. Finance Minister Henry Musasizi presented the proposal to Parliament on Tuesday, aiming to convert the 47-kilometre Hamurwa–Kerere–Kanungu Road and the 32-kilometre Kanyantorogo–Butogota–Buhoma Road to paved standard. These roads are vital arteries to Bwindi Impenetrable National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site known for its mountain gorillas.

Musasizi told Parliament on October 21, 2026, that the project is an investment in tourism revenue, describing tourism as "the only sure way now of attracting revenue." Deputy Speaker Thomas Tayebwa supported the economic rationale, noting a significant price difference in tourism experiences compared to neighbouring countries. The proposal has been referred to the Committee on National Economy for review before any parliamentary approval.

The road upgrades align with President Yoweri Museveni's 2025 campaign pledges to improve infrastructure in the Kigezi region to boost trade and service delivery. Uganda's tourism sector has shown strong recovery, with international visitor arrivals increasing by 19.7 percent to 1.64 million in 2025, generating about US$1.62 billion. Gorilla tourism is a high-yield segment for parks like Bwindi.

Why it mattersUganda's tourism sector, a significant source of revenue and jobs, will benefit from improved infrastructure to key wildlife parks.

Stanbic Bank upgrades Kitgum branch

The bank aims to boost economic growth in Northern Uganda by expanding financial services.

Stanbic Bank Uganda has unveiled its upgraded branch on Market Street in Kitgum, Uganda, to provide expanded financial services and support economic growth in Northern Uganda. The modernised facility was officially opened by Mark Ocitti Ongom, Chief Executive of Stanbic Uganda Holdings Limited, and Sam Fredrick Mwogeza, Executive Director and Head of Personal and Private Banking.

The upgraded branch will serve individuals, businesses, and entrepreneurs in Kitgum, Lamwo, and Agago districts, featuring two multi-currency ATMs, an Executive Banking suite, and meeting rooms. Ocitti stated the branch demonstrates Stanbic’s confidence in East Acholi’s economic potential and the role local businesses play in job creation.

This investment comes as Stanbic Bank Uganda celebrates 35 years of operations in the country. The bank aims to connect customers to its wider ecosystem, including SBG Securities and the Stanbic Business Incubator, to support farmers, traders, entrepreneurs, and women-led businesses.

Why it mattersThe upgraded Stanbic Bank branch in Kitgum will offer expanded financial services to individuals, businesses, and entrepreneurs in the region.

Bank of Uganda and EIB sign climate finance deal

The agreement aims to strengthen the financial sector's ability to manage climate risks and boost green investments.

The Bank of Uganda and the European Investment Bank (EIB) signed a collaboration agreement on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, in Kampala. This partnership will provide technical assistance to Uganda's central bank to improve how financial institutions manage climate-related risks and report on them. The initiative is part of the EIB Global Greening Financial Systems Technical Assistance Programme.

Bank of Uganda Deputy Governor Prof. Augustus Nuwagaba stated that climate change threatens economic performance and financial stability through extreme weather and infrastructure damage. He explained that the agreement will enhance the central bank's ability to help supervised financial institutions identify, assess, manage, and disclose these risks. Uganda needs approximately $28.1 billion in climate-action financing by 2030.

EIB Vice-President Marko Primorac noted that improved risk assessment will help banks make better lending decisions and increase confidence in financing sustainable projects. The partnership will also support the implementation of Uganda’s National Green Taxonomy, launched in 2025, and will extend to selected commercial banks, with Housing Finance Bank being the first expected beneficiary.

Why it mattersUganda's financial sector will receive support to manage climate risks and attract private capital for climate action.

Museveni urges European investors to tap Uganda's opportunities

President Museveni highlighted infrastructure, energy, manufacturing, and agriculture as key sectors for European investment.

President Yoweri Kaguta Museveni has called on European investors to explore Uganda's economic potential, particularly in infrastructure, energy, manufacturing, and agriculture. He stated that investment in these productive sectors is key to expanding markets, creating jobs, and accelerating economic transformation. The President made these remarks today at State House Entebbe during a meeting with European Union Ambassadors and investors.

Museveni emphasized Africa's growing population and expanding market as a major opportunity for European businesses seeking investment and stronger economic partnerships. He noted that Uganda has learned from past challenges, including identity-based politics, and stressed the need for a stable political and economic environment that enables private businesses to invest. The President welcomed ongoing and proposed European investments, seeing them as a way to link Uganda's market with Europe's.

The President identified affordable electricity, railway transport, and accessible financing as key priorities for Uganda's manufacturing sector. He highlighted the Uganda Development Bank as an important source of financing for businesses engaged in productive activities and urged the European Investment Bank to channel more financing into strategic infrastructure like railways and electricity. Museveni also reaffirmed the government’s support for tax incentives, including a 10-year tax holiday for eligible manufacturing investments.

Why it mattersUgandan President Museveni is encouraging European investors to invest in key economic sectors of Uganda, aiming to boost job creation and economic growth.
