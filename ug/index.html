Sport · 2026-10-31

Uganda Tourism Board secures AFCON 2027 marketing deal

The board will use the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations to promote Uganda's tourism attractions internationally.

The Uganda Tourism Board (UTB) signed a partnership agreement with the Confederation of African Football (CAF) in Kampala on Friday, securing official destination sponsorship rights for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON). This deal allows Uganda to promote its tourism attractions through CAF’s international broadcast, digital, and stadium platforms.

Deputy Speaker Thomas Tayebwa stated that the partnership offers Uganda a chance to leverage football's popularity to attract tourists and enhance its international profile. He urged UTB, the Ministry of Tourism, Wildlife and Antiquities, and private sector players to prepare for an influx of visitors by improving transport, accommodation, hospitality, and security.

AFCON 2027, also known as PAMOJA 2027, will be jointly hosted by Uganda, Kenya, and Tanzania from June 19 to July 17, 2027. Tayebwa emphasized that the tournament provides an enormous opportunity for tourism and business, encouraging visitors to explore Uganda and create lasting memories.

Why it mattersUganda will use the AFCON 2027 football tournament to market its tourism attractions to an international audience, potentially attracting tourists and investment.

Health · 2026-10-31

Uganda child dies after epilepsy drug mix-up

16 children were hospitalised in Butebo after health workers administered an epilepsy drug instead of dewormer.

A two-year-old boy died and 16 children were hospitalised in Butebo district after health workers at Kabwangasi health centre III mistakenly gave them an epilepsy drug. The incident occurred during a child health outreach on October 30, 2026, when an epilepsy drug, carbamazepine, was administered instead of the deworming medicine mebendazole. Preliminary investigations suggest the tablets and their containers looked similar, leading to the error.

Dr Julian Abeso, head of paediatrics at Mbale Regional Referral Hospital, stated the children suffered severe central nervous system depression. One child died from respiratory depression about four hours after admission. Butebo district health officer Dr Lawrence Oowunye confirmed 23 children received the wrong medication, with 17 developing severe symptoms. The health workers involved have been suspended pending investigation.

All community health outreaches in the district are suspended for an audit of medicine supply systems. The district is working with the police and the Ministry of Health to investigate. The district security committee recommended the arrest of the individual who issued the medication for the outreach.

Why it mattersA two-year-old child died and 16 others were hospitalised due to a medication mix-up during a health outreach in Butebo.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Ex-minister threatens retired general over coup allegations

Musa Ecweru warned Lt Gen Peter Elwelu to surrender within 24 hours or face death if cornered.

Former state minister Musa Ecweru has threatened to have retired Lt Gen Peter Elwelu killed if he is cornered by security forces. Ecweru, a former Amuria County MP, issued the ultimatum yesterday, Thursday, demanding Elwelu surrender to Soroti Catholic Diocese Bishop Joseph Oliach Eciru within 24 hours. Ecweru stated he would not permit anyone to disrupt the peace in the Teso sub-region.

The Chief of Defence Forces, Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba, accused Elwelu, the former deputy army chief, of plotting a coup against the NRM government. However, Teso elders have criticised Ecweru, accusing him of escalating tensions and overstepping the mandate of security institutions. They urged him to withdraw his remarks and allow official investigations.

Former Uganda ambassador to Nigeria Nelson Ocheger dismissed claims of rising tensions in Teso, calling Ecweru's statements politically motivated. Ocheger stated he found no evidence of tension upon returning from Teso on Tuesday. He advised the public to let the Uganda People’s Defence Forces (UPDF) handle the investigation professionally.

Why it mattersFormer minister Musa Ecweru's threats and the subsequent accusations of coup plotting by Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba directly involve the stability of Uganda's government and security forces.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Salim Saleh found Besigye near death in prison

Retired General Caleb Akandwanaho revealed he intervened to get medical attention for opposition leader Dr Kizza Besigye.

Retired General Caleb Akandwanaho, also known as Salim Saleh, stated he found opposition leader Dr Kizza Besigye in a critical condition at Luzira Prison. Saleh intervened after receiving a directive on August 25 from Chief of Defence Forces General Muhoozi Kainerugaba to handle Besigye's situation. He arranged for Besigye's transfer to the Joint Clinical Research Centre (JCRC) for better medical care.

Saleh described Besigye as "almost dead" when he visited him in prison, prompting his immediate transfer. He explained that JCRC was a facility he helped establish and would personally use for intensive medical needs. This intervention followed Besigye's collapse during a court session in July 2026, after which he was initially taken to Mulago National Referral Hospital.

Saleh addressed chairpersons of parliamentary committees at Namunkekera, Kapeeka, about his reconnection with Besigye. He avoided labelling his engagement as peace talks, stating it was an opportunity to reconnect with an old colleague.

Why it mattersOpposition leader Dr Kizza Besigye's health and detention are of direct concern to Ugandans following his collapse and transfer for medical treatment.

Education · 2026-10-31

Uganda ministry to sanction unauthorised boarding schools

The Ministry of Education and Sports will act against schools operating boarding sections without official authorisation.

The Ministry of Education and Sports in Uganda will impose sanctions on boarding schools operating without official authorisation. Minister of State for Education (Sports) Peter Ogwang stated on Thursday, October 29, 2026, that new guidelines require boarding sections to be properly authorised, resourced, and regularly inspected.

Ogwang emphasised that boarding facilities must meet standards for supervision, abuse reporting, food, nutrition, and hygiene, and that financing and maintenance responsibilities must be clear. Schools must address identified gaps, with action taken against unlawful operations or those exposing learners to unacceptable risk.

The ministry also clarified that while boarding for Universal Primary Education (UPE) and Universal Secondary Education (USE) is voluntary, it must not hinder access to free basic education. MPs welcomed the guidelines, with some calling for regular supervision and consideration for children with special needs and those in hard-to-reach areas.
