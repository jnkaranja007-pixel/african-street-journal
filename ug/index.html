Man shot during Uganda National Cleaning Day operation

Kizito Ronald Jjuuko, 38, died after being shot from behind by police during a National Cleaning Day event.

A 38-year-old boda boda rider, husband, and father, Kizito Ronald Jjuuko, was killed during a police operation on August 29. Jjuuko was shot from behind while police enforced movement restrictions during the second National Cleaning Day. The incident has raised questions about the methods used to ensure compliance with the national initiative.

The National Cleaning Day, aimed at addressing visible refuse in the capital, was met with both support and skepticism due to a perceived trust deficit between the government and citizens. Previous events have seen controversial police actions, including reports of officers using excessive force against individuals during the first cleaning day in July.

Jjuuko's death, described as occurring in "needless circumstances," highlights concerns over the police's approach to enforcing public order during civic initiatives. The incident has prompted calls for an immediate review of the operational procedures employed during these national events.

Uganda Halts New Mineral Licenses for One Month

The government aims to tighten oversight and ensure mineral wealth benefits the nation by pausing new grants for exploration and mining.

Uganda has imposed a one-month moratorium on issuing new mineral licenses and exploration rights, a move announced by Energy Minister Dr. Monica Musenero Masanza. This temporary freeze is intended to tighten government oversight, eliminate speculative claims, and align the mining sector with the country's economic transformation agenda. The minister described the suspension as a regulatory reset, emphasizing the goal of ensuring Uganda's mineral wealth translates into greater domestic value, job creation, and public revenue.

The intervention places the mining sector at the core of Uganda's Tenfold Growth Strategy, which targets an economy of approximately US$500 billion by 2040. Minerals are identified as a priority sector alongside agro-industrialization, tourism, and science, technology, and innovation. This aligns with the Fourth National Development Plan (NDP IV), which prioritizes value addition, industrialization, employment, and private sector growth as key economic drivers.

This moratorium follows a significant expansion in mineral licensing, particularly after the introduction of the digital Mining Cadastre and Registry System. By June 30, 2026, the ministry had granted 212 prospecting licenses, 497 exploration licenses, and various other mining, smelting, refining, processing, and dealer licenses. The Directorate of Geological Survey and Mines acknowledges ongoing data cleaning and system migration as part of the digital system's transition.

Why it mattersThe one-month freeze on new mineral licenses affects investors by pausing their ability to acquire new rights for mineral exploration and exploitation in Uganda.

Uganda parishes become agro-industrial nodes

Government shifts focus for Parish Development Model funds from financing to value-addition hubs for agricultural products.

The Ugandan government is re-envisioning its Parish Development Model (PDM) parishes as potential agro-industrial nodes, moving beyond their current role as financing units. Since 2022, Shs 4.4 trillion has been disbursed to 10,589 parishes to boost production, with over three million people receiving funds by June 2026. However, the government recognizes that simply increasing production is insufficient for sustained household income growth, especially for farmers selling unprocessed goods who face low prices and post-harvest losses.

Value addition is identified as crucial for agro-industrialization, improving product quality, extending shelf life, and accessing higher-value markets. While past programs invested in processing facilities like maize mills and coffee hullers, adoption remains limited due to issues such as unreliable electricity, weak management, and poor market linkages. The government's 2026/27 budget allocates significant funds to agro-industrialization, transport, and energy, aiming to address these infrastructure gaps and support parish-level production and processing systems.

The PDM's integrated design, with pillars focusing on agricultural value chains and infrastructure, should synergize to support value addition and commercialization. Instead of large processing plants in every parish, the strategy suggests Parish SACCOs and producer groups can aggregate produce and operate shared facilities like solar dryers or grain mills on a fee-for-service basis. Infrastructure development should prioritize areas with high production potential and strong market links.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by the shift towards value addition, which aims to improve their access to higher-value markets and reduce post-harvest losses.

Conservationists Restore Forest Corridor for Uganda's Chimpanzees

ECOTRUST and partners are securing and restoring over 50,000 hectares of forest to reconnect isolated chimpanzee populations.

Environmental groups are working to restore a vital forest corridor in Western Uganda to protect the country's endangered eastern chimpanzees. The Budongo-Bugoma landscape, home to approximately 5,000 chimpanzees, has been fragmented by agricultural expansion and oil development, isolating wildlife. The Environmental Conservation Trust of Uganda (ECOTRUST) is leading an initiative to conserve and reconnect over 50,000 hectares of natural forest across Kikuube, Hoima, and Masindi districts.

The project aims to re-establish critical ecological linkages between the Bugoma and Budongo forest reserves, which are essential for the free movement of wildlife. Historically, a lack of sustainable funding has hampered restoration efforts. ECOTRUST, through its Northern Albertine Rift Conservation Group (NARC-G), secured foundational support from the World Land Trust in 2020 and mobilized additional resources in 2022 from IUCN Netherlands and Total Energies to implement the corridor restoration plan.

This restoration is crucial for the long-term survival of the eastern chimpanzee population, preventing genetic isolation. The region is a biodiversity hotspot, supporting 52% of Africa’s bird species and 39% of its mammal species, alongside other primates. The initiative addresses severe environmental pressures that have turned a continuous forest belt into isolated patches on community and private lands.

Why it mattersUganda's eastern chimpanzees are affected by habitat fragmentation, which threatens their long-term survival due to genetic isolation.
