Bank of Uganda keeps key rate at 12 per cent as inflation rises

The central bank held its benchmark rate after an October increase, warning that a weaker shilling and fuel costs could push prices higher still.

The Bank of Uganda kept its Central Bank Rate at 12 per cent on Monday, November 9, 2026, after a special meeting in October lifted it from 9.75 per cent. Governor Michael Atingi-Ego described the tightening as temporary, meant to secure medium-term stability.

Inflation climbed to 5.8 per cent in October, up from 4.6 per cent in September and above the bank's 5 per cent medium-term target. Food prices were up 7.6 per cent, and the bank now expects headline inflation to average 7.5 to 8 per cent over the next year. The shilling averaged 4,170 to the dollar in October, 20.4 per cent weaker than a year earlier.

The bank raised the cash reserve requirement to 13.5 per cent and cut its growth forecast for the 2026/27 financial year to 5.5 to 6 per cent. Borrowers are under pressure, with private sector credit growing 19.8 per cent in the three months to September, and dollar loans averaging 7.7 per cent interest.

Why it mattersBorrowers with dollar loans face heavier repayment costs as the shilling weakens, according to the central bank's monetary report.

Uganda signs deal to host African Union humanitarian agency

The Host Country Agreement was signed at State House, Entebbe, and the agency is expected to be fully operational within three years.

Uganda has signed a Host Country Agreement with the African Union to base the continental humanitarian agency's headquarters in the country. The signing took place on Wednesday at State House, Entebbe, and President Yoweri Kaguta Museveni witnessed it. Attorney General Sam Mayanja signed for the Ugandan government, while Ambassador Amma Twum-Amoah, the AU Commissioner for Health, Humanitarian Affairs and Social Development, signed for the Union.

Museveni said the headquarters would help coordinate aid, including food and other essential supplies for people hit by disasters and emergencies. Mayanja said Uganda scored 85 per cent in the selection process. He said the establishment process will begin in December 2026 and take about three years before the agency is fully operational. Minister of Relief, Disaster Preparedness and Refugees Sam Engola credited Uganda's refugee policy and long record of humanitarian support for its selection.

Twum-Amoah pointed to Uganda's history of solidarity with displaced people and to Kampala's link to the 2009 Kampala Convention, the African Union's legal framework for internally displaced persons. She said the headquarters could become a continental hub for coordinating humanitarian action and mobilising support for affected populations.

Why it mattersPeople affected by disasters across the continent will receive humanitarian aid coordinated from Uganda once the agency is operational, which the government expects within three years.

13 airport authority staff charged over neglect and wire theft

Court papers list far narrower charges than the minister's claims of terrorism and a Shs23 billion repair bill at Entebbe airport.

13 staff of the Uganda Civil Aviation Authority, among them the Chief Engineer, the IT Manager and the Aerodrome Maintenance Head, were brought before the Entebbe Chief Magistrate's Court on 9 November 2026 and denied all charges. They were remanded to Kigo Prison until 18 November. 12 face neglect of duty charges under the Penal Code, and four are accused under the Electricity Act of cutting or removing wires.

State Minister for Works and Transport Fred Byamukama had inspected damaged installations at Entebbe International Airport on 5 November, announced the arrests and said investigators were exploring terrorism charges. He put the repair bill at up to Shs23 billion. No terrorism counts appear on the charge sheet, according to local media accounts of the hearing.

Former Director of Airports and Aviation Security Ayub Sooma, whom the minister called a fugitive, retired from the authority on 17 July 2026. A CID letter dated 6 November invited him to an interview on 9 November about lapses between August and October 2026.

Why it mattersStaff at the authority now face criminal proceedings, and the minister's terrorism claim is not part of the case before the court.

UK seeks coffee investment in Uganda

Britain wants to boost Uganda's coffee sector through processing and market access.

The United Kingdom is seeking to increase investment and trade in Uganda's coffee sector, focusing on processing, value addition, and market access. British High Commissioner Lisa Chesney stated that the UK aims to support Uganda's coffee exports through commercial partnerships and investment across the entire value chain. This initiative aims to connect international buyers with Ugandan farmers, cooperatives, and exporters.

Uganda exported approximately 8.1 million bags of coffee valued at US$2.1 billion last year, highlighting the sector's importance for foreign exchange. Chesney noted that stronger commercial ties could help Ugandan businesses capitalize on growing global demand and encourage investment in processing, packaging, and logistics. She pointed to British company Hats Hats as an example of successful foreign investment in Uganda's coffee market.

The UK itself represents a significant export market, with daily consumption of around 103 million cups of coffee. Accessing this market requires consistent supply, quality, and traceability. The Uganda-UK and Ireland Coffee Familiarisation Trip aims to build these connections, bringing producers closer to international buyers and fostering lasting commercial relationships and investment opportunities.

Why it mattersUgandan coffee farmers and businesses can benefit from increased investment in processing and direct access to the UK market.

Uganda's Tayebwa presses EU for climate finance without more debt

The Deputy Speaker, who leads the OACPS Parliamentary Assembly, wants climate aid for vulnerable states to stop adding to their debt burdens.

Thomas Tayebwa, Uganda's Deputy Speaker of Parliament and president of the Organisation of African, Caribbean and Pacific States (OACPS) Parliamentary Assembly, told delegates in Brussels that member states must demand climate justice. He said the countries causing most global emissions should bear the cost of addressing climate change.

Tayebwa said OACPS countries together emit, on average, no more than 6% of global emissions, yet face rising sea levels, coastal erosion, drought and hunger. The 65th assembly agreed to put climate finance at the centre of talks with the European Union, which begin on Monday and run until Friday.

Dr James Murombedzi, former head of the Africa Climate Policy Centre at the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa, said climate finance is dominated by loans. He warned that relying on them risks worsening the debt of countries already hit by climate disasters.

Why it mattersUganda and other OACPS members are seeking climate finance from the EU that does not add to the debt they already carry from climate disasters.
