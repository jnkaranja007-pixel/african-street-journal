Karamoja hunger deaths prompt questions over state food relief

About 473,000 people in the subregion face high levels of acute food insecurity, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC).

Karamoja, a predominantly nomadic pastoralist subregion in north-eastern Uganda, is facing acute hunger again, with children and the elderly bearing the heaviest burden. In Acherer village, Nadunget Sub-county, Moroto District, 74-year-old Veronica Ngio said she lost her daughter to hunger.

The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) puts about 473,000 people in the subregion in high acute food insecurity, with 5% in emergency conditions and 45% in crisis across Moroto, Kotido and Kaabong. At Moroto Regional Referral Hospital, the paediatric malnutrition burden has risen to 10.5%, up from 7.7% last year. Simon Ondoga, the hospital's Principal Nutritionist, said 53 people had died from malnutrition and food insecurity by mid-July this year, most of them children under five, pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers.

In July 2026, Prime Minister Robinah Nabbanja said the President had directed the release of Shs45 billion for food relief, targeting 313,987 households with more than 9.4 million kilogrammes of food. Local leaders said the food was not enough, and an MP for Nakapiripirit, Longoli Simon Peter, said some households in his constituency, around four to six parishes, did not receive any.

Why it mattersHouseholds in the three hardest-hit districts depend on food relief that local leaders say reached some villages only once and missed others entirely.

Kampala Mayor recounts crash that cost his leg as road deaths rise

Uganda recorded over 5,300 traffic deaths in 2025 as officials push for automated enforcement.

Kampala Lord Mayor Ronald Balimwezo recounted losing a leg in a motorcycle crash while addressing the World Day of Remembrance for Road Crash Victims at Bwaise Transport Terminal on Sunday, November 15. Speaking alongside the Uganda Professional Drivers Network and the Road Safety Coalition Uganda, he said the tragedy pushed him into politics after residents blamed ghosts for accidents caused by potholes.

Police recorded 26,044 road crashes in 2025, according to the Uganda Police Annual Crime Report, up from 25,107 in 2024. Fatal crashes rose from 4,434 to 4,602, and deaths climbed from 5,144 to more than 5,300, averaging at least 14 a day. People under 35 accounted for 56.2% of fatalities, and males made up 83% of those killed.

The rising toll has strengthened support for the government's Intelligent Transport Monitoring System, which features automated enforcement through surveillance cameras. Robert Kisakye, a Senior Licensing Officer at the Ministry of Works and Transport, noted that Uganda has no more than 2,000 traffic officers to enforce regulations nationwide.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Works and Transport is pressing for automated camera enforcement after traffic deaths rose past 5,300 in 2025.

Uganda launches $17.7 billion energy compact

The government targets 85% electricity access and 50% clean cooking by 2030 under a new energy plan.

Uganda and the African Development Bank Group launched a National Energy Compact in Kampala requiring US$17.7 billion in investments over five years. The plan aims to extend modern electricity services to more than 13 million additional people by 2030. National electricity access stood at approximately 60% in 2025, with rural access at 43% and urban access at 80%.

The government plans to reach 75% rural electricity access and 95% urban access by 2030 through grid expansion, about 2,712 mini-grids, and six million solar home systems. The document notes that operational installed capacity must rise from about 2,099 megawatts in 2025 to 2,500 megawatts by the end of the decade. An additional 5,800 megawatts of generation projects are scheduled to reach financial close within the same five-year period.

The Compact seeks to mobilize approximately US$9.4 billion in private capital and forms part of Mission 300, a joint initiative with the World Bank Group. Uganda also targets 50% access to modern cooking solutions by 2030. Hydrological variability, the performance of the Karuma hydropower plant, and transmission bottlenecks are identified as constraints on reliable supply.

Why it mattersInvestors and developers must commit to a US$17.7 billion program over five years to expand electricity and clean cooking infrastructure.

Business · 2026-11-17

Equity banks insist on auction of Dei properties over loan debt

Lenders maintain the sale of commercial and industrial assets to recover over Shs 148 billion is lawful.

Equity Bank Uganda and Equity Bank Kenya said the planned auction of properties belonging to companies owned by businessman Dr Mathias Magoola will proceed to recover over Shs 148 billion in loans. The lenders, represented by Katende, Ssempebwa and Company Advocates, said in a public notice that the sale is lawful under the Mortgage Act and that no court order has stopped it.

The notice responded to a November 9, 2026 warning from Muwema and Company Advocates urging buyers to avoid the purchase. JALD (U) Ltd advertised the intended sale on November 5, listing a pharmaceutical manufacturing facility occupying about 20.673 acres in Matugga, a wheat milling plant in Luzira, and properties in Kampala and Wakiso districts.

The Dei companies argue the auction is premature because the debt remains contested and court proceedings over a loan audit are not concluded. The dispute touches government investment of Shs 723.4 billion in Dei BioPharma, which the Parliamentary Committee on Presidential Affairs has previously questioned.

Why it mattersBuyers of the listed Kampala, Wakiso and Matugga properties face a sale the companies say is premature while the audit dispute is unresolved.

Business · 2026-11-17

MTN Uganda awards Shs 500m to community groups

25 community organisations receive equipment worth Shs 20 million each under the Changemakers initiative.

MTN Uganda Foundation has allocated Shs 500 million to back 25 community groups tackling health, education, water and sanitation, environmental conservation and economic empowerment across the country. The recipients were chosen from 2,607 applications spanning five regions in the fourth phase of the Changemakers programme, bringing total scheme funding to Shs 2 billion across 100 total projects.

Among the selected bodies is Beles Bubu Africa, a refugee-led cultural and media group based in Bunga, Kampala, which received ICT equipment valued at Shs 20 million. Founder Kisanet Tedros stated that the grant will allow the organisation to broaden its educational offerings for children and refugees. MTN Uganda chief executive officer Sylvia Mulinge noted that the initiative targets grassroots solutions developed by individuals addressing local needs.

The first three phases of the programme backed 75 community projects with Shs 1.5 billion, reaching more than 119,000 people. Each of the 25 new beneficiaries receives in-kind support and equipment valued at Shs 20 million to scale their operations.
