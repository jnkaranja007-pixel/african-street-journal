WHO declares Uganda Ebola-free, hands over mobile lab

The World Health Organization officially declared Uganda free of Ebola after the country completed 42 days without a new confirmed case, a global standard for ending outbreaks.

Uganda has been declared Ebola-free by the World Health Organization (WHO) following a 42-day period without any new confirmed cases, a global standard for declaring an outbreak over. This declaration, made by Dr. Kasonde Mwinga, the WHO Representative in Uganda, provides additional assurance that no transmission chains linked to the imported case in May were missed.

The Ministry of Health had previously declared the country Ebola-free almost a month earlier, based on an assessment of no active transmission. Dr. Diana Atwine, Permanent Secretary in the Ministry of Health, stated that this official declaration should prompt countries that imposed travel restrictions on Uganda to lift them, as these measures have impacted the economy.

Despite the declaration, the Ministry of Health will maintain surveillance, including monitoring deaths and screening travelers, especially at borders, due to ongoing Ebola transmission in the Democratic Republic of Congo. WHO also handed over a truck-mounted mobile laboratory, valued at 500,000 US dollars and donated by the African Development Bank, to strengthen Uganda's preparedness for public health emergencies.

Why it mattersUgandans and international travelers are affected by the lifting of travel restrictions and continued health surveillance measures at borders.

Kassanda Police Investigate Death of Theft Suspect After Mob Attack

Police in Kassanda District have opened a murder investigation after Derrick Kasumba, a 30-year-old burglary and theft suspect, died following a mob assault shortly.

Derrick Kasumba, also known as Kalevu, died on August 23, 2026, after being intercepted and assaulted by a mob in Lwenyange Village. Kasumba had escaped from Manyogaseka Sub-county Police Station at approximately 9 am while being escorted to the toilet, according to Wamala Region Police spokesperson SSP Lameck Kigozi.

Police Constable Aaron Nalyanya, who was guarding Kasumba, reportedly failed to prevent his escape. About an hour later, police received information that Kasumba was being assaulted by a mob. PC Nalyanya was instructed to rescue and re-arrest him, finding the mob assaulting Kasumba before they fled.

Kasumba was transported back to the police station, but his condition worsened, and he was evacuated to Kyansaswa Health Centre III, where he was pronounced dead about 40 minutes after arrival. Police are now investigating the murder, working to identify and arrest members of the mob, and examining the circumstances of Kasumba's escape and death.

Why it mattersThis event highlights a breakdown in law enforcement and public trust, directly affecting the safety of individuals in police custody and the community's adherence to legal processes.

Gen. Muhoozi, Tyrese Gibson discuss Uganda tourism and investment

General Muhoozi Kainerugaba and American actor Tyrese Gibson met in Entebbe to explore promoting Uganda as a tourism and investment destination.

Chief of Defence Forces, Gen. Muhoozi Kainerugaba, held discussions with American actor and musician Tyrese Gibson in Entebbe to promote Uganda's tourism and investment opportunities. The meeting focused on leveraging Uganda's natural resources, cultural heritage, and growing creative economy to attract international attention and partnerships. Gibson's visit comes as Uganda aims to position tourism and creative arts as key contributors to economic growth.

The discussions explored using global entertainment personalities to showcase Uganda's tourism potential and strengthen ties between its creative industry and international networks. This includes creating opportunities for local filmmakers, actors, musicians, and technicians to gain exposure, while attracting productions and investments. Uganda possesses diverse attractions, including mountain gorillas, wildlife, forests, lakes, and rivers.

Beyond tourism, the engagement also touched on the creative industry's role in youth empowerment, providing avenues for skills development, employment, and entrepreneurship for Uganda's large, youthful population. Gibson also met with President Yoweri Kaguta Museveni to discuss creative arts, tourism, and promoting Africa's shared heritage, aiming to strengthen Uganda's film and entertainment industry as an economic driver.

Why it mattersUgandan youth and creative professionals are affected by potential new opportunities for skills development, employment, and entrepreneurship in the film, music, digital content production, tourism, and related services sectors.

Police arrest former Sky Pins director for alleged recruitment fraud

Christian Asiimwe, known as Don Chris, was arrested in Zana for allegedly defrauding over 500 job seekers of up to Shs 1.75 billion.

Police arrested Christian Asiimwe, also known as Don Chris, a former director of the defunct labour recruitment company Sky Pins, on Thursday, August 27, 2026. He faces allegations of obtaining money by false pretenses and fraudulent labour recruitment. Asiimwe was apprehended at a bar in Zana, Makindye-Ssabagabo Municipality, Wakiso District, following a nationwide manhunt that began in June 2025.

Asiimwe is accused of collecting between Shs 5 million and Shs 10 million from each of more than 500 alleged victims between 2022 and 2025. He allegedly promised them jobs abroad in countries like Canada, the United Kingdom, Qatar, and Dubai, for roles such as waiters, cleaners, and caregivers. The total amount collected from victims is estimated to be between Shs 1.4 billion and Shs 1.75 billion.

Investigations by the Criminal Investigations Directorate (CID) intensified after 85 job seekers petitioned the state minister for Youth and Children Affairs in June 2025. Sky Pins had advertised extensively, promising employment on credit with an upfront payment. None of the applicants reportedly traveled as promised, and the company's offices were found abandoned. Asiimwe is currently in police custody, and authorities urge caution when dealing with recruitment companies.

Why it mattersHundreds of job seekers lost significant amounts of money to a fraudulent scheme, directly impacting their financial stability and employment prospects.

Uganda targets mortgage financing to boost tenfold economic growth

Uganda's ambition to increase its economy from US$50 billion to US$500 billion by 2040 relies on stronger financial systems, with mortgage financing identified as a key mechanism.

Uganda aims to leverage mortgage financing to achieve its ambitious goal of tenfold economic growth by 2040, as highlighted by Andrew Musanje. The country's housing sector, which currently contributes about 11 percent to GDP, faces a deficit of 2.4 million units, with only about 60,000 units constructed annually against an estimated demand of 200,000.

Mortgage loans account for less than 1 percent of Uganda's GDP, a figure significantly lower than many emerging economies. This underdevelopment in housing finance means many Ugandans rely on personal savings to build homes, often resulting in substandard structures and tying up household capital for years, according to Musanje.

The National Development Plan IV recognizes affordable housing as crucial for Uganda's transformation. Increased mortgage financing could accelerate construction, create jobs across various sectors like manufacturing and banking, and allow households to convert future income into productive investments, thereby fostering asset accumulation and financial security.

Why it mattersUgandan households and businesses are affected by the current lack of affordable mortgage financing, which limits their ability to invest in productive assets and acquire decent housing.
