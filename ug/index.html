World Vision Uganda Celebrates 40 Years, Launches 2030 Strategy

The organization marked four decades of operation in Uganda by unveiling its new strategic plan for 2026-2030, aiming to continue transforming lives and communities.

World Vision Uganda celebrated its 40th anniversary on Wednesday, marking four decades since its founding in 1986 amidst the Luwero Triangle conflict. The event, held at Speke Resort Munyonyo, also served as the launch of its new Strategic Plan for 2026–2030. Prime Minister Robinah Nabbanja, representing President Yoweri Museveni, lauded the organization's partnership with the government, highlighting its contributions to Uganda's social and economic development over the years.

The organization has expanded its reach to over 50 districts through 52 Area Development Programmes, impacting millions of lives through interventions in health, nutrition, education, child protection, resilience, livelihoods, water, sanitation, and hygiene. Prime Minister Nabbanja noted that areas where World Vision operates have seen reduced household poverty, fewer child marriages, and improved education outcomes, attributing these successes to the organization's collaborative efforts with the government's development agenda.

World Vision Uganda's work since 1986 has focused on transforming communities and restoring dignity, particularly for children. The commemoration brought together government leaders, development partners, donors, faith leaders, civil society organizations, and community representatives. Children who have benefited from World Vision's programs shared their personal stories, underscoring the organization's impact on their young lives and futures.

Why it mattersFarmers may benefit from World Vision's focus on resilience and livelihoods, which could include agricultural support and improved water access.

Attorney General Warns Against Expressway Contract Termination

Uganda risks costly international arbitration if the contract with French operator EGIS Road Operation S.A.

The Attorney General has cautioned against the immediate termination of the contract for the Kampala-Entebbe Expressway's private operator, warning that Uganda could face expensive international arbitration if contractual procedures are not followed. This warning comes as Parliament's Committee on Physical Infrastructure recommended terminating the agreement with French company EGIS Road Operation S.A. and transferring operations to the Ministry of Works and Transport, with the Uganda Revenue Authority taking over toll collection.

The committee cited incomplete installations of traffic monitoring and vehicle classification systems, inadequate lighting and fencing, and shortcomings in weigh-in-motion systems as justification for direct government management. However, Deputy Attorney General Jackson Karugaba Kafuuzi advised that the operator must first be formally notified of any breaches and given 14 days to rectify them, with termination only possible after specified contractual thresholds for persistent failure are met.

The five-year contract, valued at Shs122.86 billion, began on May 24, 2021, and includes obligations beyond toll collection, such as road-safety improvements and infrastructure maintenance. The Attorney General recommended that the Ministry of Works and Transport conduct a comprehensive assessment to determine if EGIS's performance failures meet the contractual threshold for termination, rather than immediate contract termination.

Why it mattersInvestors face potential financial risks from costly international arbitration if the government unilaterally terminates the expressway operator's contract.

Tabula captains Uganda's 37-rider motocross team in Namibia

Gift Tabula Ssebuguzi leads Uganda at the FIM Motocross of African Nations Championship in Windhoek, aiming to improve on recent podium finishes with a record 10 female riders.

Uganda's Motocross Cranes are set to compete in the 2026 FIM Motocross of African Nations Championship in Windhoek, Namibia, this weekend, with Gift Tabula Ssebuguzi captaining the team for the second consecutive year. The event will feature approximately 180 riders from across Africa at the Gallina Motocross Track. Tabula, a former MX Lite champion, led Uganda to third place last year in Zimbabwe and has secured two previous African Nations class championships.

The Ugandan contingent includes a record 37 riders, with 10 female competitors, the highest number ever fielded by a nation at the MXoAN. Tabula's younger sister, Ronnieta Ssebuguzi Muganzi, will also compete in the MX 50 Ladies class. The team's preparation has received support from the National Council of Sports, with General Secretary Dr Bernard Patrick Ogwel commending the Federation of Motorsport Clubs of Uganda for its governance and grassroots development efforts.

Uganda aims to build on its consistent recent success, having finished third in Cape Town in 2023, runner-up in Marrakesh in 2024, and third again in 2025. Much of the team's success will depend on Tabula's experience and leadership, both on the track and in inspiring the diverse, young team. His father, Ronald Ssebuguzi, expressed pride in his son's leadership role, urging him to support his fellow riders.

Why it mattersUgandan motocross riders and their families are directly affected by the team's participation and performance at the continental championship, with leadership and national representation at stake.

Stanbic Bank Funds Marathon for Maternal Health Services

The bank is sponsoring the second annual Independence Marathon, themed "Save a Mother," with proceeds directed to Mengo Hospital's maternal health programs.

Stanbic Bank Uganda has committed to supporting the second Independence Marathon, scheduled for Sunday, October 4, under the banner "Save a Mother." The initiative aims to raise funds to enhance maternal health services at Mengo Hospital. Diana Ondoga, manager for Corporate and Social Investment at Stanbic Bank Uganda, stated that the partnership highlights sport's capacity to unite communities for national causes.

The marathon is expected to draw participation from professional and amateur runners, corporate teams, families, and the general public. The Mandela National Stadium will serve as the start and finish line, with the Northern Bypass designated as the route for the professionally managed and timed race. This event seeks to leverage community engagement for a critical health issue.

Uganda's maternal mortality ratio was recorded at 207 deaths per 100,000 live births in the 2024 National Population and Housing Census, a decrease from 336 deaths per 100,000 in 2016. Mengo hospital's Dr. Frank Kakuba and safe-motherhood advocate Daniel Lubwama Kigozi, also known as Navio, have urged increased voluntary blood donations to aid mothers experiencing severe bleeding during childbirth.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects maternal health services at Mengo Hospital by providing funding, and it calls for increased blood donations to support mothers during childbirth.
