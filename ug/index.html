Uganda Breweries fined Shs 1.5 billion in commercial dispute

The Supreme Court also quashed a murder conviction, while local officials face corruption charges.

Uganda Breweries has been ordered to pay over Shs 1.5 billion following a commercial dispute that concluded last week. The ruling came as courts across Uganda delivered several significant judgments, including the Supreme Court overturning a murder conviction and four local government officials facing corruption allegations.

In the case involving the brewery, the specific details of the commercial dispute were not provided, but the judgment resulted in a substantial financial penalty. This case highlights ongoing legal and commercial activities within the country.

The Supreme Court's decision to quash the murder conviction of Nakku Jackline, who had served nearly six years of an 18-year sentence, also marked a significant legal development. Separately, four local government officials from Mitooma District and Mbarara City were arraigned on charges of abuse of office and financial mismanagement, indicating a broader anti-corruption effort.

Rubondo Departs Petroleum Authority, Acting Head Takes Over

Ernest Rubondo has completed his second term as Executive Director of the Petroleum Authority of Uganda, with an acting replacement appointed as commercial oil production nears.

Ernest Rubondo has left his position as Executive Director of the Petroleum Authority of Uganda (PAU) after serving the maximum two terms. The PAU Board and the Ministry of Energy have appointed the Director of Finance, Otonga Ochan, as acting head while a permanent successor is sought. Rubondo’s decade-long tenure concluded on August 31, 2026, a period marked by significant progress towards Uganda's first oil.

The Ministry of Energy and Mineral Development confirmed the transition, with Permanent Secretary Irene Pauline Batebe stating that an acting director is in place as they prepare to announce a substantive appointment. Ochan, a Fellow of the Association of Chartered Certified Accountants with over 21 years of experience in financial management and strategic planning, steps into the role. His appointment comes as the PAU prepares to regulate the sector during the critical shift from project development to commercial production.

Rubondo, the inaugural Executive Director, oversaw regulatory aspects of major projects like the Kingfisher and Tilenga oil fields and the East African Crude Oil Pipeline. The Kingfisher Development Area is nearing completion, with 98 percent readiness for first oil. The PAU had advertised the Executive Director position in December 2025, anticipating a smooth handover, but the appointment process was not finalized before Rubondo's departure.

DPP wants more plea-bargaining sessions to clear Kiryandongo case backlog

Director of Public Prosecutions Lino Anguzu called for doubling plea-bargaining sessions to four times annually to expedite justice for refugees.

Director of Public Prosecutions Lino Anguzu has proposed increasing plea-bargaining sessions in Kiryandongo Refugee Settlement from twice to four times a year. This measure aims to address a growing backlog of criminal cases and improve access to justice for the refugee population. Anguzu made the call while officiating at a Mobile Court Session, an initiative organized by the Refugee Law Project and supported by the Kingdom of the Netherlands to bring legal services closer to vulnerable communities.

Anguzu emphasized that timely case resolution is crucial for maintaining peace, fostering economic growth, and creating a conducive environment for development. He urged refugees to respect Ugandan laws and cooperate with justice institutions, noting that plea bargaining should be used for genuine closure, not solely to avoid prison. The DPP also highlighted a shortage of prosecutors in Kiryandongo, a district hosting approximately 170,000 refugees from 15 countries, and stressed the need for special consideration.

The settlement commandant, Vincent Amaruma Mariza, explained that the mobile court was established due to dwindling resources that made transporting refugees to the main court challenging. He noted that about three-quarters of attendees at the session were refugees, and reiterated concerns about the prosecutor shortage amidst an increasing crime rate. Kiryandongo Chief Magistrate Samson Abitti agreed that increased plea bargaining could significantly reduce the case backlog.

Why it mattersRefugees in Kiryandongo are affected by the proposed increase in plea-bargaining sessions as it aims to provide them with more accessible and timely justice for criminal cases.
