Kampala traders shut shops to protest URA taxes and enforcement

The shutdown closed arcades and commercial buildings as traders called for talks with the Uganda Revenue Authority and a review of business costs.

Traders shut shops across downtown Kampala on Tuesday, 17 November, protesting what they described as excessive taxes and harsh enforcement by the Uganda Revenue Authority (URA). Several arcades and commercial buildings, including Skylight, Dembe and Pentagon arcades, remained closed during the day.

Yusuf Kalungi, chairperson of the Kikubo business hub, said traders chose a peaceful shutdown after written requests for a meeting with URA. He said the closure would continue until the government addressed their concerns. Kampala City Traders Association members are seeking changes to tax policy, enforcement and the cost of doing business.

Trader Robert Kagwa Matovu said taxes and rent were making businesses unsustainable, while another trader, Mathias Bwanika, said earnings largely went to those costs. Traders also want representation in Parliament, Kalungi said. Traffic police, military police and Kampala Capital City Authority enforcement teams patrolled the city centre.

Why it mattersShop owners say rent and taxes are consuming earnings, and the shutdown is to continue until the government hears their concerns.

Uganda's GROW project has lent Shs161 billion to women-owned businesses

The World Bank-backed programme has made 10,096 loans, with repayment terms of up to 24 months and interest capped at 10 per cent a year.

Uganda's Generating Growth Opportunities and Productivity for Women Enterprises (GROW) Project had recorded 10,096 loans with cumulative disbursements of about Shs161 billion as of June 30, 2026, according to project coordinator Dr Aisha Ruth Biyinzika Kasolo. The project is run by the Ministry of Gender, Labour and Social Development with the Private Sector Foundation Uganda.

Eligible micro and small enterprises must be at least 51 per cent women-owned. Loans range from Shs2 million to Shs200 million, with a minimum of Shs1 million for refugee entrepreneurs, and are repayable over up to 24 months at an annual interest rate capped at 10 per cent. 14 participating financial institutions, including the cooperative lenders UGAFODE, Opportunity Bank and EBO Bank, handle the lending.

Kasolo said credit is paired with business-development services, technical assistance, mentorship, networking and market linkages. The facility covers agriculture, agro-processing, manufacturing, construction, science and technology, hospitality, creative arts and ICT.

Why it mattersWomen running small firms in these sectors can borrow up to Shs200 million at a capped 10 per cent annual rate, repayable over 24 months.

Health Ministry schedules 3.3 million-net drive for Kampala and Wakiso

The three-day distribution, set for November 20 to 22, will send 1,168,700 nets to Kampala and 2,000,216 to Wakiso.

Uganda’s Ministry of Health has scheduled its final 2026 mosquito-net distribution phase for Kampala and Wakiso District, with approximately 3.3 million insecticide-treated nets due to be distributed from November 20 to 22. The ministry says the campaign targets vulnerable people, particularly children under five and pregnant women.

Kampala is allocated 1,168,700 nets for an estimated two million residents, while Wakiso will receive 2,000,216 for about 3.9 million people. The distribution will run through 32 operational units aligned with administrative divisions and municipalities, with access prioritised for low-income urban communities. Acting malaria control programme head Dr Richard Kabanda said Uganda recorded more than 11.7 million malaria cases in 2025 and over 2,300 deaths; more than half of those who died were children under five.

Kabanda said consistent, proper net use can cut malaria transmission by about 50 per cent and reduce childhood mortality by up to 25 per cent. The Kampala-Wakiso phase follows the distribution of more than 17 million nets across several sub-regions since January. The ministry advises recipients to hang new nets for 24 hours before use and seek medical care promptly for malaria symptoms.

Why it mattersResidents of Kampala and Wakiso are due to receive nets through 32 distribution units from November 20 to 22, with low-income urban communities among those targeted.

Savanna Fibre targets 1 million more homes across East Africa in 2027

The rollout follows 250,000 active subscribers in Uganda, Tanzania and Kenya and would take the company's network past 2 million homes.

Savanna Fibre plans to extend its network to 1 million additional homes across Uganda, Tanzania and Kenya in 2027, after passing 250,000 active subscribers across the three countries. The company announced the target in Cape Town on Monday, ahead of the Africa Tech Festival.

Savanna Telecom (Africa) Limited, the Mauritius-based parent company, said engineering designs are complete and construction will run evenly over 12 months. Once built, the new home passes, meaning homes within reach of the network, will take its footprint from slightly more than 1 million to past 2 million. A sizeable portion will serve towns and suburbs where no operator has laid fibre before.

Savanna says it holds 56% of Uganda's fibre-to-the-home market but has not disclosed how many of its subscribers are in the country. Across the nation, the Uganda Communications Commission logged roughly 196,000 active fixed internet subscriptions in the third quarter of 2025. The 2027 build in Uganda will fall under the commission's optical-fibre installation framework, which took effect on Jan. 1.

Why it mattersThe 2027 build in Uganda falls under the Uganda Communications Commission's optical-fibre installation framework, in force since Jan. 1.

Business · 2026-11-18

Absa Uganda completes Standard Chartered retail and wealth banking acquisition

The transfer brings 162 former Standard Chartered employees to Absa, and migrated customers keep their existing products and services.

Absa Bank Uganda has completed its acquisition of Standard Chartered Bank Uganda’s wealth and retail banking business, a deal first announced in October 2025. The transfer expands Absa’s personal, affluent wealth and business banking operations in Uganda.

The bank said 162 former Standard Chartered employees have joined Absa. Customers have moved to Absa while retaining their existing products and services, and can use its digital platforms, branches and ATMs. Absa will assist customers with account access, digital registration, cards and other service enquiries.

Absa Managing Director David Wandera said the bank would combine the two institutions’ expertise in relationship management, financial advice and personalised banking. The expanded offer includes transactional banking, savings, lending and investment services; Absa also says it has upgraded its digital platforms and introduced Absa Investments, an investment and advisory service.

Why it mattersThe 162 transferred employees join Absa, while migrated customers retain their products and can seek help with account access, cards and digital registration.
