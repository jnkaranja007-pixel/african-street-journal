Ugandan MPs demand returns from Malaysia mission's diplomacy funds

The eight-member delegation is examining whether taxpayer money sent abroad delivers trade, investment and tourism gains rather than VIP allowances.

Source: Watchdog Uganda · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Ugandan MPs are pressing their High Commission in Malaysia to show measurable returns from economic and commercial diplomacy spending. The eight-member Parliamentary Committee on Foreign Affairs delegation, led by vice-chair Bruce Balaba, arrived in Kuala Lumpur for an oversight mission scheduled for November 22–30.

At a session hosted by High Commissioner Betty Bigombe, Kassanda South MP Abdul Bisaso warned that the funds should support market access, investment and exports, not allowances or personal spending. The mission's November performance brief cited coffee orders: COFFEX, a regional importer and roaster, began importing Ugandan robusta after a Kuala Lumpur tasting in September 2025, replacing Vietnamese shipments; De'Esprezza Coffee imports five containers a year, while 2nd Mile Specialty Coffee imports 10 tonnes.

The High Commission said it is accredited to nine countries in the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN), a bloc of 700 million people. It reported that the three importers plan to expand orders across the regional market.

Why it mattersUganda's High Commission must account to the MPs for spending on commercial diplomacy, with coffee orders and plans to expand providing measures of its reported returns.
