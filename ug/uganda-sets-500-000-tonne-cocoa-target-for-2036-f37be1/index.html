Uganda sets 500,000-tonne cocoa target for 2036

The 10-year strategy also targets US$3 billion in annual export earnings and higher domestic processing.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Uganda has launched a 10-year cocoa strategy targeting annual production of 500,000 metric tonnes by 2036, up from approximately 72,000 tonnes. The plan also targets annual export earnings of US$3 billion and aims to raise domestic processing from less than five per cent to 30% of national output.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Animal Industry and Fisheries launched the strategy, developed with support from the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, Enabel and industry stakeholders. More than 100,000 farming households are supported by the industry. The plan targets average farm productivity of 1,000 kilogrammes per hectare, compared with 530 kilogrammes, and at least 45% participation by women and young people in the value chain.

Cocoa export earnings rose from $70.6 million in the 2015/2016 financial year to US$620.4 million in 2024/2025, according to ministry and Bank of Uganda figures. Implementation is intended to address low productivity, limited processing and access to finance, as well as climate risks and traceability requirements.

Why it mattersCocoa farmers are covered by productivity targets rising from 530 kilogrammes to 1,000 kilogrammes per hectare, while the strategy targets 500,000 tonnes of annual production by 2036.
