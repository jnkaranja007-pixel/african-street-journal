Uganda Urges Refugees to Respect Laws Amidst Unrest

Authorities call for refugee compliance with Ugandan laws and public order following riots and property destruction in West Nile.

Source: The Independent Uganda · 2026-10-21 · 2 min read

WhatsApp X

Refugees and asylum seekers in Uganda's West Nile region are being urged to understand and adhere to Ugandan laws to foster peaceful coexistence and prevent conflicts. This initiative follows recent riots at Imvepi Refugee Settlement, which resulted in fatalities and significant property damage. West Nile hosts over 1.7 million refugees, primarily from South Sudan and the Democratic Republic of Congo, spread across several districts and urban centers.

Francis Ayume, Executive Director of Refugee Network-RedNet, stated that many refugees are unaware of their rights and obligations, making them susceptible to legal violations. He emphasized that refugees are entitled to documentation, fair treatment, social services, and the ability to open financial accounts, provided they meet legal requirements. Sustained awareness campaigns are deemed crucial for refugees to grasp the laws governing their stay and their duties to the government and host communities.

Moses Batali, chairperson of the South Sudanese Refugees Association in West Nile, highlighted that refugees must register with the Office of the Prime Minister, undergo security screening, and report life changes. He cautioned against activities threatening national security or public order, noting that those employed must pay taxes. Urban refugees have also raised concerns about higher school fees compared to local learners.

Why it mattersRefugees are affected by needing to understand and follow Ugandan laws to avoid conflict and legal issues.
