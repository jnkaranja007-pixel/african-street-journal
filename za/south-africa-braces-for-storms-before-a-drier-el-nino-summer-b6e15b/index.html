South Africa braces for storms before a drier El Niño summer

Forecasters say thunderstorms are due over the Free State and Gauteng, and temperatures will climb to 32ºC-36ºC once the cloud over the interior clears.

Sources: The Citizen SA and The South African · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Heavy rain, hail and severe thunderstorms have struck many parts of the country as El Niño takes hold. South African Weather Service senior forecaster Tokelo Chiloane said temperatures are set to rise once cloud over the interior breaks, mostly staying between 32ºC and 36ºC.

Weather forecaster Juandre Vorster said the storms were caused by the El Niño effect and that another round is expected over the Free State and Gauteng. Michelle du Plessis of Vox said the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration expects the strong-to-very strong event to last through January to March.

Du Plessis said the South African Weather Service's outlook shows a higher chance of below-normal rain across large parts of the summer rainfall region from November to March. She said climate models still predict the event will peak in December or January.

Why it mattersFarmers in the summer rainfall region face a higher chance of below-normal rain from November to March, with the strongest effects expected in December and January.
