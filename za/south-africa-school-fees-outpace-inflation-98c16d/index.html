South Africa school fees outpace inflation

Public primary and secondary school fees have risen significantly faster than consumer inflation over six years.

Source: Mail & Guardian · 2026-11-10 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Public school fees in South Africa have increased at rates significantly higher than consumer inflation, making education harder to afford. Primary school fees rose by an average of 6.5% annually and secondary school fees by 7.1% over six years, outpacing a cumulative consumer inflation of about 36%. This rise means families pay more for education while the economy struggles to create jobs.

The Competition Commission reported these figures, showing that primary school fees increased by approximately 46% and secondary school fees by 51% over the period. This situation occurs as South Africa faces an unsustainably high youth unemployment rate. The economy, growing at around 1.1%, cannot generate enough opportunities for young people to secure financial stability after completing their education.

This trend contributes to a broader societal pressure, where the economic escalator for upward mobility has broken down. The rising cost of education, coupled with stagnant wages and limited job prospects, affects social stability. This is similar to pressures seen in Western Europe, though South Africa faces chronic service delivery protests and deep structural exclusion.

Why it mattersSouth African families are paying more for their children's education while facing a difficult job market.
