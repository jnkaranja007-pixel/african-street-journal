four arrested in foiled Limpopo ATM bombing plot

Police seized an AK-47 rifle, 36 rounds of ammunition, and four cellphones from suspects in the Vaalbank area.

Law enforcement officers in Limpopo foiled a suspected ATM bombing plot by arresting four suspects on Saturday, 24 October 2026, in the Zebediela area. The suspects were allegedly planning a series of robberies targeting ATMs and were found in possession of unlicensed firearms and explosives.

Police spokesperson Colonel Dimakatso Nevhuhulwi stated that a multidisciplinary team, including SAPS units and private security, intercepted the suspects' silver Toyota Starlet along the R568 in the Vaalbank area after a pursuit. Recovered items included an AK-47 rifle, two rifle magazines, 36 rounds of live rifle ammunition, and four cellphones. The vehicle was also seized.

The arrested suspects, aged 28, 30, 46, and 50, are expected to appear in court soon. Investigations are ongoing to determine their alleged involvement in other serious crimes, including ATM bombings and CIT robberies, with police believing the arrests have dealt a significant blow to criminal activities.

Why it mattersFour individuals were arrested in Limpopo for allegedly planning ATM bombings and robberies.

Eastern Cape faces severe thunderstorms on Monday

The South African Weather Service issued a Yellow Level 2 warning for heavy downpours, strong winds, and hail.

The South African Weather Service (SAWS) issued a Yellow Level 2 warning for severe thunderstorms across the eastern half of the Eastern Cape on Monday, October 26, 2026. The warning is active from 08:00 to 20:59, with storms potentially bringing heavy downpours, strong winds, and hail.

These severe weather conditions could damage homes, property, and temporary structures, while heavy rainfall may flood roads and low-lying bridges, making travel difficult. Strong winds and hail also pose a risk to crops and livestock, and there is a possibility of disruptions to electricity and communication services.

SAWS advised residents to avoid flooded roads and bridges, stay indoors during storms, and for motorists to drive carefully. Farmers were encouraged to move livestock to safer areas, and all residents were urged to follow SAWS updates.

Why it mattersResidents in the eastern half of the Eastern Cape face potential damage to property, infrastructure, and risks to livestock due to severe thunderstorms.

Business · 2026-10-26

South Africa cuts fuel levy by up to R3 per litre

Motorists will see temporary relief from November 4, with further reductions planned until January 5.

South Africa will temporarily cut its fuel levy by up to R3 per litre starting November 4, 2026, announced Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana. This reduction aims to ease the cost of living for households and businesses affected by rising international oil prices and Middle East conflict. The initial levy cut will last until December 1, 2026, followed by smaller reductions until January 5, 2027.

The November fuel levy cut amounts to R1.50 per litre for petrol and R3 per litre for diesel. For a 50-litre petrol tank, this offers a potential saving of R75, while a diesel vehicle of the same capacity could save R150. From December 1, these savings will decrease to R50 for petrol and R75 for diesel. The Central Energy Fund (CEF) expects to lose R10 billion in revenue over the two months due to these cuts.

Diesel prices have significantly increased in 2026, impacting freight and agriculture. The levy reduction is intended to alleviate pressure on businesses and motorists until prices stabilize. Motorists are advised to monitor official fuel price announcements before refueling, as this relief is not a permanent solution to global energy price volatility.

Why it mattersSouth African motorists and businesses will benefit from lower fuel prices, easing the burden of increased living costs.
