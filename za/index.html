UDM councillor shot dead in Gqeberha

Luxolo Namette was killed on Saturday, November 7, 2026, as vote counting for local elections continues.

United Democratic Movement (UDM) councillor Luxolo Namette was shot dead on Saturday, November 7, 2026, in Gqeberha, Eastern Cape. The 45-year-old man was found with a gunshot wound to the head on Umnulu Street in Motherwell at approximately 3:50pm. Motherwell police have opened a murder case and are investigating the circumstances and motive.

The UDM confirmed Namette's death and expressed shock, with secretary-general Bulelani Bobotyane calling the killing "tragic" and stating it cast a "dark shadow" over the party's election efforts. The party suspects the murder may be politically motivated due to Namette's public role and plans to refer the case to the police’s political killings task team.

Eastern Cape police spokesperson Brigadier Nobuntu Gantana stated the investigation is in its early stages and further details will be shared. The UDM urged the South African Police Service (Saps) to thoroughly investigate the motive and bring those responsible to justice, emphasizing that political differences should not be resolved through violence.

Why it mattersThe murder of UDM councillor Luxolo Namette in Gqeberha on November 7, 2026, has raised concerns about political violence during local government election vote counting.

South Africa braces for severe thunderstorms and flooding

Heavy rainfall warnings issued for KwaZulu-Natal, with severe thunderstorms expected in other provinces.

South Africa faces severe weather on Sunday, 8 November 2026, with extreme fire danger in the Northern Cape, Western Cape, and Limpopo. KwaZulu-Natal is under an orange level five warning for heavy rainfall expected to cause significant flooding, threatening lives and property in settlements.

Yellow level four warnings for severe thunderstorms are in effect for northern KZN, Mpumalanga, and Limpopo, bringing hail, strong winds, and lightning that could damage infrastructure. Yellow level two warnings for disruptive rainfall and severe thunderstorms cover the eastern North West, Free State, eastern Eastern Cape, and Gauteng, potentially causing localised flooding and disruptions.

The South African Weather Service (Saws) also warned of potential damage to informal settlements, vehicles, and livelihoods across affected areas. Motorists are advised to exercise caution on roads and avoid flooded areas.

Why it mattersResidents across multiple South African provinces face risks from severe thunderstorms, heavy rainfall, flooding, and extreme fire danger.

Cape Town records 344 fireworks incidents

Incidents more than doubled from last year, with 243 reported on Guy Fawkes Day alone.

The City of Cape Town recorded 344 fireworks-related incidents between November 3 and 5, a significant increase from the 156 calls received during the same period last year. On Guy Fawkes Day, November 5, authorities documented 243 incidents involving the discharge of fireworks across areas including Saxonsea, Lentegeur, and Hanover Park.

Wayne Dyason, spokesperson for the Law Enforcement Department, condemned the incidents, stating that Guy Fawkes Day brings unnecessary disruption, noise, and criminal elements. He noted that the annual event traumatises residents and burdens enforcement resources, urging communities to report fireworks use to the City’s Public Emergency Communication Centre at 021 480 7700.

Officers faced challenges apprehending individuals due to groups constantly moving and also responded to reports of stone throwing and anti-social behaviour. The City hopes for a decline in incidents next year, emphasizing the need for responsible community action.

Why it mattersResidents in affected communities experienced trauma and disruption due to fireworks incidents.

Cape Town rent control faces official opposition

Deputy Mayor Eddie Andrews stated there is 'no chance' of implementing rent control measures in the city.

Rent in Cape Town has become increasingly unaffordable, with property prices rising approximately 30% since the end of 2020, significantly outpacing other major cities. The average monthly rent now stands at around R12,500, marking a nearly 10% increase over the past year. This situation has led to widespread concern, with some stating the city is now completely unaffordable for many residents.

The Rent Control Group, a volunteer collective, has proposed targeted rent control measures and suggested capping Airbnb operations in the central business district. Group member Adam Narain argued that regulation, which costs nothing, is a viable tool the state can employ. However, the City of Cape Town, through Deputy Mayor Eddie Andrews, has rejected rent control, with Andrews stating the market dictates rental prices.

Mayor Geordin Hill-Lewis has previously written that rent control "not only fails, it entrenches inequality." The Rent Control Group's proposals also include adopting an affordable housing policy and urging the national government to implement an existing rental law. The group launched its Fair Rent Campaign at an event attended by struggle veteran Zackie Achmat.

Why it mattersCape Town residents are facing escalating rental costs, making housing unaffordable for many.

IEC orders recounts in five wards after election objections

The Electoral Commission of South Africa is reviewing 197 objections, upholding two and dismissing 54 as it finalises local election results.

The Electoral Commission of South Africa (IEC) has ordered recounts in five wards across the City of Tshwane and Mangaung following objections to the 2026 local government elections. The commission received 197 objections by 3 pm on Saturday, dismissing 54 and upholding two. Provisional results could change before the final declaration on Sunday.

IEC chairperson Mosotho Moepya stated that 8 objections were withdrawn and the commission was still considering the remaining ones. He reminded contestants that results remain provisional until all objections are determined. If an objection has merit, the IEC may decide votes should not count, or allocated seats should be adjusted before official declaration.

The recount process involves reprinting result slips and allowing political parties to send agents to observe. Moepya explained that the commission would inform affected parties and ensure their participation. He also cautioned against drawing conclusions from incomplete statistics like voter turnout and spoiled ballots, as these affect seat allocation.

Why it mattersSouth African voters in specific wards of Tshwane and Mangaung may see changes to their local election results.
