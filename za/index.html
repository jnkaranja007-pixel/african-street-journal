Godongwana cuts fuel levy to offer relief

Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana announced a reduction in the fuel levy to provide temporary relief to South Africans from November 4.

Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana has reduced the fuel levy to offer relief to South Africans, announcing a cut of R3 per litre for diesel and R1.50 per litre for petrol. This temporary measure will be in effect from November 4, 2026, until December 1, 2026. Following this initial reduction, a lower amount of relief will continue until January 5, 2027, with the diesel levy reduced by R1.50 per litre and the petrol levy by R1 per litre.

The announcement came as Godongwana delivered his medium-term budget policy statement on Wednesday, October 21, 2026. The National Treasury has also lowered growth forecasts and warned of short-lived revenue gains, while introducing R10 billion in temporary fuel relief to ease household pressure. Opposition parties have dismissed the cuts as electioneering, though the GNU offered muted support.

The decision aims to ease household pressure amid economic concerns. While the full impact on government revenue is yet to be determined, the relief is intended to provide immediate financial respite for consumers facing rising costs. The duration and scale of the cuts reflect a balance between offering support and managing fiscal implications.

Why it mattersSouth Africans will pay less for petrol and diesel due to the fuel levy reduction.

Swimmer killed in shark attack at Orient Beach

A 65-year-old woman died after a shark attack while swimming at Orient Beach, leading to the closure of local beaches.

A fatal shark attack occurred at Orient Beach in KuGompo City on Tuesday morning, claiming the life of 65-year-old Janine Lochrenberg. Lochrenberg, a retired nuclear radiographer and musician, was open water swimming with a group when she was attacked. She sustained severe injuries to her right arm and leg and was declared dead at the scene by a medic from the swimming group. Police have opened an investigation into the incident.

Following the attack, Orient Beach and Eastern Beach were closed until further notice by the Buffalo City Metropolitan Municipality. Municipal spokesman Luzuko Buku confirmed the tragic loss of a swimmer. Fellow swimmer Mike Loewe, who was in the water at the time, recounted that members of their open-water swimming group responded to Lochrenberg's distress signal and brought her ashore. Loewe described Lochrenberg as courageous and someone who liked to swim solo.

Tributes have poured in for Lochrenberg from across South Africa and her swimming community. East London Open Water Swimming described her as a treasured member whose loss leaves an enormous hole in their ocean family. They remembered her as someone who quietly did what she loved most, finding joy in the ocean, and being a vital part of the heart of their group.

Why it mattersSwimmers and beachgoers are affected by the closure of Orient Beach and Eastern Beach following the fatal shark attack.

Kaizer Chiefs seek CAF Confederation Cup win at home

The South African club must overcome a first-leg deficit against Red Arrows to advance to the group stages of the continental competition.

Kaizer Chiefs face Red Arrows on Friday evening in the second leg of their CAF Confederation Cup tie, needing to overturn a 1-0 loss from the first match in Zambia. Failure to win by at least two clear goals will result in their elimination from the competition. Coach Fernando Da Cruz is expected to deploy an attacking formation to put immediate pressure on the visitors.

The predicted starting lineup includes Renaldo Leaner in goal, with Reeve Frosler at right-back due to Thabiso Monyane's injury. Miguel Ignacio and Thabo Moloisane are set to form the central defense, and Bradley Cross will play left-back. In midfield, Sibongiseni Mthethwa and Lebohang Maboe are expected to provide balance, while Mduduzi Vilakazi will operate as an attacking midfielder.

The forward line is anticipated to feature Pule Mmodi on the left wing, Duba on the right, and Flavio Silva as the lone striker. Kaizer Chiefs will rely on their attackers to convert chances and secure a home victory to progress to the CAF Confederation Cup group stages.

Media wins sight of SSA secrecy application in R12 million fraud trial

News organisations have obtained a redacted copy of the State Security Agency’s application to close the corruption trial of two former senior officials.

Several South African media organisations have obtained a redacted copy of the State Security Agency’s (SSA) application to close the corruption trial of two former senior officials, Patrick Mshothola and Thulani Dlomo. The SSA is seeking a total ban on media and public access to the remaining proceedings, citing risks to intelligence sources and methods. The Mail & Guardian, Media24, amaBhungane, Daily Maverick, and the Campaign for Free Expression were among the outlets that secured the document.

Mshothola and Dlomo, who led the SSA’s special operations directorate between 2012 and 2017, face charges of theft, fraud, forgery, and uttering involving approximately R12 million. They were arrested on November 30, 2025, marking the first prosecution of SSA personnel stemming from the High Level Review Panel and the Zondo Commission. The trial commenced in the Pretoria High Court on October 12, 2026.

The court had previously ordered restrictions on public access, including closed proceedings for evidence related to SSA members, operational methods, and classified documents, following the State’s section 153(1) application on October 14, 2026. This was done without prior notice to the media or the public. The Mail & Guardian intervened as a co-applicant, seeking access to the sealed record and any future sealed documents.

Mabandla denies halting TRC prosecutions

Former justice minister Brigitte Mabandla told the Khampepe Commission of Inquiry she did not instruct a moratorium on post-TRC cases.

Former justice minister Brigitte Mabandla has denied instructing a halt to prosecutions related to the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC). Testifying before the Khampepe Commission of Inquiry on Wednesday, Mabandla stated she never made a phone call on November 11, 2004, to National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) senior official Dr Silas Ramaite, requesting a moratorium on TRC cases.

Ramaite, who oversaw TRC prosecutions and acted as head of the NPA, had previously alleged that Mabandla informed him of an "outside mechanism" and a declared "moratorium" on post-TRC prosecutions. He claimed this instruction led him to direct advocate Anton Ackermann to put investigations on hold, specifically mentioning the attempted murder of Reverend Frank Chikane in 1989 as a case potentially affected.

Mabandla asserted that neither she nor any official from her ministry issued such an instruction to Ramaite. She expressed her determination to uncover the truth, stating she had consulted with former NPA head Vusi Pikoli to clarify her certainty that the alleged phone call could not have occurred.

Why it mattersThe public and victims of apartheid-era crimes are affected by the potential delay and non-prosecution of perpetrators.
