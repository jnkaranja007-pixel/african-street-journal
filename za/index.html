KwaZulu-Natal Winds Reach 90km/h Amid Severe Storm Warnings

The South African Weather Service issued a Yellow Level 4 warning for severe storms and a Level 2 warning for rough seas along the coast.

Motorists in KwaZulu-Natal are advised to exercise caution on Friday due to severe weather expected across the province. The South African Weather Service (SAWS) has issued a Yellow Level 4 warning for severe thunderstorms in southern KZN, with heavy downpours potentially causing road flooding and lightning strikes damaging property. Gusts are predicted to reach up to 90km/h along the coast and inland, creating difficult driving conditions, especially for high-sided vehicles.

The SAWS attributes the unstable weather to an upper cut-off low, a low-pressure system, and ridging high pressure. Along the coast, a Yellow Level 2 warning for damaging waves has been issued, with wave heights potentially reaching four to five metres from Port Edward to Kosi Bay, which could disrupt small harbours and navigation. eThekwini Municipality has placed its disaster management and emergency services on high alert.

Residents are urged to reduce speed, increase following distances, and remain alert to sudden road condition changes. Authorities advise against crossing flooded roads, bridges, or fast-flowing streams. The public should continue monitoring official SAWS forecasts and warnings as the situation develops, with potential hazards including fallen trees, debris, damaged infrastructure, and power lines.

Why it mattersResidents and motorists in KwaZulu-Natal face risks of property damage, infrastructure disruption, and hazardous travel conditions due to severe storms, heavy downpours, and high winds.

Sharpeville survivors challenge indemnity law for justice

Survivors and relatives of those killed in the 1960 Sharpeville massacre are launching legal action to overturn a law preventing compensation claims.

Survivors of the 1960 Sharpeville massacre have initiated legal proceedings to challenge an apartheid-era indemnity law that has barred them from seeking compensation. Abraham Mofokeng, who was 20 at the time of the protest and survived a gunshot wound to the leg, is among those leading the legal fight. He described the event as a bloody suppression where police opened fire on thousands of peaceful demonstrators protesting against the white-minority government's laws.

The legal action, announced on Thursday, aims to overturn the indemnity law passed a year after the massacre, which prevented any perpetrators from being prosecuted. At least 69 protesters were killed by police on March 21, 1960, an event that became a pivotal moment in the anti-apartheid movement and exposed the violence of the racist system to the world.

Lawyers representing survivors and families of the deceased are seeking to enable compensation claims against the South African government. This legal challenge seeks to address the historical injustices and provide a pathway for redress for those who endured the violence and its lasting consequences, including individuals like Mofokeng who still carries physical wounds from the incident.

Why it mattersSurvivors and relatives of those killed in the Sharpeville massacre are affected by a law that prevents them from seeking compensation for the violence they endured.

Johannesburg Weather Threatens Springboks vs All Blacks Match

South African Weather Service warns of showers and thundershowers with damaging winds and hail possible at FNB Stadium.

Fans attending the Springboks versus All Blacks rugby match at FNB Stadium on Saturday must prepare for inclement weather. The South African Weather Service (SAWS) forecasts a 60% chance of rain, with showers and thundershowers expected from 14:00 until around 20:00, potentially impacting the 17:10 kick-off and the journey home. Temperatures will range from a minimum of 12 degrees Celsius to a maximum of 20 degrees Celsius.

SAWS has issued a Yellow Level 2 warning for severe thunderstorms covering southern Gauteng, which could bring damaging winds, hail, falling trees, and disruptions due to lightning. Supporters heading to the stadium are advised to bring warm clothing and a rain jacket, as conditions may change significantly between leaving home and arriving at the venue.

The unsettled weather is expected to persist after the final whistle, posing potential challenges for fans traveling home via car or public transport. The match, which follows pre-game verbal exchanges between coaches Rassie Erasmus and Dave Rennie regarding scrum legality, will proceed with these weather conditions as a backdrop.

Why it mattersRugby fans traveling to and from FNB Stadium in Johannesburg face potential disruptions and discomfort due to severe weather warnings.

ANC, DA Neck-and-Neck in Metros; MK Leads eThekwini

A new Ipsos poll shows the ANC and DA in a tight race across key South African cities, with the MK Party poised for a significant win in eThekwini.

The upcoming November 4 local elections show a close contest between the ANC and the DA in four major South African metros, according to the latest Ipsos poll. The ANC holds a slight lead in Johannesburg at 31% to the DA's 27%. However, in Tshwane, the DA is ahead with 31% compared to the ANC's 22%. The MK Party, led by Jacob Zuma, is projected to dominate eThekwini with 55% of the vote, establishing itself as the country's third-largest political party.

The Ipsos Pulse of the People survey, which polled 3,500 people, also revealed national support figures: ANC at 31%, DA at 25%, MK at 14%, and the EFF at 11%. While the ANC's urban support has declined, its strongholds remain in rural areas like Limpopo and the Eastern Cape. The DA, strong in the Western Cape, is also showing gains in the Free State, a province affected by poor service delivery.

The poll indicates that final election outcomes will depend on voter turnout strategies in the contested provinces. Other parties like Action SA are performing well in Gauteng metros, while the IFP and Patriotic Alliance suggest Ipsos may be under-polling their support. The Social Research Foundation also noted the DA's higher polling in Johannesburg, though its urban sample size was smaller.

Why it mattersVoters in South African metros face a close electoral choice between the ANC and DA, with the MK Party showing significant strength in eThekwini, potentially altering local governance.

Researchers Warn of Preventable Harm to South African Women

Weak enforcement and services that exclude at-risk individuals are allowing preventable harm to continue despite decades of evidence.

Researchers at the Safety 2026 conference in Cape Town warned that South Africa's failure to act on known risks is allowing preventable harm to continue. Despite progressive laws and extensive research, intimate-partner femicide is rising, and most reported rape cases do not result in convictions. The issue is not a lack of evidence but a failure in implementation and enforcement.

The 16th World Conference on Injury Prevention and Safety Promotion, hosted by the Foundation for Professional Development and co-hosted by the South African Medical Research Council, highlighted systemic failures. Researchers challenged governments and the scientific community to address the persistent issues that allow harm to persist, particularly for women.

The conference, held under the theme Ubuntu: United for a Safer Future, returns to Africa after two decades. It marks 30 years since the World Health Assembly recognized violence as a major public health problem. Experts noted that South Africa knows where women are most at risk and how factors like alcohol and firearms contribute to their deaths.

Why it mattersSouth African women are affected by rising intimate-partner femicide and a justice system that fails to convict most reported rape cases.
