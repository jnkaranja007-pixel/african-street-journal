17 killed in two South African mass shootings

Gunmen killed nine people in Gauteng and eight in KwaZulu-Natal on Sunday, prompting a police manhunt.

17 people were killed in two separate mass shootings across two provinces on Sunday, 15 November 2026. General Fannie Masemola, the National Commissioner of the South African Police Service, vowed to bring the perpetrators to justice after gunmen gunned down nine people at an informal settlement in Atteridgeville, Gauteng, and another eight in Sweetwaters outside Pietermaritzburg, KwaZulu-Natal.

A schoolchild discovered the bodies of six men and three women inside a shack in the Jeffsville informal settlement on Monday morning at around 06:45 after officers found them in four separate rooms. Gauteng police commissioner Lieutenant-General Tommy Mthombeni revealed that one of the Atteridgeville victims was a person of interest in a December 2025 massacre that claimed 12 lives, while Gauteng Premier Panyaza Lesufi called on police to investigate whether revenge, territorial disputes or extortion motivated the latest killings. Meanwhile, KwaZulu-Natal police found the bodies of eight men inside a bullet-riddled vehicle in Sweetwaters on Sunday night.

Masemola stated that an intelligence-led multidisciplinary team was following up on leads and noted that environmental challenges in the Jeffsville informal settlement make crime prevention operations extremely difficult. Police launched a manhunt for the Atteridgeville gunmen and urged anyone with information to contact Crime Stop on 08600 10111 or use the MySAPS app.

Why it mattersResidents and communities in Atteridgeville and Sweetwaters face ongoing violent crime and illegal firearms as police pursue the gunmen responsible for 17 deaths.

Proposed fiscal anchor could tighten budgets for health and education

Treasury's plan to write a binding debt target into law would cap state spending at a time when Gauteng is losing nurses.

Gauteng's Estimates of Provincial Revenue and Expenditure show 2,899 medical and related personnel were lost between 2024/25 and 2025/26, and the Mail & Guardian reports the province is losing 100 nurses a month. Against that, the Minister of Finance announced in February that R21.3 billion and R9.9 billion were added to health and education over the medium term, with both increases set above consumer price inflation.

The Human Resources for Health Strategy projects a shortage of about 97,000 healthcare workers across South Africa in 2030. Health Minister stated in a parliamentary reply last year that the country has 4 doctors for every 10,000 people, below the international benchmark of 10. Treasury Director-General Duncan Pieterse has noted three consecutive years of primary budget surpluses.

Parliament's Standing Committee on Finance told National Treasury in its March 2024 report that the binding fiscal anchor should not be the trajectory of fiscal policy in relation to public expenditure. The proposed fiscal anchor is a binding debt or primary surplus target that would cap state borrowing and spending within a set range.

Why it mattersHealth workers and teachers would compete for a tighter budget if the proposed fiscal anchor is written into law.

Business · 2026-11-17

Johannesburg reverses Rea Vaya bus suspension after payments

Commuters will not need alternate transport after operators received partial funds.

The City of Johannesburg reversed its decision to suspend Rea Vaya Bus Rapid Transit services after resolving payment issues with operating companies on Monday. Bus companies received partial payment on Monday afternoon, allowing the subsidized public transport network to continue running on Tuesday, 17 November.

The municipality had initially announced the shutdown due to financial troubles, mirroring chlorine shortages that forced public pools to close from Monday. Tabisa Mogapi, an Economic Freedom Fighters caucus whip, stated that worsening municipal financial challenges have contributed to recurring disruptions in public services relied upon by working-class communities.

In a separate incident on Monday morning, an empty Rea Vaya bus crashed along the Soweto Highway near Immink Drive before 5am. The driver was rushed to Tshepo Themba hospital for a medical check-up after the vehicle, which was travelling between Thokoza and the Soweto depot without passengers, collided.

Why it mattersCommuters relying on the Rea Vaya bus network retain their daily transport service instead of arranging alternate travel for Tuesday, 17 November.

Tech · 2026-11-17

Cyril Ramaphosa turns 74 during Silicon Valley working visit

South Africa's president marks his 74th birthday in California on a working visit focused on artificial intelligence.

President Cyril Cyril Matamela Ramaphosa celebrated his 74th birthday on Tuesday, 17 November, while leading a delegation on a working visit to Silicon Valley in the United States. According to the Presidency, the trip began on Saturday, 14 November, and spans a week of engagements with technology companies, investors and artificial intelligence experts. The president also called on South African-born billionaire Elon Musk to give back to his country of birth.

The working visit focuses on data centres, cloud infrastructure and the artificial intelligence economy alongside MTN testing a Telkom takeover. The African National Congress dismissed speculation of a resignation last week, following his re-election by the National Assembly in June 2024 after succeeding Jacob Zuma on 15 February 2018.

The president was born in Johannesburg on 17 November 1952, obtained his law degree from Unisa in 1981, founded the National Union of Mineworkers in 1982, and established the Shanduka Group in 2001. He is scheduled to return from the United States this weekend.

Why it mattersPresident Cyril Ramaphosa is in California for a week-long working visit ending this weekend, focusing on artificial intelligence and technology investments.
