Politics · 2026-11-16

ANC factions clash over election results and GNU

Divisions within the ANC deepen as some members blame the DA partnership for poor local election results and seek to reconfigure the government of national unity.

Factions within South Africa's ruling ANC are reportedly using the party's poor performance in the recent local government elections to undermine the government of national unity (GNU). Some members of the ANC's national executive committee (NEC) are calling for the GNU to be reconfigured, blaming the partnership with the Democratic Alliance (DA) for the electoral setbacks.

Political analyst Professor Andre Duvenhage stated that a strong grouping within the ANC NEC opposes working with the DA and Freedom Front Plus, preferring a "centre-left" narrative. He noted that the ANC's top seven officials were accused at a NEC meeting of sidelining party members in decision-making, indicating "huge" divisions that could influence leadership choices next year.

Professor Theo Neethling added that these tensions reflect disagreements over coalition partners and an internal struggle for control over strategic decisions, which will likely intensify ahead of the ANC's elective conference in 2027. The NEC decided to form coalitions with the DA only as a "last resort," with President Cyril Ramaphosa reportedly expressing fatigue with the DA challenging ANC policies.

Why it mattersInternal ANC disagreements over coalition partners and control of strategic decisions are intensifying ahead of the party's 2027 elective conference.

Security · 2026-11-16

Nine shot dead in Atteridgeville shack

Police are hunting gunmen after six men and three women were found with gunshot wounds in Jeffsville informal settlement on Monday morning.

Nine people were killed in a mass shooting at the Jeffsville informal settlement in Atteridgeville, west of Pretoria, on Sunday night. Police found the bodies of six males and three females inside a four-roomed shack with gunshot wounds after responding to an alert on Monday morning. The shooting reportedly occurred between 22:00 and 23:00 on Sunday, 15 November.

Police are hunting the gunmen, and national police commissioner General Fannie Masemola and Gauteng police commissioner Lieutenant-General Tommy Mthombeni are on their way to the scene. Mthombeni has deployed several units, including Crime Intelligence and the provincial tracking team, in the search. Officers have not yet established a motive, and no arrests have been made. The Gauteng Serious and Violent Crimes, Murder and Robbery Unit is investigating.

This incident follows a mass shooting in the same settlement on 21 January 2026, when gunmen killed five men. In December last year, 12 people died in a shooting at the Saulsville hostel in Atteridgeville. Across the three attacks, 26 people have died in Atteridgeville in under a year, though police have not established links between them.

Why it mattersThe Gauteng Serious and Violent Crimes, Murder and Robbery Unit is investigating the shooting that killed nine people in Atteridgeville.

South Africa braces for severe storms and heavy rain

The South African Weather Service has issued an Orange Level 5 warning for Gauteng, Limpopo, and Mpumalanga, anticipating flooding, falling trees, and power disruptions.

South Africans in Gauteng, Limpopo, and Mpumalanga are facing severe storms until Sunday night, with the South African Weather Service (SAWS) maintaining an Orange Level 5 warning. The alert signals a heightened risk of flooding, falling trees, power outages, and damage to homes, vehicles, and infrastructure across these provinces.

Residents are advised to clear gutters and drains of debris to prevent water from entering properties and to identify safer routes and higher ground in flood-prone areas. Loose outdoor items like garden furniture and refuse bins should be secured or brought inside to prevent damage from strong winds. An emergency kit with essentials like a torch, water, medication, and a charged cellphone is recommended.

The SAWS also warned of damaging interior winds in eastern KwaZulu-Natal and damaging coastal winds along its coast and parts of the Western Cape. Extremely high fire danger conditions are expected in the Western Cape and parts of the Northern Cape and Free State. Scattered showers and thundershowers are forecast for Gauteng, Mpumalanga, Limpopo, North West, Free State, and KwaZulu-Natal on Monday.

Why it mattersResidents in Gauteng, Limpopo, and Mpumalanga must prepare for potential disruptions including flooding and power outages due to severe storms until Sunday night.

South Africa struggles to police online betting sites

Gross gambling revenue rose 13.4% to R84.5-billion in the 2025/26 financial year, with betting taking 74% of it.

South Africans wagered R1.76-trillion last year, most of it from a phone, and the state is struggling to decide who should regulate the online betting market. Foreign betting sites that accept rands and carry a South African web address are a central problem, and the Daily Maverick reports they often rely on a gambling licence from Curaçao, a Caribbean territory, that is printed in small grey type at the foot of the page.

Sean Coleman, chief executive of the South African Bookmakers' Association, warned that appearances are misleading. The industry runs in two parts: a licensed, taxed betting sector and an illicit one that operates in a grey area. The key law governing gambling dates from 2004, and it divides power between national government and nine provinces, which makes decisive action difficult.

Provinces collected R6.45-billion in taxes and levies in the 2025/26 financial year, up 10.9%. Betting grew to R62.2-billion in gross gambling revenue, more than two-and-a-half times its level three years earlier, when it stood at R23.7-billion.

Why it mattersProvinces collected R6.45-billion in gambling taxes and levies last year, so any delay in agreeing who regulates offshore sites affects the money they can spend.

Sport · 2026-11-16

Bangladesh reach 327/6 on day one against South Africa

Towhid Hridoy hit 137 as Bangladesh fought back after a poor start, with Kagiso Rabada taking 5 wickets for South Africa.

Bangladesh reached 327 runs for the loss of six wickets by the close of play on the first day of the first Test against South Africa at the Wanderers on Sunday. The touring team's top order crumbled early after winning the toss and choosing to bat, leaving them at 22/3. Middle-order batsman Towhid Hridoy scored 137 runs off 227 balls, an innings that lasted almost six hours.

Hridoy shared a 206-run partnership with Litton Das, who scored 92 runs off 138 balls, for the sixth wicket. Fast bowler Kagiso Rabada took 5 wickets for 51 runs for South Africa, including the wicket of debutant Mahfijul Islam off the first ball of the match. Rabada's effort was his 18th five-wicket haul in his Test career.

South Africa recalled Zubayr Hamza to bat at number three in place of David Bedingham for the Test. Seam bowler Dane Paterson replaced Gerald Coetzee in the only other change to the Proteas squad. Bangladesh captain Najmul Hossain Shanto indicated his team would likely pick three seam bowlers and one specialist spinner.

Why it mattersCricket fans in South Africa will follow the progress of the first Test match against Bangladesh, which continues on Monday.
