Zille highlights Joburg water crisis on election day

DA mayoral candidate Helen Zille stated that 50 suburbs have been without water, calling it the city's most urgent challenge.

Johannesburg's severe water crisis became a central issue on election day, with DA mayoral candidate Helen Zille highlighting the desperation of residents in dozens of suburbs facing repeated outages. Millions of South Africans voted on Wednesday amidst a deepening service delivery crisis, including a reported R220 billion infrastructure backlog and failing municipal services. Zille, casting her ballot at Emmarentia Primary School, declared the water crisis the most urgent challenge facing Johannesburg.

Zille stated that 50 suburbs had no water over the past weekend, describing it as a major crisis for a city of Johannesburg's size. She noted that the outages disproportionately affect residents who cannot afford alternatives, making the desperation palpable. Zille acknowledged expectations for immediate action but warned that years of neglect have created a long-term infrastructure crisis requiring emergency short-term solutions and medium-to-long-term infrastructure fixes.

Beyond service delivery, Zille drew a line on coalition politics, stating the DA would not enter unstable governing arrangements dependent on the EFF, preferring opposition to unworkable coalitions. She argued that political instability significantly contributed to Johannesburg’s decline, emphasizing the need for stable government to reverse deterioration. Zille reserved strong criticism for the ANC, blaming its governance failures for the collapse of state and municipal entities.

Why it mattersJohannesburg residents are facing a severe water crisis impacting daily life and service delivery during local government elections.

South Africa faces severe thunderstorms and high fire danger

Yellow Level 4 warnings issued for Gauteng, Mpumalanga, Limpopo, Free State, North West, and KwaZulu-Natal, with extreme fire conditions in the Northern Cape and Eastern Cape.

South Africa is bracing for severe weather on Thursday, November 5, 2026, with widespread thunderstorms, heavy rain, and hail expected in several provinces. A yellow Level 4 warning for severe thunderstorms, bringing flooding and property damage, covers Gauteng, Mpumalanga Highveld, central-southern Limpopo, eastern Free State and North West, and most of KwaZulu-Natal. These storms may cause localized power and communication disruptions.

Extreme fire danger conditions are forecast for the Northern Cape, most of the Eastern Cape interior, and parts of the Western Cape, Free State, North West, Gauteng, Limpopo, and Mpumalanga. The South African Weather Service has issued a Red Level 10 warning for veld fires in areas including Molteno, Queenstown, and Grahamstown, advising the public against starting fires due to risks of property damage, loss of grazing, and potential loss of life.

Specific storm warnings are in place for areas like Rustenburg, Frankfort, Hartebeespoort, and Reitz, with storms expected between 13:00 and 23:59. Meanwhile, Musina, Kimberley, and Mthatha are predicted to be the hottest cities, reaching 38°C. The weather service also noted very high UVB sunburn index levels for Gauteng, advising precautions against sun exposure.

Why it mattersResidents across multiple South African provinces face risks of property damage, flooding, and dangerous fire conditions due to severe weather warnings.

South Africans vote in local government elections

Voters cast ballots across Johannesburg on November 4, 2026, braving rain and queues.

South Africans participated in local government elections on November 4, 2026, with voting taking place across the country. In Johannesburg, residents in areas like Alexandra, Ivory Park, and Emmarentia cast their ballots.

Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) officials managed polling stations, with some staff seen setting up signs and delivering materials escorted by police in Alexandra. Voters marked their fingers with indelible ink before casting their votes at stations including Emmarentia Primary School.

Voting continued after sunset in Alexandra, with determined voters turning out to cast their ballots. The Mail & Guardian is providing live updates on the election results and counts.

Why it mattersSouth African citizens exercised their democratic right to vote in local government elections on November 4, 2026.

South Africa petrol price drops by 1 cent

Diesel prices fall by up to R1.20 per litre following government fuel levy adjustments.

South African motorists will pay less for fuel from Wednesday, November 4, 2026, after petrol prices decreased by 1 cent per litre. Diesel prices saw more significant reductions, with 0.05% sulphur diesel falling by R1.10 per litre and 0.005% sulphur diesel dropping by R1.20 per litre. Wholesale illuminating paraffin, however, increased by R2.75 per litre.

These changes follow the National Treasury's announcement of a R10 billion fuel relief package, which included a temporary reduction of R1.50 per litre for petrol and R3.00 per litre for diesel. This intervention was introduced after motorists faced potential substantial price hikes due to deteriorating fuel recoveries in October. The slate levy increase largely negated the petrol levy reduction.

The inland price for Petrol 95 is now R30.24 per litre, down from R30.25 in October, while coastal Petrol 95 is R29.37, down from R29.38. Inland Diesel 0.005% is R31.60, down from R32.80. The Brent crude oil price is $102.18 a barrel, and the rand/dollar exchange rate is R16.44/$.

Why it mattersSouth African motorists will pay less for petrol and diesel, though illuminating paraffin prices have risen.

Activist walks SA to protest farm attacks

Petrus Sitho left a job in 2018 to speak out about farm murders and has walked hundreds of kilometres.

Activist Petrus Sitho has walked hundreds of kilometres across South Africa to protest farm attacks, including a 129km walk from Frankfort to Memel in the Free State. Sitho left a well-paid job in 2018, feeling called to speak out about farm murders after growing up on a farm in Bultfontein. He has described himself on Facebook as a digital creator aiming to make a difference.

Sitho’s activism intensified after he was dismissed from his job in 2020 for travelling to Senekal to support farmers following the murder of Brendin Horner. He has interviewed survivors and claims to be the first activist to travel around South Africa interviewing farmers and farm workers. Sitho has also stated he was the first activist to debate a petition on farm killings in Parliament.

The activist's campaigns have led to death threats and an assault outside the Union Buildings in 2018. Sitho has openly criticised the government, writing an open letter to President Cyril Ramaphosa stating he does not believe the president cares about farm killings. He recently asked the public to fund a trip to the United States to present his cause to US President Donald Trump.

Why it mattersPetrus Sitho is protesting farm attacks, which directly affect farmers and rural communities in South Africa.
