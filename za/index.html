South Africa matric exams paused for local elections

Matric exams will take a three-day break from November 3 to November 5 to accommodate the November 4 local government elections.

South Africa’s National Senior Certificate examinations will pause for three days, from Tuesday, November 3, to Thursday, November 5, to accommodate the local government elections scheduled for Wednesday, November 4. The exams will resume on Friday, November 6, with Monday, November 9, also designated a non-examination day. Umalusi chief executive officer Mafu Rakometsi confirmed the timetable amendment on October 8, stating it was to minimise disruptions from election-related activities like political gatherings and protests.

Rakometsi explained that Umalusi had identified political activities and community protests as potential threats to the smooth running of examinations, especially as many schools serve as both voting stations and extra class venues. He appealed to political and community leaders, principals, teachers, and parents to protect learners from disruptions and ensure a suitable environment for exam preparation. Umalusi will work with assessment bodies and provincial education departments to prevent election-related issues.

The NSC examinations began nationwide on Tuesday, October 13, and are scheduled to conclude on Thursday, November 26. Physical Sciences Paper 2 (Chemistry) is scheduled for Monday, November 2, with the break following before Life Sciences and Business Studies revision can take place. The council stressed that every effort should be made to ensure no learner is unfairly disadvantaged by election-related activities.

Why it mattersMatric exam candidates in South Africa will experience a temporary pause in their examinations due to the local government elections.

Politics · 2026-10-29

Cachalia backs Mkhwanazi for Hawks top job

Acting police minister Firoz Cachalia wants Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi to lead the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation.

Acting Police Minister Firoz Cachalia has asked Cabinet to approve the appointment of KwaZulu-Natal police commissioner Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi as the national head of the Hawks. The Ministry of Police confirmed this move on Thursday, 29 October. Mkhwanazi previously exposed alleged political interference and corruption in July 2025, leading to a commission of inquiry.

Mkhwanazi is now in line to lead the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation (DPCI) as part of Cachalia’s Police Reset Agenda. The ministry stated that appointing a National Head ensures strong leadership and effectiveness in fighting organised crime and corruption. Cabinet will consider the memorandum at its next sitting.

Cachalia also involved Mkhwanazi in the investigation into women's murders in Ekurhuleni starting in September. By early October, police were investigating 12 such cases, having resolved three and identified persons of interest in three others. The ministry has not confirmed if these killings are linked.

Why it mattersNhlanhla Mkhwanazi's potential appointment as Hawks boss affects the fight against organised crime and corruption in South Africa.

News · 2026-10-29

South African food basket costs more than SRD grant

Record fuel prices are forcing transport costs to compete with access to food for low-income households.

South Africa's R370 Social Relief of Distress grant is now R20.86 short of covering the cost of 14 basic food items tracked by Maverick Citizen. Between September and October, the price of this basic food basket rose by R2 to R390.86, driven by increases in sugar beans and beef stock.

The Pietermaritzburg Economic Justice and Dignity Group reported that a basic food basket for a family of seven now costs R5,512.53 per month, an increase of R24 from September. This basic basket is not nutritionally complete, with a truly nutritious diet for seven people costing R6,692.25 in October.

Record high fuel prices, with petrol exceeding R30 a litre and diesel over R35, are squeezing household budgets. South African households typically spend 14% of their expenditure on transport, a figure that is higher for low-income families relying on taxis and buses.

Why it mattersLow-income South African households are struggling to afford basic food and transport due to rising prices and an insufficient social grant.

Cape Town terminal expects 13% fruit export rise

South Africa's deciduous fruit exports are set to increase for the 2026/27 season, with new measures to manage congestion.

The Cape Town Container Terminal anticipates a 13% increase in South Africa’s deciduous fruit export volumes for the 2026/27 season, running from November 2026 to April 2027. This rise follows a challenging prior season where operational issues at the terminal led to an estimated 55,000 tons of table grapes being diverted. Deciduous fruits like table grapes, plums, peaches, and apricots are grown in the Western Cape and exported mainly to Europe.

Transnet Port Terminals has implemented new measures following customer and stakeholder engagements, including additional fixed-term employees and enhanced leave management for workforce continuity. A dedicated 24-hour hotline and a fixed weekly vessel schedule with a 21-day berthing plan are now active. Exporters must deliver 95% of planned refrigerated container volumes by stack closure to avoid missing vessels, shifting risk allocation.

These improvements are supported by 28 new rubber-tyred gantry cranes and four straddle carriers. Transport Minister Barbara Creecy reported that average vessel waiting time has been reduced from 115 hours to 44.3 hours, and ship turnaround time has decreased from 103 hours in 2023/24 to 55 hours year-to-date in 2026/27.

Why it mattersSouth African farmers exporting deciduous fruits like grapes and plums will benefit from improved terminal efficiency and reduced waiting times for their produce destined for European markets.

Agriculture · 2026-10-29

South African farmer grows business with social fund support

Tshepo Mochekgechekge expanded his crop farm from a small plot to a quarter hectare, creating jobs.

Tshepo Mochekgechekge, a 29-year-old crop farmer in Saulsville, Tshwane, has grown his business significantly after participating in the Social Employment Fund (SEF) programme. Previously relying on a student stipend, he transitioned from an informal farming interest to a registered enterprise in 2025.

The SEF programme helped Mochekgechekge develop technical and leadership skills, improving his production planning and crop management. His farm expanded from a 62 x 25 metre plot to a quarter hectare, increasing output and customers. He currently employs three seasonal workers and two part-time staff.

Despite challenges with water access requiring manual irrigation, Mochekgechekge aims to transition to commercial farming. He credits the SEF programme for providing the foundational support, estimating it contributed to 80% of his enterprise's establishment.

Why it mattersTshepo Mochekgechekge's farm expansion and job creation directly benefit the local economy in Tshwane.
