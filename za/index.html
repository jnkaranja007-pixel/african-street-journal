ANC faces worst election result with 31.73% of votes counted

The ruling party's support has fallen below a third nationally as counting for the 2026 local government elections continues.

South Africa's governing African National Congress (ANC) is heading for its worst-ever election result, with support slipping to 31.73% of the national vote as counting in the 2026 local government elections continues. With 87% of voting districts counted on Friday, 6 November, the Democratic Alliance (DA) was in second place with 23.99%, and former president Jacob Zuma’s uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) Party had climbed to 9.41%.

These figures represent a significant setback for the ANC, which has governed South Africa since 1994, and has seen a steady decline in electoral support over two decades. The party's declining support has been attributed to voter dissatisfaction over unemployment, crime, corruption, and poor service delivery, with municipalities facing criticism over failing water supplies, power interruptions, and deteriorating infrastructure.

The results are expected to have significant consequences for major metropolitan municipalities, with coalition negotiations likely to play a central role in determining who governs. In Johannesburg, the DA was leading the partial count, while the ANC faced a substantial challenge from smaller parties. The MK Party was putting pressure on the ANC in KwaZulu-Natal, with the outcome in eThekwini municipality emerging as a key contest.

Why it mattersVoters are dissatisfied with unemployment, crime, corruption, and poor service delivery, impacting municipal functions.

Bail denied for man accused of killing TUT student

Sizwe Nkululeko Yende is accused of strangling Botshelo Mabua after a verbal argument.

A 31-year-old man, Sizwe Nkululeko Yende, was denied bail by the Pretoria Magistrates’ Court in connection with the alleged murder of Tshwane University of Technology (TUT) student Botshelo Mabua. Yende is accused of killing Mabua, a geomatics student, on 25 September 2026 at her parents’ home in Rockville, Soweto. The state alleges that Yende pushed Mabua during a verbal altercation, causing her to hit her head, and then strangled her.

National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) spokesperson Magaboke Mohlatlole stated that the court rejected Yende’s bail application, finding his version of events contradicted the post-mortem report. The post-mortem identified manual strangulation as the cause of death, while Yende claimed Mabua fell and hit her head. Mabua was found dead at the scene by emergency personnel.

The court postponed the case to 12 November 2026 for the Director of Public Prosecutions to decide on the trial venue. Yende remains in custody as investigations are complete. The NPA highlighted gender-based violence as a prosecutorial priority. The TUT Geomatics Student Society mourned Mabua, who was expected to graduate in 2027, and spoke out against gender-based violence.

Why it mattersSizwe Nkululeko Yende will remain in custody while facing charges for the alleged murder of TUT student Botshelo Mabua.

South Africa debates electoral reform amid large ballots

Analysts and politicians are divided on whether to introduce tougher entry requirements for smaller parties.

South Africa's large ballot papers have reignited debate over the role of smaller political parties in elections. Political analysts and politicians are divided on whether stricter entry requirements and electoral thresholds could simplify elections without undermining democratic rights. Many parties contesting elections this week have led to calls for reform.

Political analyst Theo Neethling supports a five percent threshold, stating that too many parties complicate the system and pressure election administration. He questioned if parties attracting less than one percent of the vote justify the complexity, suggesting simplification is possible without altering outcomes. DA member Mike Waters also called for reform to reduce the number of parties contesting and winning seats, favouring a one or two percent threshold.

Waters cited Ekurhuleni, where seven parties each secured a single council seat in the previous election, holding the council hostage. Germany uses a 5% threshold in federal elections. ActionSA's Michael Basch argued that restricting smaller parties could harm democracy, as the strength of South Africa's system lies in its openness to independent candidates and smaller parties.

Why it mattersVoters and politicians are affected by the complexity and potential instability caused by numerous small parties in elections.

Cape Town rent control faces official opposition

Deputy Mayor Eddie Andrews stated there is 'no chance' of implementing rent control measures in the city.

Rent in Cape Town has become increasingly unaffordable, with property prices rising approximately 30% since the end of 2020, significantly outpacing other major cities. The average monthly rent now stands at around R12,500, marking a nearly 10% increase over the past year. This situation has led to widespread concern, with some stating the city is now completely unaffordable for many residents.

The Rent Control Group, a volunteer collective, has proposed targeted rent control measures and suggested capping Airbnb operations in the central business district. Group member Adam Narain argued that regulation, which costs nothing, is a viable tool the state can employ. However, the City of Cape Town, through Deputy Mayor Eddie Andrews, has rejected rent control, with Andrews stating the market dictates rental prices.

Mayor Geordin Hill-Lewis has previously written that rent control "not only fails, it entrenches inequality." The Rent Control Group's proposals also include adopting an affordable housing policy and urging the national government to implement an existing rental law. The group launched its Fair Rent Campaign at an event attended by struggle veteran Zackie Achmat.

Why it mattersCape Town residents are facing escalating rental costs, making housing unaffordable for many.

South African municipalities fail to spend capital budgets

66 percent of municipalities spend funds but miss quality and audit standards.

No municipality in South Africa has consistently achieved a 100% expenditure rate for capital budgets and conditional grants between 2021 and 2026.

While 66% of municipalities spend their fully funded capital budgets, they struggle with quality standards and accounting, with 63% facing financial distress or non-compliance.

The National Planning Commission shows the municipal sector failed to meet NDP targets from 2021 to 2026, citing a general lack of political will to implement credible, fully funded IDPs.

Why it mattersSouth African municipalities are failing to meet national development targets and maintain financial compliance.
