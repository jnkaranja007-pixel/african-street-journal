Water experts warn South Africa is not yet ready for El Niño drought

Dam levels are higher than before the 2015/16 drought, but ageing pipes and municipal failures still threaten supply.

South Africa is better placed to withstand a possible El Niño-induced drought than it was before the 2015/16 crisis, with dam levels currently relatively high. Water experts, however, warned during a WaterCAN webinar on Wednesday that ageing infrastructure, municipal delivery failures and vulnerability to food price rises leave little room for complacency.

Anja du Plessis, an associate professor and water management expert at Unisa, described El Niño as "a stress test that we can see coming". She said the dams gave the country some time, but that time only helped if it was spent fixing leaks, building buffers and making decisions early, and that water in dams did not necessarily reach households.

Du Plessis identified three pressures that could emerge during an El Niño: reduced inflows into dams, increased evaporation as temperatures rise, and higher demand for water. The webinar, titled "Is South Africa ready for El Niño?", considered what a prolonged dry spell could mean for water supplies and food prices, and what the government should do to prepare.

Why it mattersHouseholds depend on municipal supply that experts say is failing, and food prices are exposed to any drought.

Security · 2026-11-13

Hawks arrest tenth suspect in alleged Russia-Ukraine recruitment case

The suspect, a 38-year-old foreign national, was arrested on Wednesday and faces charges under the Regulation of Foreign Military Assistance Act 15 of 1998.

South African police arrested a 38-year-old foreign national on Wednesday for allegedly attempting to travel from South Africa to fight in the Russia-Ukraine war. The arrest brings to 10 the number of foreign nationals arrested in South Africa for the same alleged crime this week.

The arrest was made by an integrated investigation team that included Interpol Pretoria, the Hawks, Crime Intelligence and the Gauteng Provincial Tracking Team, according to the South African Police Service. The suspect faces charges under the Regulation of Foreign Military Assistance Act 15 of 1998.

On Monday, nine Zimbabwean nationals appeared in two Gauteng courts after their arrest on 8 November 2026. Five appeared in the Kempton Park Magistrate's Court after being arrested at OR Tambo International Airport, and four appeared in the Johannesburg Magistrate's Court after being arrested at a hotel in Bruma. All nine remain in custody.

Why it mattersAnyone travelling from South Africa to join a foreign conflict faces arrest and charges under the Foreign Military Assistance Act, which bars citizens from taking part in foreign armies without government authorisation.

Sport · 2026-11-13

Tatjana Smith switches allegiance to Netherlands

South Africa's most decorated Olympian cited financial challenges and a dysfunctional relationship with the national swimming federation.

South Africa's most decorated Olympian, Tatjana Smith, has switched her international swimming allegiance to the Netherlands. The four-time Olympic medallist announced the decision in an emotional social media statement on Tuesday, November 10, stating it would allow her to "thrive" in her career.

Smith has previously spoken about the poor relationship with Swimming South Africa and the financial difficulties faced by South African swimmers. Former Springbok Bryan Habana offered support, while ex-player Kobus Wiese commented that athletes are often prioritised below politicians, with budgets favouring ministerial travel over athlete support.

Ryk Neethling, an Olympic gold medallist, described the challenges South African swimmers have faced for a "very long time," recalling a "horrific" experience at the 2004 Olympics. Swimming South Africa acknowledged Smith's prize money from the 2023 World Aquatics Championships should not have been withheld. The Minister of Sport, Arts and Culture, Gayton McKenzie, wished Smith well, noting that national federations are independent and responsible for their own funding and sustainability, though the department is aware of problems at Swimming South Africa and a reform process is underway with World Aquatics oversight.

Why it mattersSwimmers like Tatjana Smith face financial challenges and a dysfunctional national federation, impacting their ability to compete internationally.

Security · 2026-11-13

Farm attack survivor Donovan Marx leaves hospital

Donovan Marx, who was shot, stabbed, and had both ears cut off, was discharged from Mediclinic Potchefstroom on Thursday, 12 November.

Donovan Marx, a survivor of a farm attack near Ventersdorp, walked out of Mediclinic Potchefstroom on Thursday, 12 November, 16 days after being shot, stabbed, and having both ears severed by assailants.

His family described his recovery as a miracle, and an emotional video showed Marx singing praises with medical staff and church members. Musician The Kiffness, David Scott, shared the footage, calling the spirit of the Afrikaner unbreakable and criticising President Cyril Ramaphosa for denying the existence of targeted farm attacks.

Four men accused of the 28 October attack are due in Ventersdorp Magistrate’s Court on Friday, 13 November, for a bail application, with the state opposing bail. One accused previously lived on Marx’s farm.
