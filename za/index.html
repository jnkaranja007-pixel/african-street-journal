South Africa cuts fuel prices ahead of elections

Diesel prices to fall by over R1.60 per litre and petrol by up to 49c/l due to government levy reductions.

South African motorists will see lower fuel prices in November, with diesel potentially dropping by more than R1.60 per litre and petrol prices also decreasing. The government announced a temporary R1.50 per litre reduction for petrol and R3.00 per litre for diesel on the general fuel levy for November. These changes are part of a R10 billion relief package aimed at easing consumer pressure.

Indicative inland prices show diesel 0.005% could fall by R1.68 per litre and diesel 0.05% by R1.58 per litre. Petrol 95 may decrease by 49c per litre, and petrol 93 by 33c per litre. These figures are projections based on Central Energy Fund (CEF) data from October 29 and government relief measures.

The final pump prices will depend on international market movements, the rand's value against the US dollar, and other fuel pricing factors. Rising oil prices to around $115 per barrel and the rand trading at R16.50 to the dollar could erode these projected savings. The new prices are scheduled to take effect on November 3.

Why it mattersSouth African consumers will pay less for fuel in November, saving money on diesel and petrol.

Ex-Idac head Johnson and ex-Crime Intelligence Khan granted bail

The Pretoria Magistrate’s Court granted bail to Andrea Johnson and Feroz Khan on charges of defeating the ends of justice.

Former Investigating Directorate Against Corruption (Idac) head Advocate Andrea Johnson and ex-deputy head of Crime Intelligence Feroz Khan were granted R10 000 bail each by the Pretoria Magistrate’s Court on Friday. They face charges of defeating the ends of justice and assault with intent to do grievous bodily harm, with Johnson also charged for contravening the National Prosecuting Authority Act.

Police arrested Johnson and Khan on Friday morning in connection with allegations that Johnson gave Khan information about a case authorities had opened against him. Crime Intelligence Officer Manana Leonora Phetlhe had opened a case of harassment and assault against Khan in June 2018, alleging he physically attacked her with a pen and his hands, causing injuries. Former Hawks colonel Kobus Roelofse told the Madlanga commission on 10 July 2026 that Johnson compromised the criminal investigation by improperly involving herself.

The state alleges that Johnson, without permission, disclosed information to Khan, and they acted in common purpose to hinder prosecution against Khan. This prompted an investigation into how Khan submitted a warning statement before the National Prosecuting Authority received the docket. The Commission’s Recommendation Task Team (CRTT) arrested the duo following allegations before the Madlanga commission. The state did not oppose bail, stating the offences fall under Schedule 1.

Why it mattersFormer anti-corruption boss Andrea Johnson and ex-Crime Intelligence official Feroz Khan are facing criminal charges for allegedly obstructing justice.

South Africa minister condemns brutal farm attack

Cattle farmer Donovan Marx was shot, stabbed, and had both ears cut off on his farm near Potchefstroom.

Agriculture minister Willie Aucamp has condemned the brutal attack on 50-year-old cattle farmer Donovan Marx, who was tortured on his farm between Potchefstroom and Ventersdorp on Wednesday. Marx was shot, stabbed, and had both of his ears severed before being left to recover in hospital.

Aucamp stated that the extreme cruelty of the attack highlights the challenges faced by rural communities and that such barbaric acts have no place in South Africa. He emphasized that farmers are the backbone of the nation's food security and that risks to their safety directly endanger the country's food supply.

The minister called for reinforced rural crime responses, including dedicated intelligence, specialised investigative teams, and increased police visibility. Ian Cameron, Chairperson of Parliament’s Portfolio Committee on Police, also called for farm attacks to be recognised as violent crimes.

Why it mattersCattle farmer Donovan Marx suffered severe injuries including having both ears cut off during a violent farm attack.

Cape Town urges low-income residents to apply for rates relief

City of Cape Town encourages households earning under R7 500 monthly to seek assistance with municipal bills.

Cape Town is encouraging households with a combined monthly income below R7 500 to apply for the City’s indigent grant. This follows a General Valuation that increased some property values, leading to changes in electricity tariffs and new refuse removal charges for certain residents. The city offers relief on property rates, water, sanitation, and refuse removal for qualifying applicants.

Approved applicants can receive monthly assistance for electricity consumption below 250kWh, or 450kWh for prepaid meter users, excluding Eskom customers. Property owners whose valuations now exceed R620 000 are responsible for property rates and all applicable services unless they qualify for assistance. Those with property values between R500 001 and R620 000 may see shifts from the Lifeline to the Domestic electricity tariff, potentially incurring refuse charges.

The City of Cape Town warns that unpaid refuse charges could lead to debt deductions from electricity purchases and further debt collection actions. Residents whose property values have increased are urged to apply for indigent benefits if their household income is below R7 500 per month. The indigent grant applies only to the first refuse bin registered on a property.

Why it mattersCape Town households earning less than R7 500 monthly may face increased municipal bills due to property value changes and new service charges.

Airports company focuses on passenger dignity

Airports Company South Africa acknowledges challenges faced by neurodivergent travellers.

Airports Company South Africa (ACSA) is reflecting on how to move passengers with dignity during Transport Month. ACSA's business involves managing aircraft, terminals, and runways, but the company states that behind every passenger statistic is a human being with a story and potential challenges.

The company acknowledges that navigating airports can be demanding for people with ADHD, dyslexia, and dyspraxia, citing difficulties with reading information quickly, following instructions in noisy environments, and sensory pressures from crowds and flashing screens. ACSA is questioning how people are experiencing airports, not just how efficiently they are moving through them.

ACSA aims to make Ubuntu practical by designing institutions and treating people with compassion, recognising that another person's dignity is connected to one's own. This approach seeks to ensure that technically functional systems do not unintentionally exclude people who may need more time or clearer information.

Why it mattersSouth African travellers, particularly those with neurodivergent conditions, face difficulties navigating airports.
