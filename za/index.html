Swimmer killed by shark; East London beaches closed

A 65-year-old open-water swimmer died after a shark attack at Orient Beach, prompting the closure of popular local beaches.

Janine Lochrenberg, a retired nuclear radiographer and musician, was attacked during an early morning swim at Orient Beach on Tuesday. Fellow swimmers, including her son, attempted to rescue her and administered CPR, but she succumbed to her injuries at the scene. The Buffalo City Metropolitan Municipality has closed Orient Beach and adjacent Eastern Beach indefinitely and urged the public to stay out of the water while safety assessments are conducted.

Colonel Siphokazi Mawisa, a police spokesperson, confirmed that an inquest docket has been opened to investigate the circumstances of the death. Tributes have poured in for Lochrenberg, who was described as a cherished member of the East London open-water swimming community, known for her warmth and positive presence. The community expressed deep sadness, noting the significant void left by her loss.

The municipality has deployed emergency services and law enforcement to secure the coastline and is urging beachgoers to remain vigilant. Safety warnings have also been issued for other beaches in the area, with authorities advising the public to comply with all safety notices and avoid unnecessary risks. Further updates will be provided as more information becomes available.

Why it mattersBeach closures and safety warnings directly affect public access to coastal areas for recreation and tourism.

Tshwane Water Contamination Sickens 408

Authorities issued a no-tap-water advisory across five Tshwane communities due to E. coli and coliform bacteria contamination, leaving hundreds ill.

Residents in five Tshwane communities are under a strict advisory not to use tap water after contamination led to 408 people falling ill, primarily children under four. The warning, in effect on Tuesday, October 20, follows an investigation into failures at the Bronkhorstspruit Water Treatment Works. Water and Sanitation Minister Pemmy Majodina confirmed that untreated water was supplied for three weeks without prior notification to residents in areas including Bronkhorstspruit, Zithobeni, Dark City, Rethabiseng, and Sokhulumi.

The contamination stemmed from the municipality failing to chlorinate water between September 24 and October 14. Water drawn from the Bronkhorstspruit River, which receives sewage from malfunctioning infrastructure, showed extremely high levels of E. coli and total coliform bacteria on October 14. Health authorities recorded 408 cases of gastrointestinal illness, with patients reporting diarrhoea and stomach cramps. Norovirus, Campylobacter, and Shigella were detected in patient samples, though no cholera bacteria were found.

The City of Tshwane restored chlorine supplies on October 15 and reopened the treatment plant the next day, but the water has not yet been declared safe. Emergency water tankers have been deployed, with plans to increase the fleet to 36. The Water Research Commission will conduct independent tests, with an investigation report expected by October 28. Residents are advised to discard stored water and seek medical attention if symptoms appear.

Why it mattersFour hundred and eight residents, mostly young children, are suffering from gastrointestinal illnesses due to the consumption of contaminated tap water.

Bavuma's 179 Leads South Africa to 425/9 Against Australia

Captain Temba Bavuma scored a career-best 179, anchoring South Africa's recovery to 425/9 before stumps on day three.

South Africa recovered from a precarious 97/5 to post 425/9 on day three of the second Test against Australia in Gqeberha. Captain Temba Bavuma led the charge with a career-best 179, his fifth Test century, batting for over nine hours. He found crucial support from all-rounder Marco Jansen, who contributed 85, and spinner Simon Harmer, who made 82. Their 183-run partnership for the eighth wicket set a new Test record for South Africa.

Australia's bowlers struggled for wickets, with Scott Boland being the most effective, taking 5/81. Mitchell Starc chipped in with 3/89. Despite the strong batting performance, South Africa declared their innings at 425/9 shortly before the close of play. Australia finished day three at 3/1, trailing by 422 runs, with Kagiso Rabada taking the early wicket of opener Nic Maddinson.

The match, affected by significant rain delays on the first two days, now heads into its final two days with South Africa holding a strong position. The Proteas will aim to build on their bowling performance from Tuesday evening when play resumes on Wednesday, hoping to level the series against Australia, who are currently leading 1-0.

Why it mattersThis event is relevant to investors because it impacts the potential World Test Championship defense for South Africa.

Former SAPS general arrested with over 100 rounds of ammunition

A 57-year-old former police general faces charges for unlawful possession of ammunition discovered during a murder investigation raid.

Investigators found more than 100 rounds of South African Police Service (SAPS)-issued ammunition, including R5 and 9mm calibers, at the Centurion home of a 57-year-old former SAPS general. The discovery occurred during a raid on July 6, 2026, related to the murder investigation of former deputy national police commissioner Lieutenant-General Sindile Mfazi. SAPS confirmed the arrest on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, stating the former general left the service in 2009 and has no valid reason for possessing the ammunition.

The former general now faces a charge of unlawful possession of ammunition under the Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000. His identity has not been released pending his first court appearance. The arrest adds a new dimension to the ongoing investigation into Mfazi’s death, which was initially attributed to COVID-19 complications but is now being treated as a murder case following evidence of a poisonous substance detected after his body was exhumed.

While police seized documents and electronic devices potentially relevant to Mfazi’s murder, they have not disclosed if the ammunition discovery is directly linked to the former commissioner's death, nor has the former general been charged with Mfazi’s murder. The suspect is scheduled to appear before the Johannesburg Magistrate’s Court on Tuesday.

Why it mattersA former police general is facing charges for possessing ammunition, impacting the ongoing murder investigation of a high-ranking former police official.

Politics · 2026-10-21

Mbeki Denies Apartheid Crimes Cover-Up

Former president Thabo Mbeki told a judicial inquiry he did not order prosecutors to halt apartheid-era crime cases or shield officers.

Former South African President Thabo Mbeki denied on Monday that he ordered prosecutors to stop cases involving apartheid-era crimes or made secret deals to protect former security officers. Mbeki stated to a judicial inquiry in Johannesburg that the allegations were a "complete fabrication." The inquiry is examining claims of political interference that allegedly prevented investigations and prosecutions of crimes committed during white minority rule.

Mbeki, who led South Africa from 1999 to 2008, suggested the National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) should explain its failure to pursue cases referred by the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC). His testimony follows renewed efforts to address unprosecuted apartheid-era crimes, with the Foundation for Human Rights and 25 victim families calling for explanations and apologies. The African National Congress government has faced criticism for prioritizing reconciliation over justice for victims.

President Cyril Ramaphosa initiated the inquiry after survivors and victims' families sued successive governments for inaction on unresolved killings and disappearances. The TRC, chaired by Archbishop Desmond Tutu, offered amnesty for full disclosure, but individuals denied amnesty or who did not apply remained liable for prosecution, leaving many families waiting decades for justice. Mbeki also submitted a sound clip of a Radio Freedom broadcast from the eve of the Cradock Four burial to the commission.

Why it mattersFormer president Thabo Mbeki's testimony before a judicial inquiry addresses allegations of political interference in prosecuting apartheid-era crimes, directly impacting victims' families seeking justice.
