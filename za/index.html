Astral Foods holds firm on pay as Festive Chicken strike spreads

The poultry group says it cannot meet workers’ demands for increases above 74%, while some employees are seeking more than 100%.

Astral Foods is enforcing “no work, no pay” as employees at its Festive Chicken factory in Olifantsfontein, Gauteng, continue a strike that has spread to workers at some group farms. The strike, led by the Simunye Workers’ Forum (SWF), has lasted more than two weeks.

Astral chief executive Gary Arnold told The Citizen that some factory workers have returned, while others remain on strike. SWF members seek a monthly salary of R13 500, up from R6 500 for the lowest-paid workers. Arnold said the group is still recovering from severe losses caused by load shedding and cannot provide the demanded increase of more than 74%; some workers seek increases above 100%.

Arnold said workers approached the Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration (CCMA) over the pay dispute and received a certificate of non-resolution. He said they had no picketing rules and were acting under the Public Gatherings Act. A 2023 change to CCMA rules clarified commissioners’ responsibilities in disputes that could lead to strikes, according to law firm Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr, which was not commenting on this strike.

Why it mattersThe strike involves workers seeking R13 500 a month from R6 500, while Astral says it cannot provide the demanded increase of more than 74%.

Politics · 2026-11-18

DA weighs mayoral swap as Johannesburg coalition talks stall

The proposal would send party leader Geordin Hill-Lewis to Johannesburg and Helen Zille to Cape Town, but Zille says she is not taking it seriously.

Helen Zille has dismissed talk of replacing Geordin Hill-Lewis as Cape Town mayor, as reports describe a proposed swap that would make Hill-Lewis Johannesburg mayor. The idea has been raised with ActionSA during coalition negotiations with the Democratic Alliance (DA), according to sources in both parties.

The DA won 91 of Johannesburg’s 270 council seats in the 4 November local government elections, leaving it short of a majority. ActionSA leader Herman Mashaba has said his party will not back Zille for Johannesburg mayor and offered to leave the race if she does. Hill-Lewis told 702 that ActionSA’s demand to drop Zille was difficult for the DA to accept, but he has not ruled out working with the party.

Zille, who was Cape Town mayor from 2006 to 2009 before becoming Western Cape premier, said she was not taking the rumour seriously. She has previously said she would lead the DA’s opposition caucus in Johannesburg if she did not secure the mayoralty; the DA has not announced changes to its mayoral candidates.

Why it mattersJohannesburg’s 270-seat council has no DA majority, and ActionSA has offered to withdraw its mayoral candidate if Zille does the same.

Weather service warns of flooding and power cuts in two provinces

A Yellow Level 2 alert covers severe thunderstorms in parts of the Free State and North West on Wednesday, 18 November.

South Africa's weather service issued a Yellow Level 2 warning for severe thunderstorms in parts of the Free State and North West on Wednesday, 18 November. The storms are expected to bring localised flooding, property damage and disruptions to power and communication services.

The service said the warning covers northern and northeastern parts of the Free State and southeastern parts of the North West. It forecast isolated to scattered showers and thundershowers in the central and eastern Free State, and isolated showers and thundershowers in eastern North West.

The service forecast extremely high fire danger in western parts of both provinces, as well as the Northern Cape and parts of the Western Cape. Very hot to extremely hot conditions are also expected in the interior of the Namakwa District and along parts of the Western Cape coast and interior.

Why it mattersResidents and businesses in the warning areas face expected localised power and communication disruptions alongside flooding on Wednesday, 18 November.

Security · 2026-11-18

Four Zimbabweans abandon bail bids in South Africa war-recruitment case

The fifth accused, Abel Muchenje, plans to seek release, but prosecutors told the Kempton Park court they will oppose his application.

Four of five Zimbabwean nationals accused of trying to leave South Africa to join the Russia-Ukraine war abandoned their bail applications at Kempton Park Magistrate’s Court on Tuesday, 17 November. The fifth, Abel Muchenje, 40, said he intends to apply for bail.

National Prosecuting Authority spokesperson Magaboke Mohlatlole said prosecutors would oppose Muchenje’s application. The five were arrested on 8 November after an intelligence-led investigation, while allegedly preparing to board flights out of South Africa. They face charges under the Regulation of Foreign Military Assistance Act 15 of 1998.

The court postponed the case to 24 November for possible plea and sentence negotiations under the Criminal Procedure Act. The four who dropped their applications are Weight Mudhanyane, 23, Javangwe Munyaradzi, 41, Wesley Sothole, 24, and Knowledge Ndunduma, 46.

Why it mattersMuchenje faces prosecutors’ opposition to his bail application, with the court due to consider possible plea and sentence negotiations on 24 November.

Netflix schedules South Africa’s Love Is Blind debut for 4 December

The first six episodes of the dating series will premiere on that date, with two more released on 11 December and the finale on 18 December.

Netflix will launch the first South African edition of Love Is Blind on 4 December, with 27 single contestants trying to find a spouse without seeing their potential partners. The series is hosted by Dineo and Solo Langa.

Six episodes are scheduled for the opening release, followed by two on 11 December and the final episode on 18 December. Contestants date in specially designed pods, where they can speak through a wall; they must get engaged to progress and meet face to face.

Cape Town production house afrOkaans produces the show. After the pod dates, couples who progress travel to a location where they can spend time together in person and meet the other couples.

Why it mattersThe 27 contestants must get engaged before they can meet their potential partners face to face.
