Police arrest four suspects in Zolani Tete murder investigation

South African police have arrested four individuals in connection with the shooting death of former two-division boxing world champion Zolani Tete outside his Mdantsane home.

Eastern Cape police have arrested three additional suspects in the killing of Zolani Tete, bringing the total number of people in custody to four. Tete, a celebrated boxer known for his power and precision, was shot dead on Friday, August 21, outside his residence in Mdantsane, according to The South African.

One of the arrested suspects is scheduled to appear in court on Monday to face charges of murder and attempted murder, as reported by BBC Africa. The arrests follow the initial police investigation into the death of the former world champion, which left South Africans in shock and heartbreak.

Tete, born on March 8, 1988, in Mdantsane, was a two-weight world champion who turned professional in 2006. He held the IBF junior-bantamweight title in 2014 and the WBO bantamweight title in 2017, setting a Guinness World Record for the fastest knockout in a world title fight in November 2017.

Why it mattersThe arrests provide a development in the investigation into the death of a prominent South African sports figure, affecting his family, the boxing community, and those seeking justice.

Dolly Parton, music icon, dies at 80

Country music legend Dolly Parton, whose career spanned almost six decades, passed away on Tuesday at the age of 80, her family confirmed.

Dolly Parton, the influential country music icon, died on Tuesday at 80 years old, her family announced. Her passing follows a period of health difficulties and the death of her husband, Carl Dean, in March 2025. The cause of death has not been disclosed by her family.

Born in Tennessee in 1946, Parton rose from humble beginnings to become a globally recognized entertainer. She released her debut album in 1967 and achieved her first US country No. 1 hit with “Joshua” in 1971. Her career included numerous hits like “Jolene” and “I Will Always Love You,” selling over 100 million records worldwide.

Beyond music, Parton also starred in films such as "9 to 5" and "Steel Magnolias," and developed a substantial business empire including Dollywood, a theme park in Tennessee. Her philanthropic efforts included the Dolly Parton’s Imagination Library, which provides free books to children, and a $1 million donation to Moderna’s Covid-19 vaccine research.

Why it mattersThe global music industry and fans worldwide are affected by the loss of an artist who sold over 100 million records and influenced multiple genres.

US National Institutes of Health lifts South Africa funding ban

The US National Institutes of Health has reversed its nearly 18-month ban on South Africa, allowing funding for meritorious research projects to resume.

The US National Institutes of Health (NIH) has lifted its ban on South Africa, which had halted many clinical trials and led to researcher retrenchments since February of last year. An internal memo dated August 17, signed by NIH director Jay Bhattacharya, confirmed that funding for “meritorious research projects” in South Africa may now proceed.

The ban, which began in January 2025, affected NIH grants and US Agency for International Development (USAID) aid, creating a crisis for HIV and TB research in the country. Doctors Without Borders, Treatment Action Group, and the South African Medical Research Council (SAMRC) reported in May 2025 that critical research initiatives and infrastructure investments were impacted.

Ntobeko Ntusi, president and CEO of the SAMRC, confirmed that the decision follows 12 months of negotiations with the NIH. Research in South Africa, largely led by local scientists with NIH funding, has significantly influenced global health policies for TB and HIV prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and care over the past two decades.

Why it mattersSouth African researchers and health initiatives will benefit from renewed funding, allowing critical HIV and TB research to continue after many clinical trials were halted and researchers retrenched.

South African Women Leaders Discuss Power and Legacy

Seventy years after a significant women's march, leaders from various generations reflected on progress and persistent challenges in achieving real power for women in South Africa.

A recent Women's Month conversation, "From the Union Buildings to the Boardroom," brought together women leaders to discuss the journey from gaining access to wielding actual power. Hosted by A Seat At The Table Connect Board Mastery, the event highlighted that while much has changed since 1956, securing leadership positions for women is only one aspect of ongoing efforts.

The host emphasized that many attendees were the first in their families to achieve milestones like attending university or entering corporate South Africa, urging them to ensure they are not the last. This idea of passing on opportunities and fighting for those who follow became a central theme of the evening's discussions.

Former minister Ayanda Dlodlo delivered the keynote address, dedicating it to women and a man from her unit killed in 1988, and a friend assassinated in Eswatini. Dlodlo argued that the history of women's leadership in South Africa predates the 1956 march, citing figures like Queen Mmantatisi and Queen Nandi as examples of African women who led communities and resisted oppression long before formal structures existed.

Why it mattersThis discussion directly affects women in South Africa by highlighting the ongoing struggle for leadership and power in both political and corporate spheres, urging them to create pathways for future generations.

Politics · 2026-08-25

Johannesburg panel urges youth inclusion for city renewal

A Metro Lives dialogue highlighted that Johannesburg's urban renewal efforts will fail without the active participation of its young people, who face food insecurity and exclusion.

Johannesburg's renewal efforts must prioritize its young people, according to speakers at a Metro Lives dialogue hosted by the DG Murray Trust at Constitution Hill. Matshidiso Lencoasa, a budget analyst at SECTION27, stated that the city's future depends on treating youth as co-authors, not just beneficiaries, of its development.

The discussion, held on Monday, August twenty-fourth, included Dr. Tlaleng Mofokeng, EFF mayoral candidate; Nomzekelo Mohamed, Democratic Alliance PR councillor; Mark Heywood, co-founder of Union Against Hunger; and ActionSA MMC candidate Ayanda Allie. They explored how local governments can build safer, healthier, and more inclusive communities by centering children and young people.

A high school learner shared her experience of going to school hungry, underscoring the failing urban food system described by Heywood. He noted that the City of Johannesburg demolished a long-standing inner-city farm, where young people learned agroecology, to build a multi-purpose center, instead of nurturing such initiatives to address food insecurity.

Why it mattersJohannesburg's young people are directly affected by food insecurity and exclusion, and their lack of participation in city planning impacts their daily lives and future prospects.
