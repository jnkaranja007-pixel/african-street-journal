South Africa youth unemployment hits 47.3%

The skills revolution aims to bridge the gap between young people's aspirations and available opportunities.

South Africa's youth unemployment rate reached 47.3% in the third quarter of 2026, affecting approximately five million people aged 15 to 34. The official unemployment rate increased to 33.7% in the same period, with the expanded unemployment rate at 44%. Although 101,000 additional people found employment, unemployment rose by 97,000 as more people entered the labour market.

Minister of Higher Education and Training Buti Manamela is advancing a coordinated approach to skills development as part of the Skills Revolution. Initiatives like the Mpumalanga Skills Development Zone in Nkangala and the Dan Nkosi Skills Development Centre in Paulpietersburg, KwaZulu-Natal, aim to bring skills development closer to communities and connect them to economic opportunities.

The Skills Revolution is guided by President Cyril Ramaphosa's national development priorities and involves government, educational institutions, business, and social partners. It aims to provide a long-term foundation for expanding economic participation, improving productivity, and preparing South Africans for a changing economy.

Why it mattersThe skills revolution aims to equip young South Africans with the capabilities needed for existing and emerging economic opportunities.

Cosatu slams Ramaphosa over 3.5% politician salary hike

The trade union federation argues the increase is a poor message amid high unemployment and failing public services.

Cosatu has criticised President Cyril Ramaphosa for approving a 3.5% salary increase for politicians and public office-bearers, effective from April 1, 2026. This decision comes as South Africans grapple with high unemployment and deteriorating public services.

Cosatu Parliamentary Coordinator Matthew Parks questioned the timing, noting that municipalities struggle with finances and service delivery while 13 million people are unemployed. He highlighted that municipal workers have often not been paid, and their medical aids have defaulted.

The federation supports a separate 4% increase for magistrates, deeming it crucial for retaining skilled professionals in the judiciary. The salary adjustments were based on recommendations from the Independent Commission for the Remuneration of Public Office-Bearers.

Why it mattersSouth African politicians will receive a 3.5% salary increase while the public faces unemployment and service delivery failures.

Politics · 2026-11-12

Constitutional Court hears challenge to Northern Cape liquor hours law

The DG Murray Trust asks the court to confirm that the 2024 law extending alcohol trading hours was passed without meaningful public participation.

On Tuesday, 10 November, the Constitutional Court heard a case over the Northern Cape Gambling and Liquor Act of 2024, which allowed bars and taverns to sell alcohol until 2am seven days a week, stores to sell liquor on Sundays, and nightclubs to sell until 6am from Monday to Saturday. The DG Murray Trust, a public health organisation, is asking the court to confirm that the provisions are invalid.

The Northern Cape High Court ruled in April that the provincial legislature failed in its constitutional duty to facilitate public involvement before passing the law, and declared the Act invalid. The trust is also asking the court to suspend that declaration for 24 months so the legislature can fix the defects, with a midnight closing time for on-consumption sales and no Sunday off-consumption sales in the meantime.

The trust also wants the court to find that the legislature had a duty to protect dignity, safety, health and children's rights when regulating alcohol. The high court declined to make that finding. Maurice Smithers of Working for an Alcohol-Safer South Africa said the World Health Organization lists decreasing availability, increasing cost and limiting advertising as critically necessary steps to reduce alcohol harm.

Why it mattersBars, taverns, nightclubs and liquor stores in the Northern Cape are directly affected, as the 2024 law set their trading hours and the court's ruling will determine whether those hours stand.

Sport · 2026-11-12

Swimmer Matt Sates reportedly seeks Australian citizenship

The two-time South African Olympian could compete for Australia at the 2028 Los Angeles Games, according to a broadcaster's report.

Two-time South African Olympian Matt Sates is reportedly seeking Australian citizenship, a move that could let him represent Australia at the 2028 Olympic Games in Los Angeles. Broadcaster Mat Thompson revealed the pursuit during the 2026 Australian Swimming Trials in Sydney in June, and The South African first reported it that month.

Sates, 22, represented South Africa at the Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 Olympics. He won gold in the 200m individual medley and bronze in the 400m individual medley at the 2022 World Short Course Championships. At Tokyo he finished 14th in the 200m individual medley, and at Paris he placed 20th in the 200m butterfly.

The move follows Tatjana Smith's announcement that she intends to represent the Netherlands at the 2028 Olympics, a switch the Mail & Guardian called a bitter blow for South African sport. Darian Townsend (US), Jessica Pengelly (Australia), Sarah Poewe (Germany) and Keri-anne Payne (Great Britain) have also changed allegiance.

Why it mattersSouth African swimmers switching allegiance to other countries reduces the pool representing South Africa at major international competitions such as the 2028 Olympics.
