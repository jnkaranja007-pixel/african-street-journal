Suliman Carrim granted bail on contempt charge

North West businessman Suliman Carrim was arrested on October 15 for failing to appear before a commission.

Pretoria Magistrate’s Court granted bail to North West businessman Suliman Carrim on Wednesday, October 28, 2026. Magistrate Curlewis ruled Carrim had met stringent conditions for release after his arrest at OR Tambo International Airport on October 15. Carrim faces a charge of failing to appear before a commission after a criminal complaint for alleged contempt of court was lodged.

Carrim last appeared before the commission on March 9 and 10, 2026, and was due to return on April 15 but allegedly suffered a heart attack. His repeated absences between April and July due to ill health raised concerns, and he was ordered to appear in person on August 14 but failed to do so. He also faces separate charges of attempted theft of motor vehicles, fraud, and uttering before the Musina Magistrate’s Court, related to an incident on July 22, 2026.

The businessman is also under investigation for alleged fraud and corruption involving the Ngaka Modiri Molema District Municipality, a matter handled by the cold case unit since 2025. President Cyril Ramaphosa authorised the Special Investigating Unit (SIU) in September to probe maladministration, corruption, and irregular expenditure at North West Transport Investments (NTI), including a R168 million tender awarded to a joint venture including Carrim’s company, Ziggy Investments.

Australia fight back after South Africa reach 329

Mitchell Starc took six wickets as South Africa were bowled out for 329 on day one of the third Test in Cape Town.

South Africa reached 329 runs on Tuesday before being bowled out on the first day of the third Test against Australia in Cape Town. The Proteas were sent in to bat at Newlands, with four players scoring half-centuries and four dismissed for ducks. Marques Ackerman top-scored with 95 runs, sharing a 158-run opening partnership with Aiden Markram, who made 78.

The home team were in a strong position at 203/2 but lost four wickets for 29 runs. Middle-order batters David Bedingham scored 50, Ryan Rickelton made 42, and Marco Jansen added 52. Fast bowler Mitchell Starc led Australia's fightback, taking 6/72, his 20th five-wicket haul, to end South Africa's innings.

Starc stated a "reset at lunch" helped Australia's recovery after South Africa's strong start. He conceded 39 runs in his first seven overs but took the last six wickets. Stumps were drawn before the scheduled close of play after South Africa were dismissed.

Johannesburg residents protest water cuts

A home for disabled residents faced a hygiene crisis during a five-day water outage, while protests spread over service failures.

A five-day water outage in Johannesburg plunged a home for people with disabilities into a hygiene crisis, with soiled nappies and dirty linen piling up. At St Giles Association for the Physically Disabled in Dewetshof, 29 frail or disabled residents and their caregivers struggled with basic sanitation. Emergency water reserves ran out within a morning, making it difficult to prepare meals and provide drinking water.

Manager Palesa Nhlapo described the past few days as "hell" and a "complete nightmare" due to the unprecedented length of the outage. Staff resorted to hiring a vehicle to collect water from the Jeppe Fire Station, an additional expense for the nonprofit which relies on residents' contributions for only 40% of its running costs. Fears of disease spread grew as staff struggled to keep toilets and floors clean.

Meanwhile, Johannesburg Water lifted a precautionary water quality alert in Bezuidenhout Valley, allowing residents to resume normal water use after samples passed microbiological and chemical checks. The utility advised residents to flush their internal pipes before using tap water again. Rand Water has warned of "exceptionally high" water usage straining the system.

Why it mattersJohannesburg residents faced severe hygiene issues and service disruptions due to prolonged water cuts.

Moriel Infrastructure Group removed from South African contractor register

Public Works and Infrastructure Minister Dean Macpherson announced the company's removal on Tuesday, 27 October 2026.

Moriel Infrastructure Group, a construction company linked to Zimbabwean businessman Lawrence Mudzinganyama, has been removed from South Africa’s National Register of Contractors. Public Works and Infrastructure Minister Dean Macpherson announced the decision on Tuesday, 27 October 2026, following findings of misrepresentation in its registration applications.

The Construction Industry Development Board (CIDB) approved the removals on 15 October after investigating Moriel since 2025 over concerns about its financial and technical capabilities. Macpherson stated that the company was served formal notices in 2025 and again on 11 September 2026, and was given an opportunity to respond. Moriel previously held several high-level CIDB designations.

Moriel was among 13 construction companies removed from the register in the latest action, bringing the total number of contractors removed since June 2024 to 75. The minister warned that misrepresenting capabilities could allow unqualified companies to compete for government projects. Mudzinganyama's criminal case remains a separate matter for the justice system.

Why it mattersMoriel Infrastructure Group was removed from South Africa's National Register of Contractors, barring it from state construction work.

five polls show MK party ahead of ANC in Durban

Jacob Zuma's MK Party leads the ANC in five election polls for eThekwini municipality ahead of November 4 polls.

Five election polls indicate the MK Party, led by Jacob Zuma, is poised to defeat the ANC in the eThekwini municipality. The latest eNCA/MarkData survey shows MK with 48% support, while the ANC trails in third place with 14.5%. The DA holds 25.2% and the IFP 6% in this survey released on Sunday, October 25.

These findings suggest Zuma's party could control KwaZulu-Natal's largest municipality after the November 4 elections. Four other surveys conducted recently also place MK ahead of the ANC in eThekwini. An Ipsos survey in September showed MK at 55% and the ANC at 10%, while an earlier Social Research Foundation survey in July gave MK 33% and the ANC 18%.

Service delivery failures, particularly water shortages and flood damage response, are cited as reasons for voter frustration, with 74.6% of respondents blaming the ANC. Despite its lead, MK faces a challenge to secure an outright majority, and voter turnout and coalition agreements could be decisive.

Why it mattersSouth Africans in eThekwini face a potential shift in municipal control from the ANC to Jacob Zuma's MK Party.
