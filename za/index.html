News · 2026-10-24

three cops convicted over nathaniel julies murder

Constable Caylene Whiteboy fired the fatal shot, while two other officers planted evidence.

Three police officers have been convicted in the Gauteng Division of the High Court in Palm Ridge for the August 2020 murder of 16-year-old Nathaniel Julies in Eldorado Park, Johannesburg. Constable Caylene Whiteboy was found guilty of murder and defeating the ends of justice for firing the fatal shot. Sergeant Simon Ndyalvane was convicted of murder, unlawful possession of ammunition, defeating the ends of justice, and perjury. Detective Voster Netshiongolo was found guilty of unlawful possession of ammunition and perjury but acquitted of being an accessory after the fact.

The court heard that Whiteboy claimed she believed her firearm was loaded with rubber bullets when she shot Julies, who had Down syndrome, after he left his home for a tuckshop during Covid-19 lockdown. Evidence presented by the National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) revealed that Netshiongolo, with Ndyalvane's assistance, planted ammunition and fabricated a story that Julies was caught in a crossfire. Julies was pronounced dead at Chris Hani Baragwanath Hospital.

The officers remain on bail pending pre-sentencing proceedings scheduled for November 11. NPA regional spokesperson Magaboke Mohlatlole stated that the convictions represent an important step towards accountability and reaffirm that no one is above the law. The NPA hopes the conclusion of the trial will provide some measure of closure for the Julies family.

Why it mattersThree police officers were convicted for the murder of Nathaniel Julies, a 16-year-old boy with Down syndrome.

South Africa court halts illegal weight-loss jab production

Regulators won a legal battle against a pharmacy compounding popular weight-loss drugs, but illegal sales continue.

South African regulators have won a court case against iDexis, a Tshwane pharmacy illegally compounding weight-loss injections like Ozempic. The Gauteng High Court ordered the pharmacy to stop producing semaglutide, the active ingredient in Ozempic, after large quantities of its compounded drugs were seized. This action follows a surge in demand for weight-loss jabs, which suppress appetite and prolong feelings of fullness.

Stavros Nicolaou, chairperson of the Pharmaceutical Task Group, stated on October 14 that while the court order is a victory, regulators like Sahpra and the South African Pharmacy Council need to act swiftly with clearer guidelines. He warned that momentum could be lost if action is not taken quickly, leaving consumers at risk from illegal and experimental drugs. The panel included representatives from Sahpra, the police, and border control to address the trade in fake medicines.

Sahpra has received approximately 600 complaints this financial year regarding illegitimate and fake medicines sold through informal channels, including online, gyms, and hairdressers. Australia has already banned similar pharmacy compounding of weight-loss jabs, deeming them potentially unsafe. New rules to combat this trade in South Africa are still months away, leaving enforcement gaps.

Why it mattersSouth African consumers are at risk from illegal and experimental weight-loss injections being sold openly.

Kaizer Chiefs lose CAF Confederation Cup tie on penalties

Amakhosi won the match 1-0 against Red Arrows but could not convert three penalties in the shootout.

Kaizer Chiefs have been eliminated from the CAF Confederation Cup after a 3-2 penalty shootout loss to Red Arrows. The Soweto giants won the second leg match 1-0, with Bradley Cross scoring a header in the 66th minute to level the aggregate score at 1-1. However, the team failed to convert three of their five penalties during the shootout, resulting in their exit.

Coach Fernando da Cruz made several changes to the team that lost the previous week, including starting Bruce Bvuma in goal and bringing back Lebohang Maboe. The attack was led by Wandile Duba, Pule Mmodi, and Mduduzi Shabalala, who struggled to convert crosses into goals. Shabalala missed two clear chances to win the tie for Chiefs before the penalty shootout.

The starting XI for Kaizer Chiefs included Bvuma, Frosler, Cross, Miguel, Moloisane, Mthethwa, Mabaso, Mmodi, Duba, Shabalala, and Abrahams. The team opted to play wide, delivering crosses into the box that found no effective finishing. Despite winning the match 1-0, the penalty shootout determined their progression to the group stage.

Why it mattersKaizer Chiefs, a South African football club, crashed out of the CAF Confederation Cup.
