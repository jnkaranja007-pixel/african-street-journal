DA wins Cape Town, claims black voter gains

The Democratic Alliance declared victory in Cape Town with 58% of the vote, highlighting significant gains among black South Africans.

The Democratic Alliance (DA) declared victory in Cape Town on Thursday, November 5, securing an expected 58% of the vote. DA leader Geordin Hill-Lewis announced the win, emphasizing the party's breakthrough gains among black voters in the city's townships.

DA federal council chairperson Ashor Sarupen stated that projections indicated the DA would receive more votes from black South Africans than the EFF. This would position the DA as a mainstream party across all racial groups in South Africa.

Voter turnout for the 2026 local government elections reached 15.9 million, with preliminary results pointing to several hung councils. The Electoral Commission of South Africa expects to announce final election results on Sunday, November 8.

Why it mattersThe Democratic Alliance's claimed gains among black voters could reshape the party's national political ambitions.

Democrats seize US Congress, vow investigations into Trump

US President Donald Trump faces a hostile political landscape after Democrats won control of the House and Senate.

Democrats seized control of both the US House of Representatives and the Senate on Tuesday, ushering in a new political reality for President Donald Trump. The result leaves the 80-year-old president as a lame duck for his remaining two years in office. Democrats have vowed to launch investigations into Trump and his administration, focusing on accountability and affordability.

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries stated that the "massive corruption" from the "Trump Cartel" has hindered progress for Americans and pledged immediate change. Vice President JD Vance publicly diverged from Trump, attributing the midterm losses to the Iran war. Trump acknowledged the war's impact on gasoline prices but defended the decision for long-term security.

Florida Governor Ron DeSantis described the defeat as an "extinction-level event" for Republicans in many areas. Trump, however, warned Democrats against impeachment attempts, predicting it would "destroy" their party. The US dollar and oil prices dropped on Wednesday following the election outcome, impacting global markets.

Why it mattersSouth Africans will be affected by potential US political gridlock and its impact on global markets, including oil prices and the rand/dollar exchange rate.

South African police open over 120 criminal cases during elections

Arrests made across the country for offences including fraud, assault, and contraventions of the Electoral Act.

South African Police Service (Saps) opened more than 120 criminal cases and arrested 59 people over three days of voting for the 2026 local government elections. The National Joint Operational and Intelligence Structure (Natjoints) reported these figures on Thursday, noting incidents occurred nationwide despite a focus on 331 high-risk voting stations. Police intervened to ensure voting continued at stations facing operational or logistical challenges.

KwaZulu-Natal (KZN) recorded the highest police activity with 18 cases and seven arrests, including eight contraventions of the Electoral Act and four intimidation cases. A police sergeant was removed from duty for arriving at a voting station with political party posters, and a burglary at Mnyameni High School involved the theft of sensitive IEC materials like ballot papers and the voters' roll. Acting KZN police commissioner Phumelele Makoba stated internal investigations are underway for these breaches.

Election day saw 57 cases opened and 27 arrests, with contraventions of the Electoral Act being the most frequent offence. Special voting days resulted in 65 cases and 32 arrests. Lieutenant General Tebello Mosikili confirmed police responsibility continues to protect voting materials and secure results. Police have also recorded 21 murder cases in recent months considered politically motivated.

Why it mattersOver 120 criminal cases and 59 arrests were made during the 2026 local government elections, impacting public order and the integrity of the electoral process.

South Africa faces level 4 hail storms

Severe thunderstorms with heavy downpours and hail are expected in Johannesburg and Newcastle.

South Africa's northern KwaZulu-Natal, Gauteng, and the south-western Highveld of Mpumalanga face a Yellow Level 4 warning for severe thunderstorms on Friday, November 6, 2026. These storms are predicted to bring heavy downpours, hail, and severe lightning, with the potential for flooding and damage to infrastructure.

A Level 2 storm warning covers the eastern Free State, North West, central KwaZulu-Natal, and the Western Bushveld of Limpopo. Meanwhile, Musina is forecast to reach 39°C, and extremely high fire danger conditions are expected in parts of the Northern Cape, Free State, North West, and Limpopo.

Johannesburg will experience rain and storms with a high of 31°C and a 60% chance of thundershowers. Pretoria is expected to reach 33°C, and Soweto and Vereeniging 31°C, also with a 60% chance of thundershowers.

Why it mattersResidents in Johannesburg and Newcastle face potential damage to infrastructure and flooding due to severe thunderstorms.

Cape Town dog impaled fleeing fireworks

Max suffered fatal injuries trying to escape Guy Fawkes celebrations in Delft on November 5.

A Cape Town dog named Max died on Thursday, November 5, after being impaled on a security gate while trying to escape Guy Fawkes fireworks. Max panicked when fireworks began shortly after dark at his family’s home in Delft. His family had secured the gate, but he attempted to squeeze through the bars, sustaining severe injuries.

The Cape of Good Hope SPCA reported an 59% increase in calls on Guy Fawkes night, with 81 calls compared to 51 the previous year. Eight inspectors worked through the night, and at least five animals needed emergency veterinary care. SPCA communications manager Belinda Abraham stated Max should still be alive and urged residents to consider animals when celebrating with fireworks.

Veterinarians euthanised Max after a scan revealed extensive damage to his intestines and bladder. The SPCA is warning the public against donating to unverified animal rescue appeals, citing a recent social media appeal by Youth Pet Care founder Nazrudeen Adams that could not be verified. Residents can report distressed animals to the SPCA on 021 700 4158 or 021 700 4159.

Why it mattersA dog named Max died from injuries sustained while trying to escape fireworks, affecting his family and highlighting the dangers of fireworks to animals.
