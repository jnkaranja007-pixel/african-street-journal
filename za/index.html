ANC targets 90% vote in Limpopo elections

President Ramaphosa urged Limpopo to deliver over 90% of votes in the November 4 local government elections.

The African National Congress (ANC) in Limpopo faces pressure to achieve President Cyril Ramaphosa's goal of over 90% of the vote in the local government elections on November 4. Provincial leaders, including chair Phophi Ramathuba, have been campaigning across the province to secure this target. Ramaphosa had previously expressed his expectation for Limpopo to deliver higher numbers than the 70% received in the 2024 elections.

Provincial chair Phophi Ramathuba urged voters to cast their ballots for the ANC, referencing the sacrifices of past leaders like Oliver Tambo. ANC provincial spokesperson Tony Rachoene stated the party views the 90% target as a challenge to intensify campaign efforts, aiming to win all wards and municipalities. He expressed confidence in achieving a decisive majority due to strong internal unity and support mobilisation.

The uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) Party leader Jacob Zuma released a video urging South Africans to vote in the November 4 elections, stating his party is ready to govern. Zuma is expected to address supporters at KwaMashu on November 1. Political analyst Solly Rashilo suggested the 90% target might be ambitious, noting that municipal elections are influenced by service delivery and local dissatisfaction.

Why it mattersSouth African voters in Limpopo are being urged by the ruling ANC party to vote for them in the upcoming local government elections.

Simon Harmer leads South Africa to series win

Spin bowler Simon Harmer took career-best figures of 6/16 in the final innings to secure a 176-run victory against Australia.

Spin bowler Simon Harmer played a key role for South Africa in Cape Town on Saturday, taking career-best figures of 6/16 in Australia’s second innings. The 37-year-old’s performance helped the Proteas to a 176-run victory in the Test series decider at Newlands, securing a 2-1 series win. Harmer also achieved 10 wickets in the match for the first time in a five-day game, earning him the Player of the Match award.

Harmer, playing in his 17th test, credited his lengthy domestic career experience for his performance, stating he focused on dealing with expectations. He had struggled in the first two matches of the series, taking only two wickets in each. "It’s been a tough few weeks but I will enjoy tonight," Harmer said after the match. The Proteas secured their first Test series victory over Australia since 2018.

South Africa scored 329 in their first innings, with contributions from Marques Ackerman (95) and Aiden Markram (78), before Australia replied with 429, led by Josh Inglis (102). The Proteas then posted 387 in their second innings, featuring centuries from Wiaan Mulder (115) and Ryan Rickelton (101), setting Australia a target of 288. Australia were bowled out for 111 in their second innings on October 31, 2026.

Why it mattersSouth African cricket fans can celebrate a significant Test series victory over rivals Australia.

The Kiffness shows Johannesburg decline

Musician David Scott shared a video comparing Johannesburg in 2010 to 2026, highlighting infrastructure decay.

Musician David Scott, known as The Kiffness, has released a video showing the rapid deterioration of Johannesburg over 15 years. The 38-year-old artist used Google Street View images from 2010 and 2026 to illustrate the city's decline, comparing well-maintained streets then to neglected buildings and rubbish-strewn roads now.

Scott shared the video on X after a Polymarket report claimed Johannesburg was on the "brink of a total collapse" due to water shortages from damaged infrastructure. He stated his intention was to show how a functioning city can become derelict quickly, warning other major global cities of a similar fate if they do not act.

Johannesburg voters will elect councillors on Wednesday, November 4, during local government elections. A Polymarket poll indicates Helen Zille has a 57% chance of becoming the next mayor, with her campaign focusing on restoring basic services, infrastructure, financial management, and combating crime and corruption.

Why it mattersJohannesburg residents face deteriorating infrastructure, neglected public spaces, and potential water shortages.

Helen Zille warns Joburg voters against election boycott

The DA's Johannesburg mayoral candidate urged voters to cast ballots on November 4 to prevent corruption.

Democratic Alliance mayoral candidate Helen Zille warned Johannesburg voters on Saturday, October 31, that staying away from the November 4 polls risks allowing corrupt officials another five years in power. Speaking at the DA's final rally in Mamelodi, Tshwane, Zille urged frustrated residents to vote rather than boycott, stating that not voting rewards the politicians they wish to hold accountable.

Zille argued that Johannesburg cannot endure another five years of failed governance and highlighted her five-point plan to address service delivery issues. Her pledges include fixing potholes within 72 hours, ensuring 95% of traffic lights function, and restoring water and electricity supplies. The plan also aims to tackle corruption and professionalize the city's administration.

Johannesburg is a key battleground for Wednesday's local government elections, with issues like water outages and failing infrastructure dominating the debate. Approved special voters will cast ballots on November 2 and 3, ahead of the general election on November 4.

Why it mattersJohannesburg voters face a choice between voting for improved services or risking continued poor governance and corruption.

Into the Woods musical opens in Cape Town

Sondheim's dark fairy tale reimagining features 25 student performers at the Theatre on the Bay.

A new production of the musical Into the Woods, a reimagining of fairy tales by Sondheim and James Lapine, has opened at Cape Town's Theatre on the Bay. Directed by Sylvaine Strike, the show features a cast of 25 students from the Lamta musical theatre academy.

The production brings to life classic fairy tale elements including a Giant, a Wolf, Jack and the beanstalk, a Witch, and two princes. Actors use blackened planks to represent the dark, perilous woods, creating an interactive and dynamic forest setting.

The show revisits darker, pre-Disney versions of tales attributed to the Brothers Grimm, with characters like Cinderella and Rapunzel receiving modern updates. Cinderella's stepsisters' feet are described as being mutilated in an attempt to fit the slipper.

Why it mattersSouth African theatregoers in Cape Town can experience a dark and unhinged reimagining of classic fairy tales.
