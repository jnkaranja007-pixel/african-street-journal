Saftu rejects 3.5% salary hike for public officials

The union calls the increase indefensible amid millions struggling with unemployment and poverty.

South Africa's President Cyril Ramaphosa has approved salary increases of 3.5% for ministers, MPs, premiers, and MECs, and 4% for judges, effective April 1, 2026. These decisions follow recommendations from the Independent Commission for the Remuneration of Public Office-Bearers, with the president considering economic challenges.

The South African Federation of Trade Unions (Saftu) rejected the 3.5% raise for most public office-bearers, deeming it indefensible. Saftu general-secretary Zwelinzima Vavi stated that in the second quarter of 2026, 8.5 million people were officially unemployed and another 4.6 million were part of the potential labour force.

Saftu highlighted the country's poverty, inequality, and living costs, calling the increase for leaders inappropriate when many households face hardship. The union urged Ramaphosa and parliament to reverse the 3.5% salary adjustment.

Why it mattersMillions of South Africans face unemployment and poverty while public office-bearers receive salary increases.

South Africa's unemployment rate rises to 33.7%

Graduates still have a better chance of finding work, with an 11.9% unemployment rate in the third quarter of 2026.

South Africa's official unemployment rate edged up to 33.7% in the third quarter of 2026, leaving about 8.58 million people actively seeking work, according to Statistics South Africa's Quarterly Labour Force Survey. Employment rose by 101 000 from the previous quarter to 16.84 million, but the number of unemployed people also grew, by 97 000.

Graduates recorded an unemployment rate of 11.9% between July and September, down 0.6 of a percentage point from the previous quarter, while people without a matric qualification faced 39.2%. Compared with the same quarter of 2025, the national rate climbed from 31.9%, and unemployment rose across all education levels.

Youth unemployment among people aged 15 to 24 stood at 63.6%, and 35.7% of them were not in employment, education or training.

Why it mattersJob seekers without a matric qualification face unemployment of 39.2%, while graduates face 11.9%, according to the latest survey.

Nine Zimbabweans arrested in South Africa over alleged plans to fight in Ukraine war

Men arrested at OR Tambo airport and a Bruma hotel are charged under South Africa's foreign military assistance law.

Nine Zimbabwean men were arrested at OR Tambo International Airport and a hotel in Bruma, Johannesburg, and appeared in two Gauteng magistrates' courts on Monday, 9 November 2026. The National Prosecuting Authority says they allegedly planned to leave South Africa to take part in the Russia-Ukraine war, and they face charges under the Regulation of Foreign Military Assistance Act 15 of 1998.

Authorities have not said who allegedly recruited the nine men or how their travel was arranged. The arrests have renewed concern about recruitment networks that allegedly lure Zimbabweans with promises of civilian jobs in Russia, with some recruits later ending up on the frontline.

Investigations by Zimbabwean outlet CITE, published earlier in 2026, describe recruiters using WhatsApp groups and social media, and reportedly seizing passports after arrival in Russia. In March, four Harare men faced human trafficking charges over alleged recruitment, and Information Minister Zhemu Soda said 15 Zimbabweans had been killed in the conflict, a toll CITE later revised to 18.

Why it mattersZimbabweans lured by recruiters with job promises in Russia face the risk of being sent to fight, and some have reportedly had their passports confiscated.

Johannesburg and Cape Town set fines for parties that leave election posters up

Cape Town will remove leftover posters by November 18 and bill parties R123.48 each, excluding VAT, while Johannesburg issues R500 per poster after a warning.

The City of Cape Town has set Wednesday, November 18, as the deadline for political parties and independent candidates to remove election posters and swivel banners from streetlights and poles. Any left after that date will be taken down by the city and charged to the party at R123.48 per poster or banner, excluding VAT.

Johannesburg has a separate process under its outdoor advertising by-laws. Posters still up two weeks after the election will first trigger a warning from the Johannesburg Metropolitan Police Department, then a formal notice setting a deadline to clear them. If they remain, the city will bill the non-compliant party a penalty of R500 per poster to recover removal costs.

Both municipalities say leaving campaign material up is public littering that makes the city unsightly and compromises road safety. Residents who see posters after the deadline can report them in writing to Deputy Municipal Electoral Officer Catherine Overmeyer.

Why it mattersParties that miss the deadline will pay the removal cost themselves, at R123.48 per item in Cape Town and R500 per poster in Johannesburg.

Business · 2026-11-11

TNPA starts US$22.5M road upgrade at Richards Bay

The 18-month programme on Urania, Newark and Ventura Roads will strengthen pavements, widen lanes and improve drainage at the port.

Transnet National Ports Authority has started road rehabilitation work at the Port of Richards Bay worth R375 million (US$22.5 million). The programme covers Urania Road, Newark Road and Ventura Road and began in October 2026 with an 18-month schedule.

Winnie Mpanza, acting Port Manager at Richards Bay, said the investment showed the authority's commitment to maintaining port infrastructure for trade. The work includes pavement strengthening, road widening, concrete surfacing, drainage and road-safety upgrades, and is expected to create about 72 construction jobs.

The roads give access to terminals that handle coal, bulk cargo and breakbulk freight at Richards Bay, South Africa's main bulk export gateway. The port handled about 24 million tonnes of coal exports in 2025, well below the terminal's 91 million-tonne capacity, with rail constraints the main limit.

Why it mattersLogistics operators using the port's access roads will face disruption during the 18-month works and should see shorter truck turnaround times once they are finished.
