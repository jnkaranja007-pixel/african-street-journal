Khomotso Phahlane corruption trial delayed until November 9

Former acting police commissioner faces R54 million fraud and corruption charges linked to software procurement.

The R54 million fraud and corruption trial of former acting national police commissioner Khomotso Phahlane has been postponed to November 9, 2026. The High Court of South Africa, Gauteng Division, Pretoria, delayed the proceedings to allow accused individuals to vote in the local government elections. Phahlane and five others face charges including fraud, corruption, and contravention of the Public Finance Management Act.

Investigating Directorate Against Corruption (IDAC) spokesperson Henry Mamothame stated the postponement enables some accused residing outside Gauteng to cast their votes. The charges stem from two alleged irregular procurements: RIPJAR software for the #FeesMustFall protests and Daedalus communication-encryption software. The Daedalus software was reportedly used to encrypt calls and delete records while Phahlane was under investigation for other matters.

Phahlane is also involved in a separate R191 million police tender case for emergency warning equipment, where IDAC provisionally withdrew charges. In that matter, approximately R65 million was paid to the tender winner, with investigators preventing a further R22 million payment. The Pretoria High Court will hear both cases on November 9.

South Africa faces Agoa trade exclusion from January 2027

The EFF party stated that South Africa should not be bullied by the US and should pursue its own trade partnerships.

South Africa will be excluded from the Agoa trade agreement starting January 1, 2027, following an announcement by the Trump administration. The US cited reasons including barriers to American trade and investment, and practices threatening US national security and foreign policy interests. The Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF) party stated that South Africa must not allow the United States to use Agoa as leverage and should pursue global trade partnerships on its own terms.

The EFF described the exclusion as a culmination of sustained hostility, intimidation, and economic pressure against South Africa for exercising its sovereign right to determine its own affairs. The party noted that the US has been uncomfortable with South Africa's relations with Russia, China, and Iran, as well as its participation in Brics. The Cape Chamber of Commerce and Industry reported that Western Cape exporters are assessing operational costs, with agriculture and maritime manufacturing sectors most directly impacted.

The Citrus Growers Association highlighted potential supply chain and margin impacts for citrus producers in the Western and Northern Cape. Some organizations noted that Agoa-eligible goods represent a small fraction of total bilateral trade, suggesting limited immediate cause for panic. The EFF reiterated that the country will be fine without Agoa, stating access to markets should not be a privilege withdrawn due to political demands.

Why it mattersSouth African exporters, particularly in agriculture, face potential supply chain and margin impacts due to the exclusion from the Agoa trade agreement.

SAHRC flags R777m tanker bill amid Gauteng water crisis

The South African Human Rights Commission found infrastructure failure, not water shortage, causes Gauteng's water problems.

The South African Human Rights Commission (SAHRC) has raised concerns over millions spent on water tankers in Gauteng while residents face prolonged outages. SAHRC commissioner Dr Henk Boshoff stated on Monday, November 2, that water tankering has become a form of corruption and financial mismanagement.

The City of Tshwane spent R777 million on water tankers in the 2024/25 financial year, an increase from R47 million in 2021/22, according to the commission. Boshoff questioned this expenditure, suggesting the funds could have upgraded infrastructure, noting emergency water deliveries have become a regular municipal service.

The SAHRC inquiry, conducted from May 19-21, identified ageing infrastructure, poor maintenance, water losses, inadequate storage, and electricity interruptions as key contributors to Gauteng's water crisis. The commission made eight recommendations, including repairing wastewater plants and improving reservoir capacity, and will monitor their implementation.

Why it mattersGauteng residents are affected by prolonged water outages due to failing infrastructure and mismanagement of funds meant for repairs.

Johannesburg zoo investigates over 20 bird deaths at Zoo Lake

Veterinarians will conduct post-mortem examinations on selected carcasses to determine the cause of death for the waterbirds.

More than 20 waterbirds, including Egyptian geese, were found dead at Johannesburg's Zoo Lake on Sunday, November 1, 2026. Park visitors raised the alarm after discovering the carcasses, prompting an investigation by city authorities.

The Johannesburg City Parks and Zoo’s Environmental Conservation team collected the dead birds on Monday, November 2. Veterinarians from the Johannesburg Zoo will perform post-mortem examinations to establish the cause of death, while water samples have also been collected for testing.

The JCPZ suspects seasonal avian botulism, a disease fatal to birds, may have caused the deaths due to hot conditions and decomposing organic matter. Residents are advised to avoid swimming, consuming fish, and handling dead wildlife, and to report any findings to emi@jhbcityparks.com.

Why it mattersResidents of Johannesburg are advised to exercise caution around Zoo Lake due to potential water contamination and the discovery of dead birds.

South Africa allows Brazilian poultry imports despite disease

The South African Poultry Association stated that existing veterinary controls are sufficient to protect local producers from Newcastle disease.

The South African Poultry Association (SAPA) supports the Department of Agriculture's decision to continue poultry imports from Brazil, even after a Newcastle disease outbreak in Santa Catarina. SAPA's Broiler Organisation CEO, Izaak Breitenbach, stated that the risk to South Africa's poultry industry is low if affected areas are excluded from exports and Brazil adheres to veterinary health certification. Brazil notified South Africa of the outbreak on October 20.

The Department of Agriculture confirmed that imports of fresh poultry meat, hatching eggs, and day-old chicks can proceed under existing veterinary health certificates, with specific disease-control conditions. These include sourcing fresh poultry meat from farms disease-free for six months and locating abattoirs outside infected zones. Supplying farms for hatching eggs and chicks must be disease-free for 12 months and outside infected zones.

South Africa imports about 18,000 tonnes of poultry meat from Brazil monthly, a volume Breitenbach does not expect to change significantly, nor does he anticipate an impact on domestic prices. SAPA also stated that South African producers do not depend on Brazilian hatching eggs or chicks, possessing adequate domestic breeder capacity. Highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) is considered a greater concern than Newcastle disease.

Why it mattersSouth African consumers and producers face continued access to Brazilian poultry imports, with existing controls aimed at preventing disease spread.
