Airlink Cancels Springboks Flyover Amid Aviation Probe

The airline withdrew its application for a fly-past at FNB Stadium as the aviation authority investigates a previous event.

Airlink has withdrawn its application for a planned fly-past over FNB Stadium this weekend, ahead of the Springboks-All Blacks rugby match. The decision comes as the South African Civil Aviation Authority (SACAA) continues its investigation into a previous controversial fly-past by the airline over Cape Town Stadium on August 29. The SACAA confirmed that Airlink notified them of the withdrawal on Wednesday, September 2.

The regulator launched compliance verifications after questions arose regarding the August 29 operation, which had received prior approval. The SACAA has engaged with Airlink, obtained flight data recorder information, and requested further details from the airline. A full report with recommendations will be shared with Airlink once the analysis is complete. This investigation scrutinizes the low-level fly-past that occurred before the Springboks faced the All Blacks in Cape Town.

This planned event was the third fly-past Airlink had applied for in relation to the rugby tests. The first took place at Ellis Park on August 22 with SACAA approval, and the second was the August 29 event in Cape Town. The SACAA emphasized that aviation safety is paramount and non-negotiable, stating that the events of the past weekend needed proper examination to establish any safety concerns. South Africa has maintained a zero fatal-accident record in commercial airline operations for over four decades.

Accused Murderer Seeks Bail to Campaign for MK Party

A suspect in the killing of a DA councillor has asked the Howick Magistrate’s Court for bail, stating he needs to campaign for the MK Party.

Menelisi Zuma, the fifth person accused in the murder of uMngeni councillor Nhlalayenza Ndlovu, has applied for bail at the Howick Magistrate’s Court. The Democratic Alliance (DA) claims Zuma stated in court that his release is necessary to campaign for the uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) Party ahead of the November 4 Local Government Elections. Zuma, 44, was arrested in August by the KwaZulu-Natal Political Killings Task Team (PKTT).

Ndlovu, the DA chief whip in the uMngeni Municipality, was shot and killed at his home in December 2023. The investigation, led by the PKTT, has identified Chief Simphiwe Zuma as the alleged mastermind, who remains in custody. Two other accused, Khayelihle Shabalala and Thabo Mathonsi, pleaded guilty to murder in July and detailed their involvement, including alleged payment offers. Their cases have been separated.

DA leader in KwaZulu-Natal, Sithembiso Ngema, expressed concern over Zuma’s bail request, stating that a political party’s campaign should not supersede the justice system or the victim’s family. The DA questions the necessity of an accused murder suspect for an election campaign, highlighting the ongoing police commitment to prosecuting all individuals involved in political killings.

Why it mattersMenelisi Zuma, accused in a political murder, seeks bail to campaign for the MK Party in upcoming local government elections.

Springboks Scrum Coach Denies Tactical Infringements

Daan Human stated the team focuses on forward momentum and adapting to referee calls, dismissing claims of manipulating set-piece tactics.

Springboks scrum coach Daan Human addressed controversy surrounding the team's scrum technique following their victory over the All Blacks. Responding to accusations of manipulating the set-piece, Human asserted that the team's focus is on achieving forward momentum, not on "walking around" the mark. He emphasized that the Springboks aim to improve their own performance and are prepared to adapt to any directives from referees in upcoming matches.

Human expressed respect for referee Angus Gardner's consistency in the previous match and stated that the team does not question his decisions. He acknowledged that scrums can be complex and difficult to understand, but assured that any potential areas for improvement are addressed. The scrum coach highlighted that the team's concentration, estimated at 80% to 90%, is directed towards self-improvement and preparing for future games.

The scrum has been a central point of discussion in the lead-up to the next Test against the All Blacks. Human reiterated that the team's strategy is to go forward and that "walking around" is not an ideal tactic for them. He stressed that the scrum is a vital part of the team's culture and identity, and while opinions may differ, the focus remains on their own game and development.

Sharpeville survivors launch legal bid to overturn indemnity law

Survivors of the 1960 Sharpeville massacre are seeking to challenge a law that has prevented prosecution of perpetrators of the violence.

Abraham Mofokeng, who was 20 at the time of the Sharpeville massacre, is among survivors initiating a legal challenge to overturn an indemnity law. The law, passed a year after police opened fire on peaceful demonstrators on March 21, 1960, has prevented any perpetrators from being prosecuted for the killings.

The massacre, where police shot and killed at least 69 protesters, exposed the brutality of the apartheid regime. Mofokeng, now 86, still carries physical wounds from the event, including a bullet lodged in his spine and a limp from a leg gunshot. He stated that protesters were not seen as people but as laborers expected to obey without question.

The legal case, set to be announced on Thursday, aims to challenge the indemnity law that has shielded those responsible for the violence. The Sharpeville massacre remains a pivotal event in the history of the anti-apartheid movement, highlighting the systemic oppression faced by Black South Africans under the white-minority government.

Why it mattersSurvivors of the Sharpeville massacre are seeking justice and accountability for the 1960 killings by challenging a law that has prevented prosecution of perpetrators.

Messi Retires from Argentina National Team After World Cup Final Loss

The 39-year-old soccer superstar announced his international retirement, concluding a decorated career that included a World Cup win.

Lionel Messi has retired from international football with Argentina, marking the end of an era for the 39-year-old superstar. This decision follows Argentina's loss in the 2026 World Cup final, where Messi contributed significantly to the team's journey. He had previously retired in 2016 but returned, eventually winning major titles including the 2021 Copa América.

Messi's final international appearance saw him play a crucial role in Argentina reaching the World Cup final, directly contributing to 12 of the team's 19 goals. He scored eight times and provided four assists during the tournament. Despite not securing a World Cup victory this time, his presence was impactful throughout the competition.

The legendary player stated that it was time to step aside for younger talent, expressing deep personal hurt but understanding the decision. Messi, who debuted internationally in 2005, acknowledged giving his all for the national team over two decades, a period marked by intense pressure and comparisons to Diego Maradona.

Why it mattersSoccer fans worldwide, particularly those who follow Argentina and Lionel Messi's career, are affected by his retirement from international football.
