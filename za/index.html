High Court Review Delays Danny Jordaan Fraud Case

A fraud case against SAFA president Danny Jordaan and co-accused is postponed to November 27, 2026, pending a High Court decision on a review application concerning procedural.

The fraud case involving South African Football Association (SAFA) president Danny Jordaan and four co-accused has been postponed to November 27, 2026. The Specialised Commercial Crimes Court in Palm Ridge delayed the proceedings to await a High Court ruling on a review application. This application challenges the dismissal of an earlier bid to investigate alleged delays under Section 342A of the Criminal Procedure Act. The accused, including former SAFA CFO Grown Hluyo and Grit Communications director Trevor Neethling, face charges of fraud, theft, and conspiracy, with alleged fraud involving over R1 million.

The State, represented by the National Prosecuting Authority (NPA), stated its readiness to proceed with the trial. NPA spokesperson Magaboke Mohlatlole confirmed the State will wait for the High Court's decision before moving forward. Prosecutors allege Jordaan unlawfully signed and backdated a service agreement with Grit Communications in December 2017, bypassing SAFA approval processes to protect his personal image. He is also accused of procuring personal security services disguised as SAFA business.

Co-accused Grown Hluyo allegedly authorised payments to Grit Communications and a security firm without proper SAFA authorisation. Former acting SAFA CEO Russell Paul is accused of colluding in an unlawful 2018 service level agreement with Grit Communications. The NPA reiterated its commitment to concluding the case lawfully and allowing the justice system to function.

Why it mattersThe ongoing fraud trial and its delays could impact the reputation and leadership stability of SAFA, potentially affecting business operations and investor confidence in the organization.

Nepal Floods Leave Four South Africans Missing

Hundreds of tourists remain unaccounted for after flash floods and landslides struck Nepal's Rasuwa district near the Tibet border, killing at least 95 people.

At least four South African nationals are among hundreds of tourists missing following devastating flash floods in Nepal's Rasuwa district, close to the Tibet border. The disaster has resulted in at least 95 confirmed deaths, with authorities anticipating a higher toll as rescue efforts expand into isolated areas. Nepal's Tourism Board reported over 403 travellers unaccounted for, including the four South Africans.

The floods, which struck the popular trekking route between Nepal and Tibet, caused a massive surge of water that destroyed buildings, homes, and a bridge within minutes. Initial reports suggest an earthquake may have triggered an ice-rock avalanche, leading to the Lhende Khola river overflowing. China's Xinhua news agency reported a mudslide on the Nepali side impacting Tibet's Gyirong port, disrupting roads and infrastructure.

A large-scale rescue operation is underway, involving Nepal's armed forces and paramilitary units. Helicopters have been deployed, but high water levels and damaged infrastructure, including roads and bridges, are hampering access to some of the worst-affected regions. Communication lines are also disrupted, complicating efforts to identify and locate the missing individuals.

Why it mattersFour South Africans are among hundreds of foreign nationals missing after flash floods in Nepal, affecting families and communities.

Police Official Receives Fifth Arrest Warrant in Blue Lights Case

Suspended Ekurhuleni Metro Police Department Deputy Chief Julius Mkhwanazi faces fraud and corruption charges related to the irregular fitting of emergency lights on private.

Suspended Ekurhuleni Metro Police Department Deputy Chief Julius Mkhwanazi has been served with his fifth arrest warrant. The warrant is in connection with the ongoing "blue lights saga," which involves allegations of fraud and corruption. Mkhwanazi received the warrant after leaving a bail hearing for a separate murder case at the Brakpan Magistrates Court. He is scheduled to appear at the Germiston Magistrates’ Court on Friday.

The charges stem from allegations heard at the Madlanga Commission, which investigated the death of Emmanuel Mbense, who was allegedly tortured and then dumped in a dam. The "blue lights saga" specifically relates to Mkhwanazi's alleged unlawful approval of fitting blue lights to four private luxury vehicles. These vehicles were reportedly registered as City of Ekurhuleni assets and linked to businessman Vusimuzi 'Cat' Matlala's security company, Cat Protection Services.

The Independent Police Investigative Directorate (IPID) arrested two suspects on Thursday in connection with the blue lights case. An EMPD official and a private citizen face charges of fraud and corruption and are set to appear in court on August 28, 2026. A third suspect, also an EMPD official, was expected to be arrested later that night. The IPID stated that further details would be revealed in court.

Why it mattersThis case affects public officials and private citizens involved in alleged corruption and fraud related to the misuse of emergency vehicle equipment.

Thousands of dead fish wash up on Gqeberha beaches

Scientists are investigating the cause of a massive fish die-off, with pilchard herpesvirus suspected, prompting warnings for the public and potential impacts on the fishing.

Thousands of dead fish, predominantly sardines, have washed ashore along Gqeberha's coastline. Authorities are urging the public to avoid contact with the dead fish while scientists investigate the cause, with potential culprits including pilchard herpesvirus, an algal bloom, or sudden changes in ocean temperature. The scale of the die-off is significant, with experts noting that the visible beach casualties represent only a fraction of the total mortality occurring on the ocean floor.

Nelson Mandela Bay University Professor Nadine Strydom indicated a strong suspicion that the pilchard herpesvirus (PHV), recently identified in the Western Cape, is responsible for the widespread deaths. While other species have been affected in smaller numbers, the current event is primarily impacting the sardine population. This virus has previously caused large-scale mortality events in sardines in Australia during the 1990s, though it was later detected in healthy populations without continuous fatalities.

The extensive fish mortality is expected to have significant and far-reaching consequences for the small-scale fishing industry. Professor Strydom warned that the die-off will lead to starvation among marine life such as penguins and advised the government to prepare for adjustments in pilchard harvesting quotas to safeguard the industry's future. Commercially canned sardines have been confirmed as safe to eat, despite the virus detection in wild populations.

Why it mattersThe massive fish die-off threatens the livelihoods of small-scale fishers and could impact the broader fishing industry's sustainability.

Salif Keïta Cancels South African Shows Amid Xenophobic Violence

The Malian musician cited 'xenophobic violence' as the reason for canceling all future performances in South Africa, disappointing festival organizers.

World-renowned Malian musician Salif Keïta has canceled all upcoming performances in South Africa due to what he described as "xenophobic violence." The decision has caused disappointment for a provincial festival that was scheduled to feature the artist next month. This action comes as South Africa experiences a surge in protests demanding the departure of undocumented migrants, with an unofficial deadline of September 30 set for their exit.

Recent months have seen tens of thousands of Africans voluntarily repatriated, with some reporting intimidation and attacks. The South African government states that individuals taking the law into their own hands are being arrested while authorities address illegal immigration. South Africa, the continent's wealthiest nation, has historically attracted migrants seeking economic opportunities, but undocumented immigration has become a significant political issue.

Protesters accuse undocumented migrants of straining public services and contributing to crime. Keïta stated that the xenophobia did not "match the South Africa I know." The country faces ongoing challenges related to illegal immigration, which has fueled public debate and protests, impacting both the perception of the nation and its cultural exchanges.

Why it mattersMalian musician Salif Keïta has canceled all future South African performances due to xenophobic violence, affecting cultural exchange and potentially future tourism.
