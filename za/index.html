Danny Jordaan fraud case delayed by High Court review

The R1 million fraud and theft case against SAFA president Danny Jordaan and co-accused is postponed to November 27, 2026, pending a High Court decision on a review application.

The fraud case involving South African Football Association (SAFA) president Danny Jordaan and four co-accused has been postponed to November 27, 2026. The Specialised Commercial Crimes Court in Palm Ridge delayed the proceedings to allow the High Court to rule on a review application. This application challenges the dismissal of an earlier attempt to investigate alleged delays in the criminal proceedings under Section 342A of the Criminal Procedure Act.

State prosecutors allege that Jordaan unlawfully signed and backdated a service agreement with Grit Communications in December 2017, without following SAFA’s approval processes, to protect his personal image. He is also accused of procuring security services for his personal benefit, presenting them as SAFA business operations. Co-accused, including former SAFA CFO Grown Hluyo and former acting CEO Russell Paul, face charges of fraud, theft, and conspiracy related to payments and agreements allegedly made without proper authorization.

The National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) stated its readiness to proceed with the case, awaiting the High Court's decision. "The NPA reiterates its readiness to proceed with the case and will await the outcome of the High Court review application before the matter can proceed further," said NPA communications officer Magaboke Mohlatlole. The State remains committed to finalizing the case according to the law.

Why it mattersInvestors may be affected by the prolonged legal proceedings and potential implications for the governance and financial dealings of SAFA, a significant sporting body.

Police Official Receives Fifth Arrest Warrant in Blue Light Scandal

Suspended Ekurhuleni Metro Police Department Deputy Chief Julius Mkhwanazi faces fraud and corruption charges related to the illegal fitting of blue lights on private vehicles.

Suspended Ekurhuleni Metro Police Department Deputy Chief Julius Mkhwanazi was served with his fifth arrest warrant on Thursday, August 27, 2026, following his bail hearing for the Emmanuel Mbense murder case. The warrant relates to the ongoing "blue lights saga," which involves allegations of fraud and corruption.

Mkhwanazi is accused of unlawfully approving the installation of blue emergency lights on four private luxury vehicles owned by businessman Vusimuzi 'Cat' Matlala's security company, Cat Protection Services. These vehicles, including two BMWs, a Mercedes-Benz, and a Volkswagen, were allegedly registered as City of Ekurhuleni assets, a matter that has been under scrutiny at the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry.

The Independent Police Investigative Directorate (IPID) arrested two suspects, an EMPD official and a private citizen, on Thursday in connection with the case. They are scheduled to appear at the Germiston Magistrate Court on August 28, 2026, facing fraud and corruption charges. A third EMPD official implicated in the matter was expected to be arrested later that night.

Why it mattersThe arrest of a senior police official and the ongoing investigation into the misuse of official assets affects public trust and the administration of law enforcement.

Creators Seek More Power in Digital Economy

South African content creators are building businesses and attracting audiences, but are pushing for greater economic control beyond brand deals and platform monetization.

South Africa's creator economy is expanding, fostering new businesses and opportunities for young entrepreneurs. However, a key issue emerging is the distribution of revenue generated from digital audiences, as creators seek a larger share of the economic value they help create.

Brands are increasingly leveraging creators to connect with consumers, while creators themselves are diversifying into advertising, podcasts, and events. Weza Matomane, director of the South African Social Media Awards, notes that creators are becoming akin to new broadcasters due to their audience reach and cultural influence.

Matomane emphasizes the need for creators to organize and present a unified voice when negotiating with major technology companies like TikTok and Meta. He argues that individual approaches yield little change, whereas collective action can lead to policy adjustments and greater negotiation power within the industry.

Why it mattersCreators are affected by the need to organize and negotiate with platforms for better revenue sharing and policy changes, impacting their business models.

Safa delays Bafana Bafana coach choice as Afcon qualifiers approach

The South African Football Association has yet to name a successor for Hugo Broos, with the national team's 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers set to begin in September.

The South African Football Association (Safa) faces a critical decision as the September 23 start date for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers nears, with no permanent coach appointed for the Bafana Bafana. The federation is reportedly in negotiations with Pitso Mosimane to replace Hugo Broos, who departed after a five-year tenure. Broos, citing family reasons and the demands of coaching at his age, had indicated his departure was likely after qualifying the team for their first World Cup in 16 years.

Safa's technical committee chairperson, Jack Maluleke, confirmed in May that the search for Broos's successor had begun, stating the process was open to all interested candidates, including Broos himself if he wished to continue. However, reports in July suggested Safa had attempted to persuade Broos to extend his contract for another year. The team is scheduled to play Guinea and Eritrea on September 23 and 27, respectively, followed by a match against Kenya in November.

The uncertainty surrounding the coaching position comes at a crucial time for the national team as they prepare to compete in the 2027 Afcon, which will be co-hosted by Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania. The lack of a formally appointed coach could impact team preparation and strategy leading into these important qualification matches.

Why it mattersThe delay in appointing a new coach affects the Bafana Bafana team's preparation and strategy for upcoming Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers.

Teeks' South African Tour Connects Indigenous Roots to Soul Music Origins

Māori soul singer TEEKS draws parallels between his heritage and the African roots of soul music during his debut South African performances.

Māori soul singer TEEKS is performing in South Africa for the first time this week, exploring the connections between his indigenous heritage and the African origins of soul music. The artist, whose real name is Te Karehana Gardiner-Toi, views live performance as a sacred space for healing and reciprocity, a concept he feels resonates with South Africa's rich cultural tapestry.

TEEKS believes his Māori heritage, which emphasizes lineage and self-awareness, informs his approach to music and navigating the global industry. He acknowledges the profound influence of African culture and black culture on global music genres, including soul, stating that the world has benefited from these origins. This understanding fuels his anticipation to discover synergies within South Africa's diverse cultural landscape.

His introspective soul music, including tracks like 'First Time' and 'Remember Me,' has already gained significant traction in South Africa. TEEKS suggests this resonance stems from listeners connecting with the essence and origins of soul music, acknowledging the foundational role of Africa and black culture in shaping popular music worldwide.

Why it mattersSouth African listeners are connecting with TEEKS' music because it acknowledges the African origins of soul music, resonating with their cultural heritage.
