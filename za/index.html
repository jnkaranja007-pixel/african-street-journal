Springboks beat All Blacks to level series

Cheslin Kolbe scored 18 points as the Springboks defeated the All Blacks 33-16 in Cape Town to tie the Rugby's Greatest Rival series.

The Springboks secured a 33-16 victory over the All Blacks at DHL Stadium in Cape Town on Saturday, leveling the Rugby's Greatest Rival series. Jesse Kriel opened the scoring in the second minute, and the Springboks built a ten-point lead despite early defensive concerns. The team responded to the All Blacks' physicality and illegal tactics, maintaining their advantage through halftime.

Despite an injury to fullback Damian Willemse just before the break, which saw him replaced by Manie Libbok, the Springboks maintained their momentum. Libbok contributed effectively on attack and defense in the second half. The win came in front of a boisterous home crowd, turning the tables on the All Blacks after a previous loss.

The match saw the Springboks outlast the All Blacks in an enthralling contest. Cheslin Kolbe's kicking was instrumental in guiding the team to the win. The series is now tied at 1-1, with the Springboks demonstrating resilience and power to secure the result.

Why it mattersThe Springboks' victory over the All Blacks in Cape Town levels the series, impacting national pride and the competitive landscape of rugby.

News · 2026-08-30

Police report murder decline amid focus on farm attacks

South Africa's murder rate dropped for a consecutive quarter, but farm attacks remain a concern following six killings.

South African Police Service (SAPS) released its first-quarter crime statistics for the 2026/27 financial year, covering April to June 2026. The figures show a continued decrease in the national murder rate, marking another quarter of decline. However, the statistics also highlight six murders linked to attacks in farming communities during the previous quarter, including farm owners, employees, a manager, and a dweller. These figures emerge amid ongoing concerns about violence in rural areas and overall crime levels.

Acting Police Minister Firoz Cachalia confirmed the overall murder decline, noting decreases in carjackings, residential and business robberies, and cash-in-transit heists. He attributed a significant portion of murders and assaults to alcohol consumption and trivial arguments, stating that many incidents involve ordinary individuals under the influence rather than organized criminals. Cachalia emphasized the need for stronger enforcement of liquor licensing laws and municipal by-laws to address this issue.

In response to crime patterns, SAPS will allocate additional resources to 140 high-crime police station precincts nationwide, providing targeted operational support and improved resource allocation. The service also plans to strengthen detective services and crime intelligence while intensifying operations against extortion, illegal mining, organized crime, and gang violence. Despite the overall crime reduction, police flagged increases in crimes affecting women and children, particularly gender-based violence and femicide, which require urgent attention.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected by the six murders in farming communities, while the police's focus on crime hotspots and specific crime types may indirectly impact rural safety.

Budget Fails Women as GBVF Remains Unfunded

Despite being declared a national disaster, gender-based violence and femicide initiatives received no new funding in the 2026 budget.

South Africa's health facilities are crucial for identifying and treating violence, yet the government has failed to allocate new funding for gender-based violence and femicide (GBVF) initiatives. Although GBVF was classified as a national disaster in November 2025, the 2026 budget provided no dedicated financial support. This lack of investment critically impacts women, particularly those in poor and rural communities who rely heavily on public healthcare for essential services and support after experiencing violence.

Women constitute a significant portion of the healthcare workforce, making up nearly 90% of nurses and up to 96% of community health workers. These women are on the frontlines of identifying violence, caring for survivors, and delivering the broader GBVF response. Investing in health therefore not only strengthens women's livelihoods and working conditions but also enables them to provide dignified and timely care to survivors, highlighting the dual role women play in both receiving and delivering care.

While policy commitments exist, including the National Strategic Plan on GBVF and various health policies promising survivor-centred care, their effectiveness is undermined by a lack of resources. The budget is presented as a vital point for change, capable of equipping the health system to address GBVF or perpetuating inequalities that leave marginalized women without support. Without matching healthcare investment, classifying GBVF as a national disaster fails to protect women.

Why it mattersWomen are affected because the lack of healthcare investment means that survivors of gender-based violence and femicide may not receive timely and dignified care.

Business · 2026-08-30

Cape Town Wins Top African Travel Awards

The city secured two major accolades at the World Travel Awards Africa Gala Ceremony for its leisure and business tourism offerings.

Cape Town has been awarded Africa’s Leading City Destination and Africa’s Leading Meetings & Conference Destination at the World Travel Awards Africa Gala Ceremony held in Zanzibar. These awards recognize the city's appeal for both leisure travelers, citing attractions like Table Mountain and its culinary scene, and for business events, highlighting its infrastructure. The wins were announced on Thursday, August 27.

The dual victory is significant for Cape Town's tourism sector, according to Enver Duminy, CEO of Cape Town Tourism. He stated that winning both awards sends a strong message about the city's diverse appeal. Duminy also noted that business delegates often extend their stays, potentially becoming tourists or even investors, further boosting the local economy.

These accolades coincide with a notable increase in travel between Cape Town and the rest of Africa. Arrivals at Cape Town International Airport from the continent rose by 17% in 2025, marking it as the fastest-growing visitor segment. Foreign visitor spending also saw an 18% increase during the same period, underscoring Cape Town's growing role as a continental hub.

Why it mattersCape Town's recognition as a leading city and conference destination directly impacts its tourism industry and business event infrastructure.

Business · 2026-08-30

Grant Debate Ignites Over Inflation's Impact on Pensioners

Pensioners argue that official inflation figures do not reflect their actual cost of living, despite grant increases.

A recent increase in the Older Persons Grant, from R2 320 to R2 400, has sparked debate as recipients claim the rise does not adequately address rising living costs. Patrick Maloka voiced frustration on social media, stating that the grant should be adjusted for inflation to provide a "reasonable grant." While the government described the increase as slightly above current inflation, grant recipients argue that the cost of essential goods like food, electricity, and transport has outpaced official inflation rates.

This sentiment is echoed by researchers studying the Child Support Grant, who have noted a loss of purchasing power despite annual increases. Advocates for pensioners contend that the Older Persons Grant faces a similar erosion of real value. Maloka's subsequent post, "RISE POSA," has led to speculation about a potential pensioner advocacy movement, though its exact nature remains unconfirmed.

The complaint that grant adjustments fail to keep pace with living expenses is a recurring theme during each grant cycle. Despite official figures indicating percentage increases technically outpacing inflation, the lived experience of grant recipients suggests otherwise. Whether "RISE POSA" signifies an organized campaign or individual frustration, it reflects a widespread feeling of relief on paper but not in practical financial terms for many.

Why it mattersPensioners are affected by the grant increase not keeping pace with their actual cost of living, diminishing their purchasing power.
