foreign national beaten to death in Durban

Police are investigating the murder of a man assaulted by about 50 people on Dr Pixley kaSeme Street.

Police in KwaZulu-Natal have opened a murder case after a foreign national was allegedly beaten to death in Durban CBD on Thursday, 22 October 2026. The man, believed to be in his late 30s or early 40s, was allegedly assaulted by a group of about 50 people and was declared dead at the scene.

Information suggests a group of people was marching along Dr Pixley kaSeme Street when two males broke away and approached the victim. CCTV footage showed the pair speaking to the man before he was later found lying on the ground with swelling at the back of his head. The motive for the killing is unknown, and police are following several leads.

Investigators are working to trace those responsible for the assault, which occurred on Dr Pixley kaSeme Street. Police have urged community members not to spread unverified information as the investigation continues.

Proteas beat Australia by an innings to level Test series

South Africa's bowlers secured an innings and 31-run victory over Australia in Gqeberha on Thursday, levelling the three-match series at 1-1.

South Africa defeated Australia by an innings and 31 runs on Thursday to level the Test series at 1-1. The Proteas secured the victory on the fifth day at St George’s Park in Gqeberha after bowling Australia out for 295 in their second innings. Fast bowler Kagiso Rabada took 4 wickets for 77 runs, and all-rounder Wiaan Mulder took 3 wickets for 64 runs to lead the Proteas attack.

Australia resumed their second innings on 156/4, needing 170 runs to make the Proteas bat again. Travis Head scored 121 runs off 190 balls before being dismissed by Mulder. Nightwatchman Scott Boland added 40 runs off 166 balls, but the rest of Australia's tail collapsed. Proteas captain Temba Bavuma said his team made Australia work hard for the six wickets taken on the final day.

The series is now tied at 1-1 heading into the deciding Test match, which starts in Cape Town on Tuesday. Australia captain Pat Cummins admitted the team needs to improve significantly after the heavy loss, with questions arising over the form of several players. Travis Head's 121 and Boland's 40 were the only significant contributions in Australia's second innings.

Why it mattersSouth African cricket fans will be able to watch the deciding Test match in Cape Town.

Addo Elephant National Park gets R136.5 million upgrade

New accommodation, a spa, and a solar plant are among upgrades at the Eastern Cape park ahead of the holiday season.

Addo Elephant National Park has received a R136.5 million investment from SANParks to improve tourism infrastructure and visitor facilities. The upgrades, which aim to enhance operational sustainability, include eight new family accommodation units that are now open. Minister David Maynier visited the park on Monday, October 19, to inspect the ongoing construction projects.

Several new facilities are expected to be completed in time for the December holiday season. These include additional accommodation units, a refurbished guest house, and a swimming pool. A new spa is already operational, and a 10-unit Community Lodge is scheduled for completion by December 2026, with operations beginning in January 2027.

A 705-kW solar plant is also nearing completion and will be commissioned in January 2027 to reduce the park's reliance on conventional electricity. Road maintenance is underway, and upgrades to ablutions and hiking trails will be prioritised. These improvements come after years of concerns about the condition of the park's basic infrastructure.

Why it mattersVisitors to Addo Elephant National Park will experience improved accommodation and facilities during the December holidays.

South Africa's Hyron Andrews died of natural causes

An autopsy revealed the 31-year-old Lions player suffered a fatal tear in his aorta.

Hyron Andrews, a 31-year-old player for the Johannesburg-based Lions, died of natural causes. An autopsy found he suffered a tear in his aorta, the main artery carrying blood from the heart, which led to blood accumulating around his heart.

Andrews was found unconscious in a hotel shower in France before a Champions Cup match. Investigators initially considered a fall or heart attack but the autopsy confirmed an aneurysm, a rupture of the bulging blood vessel, as the cause. His body has not yet been returned to South Africa.

Andrews' wife, Maighlin, has requested privacy as the repatriation process begins. Lions assistant coach Julian Redelinghuys stated the team is supporting players through the unexpected death that occurred just before a game.

Why it mattersHyron Andrews, a player for the South African team the Lions, died while on tour in France.

Tshwane supplied unsafe water for three weeks

City of Tshwane supplied unchlorinated water to Bronkhorstspruit residents, leading to 408 gastrointestinal illness cases.

The City of Tshwane supplied unchlorinated drinking water to Bronkhorstspruit residents for three weeks, from September 24 to October 14, without informing them of the risk. Between October 7 and 18, 408 cases of gastrointestinal illness were reported, with most affected being children. Mayor Nasiphi Moya admitted negligence, stating the city ran out of chlorine due to a supplier dispute that quadrupled prices.

Moya said officials failed to inform city leadership about the chlorine shortage. The city's failure to warn residents about potentially unsafe water is inexcusable, as access to safe drinking water is a constitutional right. The priority in Bronkhorstspruit is to ensure residents receive safe water and to establish the full circumstances of the outbreak.

This incident follows a 2023 cholera outbreak in Hammanskraal, which exposed a long-running water and sanitation crisis and resulted in 47 national deaths. With local government elections on November 4, voters can judge municipalities on their service delivery, including the provision of safe water.

Why it mattersResidents of Bronkhorstspruit were exposed to unsafe drinking water and subsequently suffered gastrointestinal illnesses due to municipal failure.
