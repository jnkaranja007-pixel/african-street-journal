Experts warn South Africa's ageing water system leaves it exposed to El Niño drought

Dam levels are higher than before the 2015/16 drought, but water specialists say leaks, failing municipal supply and rising food prices leave little room for complacency.

South Africa is better placed to withstand a possible El Niño-induced drought than it was before the 2015/16 crisis, with dam levels currently relatively high. Water experts, however, warned at a WaterCAN webinar on Wednesday that ageing infrastructure, failures in municipal water delivery and vulnerability to food price rises leave little room for complacency.

The webinar, titled Is South Africa ready for El Niño?, looked at what a prolonged dry spell could mean for water supplies and food prices, and what government should do to prepare. Anja du Plessis, an associate professor and water management expert at Unisa, called El Niño "a stress test that we can see coming", saying the higher dam levels give time that is only useful if it is spent fixing leaks, building buffers and deciding early.

Having water in dams does not guarantee it reaches households, she said. Du Plessis identified three pressures that could emerge during El Niño: reduced inflows into dams, increased evaporation as temperatures rise, and higher demand for water.

Why it mattersHouseholds depend on municipal systems that have already failed to deliver water, so high dam levels alone will not protect their taps during a dry spell.

Microsoft outage disrupts South African services

Email, Teams, and Copilot services were affected by a global technical issue on Thursday.

Microsoft apologised on Friday for a global outage that disrupted services in South Africa and 74 other countries. The company stated the disruption, which began on Thursday, was caused by a technical issue related to a network capacity upgrade, ruling out malicious cyber or AI activity. Services affected included Outlook email, Microsoft Teams, and the AI assistant Copilot.

In South Africa, the disruption hit businesses, municipalities, and universities, with the South African Revenue Service (SARS) warning taxpayers of potential access issues to Microsoft 365 applications. SARS confirmed its technical teams were engaging with Microsoft to resolve the problem, which stemmed from an overwhelmed process responsible for secure connections on affected servers. This issue was compounded by some servers becoming unexpectedly unavailable.

Microsoft reported a broad recovery and stability across its services on Friday, though full restoration was ongoing. The company has been working to restart affected servers and implement configuration changes. The outage highlighted the dependence of businesses and public services on Microsoft's infrastructure, prompting calls for national reviews of IT risk, particularly for small businesses that may lack workarounds for such failures.

Why it mattersSouth African businesses and government services experienced disruptions to essential communication and productivity tools due to a global Microsoft outage.

Security · 2026-11-14

Greytown police commander killed in shootout

At least 10 suspects fled after a business robbery, leading to a chase and gun battle that left one officer dead and another wounded.

KwaZulu-Natal police are hunting for at least 10 suspects who shot and killed Greytown station commander Lieutenant Colonel TG Sithole on Thursday afternoon. The officer died during a shootout after responding to a business robbery in progress where the shop owner was shot and employees assaulted.

A sergeant was wounded in the arm during the exchange of gunfire, while two suspects were fatally wounded and one arrested with injuries. The remaining suspects fled into the mountains in a Toyota Quantum. Police recovered two rifles and a handgun from the scene.

Acting provincial commissioner Major General Phumelele Makoba vowed that police would use all available resources to bring the suspects to book, stating that an attack on police officers is an attack on the state. KZN Premier Thami Ntuli praised Sithole's bravery in confronting armed criminals.

Why it mattersPolice are hunting for suspects who killed a station commander and wounded another officer during a robbery response, with police vowing to use all resources to find them.

Security · 2026-11-14

Four men accused over Donovan Marx farm attack drop bail bid

The four accused were arrested after the cattle farmer was shot, stabbed and had both ears cut off on his farm near Ventersdorp.

Four men accused of attacking North West farmer Donovan Marx abandoned their bail application at the Ventersdorp Regional Court on Friday, 13 November. The National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) said the state had been ready to proceed.

The accused are Phindile Nomngcayi, 30; Thomas Motlhangtlhong, 27; Mothoka Mathipa, 56; and Mokhema Maishane, 53. They face charges including robbery with aggravating circumstances, attempted murder and unlawful possession of firearms and ammunition. The NPA said one other person had been withdrawn as an accused after evidence linking him was found insufficient, and he has offered to testify for the state.

The case was postponed to 15 February 2027 for further investigations, and the accused remain in custody. Marx, 50, was discharged from Mediclinic Potchefstroom on Thursday, 12 November, 16 days after the attack on his farm on 28 October.

Why it mattersThe accused stay in custody until at least 15 February 2027, so the farming community near Ventersdorp must wait nearly three months for the case to move forward.

Politics · 2026-11-14

Santos urges South Africa to defend principles in US dealings

Former Colombian president Juan Manuel Santos says economic interests cannot be the only consideration in relations between countries.

Former Colombian president Juan Manuel Santos said South Africa should defend its principles and values in its dealings with the United States, arguing that economic interests cannot be the only consideration in relations between countries. He made the case at the 24th Nelson Mandela Annual Lecture in Cape Town on 6 November.

In the lecture, Santos urged nations to defend human dignity and uphold international law. He warned that political and economic interests could make governments reluctant to confront injustice. Speaking to the Mail & Guardian afterwards, he said: "For me, the most important thing in life is to respect the dignity of others and to defend my dignity."

The theme, "When is silence complicity?", drew on Santos's experience of Colombia's decades-long armed conflict and his government's negotiations with the Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia (FARC). The Nelson Mandela Foundation hosted the event with One Young World.

Why it mattersGovernments weighing trade ties against human rights concerns are now being urged to put principles first in their dealings with partners such as the United States.
