Minister Urges All Blacks Fans to Support Springboks

Sport Minister Gayton McKenzie appealed to South Africans to switch allegiance from the All Blacks to the Springboks during a rugby rivalry series.

Sport, Arts and Culture Minister Gayton McKenzie has urged South African All Blacks supporters to trade black for green and gold and back the Springboks. McKenzie made the appeal at the recent 19th annual SA Sports Awards at Sun City. The appeal comes as the Springboks and All Blacks are engaged in their first dedicated four-Test series in 30 years, with the Springboks having leveled the series at 1-1 after two matches. The third Test is scheduled for September 5.

McKenzie, who admitted to previously supporting the All Blacks himself, cited the historical context of some South Africans backing New Zealand during apartheid due to associations with segregated sport. He highlighted the modern Springboks, particularly captain Siya Kolisi, as representing a new South Africa and urged supporters to embrace national pride. McKenzie's own past support for the All Blacks, notably during the 2015 Rugby World Cup, was also mentioned.

The minister's remarks, captured on video and circulated on TikTok, come at a critical juncture in the century-old rivalry. New Zealand leads the overall Test record with 64 wins from 112 matches, while South Africa has secured 44 victories. McKenzie's call aims to unify national support behind the Springboks as they continue their battle against the All Blacks in the ongoing series.

Why it mattersSouth African rugby fans who previously supported the All Blacks are being asked to switch their allegiance to the national Springbok team.

SARB Releases R2 Coins Honoring Maxeke and 1956 Women's March

The South African Reserve Bank has issued two new R2 circulation coins, marking the first time women are featured on commemorative currency.

The South African Reserve Bank has launched two commemorative R2 circulation coins, one honoring Charlotte Maxeke and the other the women of the 1956 Women’s March. These coins, intended for everyday use, are the first South African commemorative circulation coins to feature women. They were launched at the SARB’s head office in Pretoria.

One coin marks 125 years since Maxeke became the first black South African woman to earn a university degree. The second coin commemorates 70 years since more than twenty thousand women marched to the Union Buildings to protest apartheid pass laws. Both coins remain legal tender at their face value of R2.

SARB governor Lesetja Kganyago stated that currency can shape national memory and public meaning, reflecting whom a nation represents and what it remembers. The coins are part of the Milestones of Freedom program and aim to address the historical overlooking of women’s contributions to the country.

Why it mattersSouth Africans will use R2 coins featuring Charlotte Maxeke and the 1956 Women's March, recognizing their historical significance in daily transactions.

Hawks Officer Testifies on Marimuthu Visits and Mchunu Pressure

A KwaZulu-Natal Hawks tracing team head told the Madlanga Commission he visited Timothy Marimuthu, a businessman with a criminal past, and faced pressure from a minister.

Deenadayalan Govender, who leads a Hawks tracing team in KwaZulu-Natal, testified before the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry that he took colleagues to visit Timothy Marimuthu. Marimuthu is a politically connected figure with a past conviction for Mandrax dealing and has been implicated in various scandals. Govender stated he knew Marimuthu was involved in industries like the taxi sector and that they had visited each other's homes, though he denied socializing extensively with him.

Govender also testified that suspended police minister Senzo Mchunu had pressured him to implicate provincial police commissioner Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi in over 170 police shootings. This testimony comes as Govender himself has been portrayed by other witnesses as part of a rogue police team involved in criminality, while he claims to be a pawn in a larger scheme and alleges a fellow policeman is targeting him.

This is not the first time Govender has appeared before the Madlanga Commission. His previous appearances have seen him present himself as a victim of a "dirty game" orchestrated by others within the police force. He identified Sergeant Thomas, nephew of the State Security Agency's Foreign Branch head, as someone he believes is targeting him.

Why it mattersThe testimony of a Hawks officer before the Madlanga Commission reveals potential corruption and political interference within the South African police force, affecting public trust and the administration of justice.

Cape Town Desalination Plant Delayed by Port Expansion Plans

A critical desalination plant component of the city's New Water Programme is now expected to be operational five years later than planned.

The City of Cape Town's plan for a permanent desalination plant at Paarden Eiland has been halted due to Transnet's port expansion intentions. This delay, coupled with the need to find a new site and conduct environmental assessments, pushes the project back by five years. The city's New Water Programme, which includes the desalination plant, is now not expected to be fully operational until 2036, while water demand is projected to surpass current dam allocations within two years.

Water and sanitation mayco member Zahid Badroodien announced the search for alternative locations on August 21, presenting the latest Water Outlook report. Current dam levels stand at 77%, indicating an "early drought caution stage" for this time of year. Except for May's floods, winter rainfall was notably low, and a hot summer is anticipated.

The delay in the desalination project, a key element in securing future water supply, raises concerns as demand continues to rise. The original site had completed its scoping phase for environmental impact assessments, and finding a new suitable location will add further time and potential cost implications to the overall New Water Programme.

Why it mattersThe delay in the desalination plant construction affects Cape Town residents by potentially exacerbating water shortages as demand outstrips supply.
