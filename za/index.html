ANC leads local election results amid resignation rumours

The African National Congress secured 30% of the vote in the 2026 local government elections, with President Ramaphosa reportedly offering to resign.

The African National Congress (ANC) won the 2026 local government elections with 30% of the national vote, a significant drop from its 2021 share. President Cyril Ramaphosa was reportedly willing to resign following the party's electoral performance. The Electoral Commission of South Africa (IEC) announced the final results on Sunday, 8 November 2026, at the Gallagher Convention Centre in Midrand.

Sources within the ANC suggest that even President Ramaphosa offered to step down after the party's vote share fell from 45.59% in 2021 to approximately 30.81% in the recent elections. The IEC declared the results, which will lead to coalition talks for governing municipalities across the country.

Meanwhile, residents in Bronkhorstspruit and surrounding areas of Tshwane face continued water restrictions as the municipality addresses supply and quality issues following a gastroenteritis outbreak. The City of Tshwane has not yet provided a timeline for the resumption of normal water services.

Why it mattersSouth African voters will be affected by the election results through potential changes in municipal service delivery, including water supply, and by the political stability of the ruling party.

Police officer filmed striking pizza driver

A South African Police Service member is under investigation after a video showed him striking a motorcycle delivery driver in Rustenburg.

A police officer in Rustenburg, North West, is being investigated after a video surfaced showing him striking a pizza delivery driver on Saturday, November 7, 2026. The incident occurred in Tlhabane and was filmed by motorists. The officer, in civilian clothes but using a state vehicle, approached the delivery driver and struck him multiple times.

North West police commissioner Arthur Peter Adams called the altercation “deeply concerning” and stated the officer’s actions were against Saps expectations. A criminal case for assault and crimen injuria has been opened by the 33-year-old delivery driver. Adams confirmed that appropriate steps would be taken if the investigation finds misconduct.

The police are seeking additional information from the public regarding the circumstances of the assault. The investigation aims to determine if the officer acted outside the law or violated the code of conduct.

Why it mattersA 33-year-old pizza delivery driver faces assault charges against a police officer in Rustenburg.

UDM councillor shot dead in Gqeberha

Luxolo Namette, a former deputy mayor, was killed days after the 2026 local government elections.

United Democratic Movement (UDM) councillor Luxolo Namette was shot and killed in Motherwell, Gqeberha, on Saturday afternoon. The incident occurred just days after the 2026 local government elections, which concluded on Wednesday, November 4.

UDM secretary-general Bulelani Bobotyane stated that the killing has cast a dark shadow over the party's election participation and expressed concern that it may have been politically motivated. Namette, a former deputy mayor of the Nelson Mandela Bay Municipality, was described as jovial, quick-witted, and intelligent. The UDM plans to escalate the matter to the Political Killings Task Team (PKTT).

The UDM expects the South African Police Service (SAPS) to thoroughly investigate the motive behind the killing and bring those responsible to justice. The party extended its condolences to Namette's wife, family, friends, and fellow UDM members, emphasizing that political differences should not be resolved through violence.

Why it mattersThe killing of UDM councillor Luxolo Namette raises concerns about political violence and the safety of public representatives in South Africa.

Cape Town flats auction from R600 000

Three 2-bedroom properties in Kenilworth and Lansdowne are available with reserve prices starting at R600 000.

Three 2-bedroom flats in Cape Town suburbs Kenilworth and Lansdowne are up for auction with reserve prices starting at R600 000. These properties are located about 20 minutes from the city centre. One flat in Lansdowne has a R700 000 reserve and R12 567 in levy arrears, having last sold for R1.29 million in 2021.

A 57-square-metre flat in Kenilworth on Punters Way has a R735 000 reserve and includes R44 172 in levy debt. The City of Cape Town’s General Valuation Roll places this Kenilworth flat at R1.32 million, meaning the auction price would only cover 57% of its market value. Buyers must pay the bid price plus fees and commission within days of the auction.

Average sale prices for sectional titles in Lansdowne have decreased by 1.57% since the start of the year, reaching R935 000 by October. In contrast, Kenilworth sectional titles have increased by 12.43% year-on-year. Buyers with access to bridging loans could leverage these auction bargains as the first signs of an economic downturn become apparent.

Why it mattersBuyers seeking affordable family units in Cape Town may find opportunities at auction, but must be aware of additional debts and the quick payment required.

South Africa parties urged to pass coalition bill

Parliament must approve the Municipal Structures Amendment Bill to bring order to hung councils after the 2026 elections.

South African parties in the Government of National Unity (GNU) are urged to unite and pass the proposed Municipal Structures Amendment Bill, also known as the coalition bill. This legislation aims to establish order, improve governance, and create more stable coalitions in municipalities. The bill was intended for adoption before the November 4, 2026 elections but remains unfinalised due to disagreements.

The GNU partners have an opportunity to collectively navigate this significant legislation through Parliament, marking their first joint bill. Following the November 4, 2026 elections, South Africa anticipates a record number of 'hung councils' where no single party holds a majority, necessitating the formation of municipal coalitions without a pre-existing governance framework.

Past municipal governance has been marked by instability, frequent ousting of mayors, conflicts between managers and councils, and small parties holding coalitions hostage. The coalition bill is considered critical for fostering accountable, functional, and stable municipalities.

Why it mattersSouth African citizens will be directly affected by the stability and functionality of their local governments, which are currently operating without a coalition governance framework.
