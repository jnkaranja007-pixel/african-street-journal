Matlala testifies at Madlanga Commission

Vusimuzi Matlala appeared at the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry in Pretoria on November 9, 2026.

Vusimuzi Matlala appeared at the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry in Pretoria on November 9, 2026. President Cyril Ramaphosa established the commission to investigate allegations that South Africa’s criminal justice system is compromised.

Matlala's testimony challenged suspended Idac investigator Dylan Perumal's investigation and revived claims of threats by Shadrack Sibiya. His lawyer, Annelene van den Heever, accused the NPA of violating rules by providing an unsigned statement from Matlala’s collapsed plea deal.

The commission dismissed efforts to block the evidence. The inquiry is investigating allegations made on July 6, 2025, by KwaZulu-Natal Police Commissioner Lieutenant-General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi.

Why it mattersVusimuzi Matlala testified at the Madlanga Commission of Inquiry in Pretoria regarding allegations about the criminal justice system.

Nine Zimbabweans arrested for alleged Russia-Ukraine war plan

Five appeared in Kempton Park and four in Johannesburg courts, facing charges under the Regulation of Foreign Military Assistance Act.

Nine Zimbabwean nationals appeared in two separate Gauteng courts after being arrested for allegedly attempting to travel from South Africa to participate in the Russia-Ukraine war. Five of the accused, including Weight Mudhanyane, 23, and Javangwe Munyaradzi, 41, appeared before the Kempton Park Magistrates’ Court after their arrest at OR Tambo International Airport on Sunday night. The other four, Bobo Chinhondo, 43, and Liberty Shereni, 44, appeared before the Johannesburg Magistrate’s Court following their arrest at a hotel in Bruma.

All nine are charged with contravening the Regulation of Foreign Military Assistance Act 15 of 1998, following an intelligence-driven operation by the Hawks, Interpol, and international law enforcement agencies. The National Prosecuting Authority stated its commitment to upholding the law, with spokesperson Magaboke Mohlatlole emphasizing that the accused are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

The Kempton Park matter was postponed to November 10 for legal representation, while the Johannesburg case was postponed to November 12 for the same reason. All nine accused remain in custody.

Why it mattersNine Zimbabwean citizens were arrested in South Africa for allegedly planning to join the Russia-Ukraine war.

ANC vote share drops to 30.76% in 2026 local elections

The ruling party lost over 1.5 million votes compared to the 2021 municipal elections, facing coalition challenges.

The African National Congress (ANC) secured 30.76% of the national vote in the 2026 Local Government Elections, its lowest share since 1994. The party received 9 160 691 votes, a decrease of more than 1.5 million from the 10 699 300 votes in 2021. This result places President Cyril Ramaphosa and the party leadership under pressure regarding their political future and ability to govern.

The Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) confirmed that 127 councils are now hung, meaning no single party achieved an outright majority. The ANC, despite remaining the largest party nationally, will need to negotiate coalition agreements to govern many municipalities. The Democratic Alliance (DA) increased its vote share to 24.64%, and the new MK Party secured 9.76% of the vote.

Coalition politics will shape municipal administrations over the next five years, with parties like the DA, MK Party, and ActionSA playing significant roles. The ANC's support has declined across successive elections since 1994, with the 2021 local elections marking the first time it fell below 50% nationally.

Why it mattersThe ANC's declining support and the rise of coalition politics directly affect how South Africa's municipalities will be governed.

Business · 2026-11-10

South Africa apple exports to China surge 50%

Zero tariffs on South African fruit entering China have boosted Tru-Cape’s exports by almost half this year.

South African apple exports to China have risen by nearly 50% this year following the removal of import tariffs. Tru-Cape Fruit Marketing, which handles over 20% of the country's apple exports to China, is experiencing this growth. The change means South African fruit now faces a 0% import tariff in China, a reduction from the previous 10% rate applied until May 1, 2026.

Conrad Fick, marketing director at Tru-Cape, stated that the playing field is now level for all Southern Hemisphere countries exporting to China. He believes this tariff reduction offers a significant opportunity to increase market share in Chinese retail. Specific apple varieties like Royal Gala Red and Royal Beaut are performing well, alongside growing acceptance of the Flash Gala brand, which is contributing to higher volumes and prices. Fick also noted that China's large population presents opportunities for significant volumes, provided consumer preferences are met.

While the tariff reduction enhances competitiveness, Fick stressed that China remains a premium market where quality is essential. South African apples are gaining popularity in both retail and snacking markets. Although New Zealand has advantages due to its proximity and established market for larger apples, South Africa possesses other competitive strengths. The report also highlighted India as a market where more competitive access is needed.

Why it mattersSouth African apple exporters are seeing increased sales to China due to a tariff reduction, while facing challenges in accessing the Indian market.

South Africa school fees outpace inflation

Public primary and secondary school fees have risen significantly faster than consumer inflation over six years.

Public school fees in South Africa have increased at rates significantly higher than consumer inflation, making education harder to afford. Primary school fees rose by an average of 6.5% annually and secondary school fees by 7.1% over six years, outpacing a cumulative consumer inflation of about 36%. This rise means families pay more for education while the economy struggles to create jobs.

The Competition Commission reported these figures, showing that primary school fees increased by approximately 46% and secondary school fees by 51% over the period. This situation occurs as South Africa faces an unsustainably high youth unemployment rate. The economy, growing at around 1.1%, cannot generate enough opportunities for young people to secure financial stability after completing their education.

This trend contributes to a broader societal pressure, where the economic escalator for upward mobility has broken down. The rising cost of education, coupled with stagnant wages and limited job prospects, affects social stability. This is similar to pressures seen in Western Europe, though South Africa faces chronic service delivery protests and deep structural exclusion.

Why it mattersSouth African families are paying more for their children's education while facing a difficult job market.
