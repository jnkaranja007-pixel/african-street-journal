MK parliamentarian faces sanction over farm attack post

Andile Mngxitama's social media comments on a farm attack prompt calls for investigation by the SAHRC and parliament's ethics committee.

Political parties plan to have uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) Party parliamentarian Andile Mngxitama sanctioned for a social media post about a farm attack near Ventersdorp. Mngxitama, formerly of the Black First, Land First movement, posted his assessment of the attack on Donovan Marx, who was allegedly stabbed, shot, and had his ears severed on 28 October.

Mngxitama posted on X that attacking ears symbolised a refusal to listen to land issues and stated, "Farm life is overdetermined by systematic violence. Blacks got killed and fed to pigs." The post was later deleted, but screenshots circulated. The Freedom Front Plus (FF+) and the DA have stated they will ask the South African Human Rights Commission (SAHRC) to investigate hate speech violations.

FF+ parliamentarian Heloïse Denner said the party would also lodge a complaint with parliament’s ethics committee, calling Mngxitama's remarks "blatant hate speech" and stating he "does not belong in parliament." Police arrested five suspects aged between 26 and 56 in connection with the attack on Marx, seizing firearms and clothing. The suspects are due in court on Tuesday.

Jacob Zuma reaffirms ANC loyalty at MK party rally

Former president Jacob Zuma addressed thousands of supporters at the MK party's final election rally in Durban on Sunday.

Former president Jacob Zuma declared his lifelong loyalty to the ANC at the MK party’s final election rally in Durban on Sunday. The 84-year-old spoke to thousands of supporters at the Princess Magogo Stadium in KwaMashu, days after his hospital discharge.

Zuma launched a strong attack on the Government of National Unity (GNU), stating that its alliance with parties representing white interests showed South Africa was not yet free. He warned that the nation was “dying a second death” and vowed to fight the GNU until it ended.

The MK party leader expressed confidence in his party’s electoral chances, predicting substantial gains in local government. Zuma assured supporters that they should not fear, as the party is currently governing and will continue to do so.

Why it mattersFormer president Jacob Zuma's declaration of ANC loyalty while leading the breakaway MK party highlights a political paradox for voters.

Thunderstorms threaten South Africa special voting

Severe weather warnings issued for parts of Gauteng and Free State for November 2 and 3.

Special voting in South Africa is underway on November 2 and 3, but severe thunderstorms could disrupt electricity and communication services. The South African Weather Service (SAWS) issued a Yellow Level 1 warning for parts of Gauteng, the Free State, Mpumalanga, and the Eastern Cape, citing risks of localised flooding and property damage.

SAWS warned that lightning strikes and strong winds from the storms may damage electricity infrastructure and interrupt mobile network services. Over 2.6 million voters have applied for special voting, with some casting ballots at stations and others receiving home visits.

Voters are advised to monitor official weather updates from SAWS and announcements from the IEC. Residents in affected areas should also check municipal warnings and report any service outages to providers. The weather warning is subject to change as conditions evolve.

Why it mattersOver 2.6 million South Africans applying for special voting may face disruptions to electricity and communication services due to severe thunderstorms.

South African firm equips Zim school lab

Afribiz Invest partnered with Zodwa Mkandla Foundation to build a science facility at Sojini Secondary School.

South African company Afribiz Invest helped build a science laboratory at Sojini Secondary School in Mbembesi Village, Zimbabwe. The facility was handed over on Saturday, October 31, after a 14-day construction period. The project was a collaboration with the Zodwa Mkandla Foundation.

The laboratory can accommodate 60 learners and aims to provide more opportunities for practical experiments. Afribiz Invest, founded in South Africa in 2005, operates in several African markets, including Zimbabwe since 2013. Technical support for the lab came from Laboratory Equipment and Consumables (Private) Limited.

Businesswoman Zodwa Mkandla, an alumna of Sojini Secondary School, spearheaded the initiative to help local children pursue careers in science and medicine. The foundation also distributed food parcels, blankets, wheelchairs, and sanitary pads to vulnerable residents and schoolgirls.

Why it mattersSouth African company Afribiz Invest's investment in a rural Zimbabwean school laboratory directly impacts the education of Zimbabwean learners.

South Africa launches new insecticide for potato moths

Syngenta’s SILVALIS®, powered by PLINAZOLIN® technology, offers a new mode of action for crop protection.

Syngenta South Africa has launched SILVALIS®, the nation's first registered IRAC Group 30 insecticide, to combat the destructive potato tuber moth. This new product, powered by PLINAZOLIN® technology, introduces a novel mode of action to South African agriculture, providing potato growers with a new tool against a challenging pest.

The insecticide is registered for controlling the potato tuber moth (Phthorimaea operculella), which can cause up to 100% crop loss by damaging leaves, stems, and tubers in fields and storage. Sarike le Roux, Syngenta’s Head of Marketing, stated the product reflects the company's aim to turn science into practical breakthroughs for farmers.

SILVALIS® contains the active ingredient isocycloseram and was developed over the past 10 years. Chris Steyn, Syngenta’s Portfolio Manager Insect Control, noted that grower feedback has exceeded predictions and trials, offering new chemistry for integrated pest management programmes.

Why it mattersSouth African potato farmers gain a new tool to protect their crops from severe losses caused by the potato tuber moth.
