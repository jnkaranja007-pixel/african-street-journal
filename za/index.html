South Africa reach 140/4 against Australia

Proteas hold a 40-run lead after Australia were bowled out for 429 in the third Test in Cape Town.

South Africa ended day three of the third Test against Australia in Cape Town on Thursday at 140/4, leading by 40 runs. Matthew Breetzke was 19 not out and Ryan Rickelton was unbeaten on eight. Australia were bowled out for 429 in their first innings, a lead of 100 runs.

Spin bowler Simon Harmer led the Proteas attack with 4/104, removing Josh Inglis (102) and Alex Carey (80) who had put on 185 runs for the fifth wicket. Seamer Marco Jansen took 3/60 as South Africa's bowlers claimed the last five Australian wickets for 59 runs. Captain Temba Bavuma scored 31 off 115 balls before being bowled by Nathan Lyon.

The Proteas need a strong performance on day four to stay in the series, which is currently level at 1-1. Bavuma and Marques Ackerman (62) attempted to stabilise the innings after South Africa fell to 95/3. Wiaan Mulder, Marco Jansen, and Simon Harmer are still to bat for the hosts.

Why it mattersSouth African cricket fans will be watching to see if the Proteas can secure a series win at home.

Lesufi and Zille trade barbs over Joburg service delivery

Gauteng Premier Panyaza Lesufi and DA mayoral candidate Helen Zille exchanged insults ahead of local elections.

Gauteng Premier Panyaza Lesufi and DA mayoral candidate Helen Zille traded insults on Thursday over service delivery in Johannesburg. Lesufi referred to Zille as a "Ford Cortina," an old car, while Zille accused Lesufi of taking credit for a transformer replacement in Soweto that the DA had funded. Johannesburg faces a R220 billion infrastructure backlog and less than half of its service delivery targets are being met.

Zille claimed on X that the DA had paid for the transformer and arranged its installation with Eskom, criticizing Lesufi for claiming credit. Lesufi responded by sharing older posts on X, stating he had been involved with transformers since 2024 and that the Gauteng government invested R82 million for transformers this year alone. He mentioned opening two in Snake Park and one in Rietvallei, with three more planned for Kagiso.

The exchange occurred as the battle for the City of Johannesburg intensifies ahead of local government elections. Disgruntled residents have protested deteriorating municipal services, including power and water outages, potholes, drug activity, and crime. The contest involves severe municipal decay, a large Eskom debt, and clashes between the ANC and opposition parties like the DA and ActionSA.

Why it mattersJohannesburg residents are affected by worsening service delivery, including power outages and crime, as political parties clash over responsibility.

South Africans debate realistic SASSA grant increase

Some citizens favour a smaller, achievable rise in the old-age grant over larger, unmet promises.

South Africans are discussing a proposed increase to the SASSA old-age grant, with some favouring a R3 000 monthly amount as more realistic. Detlef Weber commented on The South African’s Facebook page that this figure strikes a balance, unlike calls for R5 000 or R6 000 grants. Tania Steenkamp added that even an extra R600 monthly is valuable for pensioners.

Grant recipients who depend solely on the payment could use additional funds for groceries, transport, or utility bills. Those supporting a moderate rise believe the government is more likely to approve an amount that fits current budget constraints. The debate acknowledges that the current grant struggles to keep pace with rising food and electricity prices.

Any adjustment to grant amounts requires announcement by the Minister of Finance in the February Budget Speech, with changes effective from April. Until then, current payment rates remain. The South African reported that 41% of the population receives a grant today, up from 25% in 2006.

Why it mattersSouth Africans receiving the old-age grant face difficult choices about how much to ask for, balancing immediate needs against the risk of no increase at all.

Stellenbosch mother dies after son killed in crash

Gaynor Adams, 38, died in hospital five days after her six-year-old son died at the scene of a crash on Helshoogte Pass.

A Stellenbosch mother, Gaynor Adams, 38, died in Tygerberg Hospital on Tuesday, October 27, after sustaining critical injuries in a crash five days earlier. Her six-year-old son, Berto-Lee Adams, died at the scene of the collision on Helshoogte Pass on Thursday, October 22. The mother and son were travelling from Kylemore towards Idas Valley when their vehicle collided with another car.

Stellenbosch municipal spokesperson Stuart Grobbelaar stated that a white sedan travelling towards Stellenbosch lost control and collided with a silver Toyota. Emergency personnel attended to Gaynor Adams at the scene before transporting her to hospital, while paramedics found Berto-Lee near the vehicle and declared him dead. The occupants of the second vehicle were not injured.

Cloetesville police have registered a culpable homicide case and are investigating the circumstances surrounding the fatal crash. St Idas Primary School, where Berto-Lee was a learner, and Speelkring Pre-Primary, where he attended an after-school programme, have both expressed shock and extended condolences to the family.

Why it mattersA mother and her six-year-old son have died following a car crash in the Western Cape, with police investigating the incident.

Cape Town terminal expects 13% fruit export rise

South Africa's deciduous fruit exports, including grapes and stone fruit, face new delivery rules for the upcoming season.

The Cape Town Container Terminal anticipates a 13% increase in South Africa’s deciduous fruit export volumes for the 2026/27 season, running from November to April. This growth, expected to reach 13% compared to the prior season, involves table grapes, plums, peaches, and apricots primarily destined for European markets.

To manage the anticipated surge, the terminal has implemented new measures following engagements with industry stakeholders and Transport Minister Barbara Creecy. These include hiring extra staff, enhancing leave management, and establishing a 24-hour hotline, alongside a fixed weekly vessel schedule with a 21-day berthing plan. Exporters must now deliver 95% of refrigerated containers by stack closure to avoid missing vessels.

These changes follow a challenging 2025/26 season where operational issues at Cape Town diverted an estimated 55,000 tons of table grapes. The terminal has since reduced average vessel waiting time from 115 hours to 44.3 hours and ship turnaround time from 103 hours to 55 hours. The terminal also added 28 new rubber-tyred gantry cranes and four straddle carriers.

Why it mattersSouth African farmers exporting fruit face new delivery deadlines and risk missing vessels if containers are not ready by stack closure.
