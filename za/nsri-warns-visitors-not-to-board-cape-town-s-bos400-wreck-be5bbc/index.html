NSRI warns visitors not to board Cape Town’s BOS400 wreck

The warning followed the rescue of an injured American tourist from rocks near Maori Bay after he was caught in rough water.

Sources: The Citizen SA and The South African · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

A rescue team pulled an injured American tourist from rocks near the BOS400 shipwreck at Maori Bay, near Cape Town, on Monday, 23 November. He had fallen into the sea while reportedly trying to board the wreck and was caught in rip currents and heavy swells.

The National Sea Rescue Institute (NSRI) said the man reached the wreck, then re-entered the water to retrieve his cellphone from nearby rocks. His brother and a friend got help from a passer-by, and rescue teams approached from land and sea. Medics treated him for hypothermia before taking him to the NSRI’s Hout Bay base; he was released to his companions without needing further treatment.

The NSRI warned the public against boarding the wreck, citing unstable structures, submerged metal, cold water and dangerous rip currents. The three Americans had competed in a Cape Town triathlon over the weekend.

Why it mattersVisitors approaching the wreck face the NSRI’s explicit warning not to board because of unstable structures and dangerous water.
