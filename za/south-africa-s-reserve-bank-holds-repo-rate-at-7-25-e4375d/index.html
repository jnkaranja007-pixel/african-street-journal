South Africa's Reserve Bank holds repo rate at 7.25%

The decision keeps the prime lending rate at 10.75%, although three of seven committee members favoured a 25-basis-point increase.

Sources: The South African and Daily Maverick · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

South Africa’s Reserve Bank kept its repo rate at 7.25% at its final Monetary Policy Committee meeting of 2026 on Thursday, leaving the prime lending rate at 10.75%. The decision means no further rate increase for borrowers with loans linked to prime.

Four committee members voted to hold rates, while three wanted a 25-basis-point increase. Governor Lesetja Kganyago said the bank had paused “for now” but remained ready to raise rates if inflation risks required it. Inflation rose to 5.2% in October from 4.5% in September, above the bank’s 3% target; the bank expects it to stay above 5% into early 2027, largely because of higher fuel costs.

The hold also brings no new relief from a rate cut. The bank’s next move will depend on inflation, fuel prices, the rand and wider economic conditions.

Why it mattersHouseholds with prime-linked home loans, vehicle finance and credit agreements keep the 10.75% rate, but receive no cut in repayments.
