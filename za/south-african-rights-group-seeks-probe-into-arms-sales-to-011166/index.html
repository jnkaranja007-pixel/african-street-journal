South African rights group seeks probe into arms sales to Myanmar

The Southern Africa Litigation Centre wants prosecutors to examine exports authorised while Myanmar’s military was committing genocide against the Rohingya.

Sources: Daily Maverick and Mail & Guardian · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

The Southern Africa Litigation Centre, a South African legal rights group, has asked the National Prosecuting Authority and the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation to investigate South Africa’s authorisation and export of arms to Myanmar from 2017 to 2021. The centre also wants investigators to examine the National Conventional Arms Control Committee, the body of ministers and senior officials that approved the sales.

The call followed the International Court of Justice’s ruling on Thursday, 19 November, that Myanmar’s military government committed genocide against the Rohingya minority and violated the Genocide Convention. The court’s verdict was its first finding that a state had committed genocide, according to the Mail & Guardian.

The litigation centre said the ruling could affect South Africa’s separate genocide case against Israel at the court, including how states’ obligations and evidence are assessed. Its international justice lead, Atilla Kisla, said the judgment addressed issues relevant to that case.

Why it mattersSouth Africa’s prosecutors and crime investigators now face a request to examine arms approvals and exports made over the five years from 2017 to 2021.
