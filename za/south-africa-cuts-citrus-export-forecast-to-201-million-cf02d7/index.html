South Africa cuts citrus export forecast to 201 million cartons

The Citrus Growers Association says flooding and port constraints drove the 2026 estimate 1.5% below 2025 and 4% below its April forecast.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

South Africa’s Citrus Growers Association of Southern Africa forecast 201 million 15kg cartons for the 2026 citrus season, 1.5% below 2025 and 4% below its April estimate of 209.4 million. Flooding between May and June in parts of the Eastern and Western Cape, Limpopo and Mpumalanga, along with domestic port constraints, prompted the revision.

The South African Table Grape Industry’s preliminary estimate for 2026-27 is 78.4 million cartons inspected for export, down 4% from the previous season but 1% above the five-year average. Citrus and table grape exports made up 24% of South Africa’s US$15.1 billion in agricultural exports in 2025; citrus was valued at US$2.5 billion and table grape and raisin exports at US$1.1 billion.

Higher dam levels after the 2025-26 summer rains support irrigated production, but the analysis warns that El Niño drought could weigh on field crops and some fruit and vegetable yields in 2027. It also cites a current heatwave and possible rainfall disruption as risks.

Why it mattersCitrus and table grape growers face lower forecast export volumes, while domestic ports remain a constraint ahead of the 2027 export season.
