South Africa inflation climbs to 5.2% as fuel and vegetable costs rise

Statistics South Africa says fuel inflation accelerated to 42.9%, while vegetable prices rose 8.1% year on year in October.

Sources: The South African and Farmers Review Africa · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

South Africa’s annual consumer inflation rose to 5.2% in October from 4.5% in September, Statistics South Africa reported. The 0.7 percentage-point increase put inflation at its highest level since May 2024, with fuel and food prices among the main pressures on household budgets.

Annual fuel inflation accelerated to 42.9%, up from 27.9% in September, and fuel prices rose 11.8% between the two months. Inland 95-octane petrol averaged R30.251 a litre in October and diesel R35.622. Vegetable inflation reached 8.1%, compared with 3.5% in September; onions cost 53.6% more than a year earlier, while potatoes rose 14.9%.

Food and non-alcoholic beverage inflation increased for a third consecutive month to 1.4%. Lower cereal and meat prices helped contain the overall food figure, while annual fish and seafood inflation reached 8.4%.

Why it mattersMotorists and businesses paying transport and distribution costs face fuel prices that rose 11.8% between September and October.
