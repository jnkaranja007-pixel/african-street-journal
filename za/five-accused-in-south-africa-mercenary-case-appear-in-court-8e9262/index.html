Five accused in South Africa mercenary case appear in court

Police say the group was held on suspicion of planning to travel to Russia to fight in Ukraine, and the case rests on an investigation involving Interpol.

Source: Africanews · 2026-11-19 · 1 min read

Five of the 10 foreign nationals charged under South Africa’s mercenary laws were brought before a Johannesburg court on Tuesday, the court heard. Police arrested the group on Sunday on suspicion of trying to reach Russia to fight in the war in Ukraine.

Police credit the arrests to an investigation that involved Interpol and local and international law enforcement agencies. The law bars mercenary activity and regulates military assistance to parties in foreign conflicts, and it applies to foreigners living in South Africa.

Nine of the accused are reportedly from Zimbabwe. In March, Harare said 15 of its citizens had been killed in Ukraine and it was seeking the return of 66 others, saying recruits were promised well-paid jobs, had their travel documents taken and were forced into combat.

Why it mattersThe five accused who appeared in Johannesburg must answer charges under South Africa's law regulating mercenary activity and military assistance in foreign conflicts.
