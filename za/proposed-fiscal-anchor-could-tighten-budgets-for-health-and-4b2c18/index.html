Proposed fiscal anchor could tighten budgets for health and education

Treasury's plan to write a binding debt target into law would cap state spending at a time when Gauteng is losing nurses.

Source: Mail & Guardian · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Gauteng's Estimates of Provincial Revenue and Expenditure show 2,899 medical and related personnel were lost between 2024/25 and 2025/26, and the Mail & Guardian reports the province is losing 100 nurses a month. Against that, the Minister of Finance announced in February that R21.3 billion and R9.9 billion were added to health and education over the medium term, with both increases set above consumer price inflation.

The Human Resources for Health Strategy projects a shortage of about 97,000 healthcare workers across South Africa in 2030. Health Minister stated in a parliamentary reply last year that the country has 4 doctors for every 10,000 people, below the international benchmark of 10. Treasury Director-General Duncan Pieterse has noted three consecutive years of primary budget surpluses.

Parliament's Standing Committee on Finance told National Treasury in its March 2024 report that the binding fiscal anchor should not be the trajectory of fiscal policy in relation to public expenditure. The proposed fiscal anchor is a binding debt or primary surplus target that would cap state borrowing and spending within a set range.

Why it mattersHealth workers and teachers would compete for a tighter budget if the proposed fiscal anchor is written into law.
