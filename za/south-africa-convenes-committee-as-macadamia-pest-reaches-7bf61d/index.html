South Africa convenes committee as macadamia pest reaches 34,000 hectares

The felted coccid affects nearly half the country’s cultivated macadamia area, with severe infestations cutting yields and putting jobs at risk.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

South Africa’s Department of Agriculture says the macadamia felted coccid pest has spread across about 34,000 hectares of orchards, nearly half the country’s cultivated area. It was confirmed in Ohrigstad, Limpopo, in November, after spreading across Mpumalanga from its first detection in Barberton Valley in 2017.

Farm inspections found leaf damage, dying canopies and fewer nuts; some trees had more than 80% canopy damage. In a severe-spread scenario, about 14,600 hectares in Mpumalanga face yield losses of up to 70%, with 5,950 jobs at risk. Macadamias South Africa says it has spent more than R6.3 million on research through 2026. Growers report pesticide costs rising from R60,000-R70,000 per hectare without the pest to R100,000 when control is added.

After November farm visits, the government convened stakeholders to discuss an action plan. A steering committee led by national and provincial governments is proposed to coordinate research, registrations, grower support and orchard rehabilitation.

Why it mattersMacadamia growers face pesticide bills of R100,000 per hectare in affected orchards, and a government-led committee is to coordinate support and rehabilitation.
