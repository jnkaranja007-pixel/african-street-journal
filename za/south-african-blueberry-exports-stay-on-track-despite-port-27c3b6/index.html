South African blueberry exports stay on track despite port delays

Growers report yields rising as older plants are replaced, while European and UK demand offsets Middle East shipment disruptions.

Source: Food Business Africa · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

South Africa’s blueberry export season remains on course to meet its initial forecasts through week 44, despite port delays and changing weather, the International Blueberry Organization said. Production is moving from northern growing areas to the southern regions expected to supply most exports for the rest of the season.

Brent Walsh, chief executive of Berries ZA, said northern production matched expectations because there were no material frost events. Short rain in the south requires growers to time orchard work carefully, he said. Replacing older varieties with newer genetics has lifted yields per hectare and fruit quality, while planted area has stayed relatively stable.

Wind-related disruptions at South African shipping terminals have required the industry to work with container terminal operators on efficiency. Middle East instability has reduced targeted shipments there, but demand from Europe and the UK has made up the shortfall. Berries ZA is also advancing market-access processes in China and India and has taken part in visits to Japan, Vietnam and South Korea.

Why it mattersBlueberry growers are directing shipments toward stronger European and UK demand as Middle East instability hampers targeted exports.
