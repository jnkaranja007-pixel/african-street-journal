Helen Zille warns Joburg voters against election boycott

The DA's Johannesburg mayoral candidate urged voters to cast ballots on November 4 to prevent corruption.

Sources: The South African and The Citizen SA · 2026-11-01 · 1 min read

Democratic Alliance mayoral candidate Helen Zille warned Johannesburg voters on Saturday, October 31, that staying away from the November 4 polls risks allowing corrupt officials another five years in power. Speaking at the DA's final rally in Mamelodi, Tshwane, Zille urged frustrated residents to vote rather than boycott, stating that not voting rewards the politicians they wish to hold accountable.

Zille argued that Johannesburg cannot endure another five years of failed governance and highlighted her five-point plan to address service delivery issues. Her pledges include fixing potholes within 72 hours, ensuring 95% of traffic lights function, and restoring water and electricity supplies. The plan also aims to tackle corruption and professionalize the city's administration.

Johannesburg is a key battleground for Wednesday's local government elections, with issues like water outages and failing infrastructure dominating the debate. Approved special voters will cast ballots on November 2 and 3, ahead of the general election on November 4.

Why it mattersJohannesburg voters face a choice between voting for improved services or risking continued poor governance and corruption.
