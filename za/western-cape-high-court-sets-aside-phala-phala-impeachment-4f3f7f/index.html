Western Cape High Court sets aside Phala Phala impeachment report

The court ruled the panel had to find sufficient evidence to justify an inquiry, not merely information about the theft at Ramaphosa's farm.

Sources: The Citizen SA, Daily Maverick and Mail & Guardian · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

The Western Cape High Court on Wednesday set aside in its entirety the independent panel report that underpinned Parliament's impeachment proceedings against President Cyril Ramaphosa. Judge President Nolwazi Penelope Mabindla-Boqwana, delivering the judgment in Cape Town, ruled the report fundamentally flawed. The case concerns the theft of approximately $580 000 (about R9.6 million) from Ramaphosa's Phala Phala game farm in Limpopo, reported on 9 February 2020.

Mabindla-Boqwana said the panel had to establish whether sufficient evidence existed to justify an impeachment inquiry, and that misreading that duty would undermine the whole report. She held that the Constitutional Court's earlier ruling concerned the constitutionality of Parliament's December 2022 vote, not the validity of the report, so it made no binding finding on the report.

Ramaphosa had obtained an interdict in late July halting public hearings by the impeachment committee until the court decided the matter. The case was heard over three days in September. The court also found his delay in bringing the review was not unreasonable, as it was linked to the Constitutional Court proceedings.

Why it mattersThe impeachment committee cannot rely on the report the court set aside, so any inquiry must now rest on a new finding of sufficient evidence.
