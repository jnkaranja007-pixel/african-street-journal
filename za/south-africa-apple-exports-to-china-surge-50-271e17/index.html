South Africa apple exports to China surge 50%

Zero tariffs on South African fruit entering China have boosted Tru-Cape’s exports by almost half this year.

Source: Farmers Review Africa · 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

South African apple exports to China have risen by nearly 50% this year following the removal of import tariffs. Tru-Cape Fruit Marketing, which handles over 20% of the country's apple exports to China, is experiencing this growth. The change means South African fruit now faces a 0% import tariff in China, a reduction from the previous 10% rate applied until May 1, 2026.

Conrad Fick, marketing director at Tru-Cape, stated that the playing field is now level for all Southern Hemisphere countries exporting to China. He believes this tariff reduction offers a significant opportunity to increase market share in Chinese retail. Specific apple varieties like Royal Gala Red and Royal Beaut are performing well, alongside growing acceptance of the Flash Gala brand, which is contributing to higher volumes and prices. Fick also noted that China's large population presents opportunities for significant volumes, provided consumer preferences are met.

While the tariff reduction enhances competitiveness, Fick stressed that China remains a premium market where quality is essential. South African apples are gaining popularity in both retail and snacking markets. Although New Zealand has advantages due to its proximity and established market for larger apples, South Africa possesses other competitive strengths. The report also highlighted India as a market where more competitive access is needed.

Why it mattersSouth African apple exporters are seeing increased sales to China due to a tariff reduction, while facing challenges in accessing the Indian market.
