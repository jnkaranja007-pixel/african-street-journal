Liberia's House accuses Senate of delaying bills

Representatives claim some Senate-passed bills have been pending for two years, causing frustration.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-10-26 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Members of Liberia's House of Representatives accused the Senate on Thursday of disrespect and delaying legislative action on bills. Lawmakers in the lower chamber stated that while they promptly consider bills from the Senate, their own forwarded legislation remains unattended to, without a decision. This dispute arose during the third quarter of the 55th Legislature's third session in Monrovia.

Grand Gedeh County Representative Jeremiah Garwo Sokan said the Senate's inaction on House bills is a failure to reciprocate the urgency shown by the lower chamber. Montserrado County Representative Sekou Kanneh threatened to stop voting on Senate bills, citing that some have been pending for up to two years. He argued this reflects a lack of coordination and sets a bad precedent.

Lawmakers suggested that if the Senate has concerns about House bills, it should communicate them directly. They proposed using conference committees to resolve disagreements instead of allowing bills to stall indefinitely. The frustration highlights an imbalance in how legislative business is handled between the two chambers.

Why it mattersTensions between Liberia's legislative chambers could delay the passage of laws affecting citizens.
