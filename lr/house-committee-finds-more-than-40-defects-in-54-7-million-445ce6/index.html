House committee finds more than 40 defects in $54.7 million registry deal

Liberia’s lawmakers flagged procurement and biometric-data concerns in a proposed agreement with Austria’s OeSD International GmbH.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

A Liberian House joint committee identified more than 40 defects in a proposed $54.7 million concession agreement between the National Identification Registry and Austria’s OeSD International GmbH, according to its November 18, 2026 report. The registry is the state body responsible for national identification.

The committee reported procurement violations, questionable financial arrangements and inadequate safeguards for biometric information, among other concerns. It also cited excessive delegation of statutory authority and possible exposure of national assets. The findings are reported in an opinion article by Emmanuel Yartoe, which draws on the committee report and FrontPage Africa’s investigative reporting.

The government signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding with OeSD on December 18, 2024. Liberian officials travelled to Austria for due diligence from February 9 to 14, 2025; the reporting says OeSD financed the trip.

Why it mattersThe president and oversight institutions now face calls to examine the registry’s leadership after lawmakers reported more than 40 defects in the proposed agreement.
