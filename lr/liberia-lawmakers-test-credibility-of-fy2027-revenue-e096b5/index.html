Liberia lawmakers test credibility of FY2027 revenue estimates

A House committee hearing will examine collection performance, tax exemptions and the capacity of institutions that raise domestic revenue.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Liberia’s House Committee on Ways, Means, Finance and Budget opened a hearing on the proposed FY2027 revenue framework, with Chairman P. Mike Jurry saying lawmakers will test whether its estimates are realistic, fair and achievable. The hearing is part of scrutiny of the FY2027 National Budget.

Jurry said the committee will examine the assumptions behind the projections, compare FY2026 revenue collection with approved targets and assess revenue agencies’ capacity. It will also review revenue leakages, exemptions, concessions, state-owned enterprises’ statutory obligations and possible ways to widen the domestic tax base.

The hearing is expected to let lawmakers question the Ministry of Finance, the Liberia Revenue Authority and other revenue-generating institutions about the estimates’ assumptions, risks and policy measures. Jurry said compliant taxpayers and legitimate businesses should not carry an unreasonable burden while evasion and smuggling persist.

Why it mattersThe Ministry of Finance, the Liberia Revenue Authority and other revenue bodies must answer lawmakers’ questions on their FY2027 estimates and collection capacity.
