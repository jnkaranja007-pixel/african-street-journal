Boakai ties Liberia’s US$5.7 billion infrastructure plan to future gold revenue

The agreement with Bea Mountain Mining Corporation covers four power plants, a transmission network and 14 road and bridge projects over five years.

Sources: FrontPage Africa and GNN Liberia · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Liberian President Joseph Nyuma Boakai signed a US$5.7 billion agreement with Bea Mountain Mining Corporation on Wednesday to build four power plants, a national transmission network and 14 road and bridge projects within five years. The government says it will finance the work with new revenue from the mining company beginning in 2027, rather than cash up front.

The agreement provides for 270 megawatts of additional electricity and a 146-kilometer highway. The company also agreed to a US$500 million bridge financing facility, according to FrontPage Africa. Boakai said the government would set aside the new revenue streams to fund the projects.

Alexander B. Cummings, political leader of the opposition Alternative National Congress, questioned the price of the deal at a news conference Thursday, warning that future generations could bear the cost. The government insists the agreement adds no borrowing.

Why it mattersPeople who depend on roads and reliable electricity are due to see projects built over five years, with financing tied to company revenue from 2027.
