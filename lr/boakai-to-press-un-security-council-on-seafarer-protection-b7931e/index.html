Boakai to press UN Security Council on seafarer protection

Liberia plans a high-level debate on civilian crews in conflict zones when it assumes the council presidency on December 3.

Sources: FrontPage Africa and GNN Liberia · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Liberian President Joseph Nyuma Boakai said Thursday that Liberia will use its upcoming presidency of the United Nations Security Council to push for stronger protection of civilian seafarers in conflict zones. He plans to seek a high-level debate at the United Nations on threats to workers aboard ships.

Boakai spoke at a maritime safety dialogue in Marshall City, Margibi County. He said piracy, armed conflict, drug trafficking, illegal fishing and cyberattacks endanger seafarers, whom he described as civilians entitled to protection under international humanitarian law. Liberia’s registry has more than 300 million gross tons, making it the world’s largest by tonnage, he said.

Liberia is due to assume the council’s rotating presidency on December 3, 2026. Boakai also called for stronger medical evacuations, crew changes and safe returns for seafarers stranded in conflict-affected areas, and for Gulf of Guinea countries to cooperate under the Yaoundé Code of Conduct.

Why it mattersSeafarers on Liberian-flagged vessels are the focus of Boakai's proposed UN debate, while Liberia's maritime agencies face his call to strengthen protection and response.
