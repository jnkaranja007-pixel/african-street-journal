Liberia's justice ministry plans legal redress after split jury in child rape trial

Eight jurors voted to acquit Patrick Weefur and four to convict, leaving a case involving a 13-year-old girl without a verdict.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

A split jury at Criminal Court E at the Temple of Justice in Monrovia returned a hung verdict in the rape case against Patrick Weefur, producing a mistrial. Eight jurors voted to acquit him and four to convict.

Cllr. Isaac L. George, director of the Sexual and Gender-Based Violence Crimes Unit at the Ministry of Justice, said prosecutors were troubled that Weefur had sought to change his not-guilty plea to guilty after the government had called about five witnesses, including the alleged victim. Judge Wesseh Alphonsus Wesseh declined to accept the guilty plea under Chapter 16.4 of Liberia's Criminal Procedure Law, but allowed Weefur to waive his right to testify.

The unit said it plans to pursue legal redress over the jury's handling of the case.

Why it mattersThe mistrial leaves the 13-year-old's case unresolved, and the ministry's unit must now decide how to challenge the jury's handling of it.
