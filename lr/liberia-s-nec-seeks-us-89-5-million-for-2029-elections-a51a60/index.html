Liberia’s NEC seeks US$89.5 million for 2029 elections

The proposal is 40% above the cost of the 2023 general elections and would fund 420 additional voting precincts.

Sources: FrontPage Africa and The New Dawn Liberia · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Liberia’s National Elections Commission (NEC) has asked the legislature and Finance Ministry to approve US$89,545,353.92 for presidential and legislative elections in 2029, a 40% increase on the cost of the 2023 general elections. NEC Chairman Jonathan K. Weedor announced the proposal at a news conference in Monrovia on November 23.

The commission plans to expand precincts from 2,080 to 2,500 and expects to register about 2.7 million eligible voters in the first quarter of 2028. It also plans to buy more than 10,000 biometric verification devices and use Starlink internet services to connect its headquarters with field offices.

The proposed budget divides spending across three years: US$32.5 million in 2027, US$12.9 million in 2028 and US$44.1 million in 2029. The NEC says the first tranche should be available by March 2027 to prevent procurement delays; the 2029 allocation includes provision for a possible presidential runoff.

Why it mattersThe legislature and Finance Ministry must assess the commission’s request, including whether to make US$32.5 million available by March 2027 for equipment procurement.
