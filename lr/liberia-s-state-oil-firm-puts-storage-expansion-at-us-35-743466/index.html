Liberia's state oil firm puts storage expansion at US$35 million

The company says it will fund US$15 million from internal resources and borrow US$20 million, after senators questioned whether the loan had approval.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Amos Tweh, managing director of the Liberia Petroleum Refining Company (LPRC), the state-owned oil firm, told the Senate on Tuesday, November 24, that a petroleum storage-terminal expansion would cost US$35 million, denying allegations that the company had secured an unauthorized US$30 million loan. The project combines internal funding and borrowing, he said.

Tweh said the company would contribute about US$15 million from its own resources and borrow US$20 million. The expansion is designed to add 50,000 metric tons of storage across petrol, automotive gas oil and aviation fuel, alongside pumping, tanker-loading and fire-protection systems.

Senators had summoned Tweh to clarify reports that the company borrowed without legislative consent. The Senate's concern was whether the proposed borrowing had received the required authorization.

Why it mattersThe oil firm must answer senators' authorization concerns before proceeding with the US$20 million borrowing for the expansion.
