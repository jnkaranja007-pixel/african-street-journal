Liberia faces 323 more maternal deaths from funding cuts

US$2.4 million in reproductive health support lost since 2025, risking access to contraception for 327,700 women.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-09 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Liberia could see 323 additional maternal deaths each year if reproductive health funding cuts continue, a Guttmacher Institute report states. The country has lost at least US$2.4 million in support since 2025, risking access to contraception for an estimated 327,700 women and couples annually. These reductions follow the withdrawal of United States health support in early 2025 and Sweden's decision to end development assistance.

The Guttmacher report, released in November 2026, projects over 107,600 unintended pregnancies and 37,800 unsafe abortions annually due to the funding gaps. US family planning disbursements dropped 34 percent, with support for 2026 nearing zero. Sweden, Liberia's second-largest development partner, phased out its 2021-2025 cooperation strategy which included reproductive health programs.

Adolescent girls face growing risks, with potential for 22,400 additional unintended pregnancies and 9,300 unsafe abortions yearly. The report warns these outcomes could hinder girls' education and their contribution to national development. Before the cuts, over half of adolescent girls wanting to avoid pregnancy were not using modern contraception.

Why it mattersLiberian women and adolescent girls face increased risks of maternal deaths, unintended pregnancies, and unsafe abortions due to reduced funding for reproductive health services.
