Liberia's economy grew 5.1 percent in 2025

Despite strong economic growth, poverty increased from 34.6 percent to 36.1 percent of the population.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-08 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Liberia's economy expanded by 5.1 percent in 2025, the strongest growth in five years according to the World Bank. Inflation ended the year at 4 percent, and export earnings rose by nearly 58 percent, driven by gold and iron ore. However, the World Bank estimates that the proportion of Liberians living in extreme poverty increased from 34.6 percent in 2024 to 36.1 percent in 2025.

The economic growth was fueled by mining, with industrial output jumping 10.8 percent and overall mining output rising 17 percent. Sectors where most Liberians work, such as agriculture, saw slower growth of 2.6 percent, and manufacturing shrank by 2.8 percent. The government's 62 state-owned entities have finances that the government itself cannot reliably track, the report noted.

The World Bank expects poverty to decrease to 33.2 percent in 2026 if farming recovers. Fiscal numbers improved with the budget deficit narrowing to 2.1 percent of GDP, but spending on capital projects was reduced from US$406 million to US$297 million.

Why it mattersLiberians experienced increased poverty despite economic growth, and the finances of state-owned companies remain unclear.
