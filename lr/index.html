Central Bank Launches Digital Registry to Boost Secured Lending

Liberia's Central Bank introduces an enhanced collateral registry system, a digital platform designed to streamline credit access for businesses and individuals by allowing more.

The Central Bank of Liberia (CBL) has launched an Enhanced Collateral Registry System, a digital platform aimed at expanding access to credit for businesses and individuals. This reform allows lenders and borrowers to more efficiently register and verify collateral through a modern, unified digital system. CBL Executive Governor Henry F. Saamoi stated the system is designed to unlock financing opportunities for entrepreneurs, small businesses, farmers, and other productive sectors that often struggle to access formal credit despite owning valuable assets.

The new system modernizes Liberia's collateral registration framework and strengthens the country's credit infrastructure, enabling both movable and immovable assets to be registered. Financial institutions can now electronically register, amend, assign, discharge, and search security interests more efficiently, reducing delays and uncertainty in lending transactions. The registry is linked with national databases such as the National Identification Registry, Liberia Land Authority, Liberia Business Registry, and vehicle registration systems to improve verification.

This initiative is part of Liberia’s broader effort to build a modern financial system capable of supporting investment and job creation, aligning with the government’s ARREST Agenda for Inclusive Development. The Enhanced Collateral Registry represents a significant milestone in modernizing Liberia’s financial infrastructure, creating a system intended to work for all Liberians by promoting responsible borrowing and expanding economic opportunity across all sectors.

Why it mattersFarmers and businesses can now use a wider range of assets, including land and equipment, to secure loans, potentially increasing access to capital for production and expansion.

Liberian Finance Minister Praises Departing Deputy Minister for ECOWAS Role

Finance Minister Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan lauded Deputy Minister Dehpue Y. Zuo as a top economic mind as Zuo prepares to assume a senior position at the ECOWAS Commission.

Liberia's Finance and Development Planning Minister, Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan, has bid farewell to Deputy Minister Dehpue Y. Zuo, who is set to take on a significant role at the ECOWAS Commission in Abuja, Nigeria. Ngafuan described Zuo as "one of Liberia’s finest economic minds" and highlighted his upcoming responsibilities, which include economic affairs, regional integration, agriculture, trade, and food security.

Speaking at a farewell event in Monrovia, Ngafuan acknowledged Zuo's departure as a moment of both national pride and a notable transition for the Ministry of Finance. He recalled Zuo's meticulous approach to economic policy, often referred to as "Deep Dive," emphasizing his thorough examination of proposals and data before making recommendations. Ngafuan also noted Zuo's humility, describing him as a "Gentle Giant" despite his professional achievements.

The two officials' professional relationship dates back to the late 1990s at the University of Liberia. Ngafuan appointed Zuo to lead the student union's Scholarship Committee, which significantly expanded scholarship beneficiaries. Their collaboration continued when Ngafuan appointed Zuo to roles within the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and later at the Ministry of Finance, where Zuo played a crucial part in Liberia's IMF program and efforts to secure international development partner support.

Why it mattersThe departure of a key economic official to a regional body impacts Liberia's economic management and international cooperation efforts.

Supreme Court Frees Murder Convict Kaiser After Evidence Weakness

Valington B. Kaiser, convicted of murder in March 2023, has been released after the Supreme Court found the state's evidence insufficient to corroborate his conviction.

Valington B. Kaiser, known as “Bishop,” has been freed from prison after the Supreme Court of Liberia overturned his murder conviction. The court cited fundamental weaknesses in the state's evidence, stating it failed to sufficiently corroborate the case against him. Kaiser had spent over three years incarcerated at the Monrovia Central Prison following his conviction in March 2023 for the May 2022 murder of 18-year-old Fatu Blasuah.

The original case stemmed from the discovery of Blasuah's body at the former LPRC compound in Monrovia, with police reporting multiple stab wounds and the recovery of a knife. Investigators presented CCTV footage and testimony from Blasuah's mother, Maima Sackie, who testified about her daughter's relationship with Kaiser and the events leading to her disappearance. The prosecution argued that Kaiser and Blasuah were romantically involved.

A jury at Criminal Court "A" found Kaiser guilty in March 2023, with ten out of twelve jurors voting to convict. However, the Supreme Court's review found the evidentiary pieces presented by the prosecution did not sufficiently support each other to sustain the conviction. With the conviction overturned, Kaiser's liberty and legal standing have been restored.

Why it mattersValington B. Kaiser, a man convicted of murder, has been released from prison due to insufficient corroborating evidence, impacting his personal liberty and legal standing.

Liberia Hosts UN Security Council E10 in New York

Liberia's UN envoy convened a working lunch with the Secretary-General and ten elected Security Council members to discuss global security and Council reform.

Liberia's Permanent Representative to the United Nations, Ambassador Lewis G. Brown II, hosted UN Secretary-General António Guterres and representatives of the E10, the ten elected members of the Security Council, for a working lunch in New York. The meeting, held on Monday, August 24, aimed to foster dialogue on pressing global security concerns and the functioning of the Council.

Liberia, currently holding coordinating roles within both the E10 and the African members of the Security Council (A3), used the platform to advocate for greater participation by elected members in decision-making and resolution drafting. Ambassador Brown has been vocal in pushing for reforms that would give non-permanent members more influence, challenging the traditional dominance of the five permanent members in setting the Council's agenda.

The discussions occurred amidst significant Security Council activity concerning conflicts in Africa, the Middle East, and Europe. Liberia's diplomatic efforts also involve harmonizing African positions on issues such as the conflicts in Libya, South Sudan, and eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, as the nation prepares to assume the Security Council presidency in December.

Why it mattersLiberia's diplomatic engagement with the UN Security Council affects its ability to influence international decisions on peace and security matters.

Cole Urges Bong Support for Howard-Taylor, Cites Selective Justice

Representative Marvin Cole calls for unity behind former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor, alleging unfair treatment in her drug trafficking case and questioning.

Bong County Representative Marvin Cole is urging residents to rally behind former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor, who faces drug trafficking and money laundering charges. Cole described Howard-Taylor's imprisonment as selective justice, asserting her innocence until proven guilty and calling for a free and fair trial. He questioned why other individuals allegedly involved in drug-related investigations have not faced similar swift legal action or detention.

Cole specifically cited the cases of Johnny Dean and a former LDEA Officer-in-Charge, as well as the GLS company operating at Roberts International Airport, suggesting these entities and individuals should also be under scrutiny. He pointed out that the government continues to conduct business with GLS, which he believes indicates a selective approach to fighting drug-related crimes. These allegations of selective prosecution were made during a Tuesday appearance on Sky FM’s 50-50 Talk Show.

Howard-Taylor was arrested on August 19, 2026, at Roberts International Airport and subsequently remanded to Monrovia Central Prison on August 25, after her lawyers' request for alternative accommodation was denied. The charges against her include unlicensed importation, sale, distribution, and transportation of controlled substances, along with criminal conspiracy and facilitation. The lawmaker also raised questions about the alleged role of Sheikh Bashiru Kante as an intermediary in the alleged transnational cocaine-trafficking network.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by Representative Cole's allegations of selective justice, which could indicate instability or corruption within the government that impacts rural livelihoods.
