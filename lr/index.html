Liberia House Summons Education Minister Over School Closures

Lawmakers will question education officials and teachers' leaders about the prolonged shutdown of public schools in Monrovia.

The House of Representatives has summoned Liberia's Education Minister, Dr. Jarso Maley Jallah, the Superintendent of the Monrovia Consolidated School System (MCSS), Dr. Augurie Stevens, and the MCSS Teachers Association leadership. This action follows concerns raised by Representative Dixon W. Seboe regarding the continued closure of public schools in Monrovia, which has disrupted academic activities for over a month into the new school year. Lawmakers aim to understand the dispute, negotiation status, and plans to resume normal classes.

The summons comes as MCSS Teachers Association President Augustine N. Nyormui has instructed teachers and staff to stay off school premises until their demands, including the resignation of Superintendent Dr. Augurie Stevens, are met. This has intensified the standoff, leaving thousands of students without instruction and prompting some to consider protests. The MCSS Students’ Union has also issued an ultimatum, demanding government action to resolve the dispute and implement a March 2025 Memorandum of Understanding.

Students are frustrated by lost instructional time and deteriorating school conditions, including inadequate learning materials and poor infrastructure, which they attribute to insufficient budgetary allocations. The dispute involves long-standing tensions over salary adjustments, employment status, and payroll commitments, with teachers having previously protested unpaid salaries and the treatment of volunteer educators. The House seeks to establish facts and find a resolution to safeguard students' right to education.

Why it mattersThousands of students are deprived of education due to the prolonged closure of public schools in Monrovia.

Liberia Tightens Payroll Controls for 2027 Budget

The Civil Service Agency is implementing new measures to verify staffing and expenditures before allocating funds for the upcoming national budget.

The Liberian government, through the Civil Service Agency (CSA), is enhancing controls over its payroll system in preparation for the 2027 National Budget. Director-General Dr. Josiah F. Joekai Jr. announced that personnel expenditures will now require credible employment records, demonstrated institutional needs, and confirmation of available financial resources. This initiative follows the submission of an Annual Manpower Hearing Report to the Ministry of Finance and Development Planning, aiming to align compensation allocations with verified staffing requirements.

The Annual Manpower Hearing, conducted from August 18 to September 1, 2026, involved technical representatives from the CSA, the Ministry of Finance and Development Planning, and participating government institutions. Spending entities presented their staffing needs and compensation proposals to justify workforce-related expenditures. Out of 109 invited entities, 77 participated, representing over 70 percent of those expected, with the CSA completing the exercise within its approved timeframe despite the absence of 32 institutions.

Dr. Joekai stated that this move addresses long-standing concerns regarding personnel expenditure management and discrepancies between staffing needs and requested allocations. The process is designed to foster a more transparent and disciplined approach to managing public sector employment and compensation, ensuring that budget allocations are based on legitimate needs and available funds, rather than unsupported demands.

Why it mattersGovernment employees and ministries are directly affected by the new verification requirements for payroll and compensation.

Morocco Delivers Military Aid to Liberia

Morocco has supplied military vehicles and supplies to Liberia, with 140 soldiers set for commando training in North Africa.

Morocco has delivered military vehicles and supplies to Liberia as part of an expanded defense cooperation agreement. Approximately 140 soldiers from the Armed Forces of Liberia (AFL) are scheduled to travel to Morocco for specialized commando training. A Moroccan C-130 military cargo aircraft landed at Roberts International Airport on Monday, October 19, initiating a series of flights planned between October 19 and 22 to transport military equipment and supplies.

This operation is a key component of a military cooperation agreement signed in October 2025, which aims to enhance military training, technical assistance, and healthcare cooperation between the two nations. The agreement has seen recent momentum with a September 2026 visit by AFL Chief of Staff Major General Davidson Fayiah Forleh to Morocco, where plans for military support and capacity-building were advanced, including a memorandum of understanding to boost the Liberian military's operational capabilities.

The partnership encompasses military equipment, specialized training, maritime security, and healthcare services for military personnel, including efforts to develop Liberia's military air wing and strengthen the Liberia Coast Guard. Cooperation with Mohammed V Military Hospital is also part of the agreement, facilitating medical personnel exchanges and specialized referrals.

Why it mattersLiberia's armed forces will receive new equipment and specialized training, enhancing their operational capabilities and professional development.

China's Development Initiative Funds Over 2,000 Projects Globally

More than 130 countries and organizations support the initiative, which has funded over 2,000 projects in more than 120 nations.

The Global Development Initiative (GDI), launched by China in September 2021, has seen over 2,000 cooperation projects implemented across more than 120 countries. The initiative, which aims to accelerate the UN 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, has garnered support from over 130 countries and international organizations. These projects are tailored to the specific needs of recipient nations, fostering collective global action and promoting inclusive, sustainable, and resilient development.

China has actively supported Liberia's national development through various GDI-related projects. Infrastructure improvements include two overpasses on Tubman Boulevard, a National Clinical Diagnosis and Treatment Laboratory, a Fingerprint Laboratory, and the refurbishment of the Liberia Broadcasting System. These projects aim to energize Liberia's economic and social progress, demonstrating China's commitment to partnership.

Beyond infrastructure, China has provided ongoing support in agriculture, education, and human resources through food assistance and professional training programs. These efforts aim to build capacity for self-reliant development in Liberia and other developing countries. Over 10,000 training programs have been conducted globally to share development approaches and practical skills.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the scale of Chinese-funded development projects globally, which can create opportunities for business and infrastructure development.
