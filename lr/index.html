Liberia retires 2,718 civil servants

The government aims to improve pension administration and ensure timely benefit payments.

Liberia will retire 2,718 civil servants at the end of December 2026 as part of nationwide pension reforms. The Civil Service Agency (CSA) identified these employees through a pre-retirement counseling and data collection exercise. This exercise covered all 15 counties and 67 government spending entities.

CSA Director General Dr. Josiah F. Joekai Jr. stated that the exercise captured 2,835 employees, including 2,718 scheduled for retirement and 117 with outstanding pension cases from 2020-2024. He said the government is determined to correct administrative weaknesses that caused delays and uncertainty for retirees. The Ministry of Education has the highest number of retirees with 1,493 employees.

The government has improved coordination between the CSA and the National Social Security and Welfare Corporation (NASSCORP) to facilitate pension enrollment. Retirees scheduled for December 31, 2026, are expected to receive their first monthly pension benefits in January 2027. Those registered during an extended period until November 30, 2026, will receive their first payments in February 2027.

Why it mattersThe retirement of 2,718 civil servants will affect their financial future and the efficiency of government operations.

US warns Liberia on investment climate

American firms face corruption, delays, and demands for bribes, says State Department report.

The United States government has issued a stark warning to American companies considering investment in Liberia, describing corruption as the "primary brake" on economic growth and democratic progress. The State Department's 2026 Investment Climate Statement highlights that officials often prioritize personal gain, leading to delays, increased costs, and legal risks for investors. Businesses that follow rules may face bribe demands, while those paying political interests can find it easier to operate illegally.

The report details that extortion is common at multiple government levels, particularly in procurement, contract awards, customs, and tax administration. Liberia's adherence to anti-corruption conventions has not yielded measurable reductions, with domestic law enforcement being inconsistent. While the report acknowledges nascent efforts by President Joseph Boakai's administration, such as asset declaration requirements and investigations into irregular contracts, it notes these have not yet shown visible results.

Disputes over contracts can lead to prolonged legal battles, as Liberia's Commercial Court faces severe backlogs, with cases taking two to five years to resolve. Despite economic growth of 5.1 percent in 2025 and improvements in mining and exports, the U.S. assessment suggests a challenging environment for foreign investment due to governance issues.

Why it mattersAmerican businesses looking to invest in Liberia face significant hurdles due to widespread corruption and an inefficient judicial system.

Liberian war rights office protests exclusion from memorial unveiling

The OWECC-L says war victims, survivors and people with disabilities were left out of the November 6 ceremony at the Executive Mansion.

The Office for the Establishment of War and Economic Crimes Court for Liberia, known as OWECC-L, has objected to the exclusion of war victims, survivors, persons with disabilities and key transitional justice stakeholders from the unveiling of a proposed National Memorial in Monrovia. In a statement issued on Tuesday, November 10, the Office called the decision unacceptable.

OWECC-L Executive Director Cllr. Jallah A. Barbu described the exclusion of his Office, and above all of victims' representatives, as "a shock and unacceptable." He said memorialising Liberia's civil wars should not be run solely as a government project, and that people whose lives were directly affected must have meaningful opportunities to shape how the country remembers its past.

The Office welcomed the government's plan for a National Memorial as part of transitional justice and reconciliation, but urged the Executive Mansion to reconsider and hold broad national consultations with victims, survivors, persons with disabilities and affected communities. It stressed that people disabled during the wars should help design and manage the memorial, not only observe it.

Why it mattersVictims, survivors and persons with disabilities are the people shut out of deciding how the memorial to the 14 years of conflict is designed and managed.

ArcelorMittal Liberia steps up railway safety drive in Grand Bassa

The company and police met Compound #3 residents to warn that trains cannot stop quickly, so people must stay off the tracks.

ArcelorMittal Liberia (AML) has stepped up its railway safety awareness campaign with residents of Compound #3 in Wee Statutory District, Grand Bassa County, on November 11, 2026. The sessions focus on keeping people away from active tracks along the Yekepa-Buchanan railway corridor.

AML Community Relations Manager Vaanii Kiazolu II said preventing accidents depends on both the company and communities, and asked residents to pass the messages on to children and young people. AML Deputy General Manager for Rail Mulbah Gbozee warned against walking or playing on tracks, crossing at unauthorized points and placing objects on them, saying trains need considerable distance to stop. Liberia National Police representatives and local authorities reinforced the warnings and urged residents to report activities that endanger lives.

The engagement is part of a wider program that includes radio campaigns, school outreach, community meetings and an annual Rail and Road Safety Caravan. Community participants pledged to work with AML, local authorities and law enforcement.

Why it mattersResidents living near the Yekepa-Buchanan line are asked to keep off the tracks and report dangerous activity around active rail operations.

Lofa to host Liberia's third National Agriculture Fair

The December 10-12 event in Voinjama will bring farmers and agribusinesses from every county together to boost local food output.

Liberia's third National Agriculture Fair will be held in Voinjama, Lofa County, from December 10 to 12, 2026. Agriculture Minister Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah announced on November 10 that the three-day event will draw farmers and agribusinesses from all 15 counties, with the aim of producing more food at home and relying less on imports.

Nuetah said the fair, under the theme "Growing More, Importing Less for Sustainable Development," is meant to strengthen agricultural value chains and open opportunities for processors, transporters and input suppliers. He argued that local production would save foreign exchange and draw investment into the sector. The fair opens on December 10 with a World Food Day observance run with the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations.

On December 11, FAO will host a forum on how farmers and agribusinesses obtain loans and other financial services. That day also brings the inauguration of an agricultural mechanization service center in Voinjama, as the government has received 304 pieces of farm machinery and set up such centers in six counties, four of them in Lofa. The fair closes on December 12 with an exhibition of products from all 15 counties and a food festival.

Why it mattersFarmers and agribusinesses from all 15 counties are invited to exhibit products and take part in investment discussions and financial services forums.
