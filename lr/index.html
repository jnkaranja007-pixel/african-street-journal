Nimba Labour Office Orders Hospital to Pay Salary Arrears

Ganta United Methodist Hospital must pay owed wages and meet Liberia's minimum monthly wage of US$143 by September 30.

The Ministry of Labour in Nimba County has ordered Ganta United Methodist Hospital to settle salary arrears owed to employees. An investigation by the Labour Office found that some workers were being paid below Liberia’s legally required minimum wage of US$143 monthly. The hospital must bring its compensation structure into compliance with the Decent Work Act of 2015.

Nimba County Labour Commissioner Alexander Suomie stated that the hospital’s salary structure was inconsistent with Liberian labor laws. He announced that beginning September 2026, all employees must receive a minimum of US$143 per month. The hospital has been given 30 days, from August 31 to September 30, 2026, to complete these payments.

Commissioner Suomie confirmed that the investigation established some employees were receiving salaries below the prescribed benchmark. The order mandates the hospital to settle all outstanding salary arrears and ensure its compensation structure aligns with national labor laws and the Decent Work Act within the specified 30-day period.

Why it mattersEmployees at Ganta United Methodist Hospital are affected by the order to receive back pay and a minimum monthly wage of US$143.

NEC Launches US$75 Million Strategic Plan for 2029 Elections

The National Elections Commission aims to modernize its systems and build public trust over five years with a significant financial investment.

Liberia's National Elections Commission (NEC) has unveiled a five-year strategic roadmap designed to bolster the credibility of the 2029 General Elections and ensure democratic stability. The plan, spanning from 2026 to 2030, prioritizes institutional reform, digital transformation, financial sustainability, and public confidence. The NEC estimates the initiative will require over US$75 million for implementation, addressing structural weaknesses that could impact electoral processes.

NEC Chairperson Jonathan K. Weedor launched the plan at the commission's headquarters, calling it a "bold new chapter in Liberia’s democratic evolution." He emphasized that electoral integrity must be intentionally designed, protected, and continuously strengthened. Weedor stated the NEC's goal is to consistently deliver credible, transparent, and inclusive elections through a modernized system and a professional workforce, thereby earning the enduring trust of the Liberian people.

This strategic plan signifies a commitment to enhancing the strength, independence, efficiency, and public legitimacy of the institution responsible for the entire electoral cycle. The proposed investment of more than US$75 million over five years is notable, particularly in a country where public institutions frequently face severe financial constraints. The NEC's success hinges on collaboration with the government, political parties, civil society, development partners, media, and citizens.

Why it mattersLiberian citizens are affected by the NEC's strategic plan as it aims to enhance the credibility, transparency, and inclusivity of future elections, thereby strengthening democratic stability.

Robbers Steal US$438,722 from CRC-MOPP Offices

Armed assailants injured three guards and took company documents during the early morning raid on the Cavalla Rubber Corporation and Maryland Oil Palm Plantation offices.

Suspected armed robbers raided the joint central office of the Cavalla Rubber Corporation (CRC) and Maryland Oil Palm Plantation (MOPP) early Wednesday, making off with US$438,722 intended for employee salaries. The attack, which occurred around 3 a.m. in Pleebo-Sodoken District, also resulted in injuries to an Emergency Response Unit (ERU) officer and two private security guards. The assailants reportedly seized a 9mm pistol and company documents during their escape.

The stolen money had been withdrawn from a bank for monthly payroll and was being held at the companies’ shared office. Sources indicated that unidentified armed men overpowered security personnel before fleeing with the cash and files. ERU officer Sergeant Romeo Payne sustained multiple injuries and had his pistol taken, while an M4 rifle assigned to him was recovered. Private guards Orailey Nance and Moses Dennis were also injured and treated at CRC Hospital.

Maryland County Superintendent Nathaniel S. Toe Jr. has called for an immediate investigation into the incident, directing authorities to determine how the attackers gained access and if internal assistance was involved. Investigators will examine the crime scene, the money's movement, security measures, and the perpetrators' entry and exit methods. The nature of the missing documents remains undisclosed.

Why it mattersEmployees of CRC and MOPP are affected because their salary payments were stolen, and company documents were taken during the armed robbery.

US$19.2M Cocaine Case Faces Collapse Over Flawed Evidence

A major drug bust indictment in Liberia is at risk of dismissal due to significant forensic and procedural errors, potentially undermining a key prosecution.

Liberia's Criminal Court "C" may see the collapse of a US$19.2 million cocaine seizure case, one of the nation's largest drug busts of 2026. Senior legal professionals have raised concerns that the indictment against 11 defendants contains critical forensic, procedural, and constitutional defects. These issues could prevent the case from proceeding to trial, casting doubt on the prosecution's ability to secure convictions.

The indictment, filed on August 27, 2026, targets individuals including Paul J. King, General Manager for Operations at Global Logistics Services, and George Wah Harris, owner of Paynesville City's Private Bar Entertainment Center. The charges include unlicensed possession of controlled drugs, illicit trafficking, and money laundering. The case stems from the seizure of 200kg of cocaine, falsely declared as "lappa and Maggi cubes," intended for export to London.

The cocaine was intercepted on June 5, 2026, at Roberts International Airport after security screeners noted weight discrepancies and unclear scanner images. The boxes were processed by King, who allegedly used his position at Global Logistics Services to facilitate the shipment. The consignment was listed to Usman Ali, a UK resident. The prosecution faces challenges related to evidence handling and procedural accuracy.

Why it mattersInvestors may be affected by the potential collapse of this major drug case due to legal and procedural flaws, impacting confidence in Liberia's judicial system and its ability to enforce regulations.

Senator Denies Receiving US$10,000 Nimba Legislative Funds

Nimba County Senator Nyan D. Twahyen Jr. calls for public budget disclosure amid controversy over legislative engagement allocations.

Nimba County Senator Nyan D. Twahyen Jr. has publicly denied receiving a controversial US$10,000 legislative engagement package, urging greater transparency in the county's finances. Speaking on a Ganta radio talk show, Twahyen stated he was aware of the allocation but did not receive the latest disbursement, though he acknowledged receiving a similar amount previously for project inspections.

The senator defended the legality of such funding, noting it was part of a budget approved by the County Council and that other counties like Bong and Bassa have similar allocations. The reported payments have sparked public concern in Nimba regarding proper authorization and adherence to financial procedures for county development funds.

Twahyen called on Nimba County Superintendent Kou Meapeh Gono to make the county's budget public. He believes transparency will help prevent speculation and disagreements over the use of county resources, promoting accountability and citizen participation in financial matters.

Why it mattersNimba County residents are directly affected by the controversy over the use of legislative engagement funds, which raises questions about financial transparency and accountability.
