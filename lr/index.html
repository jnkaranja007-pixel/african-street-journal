Central Bank Launches Digital Registry to Boost Secured Lending

Liberia's Central Bank introduces an enhanced collateral registry system to expand access to credit for businesses and individuals.

The Central Bank of Liberia (CBL) has launched an Enhanced Collateral Registry System, a digital platform designed to improve how lenders and borrowers register and verify collateral. This reform aims to expand access to credit for businesses and individuals by creating a more transparent and reliable framework for secured transactions. The system allows for the registration of both movable and immovable assets, including equipment, contracts, vehicles, inventory, and land, which were previously difficult to convert into bankable collateral.

CBL Executive Governor Henry F. Saamoi stated that the new system is a significant step toward strengthening secured lending, promoting responsible borrowing, and deepening financial inclusion. He emphasized that the registry is more than just a technological upgrade; it is an instrument for economic empowerment that will help unlock capital and expand opportunities. The platform integrates with other national databases, such as those for identification, land, business registration, and vehicle records, to improve the verification of borrowers and assets.

This initiative is part of Liberia's broader effort to modernize its financial system and support investment, job creation, and sustainable economic growth, aligning with the government's ARREST Agenda for Inclusive Development. The enhanced registry allows financial institutions to electronically register, amend, assign, discharge, and search security interests more efficiently, reducing delays and uncertainty in lending transactions and potentially lowering risks for lenders.

Why it mattersBusinesses and individuals can now use a wider range of assets as collateral, potentially increasing their access to loans for productive activities.

Finance Minister Praises Departing Deputy Minister Zuo

Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan lauded Dehpue Y. Zuo as a top economic mind before Zuo takes on a new ECOWAS role.

Liberia's Finance and Development Planning Minister Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan has bid farewell to Deputy Minister Dehpue Y. Zuo, who is set to assume a senior position at the ECOWAS Commission in Abuja, Nigeria. Zuo will oversee economic affairs, regional integration, agriculture, trade, and food security for the regional body. Ngafuan described Zuo's departure as a moment of national pride and a significant transition for the ministry.

At a farewell event in Monrovia, Ngafuan honored Zuo with the colleague-given names "DZ," "Deep Dive," and "Gentle Giant." He explained "DZ" signifies dependability and discipline, "Deep Dive" reflects Zuo's meticulous approach to economic policy and public service, and "Gentle Giant" highlights his humility despite professional achievements. Ngafuan noted their long-standing relationship began in the late 1990s at the University of Liberia.

Ngafuan recounted Zuo's impactful role in expanding student scholarships at the University of Liberia and highlighted his contributions at the Ministry of Finance and Development Planning. Zuo played key roles in Liberia's IMF-supported Extended Credit Facility program, efforts to secure a Millennium Challenge Corporation compact, and coordinating international development partner budget support.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as a key official departs the Ministry of Finance and Development Planning, impacting economic policy coordination and international financial programs.

Supreme Court Frees Murder Convict Kaiser After Evidence Weakness

Valington B. Kaiser, convicted in the 2022 murder of Fatu Blasuah, was released after the Supreme Court found the state's evidence insufficient to corroborate his guilt.

The Supreme Court of Liberia has overturned the murder conviction of Valington B. Kaiser, ordering his immediate release after serving over three years in prison. The court cited fundamental weaknesses in the prosecution's evidence, stating that the presented pieces did not sufficiently corroborate each other to sustain the conviction. Kaiser, also known as “Bishop,” was found guilty in March 2023 for the May 2022 murder of 18-year-old Fatu Blasuah at the former LPRC compound in Monrovia.

The case began with the discovery of Blasuah's body, which showed multiple stab wounds, on May 16, 2022. Investigators recovered a knife from the scene and presented CCTV footage allegedly placing Kaiser, then 20, in the vicinity. The prosecution also claimed Kaiser and Blasuah were in a romantic relationship, with Blasuah's mother testifying that her daughter left home after Kaiser contacted her about a phone. A jury convicted Kaiser in March 2023, with ten of twelve jurors voting to convict.

The Supreme Court's review found significant flaws in the evidentiary chain presented by the state. Despite the lower court's conviction and a jury's guilty verdict, the nation's highest court determined the evidence, including the knife, CCTV footage, and testimony, was not strong enough to support the conviction. Kaiser's legal standing and liberty have now been restored following this landmark decision.

Why it mattersValington B. Kaiser is affected by the Supreme Court's decision to overturn his murder conviction due to insufficient corroborating evidence, leading to his release from prison.

Liberia Hosts UN Security Council E10 Members

Liberia's UN envoy convened a working lunch with the Secretary-General and 10 elected Security Council members to discuss global security and Council reforms.

Liberia's Permanent Representative to the United Nations, Ambassador Lewis G. Brown II, hosted UN Secretary-General António Guterres and representatives of the 10 elected members of the Security Council at a working lunch in New York. The meeting, held Monday, August 24, at Le Pavillon near UN Headquarters, brought together the Secretary-General and representatives of the Council’s 10 non-permanent members, known collectively as the E10. Liberia is currently playing coordinating roles within both the E10 and the African members of the Security Council, known as the A3.

The working lunch occurred amid busy Security Council diplomacy concerning conflicts and security concerns across Africa, the Middle East, and Europe. Liberia’s diplomatic engagements have included discussions on Security Council working methods, the Middle East, conflict-related hunger, and African peace and security issues. Liberia is preparing to assume the presidency of the Security Council in December, where it is expected to lead the Council’s work.

Ambassador Brown has used Liberia’s coordinating role to advocate for greater participation by elected Council members in decision-making and resolution drafting. Speaking on behalf of the E10, Brown called for reforms to strengthen the role of elected members and pushed for broader “penholdership” arrangements, traditionally dominated by the five permanent members. Liberia and other elected members argue for a more inclusive approach giving non-permanent members greater influence.

Why it mattersLiberia's role in hosting UN Security Council members and advocating for reform impacts global diplomatic processes and decision-making on international peace and security.

Liberia Opens 2026 Oil Licensing Prequalification

The Liberia Petroleum Regulatory Authority is seeking companies to prequalify for direct negotiations on new oil exploration licenses.

The Liberia Petroleum Regulatory Authority has opened prequalification for its 2026 Direct Negotiation Petroleum Licensing Round, set to launch in the fourth quarter. Director General Marilyn Logan announced the initiative at Liberia Investor Day in Houston, Texas. The prequalification period allows companies to establish eligibility and complete internal reviews before negotiations commence. Applicants will be evaluated on technical capacity, financial capability, and offshore exploration experience, with prequalification remaining valid for five years.

The licensing round will proceed through direct negotiation, with contract terms tailored to each company's proposed work program. Logan stated that frontier exploration necessitates adaptable agreements to accommodate companies at various stages of technical and financial readiness. Liberia’s petroleum data partners, including TGS-NOPEC, Core Laboratories, and KC Geoscience Consulting, also presented at the event, highlighting Upper and Lower Cretaceous plays and opportunities beyond deep water.

This new round aims to attract partners for developing Liberia’s petroleum system through a transparent, rules-based process. Liberia’s previous licensing round resulted in eight production sharing contracts, with companies like TotalEnergies and Oranto Petroleum actively exploring blocks. The authority emphasized published criteria and equal treatment for all applicants in the upcoming round.

Why it mattersCompanies interested in exploring Liberia's offshore oil reserves can now begin the process of prequalification for new licensing agreements.
