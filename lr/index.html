Liberia's Supreme Court to hear appeal on ousted health chief

The government is appealing a ruling that ordered reinstatement or compensation for Dr. Dougbeh Christopher Nyan.

Liberia’s Supreme Court will hear arguments on October 26, 2026, in the government’s appeal of a February ruling. The earlier ruling ordered President Joseph Nyuma Boakai’s administration to reinstate or fully compensate Dr. Dougbeh Christopher Nyan, the biomedical scientist removed a year ago as head of the National Public Health Institute of Liberia (NPHIL). The case comes before the full court as an appeal from Nyan’s successful petition for a writ of prohibition.

The government, represented by the Ministry of Justice, filed the appeal against the February 16, 2026, ruling by Associate Justice Jamesetta Howard Wolokolie. Nyan, appointed by Boakai on August 1, 2024, was dismissed on October 15, 2025, along with his deputy, Dr. Adams K. Lincoln. Nyan's tenure saw the institute lead responses to mpox, Lassa fever, and measles outbreaks.

Nyan's dismissal is the latest turn in a dispute testing a president's power to remove officials with protected terms. The NPHIL Act states its Director General serves a fixed five-year term and can only be removed for specified causes after due process. The government's appeal follows an 11-count resolution from the NPHIL Board of Directors.

Why it mattersLiberia's Supreme Court will decide if President Boakai's administration must reinstate or compensate the former head of the national public health agency.

Liberia Supreme Court hears SRIMEX-LPRC fuel pricing dispute

The court will decide if an arbitration clause in a petroleum storage agreement overrides government fuel price changes.

The Supreme Court of Liberia is hearing a dispute between SRIMEX Oil & Gas Company and the Liberia Petroleum Refining Company (LPRC) over an arbitration clause in a petroleum storage agreement and the government's power to change fuel prices. SRIMEX, led by CEO Musa Hassan Bility, is asking the court to review how the Commercial Court handled its request to force arbitration. The company argues a presidential directive changing fuel prices violates their contract terms.

The case began after SRIMEX and LPRC signed a Petroleum Storage Agreement on September 3, 2025. Less than a month later, the President's office issued a directive that SRIMEX claims altered the agreement's commercial and tariff arrangements. SRIMEX filed a petition on September 29, 2025, seeking to compel LPRC to arbitrate the dispute and requesting an injunction against the new pricing formula.

SRIMEX's lawyers argue that the agreement's arbitration clause should be enforced, even if the dispute arises from later government actions. They warn that implementing the new prices before arbitration could cause irreparable financial harm and damage investor confidence. The Commercial Court had previously issued a temporary order to maintain the status quo and held a pretrial conference on October 21, 2025.

Why it mattersThe Supreme Court's decision will affect contractual rights and state regulatory powers in Liberia.

Liberia watchdog blasts official benefits

CENTAL director says US$9,000 salary and US$15,000 housing allowance for CBL deputy governor are excessive.

The Center for Transparency and Accountability in Liberia (CENTAL) has criticized the reported benefits for the Central Bank of Liberia (CBL) Deputy Governor, calling them excessive. Executive Director Anderson D. Miamen stated that the Deputy Governor receives a US$9,000 monthly salary and a US$15,000 housing allowance, alongside other benefits. Miamen described this package as a misallocation of public resources in a nation facing widespread poverty.

Miamen questioned the economic sense of providing such high allowances, especially for housing, to officials who may already own homes. He urged the Liberian Legislature and Executive Branch to review and reverse laws enabling these disproportionate financial benefits. The CENTAL director highlighted that many civil servants earn less than US$500 monthly, contrasting sharply with the senior officials' compensation.

CENTAL argues that these spending priorities raise serious concerns given the country's unemployment, inadequate healthcare, and limited educational opportunities. Miamen stated that such disparities undermine public confidence in national resource management and that the pattern of spending is unjustifiable and must stop. He believes that even a significantly increased national budget would not improve citizens' living conditions with this spending pattern.

Why it mattersLiberians face unemployment, inadequate healthcare, and limited educational opportunities while senior public officials receive excessive benefits.

Liberia imposes 21-day traveler isolation over Ebola threat

New rules require travelers from DRC to self-isolate, while those from Uganda, Kenya, and Nigeria face monitoring.

Liberia has implemented a 21-day isolation and monitoring period for travelers arriving from or passing through the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and for those arriving from Uganda, Kenya, and Nigeria. These measures follow Nigeria’s confirmation of an Ebola case in a traveler who flew from the DRC via Uganda. Sia Wata Camanor, Interim Director General of the National Public Health Institute of Liberia (NPHIL), announced these updated regulations in Monrovia.

The new rules, detailed in Travel Advisory No. 2, require individuals coming from Uganda, Kenya, and Nigeria to undergo 21 days of continuous monitoring, though not necessarily self-isolation. Camanor stated these steps aim to strengthen early detection and reduce cross-border disease spread. The advisory takes effect immediately, with enhanced health screening at all entry points, including Roberts International Airport and land borders like Ganta Junction and Bo Waterside.

All arriving passengers must complete surveillance forms detailing their health, travel history, and potential exposure. Port health officers will conduct temperature checks and clinical screenings, isolating any suspected cases. Conveyances suspected of carrying infection will undergo decontamination. Health officials are also monitoring international athletes and delegates at an ongoing African championship in Liberia.

Why it mattersTravelers arriving in Liberia face new isolation and monitoring rules due to regional Ebola threats.

Liberia's House accuses Senate of delaying bills

Representatives claim some Senate-passed bills have been pending for two years, causing frustration.

Members of Liberia's House of Representatives accused the Senate on Thursday of disrespect and delaying legislative action on bills. Lawmakers in the lower chamber stated that while they promptly consider bills from the Senate, their own forwarded legislation remains unattended to, without a decision. This dispute arose during the third quarter of the 55th Legislature's third session in Monrovia.

Grand Gedeh County Representative Jeremiah Garwo Sokan said the Senate's inaction on House bills is a failure to reciprocate the urgency shown by the lower chamber. Montserrado County Representative Sekou Kanneh threatened to stop voting on Senate bills, citing that some have been pending for up to two years. He argued this reflects a lack of coordination and sets a bad precedent.

Lawmakers suggested that if the Senate has concerns about House bills, it should communicate them directly. They proposed using conference committees to resolve disagreements instead of allowing bills to stall indefinitely. The frustration highlights an imbalance in how legislative business is handled between the two chambers.

Why it mattersTensions between Liberia's legislative chambers could delay the passage of laws affecting citizens.
