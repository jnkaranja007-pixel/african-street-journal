Court Rejects Jewel Howard-Taylor's Evidence Request as Hearing Starts

Monrovia City Court denied the former vice president's bid for early evidence disclosure, proceeding with a preliminary hearing on drug trafficking charges.

Former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor appeared in court Friday as prosecutors began presenting evidence in a preliminary hearing for criminal charges including drug trafficking and money laundering. The Monrovia City Court rejected Howard-Taylor's request to compel prosecutors to share evidence before the hearing, ruling that the proceeding's purpose is to establish probable cause, not guilt. Her defense argued for access to prepare adequately, while prosecutors stated the court's role is limited to determining if the case should advance.

The prosecution called Liberia National Police Crime Services Commissioner Raphael Wilson as its first witness. Under Liberian law, defense lawyers can cross-examine witnesses during preliminary proceedings to challenge the state's testimony. Howard-Taylor, who denies the allegations, arrived at the courthouse in a prison bus and was escorted into the courtroom by security. The court will decide whether sufficient probable cause exists to send the case to the Circuit Court for trial.

Howard-Taylor faces charges alongside three foreign nationals in connection with an alleged drug-trafficking investigation. The charges include importation of controlled drugs, unlicensed possession, sale, and transportation of controlled substances, criminal conspiracy, facilitation, and aiding and abetting. The preliminary examination aims to allow prosecutors to present the basis of their charges and establish grounds for the case to proceed, with defense lawyers expected to challenge the state's evidence.

Why it mattersFormer Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor is affected by the preliminary hearing on drug trafficking and money laundering charges, which is proceeding without her defense receiving evidence in advance.

Liberia Concludes UN Security Council E10 Coordinator Role

Liberia successfully led the Ten Elected Members of the UN Security Council for August, shaping global peace and security debates.

Liberia has completed its term as Coordinator of the Ten Elected Members (E10) of the United Nations Security Council for August 2026. During its tenure, Liberia hosted UN Secretary-General António Guterres for discussions on humanitarian aid in Gaza and the West Bank. The nation also chaired meetings to align consensus on the Libyan political process and security in West Africa and the Sahel.

Ambassador Lewis G. Brown II led negotiations with the International Residual Mechanism for Criminal Tribunals regarding judicial archives and downsizing. Liberia also championed expert briefings on the Ukraine crisis and advocated for the release of detained UN and aid personnel in Yemen. The country pushed for reforms of Security Council Subsidiary Bodies to improve alignment between the E10 and the Permanent Five members.

The E10 alliance formally commended Liberia for its leadership in steering the Council’s thematic priorities. Pakistan has now assumed the E10 Coordination role for September. Liberia remains a principled voice on the Security Council, committed to international peace and cooperation.

Why it mattersLiberia's leadership as E10 Coordinator elevated its diplomatic standing and influenced discussions on global peace and security matters.

Liberia Agricultural Company Adds Buses, Truck for Operations

The company commissioned two new school buses and a heavy-duty truck to improve education and logistics on its plantation.

The Liberia Agricultural Company (LAC) has introduced two new school buses and a TATA truck to enhance its educational and operational services. Commissioned over the weekend by LAC General Manager Naveen Madan, these additions aim to support the upcoming academic and operational year. LAC manages eleven schools serving approximately 7,750 students, with 45% being girls, and also provides education for 348 students from surrounding communities.

The new buses are intended to improve student transportation across the plantation, reducing travel time and potentially allowing more time for study. LAC management anticipates increased flexibility and reduced vehicle downtime, ensuring reliable school transport. The company also commissioned a heavy-duty TATA truck for rubber collection and other logistical tasks, underscoring a commitment to strengthening both educational services and plantation operations.

During the commissioning, General Manager Naveen Madan urged drivers to maintain the vehicles and cautioned students against damaging property. He emphasized responsible use of the new assets. This investment is part of a broader initiative to upgrade services for employees, students, and nearby communities, with further improvements anticipated.

Why it mattersEmployees' children and students from surrounding communities will benefit from improved school transportation, reducing their travel time and potentially increasing study opportunities.

Senator Varpilah Warns of Women's Health Crisis

Grand Cape Mount County Senator Dabah M. Varpilah highlighted ongoing health and social challenges facing women and girls, including maternal mortality and gender-based violence.

Senator Dabah M. Varpilah has raised concerns about the persistent health and social challenges affecting women and girls across Liberia. These issues include high rates of maternal mortality, teenage pregnancy, early marriage, and gender-based violence, particularly in underserved rural and urban areas. Varpilah, who chairs the Senate Committee on Health and Social Welfare, emphasized the need for increased government funding and stronger partnerships with development organizations to tackle these critical problems.

Speaking at the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) mid-year country programme review in Monrovia, Varpilah described maternal mortality as a significant national burden. She stressed that despite some progress, more effort is required to prevent deaths related to pregnancy and childbirth. The senator noted that the repercussions of these issues extend beyond statistics, leading to lost educational and economic opportunities, hindering community development, and diminishing future prospects for generations.

Varpilah called for sustained investment in reproductive and maternal healthcare, alongside robust measures to protect women and girls from violence and harmful traditional practices. She acknowledged UNFPA's contributions to Liberia in areas such as population data, health system strengthening, family planning, and combating gender-based violence. However, she cautioned that these advancements should not obscure the ongoing struggles faced by women and girls throughout the country, reaffirming the legislature's commitment to improving the health sector.

Why it mattersWomen and girls in Liberia face serious health and social challenges including maternal mortality, teenage pregnancy, early marriage, and gender-based violence.
