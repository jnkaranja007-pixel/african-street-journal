Liberian importers petition legislature over shipping delays

Business owners face seven-month cargo delays and rising port fees, petitioning for intervention.

Liberian business owners and importers gathered Tuesday at the Capitol Building in Monrovia to petition the Legislature and the National Port Authority over severe shipping delays and increased port fees. The groups, including China Business Women United with One Voice and the Consortium of Liberia Importers, are demanding urgent intervention as cargo shipments from Asia now take six to seven months to reach the Freeport of Monrovia.

Advocate Sriya Musu Momolu stated that these prolonged shipping timelines and unfair fees are pushing local enterprises to the brink of collapse. Businesses relying on bank loans fear losing personal homes and properties due to accumulating interest rates caused by the delays. The petition highlights that Delivery Orders arbitrarily expire before cargo can be retrieved, forcing owners to pay costly renewal fees and demurrage charges for delays beyond their control.

The House of Representatives has summoned key maritime and shipping stakeholders for a hearing on Friday, November 6, following the protest. Importers are also complaining about administrative surcharges and double taxation, as loan interest accumulates while they must still meet tax obligations to the Liberia Revenue Authority and pay salaries.

Why it mattersLiberian importers are facing financial distress and potential loss of property due to extended shipping delays and excessive port charges.

Liberia tells IMO seafarers must not be war targets

Liberia's Maritime Commissioner Neto Zarzar Lighe, Sr. voiced concern over attacks in key shipping lanes.

Liberia has urged the International Maritime Organization (IMO) that seafarers should not be targeted in ongoing conflicts affecting global shipping lanes. Maritime Commissioner and CEO Cllr. Neto Zarzar Lighe, Sr. stated this on Tuesday, November 3, at the IMO Council's 138th session, highlighting grave concerns over persistent attacks in the Black Sea, Arabian Sea, Red Sea, and other critical waterways. Thousands of seafarers and their vessels are currently trapped by military operations in these waters.

Commissioner Lighe stressed that seafarers are innocent civilians whose work is essential for international trade and the global economy, and they must not become casualties of geopolitical hostilities. He noted that these "unwarranted attacks" endanger crews, damage vessels, disrupt trade, and have tragically claimed lives. Liberia extended condolences to the families of seafarers killed in these incidents.

Liberia's plea carries significant weight as it is the world's largest ship registry, representing about 17 percent of the global fleet. Ships flying the Liberian flag have been directly affected by attacks, including the Liberian-flagged Express Rome struck on March 11 and the Minoan Pioneer hit by a projectile on August 3. The Liberian Maritime Authority has ordered its flagged ships to the highest security level following these events.

Why it mattersLiberia, as the world's largest ship registry, has a direct stake in the safety of seafarers and the security of international shipping lanes due to potential revenue loss and damage to its flagged vessels.

Liberia ex-NSA official gets 50 years for statutory rape

Peter Bon Jallah, 63, was sentenced Tuesday, November 3, for statutory rape of a 16-year-old boy.

Former National Security Agency official Peter Bon Jallah, 63, was sentenced to 50 years in prison on Tuesday, November 3, for the statutory rape of a 16-year-old boy. Criminal Court “E” Judge Wesseh Alphonsus Wesseh Sr. handed down the sentence at the Monrovia Central Prison. Jallah was convicted of statutory rape following weeks of legal proceedings.

The court acquitted Jallah of gang rape, stating insufficient evidence, but his kidnapping conviction was quashed due to jurisdictional error and will be pursued in a new trial. Prosecutors presented 12 witnesses, including DNA evidence, though the forensic examiner noted it could not establish when the biological material was deposited. Jallah was arrested in January 2026.

Jallah’s defense lawyer announced plans to appeal the conviction and sentence to the Supreme Court of Liberia. The case has drawn public attention due to Jallah's former position and the involvement of a minor. The judge denied Jallah’s motion for a new trial.

Why it mattersA former National Security Agency official has been sentenced to 50 years in prison for statutory rape, affecting the victim and public trust in the justice system.

Liberian businesswomen protest port delays

Hundreds of women marched to the Capitol Building demanding government action on shipping and port charges.

Liberian businesswomen protested at the Capitol Building on Tuesday, November 3, against prolonged shipping delays and excessive port charges. They warned that their businesses are on the verge of collapse due to these issues, which also include alleged violations of Liberia’s Liberianization Policy. The women, many dressed in white, carried placards highlighting their plight and demanding urgent government intervention.

Protesters accused shipping companies like Maersk Line of significant cargo delivery delays, leading to expired delivery orders and mounting bank loan obligations. Madam Matherline Nimely stated that shipments from Asia now take six to seven months to arrive in Monrovia, preventing inventory replenishment. She explained that entrepreneurs face high-interest payments on loans secured with their properties while their goods remain stranded.

Legislators, including Representative Prince Toles and Senator Abraham Darius Dillon, received the petition and pledged to examine the concerns. They assured the businesswomen that legislative efforts to address shipping and port challenges were already underway, with invitations extended to relevant stakeholders. The lawmakers stated they would act on the presented document.

Why it mattersLiberian businesswomen are suffering from prolonged shipping delays and high port charges that threaten their investments and livelihoods.

Liberia explores bamboo for eco-prosperity

Liberian experts suggest adapting China's bamboo model to boost local economies and manage natural resources.

Liberian media representatives visited Anji County, China, to observe how bamboo processing, conservation, and rural tourism have transformed the local economy. This visit aims to inform Liberia's approach to managing its forests, wetlands, and agricultural landscapes for employment and community benefit.

Researchers Forkpah Pewee and Dr. Anthony Saah Tengbh believe elements of Anji's model can work in Liberia if adapted to local conditions and supported by practical investments. China's policy, launched after President Xi Xiapin's 2020 UN speech, promotes bamboo as a biodegradable substitute for plastic to combat climate change and pollution.

Liberia, with its extensive natural resources, could potentially turn conservation into a foundation for prosperity. However, successful implementation will depend on financing, infrastructure, technical capacity, environmental enforcement, and community ownership, according to the report.

Why it mattersLiberia's environmental future and economic development could be enhanced by adopting sustainable practices observed in China.
