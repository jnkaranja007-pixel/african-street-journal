UNDP launches Liberia's law reform plan

The five-year strategy aims to modernize the legal framework and improve access to justice by 2030.

The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) Resident Representative, Aliou Mamadou Dia, launched the Law Reform Commission (LRC) Strategic Plan 2026-2030 in Monrovia on November 6. This five-year plan provides a roadmap for modernizing Liberia’s legal framework and improving access to justice, aligning with the government’s ARREST Agenda. The launch gathered government, legislative, judicial, and civil society representatives.

Dia described law reform as a cornerstone of democratic governance and economic development, essential for inclusive national progress. He praised the LRC's work in reviewing outdated laws, supporting constitutional reform, and developing an online legal portal for public access. UNDP will continue supporting the LRC in areas such as legislative drafting, legal research, and digitalization.

The strategic plan, themed “Better Laws for a More Accountable Society,” outlines priorities including strengthening the rule of law, advancing decentralization, and promoting reforms that meet citizens' needs. It also aligns with UNDP’s Country Programme Document 2026-2030, aiming for a more accessible and effective legal system by 2030.

Why it mattersLiberians will benefit from a modernized legal system and improved access to justice through the LRC's five-year strategic plan.

Liberia faces 323 extra maternal deaths annually

US$2.4 million in health funding cuts since 2025 threaten reproductive care and contraception access for 327,700 women.

Liberia could see an additional 323 maternal deaths each year if United States and Swedish funding cuts for family planning and reproductive health continue, a Guttmacher Institute report states. The report, released in November 2026, warns that the country has lost at least US$2.4 million in support since 2025, risking access to contraception for approximately 327,700 women and couples annually.

These funding reductions, stemming from the US withdrawal of health support in early 2025 and Sweden's decision to phase out development assistance in December 2025, could lead to more than 107,600 unintended pregnancies and 37,800 unsafe abortions annually. US family planning disbursements have already declined by 34 percent, with support for 2026 expected to be near zero.

Adolescent girls are particularly vulnerable, facing increased risks of unintended pregnancies and unsafe abortions due to reduced youth-friendly services and outreach. The report estimates an additional 22,400 unintended pregnancies and 9,300 unsafe abortions among adolescent girls each year if funding cuts persist, potentially impacting their education and future prospects.

Why it mattersLiberian women and girls face increased risks of maternal deaths, unintended pregnancies, and unsafe abortions due to reduced funding for reproductive health services.

Liberia launches civil servant credit union campaign

The Civil Service Agency and CSAL-CU aim to boost savings and credit access for over 60,000 government employees.

The Civil Service Agency (CSA) and the Civil Servants Association of Liberia Credit Union (CSAL-CU) have begun a nationwide awareness campaign for their new institution. The credit union officially launches on November 13, 2026, at the Ministerial Complex in Congo Town, Monrovia. This initiative targets over 60,000 government employees across Liberia, aiming to improve financial empowerment, encourage disciplined savings, and expand access to credit.

CSA Director-General Dr. Josiah F. Joekai Sr. pledged institutional support for the credit union, which kicked off its campaign on Saturday, November 7, with a parade and sports activities at Invincible Park in Sinkor. Dr. Joekai urged leaders to ensure strict accountability and cautioned against fund misappropriation, emphasizing the need to safeguard members’ contributions. He also encouraged civil servants to maintain regular savings through salary deductions.

CSAL-CU Chief Executive Officer Pertaingber Lawrence Noaus stated the campaign aims to unite credit unions and cooperatives to broaden economic opportunities for government employees. CSAL Board of Directors Chairperson Prince Leo Kponbowoe described the credit union as a key tool for improving civil servants' financial well-being. Participating organizations included the Port Authority Credit Union, the University of Liberia Credit Union Cooperative Society, and the Teachers Credit Union.

Liberia proposes record $1.538 billion budget

The proposed 2027 budget is the largest in the country's history, while extreme poverty also increased.

Liberia's government has proposed a US$1.538 billion budget for 2027, the largest in the nation's history, presented to lawmakers on Friday. This comes as the Liberia Revenue Authority reported crossing US$1 billion in domestic collections for the first time in September.

Finance Minister Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan pointed to 5.1 percent economic growth last year and controlled inflation, stating Liberia is improving though not yet where it aims to be. However, the World Bank's November 3 Liberia Economic Update found that extreme poverty grew alongside the economy.

Opposition leader Alexander Cummings has criticized the government's focus on future benefits over immediate needs, while the Citizens Movement for Change questioned the Finance Ministry's credibility due to audit gaps and unreconciled records with the LRA and Central Bank.

Why it mattersLiberians face a paradox where the national budget and revenue collections reach record highs, yet extreme poverty has also increased.

DStv slashes subscription prices in Liberia

Satellite TV provider DStv reduces its Ultimate package from US$37 to US$7 monthly, making it more accessible.

Satellite television provider DStv has significantly reduced its subscription prices in Liberia, with the Ultimate package now costing US$7 monthly, down from US$37. The changes, which took effect on November 5, 2026, also include a simplified package structure and expanded entertainment options across Africa.

Cynthia Awuor Joel, Digital Marketing Specialist at MultiChoice, stated that the price cuts were a direct response to customer feedback about DStv being perceived as too expensive. She explained that the company aims to reposition the brand as more affordable and consumer-friendly, making satellite television accessible to a wider Liberian audience.

The new DStv structure features four packages: Starter (US$7), Select (US$13), Sports, and Ultimate, replacing previous arrangements. The Starter package offers telenovelas, Africa Magic, Cartoon Network, and a weekly football match, while Select adds more football coverage like La Liga, additional cartoons, and movies.

Why it mattersLiberian households and businesses can now access satellite television entertainment at lower monthly costs.
