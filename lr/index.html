US investors eye Liberia's mining and energy sectors

American companies are exploring long-term investment in Liberia's mineral resources and energy infrastructure.

The United States has observed growing interest from American companies in Liberia's mining and energy sectors, with several businesses exploring long-term investment opportunities. U.S. Chargé d’Affaires Kristin Gilmore stated on Wednesday, October 28, that multiple private-sector delegations have visited Liberia recently, indicating significant commercial interest in the country's mineral wealth and energy potential.

Gilmore explained that Liberia's drive to attract foreign investment and develop its mining and energy sectors aligns with U.S. goals to diversify global supply chains. The Millennium Challenge Corporation (MCC) confirmed Liberia's eligibility for a second compact focused on mining and electricity reforms to spur private investment. The U.S. Trade and Development Agency is also finalizing a grant to assess Liberia’s railway infrastructure for a multi-user system.

Attracting sustained foreign investment requires transparent regulations, predictable policies, reliable electricity, and fair infrastructure access, Gilmore noted, citing that developing a new mine averages 18 years. American companies can offer technical expertise, workforce training, technology transfer, and investment, while expanding local supply chains and creating jobs.

Why it mattersU.S. companies are exploring investment in Liberia's mining and energy sectors, which could lead to job creation and economic growth.

Liberia proposes $261M disability sports village

Amb. S. Lionel Johnson envisions a 10-year project to build accessible facilities and provide specialized support for athletes with disabilities.

Amb. S. Lionel Johnson, founder of the proposed Liberia National Disability Sports Village, has put forward a plan to build a dedicated home for disability sports in Liberia. The project, estimated to cost between US$261 million and US$506 million over 10 years, aims to start with a national high-performance wheelchair basketball center and expand into an international sports destination.

This initiative seeks to reduce barriers such as limited access to specialized facilities, equipment, and training for athletes living with disabilities. The proposed village would offer accessible sports facilities, specialized coaching, sports medicine, and technical support, potentially creating employment and attracting international partnerships.

The proposal aligns with the Liberian government's ARREST Agenda for Inclusive Development (AAID), which prioritizes inclusion and opportunities for persons with disabilities. While the ambition is significant, the project faces a major hurdle in securing funding, with feasibility studies and detailed designs still required.

Why it mattersAthletes with disabilities in Liberia will have greater opportunities to develop their talents and compete on a more equal footing if the proposed sports village is realized.

Liberia Water Justice Forum launched to demand safe water access

A new advocacy group calls on the Liberian government to ensure equitable and affordable provision of safe drinking water for all citizens.

A new water advocacy group, the Liberia Water Justice Forum, was launched in Brewerville, outside Monrovia. The Forum, a collaboration between Church Aid International (CAI), the Fellowship of Christian Councils and Churches in West Africa (FECCIWA), and the Africa Water Justice Network, aims to advocate for access to safe drinking water across Liberia.

CAI Chairman Bishop Kortu Brown stated that the Forum will ask difficult questions about water access and hold leaders accountable. He emphasized that leaders must be prepared to answer these questions to address societal challenges, particularly concerning the provision of accessible and affordable safe drinking water for all Liberians, especially women and children.

The group also called on concession companies operating in Liberia to pay fees for allegedly polluting rivers and creeks. Rev. Christopher Toe, Secretary General of the Liberia Council of Churches, noted that the Forum's launch aligns with pillar six of the Sustainable Development Goals, which focuses on clean water and sanitation.

Why it mattersLiberians, particularly women and children, are directly affected by the call for equitable and affordable provision of safe drinking water.

Agriculture · 2026-10-30

Liberia's NaFAA secures US$325,000 FAO grant

The funds will support a new fish sales hub in Congo Town, Monrovia, with a cooling room and training.

The National Fisheries & Aquaculture Authority (NaFAA) in Liberia has received a US$325,000 grant from the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO). The grant, under the Technical Cooperation Programme, will fund the construction of an improved Fish Sales Hub in Congo Town, Monrovia.

The planned facility will include a 20-tonne cooling room, waste management systems, specialized delivery motorbikes, and smart-energy features. It will also provide training for fishmongers and include a small seafood grill. The initiative aims to improve market access for local fish producers and reduce post-harvest losses.

The grant will also assist the University of Liberia's Department of Fisheries and Aquaculture Sciences in developing a Small-Scale Fisheries Guideline, curriculum, and training materials. The Fish Sales Hub is intended to operate as a Public–Private Partnership.

Why it mattersLiberian artisanal fisherfolk, aquaculture farmers, and inland fisheries producers will benefit from strengthened market access and reduced post-harvest losses.

Health · 2026-10-30

Liberia Baptist Seminary opens conference on drug addiction

The Liberia Baptist Theological Seminary conference addresses drug addiction, recovery, and youth empowerment.

The Liberia Baptist Theological Seminary (LBTS) opened its Fifth International Theological Education Conference (ITEC) in Paynesville, Liberia, on October 30, 2026. LBTS President Rev. Dr. Prof. Momolu A. Massaquoi warned that Liberia’s future generation is at risk from drug addiction if the crisis is not urgently addressed.

The two-day conference, themed “Healing the Next Generation: The Theological Perspective on Drug Addiction, Recovery, and Youth Empowerment,” began at the Bradley D. and Carolyn Brown Fellowship Chapel. Keynote speaker Rev. Dr. Stephen L. Hawkins urged religious leaders to view addiction not just as a personal character failure but as a complex issue influenced by social and economic factors.

Hawkins, a sociologist of religion, highlighted hope, human dignity, and reintegration as guiding principles for addiction discussions. He cited examples from the United States, including New Brunswick, New Jersey, and New Orleans, Louisiana, to illustrate how poverty, mental health, and limited opportunities can contribute to substance abuse among young people.

Why it mattersLiberia's future generation faces risks from drug addiction if the crisis is not urgently addressed, according to the Liberia Baptist Theological Seminary.
