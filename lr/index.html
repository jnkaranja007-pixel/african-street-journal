Swiss court upholds Kosiah's 20-year war crimes sentence

Former ULIMO commander Alieu Kosiah will serve 20 years for atrocities in Lofa County during Liberia's civil war.

Switzerland's Federal Supreme Court has confirmed a 20-year prison sentence for former ULIMO commander Alieu Kosiah for war crimes committed in Liberia's Lofa County between 1993 and 1995. The ruling upholds the conviction for acts including killing civilians, rape, and using child soldiers, making it the first war crimes conviction by a Swiss civilian court and the first for a Liberian national for civil war atrocities.

The court acquitted Kosiah of crimes against humanity, a charge added by an appeals chamber in 2023, stating the relevant Swiss law was not in effect at the time of the crimes. This acquittal does not alter his 20-year sentence, the maximum allowed under Swiss law, nor his eventual expulsion from Switzerland. Alain Werner of Civitas Maxima, representing victims, welcomed the final conviction but expressed disappointment over the acquittal.

Kosiah, who settled in Switzerland in the late 1990s, has been detained since his arrest in November 2014 following a complaint by Liberian victims. Survivors traveled from Liberia to testify against him during the investigation and trials. The case was supported by the Liberian organization Global Justice and Research Project (GJRP), which has helped secure five convictions in six trials linked to Liberia's civil wars.

Why it mattersLiberians who suffered atrocities during the civil war have received final justice after a 30-year wait.

Health · 2026-11-01

Whein Town residents demand landfill closure

Residents near Monrovia's Whein Town Landfill face swarms of flies, rodents, and foul smells, fearing health risks.

Residents of Whein Town, near Monrovia, are enduring unbearable conditions due to the continued operation of the Whein Town Landfill. Swarms of flies, foul-smelling water, and the presence of rodents and snakes have made daily life difficult, with residents reporting increased costs for clean water and protection for their children.

Amos Moore, a resident, stated that the government has failed to honor its commitment to close the landfill after its original five-year lifespan expired, calling the situation a "shame" that inflicts suffering. Samuel Kofi Jones III, Secretary General of the Whein Town Community Leadership, confirmed years of complaints and protests against harmful activities at the facility.

Public health and environmental experts warn that poorly managed landfills exceeding their capacity pose serious risks to nearby populations. The Monrovia City Corporation acknowledges the landfill has outlived its usefulness and claims to be taking "immediate, aggressive action," but residents say dumping has not ended.

Why it mattersResidents of Whein Town are suffering from health hazards and increased living costs due to the prolonged operation of a landfill.

Liberia group slams Boakai-Koung over poverty, pay gaps

STAND demands probe into president's son amid claims of US$14,000 salaries for some officials.

The Solidarity & Trust for a New Day (STAND) has accused the Boakai-Koung administration of failing ordinary Liberians and allowing wide salary disparities within government. STAND stated that the government’s billion-dollar budget has not translated into meaningful relief for citizens struggling with poverty, poor public services, and economic hardship. The group also questioned why senior Central Bank of Liberia (CBL) officials reportedly receive substantial salaries and allowances while some lower-paid public workers face salary reductions.

STAND Chairman Mulbah K. Morlu Jr. said the government’s approach to public spending and accountability reflects a growing gap between senior officials' privileges and ordinary citizens' difficulties. The group cited reported monthly salaries of US$14,000 and US$15,000 in housing allowances for some senior CBL officials, contrasting this with a Governance Commission employee facing a pay cut from US$1,281.91 to US$900. STAND called on government bodies to explain the disparities and subject public-sector compensation to an independent review.

Beyond economic concerns, STAND reiterated its call for an independent investigation into drug trafficking allegations involving Joseph Boakai Jr., arguing that political and family connections should not influence legal application. The group demanded that any unlawful or improperly authorized decisions be corrected and responsible parties held accountable.

Why it mattersLiberians are struggling with poverty and inadequate public services while senior officials allegedly receive high salaries and allowances.

Education · 2026-11-01

Liberia enrolls 409 volunteer teachers on payroll

Monrovia Consolidated School System classes resume after government commits to address salary and welfare issues.

Academic activities have resumed at the Monrovia Consolidated School System (MCSS) after the Liberian government committed to resolving teachers' grievances. The government enrolled an additional 201 volunteer teachers, bringing the total formally enrolled to 409. Outstanding salaries are also being disbursed as part of the resolution.

MCSS Superintendent Dr. Augurie E. Stevens announced the resumption on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, thanking stakeholders for constructive dialogue. She stated that the enrollment addresses the plight of educators who served without full employment benefits. The decision follows recent protests by teachers and students over unresolved issues.

The government's commitment, supported by President Joseph Nyuma Boakai's administration, aims to find permanent solutions for public school teachers. Education advocates stress the need for sustained attention to prevent future disruptions. The priority for students and families remains uninterrupted learning and a stable education system.

Why it mattersStudents in Monrovia can now return to school after a dispute over teacher salaries and welfare disrupted classes.

Liberia receives 30 road machines for farming

Bong County's new heavy equipment aims to improve market access for farmers and rural families.

30 heavy earthmoving machines have arrived in Bong County, Liberia, to improve rural infrastructure. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Executive Director Dr. Emmanuel King Urey Yarkpawolo stated that these machines are intended for construction work that could connect farmers to markets and improve the lives of rural families.

Dr. Yarkpawolo, a native of Zota District, emphasized that passable roads are essential for food security and farm income, directly impacting rural households. He commended President Joseph Nyuma Boakai for placing roads at the center of his administration’s agenda, which he believes will alleviate transportation issues hindering agriculture and trade in Bong County.

The EPA chief explained that poor roads cause harvested crops like rice and cassava to spoil before reaching markets, leading to farmer losses. He added that improved roads can help children reach school and patients reach hospitals, and enable businesses to reach new customers.

Why it mattersFarmers in Bong County will benefit from improved road access to sell their produce and earn income, while rural families will see better access to food, education, and healthcare.
