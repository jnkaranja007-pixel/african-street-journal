Grebo leader urges calm after man found dead

Counsellor Kunkunyon Wleh Teh called for a swift investigation into Samuel Gekor's death near Zwedru.

A prominent Liberian lawyer and Grebo community leader, Counsellor Kunkunyon Wleh Teh, has called for calm and a swift investigation into the death of Samuel Gekor. Gekor, a young Grebo man, was found dead on a cassava farm near Zwedru in Grand Gedeh County after being missing for three days.

Teh expressed deep sadness and extended condolences to Gekor's family and the Grebo community. He urged residents to remain peaceful and law-abiding while the Liberia National Police and Ministry of Justice speed up their investigation into the disappearance and death.

The lawyer stressed that every Liberian deserves the right to life, safety, and dignity, and that no family should be left without answers. He cautioned against allowing grief and anger to lead to actions that could endanger lives or deepen divisions, stating that justice must be pursued through lawful means.

Why it mattersSamuel Gekor, a Grebo man, was found dead on a farm near Zwedru, prompting calls for a swift investigation.

Liberia seeks mining and energy investment

President Boakai to open a two-day conference in Margibi County attracting international investors and executives.

Liberia will host the Liberia International Mining and Energy Conference and Expo (LIMEC 2026) from October 28-29 in Margibi County. President Joseph Nyuma Boakai will open the event at the Farmington Hotel, aiming to attract foreign investment for the nation's mining and energy sectors. Government officials, international investors, and industry executives are expected to attend the two-day gathering.

The conference, themed “Transforming Liberia’s Economy: Driving Development Through Sustainable Mining and Energy Investments,” will focus on investment opportunities in mineral exploration, production, and renewable energy. Amara Kamara, president of the Liberia Chamber of Mines, stated the event aims to deepen ties with investors, showcase resources, and build partnerships that can create jobs and drive growth.

Key sponsors include Avesoro Resources, Huawei, and the Liberia Electricity Corporation. The event is organized by the Liberia Chamber of Mines and AME Trade Ltd., with support from the Ministry of Mines and Energy. Kamara emphasized the need for increased local participation and community benefits from these investments.

Why it mattersLiberia is seeking foreign investment for its mining and energy sectors to drive economic development and create jobs.

Liberia receives final Chinese farm machinery consignment

42 containers of agricultural equipment arrived at the Freeport of Monrovia to boost mechanization.

Liberia's Ministry of Agriculture received the final shipment of agricultural machinery donated by China on October 28, 2026. The consignment included 42 containers of equipment, with 15 containers being moved from the Freeport of Monrovia on the day of arrival. This delivery advances the government's plan to modernize farming through mechanization and increase national food production.

The machinery aims to transition Liberia's agriculture from traditional methods to more productive and commercially viable systems, according to the Ministry. Minister Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah and his team are pursuing initiatives to improve productivity, food security, and reduce reliance on imported food. The Chinese donation is expected to strengthen the country's mechanization capacity and farming efficiency.

This latest delivery highlights international partnerships, with China's contribution reflecting ongoing cooperation in agricultural development and technology transfer. The Ministry plans to ensure this modern equipment becomes accessible to farmers to boost domestic food production. Agriculture is central to Liberia's economic development, rural livelihoods, and national food security.

Why it mattersLiberian farmers will receive modern farming equipment to increase domestic food production.

Liberian Senate probes high port charges

Lawmakers summon port operators and government agencies over cargo clearing fees and delays at Freeport of Monrovia.

The Liberian Senate has summoned port operators and government agencies to explain high cargo clearing fees and long delays at the Freeport of Monrovia. Gbarpolu County Senator Amar M. Konneh raised concerns Tuesday about operational inefficiencies and high costs affecting importers, especially Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs). Containers have reportedly spent over 30 days at sea before berthing, with vessels diverted to neighboring ports due to inadequate space and dredging delays.

Senator Konneh stated these delays hurt business inventories, tie up capital, cause stock-outs, and impose heavy demurrage costs. He questioned opaque charges from APM Terminals, MEDTECH, and GTMS, arguing these costs increase commodity prices despite falling inflation. Konneh noted that port inefficiencies could undermine the government's macroeconomic stabilization program, discouraging investment and eroding purchasing power.

The Senate requested appearances from the National Port Authority, APM Terminals Liberia, MEDTECH, GTMS, Liberia Revenue Authority, Ministry of Commerce and Industry, and the Customs Brokers Association. A hearing is scheduled for next Tuesday to discuss port dredging, berthing capacity, storage limitations, fees, and congestion reduction measures. The summoned institutions have not yet responded to the allegations.

Why it mattersOrdinary Liberians face high commodity prices and reduced purchasing power due to port inefficiencies.

Liberia's House orders pay review after CBL nominee's salary revealed

Lawmakers demand transparency on executive benefits after a Central Bank nominee disclosed a US$9,000 monthly salary.

Liberia's House of Representatives voted unanimously on Tuesday to review government salaries and benefits following the public disclosure of a senior official's pay. The lower house instructed its committees on Ways, Means and Finance and Public Accounts to examine pay and perks for government officials and report back within two weeks. Representative Alex S. Noah of Sinoe County introduced the motion, arguing that ministers and heads of state-owned enterprises earn significantly more than lawmakers.

The review was prompted by Euphemia G.T. Swen-Monmia's confirmation hearing on October 22, where she stated her monthly salary is US$9,000, with an additional US$5,000 in cash benefits and a US$15,000 housing allowance. This amounts to approximately US$168,000 annually before housing costs. Lawmakers described their own compensation as "peanuts" compared to executive officials, with Representative Moima Briggs-Mensah of Bong County noting that a US$2,000 scholarship budget line often goes unfunded.

Communications from Representatives Sumo K. Mulbah and Musa H. Bility had previously called for transparency in public sector compensation, requesting details on officials earning over US$5,000 monthly. Mulbah argued that extensive allowances, including housing and external medical provisions, constitute an unsustainable drain on government funds. The House Plenary has forwarded these communications to the relevant committees for a report within two weeks.

Why it mattersLiberians are affected by the disparity in government salaries and benefits, which places an unsustainable burden on public finances.
