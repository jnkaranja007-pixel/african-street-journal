EPS Director Defends Charges Against Former Vice President Taylor

Executive Protection Service Director Sam Gaye stated that physical handling of narcotics is not required for drug trafficking charges, defending the prosecution of former Vice.

Executive Protection Service (EPS) Director Sam Gaye has publicly defended the government's decision to prosecute former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor on alleged cocaine trafficking links. Gaye, a member of the national security apparatus, asserted on his Facebook page that drug laws are structured to target individuals who facilitate or support trafficking networks, even without direct physical contact with narcotics.

Gaye cited the United States as an example, noting that American authorities use extraterritorial laws to prosecute foreign-based drug traffickers, requiring only "knowledge and intent" for drugs to be imported. This defense follows the Liberia National Police charging and forwarding Taylor to court in connection with an ongoing investigation into alleged cocaine trafficking and money laundering.

Police allege Taylor received approximately US$135,000 between 2022 and 2026 from individuals identified as members of a transnational cocaine trafficking network. The police charge sheet claims Taylor met with these individuals in Dubai, where discussions allegedly occurred about using Liberia as a transit point for cocaine shipments, and Taylor purportedly agreed to facilitate the arrangement.

Why it mattersThe ongoing drug investigation and the charges against a former vice president directly affect citizens by raising concerns about corruption, the rule of law, and the integrity of national institutions.

Former Warlords Back War Crimes Court in Liberia

Accused Liberian warlords are now among the strongest supporters of a War and Economic Crimes Court, seeking justice, closure, or to clear their names.

Seventeen years after the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC) recommended its establishment, a War and Economic Crimes Court in Liberia is gaining support from an unexpected group: some accused warlords. These former combatants are joining advocates in urging lawmakers to quickly pass legislation to establish the court, which they say will meet international standards.

Joshua Blahyi, known as “General Butt Naked” during the civil war, is a prominent ex-combatant who has expressed remorse for atrocities and now welcomes the court. He was one of only 30 perpetrators to confess to crimes during the TRC hearings, which heard over 20,000 testimonies. Blahyi stated that holding the court is essential for himself and the entire country.

Victims groups, such as the Liberia Massacre and Survivors Association, report an influx of ex-combatants, including former generals, who want to face justice and clear their names. This shift comes despite previous attempts by some accused perpetrators, like Senator Thomas Nimely, to block the court's formation.

Why it mattersLiberians affected by the civil war, including victims and accused perpetrators, are directly impacted by the potential for accountability and closure through the establishment of a War and Economic Crimes Court.

Liberia Airport Authority acquires nine vehicles, improves operational efficiency

The Liberia Airport Authority (LAA) has purchased nine new operational vehicles to enhance safety, security, and service delivery at Roberts International Airport (RIA) and James.

The Liberia Airport Authority (LAA) has acquired nine new vehicles, including two MGR-S5 Sport Utility Vehicles and seven ISUZU pickup trucks, to address long-standing transportation challenges. These vehicles will be deployed to key operational departments like Airport Security, Compliance, Electrical, and Base Support.

LAA Managing Director, Ernest R. Hughes, stated that the acquisition is a significant step towards modernizing airport operations and providing staff with necessary logistical resources. He noted that inadequate transportation previously constrained critical functions, including security operations, airside operations, and Fire and Rescue Services.

Hughes explained that the new fleet will strengthen operational capabilities, improve response times, and enhance the LAA's ability to maintain compliance with international aviation safety and security standards. The Port Managers of both airports will receive the SUVs to strengthen leadership oversight and coordination.

Why it mattersThis development affects airport staff by providing them with the necessary tools to perform their duties more effectively, and airport users by improving safety, security, and service delivery.

Tweh Vows Intensified Drug Trafficking Crackdown in Liberia

Justice Minister N. Oswald Tweh declared that the government will intensify its efforts against drug trafficking, promising no protection for individuals implicated by credible.

Justice Minister and Attorney General Cllr. N. Oswald Tweh announced an intensified crackdown on drug trafficking in Liberia, stating that the government is drawing a firm line against drug cartels and their collaborators. He emphasized that investigations and prosecutions will be guided by evidence, due process, and the rule of law, ensuring no individual is protected due to wealth, influence, friendship, or political connections.

Tweh cited recent major narcotics seizures as evidence of increased coordination and political commitment. The Liberia Drug Enforcement Agency seized over 400 kilograms of illegal narcotics and arrested more than 200 suspects in the first quarter of the year. Additionally, approximately 237 kilograms of cocaine were intercepted at Roberts International Airport in June, followed by the seizure of nearly four metric tons of cocaine in Duazon, which was the largest narcotics seizure in Liberia's history.

The Minister stressed that major seizures alone are insufficient, arguing that authorities must pursue the networks, financiers, and facilitators behind the illicit drug trade. He warned against limiting prosecutions to couriers and low-level dealers while influential individuals potentially connected to trafficking networks escape scrutiny, affirming that anyone implicated by credible evidence will face the law.

Why it mattersLiberian citizens are affected by the government's commitment to prosecute individuals involved in drug trafficking, regardless of their status, which could lead to a more secure society.

LFA enrolls first youth talents into national football academy

The Liberia Football Association, in partnership with FIFA, launched enrollment for the inaugural group of players into the Henry Nyuman Browne National Academy on Friday, August.

The Liberia Football Association (LFA) has begun enrolling its first group of young talents into the Henry Nyuman Browne National Academy. This new academy, established in partnership with FIFA's Talent Development Scheme (TDS), aims to provide elite athletic training alongside formal education and life skills for gifted boys and girls aged 10 to 16.

Guardians attended a briefing on the academic, residential, and athletic plans for their children before inspecting the George Weah Technical Center in Careysburg. This center will serve as the residential training hub where the young athletes will live, study, and develop their football skills.

LFA leadership stated that this initiative marks a new chapter for Liberian football, addressing historical infrastructure gaps by combining structured youth pathways with FIFA’s global development program. The association plans to identify and recruit young talents from all fifteen counties to foster future international success for national teams.

Why it mattersYoung Liberian athletes and their families are directly affected by the new academy, which offers a path for combining education and professional football training.
