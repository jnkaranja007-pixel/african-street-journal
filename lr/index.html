Liberia to lead UN Security Council in December

The nation, once under UN sanctions, prepares for its first presidency of the global body in 65 years.

Liberia will assume the rotating presidency of the UN Security Council in December 2026, marking its first time leading the body in 65 years. The government launched its "Road to the United Nations Security Council Presidency" on Monday, October 26, at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Foreign Minister Sara Beysolow Nyanti highlighted Liberia's transformation from a country under UN sanctions to one preparing to preside over international peace and security.

Minister Nyanti reflected on the nation's journey, noting that the Security Council had imposed sanctions on Liberia during its civil conflict, including restrictions on arms, diamonds, timber, and travel. She credited national resilience, diplomatic engagement, and the efforts of successive administrations for this achievement. The Minister also acknowledged the contributions of former foreign ministers and the Women of Liberia Mass Action for Peace.

Information Minister Jerolinmek M. Piah stated that President Joseph Nyuma Boakai is expected to preside over some Security Council deliberations in the United States. The government has planned diplomatic and commemorative activities, including a briefing for international partners on October 30. Liberia also co-sponsored a UN event on October 27, advocating for women's meaningful participation in peace processes.

Why it mattersLiberia's upcoming presidency of the UN Security Council offers a platform to influence global peace and security discussions.

Liberia's former president Ellen Johnson Sirleaf turns 88

The Nobel laureate peacefully transferred power in 2018 after a career marked by outspokenness and exile.

Ellen Johnson Sirleaf, Liberia's first female president and a Nobel laureate, celebrated her 88th birthday on October 29. She famously handed power to George Manneh Weah in January 2018, marking the first peaceful transfer of power between elected leaders in Liberia in 74 years. Sirleaf's departure followed her expulsion from her own party, and many citizens blamed her for unfulfilled promises.

Born in 1938, Sirleaf's family lore suggested she would achieve greatness. Her father was a Gola chief and legislator, while her mother was a teacher of Kru and German heritage. She studied at Madison Business College in Wisconsin and earned a master's degree from Harvard's Kennedy School. As finance minister in 1979, she warned of corruption and economic mismanagement under President William Tolbert.

Sirleaf faced political turmoil, including a prison sentence for sedition after criticising Samuel Doe's regime in 1985. She was later arrested again following a failed coup. She also served as a senator in Doe's rigged 1985 election but refused to take the seat in protest.

Why it mattersLiberians experienced a peaceful transfer of power in 2018, but many still felt unfulfilled promises from the outgoing administration.

Liberian Senate probes high port charges

Lawmakers summon port operators and government agencies over cargo delays and excessive fees.

The Liberian Senate summoned port operators and government agencies on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, to explain excessive cargo clearing fees and prolonged delays at the Freeport of Monrovia. Gbarpolu County Senator Amar M. Konneh raised concerns about operational inefficiencies and high transaction costs affecting importers, particularly SMEs. Containers reportedly spend over 30 days at sea before berthing, with vessels diverted to neighboring ports due to inadequate space and dredging delays.

Senator Konneh stated that these delays hurt business inventories, tie up working capital, cause stock-outs, and impose heavy demurrage costs. He also questioned opaque charges from APM Terminals, MEDTECH, and GTMS, arguing these costs increase consumer prices. Konneh noted that despite a 4.6 percent headline inflation rate and a strengthened Liberian dollar, consumer prices remain high, potentially undermining the government's macroeconomic stabilization program.

The Senate Plenary endorsed Konneh's request for institutions including the National Port Authority, APM Terminals Liberia, MEDTECH, GTMS, Liberia Revenue Authority, Ministry of Commerce and Industry, and the Customs Brokers Association to appear before the Committee of the Whole next Tuesday. The hearing aims to clarify port dredging status, berthing capacity, storage limitations, applicable fees, and measures to reduce congestion and costs. The summoned institutions have not yet responded.

Why it mattersOrdinary Liberians face persistently high consumer prices and reduced purchasing power due to inefficiencies and high fees at the Freeport of Monrovia.

Bishop Klayee launches book on leadership pain

The Jubilee Ministries International bishop's new 123-page book explores the hidden struggles of leaders in all sectors.

Bishop J. Allan Klayee of Jubilee Ministries International has released a new book titled The Bleeding Leader: Leading While Wounded in the Battle of Ministry and Life. The 123-page work, printed by Denva Books in Accra, argues that church cultures often fail to acknowledge the personal pain of leaders. The book will be launched this Saturday, October 31, at the Monrovia City Hall.

The book's introduction states that followers see the polished sermon and confidence but rarely the "bleeding" of leaders. Bishop Klayee, who wrote the book, insists its message extends beyond the church to "every leader in every sector." The foreword is by Bishop Nathaniel N. Zarway, Sr., who recommends it to leaders who have "shed tears in private while standing strong in public."

Across 12 chapters, Bishop Klayee addresses issues such as betrayal, loneliness, and the burden of carrying others' problems, drawing examples from biblical figures like Moses and David. He also critiques congregations for expecting protection from leaders rather than offering it to them, calling pastor protection "the most self-interested thing a community can do for its own future."

Why it mattersLiberian church leaders and other sector leaders are directly affected by Bishop Klayee's book which addresses their personal struggles while in public roles.

Liberia plans national mining company and sovereign wealth fund

President Boakai announced plans for state participation in mining and a fund for future generations at a conference in Unification Town.

President Joseph Nyuma Boakai announced plans to establish a National Mining Company of Liberia and the Liberia Sovereign Wealth Fund during an international conference in Unification Town, Margibi County. The president stated that Liberia aims to process raw materials domestically rather than exporting them as raw exports. He stressed that minerals and energy should support industrial development, create jobs, and improve public services.

The proposed National Mining Company will increase state participation through equity and strategic ownership, moving beyond just royalty collection. Liberia also aims to establish its first gold processing facility within two years, aligning with the African Minerals Strategy Group’s call for value addition before export. The Liberia Sovereign Wealth Fund will be an independent investment vehicle, directing a portion of mineral revenues into diversified investments for long-term financial security.

President Boakai also detailed plans to improve the energy sector through renewable generation, stronger transmission and distribution, and regional integration. He highlighted that unreliable electricity constrains businesses and limits services for essential facilities like hospitals and schools. The conference brought together over a thousand delegates from Liberia and around the world.

Why it mattersLiberia's plans for a national mining company and a sovereign wealth fund aim to increase state revenue and ensure long-term financial security for future generations.
