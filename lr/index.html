Agriculture · 2026-11-02

China donates 304 farm machines to Liberia

Liberia aims to cultivate 50,000 hectares of farmland with the new equipment.

Liberia received 304 agricultural machines from China to boost mechanized farming and expand cultivation. Agriculture Minister Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah stated the equipment is intended to increase domestic food production and support commercial farming operations. The donation follows engagements between the Liberian and Chinese governments and was officially handed over on October 30.

Minister Nuetah emphasized that farmers must actively use the machinery to achieve increased food production, calling the machines "opportunities for Liberians to engage in farming that will feed the nation." The government plans to partner with the private sector for the management and operation of the machines, rather than direct state control. This approach aims to ensure accessibility for individual farmers and investors while maintaining operational status.

The government's immediate goal is to develop 50,000 hectares of agricultural land. Nuetah noted that successful operation requires resources for fuel, maintenance, and skilled operators, stating, "The machine itself will not do the work." He urged farmers in beneficiary counties to utilize the services, warning that the investment's impact depends on farmer demand and utilization.

Why it mattersLiberian farmers and private investors will gain access to new machinery to expand agricultural operations and increase food production.

Politics · 2026-11-02

NPP chairman moves to strip Jewel Howard-Taylor of party leadership

Party delegates will elect a 'political leader' instead of a standard bearer at the December convention.

The National Patriotic Party (NPP) in Liberia plans to elect a "political leader" at its December convention, a move critics say will remove embattled Standard Bearer Jewel Howard-Taylor from party control. NPP National Chairman Atty. George Sylvester Mulbah Sr. stated that the position of political leader will not contest the presidency and will cease to exist after the 2029 elections. This decision is based on a 2016 Bomi amendment to the party's constitution, according to Mulbah.

Mulbah also declared that all current officials have lost their tenure, meaning new officers will be elected at the convention. This announcement follows complaints about the convention's delegation size, with Mulbah confirming that three delegates per district, totaling approximately 219 voting delegates, will attend. Critics within the party reject the plan, asserting that a "political leader" position is not in the NPP's constitution or by-laws, and that the Bomi amendment was never properly circulated.

This development deepens a crisis within the NPP, with some party insiders alleging that the convention's timing is intended to facilitate a takeover by Vice President Jeremiah Koung. A group called Concerned Partisans of the National Patriotic Party (CP-NPP) has called for the convention's postponement, citing a lack of sensitivity to Howard-Taylor's current drug trafficking and money laundering prosecution.

Why it mattersFormer Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor faces the loss of her party leadership position while also dealing with drug trafficking and money laundering charges.

Education · 2026-11-02

India offers Liberia 140 training slots

Liberian professionals will receive fully funded training in various disciplines for the 2026-2027 financial year.

India has allocated 140 professional training opportunities to Liberia for the 2026-2027 financial year through its Indian Technical and Economic Cooperation (ITEC) program. Indian Ambassador Manoj Bihari Verma announced this in Monrovia during the ITEC Day celebration. Approximately 600 Liberian professionals have previously benefited from these training programs.

The demand for training was high, with Liberia's initial 70 slots for 2025-2026 exhausted within six months, leading India to add 86 more slots. ITEC, established in 1964, offers fully funded training to developing countries, providing nearly 10,000 opportunities annually to over 160 nations. Deputy Foreign Minister Cllr. Jeddi Mowbray Armah thanked India for its investment in Liberia's human resource development.

This year, 24 Liberian students also received scholarships from the Indian Council for Cultural Relations (ICCR), including 20 for master's and doctoral studies. Specialized training has been provided to mining officers in occupational health and safety and to healthcare workers in nursing leadership. Personnel from the police, immigration, drug enforcement, revenue authority, and other government bodies have also participated.

Why it mattersLiberian professionals will gain specialized knowledge and skills through fully funded training opportunities in India.

Health · 2026-11-02

Liberia cancer survivors walk for early detection

Survivors shared recovery stories at the Liberia Cancer Resource Initiative's annual breast cancer awareness walk in Monrovia.

Survivors of breast cancer participated in an awareness walk in Monrovia on Saturday, the final day of October's Breast Cancer Awareness Month. Benejane Shaw, who underwent surgery on March 3, 2025, and chemotherapy, shared her recovery, stating, “I’m a living testimony that early detection saves lives.” The event was organized by the Liberia Cancer Resource Initiative (LCRI) to encourage women to get screened and break the stigma surrounding cancer.

LCRI Executive Director Christine E. Brooks-Jarrett described the walk as a "demonstration of solidarity and collective action," noting that cancer awareness needs to be a year-round effort. Dr. Deddeh Supuwood, an oncologist at the John F. Kennedy Medical Center who lost her mother to breast cancer, aims to help as many women as possible, stating, “When you save one woman’s life, you are probably saving a whole family.”

Lawyer and former presidential candidate Cllr. Tiawan Saye Gongloe linked the disease to governance issues, saying, "Corruption starts with C. Cancer starts with C." Participants enjoyed games and rice after the walk. Dr. Supuwood is seeking support to continue her studies.

Why it mattersLiberian women affected by breast cancer can find hope and support through early detection and treatment, as demonstrated by survivors and healthcare professionals.

Business · 2026-11-02

Liberian senator demands port operators explain delays

Senator Amara Konneh wants port operators and government agencies summoned to explain protracted delays and high costs impacting businesses.

Senator Amara M. Konneh has asked Liberia's Senate to summon port operators and government agencies to explain significant delays affecting cargo ships at the Freeport of Monrovia. This week, at least five cargo ships were seen waiting offshore for berths, impacting the import of essential goods like food, fuel, and building materials.

Konneh stated in an October 25, 2026 letter that importers and shipping agents report containers spend an average of over 30 days at sea before docking, with many vessels rerouted to ports in Côte d’Ivoire and Ghana. He cited a lack of yard space and unfinished port basin dredging as primary causes for the prolonged waits and increased transaction costs.

National Port Authority Managing Director Sekou Dukuly acknowledged the issues, stating, "We are working on dredging and expansion." Business owners warn that daily demurrage costs and other fees are passed on to consumers, raising prices in markets across Liberia.

Why it mattersLiberian businesses face increased costs and stock-outs due to shipping delays, which are ultimately passed on to consumers through higher market prices.
