China opens zero-tariff market to Liberia and 52 African countries

Eligible products from the 53 African nations that maintain diplomatic relations with Beijing can now enter China without import duties.

China has announced a zero-tariff policy covering Liberia and 52 other African countries, allowing eligible products to enter the Chinese market without applicable import duties. Chinese Ambassador to Liberia Yin Chengwu disclosed the policy at a media briefing at the Chinese Embassy in Monrovia on Wednesday, November 11. He said China has extended zero-tariff treatment to 63 countries in total, including 53 African nations that maintain diplomatic relations with Beijing.

The embassy pointed to two major Chinese trade fairs, the China International Import Expo (CIIE) and the Canton Fair, as places where African businesses can meet Chinese buyers. It said the ninth CIIE drew more than 4,100 overseas enterprises from 162 countries, regions and international organisations, and that free exhibition space for least developed countries rose by 17 percent. A matchmaking event for Asian and African food and agricultural products was also held to connect exporters with buyers.

The embassy encouraged Liberian businesses to use the zero-tariff arrangement and take part in international trade exhibitions. China remains a major trading partner of Liberia, with cooperation covering infrastructure, trade, healthcare and development assistance. Vice President Jeremiah Kpan Koung recently led a Liberian delegation to China for the opening of the ninth CIIE.

Why it mattersLiberian exporters of eligible products can now send them to China without paying import duties.

Liberia's climate finance gap delays projects

While US$213 million of US$573 million identified funding was disbursed by December 2024, key climate projects remain unfinished.

African leaders at the United Nations climate conference in Türkiye called for predictable climate finance, but Liberia faces a significant funding gap for its climate plan. The country had identified about US$573 million in financing needs, but only US$213 million had been disbursed by December 2024, leaving approximately US$360 million outstanding.

Key projects aimed at improving weather forecasting and protecting Monrovia from coastal erosion are delayed due to this funding shortfall. Experts argue that adaptation needs grants rather than loans, as debt repayments limit resources for public services. Questions remain over whether climate finance will reach vulnerable communities.

Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed warned that the cost of climate damage competes with investment in essential services like schools and healthcare. Other African nations, including Côte d’Ivoire, Seychelles, Rwanda, and Zimbabwe, also highlighted their struggles with climate finance and adaptation at the COP31 Leaders’ Summit in Antalya.

Why it mattersLiberia's climate projects, including a US$10 million Climate Information System and coastal protection, remain delayed due to a funding gap, impacting public services.

Senator seeks US$5M for Grand Kru solar power

Grand Kru County Senator Numene T. H. Bartekwa urged the Senate to allocate funds for solar electricity in five major communities.

Grand Kru County Senator Numene T. H. Bartekwa asked the Liberian Senate on Thursday, November 12, to allocate US$5 million in the Fiscal Year 2027 National Budget for solar electricity in five major communities. Bartekwa stated that Grand Kru is excluded from the government's electricity expansion program, which is active in the other 14 counties. He warned that this exclusion impacts residents and businesses.

The senator said county officials and the Rural and Renewable Energy Agency (RREA) are exploring solar power as a cost-effective solution. RREA estimates US$1 million per community for mini-solar grids, totaling the US$5 million request for Barclayville, Grandcess, Buah Geeken, Sasstown, and Garaway. RREA has assured readiness to build similar systems to the one in Totota, Bong County, if funding is secured.

Bartekwa argued that the lack of electricity contradicts the government’s ARREST Agenda for Inclusive Development. He noted that while other counties receive electricity infrastructure, Grand Kru residents rely on expensive generators. He appealed to Senate Plenary to direct budget committees to consider the allocation during budget deliberations.

Why it mattersSenator Bartekwa appealed to Senate Plenary to mandate budget committees to consider the US$5 million allocation for solar electricity in Grand Kru County.

Liberia charges seven people over attack on police officer in Sinkor

Officer Sedeke K. Kammara reportedly lost use of an eye and had his radio, cap and US$150 taken during the assault

The Liberia National Police has arrested seven people and sent them to court over the alleged assault of a police officer and obstruction of officers in Sinkor, Monrovia. The suspects are Yokie Johnson, Rachel Johnson, Obediah Yorkor, Anthony Hallie, Spencil Z. Gaye, Prince Sackie and Harris Clomo.

The police said Officer Sedeke K. Kammara was attacked by a group from the beachside while responding to a situation on 26th Street, behind ERA Supermarket, on Thursday, November 12. Two officers of the Liberia Drug Enforcement Agency were with him but reportedly left the scene during the confrontation. The attackers allegedly injured his right eye and took his police-issued radio, face cap and US$150 in cash.

The seven face charges of aggravated assault, hindering law enforcement, physical obstruction of government functions, theft of property and disorderly conduct under Liberia's Revised Penal Law. The suspects are presumed innocent until the prosecution proves the allegations in court. The police condemned attacks on officers and said those suspected of breaking the law will be held accountable.

Why it mattersOfficer Kammara reportedly lost a police radio and US$150 in the attack and must now face the court case against the seven suspects.

Benoni Urey named Liberia's seat on ECOWAS Business Council

The advisory body, chaired by Aliko Dangote, is meant to give the private sector a direct line to West Africa's presidents.

Benoni W. Urey, a Liberian businessman and founding shareholder of the mobile network Lonestar Cell MTN, has been named Liberia's representative on the ECOWAS Business Council. The council is a new advisory body chaired by Nigerian industrialist Aliko Dangote, Africa's richest man.

Ministers created the council on paper in 2021, but its launch slipped from December 2024 to September 2025. Dangote was named its first chairman by the bloc's foreign ministers in Abuja in December 2025, and West African heads of state later endorsed him.

Liberia trades heavily with Sierra Leone, Guinea and Côte d'Ivoire and imports almost all of its fuel. Whether the seat delivers cheaper fuel, easier border crossings and a bigger market for Liberian goods will depend on how hard its representative fights for a small country in the room.

Why it mattersLiberian businesses and fuel buyers depend on the council's decisions for cheaper fuel, easier border crossings and a bigger market for Liberian goods.
