Liberia pays £5.95 million to global maritime body

The payment makes Liberia the International Maritime Organization's largest financial contributor.

Liberia paid £5,946,633 to the International Maritime Organization (IMO) on November 6, 2026, reinforcing its position as the organization’s largest financial contributor. Maritime Commissioner Cllr. Neto Zarzar Lighe Sr. presented a symbolic cheque for the amount to IMO Secretary-General Arsenio Dominguez in London. This payment accounts for approximately 15 percent of the IMO’s annual operational budget.

Commissioner Lighe stated that the timely payment underscores Liberia’s commitment to the IMO and its programs. He emphasized that Liberia takes its obligations to the organization as a serious national responsibility, especially given its influence in the international maritime industry. Secretary-General Dominguez expressed appreciation for Liberia's prompt financial fulfillment and its active participation in the IMO’s technical working groups and council deliberations.

Dominguez also recognized Liberia’s contribution beyond finances, describing its representation as effective and highly engaged in advancing the IMO’s mandate. Liberia has consistently maintained an influential presence within the IMO, reflecting the country’s strategic importance to international shipping through its extensive ship registry. Liberia’s Permanent Representative to the IMO, Ambassador Robert Wilmot Kpadeh, accompanied Commissioner Lighe.

Business · 2026-11-08

Liberia's economy grew 5.1 percent in 2025

Despite strong economic growth, poverty increased from 34.6 percent to 36.1 percent of the population.

Liberia's economy expanded by 5.1 percent in 2025, the strongest growth in five years according to the World Bank. Inflation ended the year at 4 percent, and export earnings rose by nearly 58 percent, driven by gold and iron ore. However, the World Bank estimates that the proportion of Liberians living in extreme poverty increased from 34.6 percent in 2024 to 36.1 percent in 2025.

The economic growth was fueled by mining, with industrial output jumping 10.8 percent and overall mining output rising 17 percent. Sectors where most Liberians work, such as agriculture, saw slower growth of 2.6 percent, and manufacturing shrank by 2.8 percent. The government's 62 state-owned entities have finances that the government itself cannot reliably track, the report noted.

The World Bank expects poverty to decrease to 33.2 percent in 2026 if farming recovers. Fiscal numbers improved with the budget deficit narrowing to 2.1 percent of GDP, but spending on capital projects was reduced from US$406 million to US$297 million.

Why it mattersLiberians experienced increased poverty despite economic growth, and the finances of state-owned companies remain unclear.

Agriculture · 2026-11-08

China signals readiness to import Liberian farm goods

Beijing also encourages Chinese companies to invest in Liberian processing and manufacturing sectors.

China has signaled its readiness to increase imports of Liberian agricultural products, including cocoa, coffee beans, and dried chili peppers. Chinese Vice Premier Ding Xuexiang conveyed this commitment to Liberia’s Vice President Jeremiah Koung in Shanghai on November 5, following discussions on expanding trade and economic cooperation.

Vice Premier Ding stated that the next step for Liberian exporters is to submit and gain approval for eligible companies to access the Chinese market. He noted that these products already have market access under existing arrangements and encouraged Liberia to submit a list of interested exporting companies for approval.

Vice President Koung welcomed the opportunity, identifying rubber, cocoa, coffee, and other agricultural products as key areas for expanded trade. He expressed Liberia's desire for foreign investment to move beyond raw material export into domestic processing, skills development, and job creation, inviting Chinese businesses to explore special economic zones.

Why it mattersLiberian farmers and businesses stand to gain from increased export opportunities and potential investment in processing facilities.

Health · 2026-11-08

Liberia health minister accused of ignoring Sinje center fraud

National Polit-Bureau of Liberia says Dr. Louise Kpoto failed to act on internal report detailing missing funds and drugs.

The National Polit-Bureau of Liberia (NPBL) accused Health Minister Dr. Louise M. Kpoto on Thursday, November 5, 2026, of ignoring financial and administrative problems at the Sinje Comprehensive Health Center in Grand Cape Mount County. NPBL National Chairman Victor L. Balo stated that an internal Ministry of Health investigation from June 3–6, 2026, documented LD$3,705,120 in unbanked revenue and a US$20,583.59 financial discrepancy. The report also noted the disappearance of 646 patient care tablets, procurement fraud, and excessive billing.

Balo criticized the minister for five months of silence following the investigation, warning that inaction could erode public confidence in healthcare. He specifically targeted Dr. Sampson Wulu, the center's Medical Director, for accountability. The NPBL also highlighted concerns about maternal deaths and poor childbirth outcomes, calling for an independent clinical review with international partners. The organization alleged that investigators who authored the report face potential sanctions.

The NPBL demanded a public explanation from Minister Kpoto, a forensic audit, financial reconciliation, and investigations into missing pharmaceuticals, procurement, and billing. They also called for administrative action against Dr. Wulu and protection for whistleblowers. The civil society group warned that administrative lapses expose pregnant women and vulnerable patients to preventable medical risks.

Why it mattersLiberians using the Sinje Comprehensive Health Center face risks from alleged maternal deaths, poor childbirth outcomes, and administrative lapses.
