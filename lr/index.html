Boakai reintroduces civic education in primary schools

President Joseph Nyuma Boakai announced the reintroduction of civic education and standardized textbooks nationwide to strengthen understanding of Liberia's history and.

President Joseph Nyuma Boakai announced the reintroduction of civic education in primary schools and the launch of standardized civic textbooks nationwide, according to FrontPage Africa. This initiative aims to restore knowledge of the Constitution, history, and citizenship among young Liberians.

Speaking at the 179th National Flag Day celebration in Monrovia, President Boakai emphasized the importance of national identity and urged students to reject drugs, which he described as a serious threat to the youth. He also called on adults and public officials to lead by example, matching their words with actions.

The President's remarks highlighted that the flag represents more than a national symbol, embodying the country's identity, history, and aspirations. He connected the Flag Day observance to Liberia’s 179th Independence Anniversary, noting that nation-building requires the participation of all citizens.

Why it mattersThe reintroduction of civic education directly affects students by changing their curriculum and providing new learning materials about their country's history and laws.

First Lady launches reproductive health initiative in five counties

First Lady Kartumu Yarta Boakai has launched a school-based adolescent reproductive health initiative, "Her Voice, Her Future," targeting over 12,000 young people in five counties.

The initiative aims to combat teenage pregnancy and keep girls in school by establishing 50 Adolescent Reproductive Health Clubs in public and faith-based schools across Grand Bassa, Montserrado, Margibi, Bomi, and Nimba counties. These clubs will provide adolescents aged 10 to 19 with access to reproductive health information, counseling, and referrals to youth-friendly healthcare services.

Speaking at the launch in Buchanan, Madam Boakai emphasized that the program will go beyond identifying problems by offering practical support for adolescents to make informed choices. The clubs will serve as safe spaces for students to ask questions, receive reliable information, develop leadership skills, and interact with responsible adults. The initiative will also train peer educators and strengthen counseling systems.

Madam Boakai called for the involvement of parents, teachers, faith leaders, and communities in educating and protecting young people, urging parents to engage in open conversations about puberty, relationships, and personal safety. Officials from the Ministries of Health, Gender, and Education, along with county authorities and healthcare workers, attended the launch.

Why it mattersThis initiative directly affects young people aged 10 to 19 by providing them with crucial reproductive health education and support to help them stay in school and make informed decisions.

CBL backs LRA's five-year plan to raise domestic revenue to one billion

The Central Bank of Liberia supports the Liberia Revenue Authority's strategic plan to increase domestic revenue, emphasizing digital payment infrastructure for fiscal.

The Central Bank of Liberia (CBL) has endorsed the Liberia Revenue Authority’s (LRA) five-year corporate strategic plan, which aims to increase domestic revenue to the billion-dollar mark. James B. Wilfred, CBL Deputy Governor for Operations, stated that modern digital payment infrastructure is crucial for improving taxpayer compliance and strengthening Liberia’s fiscal sustainability.

The LRA’s plan, themed “Moving to Billion: Mobilizing Domestic Revenue for Fiscal Sustainability,” seeks to expand the government’s fiscal space. Wilfred noted that this revenue target is necessary for Liberia’s economic future and reflects the country’s determination to reduce reliance on external financing.

Wilfred stressed that achieving this vision requires strong institutions, effective leadership, technological innovation, and sustained efforts to improve taxpayer compliance. He added that effective domestic revenue mobilization needs collaboration among government institutions, taxpayers, the private sector, development partners, and financial institutions.

Why it mattersLiberian taxpayers and businesses will be affected by changes to payment processes and increased efforts to improve compliance as the LRA aims for a billion-dollar revenue target.

Adviser urges whole-of-government plan for UN Security Council presidency

Ambassador-at-Large B. Elias Shoniyin called for Liberia to establish measurable national objectives for its December presidency of the UN Security Council.

Liberia should adopt a whole-of-government approach to its upcoming December presidency of the United Nations Security Council, according to Ambassador-at-Large B. Elias Shoniyin. Speaking at the George W. Wallace, Jr. Ambassadorial Lecture Series, Shoniyin emphasized that the country needs to define clear national objectives and assess the lasting legacy of its one-month leadership role, rather than just tallying diplomatic activities.

Shoniyin stated that preparation for the presidency should extend beyond the Permanent Mission in New York and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. He proposed that the Ministries of Finance and Development Planning, Commerce and Industry, the National Investment Commission, sector ministries, foreign missions, academia, the private sector, media, and the diaspora all have roles to play in this national project.

He suggested a practical scorecard to evaluate the presidency, focusing on advancing peace-and-security priorities, elevating African positions, strengthening bilateral relationships, generating investment and development partnerships, and mobilizing diaspora networks. Shoniyin stressed that a successful presidency should leave Liberia diplomatically and institutionally stronger.

Why it mattersThis story is important to Liberians because it outlines a strategy for the nation to maximize its influence and secure tangible benefits during its upcoming leadership of the UN Security Council.

European Nationals Accused of Cocaine Trafficking in Liberia

Five European men, including two Croatians, a Ukrainian, a Serbian, and a Spaniard, are implicated in a multi-million dollar cocaine operation near Roberts International Airport.

Investigators have linked five European nationals to a large-scale cocaine trafficking operation in Liberia, according to a FrontPageAfrica investigation. The men, identified as Nikola Ivancic, Mihovil Vrovac, Taras Zadereiko, and two others, are accused of cultivating political access to facilitate drug transit through the country.

The investigation revealed that three of the men, Ivancic, Vrovac, and Zadereiko, had established themselves in Liberia, making connections in both government and local communities. This level of integration, investigators suggest, allowed them to operate without drawing immediate suspicion, making it easier to move South American cocaine destined for European markets.

The case, which involves an indictment of twelve individuals and ongoing court proceedings, highlights West Africa's role as a transit corridor for cocaine. Nikola Ivancic, described as a "Kingpin" by Croatian press, has a history of involvement in international trafficking networks dating back to 2005, with previous indictments for organizing a thirteen-member criminal network in Croatia.

Why it mattersThis event affects Liberian citizens by exposing how foreign nationals allegedly exploited local systems and relationships to establish a drug trafficking operation within their country.
