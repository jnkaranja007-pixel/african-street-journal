Liberian importers petition legislature over shipping delays

Business owners face seven-month cargo delays and rising port fees, petitioning for intervention.

Liberian business owners and importers gathered Tuesday at the Capitol Building in Monrovia to petition the Legislature and the National Port Authority over severe shipping delays and increased port fees. The groups, including China Business Women United with One Voice and the Consortium of Liberia Importers, are demanding urgent intervention as cargo shipments from Asia now take six to seven months to reach the Freeport of Monrovia.

Advocate Sriya Musu Momolu stated that these prolonged shipping timelines and unfair fees are pushing local enterprises to the brink of collapse. Businesses relying on bank loans fear losing personal homes and properties due to accumulating interest rates caused by the delays. The petition highlights that Delivery Orders arbitrarily expire before cargo can be retrieved, forcing owners to pay costly renewal fees and demurrage charges for delays beyond their control.

The House of Representatives has summoned key maritime and shipping stakeholders for a hearing on Friday, November 6, following the protest. Importers are also complaining about administrative surcharges and double taxation, as loan interest accumulates while they must still meet tax obligations to the Liberia Revenue Authority and pay salaries.

Why it mattersLiberian importers are facing financial distress and potential loss of property due to extended shipping delays and excessive port charges.

Liberia tells IMO seafarers must not be war targets

Liberia's Maritime Commissioner Neto Zarzar Lighe, Sr. voiced concern over attacks in key shipping lanes.

Liberia has urged the International Maritime Organization (IMO) that seafarers should not be targeted in ongoing conflicts affecting global shipping lanes. Maritime Commissioner and CEO Cllr. Neto Zarzar Lighe, Sr. stated this on Tuesday, November 3, at the IMO Council's 138th session, highlighting grave concerns over persistent attacks in the Black Sea, Arabian Sea, Red Sea, and other critical waterways. Thousands of seafarers and their vessels are currently trapped by military operations in these waters.

Commissioner Lighe stressed that seafarers are innocent civilians whose work is essential for international trade and the global economy, and they must not become casualties of geopolitical hostilities. He noted that these "unwarranted attacks" endanger crews, damage vessels, disrupt trade, and have tragically claimed lives. Liberia extended condolences to the families of seafarers killed in these incidents.

Liberia's plea carries significant weight as it is the world's largest ship registry, representing about 17 percent of the global fleet. Ships flying the Liberian flag have been directly affected by attacks, including the Liberian-flagged Express Rome struck on March 11 and the Minoan Pioneer hit by a projectile on August 3. The Liberian Maritime Authority has ordered its flagged ships to the highest security level following these events.

Why it mattersLiberia, as the world's largest ship registry, has a direct stake in the safety of seafarers and the security of international shipping lanes due to potential revenue loss and damage to its flagged vessels.

US investors propose pineapple farms and winery in Liberia

A trade delegation from Maryland, USA, plans to invest in large-scale pineapple farms and a winery, aiming to create over 350 jobs.

A trade delegation from Maryland, United States, visited Liberia and proposed investments in large-scale pineapple farms, agro-processing facilities, and a winery. The plans, disclosed on November 3, 2026, are expected to generate more than 350 jobs in the country's agricultural sector.

The delegation, including representatives from Clyopatra Winery and Libahna Produce, met with Liberia's Agriculture Minister Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah. Minister Nuetah welcomed the private sector participation, emphasizing the government's commitment to strengthening agricultural value chains and supporting smallholder farmers through off-taker arrangements and training.

The proposed investments align with Liberia’s National Agriculture Development Plan, which aims for food self-sufficiency by 2030. The delegation also expressed interest in supporting agribusiness development, with specific plans for modern machinery and processing facilities for export to the United States.

Why it mattersLiberians will benefit from over 350 new jobs created by US investment in pineapple farms and a winery.

Liberia and Japan sign 250 million yen food aid deal

The agreement will provide rice and support broader development objectives through sale proceeds.

The governments of Liberia and Japan signed a food assistance agreement valued at 250 million Japanese Yen on Monday, November 2. The deal reaffirms Japan's commitment to Liberia's food security and national development.

Ambassador Hiroshi Yoshimoto stated that the KR Food Aid Program remains an important component of Japan's Grant Aid Program. He noted that the funds generated from selling the rice will support Liberia's broader development objectives.

Foreign Minister Sara Beysolow Nyanti expressed appreciation for Japan's continued generosity and partnership. She stated that institutional reforms were undertaken to strengthen public institutions and ensure effective resource management.

Why it mattersLiberians will receive rice, a staple food, and proceeds from its sale will fund national development projects.

Agriculture · 2026-11-04

China donates over 300 farm machines to Liberia

The equipment includes tractors, tillers, and harvesters to boost food production and reduce reliance on imports.

The People’s Republic of China has donated over 300 modern agricultural machines and equipment to Liberia to increase food security and local production. The donation aims to provide farmers with modern tools to improve productivity, reduce manual labor, and increase the cultivation of crops like rice. The handover ceremony took place Friday at Camp Sandee Ware in Careysburg.

President Joseph Nyuma Boakai thanked China for its support, stating the donation is an important contribution to Liberia’s food self-sufficiency drive. He urged the Ministry of Agriculture to ensure effective distribution and maintenance across all 15 counties. Agriculture Minister Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah announced plans to expand mechanized services, building on 13 existing hubs.

A 40-day training program for 130 Liberians, including 27 women, is underway at the Central Agricultural Research Institute (CARI) in Bong County, with support from six Chinese specialists. The machinery includes tractors, tillers, transplanters, combine harvesters, and irrigation equipment, designed to assist in all farming stages from land preparation to post-harvest processing.

Why it mattersLiberian farmers will receive over 300 modern agricultural machines to improve crop yields and reduce their reliance on imported food.
