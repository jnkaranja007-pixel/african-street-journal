Former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor Faces Cocaine Trafficking Charges

A Writ of Arrest accuses former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor of years-long collaboration with a transnational cocaine trafficking ring and receiving tens of thousands of.

A newly obtained Writ of Arrest issued by the Monrovia City Court, dated August 25, 2026, orders police to arrest former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor on charges including importation and exportation of controlled drugs, unlicensed sale and trafficking, criminal conspiracy, criminal facilitation, and criminal solicitation. The writ alleges she received $45,000 from a cartel ring leader in Dubai and a further $75,000 for her Jewel Star Fish Foundation, with an additional $15,000 payment as recently as August 2026.

The charges stem from a broader investigation following a record cocaine seizure last month, valued at roughly $317–336 million, which implicated senior police officials. Justice Minister Cllr. N. Oswald Tweh announced formal charges on August 19 after security authorities stopped Taylor from leaving the country for a women’s forum in Accra.

The Coalition of Bong Eminent Citizens has called for Howard-Taylor’s immediate release, accusing authorities of violating her constitutional rights and warning against “political persecution.” The group demanded an independent investigation into her arrest and detention, emphasizing that the fight against illicit drug trafficking must adhere to Liberian law and ensure equal protection for all individuals under investigation.

Why it mattersThis event affects Liberian citizens by raising concerns about the integrity of high-level officials and the fairness of the justice system in the country.

US Congress asked to probe Liberia's escalating cocaine crisis

The Panoramic Justice Group has urged US lawmakers to investigate allegations of security sector compromise and sophisticated trafficking routes in Liberia.

The Panoramic Justice Group has called on the United States Congress to investigate Liberia’s escalating cocaine-trafficking crisis, citing allegations that elements of the country’s security sector may have been compromised by transnational criminal networks. In an August 24 letter to U.S. Congressman Christopher H. Smith, the organization urged congressional scrutiny of recent large-scale cocaine seizures and suspected maritime trafficking routes.

The group highlighted the seizure of approximately 237.6 kilograms of cocaine at Roberts International Airport in June, reportedly valued at more than US$19 million, and nearly four metric tons recovered in Duazon about six weeks later, reportedly valued at approximately US$317 million. These shipments, according to the group, raise questions about who financed, organized, owned, protected, and intended to profit from them, extending beyond the arrest of couriers.

Concerns were also raised over allegations involving personnel within Liberia’s security institutions, including the Liberia National Police, National Security Agency, and Liberia Drug Enforcement Agency. The organization stressed that while allegations do not establish guilt, the number of security personnel reportedly implicated, dismissed, suspended, or investigated warrants an independent examination of potential narcotics network penetration.

Why it mattersThe Liberian public is affected by the potential compromise of national security institutions and the unchecked flow of illicit drugs through their country.

European Nationals Cultivate Access in Liberia for Cocaine Trafficking

Five European nationals, including two Croatians, a Ukrainian, a Serbian, and a Spaniard, cultivated local connections in Liberia, allegedly facilitating a multi-million dollar.

Investigators report that five European men, some of whom are now indicted, established deep roots in Liberia, gaining access to government offices and local communities. This integration, according to investigators, provided the necessary groundwork for a large-scale cocaine trafficking operation, which was eventually traced near Duazon and Roberts International Airport.

The men, identified as Nikola Ivancic, Mihovil Vrovac, and Taras Zadereiko, reportedly lived in Liberia for years, building friendships and becoming fixtures in the community. A FrontPageAfrica investigation revealed that these three individuals also possessed Ghanaian passports, highlighting vulnerabilities in regional documentation systems that can be exploited by criminal networks.

This case illustrates how West Africa is used as a transit corridor for South American cocaine destined for European markets. Croatian syndicates, in particular, have demonstrated a practiced ease in navigating the region, not through covert smuggling, but by patiently cultivating political access to operate without needing to hide in the shadows, according to investigators.

Why it mattersThis event directly impacts Liberian citizens by exposing how foreign nationals can exploit local systems and relationships to establish criminal enterprises, potentially compromising national security and public safety.

Liberia projects US$1 billion domestic revenue for first time

Liberia's Finance Minister Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan announced the country is expected to reach US$1 billion in domestic revenue by September, a historic milestone.

Liberia anticipates crossing the US$1 billion mark in domestic revenue for the first time in September, according to Finance and Development Planning Minister Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan. This projected increase in revenue is expected to provide the government with additional resources for financing infrastructure, education, healthcare, and job creation.

Minister Ngafuan made the announcement during the National Oration for Liberia’s 179th National Flag Day celebration in Monrovia. He linked this revenue milestone to the government’s broader development agenda, emphasizing the need to translate increased domestic resources into tangible improvements for citizens.

The Finance Minister highlighted ongoing projects, including paving roads from Monrovia to Gbarnga, and from Bomi to Zorzor, Voinjama, Tappita, and Foya to Bo-Waterside. He also mentioned expanding electricity access, improving schools and healthcare, and creating jobs as part of the ARREST Agenda for Inclusive Development.

Why it mattersThis development affects Liberian citizens by potentially funding improvements in roads, electricity, education, healthcare, and job creation across the country.

Adviser urges whole-of-government plan for UN Security Council presidency

Ambassador-at-Large B. Elias Shoniyin called for Liberia to establish measurable national objectives for its December presidency of the United Nations Security Council.

Liberia should organize its upcoming December presidency of the UN Security Council as a national project involving all government ministries and sectors, according to Ambassador-at-Large B. Elias Shoniyin. Speaking at the George W. Wallace, Jr. Ambassadorial Lecture Series, Shoniyin emphasized that preparation should extend beyond the Permanent Mission in New York and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

Shoniyin argued that a successful presidency must leave Liberia diplomatically and institutionally stronger, cautioning against measuring diplomatic success solely by activities like meetings or announced agreements. He stated that "Activity is not impact," advocating for a focus on lasting national legacy.

He proposed a scorecard to evaluate the presidency, asking what peace-and-security priorities were advanced, which African positions were elevated, and how investment and development partnerships were generated. Shoniyin also highlighted the roles of the Ministries of Finance and Development Planning, Commerce and Industry, the National Investment Commission, academia, the private sector, media, and the diaspora.

Why it mattersLiberians are affected as the proposed whole-of-government approach aims to leverage the UN Security Council presidency for national benefit, including strengthening international reputation and generating investment opportunities.
