Liberia's agriculture minister says US$13.1 million budget falls far short

Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah says the proposed FY2027 allocation is US$55.9 million below the ministry's US$69 million request.

Liberia's Agriculture Minister, Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah, has voiced dissatisfaction with the ministry's proposed US$13.1 million allocation in the Fiscal Year 2027 national budget. The sum is US$55.9 million short of the US$69 million the ministry requested to expand agricultural services, recruit technical staff and support farmers nationwide.

Speaking on Tuesday, November 10, at the Ministry of Information's press briefing in Monrovia, Nuetah said the allocation is less than one-quarter of the request. He warned that some planned activities may not happen, and said the ministry is considering cost-sharing, under which farmers would contribute toward fuel and operating costs for government-provided Chinese machinery. Running one earthmoving machine needs about 50 gallons of fuel a day, roughly US$345 at the quoted price.

The ministry asked for the money in October to address shortages of agricultural technicians. The proposed US$1.53 billion national budget was submitted to the Legislature on November 6 and remains under review, leaving room for changes before it is passed.

Why it mattersFarmers would pay part of the operating costs of government machinery under cost-sharing the ministry is considering.

Politics · 2026-11-12

Liberian judges warn of court corruption

Circuit judges across Liberia used their court term openings to highlight systemic issues.

Liberian circuit judges have publicly voiced concerns about corruption, favouritism, and political interference within the nation's courts. During the November 2026 court term openings in various counties, judges used their formal addresses, known as charges, to warn that these issues are eroding public trust. This comes as the justice system faces criticism for delays and a lack of access for ordinary citizens.

In Zwedru, Grand Gedeh County, Circuit Judge J. Kennedy Peabody stated that filling state jobs with relatives and punishing dissenting staff constitutes corruption. He emphasized that no one, including judges, is above the Constitution. Meanwhile, Judge Roosevelt Z. Willie of Criminal Court ‘A’ in Monrovia questioned if Liberia had learned from past justice system failures, referencing a 2006 report on corruption and influence in the courts. Judge Peter W. Gbeneweleh in Grand Kru County cautioned against the 'commercialization of justice,' warning it leads to mob violence.

These warnings follow Judge Onesimus Banwon's August statement that Monrovia Central Prison, also known as South Beach, is dangerously overcrowded with pre-trial detainees. The Constitution mandates arrested individuals be brought to court within 48 hours, a rule often unmet. The release of two pregnant women from South Beach, reportedly aided by former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor, highlighted the plight of those awaiting trial, many without the means to expedite their cases.

Why it mattersLiberians caught in the justice system face delays and a lack of access to lawyers and judges, leading to overcrowded prisons.

Nimba University suspends student leaders over protest

Two campus political group chairmen suspended for one to two semesters after disrupting senator's visit.

Nimba University suspended two student leaders on November 12, 2026, for their alleged roles in a protest that disrupted a visit by Nimba County Senator Nya D. Twayen Jr. on October 9. Emmanuel B. Nenwon, chairman of the Students’ Liberation Party, received a two-semester suspension, while Princeton G. Quiah, chairman of the SUP-PROSA Alliance, was suspended for one semester.

The university administration stated Nenwon refused a directive to disperse an assembly, calling it disrespect to authority, while Quiah was accused of spreading false information and promoting instability. The protest reportedly centered on which student political group should receive donated solar lights and internet facilities from the senator. The standoff lasted over four hours, delaying the donation.

The university also suspended two other students, Darlington Fehn and Emmanuel Beondolo, for two semesters each, with Fehn reportedly found with a knife. Four other students received written warnings, and repairs are needed for the damaged campus gate. Senator Twayen confirmed the donation was put on hold indefinitely.

Why it mattersStudent leaders in Liberia face disciplinary action for disrupting a senator's visit and donation event.

Liberia drug agency heads test negative for drugs

Liberia Drug Enforcement Agency director general and deputies passed drug tests before Senate confirmation hearings.

Liberia Drug Enforcement Agency (LDEA) Director General Daniel Dee Ziankahn, Jr. and his two deputies tested negative for illegal drugs on Wednesday. The tests were conducted at the Wellness Health Center in Congo Town, outside Monrovia, and witnessed by journalists.

Ziankahn stated that officers fighting illegal drugs must set an example for the public through good character and discipline. He explained that undergoing the drug test before their confirmation hearing demonstrated the new leadership's commitment to upholding the laws they will enforce. Precious Rue and Prince Mulbah are the deputy directors for operations and administration, respectively.

The LDEA's strategy includes intelligence-led operations, drug prevention, institutional accountability, and partner coordination. This comes after public criticism of the agency's effectiveness and leadership changes following the suspension of former Officer-in-Charge Fitzgerald Biago amid cocaine seizures.

Why it mattersThe confirmation of the LDEA leadership affects the government's ability to combat illegal drug trafficking.

Liberia wins African backing ahead of UN Security Council presidency

Ambassadors from 17 countries pledged regional support as Liberia readies to chair the 15-member Council in December 2026.

Ambassadors from 17 countries have pledged regional backing as Liberia prepares to assume the rotating Presidency of the United Nations Security Council in December 2026. The pledges were made at an inaugural Diplomatic Breakfast Meeting hosted by the Liberian Embassy in Accra, Ghana, on November 9, at the Labadi Beach Hotel.

Liberia's Ambassador to Ghana, Musu Jatu Ruhle, told the envoys that the Boakai administration, under Foreign Affairs Minister Sara Beysolow Nyanti, is making preparations to discharge the role effectively. The ambassadors, from Sierra Leone, Nigeria, Rwanda, Kenya and South Africa among others, urged President Joseph Nyuma Boakai to advance Africa's aspirations on conflict resolution, peacebuilding and reform of international institutions.

Liberia's priorities include greater participation of women and young people in conflict prevention, the links between climate change, natural resources and conflict, and security cooperation in West Africa and the Sahel. Ruhle said Liberia's own recovery from conflict would inform its approach at the Council.

Why it mattersLiberia's seat on the Council puts its priorities on women, youth, climate and West African security onto the agenda of a body that decides on peacekeeping.
