Judge Fails to Rule on Jewel Howard Taylor's Health Leave Request

Former Vice President Jewel Howard Taylor's lawyers left court frustrated as the judge did not appear to deliver a decision on her compassionate leave request.

Monrovia – A ruling on former Vice President Jewel Howard Taylor's request for compassionate leave was not delivered on Thursday, September 3, as the presiding judge of Criminal Court “C” failed to appear. Taylor's defense attorneys, including Cllr. Cabina Ja’neh, Cllr. Finley Kangar, and Cllr. Lavala Superwood, waited at the Temple of Justice from approximately 3:00 p.m. until 6:00 p.m. for the judge's arrival and the anticipated decision.

Prosecutors were present earlier in the day but reportedly departed, while Taylor's legal team remained in anticipation. The lawyers eventually left the courthouse visibly frustrated by the absence of a ruling and the judge. The delay means Taylor's legal and medical status remains unchanged as she continues to be detained while facing criminal allegations from the Liberian Government.

Taylor's defense has argued that her health condition necessitates compassionate leave and access to medical treatment. The government has brought serious criminal allegations against her, which her lawyers maintain are politically motivated. The court's inaction leaves the former vice president's immediate future uncertain, pending further judicial proceedings.

Why it mattersFormer Vice President Jewel Howard Taylor is affected because her request for medical leave while facing criminal charges remains undecided.

President Boakai Replaces Justice Minister Amid Cocaine Probe

Liberia's President Joseph Boakai removed Justice Minister Oswald Tweh and Solicitor General Augustine Fayiah from their posts on Wednesday.

President Joseph Nyuma Boakai has dismissed Justice Minister and Attorney General Cllr. N. Oswald Tweh and Solicitor General Cllr. Augustine Fayiah. The Executive Mansion announced the leadership change Wednesday, appointing Cllr. Betty M. Lamin-Blamo as the new Justice Minister and Cllr. Abrahim Boimah Sillah, Sr. as Solicitor General. The shake-up occurs as the Ministry of Justice leads the investigation and prosecution of suspects linked to two major cocaine seizures with an estimated street value exceeding US$336 million.

The Executive Mansion stated the removals are part of President Boakai’s "ongoing efforts to strengthen key institutions of Government and enhance the effective delivery of public services." The statement did not directly link the dismissals to the ongoing cocaine investigation, though Tweh and Fayiah were publicly associated with the government’s legal handling of the cases. The outgoing officials are to be reassigned to other government positions, though specific roles were not immediately detailed.

The changes come at a critical juncture for the government, which faces the challenge of prosecuting several high-profile narcotics cases, including charges against former Vice President Jewel Howard Taylor for alleged drug trafficking and money laundering. Analysts suggest the leadership transition could impact the ongoing legal proceedings and investigations into the massive drug consignments.

Why it mattersThe removal of the Justice Minister and Solicitor General affects the government's ability to prosecute ongoing high-profile drug trafficking cases.

Ten Indicted Over 200kg Cocaine Haul, Bribe Plot

A grand jury has indicted ten individuals in connection with the alleged trafficking of 200 kilograms of cocaine through Roberts International Airport.

Ten defendants have been indicted by a Montserrado County Grand Jury in connection with the alleged trafficking of approximately 200 kilograms of cocaine through Roberts International Airport (RIA). The indictment, filed in the August Term of Criminal Court “C,” includes charges of unlicensed possession, importation, sale, and illicit trafficking of controlled substances, along with criminal conspiracy, facilitation, and money laundering. The shipment was allegedly concealed within six boxes falsely declared as Maggi cubes and lappas.

Prosecutors allege that a logistics manager arranged for the boxes to be shipped via Brussels Airlines to London, with the consignee listed in Birmingham, England. The plan reportedly began to unravel when personnel detected a weight discrepancy. The cargo proceeded through airport scanning, where an officer raised an alarm due to blurred contents. A subsequent physical inspection revealed a white powdery substance later identified as cocaine.

The indictment further alleges that one of the proprietors of Private Bar Entertainment Center attempted to bribe law enforcement officers with US$100,000 to recover the seized shipment. He is accused of giving US$5,000 to an LDEA Special Agent at his office as an inducement to facilitate the drug recovery. Investigators claim the individual stated he owned 25 percent of the drugs and was determined to retrieve them.

Why it mattersTen individuals are facing multiple charges related to drug trafficking and bribery, impacting the integrity of airport operations and law enforcement.

SUNU Assurances Opens New Monrovia Office

The insurance provider's new Sinkor location aims to improve customer access and service delivery in Liberia.

SUNU Assurances Liberia Limited has opened a new office at 13th Street, Sinkor, Monrovia. The company stated this move signifies its commitment to growth and providing accessible, professional insurance services in Liberia. The opening event, an Open House, was attended by representatives from the Central Bank of Liberia, the Liberia Insurance Association, brokers, clients, and business partners.

The new premises are designed to enhance customer experience and service delivery, offering a more accessible location for clients. Managing Director/CEO Habib Dia described the relocation as a step toward becoming closer and more responsive to customers. He reaffirmed the company's dedication to offering reliable and innovative insurance solutions for individuals, businesses, and properties.

Dia expressed gratitude to clients, regulators, brokers, partners, and the community for their ongoing support, emphasizing these relationships are crucial for SUNU Assurances Liberia's success. As part of the SUNU Group, the company aims to grow with Liberia, delivering excellent customer service, managing risks professionally, and providing peace of mind through its protection services.

Why it mattersBusinesses and individuals in Liberia can access improved insurance services and protection for their assets.

United Methodist Bishops Convene in Liberia

The president of the Council of Bishops, Ruben Saenz Jr., arrived to lead discussions on the church's mission and sustainability in Africa.

Bishop Ruben Saenz Jr., president of The United Methodist Church’s Council of Bishops, has arrived in Liberia for the 2026 Africa College of Bishops Meeting. Hosted by the Liberia Episcopal Area, the gathering brings together active and retired bishops from across the continent. The meeting is set to discuss the Church’s mission, leadership, and ministry, under the theme “Deepening Ecumenical Engagement, Expanding Continental Influence and Strengthening Institutional Sustainability.”

The Liberia Episcopal Area welcomed Bishop Saenz, emphasizing his participation as a significant element of the event. Organizers stated that the meeting will address critical issues impacting The United Methodist Church in Africa. Discussions will focus on enhancing institutional sustainability, fostering unity, improving leadership, and promoting collaboration throughout the continent.

The gathering's biblical text is Isaiah 54:2, "Enlarge the space of your tent." Bishop Saenz’s presence is also anticipated to cultivate stronger connections between African church leaders and the global United Methodist community. The discussions aim to find ways to strengthen the church's presence and impact across Africa.

Why it mattersUnited Methodist bishops from across Africa are meeting in Liberia to discuss the church's mission, leadership, and sustainability on the continent.
