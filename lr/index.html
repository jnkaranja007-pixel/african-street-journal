Liberia faces 323 more maternal deaths from funding cuts

US$2.4 million in reproductive health support lost since 2025, risking access to contraception for 327,700 women.

Liberia could see 323 additional maternal deaths each year if reproductive health funding cuts continue, a Guttmacher Institute report states. The country has lost at least US$2.4 million in support since 2025, risking access to contraception for an estimated 327,700 women and couples annually. These reductions follow the withdrawal of United States health support in early 2025 and Sweden's decision to end development assistance.

The Guttmacher report, released in November 2026, projects over 107,600 unintended pregnancies and 37,800 unsafe abortions annually due to the funding gaps. US family planning disbursements dropped 34 percent, with support for 2026 nearing zero. Sweden, Liberia's second-largest development partner, phased out its 2021-2025 cooperation strategy which included reproductive health programs.

Adolescent girls face growing risks, with potential for 22,400 additional unintended pregnancies and 9,300 unsafe abortions yearly. The report warns these outcomes could hinder girls' education and their contribution to national development. Before the cuts, over half of adolescent girls wanting to avoid pregnancy were not using modern contraception.

Why it mattersLiberian women and adolescent girls face increased risks of maternal deaths, unintended pregnancies, and unsafe abortions due to reduced funding for reproductive health services.

Liberia launches clean cities initiative in 12 locations

President Boakai's administration aims to improve sanitation and create jobs for over 1,250 young people.

The Liberian government has launched President Joseph Nyuma Boakai Sr.'s Clean Cities Initiative across 12 cities and Schieffelin Township. The program aims to tackle sanitation issues and provide short-term employment for more than 1,250 young people. Deputy Minister of State for Presidential Affairs, Bill McGill Jones, officially started the initiative on Saturday in Gbarnga.

This nationwide rollout involves eight counties, including Montserrado, Nimba, and Bong. The initiative is designed as a community-driven approach to environmental cleanliness and waste management. 30 community-based organizations will also participate in sanitation activities and awareness campaigns.

The government stated the initiative is part of a broader commitment to improving living conditions and offering opportunities for youth contribution to national development. The rollout is intended to establish a coordinated national response to sanitation challenges affecting urban communities.

Why it mattersOver 1,250 young Liberians will gain short-term employment through the Clean Cities Initiative.

Business · 2026-11-09

Liberia proposes record $1.538 billion budget

The proposed 2027 budget is the largest in the country's history, while extreme poverty also increased.

Liberia's government has proposed a US$1.538 billion budget for 2027, the largest in the nation's history, presented to lawmakers on Friday. This comes as the Liberia Revenue Authority reported crossing US$1 billion in domestic collections for the first time in September.

Finance Minister Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan pointed to 5.1 percent economic growth last year and controlled inflation, stating Liberia is improving though not yet where it aims to be. However, the World Bank's November 3 Liberia Economic Update found that extreme poverty grew alongside the economy.

Opposition leader Alexander Cummings has criticized the government's focus on future benefits over immediate needs, while the Citizens Movement for Change questioned the Finance Ministry's credibility due to audit gaps and unreconciled records with the LRA and Central Bank.

Why it mattersLiberians face a paradox where the national budget and revenue collections reach record highs, yet extreme poverty has also increased.

Business · 2026-11-09

DStv slashes subscription prices in Liberia

Satellite TV provider DStv reduces its Ultimate package from US$37 to US$7 monthly, making it more accessible.

Satellite television provider DStv has significantly reduced its subscription prices in Liberia, with the Ultimate package now costing US$7 monthly, down from US$37. The changes, which took effect on November 5, 2026, also include a simplified package structure and expanded entertainment options across Africa.

Cynthia Awuor Joel, Digital Marketing Specialist at MultiChoice, stated that the price cuts were a direct response to customer feedback about DStv being perceived as too expensive. She explained that the company aims to reposition the brand as more affordable and consumer-friendly, making satellite television accessible to a wider Liberian audience.

The new DStv structure features four packages: Starter (US$7), Select (US$13), Sports, and Ultimate, replacing previous arrangements. The Starter package offers telenovelas, Africa Magic, Cartoon Network, and a weekly football match, while Select adds more football coverage like La Liga, additional cartoons, and movies.

Why it mattersLiberian households and businesses can now access satellite television entertainment at lower monthly costs.
