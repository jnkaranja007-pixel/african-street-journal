Swiss court upholds Kosiah's 20-year war crimes sentence

Former ULIMO commander Alieu Kosiah will serve 20 years for atrocities in Lofa County during Liberia's civil war.

Switzerland's Federal Supreme Court has confirmed a 20-year prison sentence for former ULIMO commander Alieu Kosiah for war crimes committed in Liberia's Lofa County between 1993 and 1995. The ruling upholds the conviction for acts including killing civilians, rape, and using child soldiers, making it the first war crimes conviction by a Swiss civilian court and the first for a Liberian national for civil war atrocities.

The court acquitted Kosiah of crimes against humanity, a charge added by an appeals chamber in 2023, stating the relevant Swiss law was not in effect at the time of the crimes. This acquittal does not alter his 20-year sentence, the maximum allowed under Swiss law, nor his eventual expulsion from Switzerland. Alain Werner of Civitas Maxima, representing victims, welcomed the final conviction but expressed disappointment over the acquittal.

Kosiah, who settled in Switzerland in the late 1990s, has been detained since his arrest in November 2014 following a complaint by Liberian victims. Survivors traveled from Liberia to testify against him during the investigation and trials. The case was supported by the Liberian organization Global Justice and Research Project (GJRP), which has helped secure five convictions in six trials linked to Liberia's civil wars.

Why it mattersLiberians who suffered atrocities during the civil war have received final justice after a 30-year wait.

Liberia court stalls corruption case over $1.7 million fund

Rural women traders in River Cess County express anger as officials allegedly stole money meant for their empowerment.

Rural women traders in Liberia’s River Cess County are angry after officials from the Weah administration allegedly stole US$1.7 million meant for their empowerment. The funds, part of the 2023 Vulnerable Small Business Support Program, were disbursed but never reached the intended beneficiaries. The case has stalled in court with no trial date set.

Seven people, including former Commerce Minister Mawine G. Diggs and officials from the Liberia Marketing Association, were indicted in April by Liberia’s Asset Recovery and Property Retrieval Task Force. Court filings from June stated US$493,000 and LRD 190 million were used for personal gain instead of reaching vulnerable rural women. Mission Mai Sam, a mother of eight, said the stolen money could have helped expand farms and keep children in school.

This case is the latest test for President Joseph Boakai’s anti-corruption drive, which established a Task Force in March 2024. The Task Force has indicted over 60 cases but secured only one conviction. In a separate case, eight defendants were found guilty in the Saudi Rice corruption trial over diverted rice valued at US$500,000.

Why it mattersRural women traders in River Cess County are directly affected by the alleged theft of US$1.7 million intended to help them expand their farms and support their children's education.

Liberia receives 30 road machines for farmers

Heavy equipment aims to improve access to markets for agricultural produce in Bong County.

30 heavy earthmoving machines have arrived in Bong County, Liberia, to improve road infrastructure. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Executive Director Dr. Emmanuel King Urey Yarkpawolo stated that these machines are intended to connect farmers to markets and improve rural livelihoods.

Dr. Yarkpawolo emphasized that passable roads are fundamental to food security and farm income, directly impacting rural households. He commended President Joseph Nyuma Boakai for prioritizing road development, noting that the new equipment could alleviate transportation issues that have long hindered agriculture and trade in the county.

The EPA chief explained that poor roads cause harvested crops like rice, cassava, vegetables, and plantains to rot before reaching buyers, leading to farmer losses. Beyond agriculture, he added, better roads will help children reach schools and patients access clinics more reliably.

Why it mattersFarmers in Bong County will benefit from improved road access to sell their produce and earn income, and rural families will have better access to schools and healthcare.

Health · 2026-10-31

Whein Town residents demand landfill closure

Residents of Whein Town, Liberia, are calling for the permanent closure of the local landfill due to health concerns and unfulfilled government promises.

Residents of Whein Town, Liberia, are demanding the closure of the Whein Town Landfill, which has exceeded its intended lifespan and is causing significant health and environmental issues. Swarms of flies, foul-smelling water, rodents, and snakes plague the community, making outdoor life unbearable and increasing the cost of basic necessities like clean water.

Amos Moore, a resident, stated that the government has failed to honor its commitment to close the landfill after its initial five-year term, calling the situation a "shame" and accusing authorities of abandoning the community. Samuel Kofi Jones III, Secretary General of the Whein Town Community Leadership, confirmed years of complaints and protests against harmful activities at the facility.

Public health and environmental experts warn that poorly managed landfills pose serious risks to nearby populations, especially when exceeding capacity. The Monrovia City Corporation, which manages the landfill, acknowledged it has outlived its usefulness and claims to be taking "immediate, aggressive action," but residents say the dumping continues.

Why it mattersResidents of Whein Town are suffering from health risks and environmental degradation due to the prolonged operation of a landfill.

Education · 2026-10-31

Liberia resumes classes after teacher pay deal

Monrovia Consolidated School System enrolls 409 volunteer teachers on payroll and begins salary disbursements.

Academic activities have resumed at the Monrovia Consolidated School System (MCSS) after the government agreed to address teachers' grievances about employment and pay. The resolution follows protests that disrupted teaching and learning in Monrovia's public schools.

MCSS Superintendent Dr. Augurie E. Stevens announced on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, that 201 more volunteer teachers have been added to the national payroll, bringing the total to 409. Outstanding salaries are also being processed as part of the agreement.

Dr. Stevens thanked President Joseph Nyuma Boakai's administration for its commitment to finding lasting solutions for public school teachers. The government must now fulfill these promises to ensure uninterrupted learning and a stable education system for students.

Why it mattersStudents can now return to school and continue their education after a period of disruption due to unresolved teacher employment and salary issues.
