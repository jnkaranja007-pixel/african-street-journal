Liberia police suspend four officers for misconduct

One officer faces criminal investigation for alleged assault, corruption, and gross insubordination.

The Liberia National Police (LNP) suspended four officers without pay between October 28 and 29 for alleged disciplinary violations. Patrolmen Thomas W. Woart, Roderick Samukai, John V. Roques, and Melvin K. Jappah were disciplined for actions including assault, excessive force, negligence of duty, corruption, and insubordination.

Patrolman Woart received an indefinite suspension without salary and was referred for criminal investigation. Patrolman Samukai was suspended for two months without pay for negligence of duty. Patrolmen Roques and Jappah each received six-month suspensions without pay for alleged corruption and other violations.

The LNP stated these actions are part of efforts to enforce professional standards and accountability within the force. The affected officers must surrender government-issued properties and retain the right to appeal the disciplinary measures.

McGill claims CDC would have won 2023 election

Former minister Nathaniel McGill stated the ruling Unity Party would not have won the 2023 presidential elections if he had remained in government.

Former Minister of State for Presidential Affairs Nathaniel McGill stated that the ruling Unity Party (UP) would not have won the 2023 presidential elections had he remained in government. McGill, now a senator for Margibi County, asserted his continued grassroots support, citing his election to the Senate despite United States sanctions. He claimed the CDC possesses the political strength and personnel to reclaim the presidency, stating, “We have men, we have soldiers in the CDC. And we are coming back.”

McGill also challenged the current administration's development record, claiming projects like the Roberts International Airport (RIA) Road Project and an overpass bridge agreement were initiated under the former CDC-led government. He argued that the diagnostic laboratory at the 14 Military Hospital was also supposed to be dedicated under his tenure. McGill stated that the current government has not commissioned a single road project, despite the CDC completing more.

Separately, Lofa County Senator Joseph N. Jallah cautioned opposition parties against forming alliances solely to defeat the UP in 2029, urging focus on national development. Jallah stated that Liberia needs political cooperation built on shared principles and a clear development agenda, not just a desire to remove an incumbent. He advised that being the governing party does not grant ownership of the country's political sentence.

Why it mattersFormer Minister Nathaniel McGill's statements and Senator Joseph N. Jallah's remarks indicate ongoing political maneuvering and debate over governance and development priorities within Liberia.

Liberia to register 140,000 migrants

Government launches biometric exercise for foreign nationals, primarily from Burkina Faso, due to security concerns.

Liberia will begin registering approximately 140,000 foreign nationals on November 4, 2026, in an effort to manage undocumented migration and bolster border security. The exercise, coordinated by the Liberia Refugee Repatriation and Resettlement Commission (LRRRC), the Liberia Immigration Service (LIS), and the National Identification Registry (NIR), aims to create a database of foreigners in the country.

LRRRC Executive Director Cllr. Jeror Cole Bangalu announced the initiative on November 2, stating it was directed by President Joseph Nyuma Boakai. The registration will occur in Grand Gedeh, Nimba, River Gee, and Maryland counties from November 5 to December 31, 2026, following a launch in Grand Gedeh County. This action follows Bangalu's June 4, 2026, report to the President on the presence of 140,000 Burkinabè nationals in southeastern Liberia.

The government's concerns stem from the large number of Burkinabè nationals, many reportedly involved in agriculture and mining, and the challenges of monitoring them in remote areas. The biometric exercise seeks to identify individuals, their locations, and immigration status to improve national identity management and enforce immigration laws.

Why it mattersThe registration exercise directly affects foreign nationals residing in Liberia, particularly those from Burkina Faso, by requiring them to provide biometric data for identification and status verification.

Liberia senator demands speedy trial over US$425,000 equipment deal

Gbarpolu County citizens call for Superintendent Sam K. Zinnah's recusal amid allegations of financial misconduct.

Gbarpolu Senator Amara Konneh is demanding a swift conclusion to a two-year-old court case involving US$425,000 in development funds for earth-moving machines. Concerned citizens are also calling for the recusal of Gbarpolu County Superintendent Sam K. Zinnah due to alleged mismanagement and procurement irregularities.

The case centers on the purchase of three heavy-duty machines, including a bulldozer and an excavator, intended for infrastructure projects. The Liberia Anti-Corruption Commission (LACC) indictment alleges that refurbished or substandard equipment was supplied instead of new machines, with payments made through electronic checks totaling LRD 80,750,000.

Several county officials and the vendor, City Lion Inc., are implicated in the proceedings, which have stalled for over two years. Senator Konneh has urged the Ministry of Justice to expedite the trial, stating that the people of Gbarpolu deserve accountability for their development resources.

Why it mattersGbarpolu County citizens are affected by alleged financial misconduct and procurement irregularities in the use of US$425,000 in development funds for equipment.

Liberian businesswomen petition legislature over import delays

Traders cite three to six-month shipping times and high bank charges as threats to local enterprises.

Liberian businesswomen plan to petition the Legislature on Capitol Hill on Tuesday, November 3, to seek intervention against challenges threatening local businesses. The group, the Consortium of China Importing Businesswomen United, highlighted prolonged import delays, high bank charges, excessive taxation, weak enforcement of the Liberianization Policy, and unfair foreign competition.

Shipments from China now take three to six months to reach Liberia, tying up capital and disrupting operations, according to the businesswomen. They also complained about high bank interest rates and short loan repayment periods, urging the Central Bank of Liberia to mandate a minimum 90-day grace period for loan repayments.

The traders called for a review of the tax regime due to rising shipping costs and rents, and demanded stronger enforcement of the Liberianization Policy against foreign traders selling similar products at lower prices. The Senate also summoned port stakeholders on November 3 regarding delays at the Freeport of Monrovia.

Why it mattersLiberian-owned businesses face closure due to import delays, high costs, and unfair competition.
