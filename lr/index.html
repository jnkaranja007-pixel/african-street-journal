Liberia House committee urges rejection of OeSD ID concession

The lawmakers cite more than 40 alleged legal, procurement, financial and national-security defects in the proposed civil registry deal.

A joint committee of Liberia’s House of Representatives has urged lawmakers to reject a proposed concession with OeSD International GmbH to develop and manage the country’s central digital civil registry and vital statistics system. The committee identified more than 40 alleged legal, procurement, financial and national-security deficiencies.

In a report submitted to the House Plenary, the committee said the government failed to follow procurement directions for open international bidding and delegated too much authority to the private company. It recommended rejecting the agreement in full and directing the executive branch to address statutory breaches before considering another arrangement.

The report says the Public Procurement and Concessions Commission rejected a sole-source request in May 2025 and, on July 7, 2025, instructed the government to hold open international bidding. The committee said that instruction was not implemented before the agreement was signed on April 30, 2026; the House received it for ratification on July 15.

Why it mattersThe House must decide whether to ratify a deal the committee says bypassed procurement rules, after recommending rejection and a review of statutory breaches.

COP31 draft sets $120 billion adaptation finance target for 2035

The proposed agreement also sets a 35 percent electricity target, while Liberia presses for predictable support and has undisbursed climate commitments.

A draft political agreement at the 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference in Antalya, Türkiye, proposes annual adaptation finance of US$120 billion for developing countries by 2035. Released by the COP31 presidency on November 16, the text also proposes that electricity make up 35 percent of final energy use by 2035.

The draft calls on developed countries to keep their finance promise, but competing wording on fossil fuels and other issues means it is not an agreed position. Nana Dr Antwi-Boasiako Amoah, chair of the African Group of Negotiators, said African countries want commitments adequately funded, transparently tracked and delivered on fair terms.

Liberia is seeking predictable climate finance and adaptation support, while about US$360 million committed under its previous climate plan remained undisbursed as of December 2024. Civil society groups questioned whether the draft would help secure funding or distract from detailed negotiations needed to deliver it.

Why it mattersAbout US$360 million in Liberia's previous climate-plan commitments remained undisbursed as of December 2024, while the country seeks predictable adaptation support.

Liberia police reopen 2017 cheque complaint against Musa Hassan Bility

The opposition leader says his company’s cheque was never presented for payment and that the debt to former Nigerian partners was settled.

Liberia’s police are examining a 2017 complaint against opposition leader Musa Hassan Bility over a cheque issued by his company, Srimex Oil & Gas Company, to former Nigerian business partners. Bility was called in by Inspector General of Police Gregory O. W. Coleman in July 2026 for further discussions, according to a letter Bility sent Coleman on September 30.

Bility wrote that he understood the matter had been dealt with in 2017 and said the cheque was never presented to Guaranty Trust Bank for payment. He said the underlying obligation had been satisfied. Coleman told him that the police had received written confirmation from Global Bank Liberia, now Bloom Bank Africa Liberia, that the cheque was presented and returned unpaid.

A source familiar with the investigation told FrontPage Africa that Interpol contacted the Liberia National Police about the case. The source said a report was being sent to Justice Minister Bettie Lamin-Blamo but had not been made public. The police have not publicly explained what Interpol requested.

Why it mattersBility and his former business partners face a renewed police examination of a disputed debt, with a report being sent to Justice Minister Bettie Lamin-Blamo.

Health · 2026-11-18

Liberian clinician urges response to estimated 56,000 HIV cases

Patience Lincoln says weak coordination and breaches of patient confidentiality are undermining prevention and treatment across Liberia.

Patience Lincoln, a graduating public health student at the University of Liberia, urged Liberia’s government to strengthen its response to HIV, citing a 2026 estimate of about 56,000 people living with the virus. She described the situation as a national public health emergency.

Lincoln said poor coordination among the Ministry of Health, the National Public Health Institute of Liberia and the National AIDS Commission had weakened efforts to curb transmission. She also alleged that some health workers disclosed patients’ HIV status to relatives and acquaintances, exposing them to discrimination and social rejection.

She said fear of stigma discourages some people from seeking care and following treatment. Lincoln called for stricter enforcement of confidentiality rules, more public awareness, increased budget support and expanded prevention and treatment programmes; she also urged health authorities to improve professional ethics and coordination.

Why it mattersPeople living with HIV face breaches of confidentiality that Lincoln says expose them to discrimination and discourage care and treatment.

Liberia schedules maritime security dialogue in Margibi County

The Liberia Maritime Authority will convene officials and shipping leaders on Thursday, before the country assumes the UN Security Council presidency in December.

Liberia will host a maritime safety and security dialogue on Thursday, November 19, at the Liberia Maritime Training Institute in Marshall, Margibi County. The Liberia Maritime Authority (LiMA) scheduled the meeting for 10 a.m., as the country prepares to assume the rotating presidency of the United Nations Security Council in December 2026.

LiMA Commissioner and Chief Executive Officer Neto Zarzar Lighe announced the event at a Ministry of Information briefing on November 17. He said senior government officials, diplomats, United Nations representatives, security experts and shipping industry leaders would discuss threats including piracy, armed conflict, cyberattacks, illegal fishing and trafficking.

The talks are set to cover international shipping routes, seafarer protection, information sharing and coordination. Lighe said Liberia, one of the world’s leading flag states, has a responsibility to promote maritime safety, freedom of navigation and cooperation.

Why it mattersOfficials and shipping leaders are scheduled to discuss threats to international routes and seafarers at the 10 a.m. meeting on November 19.
