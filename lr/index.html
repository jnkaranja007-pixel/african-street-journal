Liberia summons health officials over Ebola preparedness

Lawmakers want details on response measures amid Nigeria outbreak and Bundibugyo Ebola concerns.

Liberia's House of Representatives has summoned health authorities to a briefing on the nation's readiness for public health emergencies. The decision followed reports of a confirmed Ebola case in Nigeria and an ongoing outbreak in Bundibugyo. The Minister of Health and the Director General of the National Public Health Institute of Liberia (NPHIL) are expected to appear before the House on Tuesday, October 27.

Montserrado County Representative Dixon Seboe requested the briefing, citing the need for detailed information on preparedness, including medical supplies, trained personnel, and emergency response resources. He also wants updates on disease surveillance, laboratory testing capacity, and the availability of isolation facilities across Liberia's 15 counties. Border and port health authorities will also explain screening procedures.

The summons also seeks information on public awareness campaigns, infection prevention supplies, frontline health worker training, and resources for enforcing preventive directives. Seboe also called for details on measures to protect public schools, markets, and densely populated areas, particularly in Montserrado County.

Why it mattersLiberian citizens' health and well-being are at risk if the country is not prepared for a potential public health emergency.

Liberian economist Jackson seeks travel for medical treatment

Samuel Jackson, facing charges in his wife's death, asked a court for permission to leave Liberia for medical care.

Liberian economist Samuel Jackson appealed to Criminal Court “A” for permission to travel abroad for medical treatment on October 22, 2026. Jackson, who is facing charges in connection with his wife Ntombi Khumalo Jackson’s March 2026 death, cited deteriorating health. He was seen struggling to walk at the Temple of Justice in Monrovia.

Jackson is seeking specialized medical care outside Liberia but remains barred from leaving the country due to ongoing legal proceedings. His wife, a South African attorney, was found dead in their Monrovia home with reports of severe head injuries. Jackson maintains her injuries resulted from an accidental fall.

The economist was released in April on a US$20,000 bond with travel restrictions and mandatory court check-ins. He has accused the prosecution of being politically motivated to silence his criticism of President Joseph Boakai’s administration. Authorities have not yet released the official autopsy findings.

Why it mattersEconomist Samuel Jackson requires medical treatment abroad while facing legal charges in Liberia.

hundreds stranded on zwედru highway

hundreds of vehicles and travelers have been stuck for over a week on the Ganta–Zwedru Highway.

Hundreds of travelers and motorists are stranded for days on the road linking Monrovia to Zwedru, Grand Gedeh, as deteriorating conditions have cut off the highway in several sections. Commercial vehicles, private cars, and passengers are affected by the muddy and swampy terrain, particularly between Toe Town and Zwedru. Some travelers have been waiting for over a week for the road to become passable.

More than 150 vehicles were reportedly trapped in muddy conditions between Duogee Town and Senneh-wen in Grand Gedeh County, according to reports. Travelers are calling on the Ministry of Public Works to intervene urgently, warning that prolonged delays are contributing to food shortages and rising prices. The ministry stated on October 21 that a team was working to stabilize a 100-meter swampy section near Duogee Town.

The highway serves as a critical transportation corridor for southeastern Liberia, affecting the movement of agricultural produce, food supplies, and fuel. The recurring deterioration is linked to heavy rainfall and poor drainage. Travelers are urging the Ministry of Public Works to restore passage quickly to prevent further hardship for passengers, traders, and residents.

Liberia minister nominee promises price transparency

Stephen M. Yekeson Jr. pledges to publish weekly prices for rice, fuel, and cement in all 15 counties within 100 days.

Liberia's acting Commerce and Industry Minister Designate Stephen M. Yekeson Jr. told senators on Wednesday that he will publish weekly prices for rice, fuel, and cement in all 15 counties within 100 days. Yekeson also pledged to protect consumers and enforce Liberian-only business sectors. He aims for Liberia to produce more of what it consumes by 2029.

Yekeson admitted that prices have continued to rise despite a stronger Liberian Dollar, which closed September at L$172 to US$1. Small traders report wholesalers still price stock at L$190 to the dollar, a gap of nearly 12 percent. A Senate probe found diesel prices rose 47 percent between October 2024 and September 2026.

The minister nominee plans to review pricing with the Liberia Petroleum Refining Company (LPRC) and ensure regulated prices reflect import costs, duties, transport, and a reasonable margin. World rice prices have increased by about 67 percent on the year, hitting a 26-month high in early October.

Why it mattersLiberians will see weekly prices for essential goods like rice, fuel, and cement, helping them manage household budgets.

Liberia anti-corruption chief sees US$5 billion revenue potential

Arthur Fumbah of the LACC stated that a corruption-free Liberia could boost annual revenue to US$5 billion.

Achieving a corruption-free Liberia is possible with stronger coordination among oversight institutions, the Legislature, and the Judiciary, according to Arthur W.B. Fumbah, Executive Vice Chairperson of the Liberia Anti-Corruption Commission (LACC). Fumbah spoke at a policy dialogue in Monrovia on October 23, 2026, emphasizing that institutional cooperation and preventive measures are key to combating corruption.

Fumbah argued that eliminating corruption could significantly increase Liberia’s annual revenue to as much as US$5 billion, a substantial rise from recent efforts to reach US$1 billion. He criticized weak penalties for corruption, citing a Supreme Court ruling where a convicted official was ordered to pay US$10,000, which he said undermines accountability given the costs of investigations.

He also called for a review of the Public Financial Management Law, suggesting that heads of institutions influencing the appointment of accountants and auditors creates corruption opportunities. Fumbah proposed that the Comptroller General’s Office and other agencies deploy qualified professionals to public institutions to strengthen oversight and reduce financial misconduct.

Why it mattersLiberians could see increased government revenue and public confidence if corruption is reduced.
