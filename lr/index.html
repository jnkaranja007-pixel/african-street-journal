China offers Liberia mining investment and US$3 million in rice aid

Beijing proposes that African countries own their mineral resources, while Liberian cocoa producers are set to gain access to the Chinese market from December 15, 2026.

China is seeking to deepen its economic partnership with Liberia, offering mining investment, approximately US$3 million in rice assistance and expanded market access. The Chinese Embassy outlined the proposals at a media briefing in Monrovia on Wednesday, November 11.

The embassy set out a four-point proposal for China-Africa mining cooperation, starting from the principle that African resources belong to Africans. It says Chinese firms should improve operational standards and transfer green technologies, and it warns against turning mineral resources into instruments of geopolitical rivalry. Liberia depends heavily on iron ore, gold and diamonds for investment and export revenue.

Liberian cocoa producers are due to gain access to the Chinese market beginning December 15, 2026. The briefing also referred to President Xi Jinping's recent engagement with U.S. President Donald Trump, which Beijing described as an effort to build a relationship of strategic stability based on respect and reciprocity.

Why it mattersLiberian cocoa producers gain access to the Chinese market from December 15, 2026, and mining firms are offered a route to processing and skills development beyond raw mineral exports.

Liberia charges seven people over attack on police officer in Sinkor

Officer Sedeke K. Kammara reportedly lost use of an eye and had his radio, cap and US$150 taken during the assault

The Liberia National Police has arrested seven people and sent them to court over the alleged assault of a police officer and obstruction of officers in Sinkor, Monrovia. The suspects are Yokie Johnson, Rachel Johnson, Obediah Yorkor, Anthony Hallie, Spencil Z. Gaye, Prince Sackie and Harris Clomo.

The police said Officer Sedeke K. Kammara was attacked by a group from the beachside while responding to a situation on 26th Street, behind ERA Supermarket, on Thursday, November 12. Two officers of the Liberia Drug Enforcement Agency were with him but reportedly left the scene during the confrontation. The attackers allegedly injured his right eye and took his police-issued radio, face cap and US$150 in cash.

The seven face charges of aggravated assault, hindering law enforcement, physical obstruction of government functions, theft of property and disorderly conduct under Liberia's Revised Penal Law. The suspects are presumed innocent until the prosecution proves the allegations in court. The police condemned attacks on officers and said those suspected of breaking the law will be held accountable.

Why it mattersOfficer Kammara reportedly lost a police radio and US$150 in the attack and must now face the court case against the seven suspects.

Liberia judge warns justice system failures risk repeating war mistakes

Judge Roosevelt Z. Willie cited a 2006 report on corruption and institutional weaknesses as reasons for his concern.

Liberia risks repeating the mistakes that led to its 14-year civil war if its justice system's weaknesses are not fixed, warned Resident Circuit Judge Roosevelt Z. Willie. He issued this warning during his charge for the November Term of Court A.D. 2026 at the 13th Judicial Circuit Court, questioning if Liberia has learned from its judicial past.

Judge Willie referenced an April 2006 International Crisis Group (ICG) report that identified corruption and institutional problems in the judiciary, police, and correctional system as key factors in Liberia's instability. The report detailed the judiciary's financial dependence on the legislature, poor facilities, unqualified staff, low pay, and unequal access to justice, noting a two-tiered system favoring the wealthy and politically connected.

While acknowledging reforms like the Judiciary Financial Autonomy Act and the establishment of the Judicial Institute in 2008, Judge Willie questioned their effectiveness for ordinary Liberians. He stressed that political affiliation, religion, financial status, and social standing should never dictate legal treatment, urging justice sector workers to demonstrate integrity and accountability to prevent a return to past failures.

Why it mattersJustice sector officials must demonstrate integrity and accountability to prevent a return to past failures that led to civil war.

Benoni Urey named Liberia's seat on ECOWAS Business Council

The advisory body, chaired by Aliko Dangote, is meant to give the private sector a direct line to West Africa's presidents.

Benoni W. Urey, a Liberian businessman and founding shareholder of the mobile network Lonestar Cell MTN, has been named Liberia's representative on the ECOWAS Business Council. The council is a new advisory body chaired by Nigerian industrialist Aliko Dangote, Africa's richest man.

Ministers created the council on paper in 2021, but its launch slipped from December 2024 to September 2025. Dangote was named its first chairman by the bloc's foreign ministers in Abuja in December 2025, and West African heads of state later endorsed him.

Liberia trades heavily with Sierra Leone, Guinea and Côte d'Ivoire and imports almost all of its fuel. Whether the seat delivers cheaper fuel, easier border crossings and a bigger market for Liberian goods will depend on how hard its representative fights for a small country in the room.

Why it mattersLiberian businesses and fuel buyers depend on the council's decisions for cheaper fuel, easier border crossings and a bigger market for Liberian goods.

Education · 2026-11-13

Nimba community opens new seven-classroom school

Residents of Yokopa in Nimba County celebrate the opening of a new school building funded by ArcelorMittal Liberia's Social Development Fund.

Residents of Yokopa, Nimba County, celebrated the opening of a new seven-classroom school building on Friday, November 13, 2026. The facility aims to address years of overcrowding and poor learning conditions at the existing wooden-plank structure, which accommodates 165 pupils.

Community Chairlady Betty Yoko expressed gratitude to ArcelorMittal Liberia (AML) for its contribution to the Social Development Fund (SDF), which made the school possible. She noted that children previously had to walk to nearby Duo for higher primary grades, facing risks like speeding motorbikes and bullying.

The new school, built with community contributions and AML's annual US$1.5 million SDF funding, includes administrative offices and restrooms. Schoolteacher Isaac Meneyengehn described the previous conditions as "deplorable and unbearable," while local resident E. Karwah Slaipeh urged the Ministry of Education to assign qualified teachers.

Why it mattersChildren in Yokopa will now attend lessons in a new seven-classroom school, a direct result of ArcelorMittal Liberia's social development funding.
