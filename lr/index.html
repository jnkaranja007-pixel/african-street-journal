Liberia seeks African investment with business reforms

The nation hosts the Prosperity Africa Conference, promising predictability and fairness to attract manufacturing and agro-processing investments.

Liberia is hosting the 7th Prosperity Africa Conference to attract investment, expand trade, and create jobs. The three-day event, organized by the Pan African Chamber of Commerce and Industry and the Liberia Chamber of Commerce, brings together African business leaders, investors, and policymakers. Acting Commerce and Industry Minister Stephen M. Yekeson Jr. urged participants to move beyond policy discussions and establish practical partnerships, emphasizing Liberia's commitment to being a predictable and fair business partner.

The conference theme, “Innovation in Business Leadership & the Economics of Tomorrow,” highlights the need for Africa to move beyond raw material exports. Yekeson identified agriculture, agro-processing, manufacturing, mineral value addition, and digital and financial services as priority investment sectors. He pointed to opportunities in rice milling, cassava processing, palm oil production, gold refining, iron ore processing, and timber manufacturing, as well as special economic zones and industrial parks.

Pan African Chamber of Commerce and Industry Executive Director Kebour Ghenna called for the African Continental Free Trade Area to become a practical tool for transformation, urging stronger economies to invest in smaller ones and facilitate technology transfer. Liberia Chamber of Commerce President O. Natty Davis II stressed the importance of tangible economic benefits for citizens, including market access for farmers and financing for women entrepreneurs, and urged participants to identify concrete partnerships and investment opportunities.

Why it mattersAfrican investors and business leaders are affected by Liberia's promises of business reforms and opportunities in manufacturing and agriculture.

Liberian journalists secure 140 India training slots

The Female Journalists Association of Liberia has secured 140 training opportunities in India for women journalists, focusing on digital media and leadership.

The Female Journalists Association of Liberia (FeJAL) has secured 140 training slots for Liberian women journalists to participate in professional media exchanges and training programs in India for the 2026–27 period. The opportunity was confirmed following a meeting between FeJAL President Lisa T. Diasay and India’s Ambassador to Liberia, Manoj Bihari Verma, at the Indian Embassy in Monrovia. This initiative aims to enhance skills in emerging areas of journalism and media technology, including digital journalism and artificial intelligence.

FeJAL requested increased capacity-building opportunities, emphasizing the need for training in digital journalism, AI, technology, newsroom leadership, and professional exchanges. Ambassador Verma stated that the 140 slots are part of India’s Indian Technical and Economic Cooperation (ITEC) program and are open to qualified Liberians. He also highlighted scholarship opportunities through the Indian Council for Cultural Relations (ICCR) as part of India’s commitment to human-capacity development in Liberia.

The discussions also explored extending these opportunities beyond Monrovia, with FeJAL proposing closer collaboration with county-based journalists to improve access to information about India-supported programs in sectors like agriculture, health, and education. Ambassador Verma welcomed the proposal, agreeing to use FeJAL’s media network to disseminate information about the embassy’s programs and future opportunities, ensuring broader benefit for women journalists across Liberia.

Why it mattersLiberian women journalists are affected by gaining access to 140 training slots in India to improve their professional skills and international exposure.

Liberian education minister to lead delegation to Canada

Liberia's education minister will head a delegation to Toronto to discuss partnerships and investment in the country's education sector.

Liberia's Minister of Education, Dr. Jarso Maley Jallah, will lead a delegation to Toronto, Canada, for an education, investment, and partnership roundtable on October 28. The event, organized by the Liberia Trade & Investment Commissioner in Canada, aims to foster practical collaborations supporting Liberia's educational priorities. The delegation includes Samuel A. Stevquaoh, Minister of State for Presidential Affairs, and Dr. Augustine Konneh, Senior Advisor to the President. Discussions will cover school infrastructure, vocational training, digital education, teacher development, university links, and innovative financing.

The roundtable seeks to identify opportunities for Canadian expertise, institutional cooperation, and investment to advance Liberia's education sector and broader development goals under the government's ARREST Agenda. The Office of the Liberia Trade & Investment Commissioner is also engaging the Liberian diaspora community in Canada, including the Federation of Liberian Associations in Canada and Liberian Associations in Canada, to help disseminate information and potentially facilitate community engagement with Minister Stevquaoh.

The visit underscores Liberia's commitment to strengthening international ties for educational advancement. The focus on partnerships and investment highlights a strategic approach to addressing key challenges in the education system. The inclusion of diaspora organizations in the outreach signals an effort to leverage community networks for national development objectives.

Why it mattersLiberian students and educators will benefit from potential new partnerships and investments in school infrastructure, training, and digital resources.

Liberia risks ECOWAS sanctions over US$17.5 million debt

Bomi County Senator Edwin Melvin Snowe warns of sanctions and loss of leadership opportunities if Liberia does not address its outstanding financial obligations to the regional.

Liberia faces potential sanctions from the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) due to an outstanding debt of US$17.5 million. Bomi County Senator Edwin Melvin Snowe, who leads Liberia's delegation to the ECOWAS Parliament, urged the Liberian Senate to work with the Ministries of Finance and Foreign Affairs to clarify the arrears and create a payment plan. Snowe emphasized that fulfilling financial obligations is crucial for Liberia's participation and its aspirations for leadership roles within ECOWAS.

The senator highlighted that Liberia is vying for significant leadership positions, including the potential chairmanship of the ECOWAS Authority of Heads of State and Government for President Joseph Nyuma Boakai and the presidency of the ECOWAS Commission for the 2030–2034 period. He stated that these opportunities are contingent upon Liberia meeting its financial commitments and other requirements, underscoring the need for immediate attention to the debt. Snowe also recognized three Liberians serving in key regional roles and members of the ECOWAS Parliament delegation.

In a separate development, Senator Snowe announced his appointment to the Continental Immunization Advisory Council of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC). He noted that his inclusion as one of three parliamentarians from across the continent reflects the vital role lawmakers can play in advancing public health agendas throughout Africa.

Why it mattersLiberia's government and its citizens risk facing sanctions and losing significant regional leadership opportunities due to unpaid ECOWAS dues.

Liberian senator questions education ministry's centralized system

Senator Prince Kermue Moye argues the Ministry of Education's central control weakens county officials' authority over teachers and payroll.

Bong County Senator Prince Kermue Moye has criticized the Ministry of Education's centralized administrative system, stating it undermines the authority of County Education Officers (CEOs) to manage teachers. Moye, who chairs the Senate Committee on Ways, Means, Finance and Budget, argued that this setup prevents CEOs from disciplining, transferring, or taking administrative action against underperforming teachers, potentially harming accountability and educational quality. He cited an instance in Bong County where a principal recruitment took nearly two years, illustrating administrative delays.

Moye also expressed frustration over the continued reliance on volunteer teachers, despite budgetary allocations meant to place them on the government payroll. He noted that inadequate staffing, particularly in rural areas, remains a significant challenge, with some schools lacking sufficient teachers. Constituents across Bong County have repeatedly raised the issue of volunteer teachers during his engagements, highlighting the persistent problem of unfilled teaching positions.

Meanwhile, the government announced plans to place 201 volunteer teachers from the Monrovia Consolidated School System (MCSS) on the national payroll effective October 2026, with outstanding arrears to be settled after October salaries are paid. This brings the total verified MCSS volunteer teachers on the payroll to 409. The government also pledged to consider salary adjustments for currently employed MCSS teachers under a broader public-sector reform aimed at addressing disparities and promoting equity.

Why it mattersLiberian students and teachers are affected by a centralized education system that hinders timely administrative decisions and delays the payment of volunteer teachers.
