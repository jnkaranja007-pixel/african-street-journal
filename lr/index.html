Liberia cabinet backs $300M housing plan

The government will build up to 5,000 homes for civil servants with a projected investment of $300 million.

Liberia's Cabinet has endorsed a plan to build up to 5,000 affordable homes for government employees, starting with 600 units. The Government Employee Housing Project requires an investment of up to $300 million, subject to financial and regulatory approvals, and is expected to create 6,000 jobs.

Dr. Josiah F. Joekai Jr., chair of the Special Presidential Housing Committee, presented the framework developed with LARIX Company Limited, the proposed developer. Eligible civil servants will access mortgages up to 20 years, with repayments via payroll deductions, and the Ministry of Public Works has completed designs for two- and three-bedroom units.

The initiative, aligned with the government’s ARREST Agenda, will be overseen by the Special Presidential Housing Committee. Before construction begins, the government must complete due diligence, finalize funding, and sign an agreement with LARIX. The Cabinet also endorsed three youth program proposals.

Why it mattersCivil servants will be able to access mortgages for up to 20 years to purchase homes, with repayments deducted from their salaries.

Liberia faces US$360 million climate finance gap

Projects for weather forecasting and coastal protection remain delayed as funding falls short of the country's plan.

Of the US$573 million Liberia identified in its previous climate plan, only US$213 million had been received by December 2024, leaving a gap of about US$360 million. African leaders meeting at the COP31 climate conference in Türkiye have urged developed countries to deliver promised finance, warning that vulnerable nations cannot keep absorbing rising climate costs.

Funding shortfalls have left the US$10 million Climate Information System and the coastal protection scheme for Monrovia behind schedule. Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed warned that every dollar spent coping with climate damage is one less for schools and healthcare, and Côte d'Ivoire's Vice President Tiemoko Meyliet Kone said unpredictable seasons are threatening farm output and household incomes.

Experts argue that adaptation needs grants rather than loans, since debt repayments limit what governments can spend on public services. Rwanda's Prime Minister Justin Nsengiyumva called for projects to be judged on their merits rather than their location.

Why it mattersCommunities facing coastal erosion in Monrovia and farmers exposed to erratic weather wait on projects that cannot start until the missing funds arrive.

Liberia officials appeal 10-year sentences in rice case

Foreign Affairs Minister Kemayah and co-defendants sentenced to prison for US$500,000 Saudi rice diversion will seek Supreme Court review.

10 years in prison: that is the sentence Foreign Affairs Minister Dee-Maxwell Saah Kemayah and some co-defendants received in Liberia's US$500,000 Saudi Rice case, and they have now lodged an appeal with the Supreme Court. Sentences of five years went to other convicted defendants. The sentencing took place on Friday, November 13, 2026, at the Temple of Justice in Monrovia.

Judge Ousman Feika, presiding over Criminal Court 'C', refused the defence's bid for a retrial after a jury on October 9, 2026 found eight of the 13 defendants guilty. Among those convicted were Kemayah and former Internal Affairs Minister Varney A. Sirleaf. The case concerns the alleged diversion of humanitarian rice donated by the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

The defence says the jury's findings do not match the evidence heard over a trial of more than 40 days. The Supreme Court, Liberia's highest judicial authority, will now review the trial proceedings, the verdicts, the refusal of a new trial and the sentences.

Why it mattersConvicted officials, including a sitting minister, remain free while the Supreme Court considers the appeal, and the outcome will decide whether the 10-year and five-year terms stand.

Seven charged over assault on Liberian police officer

Suspects accused of attacking officer, stealing radio, cap, and cash in Sinkor, Monrovia.

Seven individuals were arrested and sent to court in Monrovia on Thursday, November 12, for allegedly assaulting a police officer and obstructing law enforcement. The suspects, identified as Yokie Johnson, Rachel Johnson, Obediah Yorkor, Anthony Hallie, Spencil Z. Gaye, Prince Sackie, and Harris Clomo, are accused of attacking Officer Sedeke K. Kammara on 26th Street behind ERA Supermarket.

The Liberia National Police (LNP) stated that the officers were responding to a situation when a group attacked them. Officer Kammara reportedly suffered injuries to his right eye, and the attackers allegedly seized his police radio, cap, and US$150. Two officers from the Liberia Drug Enforcement Agency (LDEA) who were present reportedly left the scene during the confrontation.

The seven suspects face multiple charges including Aggravated Assault, Hindering Law Enforcement, and Theft of Property. The LNP condemned the violence against officers and reaffirmed its commitment to public order and accountability through due process.

Why it mattersSeven individuals face multiple criminal charges after allegedly assaulting a police officer and stealing his property in Monrovia.

Politics · 2026-11-15

Mulbah Morlu rejoins Liberia's CDC party

The former national chairman says he is returning as an ordinary member and is not seeking leadership.

Mulbah K. Morlu Jr., former national chairman of Liberia's Congress for Democratic Change (CDC), announced his return to the opposition party on Saturday in Congo Town. He accepted a petition from grassroots supporters to restore his membership, which he had resigned in June 2024.

Morlu stated that his return is as an ordinary member and not an attempt to reclaim the party's chairmanship or seek another leadership position. He explained his earlier resignation was to prevent internal divisions after the CDC's defeat in the 2023 presidential election, believing the party's survival was more important than his leadership role.

Morlu also criticized President Joseph Nyuma Boakai's administration, accusing it of politically motivated prosecutions and selective justice against opposition figures. He urged the CDC to abandon its policy of constructive engagement and adopt a more confrontational approach ahead of the 2029 general elections.

Why it mattersMulbah Morlu has rejoined the Congress for Democratic Change party, stating his intention to serve as an ordinary member.
