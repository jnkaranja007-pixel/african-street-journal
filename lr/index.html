Liberia summons health officials over Nigeria Ebola case

Lawmakers want details on preparedness, screening, and supplies amid regional outbreak.

Liberia's House of Representatives has summoned health authorities to brief them on the nation's readiness for Ebola. The summons follows a confirmed case in Nigeria and an ongoing outbreak in Bundibugyo. Lawmakers voted on Thursday, October 22, 2026, to require the Minister of Health and the NPHIL Director General to appear before the House on Tuesday, October 27.

Montserrado County Representative Dixon Seboe requested the briefing, citing the need to assess Liberia's preparedness, including medical supplies, trained staff, and emergency resources. The Ministry of Health had already directed health facilities and border points to enhance screening and infection control measures.

The health officials must explain the government's Ebola strategy, response coordination, disease surveillance, and laboratory capacity. They will also report on county-level preparedness, isolation facilities, border screening protocols, public awareness campaigns, and resources for enforcing preventive directives.

Why it mattersLiberian citizens' health and well-being are at risk from a potential Ebola outbreak.

Yekeson pledges weekly price lists for rice, fuel, cement

Liberia's commerce minister nominee aims to publish prices across all 15 counties within 100 days.

Stephen M. Yekeson Jr., Liberia's acting Commerce and Industry Minister, told senators on Wednesday that he will publish weekly prices for rice, fuel, and cement in all 15 counties within 100 days. He also pledged to protect consumers, enforce Liberian-only business sectors, and cut the cost of moving goods. Yekeson aims for Liberia to produce more of what it consumes by 2029.

Yekeson admitted that prices have continued to rise despite a stronger Liberian Dollar, which closed September at L$172 to US$1. Small traders report wholesalers still pricing stock at L$190 to the dollar, a gap of nearly 12 percent. A Senate probe found diesel prices rose 47 percent between October 2024 and September 2026.

The minister-designate will review pricing with the Liberia Petroleum Refining Company (LPRC) and promises transparent, rule-based prices reflecting import costs. World rice prices are up about 67 percent on the year, hitting a 26-month high in early October, while the domestic price was last fixed in March.

Why it mattersLiberians will have access to weekly price information for essential goods like rice, fuel, and cement.

Liberian economist Jackson seeks travel for medical treatment

Samuel Jackson, facing charges in his wife's death, appealed to court for permission to travel abroad due to declining health.

Liberian economist Samuel Jackson has asked Criminal Court “A” for permission to travel abroad for medical treatment, citing his deteriorating health. Jackson was seen struggling to walk at the Temple of Justice in Monrovia on Thursday, October 22, 2026, as he seeks specialized care outside Liberia. He remains barred from leaving the country while facing charges related to his wife's March 2026 death.

Jackson is accused in connection with the death of his wife, South African attorney Ntombi Khumalo Jackson, who was found in their Monrovia home. He told authorities he discovered her semi-conscious after returning from a television engagement. Reports indicated she suffered severe head injuries, and images allegedly showing bruises circulated on social media.

The economist was released on a US$20,000 bond with travel restrictions and mandatory court check-ins. Jackson has denied responsibility, calling the prosecution politically motivated. A major point of contention is the reported failure of authorities to release the official autopsy and toxicology findings.

Why it mattersEconomist Samuel Jackson is seeking permission to travel abroad for medical treatment due to his declining health while facing charges.

Liberia Supreme Court weighs parliament order defiance

Justices will decide if the House of Representatives legally expelled lawmaker Yekeh Kolubah after receiving a court order.

Liberia’s Supreme Court reserved judgment on Thursday in a case questioning if the House of Representatives defied a court order by expelling lawmaker Yekeh Kolubah in April. The court must determine if House leadership was properly served with a Supreme Court order to halt proceedings against Kolubah before his expulsion. If served, the vote to remove the outspoken representative may have breached a judicial directive.

Kolubah, who represented District 10 of Montserrado County, was expelled after making a controversial statement about the Makona River boundary dispute. His lawyers sought a writ of prohibition, leading Associate Justice Yussif D. Kaba to grant an order on April 16, summoning House leaders and staying further proceedings. However, the House claims it was never properly served, citing its own rules that designate the Sergeant-at-Arms as the sole recipient of court orders, not a receptionist in the Speaker’s office.

The House expelled Kolubah on April 17 with a resolution signed by 49 of its 73 members, stating his removal was for unpatriotic acts. Justice Kaba had previously dismissed a complaint for improper service, but Kolubah appealed to the full bench. His legal team argued that Liberia’s Civil Procedure Law, not the House’s internal rules, should govern the validity of service, pointing to the delivery of the document to the Speaker’s office.

Why it mattersThe Supreme Court's decision will determine if the House of Representatives acted lawfully in expelling a member after a judicial order was issued.

Liberia army discharges two sergeants for domestic violence

The military action follows the circulation of a video showing an alleged assault on a woman in Paynesville.

The Armed Forces of Liberia (AFL) administratively discharged two sergeants, Roberts P. Menyon and Abu S. B. Sheriff, on Wednesday, October 21, 2026. The action followed a social media video allegedly showing domestic violence involving Alfredia Blessing Kpaegai in Paynesville, Montserrado County. The Ministry of National Defense announced the immediate discharge.

AFL Chief of Staff Major General Davidson F. Forleh stated the incident was inconsistent with military standards at a ceremony at the Barclay Training Center in Monrovia. He reaffirmed the military's commitment to discipline and human dignity, emphasizing that conduct abusing or devaluing women would not be tolerated. The discharge, under the National Defense Act of 2008, aims to reinforce institutional standards and public confidence.

Alfredia Blessing Kpaegai is receiving medical care at the 14-Military Hospital. The Ministry of National Defense acknowledged public cooperation and urged citizens to report similar allegations. The discharge occurred amid public attention on the video and calls for accountability in cases of alleged violence against women.

Why it mattersTwo Liberian army sergeants were discharged following a video of alleged domestic violence, impacting military discipline and public trust.
