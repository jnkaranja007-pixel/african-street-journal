Liberian Warlords Back War Crimes Court for Justice

Accused former combatants, including 'General Butt Naked,' are now advocating for the establishment of a War and Economic Crimes Court to clear their names and bring closure.

Seventeen years after a commission recommended its creation, Liberia's proposed War and Economic Crimes Court is gaining unexpected support from some individuals accused of wartime atrocities. Joshua Blahyi, formerly known as 'General Butt Naked,' who admitted to his fighters killing 20,000 people, is now a vocal proponent, seeking to clear his name and find healing. He stated that only a trial can provide justice and closure, expressing shame over his past actions and the community named in his honor.

Blahyi, who confessed his crimes during the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC) hearings, is among a growing number of former combatants who want to face legal proceedings. Peterson Sonyah, director of the Liberia Massacre and Survivors Association, noted an influx of ex-fighters, including former generals, seeking to have their cases heard. This shift contrasts with earlier efforts by some accused individuals, like senator Thomas Nimely, to block the court's establishment.

Experts suggest that the prolonged delay in establishing the court has exacerbated the psychological and social consequences of the civil war for both victims and perpetrators. The TRC heard over 20,000 testimonies, but only 30 perpetrators confessed. The push for the court is seen as essential for national healing and accountability, offering a path toward resolving the trauma that continues to affect many Liberians.

Why it mattersAccused warlords and former combatants are seeking a trial to clear their names and achieve closure, potentially impacting the country's reconciliation process.

Liberia Airport Authority Buys Nine Vehicles for Operations

The Liberia Airport Authority has acquired two SUVs and seven pickup trucks to improve efficiency, safety, and service delivery at Roberts International Airport and James Spriggs.

The Liberia Airport Authority (LAA) has purchased nine new vehicles, including two SUVs and seven pickup trucks, to enhance operational efficiency and safety at Roberts International Airport (RIA) and James Spriggs Payne Airport. The acquisition aims to modernize operations and equip staff with necessary logistical resources to meet demands. The SUVs are designated for port managers at both airports, while the pickup trucks will support departments such as security, compliance, and base support.

LAA Managing Director Ernest R. Hughes stated that the purchase addresses long-standing transportation challenges that previously hindered critical functions like security operations, airside monitoring, and administrative oversight. He noted that the lack of reliable vehicles had impacted response times and coordination. The new fleet is expected to significantly improve the capabilities of key departments and ensure compliance with international aviation standards.

The vehicles were formally presented to Deputy Managing Director for Administration John Z. Buway, who thanked the LAA leadership for the investment. The acquisition is seen as timely, especially as RIA pursues full aerodrome certification. Management assured that the vehicles would be maintained and used strictly for their intended operational purposes, contributing to improved productivity and service delivery at the airports.

Why it mattersAirport operations and service delivery are directly impacted by the new vehicles, affecting staff efficiency and response times for security and maintenance.

Liberia Justice Minister Vows Crackdown on Drug Cartels

Minister Tweh declared that wealth, influence, or political connections will not shield individuals implicated by credible evidence in the fight against illicit drugs.

Liberia's Justice Minister and Attorney General, Cllr. N. Oswald Tweh, has pledged to intensify the nation's efforts against drug trafficking, stating that no one will be protected regardless of their status. Tweh emphasized that investigations and prosecutions must adhere strictly to evidence, due process, and the rule of law, drawing a clear battle line against drug cartels and their collaborators.

Recent large-scale narcotics seizures, including over 400 kilograms of illegal drugs and the arrest of more than 200 suspects in the first quarter, along with nearly four metric tons of cocaine seized in Duazon, demonstrate increased government commitment, according to Tweh. He credited various national security agencies for these operations, but stressed that dismantling trafficking networks and targeting financiers, not just low-level dealers, is crucial.

The Minister warned against limiting prosecutions to couriers and drivers while influential figures potentially linked to trafficking networks evade scrutiny. He assured that any individual, including customs officers, security officials, businessmen, politicians, or private citizens, implicated by credible evidence will face legal consequences. However, Tweh cautioned against public condemnation based on accusations or social media, stressing the importance of due process in combating lawlessness.

Why it mattersThe government's intensified crackdown on drug cartels and commitment to prosecuting influential individuals could impact the stability and security of Liberia, affecting all citizens.

Supreme Court Orders Ecobank To Pay US$250 Thousand In Damages

The Supreme Court of Liberia has ordered Ecobank Liberia Limited to pay US$250,000 to a former LISGIS official over the disclosure of confidential banking information.

The Supreme Court of Liberia has ruled in favor of Wilmot Smith, former Deputy Director General for Information Coordination at the Liberia Institute for Statistics and Geo-Information Services (LISGIS), ordering Ecobank Liberia Limited to pay him US$250,000 in special damages. The court also ordered activist Martin K.N. Kollie and former LISGIS Deputy Director General Alex M. Williams to pay Smith US$50,000 each in general damages, bringing the total award to US$350,000.

Smith had accused Ecobank and others of damaging his reputation after confidential LISGIS banking information was allegedly accessed and disclosed by an Ecobank employee, Yussif S. Kromah. Smith claimed this led to false allegations of misappropriating approximately US$1.7 million from LISGIS funds intended for national census enumerators, contributing to his loss of employment.

Ecobank denied leaking the information, arguing that Kromah acted outside the scope of his employment and without authorization. Smith initially sought US$700,000 in damages in a lawsuit filed in 2024, with the case proceeding to trial in October 2024. Smith's legal team argued the bank should be liable for its employee's actions.

Why it mattersInvestors face potential financial losses and reputational damage due to a court ruling against Ecobank for employee misconduct and data disclosure.

Liberian Officials Linked to Cocaine Seizure and Uncaught Ship

A US$19.2 million cocaine bust at Roberts International Airport has led to a sprawling investigation involving former officials and a vessel that evaded interception.

A routine customs seizure of US$19.2 million in cocaine at Roberts International Airport in June has escalated into a wide-ranging investigation implicating Liberian officials and international traffickers. The initial bust, significant for Liberia's national budget, is now seen as the beginning of a larger scandal that has reached former Vice President and exposed systemic failures in institutional oversight.

Weeks after the airport seizure, a vessel identified as the IB Atlantic IV remained off Liberia's coast for days before a patrol boat, described as too small, failed to intercept it. This maritime security lapse, occurring in Liberian waters, resulted in the ship escaping into open water, causing national embarrassment and highlighting the inability of the country's maritime apparatus to act decisively against suspicious vessels.

Investigators are tracing connections from the ship incident and the airport seizure, revealing a pattern of institutional misconduct. Mark Egon Kuiah, former operations chief at Roberts International Airport, was dismissed for issuing a letter vouching for the vessel as "distressed" but has not been formally charged. Fitzgerald Biago, Officer-in-Charge of the Liberia Drug Enforcement Agency, recused himself amid allegations of accepting vehicles from an individual tied to the case, with no public resolution following.

Why it mattersThe investigation into a major cocaine seizure and an evaded vessel raises questions about the integrity of Liberian institutions and the potential for high-level corruption, impacting the business environment.
