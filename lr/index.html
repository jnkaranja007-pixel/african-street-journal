Liberia's economy grew 5.1 percent in 2025

Despite strong growth, more Liberians slipped into extreme poverty as jobs stalled in key sectors.

Liberia's economy expanded by 5.1 percent in 2025, the fastest rate in five years according to the World Bank. Export earnings surged by nearly 58 percent, driven by gold and iron ore. However, the number of Liberians living in extreme poverty rose from 34.6 percent in 2024 to 36.1 percent in 2025.

The economic growth was fueled by a 42 percent increase in iron ore production and a 17 percent rise in overall mining output. Sectors where most Liberians work, such as manufacturing and agriculture, saw declines or slowed growth. Agriculture, employing 39 percent of the workforce, grew only 2.6 percent, and farm labour productivity has fallen about 1.1 percent annually since 2014.

The World Bank report highlights that the government cannot reliably track the finances of its 62 state-owned entities. While food inflation decreased due to lower rice prices, non-food inflation climbed to 9.9 percent, impacting housing, healthcare, and services costs for urban workers.

Why it mattersLiberia's economic growth is not reaching the majority of its population, leading to increased poverty despite strong export performance.

Liberia port authority acts on shipping delays

National Port Authority announces direct Maersk shipping and extended operations to ease congestion and rising fees.

Liberia's National Port Authority (NPA) is implementing new measures to tackle severe congestion and lengthy cargo delays at the Freeport of Monrovia. NPA Managing Director Sekou A. Dukuly announced that shipping giant Maersk will now deliver vessels directly to Monrovia, bypassing transshipment through other West African ports. This move aims to reduce transit times and associated costs for Liberian importers who have protested mounting fees and prolonged delays.

Dukuly acknowledged the port's undercapacity, which has left approximately 700 containers stranded for over 30 days, creating significant financial strain on businesses. The NPA plans to clear these containers, suspend berth repairs to allow simultaneous vessel berthing, and extend working hours. The authority is also considering granting additional free days for importers facing demurrage and storage charges.

The NPA is exploring engagement with the Central Bank of Liberia and commercial banks to offer short-term financial relief to affected businesses. This could include easing loan repayment obligations for importers whose capital is tied up in delayed shipments. These proposals come after importers, many of whom secured business loans against personal property, reported threats to their operations due to accumulating costs and delays.

Why it mattersLiberian importers and business owners face financial ruin due to prolonged shipping delays and escalating port fees.

Lofa County University president cites funding gaps

Dr. Isaac P. Podah says rapid enrollment growth strains finances, staff, and transport.

Lofa County University President Dr. Isaac P. Podah reported that the institution's rapid expansion is increasing access to higher education in rural Liberia but straining its finances, workforce, transportation, and infrastructure. Enrollment has surged from about 500-600 students two years ago to approximately 3,000 students across two campuses. Dr. Podah stated this growth requires stronger financial support to maintain operations and retain qualified lecturers.

Speaking at a workshop in Ganta, Dr. Podah explained that the university's annex in Fessibu Town, Zorzor District, was established to bring education closer to students facing financial and transport barriers to Voinjama. He identified budget constraints as the most pressing challenge, estimating that L$4 million is needed to manage operations. This funding pressure impacts staff compensation, transportation, and infrastructure.

The workshop, organized by the National Commission on Higher Education with UNDP support, focused on strengthening higher education governance and quality assurance. Leaders discussed governance, educational standards, and university development. Dr. Podah described the exchange as valuable for efforts to improve higher education in underserved areas.

Why it mattersStudents in rural Liberia are gaining access to higher education, but the university faces significant financial and logistical challenges to support this growth.

Liberia health minister accused of ignoring Sinje center fraud

National Polit-Bureau of Liberia says Dr. Louise Kpoto failed to act on internal report detailing missing funds and drugs.

The National Polit-Bureau of Liberia (NPBL) accused Health Minister Dr. Louise M. Kpoto on Thursday, November 5, 2026, of ignoring financial and administrative problems at the Sinje Comprehensive Health Center in Grand Cape Mount County. NPBL National Chairman Victor L. Balo stated that an internal Ministry of Health investigation from June 3–6, 2026, documented LD$3,705,120 in unbanked revenue and a US$20,583.59 financial discrepancy. The report also noted the disappearance of 646 patient care tablets, procurement fraud, and excessive billing.

Balo criticized the minister for five months of silence following the investigation, warning that inaction could erode public confidence in healthcare. He specifically targeted Dr. Sampson Wulu, the center's Medical Director, for accountability. The NPBL also highlighted concerns about maternal deaths and poor childbirth outcomes, calling for an independent clinical review with international partners. The organization alleged that investigators who authored the report face potential sanctions.

The NPBL demanded a public explanation from Minister Kpoto, a forensic audit, financial reconciliation, and investigations into missing pharmaceuticals, procurement, and billing. They also called for administrative action against Dr. Wulu and protection for whistleblowers. The civil society group warned that administrative lapses expose pregnant women and vulnerable patients to preventable medical risks.

Why it mattersLiberians using the Sinje Comprehensive Health Center face risks from alleged maternal deaths, poor childbirth outcomes, and administrative lapses.

Liberia hosts customs meeting on cross-border crime

More than 40 delegates from 17 West and Central African nations met in Margibi County to enhance intelligence sharing.

Customs officials and intelligence officers from 17 West and Central African countries are meeting in Liberia to strengthen their fight against cross-border crime. The 17th Joint Annual Meeting of National Contact Points of the World Customs Organization Regional Intelligence Liaison Offices is being held at the Farmington Hotel in Margibi County. The Liberia Revenue Authority is hosting the weeklong forum, which focuses on improving intelligence sharing and coordinated enforcement against smuggling and other transnational crimes.

Amadou Tidiane Cisse, Director General of RILO West Africa, opened the meeting on Monday, calling it a critical platform for cooperation. He stated that sharing intelligence helps disrupt criminal networks involved in trafficking narcotics, counterfeit medicines, and protected wildlife. Liberia’s Commissioner of Customs, Saa Saamoi, highlighted the urgent need for stronger regional intelligence sharing and coordinated border enforcement, noting that criminal groups exploit system weaknesses.

Delegates will review recommendations from the previous meeting in Bamako, Mali, and examine issues related to intelligence exchange, data quality, and regional operations. They will also discuss the use of WCO Customs Enforcement Network platforms. The meeting aims to develop practical recommendations and establish follow-up mechanisms for implementing decisions.

Why it mattersThe meeting in Liberia aims to strengthen intelligence sharing and coordinated enforcement to combat cross-border crime, directly affecting the security and safety of communities in West and Central Africa.
