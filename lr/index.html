Liberia port authority acts on congestion

National Port Authority announces direct Maersk shipping and extended hours to clear 700 stranded containers.

Liberia's National Port Authority (NPA) has announced new measures to tackle severe congestion and delays at the Freeport of Monrovia. NPA Managing Director Sekou A. Dukuly stated that approximately 700 containers have been stuck at the port for over 30 days, impacting businesses and maritime trade. The port is currently operating below its necessary capacity.

To ease the situation, shipping giant Maersk will now deliver vessels directly to Monrovia, reducing transit times and costs previously incurred through transshipment. The NPA also plans to clear the 700 stranded containers, suspend berth repairs to allow simultaneous vessel berthing, and extend port operating hours. Efforts are underway to grant additional free days for importers facing demurrage and storage fees.

APM Terminals Liberia reported that the average time containers remain at the terminal has risen from seven to eight days in 2025 to 15–16 days in 2026. The company is relocating containers and developing more stacking spaces. APM Terminals has invested $15 million in handling equipment and plans to acquire more machinery to support Liberia’s annual container traffic, which is approaching 200,000 20-foot equivalent units.

Why it mattersLiberian importers and businesses face mounting costs and prolonged shipping delays due to port congestion.

China ready to buy more Liberian farm goods

Beijing also pledges investment in processing to create jobs and boost local value.

China has signaled its readiness to increase imports of agricultural products from Liberia, including cocoa, coffee, and dried chili peppers. Chinese Vice Premier Ding Xuexiang conveyed this commitment to Liberia’s Vice President Jeremiah Koung in Shanghai on November 5. The next step involves Liberian exporting companies submitting applications for approval to access the Chinese market.

Vice Premier Ding stated that agricultural products from African nations, including Liberia, already have market access. He encouraged Liberia to submit a list of interested companies for the necessary approval procedures. Vice President Koung welcomed the opportunity, highlighting rubber, cocoa, and coffee as priority areas for expanded trade. He emphasized Liberia's desire for foreign investment to support domestic processing, skills development, and job creation.

Vice President Koung invited Chinese businesses to invest in Liberia’s special economic zones, particularly in rubber and agro-processing. Vice Premier Ding affirmed China's encouragement for Chinese companies to invest in Liberia and pledged continued support for development projects. These include infrastructure, technical assistance, and scholarships for Liberians.

Why it mattersLiberian farmers and businesses can increase exports and create jobs through domestic processing.

Liberia moves to create national railway authority

A proposed bill aims to regulate railway operations and promote multi-user access to infrastructure.

Liberia is moving to establish an independent National Railway Authority to regulate railway operations and promote multi-user access. The proposed Liberia National Railway Authority Bill seeks to create a legal framework for railway management, safety, and oversight. On November 4, the Law Reform Commission held a validation exercise in Monrovia with stakeholders including ArcelorMittal Liberia and HPX.

LRC Chairperson Cllr. Bornor M. Varmah stated the legislation would address fragmented railway management and limited access to infrastructure. "This project could no longer be viewed exclusively through the narrow lens of individual mining concessions," Varmah said, emphasizing its potential as a national economic asset. Transport Minister Sirleaf Ralph Tyler called the validation a significant step toward modernizing the sector.

Efforts to establish the authority began in October 2022 under former President George Manneh Weah. President Joseph Nyuma Boakai has advanced the initiative through executive orders since 2024. The proposed legislation aims to replace executive orders with a permanent statutory framework for legislative approval. ArcelorMittal Liberia has supported transforming the corridor into a multi-user facility.

Why it mattersLiberia's railway infrastructure, historically tied to mining, is being repositioned to support multiple economic sectors and national development aspirations.

Liberia legislature reconstruction estimated at over $10M

Speaker Koon states no funds budgeted for repairs, raising possibility of a loan for the damaged facility.

Liberian Speaker Richard Nagbe Koon and Deputy Speaker Thomas P. Fallah toured the destroyed Joint Chambers of the Legislature, over a year after a severe incident caused extensive damage. Rebuilding the heavily damaged structure will demand significant financial resources and extensive construction work, with estimates reaching at least US$10 million. A multi-stage damage assessment involving government estimators and international experts is planned.

Deputy Speaker Fallah described the destruction as a "sad," "embarrassing," and "frustrating" moment, noting that the building will have to be rebuilt from scratch. Speaker Koon confirmed that no money has been budgeted for the projected repairs, which are estimated around US$10 million. He stated that the shutdown of the Joint Chambers continues to impair the Legislature's function, especially during the State of the Nation Address.

The cause of the incident remains unestablished, but the destruction occurred amid a leadership crisis in the House of Representatives. The Liberia National Police identified arson as the cause, leading to criminal proceedings. President Joseph Nyuma Boakai delivered his 2025 and 2026 State of the Nation Addresses outdoors on the Capitol grounds due to the damage.

Why it mattersThe damaged Joint Chambers have disrupted major legislative activities, including the State of the Nation Address, affecting the functioning of the Liberian government.

Politics · 2026-11-07

Liberia leader questions war court political will

The head of the War and Economic Crimes Court establishment office doubts legislative and executive commitment to justice.

The head of Liberia's Office for the Establishment of the War and Economic Crimes Court, Cllr. Jallah A. Barbu, expressed doubts on November 4, 2026, about the political will to establish the court. He spoke during a three-day fast and prayer organized by Liberian women in Sinkor, Monrovia.

Barbu stated that lawmakers had promised constituent consultations during a legislative break but he had seen no public demonstration of these. He also questioned President Joseph Nyuma Boakai's commitment, recalling campaign promises for justice for civil war victims and survivors.

The proposed court aims to prosecute individuals for war crimes and crimes against humanity committed during Liberia's civil conflicts. The women's prayer event highlights ongoing public demands for the court's establishment beyond political assurances.

Why it mattersLiberians seeking accountability for civil war atrocities face continued delays in the establishment of the War and Economic Crimes Court.
