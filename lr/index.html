Ex-Liberia minister jailed for rice diversion

Former Foreign Minister Dee-Maxwell Saah Kemayah sentenced to 10 years for diverting 25,000 bags of Saudi rice meant for flood victims.

Former Liberian Foreign Minister Dee-Maxwell Saah Kemayah was sentenced to 10 years in prison on Friday in Monrovia for diverting over 25,000 bags of rice. Judge Ousman Feika denied Kemayah's request for a new trial, ruling that the rice, a gift from Saudi Arabia in April 2023 for flood and fire victims, was instead sold on market stalls.

Prosecutors stated the rice failed to reach disaster victims, while Kemayah's lawyers announced an appeal to the Supreme Court. The Congress for Democratic Change (CDC), former President George Weah's party, issued a statement accusing President Joseph Boakai's government of using the courts for political intimidation and warning that their patience should not be mistaken for weakness.

The CDC statement also criticized a publication it attributed to Philibert Browne for invoking Weah's name in a "reckless, provocative, and potentially harmful" manner, demanding security guarantees for the former president. Jewel Howard-Taylor, Weah's vice president, has been in Monrovia Central Prison since August on drug-trafficking and money-laundering charges, with her request to await trial outside jail denied due to asthma.

Why it mattersFormer Foreign Minister Dee-Maxwell Saah Kemayah faces 10 years in prison for rice diversion, while former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor remains jailed on drug charges.

CDC accuses government of selective justice in Jackson case

The opposition party demands economist Samuel P. Jackson receive medical treatment abroad, citing alleged double standards.

The opposition Congress for Democratic Change (CDC) accused Liberia's Unity Party-led government of selective justice on Monday, November 16, 2026. CDC National Chairman Attorney Janga Kowo demanded that economist and political commentator Samuel P. Jackson be allowed to seek specialized medical treatment abroad while facing prosecution in connection with his wife's death.

Kowo stated that Jackson's deteriorating health should not be ignored and called on judicial authorities to urgently consider his request for medical relief. Jackson reportedly petitioned Criminal Court ‘A’ Judge Onesimus Barwon on October 23, 2026, for permission to travel. He posted a photograph in November showing him in bed, stating, “I’m losing my appetite.”

The CDC chairman accused the government of double standards, referencing the reported granting of compassionate medical leave to former Chief Justice Gloria Musu-Scott. Kowo alleged Jackson’s treatment was politically motivated due to his criticism of the administration. Jackson faces charges including manslaughter and aggravated assault following his wife's death on March 13, 2026.

Why it mattersThe opposition CDC claims the government is politically persecuting Samuel P. Jackson by denying him medical treatment abroad, while he faces charges in connection with his wife's death.

US senator demands report on Liberia's human rights record over deportation deal

Senator Tim Kaine filed a resolution asking the US State Department for a formal statement on Liberia's treatment of deportees.

U.S. Senator Tim Kaine, a Virginia Democrat on the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, filed Senate Resolution 886 on September 23. It asks the U.S. Secretary of State to give Congress a formal statement on Liberia's human rights practices within 30 days of the resolution's adoption. The measure follows Liberia's August agreement to take in up to 1,200 people deported from the United States, most of whom are not Liberian and have never lived in Africa.

The resolution lists questions rather than accusations. It asks for all credible information on alleged abuses by the Liberian government, including arbitrary arrest and detention, torture, denial of due process, enforced disappearances and extrajudicial killings. It also asks how non-citizens removed to Liberia are treated, what assurances Washington sought, and every agreement and financial transaction between the two governments on removals.

The resolution cites Section 502B(c) of the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961, which allows Congress to demand a human rights report by simple resolution. If the State Department misses the 30-day deadline, U.S. security assistance to Liberia is frozen until the report is delivered. Rights monitors count at least 35 countries that have signed third-country deportation agreements with the Trump administration, and Liberia's deal is the largest by number.

Why it mattersIf the State Department misses the 30-day deadline, U.S. security assistance to Liberia is frozen until the report is delivered.

Liberia's diplomats press Washington for a second U.S. grant

Liberia has been trying to win a second five-year Millennium Challenge Corporation compact for nearly a decade, and the agency is now smaller and less certain about its future.

On November 10, the U.S. Millennium Challenge Corporation (MCC) hosted a reception in Washington for a Liberian technical team working on the analysis needed for a second compact, a five-year grant that does not have to be repaid. Liberia's ambassador to the United States, Dr. Al-Hassan Conteh, told guests that cheap and reliable electricity is the country's biggest obstacle to growth. He also listed roads, governance, private-sector jobs, skills and environmental safeguards as priorities.

The MCC grades poor countries on 22 indicators and requires them to pass at least half, along with two compulsory hurdles on personal freedom and corruption or accountability. Liberia's first compact, worth about US$257 million and signed in November 2015, rebuilt the Mount Coffee Hydropower Plant, now the country's largest source of electricity, and funded road maintenance and power-sector reform.

Liberia was picked as the only new country eligible to develop a compact in December 2024, but the board postponed a decision on its continued eligibility in August 2025 before reaffirming it that December. A Compact Development Team, led by National Coordinator Alieu Fuad Nyei, is now carrying out a constraints analysis, and local reports said work on the compact documents could run until the end of 2027.

Why it mattersElectricity and roads are the priorities Liberia has given the MCC, and the compact development work now under way determines when, and whether, that money arrives.

Health · 2026-11-16

NOCAL breaks ground on Sinje hospital trauma ward

The National Oil Company of Liberia is building a trauma ward at Sinje Hospital, due to be finished and dedicated within 16 months.

The National Oil Company of Liberia (NOCAL) has begun construction of a modern trauma ward at Sinje Hospital in Grand Cape Mount County. The project, part of the company's Corporate Social Responsibility programme, is meant to strengthen emergency care and specialised treatment for residents.

The Ministry of Health chose Sinje because it has secure land, sits on the Bo Corridor and serves about 10,000 people, compared with roughly 3,000 in Robertsport, according to NOCAL. The groundbreaking was attended by Grand Cape Mount senators Simeon Taylor and Dabah M. Varpilah, and by ministry officials on behalf of Health Minister Dr. Louise M. Kpoto.

The ward will include a triage area, emergency and resuscitation rooms, operating and recovery rooms, a blood bank, a laboratory, a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit and a pharmacy. Once completed, it is expected to widen the hospital's capacity for surgery, maternal care and newborn care.

Why it mattersResidents of Sinje and nearby communities will gain surgical, maternal and newborn services that are currently hard to reach, once the ward opens.
