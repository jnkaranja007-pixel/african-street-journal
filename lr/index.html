Sunu Assurances Liberia Opens New Sinkor Office

The insurance company aims to improve customer accessibility and service delivery with its relocation to 13th Street.

SUNU Assurances Liberia Limited has officially opened its new office at 13th Street, Sinkor, Monrovia. The company stated the move is a significant milestone in its growth and commitment to providing accessible, professional, and quality insurance services. The opening ceremony, an open house event, was attended by representatives from the public and private sectors, including the Central Bank of Liberia and the Liberia Insurance Association.

Habib Dia, Managing Director/CEO of SUNU Assurances Liberia, described the relocation as a step towards becoming closer, more accessible, and more responsive to customers. He reaffirmed the company's dedication to offering reliable insurance solutions for individuals, businesses, properties, and wealth. Dia also expressed gratitude to clients, regulators, brokers, partners, shareholders, and the community for their ongoing support, emphasizing these relationships are fundamental to the company's success.

As part of the SUNU Group, the company aims to grow alongside Liberia, delivering excellence in customer service and risk management. The new Sinkor office is strategically located to enhance client accessibility and strengthen the company's service capabilities. SUNU Assurances Liberia is now fully open for business at its new location and anticipates welcoming both existing and prospective clients.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the company's expansion and commitment to growth and improved service delivery in Liberia.

Evangelical Leader Seeks Deeper Liberia-Israel Business Ties

Association of Evangelicals of Liberia President Bishop Robert Bimba met with the Israeli Ambassador to Ghana to discuss agricultural and educational cooperation.

Bishop Robert Bimba, president of the Association of Evangelicals of Liberia (AEL), has called for enhanced cooperation between Liberia and Israel, focusing on agriculture, education, and faith-based initiatives. This call followed a meeting with Israeli Ambassador to Ghana Roey Gilad in Accra, where discussions explored deepening bilateral relations. Bimba, who also directs the Israel Allies Foundation in Liberia and heads the Community of Hope Agriculture Project, presented a sample of Liberian rice to the ambassador, highlighting the nation's agricultural potential and discussing a proposed dairy farming initiative.

The engagement aimed to leverage faith communities and government efforts to foster development, agricultural transformation, and educational opportunities. Ambassador Gilad encouraged Liberians to utilize available resources and networks for national development. The AEL, representing approximately one million evangelicals in Liberia, also distributed 2,500 solar-powered Gospel lights to pastors and church leaders across the country on August 18.

Bimba appealed to President Joseph Nyuma Boakai to consider an official visit to Israel in 2026, citing Liberia's historical support for Israel's establishment at the United Nations. He believes stronger ties could unlock opportunities in agriculture, education, youth development, and religious pilgrimage, utilizing faith-based diplomacy to pursue development goals for Liberia.

Why it mattersLiberian evangelicals and agricultural stakeholders may benefit from increased cooperation and potential development initiatives stemming from stronger ties with Israel.

Five Liberians Earn UK Chevening Scholarships

Five Liberian scholars will pursue one-year master's degrees in the United Kingdom through the Chevening program.

The British Embassy in Liberia announced five scholars have been selected for the 2026 Chevening Awards, a UK government scholarship program for future leaders. These scholars will travel to the United Kingdom to complete one-year master's degrees at British universities. The program aims to provide fully funded scholarships to individuals who may otherwise find advanced study unaffordable, fostering future leaders in fields such as finance, healthcare, and governance.

The selected scholars and their fields of study are William M. Yarsiah (Special and Inclusive Education at the University of Nottingham), Hawa M. Kamara (Education Studies at Bangor University), Darcess Dossen (Environmental Hazards and Disaster at Cardiff University), Francis M. Bee (Water, Sanitation and Health Engineering at the University of Leeds), and Molley M. Korleh (Port Management at Liverpool John Moores University). The British Embassy congratulated the scholars, wishing them success in their studies and future contributions to Liberia.

Chevening scholarships are designed to equip scholars with academic skills and professional connections, enabling them to become pioneers in their fields and bring back expertise to benefit their home countries. The program boasts a global alumni network of over 50,000, including many prominent figures in politics and business. Chevening has been active in Liberia since 2015, aiming to strengthen ties between the UK and Liberia through educational exchange.

Why it mattersFive Liberian scholars will gain advanced education in the UK, potentially bringing back skills to benefit Liberia's development.

Liberian Students in Rwanda Honor Superintendent Johnson, Receive US$1,200

Grand Bassa County Superintendent Karyou Johnson donated US$1,200 to the Bassa Students Association in Rwanda, recognizing their academic efforts and organization.

The Bassa Students Association in Rwanda (BASIR) honored Grand Bassa County Superintendent Karyou Johnson for his support of their education and welfare. During a gathering, Johnson reaffirmed his commitment to Liberian students abroad and donated US$1,200 towards BASIR’s programs, fulfilling a prior pledge. He encouraged graduating students to return to Liberia and apply their acquired knowledge to national development, emphasizing that education abroad should foster solutions for home.

Superintendent Johnson praised BASIR for its organization, noting their ability to maintain a high level of cooperation without direct sponsorship. He highlighted the achievements of Liberian students in Rwanda as evidence of their potential to positively impact their educational community. Johnson urged graduates to remain resilient in their pursuit of employment and to embrace creativity in leveraging their skills for economic contribution.

Johnson advised students to look beyond traditional job seeking and develop innovative approaches to earning a living and contributing to Liberia’s economy. He shared his own experience transitioning from university to professional life, cautioning that a degree alone does not guarantee employment. The superintendent encouraged graduates to actively seek opportunities and demonstrate the relevance of their foreign education through their work back home.

Why it mattersLiberian students studying in Rwanda are directly affected by the superintendent's donation and encouragement to apply their education back home.

Robbers Steal Over US$439,000 from Rubber Companies

Unidentified armed men overpowered security at the CRC-MOPP office in Maryland County, making off with cash and documents.

An armed robbery at the shared central office of Cavalla Rubber Corporation (CRC) and MOPP in Pleebo-Sodoken District, Maryland County, has resulted in the loss of over US$439,000, along with Liberian and CFA francs and important company documents. The incident, described as unprecedented, occurred around 3 a.m. when unidentified armed men entered the compound and overpowered security personnel. Three security officers were injured during the attack and are receiving treatment.

The stolen money was intended for the monthly payment of employees of CRC and MOPP. Preliminary information indicates CRC had US$203, L$12,150 and CFA 30,000, while MOPP reportedly had US$235,722, L$270 and CFA 190,000. An ERU officer, Sergeant Romeo Payne, sustained multiple injuries and was disarmed of his weapon, though his M4 rifle was later recovered by authorities.

Two private security officers, Orailey Nance and Moses Dennis, were also injured. The attackers reportedly made off with important company documents in addition to the cash. Authorities are conducting a comprehensive investigation into the early-morning raid, which has disrupted the planned employee payroll for both rubber corporations.

Why it mattersEmployees of CRC and MOPP are directly affected because the money stolen was intended for their monthly salaries.
