Liberia Anti-Corruption Commission Pushes for Asset Recovery

The Liberia Anti-Corruption Commission celebrated its 18th anniversary, highlighting progress in asset declarations and prosecution powers while seeking enhanced support.

The Liberia Anti-Corruption Commission (LACC) marked its 18th anniversary by emphasizing the need for stronger corruption prevention, institutional collaboration, and the recovery of public resources. Executive Chairperson Cllr. Alexandra K. Zoe noted significant institutional growth and legal reforms, including the LACC's amended Act granting direct prosecutorial powers. However, she stressed that the fight against corruption requires broader public participation and greater resources.

During the anniversary event, the LACC reported over 3,000 asset declarations under the current administration, the highest number in its history. The commission has also concluded 81 corruption-related cases between 2013 and 2023. Minister of State for Presidential Affairs Samuel A. Stevquoah underscored the importance of accountability for Liberia's development, warning that personal enrichment through public office erodes public trust.

The LACC seeks increased budgetary support and a specialized anti-corruption court to expedite trials and asset recovery. Cllr. Zoe stated that the commission remains understaffed and under-resourced to effectively tackle the scale of corruption challenges. The 2026–2030 Strategic Plan focuses on prevention, enforcement, partnerships, and institutional strengthening, with renewed calls for citizens to report corruption and raise awareness.

Why it mattersThe LACC's efforts to prosecute corruption and recover stolen assets directly impact citizens' trust in government and the potential for development.

Liberia Establishes National Drug Committee

The National Inter-Ministerial Drug Committee was formally created in 1995 to address drug control matters for the government.

The National Inter-Ministerial Drug Committee (NIDC) was established on June 2, 1995, by the Liberian Council of State, then headed by David Kpomakpor. This committee, operating under the Minister of Justice, J. Laveli Supuwood, with its Secretariat led by Cllr. Joseph K. Jallah, was mandated to handle all drug control matters for the government. This formal governmental policy mandate distinguished the NIDC from being merely an ad hoc project, as evidenced by the 1998 Act that later created the Drug Enforcement Agency (DEA).

The 1998 Act dissolving the NIDC and transferring its functions to the newly established DEA clarifies the institutional sequence. The Act's preamble states that illicit drug trafficking threatened Liberia’s economy, stability, and security, necessitating the NIDC's creation. This legal text provides stronger historical evidence of the NIDC's role than relying solely on agency websites. The NIDC's broad mandate suggests it was intended as a national coordinating mechanism for drug policy.

By 1996, the NIDC gained international visibility, as indicated by contemporary newspaper accounts from Monrovia. The committee's formal establishment and broad mandate were crucial steps in Liberia's efforts to combat narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances, particularly in the aftermath of the civil crisis. Its dissolution and the subsequent transfer of its specialized functions to the DEA marked a significant evolution in the country's drug control strategy.

Why it mattersThe NIDC was created to address drug control matters, indicating a governmental response to threats against Liberia's economy, stability, and security.

Supreme Court Dismisses Appeal, Allows Judgment Enforcement

Liberia's Supreme Court dismissed an appeal by Urban Marketing Group, allowing a lower court to enforce a November 2025 judgment.

The Supreme Court of Liberia has dismissed an appeal filed by Urban Marketing Group, led by CEO Allen R. Brown, in a dispute concerning the estate of the late John Hilary Tubman. This ruling, handed down on August 28, allows the lower court to resume jurisdiction and enforce its November 28, 2025 judgment. The court found that the respondents failed to complete mandatory steps for their appeal, including filing an appeal bond and a notice of completion.

The Supreme Court's decision, made during its March Term 2026, cited Liberia’s Civil Procedure Law, which mandates dismissal for non-compliance with appeal requirements. The Court stated that once a bill of exceptions is signed, jurisdiction transfers to the appellate court, except for limited ministerial tasks. However, the failure to file the required bond and notice meant the appeal could not proceed, leading to its dismissal. Costs were awarded against the respondents.

This procedural victory for Urban Marketing Group removes a significant obstacle to enforcing the original judgment, which stemmed from a Joint Venture Agreement and subsequent arbitration. The Supreme Court did not rule on the merits of the underlying dispute but rather on the procedural failures of the appeal. The case will now return to the lower court for the enforcement of its confirmed arbitration award.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the ruling allows for the enforcement of a business judgment, potentially resolving a long-standing dispute and clarifying financial obligations.

Senator Kogar Accuses Government of Party Expulsion Interference

Nimba County Senator Samuel G. Kogar alleges government backing in his expulsion from the Movement for Democracy and Reconstruction (MDR) party.

Nimba County Senator Samuel G. Kogar has accused the Liberian government of interfering in his expulsion from the Movement for Democracy and Reconstruction (MDR) party. The MDR Executive Committee voted on August 29 to expel Kogar following an investigation into complaints. Kogar rejected the decision, citing the presence of police officers at the MDR headquarters as evidence of government involvement and stating he would challenge any action taken outside the party’s legal procedures.

MDR Party Chairman Cllr. Cooper W. Kruah stated that the expulsion followed due process and was necessary to protect the party's alliance with the ruling Unity Party. Kruah asserted that Kogar was given opportunities to respond to allegations and that the party followed its constitutional requirements. He ordered Kogar’s photograph removed from party headquarters after the vote.

Kogar, who did not provide further evidence linking the government to the decision, warned of legal action if his rights were violated and called for international and civil society monitoring. The expulsion follows failed reconciliation efforts with Vice President Koung and could impact the 2029 elections, according to reports.

Why it mattersSenator Kogar is affected by the expulsion from his political party, potentially impacting his political standing and future electoral prospects.

ArcelorMittal Liberia Honors Rail Workers for Preventing Safety Incident

Four ArcelorMittal Liberia rail employees received awards for their vigilance in averting a potential safety hazard on the company's main railway line.

ArcelorMittal Liberia recognized four rail department employees for their quick actions in preventing a serious safety incident. Train Drivers Norman Chidarikire and Rynier McCabe, along with Train Assistants Stanley Mulbah and Horace S. Neppy, were honored at an award ceremony in Buchanan. The crew identified a potential hazard during operations on the main railway line and responded decisively, safeguarding lives and critical infrastructure.

Company officials stated that the employees demonstrated exceptional situational awareness and composure under pressure, applying their training and professional judgment. Rail General Manager Felipe Lage highlighted that while procedures and equipment offer protection, trained employees are vital for preventing incidents when other measures fall short. He emphasized that the team's actions were a testament to the skill and dedication of the railway workforce.

The railway operated by ArcelorMittal Liberia connects its mining operations in Nimba County to the Port of Buchanan in Grand Bassa County. This corridor passes through communities and includes road crossings, making safety a significant responsibility. Buchanan Health and Safety Manager Togar Carter noted that the employees' intervention protected not only company assets but also the public traveling along the rail corridor.
