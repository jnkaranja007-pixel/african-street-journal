Liberia ports to operate 24 hours to cut costs

The government announced immediate relief measures including removal of shipping fees and customs bottlenecks to ease congestion.

Liberia's government has announced relief measures and long-term reforms at the Freeport of Monrovia, including plans to introduce 24-hour port operations. The measures were announced on Monday, November 16, after a high-level meeting of the Economic Management Team, the National Security Council and port stakeholders.

Finance and Development Planning Minister Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan said shipping lines are expected to remove the Delivery Order Fee after consulting their principals, with those consultations due to conclude within one week. He said informal charges must stop and that all legitimate fees must be transparent and communicated to businesses before implementation.

The Liberia Revenue Authority has committed to deploying additional personnel to address complaints about customs inspections and cargo clearance, and the government expects meaningful progress within 14 days. The government also plans to support a designated truck parking and servicing area around the old Bong Mines section of the port.

Why it mattersImporters and traders face lower port charges and faster customs clearance, with the government expecting progress on complaints within 14 days.

Liberia launches 285 machines for rural roads

President Boakai calls for maintenance amid public criticism over the procurement and cost of the heavy equipment.

President Joseph Nyuma Boakai launched 285 heavy-duty earth-moving machines nationwide on Monday to improve rural road connectivity across Liberia's 15 counties. The initiative aims to rehabilitate feeder roads, connect farming communities to markets, and improve access to essential services. The first machines arrived in February 2026, with the final batch delivered in May, totaling 285 units.

The president described the deployment as a major infrastructure intervention, noting that poor road conditions isolate rural communities and increase the cost of transporting agricultural products. "We are launching the national deployment of 285 pieces of heavy earth-moving equipment, organized through 19 operational hubs serving all 15 counties of Liberia," Boakai announced at the Liberia Broadcasting System in Paynesville.

Boakai warned that the success of the investment depends on professional management, training, accountability, and regular maintenance, stating, "The acquisition of this heavy equipment is only the first step. The real challenge is to maintain these machines." He stressed the need for regular inspections, preventive maintenance, adequate resources, and technical training for operators and mechanics.

Why it mattersFarmers will have better access to markets for their produce as 285 machines are deployed to improve rural roads.

Sirleaf warns Liberian leaders against threatening the country's peace

Former President Ellen Johnson Sirleaf urged those in power to pursue inclusion, equal opportunity and true unity to protect Liberia from a return to conflict.

Former Liberian President Ellen Johnson Sirleaf has warned people in power to prioritise national unity, political inclusion and equal opportunity, saying these are needed to guard against a return to conflict. She said Liberia's peace must not be taken for granted and requires deliberate effort from political leaders and every Liberian.

Sirleaf made the remarks in a goodwill message at the 2026 West African Women Peacebuilding Convening in Monrovia, hosted by the GBOWEE Peace Foundation Africa with LUMA as co-host. She said Liberia's peace before the 12 April 1980 coup was not built on genuine unity, inclusion or equal opportunity, and that their absence later fed decades of conflict. She also credited women who kept advocating during the peace talks in Accra, Ghana.

Sirleaf said Liberia has enjoyed 23 consecutive years of peace and urged citizens not to become complacent. She said peace cannot rest on the mere absence of war and must be backed by justice, national cohesion and equal participation.

Why it mattersPolitical leaders and citizens are being asked to build inclusion and justice into everyday governance, which Sirleaf says is the only lasting protection against renewed conflict.

Singer Hage uses music to fight estate battle

Raquel Hage released a five-track EP to express frustration with the Liberian justice system over her mother's medical care.

Liberian singer Raquel Hage released a new five-track EP titled Justice on November 13, using music to express her family's ongoing struggle with the Liberian court system. The songs address the delays and perceived injustices surrounding the estate of her late father, Milad Hage, which the family needs access to for her mother's medical treatment in New Delhi, India.

Raquel Hage stated that the EP is a "beef album" intended to voice what years of legal filings have not achieved, as her family seeks funds from her father's estate to cover her mother's severe kidney problems, hypertension, and other health conditions. The family blames these health issues on stress experienced since Milad Hage's death in 2010, and they are petitioning the probate court for access to money held in escrow.

The EP's tracks, including "Shame on You" and "Fraudster," target the government, the justice system, and a relative accused of fraud, whom Raquel Hage claims a jury found liable. She expressed hope in exposing those she believes are responsible, referencing a Liberian proverb about a thief's luck eventually running out.

Why it mattersThe Hage family is seeking court permission to access funds from a deceased father's estate to pay for the mother's medical treatment in India.

Liberia's Road Fund wins 2026 Builder Award

The National Road Fund received continental recognition for its leadership in sustainable road maintenance financing.

The National Road Fund of Liberia (NRF) officially received the 2026 Builder Award trophy and certificate on November 9, 2026, in Monrovia. The award was conferred on May 27, 2026, at the Africa Road Builders ceremony in Brazzaville, Republic of the Congo. NRF Manager Mrs. Joseta Neufville-Wento accepted the honour, which recognizes leadership in sustainable road maintenance financing. The NRF missed the May ceremony due to health and travel concerns linked to the regional Ebola outbreak.

The award comes as the NRF reports improvements in revenue collection and institutional performance. Under Mrs. Neufville-Wento's leadership, NRF revenue grew from US$18 million to over US$33 million. The fund also reported a 93 percent implementation rate for auditor general recommendations by January 5, 2026, with 53 of 60 recommendations fully implemented. These improvements support road-user revenue streams and maintenance planning.

The NRF's October 2026 Executive Budget Hearing presentation noted a 3 percent revenue performance increase over fiscal year 2025. The fund also supported over 3,000 short-term jobs and financed road corridors like Robertsport-Medina. These initiatives, along with community-based maintenance support, aim to connect communities, markets, schools, and health facilities through reliable road financing.

Why it mattersThe National Road Fund's improved revenue collection and institutional performance support road maintenance, connecting communities, markets, schools, and health facilities.
