Liberian clinician urges response to estimated 56,000 HIV cases

Patience Lincoln says weak coordination and breaches of patient confidentiality are undermining prevention and treatment across Liberia.

Source: The New Dawn Liberia · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Patience Lincoln, a graduating public health student at the University of Liberia, urged Liberia’s government to strengthen its response to HIV, citing a 2026 estimate of about 56,000 people living with the virus. She described the situation as a national public health emergency.

Lincoln said poor coordination among the Ministry of Health, the National Public Health Institute of Liberia and the National AIDS Commission had weakened efforts to curb transmission. She also alleged that some health workers disclosed patients’ HIV status to relatives and acquaintances, exposing them to discrimination and social rejection.

She said fear of stigma discourages some people from seeking care and following treatment. Lincoln called for stricter enforcement of confidentiality rules, more public awareness, increased budget support and expanded prevention and treatment programmes; she also urged health authorities to improve professional ethics and coordination.

Why it mattersPeople living with HIV face breaches of confidentiality that Lincoln says expose them to discrimination and discourage care and treatment.
