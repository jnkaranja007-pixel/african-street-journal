CDC alleges US$50,000 paid to oust MOVEE leader Kemayah

The opposition party says Vice President Jeremiah Kpan Koung funded a faction seeking to remove the former foreign minister from party leadership.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Liberia’s Congress for Democratic Change (CDC) accused Vice President Jeremiah Kpan Koung of financing a faction in the Movement for Economic Empowerment (MOVEE) to remove its political leader, former Foreign Minister Dee-Maxwell Saah Kemayah Sr. In a statement released in Monrovia on Saturday, November 21, the CDC alleged US$50,000 was provided to MOVEE executive members to suspend Kemayah and push him out of the leadership.

The CDC did not identify the alleged recipients or provide documents or transaction records to support the claim, FrontPage Africa reported. Kemayah faces internal disciplinary action while challenging a criminal conviction. The CDC described his suspension as politically motivated; Koung’s alleged role has not been independently substantiated.

The CDC also said a MOVEE campaign vehicle had been returned to it under a 2023 electoral campaign agreement and that it holds the vehicle and title documents. It did not provide the agreement or vehicle records.

Why it mattersMOVEE's executive members are at the centre of a leadership dispute involving an alleged US$50,000 payment, which the CDC has not substantiated.
