Liberia's agriculture minister warns El Niño could cut rice yields by up to 50%

Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah told lawmakers severe conditions could reduce yields by 30 to 50% for crops planted during the affected period.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Liberia’s agriculture minister, Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah, warned lawmakers that potential El Niño conditions could disrupt planting schedules and cut rice yields by 30 to 50% for crops planted during the affected period. He spoke alongside Environmental Protection Agency Executive Director Dr. Emmanuel Urey Yarkpawolo at the House of Representatives.

Nuetah said failed rains after farmers plant in March could destroy crops, while excessive rain could rot seeds before they germinate. He said rice varieties need 90 to 150 days to mature, and shorter rainy seasons could leave long-cycle plants without enough water during flowering and grain formation.

The officials also warned that intense heat could hinder germination and increase pests, while heavy rain could flood swamp rice fields. Nuetah said these risks could threaten food security in rural communities dependent on rice.

Why it mattersRice farmers in rural communities face potential yield losses of 30 to 50% under severe El Niño conditions.
