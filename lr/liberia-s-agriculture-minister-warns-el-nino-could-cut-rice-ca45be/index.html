Liberia’s agriculture minister warns El Niño could cut rice yields by 30 to 50%

Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah told lawmakers that erratic rains and heat could disrupt planting and leave rice farmers with little or no harvest.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Liberia’s Agriculture Minister Dr. J. Alexander Nuetah and Environmental Protection Agency Executive Director Dr. Emmanuel Urey Yarkpawolo warned the House of Representatives that a potential El Niño could disrupt planting and reduce food production. They said several counties were already experiencing severe dryness.

Nuetah said failed rains after March planting could destroy crops, while excessive rain could rot seeds before they germinate. Rice varieties take 90 days to 150 days to mature, and a shortened rainy season could leave longer-growing crops without enough water during flowering. Severe El Niño conditions could cut rice yields by 30 to 50% for crops planted during the affected period, he said.

The officials warned that flooding could also submerge swamp rice already planted. Nuetah said the risks extend to rural communities that depend heavily on rice production.

Why it mattersRice farmers in rural communities face disrupted planting and possible yield losses of 30 to 50% under severe El Niño conditions.
