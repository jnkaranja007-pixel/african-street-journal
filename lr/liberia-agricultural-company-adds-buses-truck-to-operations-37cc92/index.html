Liberia Agricultural Company Adds Buses, Truck to Operations

The company commissioned two new school buses and a heavy-duty truck to improve education and plantation logistics.

The Liberia Agricultural Company (LAC) has introduced two new school buses and a TATA truck to enhance its educational and operational services across its Grand Bassa County plantation. General Manager Naveen Madan commissioned the vehicles over the weekend, ahead of the upcoming academic and operational year. LAC operates eleven schools serving approximately 7,750 students, with 45% being girls, and also admits students from surrounding communities and runs an adult literacy program.

The new buses are intended to improve student transportation, reducing travel time and potentially allowing more time for study. LAC management anticipates the expanded fleet will offer greater flexibility and minimize vehicle downtime, ensuring reliable school transport. The company also commissioned a heavy-duty TATA truck for rubber collection and other logistical tasks, signaling an investment in both educational support and plantation efficiency.

During the commissioning, General Manager Madan urged drivers to maintain the vehicles and cautioned students against damaging property. He emphasized responsible use of the new assets for their intended purposes. Madan stated that this investment is part of a broader strategy to improve services for employees, students, and nearby communities, with further enhancements anticipated.
