Liberia officials appeal 10-year sentences in rice case

Foreign Affairs Minister Kemayah and co-defendants sentenced to prison for US$500,000 Saudi rice diversion will seek Supreme Court review.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

10 years in prison: that is the sentence Foreign Affairs Minister Dee-Maxwell Saah Kemayah and some co-defendants received in Liberia's US$500,000 Saudi Rice case, and they have now lodged an appeal with the Supreme Court. Sentences of five years went to other convicted defendants. The sentencing took place on Friday, November 13, 2026, at the Temple of Justice in Monrovia.

Judge Ousman Feika, presiding over Criminal Court 'C', refused the defence's bid for a retrial after a jury on October 9, 2026 found eight of the 13 defendants guilty. Among those convicted were Kemayah and former Internal Affairs Minister Varney A. Sirleaf. The case concerns the alleged diversion of humanitarian rice donated by the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

The defence says the jury's findings do not match the evidence heard over a trial of more than 40 days. The Supreme Court, Liberia's highest judicial authority, will now review the trial proceedings, the verdicts, the refusal of a new trial and the sentences.

Why it mattersConvicted officials, including a sitting minister, remain free while the Supreme Court considers the appeal, and the outcome will decide whether the 10-year and five-year terms stand.
