Boakai signs US$5.7 billion Bea Mountain infrastructure deal

The agreement covers four power plants, reservoirs and more than 300 kilometres of transmission lines, after months of negotiation.

Source: The New Dawn Liberia · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Liberian President Joseph Nyuma Boakai signed a US$5.7 billion infrastructure agreement with Bea Mountain Mining Corporation on Wednesday, November 25, after months of negotiations. According to the government, the deal provides for four power plants with a combined generation capacity of 270 megawatts.

The agreement also provides for reservoirs with a storage capacity of 10 billion cubic metres, more than 300 kilometres of transmission lines, roads, bridges and highways, and upgrades to electricity substations.

Former President Ellen Johnson Sirleaf had called for an independent professional review of the agreement before implementation, citing its financial and other implications. Walter McCarthy, a former deputy minister who served in her government, said he resigned at the end of her first tenure and urged debate to focus on the agreement's merits.

Why it mattersPower users and rural communities will eventually draw on 270 megawatts of new generation and 10 billion cubic metres of stored water, once the plants and reservoirs are built.
