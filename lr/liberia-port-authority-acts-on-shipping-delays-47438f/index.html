Liberia port authority acts on shipping delays

National Port Authority announces direct Maersk shipping and extended operations to ease congestion and rising fees.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-06 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Liberia's National Port Authority (NPA) is implementing new measures to tackle severe congestion and lengthy cargo delays at the Freeport of Monrovia. NPA Managing Director Sekou A. Dukuly announced that shipping giant Maersk will now deliver vessels directly to Monrovia, bypassing transshipment through other West African ports. This move aims to reduce transit times and associated costs for Liberian importers who have protested mounting fees and prolonged delays.

Dukuly acknowledged the port's undercapacity, which has left approximately 700 containers stranded for over 30 days, creating significant financial strain on businesses. The NPA plans to clear these containers, suspend berth repairs to allow simultaneous vessel berthing, and extend working hours. The authority is also considering granting additional free days for importers facing demurrage and storage charges.

The NPA is exploring engagement with the Central Bank of Liberia and commercial banks to offer short-term financial relief to affected businesses. This could include easing loan repayment obligations for importers whose capital is tied up in delayed shipments. These proposals come after importers, many of whom secured business loans against personal property, reported threats to their operations due to accumulating costs and delays.

Why it mattersLiberian importers and business owners face financial ruin due to prolonged shipping delays and escalating port fees.
