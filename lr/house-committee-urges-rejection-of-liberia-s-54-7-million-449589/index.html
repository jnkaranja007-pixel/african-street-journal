House committee urges rejection of Liberia's $54.7 million identity deal

The committee found more than 40 defects in a concession that named Austrian firm OeSD as operator without a competitive bid.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

A Liberian House joint committee has recommended rejecting a $54.7 million concession to rebuild the national identity system, saying the government selected Austrian firm OeSD International GmbH without a bid. The report, dated Nov. 18, 2026, found more than 40 defects in the agreement.

The committee said some defects were statutory violations that occurred before the agreement was signed and could not be cured by legislative ratification. It also cited a $19.82 million data center that it said Liberia did not need, because the state telecommunications company LIBTELCO already has one. The proposed deal would replace a biometric platform built by Techno Brain after an open tender among 19 firms in 2017.

The identity card is required for banking, buying a SIM card, seeing a doctor, enrolling in school and dealing with immigration under an April 2025 executive order. The existing platform is used by the National Identification Registry, the National Social Security and Welfare Corporation and the National Elections Commission.

Why it mattersLiberians who need the card for banking, phone service, health care, school and immigration face uncertainty over the system those services require.
