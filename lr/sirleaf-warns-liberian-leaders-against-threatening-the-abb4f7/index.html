Sirleaf warns Liberian leaders against threatening the country's peace

Former President Ellen Johnson Sirleaf urged those in power to pursue inclusion, equal opportunity and true unity to protect Liberia from a return to conflict.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Former Liberian President Ellen Johnson Sirleaf has warned people in power to prioritise national unity, political inclusion and equal opportunity, saying these are needed to guard against a return to conflict. She said Liberia's peace must not be taken for granted and requires deliberate effort from political leaders and every Liberian.

Sirleaf made the remarks in a goodwill message at the 2026 West African Women Peacebuilding Convening in Monrovia, hosted by the GBOWEE Peace Foundation Africa with LUMA as co-host. She said Liberia's peace before the 12 April 1980 coup was not built on genuine unity, inclusion or equal opportunity, and that their absence later fed decades of conflict. She also credited women who kept advocating during the peace talks in Accra, Ghana.

Sirleaf said Liberia has enjoyed 23 consecutive years of peace and urged citizens not to become complacent. She said peace cannot rest on the mere absence of war and must be backed by justice, national cohesion and equal participation.

Why it mattersPolitical leaders and citizens are being asked to build inclusion and justice into everyday governance, which Sirleaf says is the only lasting protection against renewed conflict.
