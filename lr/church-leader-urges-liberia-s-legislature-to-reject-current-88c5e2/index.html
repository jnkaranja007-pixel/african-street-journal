Church leader urges Liberia's Legislature to reject current mining infrastructure deal

Rev. Samuel B. Reeves says the draft lets Bea Mountain Mining Corporation recover costs through tax offsets, with no all-in limit on what Liberia pays.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-23 · 2 min read

The president of the Liberia Council of Churches, Rev. Dr. Samuel B. Reeves Jr., has urged the Legislature not to ratify the current version of a proposed infrastructure agreement with Bea Mountain Mining Corporation (BMMC). Reeves said Liberia would receive roads, power plants and transmission lines, but would pay for them by redirecting future mining-related public revenue, with interest.

In his analysis of the draft Master Infrastructure Development Agreement, Reeves put the eight primary projects at a base cost of US$4.4513 billion, including US$50 million for land and resettlement compensation, plus US$1.23 billion for additional roads and grid reinforcement, for a total of US$5.6813 billion before interest and other charges. Under the draft, BMMC could offset corporate income tax, royalties, dividend withholding tax and dividends due on Liberia's 5% stake against its costs. Reeves said the copy he reviewed has no payment milestone schedule in Annex C, no technical specifications in Annex E and no all-in ceiling on what Liberia ultimately pays.

BMMC must keep US$500 million in financing available at any one time, and the facility refills as costs are recovered. Reeves warned that no minimum share of the offset payments is guaranteed to reach the Treasury in cash, which would leave less money for health, education and other priorities.

Why it mattersPublic revenue from mining that would otherwise reach the Treasury in cash is offset against BMMC's project costs, leaving less money for health and education under the draft.
