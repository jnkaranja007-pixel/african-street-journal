Taylor seeks separate trial and Feika’s removal from US$317 million case

The former vice president’s lawyers filed both motions as pretrial proceedings began in the cocaine trafficking prosecution involving 20 defendants.

Sources: FrontPage Africa and The New Dawn Liberia · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Former Liberian Vice President Jewel Howard Taylor has asked Criminal Court C to separate her trial from those of 19 co-defendants in a US$317 million cocaine trafficking case and to remove presiding Judge Ousman F. Feika. Her lawyers filed the two motions as pretrial proceedings began on Wednesday, November 25.

The severance request would let Taylor face trial separately from the other defendants. In a separate motion, her defense alleges bias, hostility and prejudgment by Feika and seeks his recusal. The allegations are the defense’s claims; the court has yet to rule on either request.

The court must consider both motions before the substantive trial can proceed. Taylor was arrested on August 19 at Roberts International Airport and was placed under home detention after Feika granted her medical release on September 4.

Why it mattersTaylor and her 19 co-defendants await the court’s decisions on separate trials and whether Feika will continue presiding before the substantive trial proceeds.
