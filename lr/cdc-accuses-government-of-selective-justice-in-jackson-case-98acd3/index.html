CDC accuses government of selective justice in Jackson case

The opposition party demands economist Samuel P. Jackson receive medical treatment abroad, citing alleged double standards.

Source: The New Dawn Liberia · 2026-11-16 · 1 min read

The opposition Congress for Democratic Change (CDC) accused Liberia's Unity Party-led government of selective justice on Monday, November 16, 2026. CDC National Chairman Attorney Janga Kowo demanded that economist and political commentator Samuel P. Jackson be allowed to seek specialized medical treatment abroad while facing prosecution in connection with his wife's death.

Kowo stated that Jackson's deteriorating health should not be ignored and called on judicial authorities to urgently consider his request for medical relief. Jackson reportedly petitioned Criminal Court ‘A’ Judge Onesimus Barwon on October 23, 2026, for permission to travel. He posted a photograph in November showing him in bed, stating, “I’m losing my appetite.”

The CDC chairman accused the government of double standards, referencing the reported granting of compassionate medical leave to former Chief Justice Gloria Musu-Scott. Kowo alleged Jackson’s treatment was politically motivated due to his criticism of the administration. Jackson faces charges including manslaughter and aggravated assault following his wife's death on March 13, 2026.

Why it mattersThe opposition CDC claims the government is politically persecuting Samuel P. Jackson by denying him medical treatment abroad, while he faces charges in connection with his wife's death.
