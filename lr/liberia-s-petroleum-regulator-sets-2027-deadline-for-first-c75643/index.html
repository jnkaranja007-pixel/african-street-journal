Liberia’s petroleum regulator sets 2027 deadline for first sector report

The five-year plan also follows US$13.8 million in budget contributions and eight production-sharing contracts ratified by lawmakers.

Sources: FrontPage Africa and The New Dawn Liberia · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Liberia’s Petroleum Regulatory Authority (LPRA) launched a five-year plan in Monrovia on Thursday, setting out priorities for the oil and gas industry through 2030 and promising its first State of the Petroleum Sector Report in 2027. Director General Marilyn T. Logan said the roadmap is intended to strengthen regulation, attract investment and build local technical expertise.

Logan said the authority contributed US$13.8 million to Liberia’s national budget over the past three years. She also said Liberia had gone more than a decade without a new Production Sharing Contract before the current leadership took office in March 2024; since then, the authority signed eight, all ratified by the Legislature.

The LPRA said reconnaissance is underway in the Harper Basin, and its 2026 Direct Negotiation Licensing Round, launched last month, is expected to beat its 2025 results. The plan includes contract management, environmental protection and transparency.

Why it mattersThe LPRA must publish its first account of the petroleum sector in 2027, giving investors and local businesses a scheduled source of industry information.
