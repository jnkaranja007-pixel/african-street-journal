Liberian judges warn of court corruption

Circuit judges across Liberia used their court term openings to highlight systemic issues.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-12 · 2 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Liberian circuit judges have publicly voiced concerns about corruption, favouritism, and political interference within the nation's courts. During the November 2026 court term openings in various counties, judges used their formal addresses, known as charges, to warn that these issues are eroding public trust. This comes as the justice system faces criticism for delays and a lack of access for ordinary citizens.

In Zwedru, Grand Gedeh County, Circuit Judge J. Kennedy Peabody stated that filling state jobs with relatives and punishing dissenting staff constitutes corruption. He emphasized that no one, including judges, is above the Constitution. Meanwhile, Judge Roosevelt Z. Willie of Criminal Court ‘A’ in Monrovia questioned if Liberia had learned from past justice system failures, referencing a 2006 report on corruption and influence in the courts. Judge Peter W. Gbeneweleh in Grand Kru County cautioned against the 'commercialization of justice,' warning it leads to mob violence.

These warnings follow Judge Onesimus Banwon's August statement that Monrovia Central Prison, also known as South Beach, is dangerously overcrowded with pre-trial detainees. The Constitution mandates arrested individuals be brought to court within 48 hours, a rule often unmet. The release of two pregnant women from South Beach, reportedly aided by former Vice President Jewel Howard-Taylor, highlighted the plight of those awaiting trial, many without the means to expedite their cases.

Why it mattersLiberians caught in the justice system face delays and a lack of access to lawyers and judges, leading to overcrowded prisons.
