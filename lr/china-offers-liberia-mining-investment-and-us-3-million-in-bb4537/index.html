China offers Liberia mining investment and US$3 million in rice aid

Beijing proposes that African countries own their mineral resources, while Liberian cocoa producers are set to gain access to the Chinese market from December 15, 2026.

Source: FrontPage Africa · 2026-11-13 · 1 min read

China is seeking to deepen its economic partnership with Liberia, offering mining investment, approximately US$3 million in rice assistance and expanded market access. The Chinese Embassy outlined the proposals at a media briefing in Monrovia on Wednesday, November 11.

The embassy set out a four-point proposal for China-Africa mining cooperation, starting from the principle that African resources belong to Africans. It says Chinese firms should improve operational standards and transfer green technologies, and it warns against turning mineral resources into instruments of geopolitical rivalry. Liberia depends heavily on iron ore, gold and diamonds for investment and export revenue.

Liberian cocoa producers are due to gain access to the Chinese market beginning December 15, 2026. The briefing also referred to President Xi Jinping's recent engagement with U.S. President Donald Trump, which Beijing described as an effort to build a relationship of strategic stability based on respect and reciprocity.

Why it mattersLiberian cocoa producers gain access to the Chinese market from December 15, 2026, and mining firms are offered a route to processing and skills development beyond raw mineral exports.
