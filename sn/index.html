Sport · 2026-11-07

Senegal lionceaux reach JOJ Dakar 2026 basketball quarter-finals

The team secured a second victory, defeating Lithuania 21-12 in their second group stage match.

Senegal's U18 basketball team, the Lionceaux, defeated Lithuania 21-12 on Friday in their second match at the JOJ Dakar 2026 tournament. This victory follows their earlier win against Algeria (16-13) and secures their place in the quarter-finals.

The match, played at 17:15 at the Complexe du tour de l’oeuf, saw Frédéric Clovice Mané lead Senegal with 13 points. Ibrahima Ndiaye contributed 4 points, Sacoura Diop scored 3, and Papa Mamadou Cissé added 1 point. The team achieved this success without losing a group stage game.

Senegal will play their quarter-final match on Sunday, November 8, in front of their home crowd. The semi-finals and finals are scheduled for Monday, November 9. Their opponent for the quarter-finals will be announced soon.

Health · 2026-11-07

Senegal reports 282 new leprosy cases

The nation has reached the World Health Organization's elimination threshold for the disease.

Senegal recorded 282 new cases of leprosy in 2025, according to data from the national elimination program. Papa Mamadou Ndiaye, president of the Senegalese association for the fight against leprosy, stated that while the country has met the World Health Organization's elimination threshold of 1 case per 10,000 inhabitants, new cases continue to be detected.

Ndiaye emphasized the ongoing challenge of eliminating leprosy transmission by 2030, a commitment Senegal has made. The government's policy aims to move affected communities from vulnerability to resilience. Ababacar Sadikh Niang, the deputy governor for development, noted that securing land and housing, ensuring access to basic social services, and providing sustainable livelihoods are key challenges.

A regional development committee meeting in Thiès discussed the resilience program for communities affected by leprosy. Senegal repealed a law from 1976 that had isolated and stigmatized people affected by leprosy and their families, replacing it with a law in June 2023 that recognizes former social resettlement villages as regular neighborhoods with full citizens.

Health · 2026-11-07

Senegal reports 548 cases of acute digestive syndrome

The Ministry of Health and Public Hygiene stated that patient conditions are stable with no severe cases or deaths recorded.

The Kébémer health district in Senegal's Louga region has recorded 548 cases of acute digestive syndrome, with 65 new cases reported between November 5 and 6, 2026. These infections were identified across nine sites within the district.

According to the Ministry of Health and Public Hygiene (MSHP), all patients are in stable condition, with three currently under observation and no severe cases or deaths reported to date. Joint investigation teams are working to identify a potential common source of contamination.

The MSHP is ensuring continued patient care and monitoring, especially in the most utilized health facilities. Awareness campaigns on good food and water hygiene practices are also underway. The ministry urges vigilance and adherence to preventive measures.

Why it mattersResidents of the Kébémer health district are affected by a widespread outbreak of acute digestive syndrome, with the Ministry of Health monitoring their conditions.
