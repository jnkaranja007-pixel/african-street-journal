Dakar Olympic Youth Games Trigger Road Restrictions

Traffic diversions and restricted access will be in place on the Autoroute de l'Avenir and around Diamniadio stadium for the opening ceremony.

Eleven days before the opening of the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games, the Governor of Dakar, Ousmane Kane, issued an order on October 20th detailing traffic and parking regulations for the Autoroute de l'Avenir and the vicinity of the Abdoulaye-Wade stadium in Diamniadio. The event is scheduled for Saturday, October 31st. A dedicated Olympic lane will operate on the left side of the Autoroute de l'Avenir's Patte-d'Oie–AIBD section from 7 AM to 11:30 PM, with traffic flow reversing direction in the evening.

Access to Exit 11, serving the stadium, will be restricted to vehicles with COJOJ accreditation from 7 AM to 11:30 PM. Spectators are directed to use shuttle buses or designated pedestrian routes, as only accredited vehicles and dedicated public transport will be permitted within the Diamniadio security perimeters. General traffic will use exits 10 and 12, with clear signage guiding spectators to parking areas and the stadium.

Heavy goods vehicles exceeding 3.5 tonnes will be prohibited on specific routes, including the toll highway between Patte-d'Oie and Sébikhotane, and sections of the RN2, from 7 AM to 11:30 PM. These restrictions aim to ensure the smooth running of the opening ceremony, with emergency access routes maintained and secured by various coordinating agencies.

Why it mattersThe restrictions will affect general commuters and heavy goods vehicles using the Autoroute de l'Avenir and surrounding roads.

Senegal Workers Mobilize for Three-Day General Strike

Hundreds of Senegalese workers marched in Dakar, signaling a 72-hour general strike set to begin Wednesday despite government appeals.

Hundreds of Senegalese workers took to the streets of Dakar on Tuesday afternoon, responding to a call from the Front national pour la défense du travail (FSDT). This mobilization precedes a planned 72-hour general strike commencing Wednesday. The FSDT, a collective representing 13 unions, is proceeding with the strike despite government efforts to dissuade them from their industrial action.

The strike action is also impacting the national electricity company, Senelec, where the SUTELEC union is maintaining pressure for October 21, 22, and 23. SUTELEC has denounced intimidation tactics by Senelec's general management. In response, Senelec has assured its customers that all necessary measures are in place to ensure the continuity and quality of electricity service, though specific details on these measures remain undisclosed.

Senelec stated its commitment to social dialogue and finding agreed-upon solutions within legal frameworks. This follows a company note from October 19, where management claimed no formal strike notice had been received from SUTELEC, with knowledge of the strike intention coming through unofficial channels. The company reminded staff of labor code regulations regarding strike notices and potential sanctions.

Why it mattersWorkers are striking for better conditions, potentially impacting their livelihoods and job security.

Senegal Dollar Bonds Drop After Sonko Budget Threat

Opposition leader Ousmane Sonko's threat to block the revised 2026 budget caused Senegal's dollar bonds to fall, impacting debt restructuring and IMF program talks.

Senegal's dollar-denominated bonds experienced their sharpest decline since early September following opposition leader Ousmane Sonko's vow to obstruct the government's revised 2026 budget and upcoming spending plan. This stance introduces risk to the West African nation's ongoing debt restructuring efforts and could disrupt the timeline for securing a new program with the International Monetary Fund (IMF).

Discussions between the government and the PASTEF-Les Patriotes parliamentary group regarding the draft supplementary finance law (LFR-2026) have stalled, with no "global and solid" consensus reached in the finance commission. While some compromises were made, significant disagreements persist on key points, leading to the postponement of the plenary session initially scheduled for October 23, 2026.

The finance commission is set to reconvene on October 22, 2026, at the government's request, which has also asked for additional supporting documents. The plenary session's review of the LFR-2026 is now slated for the following week. The PASTEF group reiterated its commitment to constructive dialogue for the benefit of the Senegalese people.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the political uncertainty and potential budget obstruction threaten Senegal's debt restructuring and IMF program, impacting bond values.

Senegal, Ivory Coast Leaders Discuss Trade and Security Cooperation

Senegalese Prime Minister Ahmadou Alhaminou Lô met with Ivorian President Alassane Ouattara and Vice President Tiémoko Meyliet Koné to strengthen bilateral ties.

Senegalese Prime Minister Ahmadou Alhaminou Lô concluded a 24-hour working visit to Abidjan, where he met with Ivorian President Alassane Ouattara and Vice President Tiémoko Meyliet Koné. The discussions focused on enhancing cooperation between the two West African nations, particularly in the critical sectors of security, trade, and public policy. These areas are identified as central to the existing relationship between Senegal and Ivory Coast.

The leaders explored avenues for deepening their partnership, including boosting commercial exchanges, enhancing security collaboration, and sharing experiences in public policy implementation. This visit underscores Dakar and Abidjan's commitment to solidifying historical bonds of friendship and solidarity. Both nations aim to increase joint actions for economic and social development and regional integration.

Through these high-level meetings, Senegal reaffirmed its dedication to a cooperative approach based on dialogue and joint problem-solving for regional challenges. The visit seeks to invigorate the Dakar-Abidjan axis and strengthen a partnership beneficial to both countries, fostering a new momentum for their collaboration.

Why it mattersFarmers may benefit from strengthened agricultural cooperation and shared policy experiences between Senegal and Ivory Coast.

CAS to Rule on Senegal AFCON Title Appeal by November End

The Court of Arbitration for Sport will announce its decision on Senegal's appeal to regain their Africa Cup of Nations title by the close of November.

Senegal's appeal to reclaim the Africa Cup of Nations title, which was overturned in March, is set for a verdict by the end of November. The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) announced Monday that deliberations are ongoing regarding the Confederation of African Football's (CAF) decision to award a 3-0 victory and the continental title to Morocco.

CAF's appeals panel had initially stripped Senegal of their title after their team walked off the field to protest a penalty awarded to Morocco, causing a 15-minute delay. The penalty was subsequently missed by Brahim Diaz. Senegal's government had vowed to pursue all avenues to regain the title, accusing the African soccer governing body of corruption.

The Switzerland-based court confirmed that its ruling will be delivered by the end of November. This decision follows Senegal's appeal against the CAF panel's judgment that faulted their protest and awarded the championship to Morocco.

Why it mattersSenegalese football fans are affected by the potential loss of their continental title due to a protest during a match.
