Senegal ONAS nears completion of 98% of flood control work

The National Sanitation Office has spent 250 million FCFA on four micro-projects ahead of the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games.

The National Sanitation Office (ONAS) in Senegal has completed 98% of its work to combat flooding and improve infrastructure for the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games. Director General Sény Dieng announced that the remaining finishing touches are being addressed. The organization has focused on key areas, including collectors near the Olympic swimming pool and the Iba Mar Diop stadium.

A total of 250 million FCFA, funded by the Games' organizing committee, supported four sanitation projects. These included a 60-meter collector at the Franco-Arab school for rainwater evacuation and a 340-meter wastewater collector with a diameter of 400-500 millimeters. Work also involved connecting Olympic village facilities to the sanitation network and addressing flooding issues in areas like Rue 22 de la Medina and Gibraltar Centenaire.

ONAS also replaced damaged grilles and renewed a collector in Gibraltar. Beyond the Games-related work, ONAS is conducting post-rainy season maintenance, including preventive desilting. Dieng stated that the Games provided an opportunity to rehabilitate collectors that were necessary but previously unfunded.

Why it mattersResidents of Dakar will experience reduced flooding and improved sanitation due to the completed infrastructure work.

Japan gives Senegal four ambulances for youth Olympics

The vehicles will boost health services during the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games.

Japan has donated four Type C medical ambulances to Senegal's Ministry of Health and Public Hygiene for the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games. Senegal's Minister of Health and Public Hygiene, Dr. Ibrahima Sy, received the ambulances from the Japanese Ambassador at a ceremony on October 30, 2026. These donations aim to strengthen health coverage and emergency response capabilities during the event.

The Japanese Ambassador stated that these gifts demonstrate the commitment of the Japanese government and people to Senegal during the Youth Olympic Games. Minister Sy acknowledged the strong cooperation between Senegal and Japan, citing past health projects like the expansion of Thiès Regional Hospital. The four ambulances are intended to enhance the medical system and emergency care capacity for the Games.

The Ministry of Health and Public Hygiene noted that 30 ambulances and over 600 health professionals have been identified for the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games. This support follows other donations for the sports sector. The Japanese government has previously supported major health projects in Senegal, including the 120-bed expansion of Thiès Regional Hospital.

Why it mattersFour medical ambulances will improve emergency response for athletes and attendees at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games.

Senegal commission rejects budget bill again

Lawmakers cite concerns over energy sector funding and potential fuel shortages.

Senegal's National Assembly finance commission rejected the revised budget bill for the third time on Friday evening. The proposed legislation, which adjusts the 2026 budget mid-year, was due for a plenary vote on Tuesday, October 27. The government had requested a further commission review to seek a compromise after facing criticism from its own parliamentary majority.

The Ministry of Economy, Finance and Planning warned that rejecting the bill could lead to fuel shortages and repeated power outages. The special fund for the energy sector, initially allocated 250 billion FCFA, has already exhausted its credits. Debts owed for commercial losses and fuel purchases by Senelec, the national electricity company, are nearing 320 billion FCFA.

To prevent supply disruptions, the government proposed an additional 540.3 billion FCFA in the revised budget for the energy fund. This aims to cover compensation for oil operators and electricity production costs. The total proposed support for the energy sector in the revised bill stands at 790.3 billion FCFA, including funds from the initial budget and other hydrocarbon support measures.

Why it mattersSenegalese citizens face potential fuel shortages and power outages due to the rejection of the revised budget bill.

Macron, Nawrocki skip Dakar 2026 Youth Olympics

Senegal's President Diomaye Faye will host the games without two key foreign leaders due to domestic crises.

The Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games opened on Saturday, October 31, without French President Emmanuel Macron and Polish President Karol Nawrocki. Both leaders cancelled their planned attendance due to domestic political issues in their respective countries. Macron's visit was called off due to unprecedented social unrest in France, while Nawrocki returned to Poland amid a sudden institutional crisis involving the Constitutional Tribunal.

President Macron had planned to visit Dakar on October 30 and 31 as part of a West African tour, but his trip was cancelled on October 23. President Nawrocki's flight to Senegal was diverted over Spain on Friday afternoon, October 30, after police entered the Constitutional Tribunal in Warsaw. Polish authorities stated their actions were legal and denied forcibly removing security agents.

The French decision was announced a week before the games, while Poland's withdrawal was a last-minute reaction to an institutional conflict between the government and the president. Neither cancellation cited issues with the games' organization or Senegal's bilateral relations. President Diomaye Faye had hoped for their presence at the games, which he considers a major event for Senegal.

Senegal court seeks one year jail for student death

A military court in Dakar heard the case of Fallou Sène, who died in 2018 clashes at Gaston Berger University.

A military court in Dakar has heard the case of Fallou Sène, who died eight years ago during student protests at Gaston Berger University in Saint-Louis. Prosecutors requested a one-year prison sentence for Captain Serigne Moustapha Sané, who was a lieutenant at the time of the incident. The court will deliver its verdict on November 27.

The incident occurred on May 15, 2018, when a protest over scholarship payments turned violent after gendarmes intervened. Captain Sané admitted to firing the shot that killed Fallou Sène, stating he acted to protect his superior who was being attacked by protesters. Student testimonies presented in court, however, contradicted the defense's account.

The prosecution argued the actions constituted voluntary assault resulting in death without intent to kill, seeking a one-year jail term. Lawyers for Fallou Sène's family demanded 200 million CFA francs in damages, while student representatives sought 50 million CFA francs. Defense lawyers argued for acquittal, citing necessity and the need to maintain order amidst violent clashes where gendarmes also sustained injuries.

Why it mattersThe court's decision will determine the legal accountability for the death of student Fallou Sène and potential compensation for his family.
