Dakar Dem Dikk mobilizes 300 buses for Maouloud pilgrims

The national public transport company has deployed 300 buses and completed over 520 rotations to transport nearly 20,000 pilgrims to Tivaouane and Kaolack for Maouloud 2026.

Dakar Dem Dikk (DDD) has mobilized 300 buses and completed more than 520 rotations since August 14 to transport pilgrims for Maouloud 2026. Aminata Fall Sarr, the operations director, stated on Monday that nearly 20,000 pilgrims have been transported from four sites in Dakar to Tivaouane and Kaolack.

The transport operation, which continues until Tuesday noon, maintains fares at 2,500 CFA francs for Tivaouane and 4,000 CFA francs for Kaolack. Tickets are sold directly at departure sites, with baggage limits per traveler. Security forces are managing queues and ensuring smooth operations at the departure points.

The special transport system will also cover the return journey, with departures from Tivaouane starting at Serigne Mansour Sy Djamil's site and from Kaolack in front of Lycée Valdiodio Ndiaye. Returns to Dakar are scheduled to begin on Wednesday, August 26, at 5 AM, according to the operations director.

Why it mattersThis event affects thousands of pilgrims by providing organized and affordable transportation to major religious sites for the Maouloud celebration.

US government praises Senegal's budget transparency efforts

The US government's 2026 Fiscal Transparency Report highlights Senegal as one of nine African nations making significant progress in budget transparency.

The US government's 2026 Fiscal Transparency Report, covering the period from January 1 to December 31, 2025, recognized Senegal for its significant progress in budget transparency. This assessment, based on information from US embassies, international organizations, and civil society groups, noted that Senegal is among nine African states making notable advancements in this area.

During the review period, the Senegalese government published audits of its annual budget in a timely manner, which were publicly accessible online and considered generally reliable. These efforts align with criteria such as public access to budget documents, sovereign debt information, public procurement, and natural resource extraction contracts. The government also monitored its budget throughout the fiscal year, with revenues and expenditures reasonably matching projections.

Despite the positive assessment, the report offered recommendations for Senegal to further enhance transparency. These include detailing financial allocations and revenues for major public enterprises within the budget, and publicly disclosing debt obligations related to these entities. The creation of a General Directorate of Finance and Debt and the recent publication of Senegal's partners list also contribute to these efforts.

Why it mattersThis report affects Senegalese citizens by indicating improved oversight of public funds and potentially better governance, while also guiding the government on further steps to increase financial clarity.

Constitutional Council rejects special funds bill

The Constitutional Council declared a bill to regulate special funds inadmissible, following a government appeal that argued the text overstepped regulatory powers.

The Constitutional Council ruled on August twenty-fifth, two thousand twenty-six, that a bill initiated by the National Assembly to regulate special funds is inadmissible. This decision, number 7/C/2026, came after the government, led by Prime Minister Mohamed Al Aminou Lô, filed an appeal on August eighteenth.

The government's appeal claimed the bill encroached on the regulatory domain reserved for the executive branch, citing articles sixty-seven and seventy-six of the Constitution. The Prime Minister's action led to the suspension of parliamentary review of the text.

This ruling by the Council reignites institutional debate regarding the oversight of sensitive special funds and the division of powers between the executive and legislative branches, as reported by Dakaractu and Seneweb.

Why it mattersThis decision affects citizens by clarifying the boundaries of legislative power concerning the management of public funds, which impacts government accountability and resource allocation.

Sadio Mané scores two goals, secures Al-Nassr victory

Senegalese international Sadio Mané scored two late goals, including a 90th-minute winner, to give Al-Nassr a 3-2 comeback victory against Al-Ettifaq in the Saudi Pro League.

Al-Nassr secured a 3-2 win against Al-Ettifaq on Tuesday, August 25, 2026, after trailing by two goals. Sadio Mané scored his first two goals of the season, reducing the deficit in the 74th minute and then scoring the winning goal in the 90th minute. This victory maintains Al-Nassr's perfect record of three wins in three league matches.

The match was challenging for Al-Nassr, who were down 2-0 and played with ten men after their goalkeeper's early exclusion. Cristiano Ronaldo also left the game at halftime. Mané's performance was crucial in turning the game around, preventing Al-Nassr from suffering their first loss of the season.

Mané's decisive contributions highlight his importance to Al-Nassr, with this performance adding to his reputation as a key player capable of influencing match outcomes. His goals ensured Al-Nassr maintained their strong start to the Saudi Pro League season.

Why it mattersSenegalese football fans and supporters of Al-Nassr will be interested in Sadio Mané's performance, as his goals directly impacted his team's league standing.

CNDH condemns Tivaouane murder, urges stronger action against gender-based violence

Senegal's National Commission for Human Rights (CNDH) called for increased efforts to combat violence against women following the murder of Ndèye Bigué Sène in Tivaouane.

The CNDH issued a statement condemning the tragic murder of Ndèye Bigué Sène, whose body was found on August 22, 2026, in the Séico neighborhood of Tivaouane. Police reports indicate a suspect has been apprehended and allegedly confessed, citing mystical practices and the involvement of another individual identified as a marabout.

The Commission emphasized that these details, while awaiting judicial confirmation, highlight the severe risks associated with exploiting beliefs and vulnerabilities for criminal purposes. The CNDH reiterated that no belief, mystical practice, or promise of wealth justifies harming a person's life, physical integrity, or dignity.

This incident underscores the ongoing issue of violence against women and girls in Senegal, where a national survey reveals that approximately three out of ten women experienced at least one form of violence in the past twelve months. The CNDH urged judicial authorities to fully investigate the case, assign responsibility, and prosecute all involved parties.

Why it mattersThis event affects women and girls in Senegal by highlighting the persistent threat of violence they face, as evidenced by the murder and the statistic that three out of ten women experience violence.
