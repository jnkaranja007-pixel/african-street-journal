Senegal parliament launches 11 inquiry commissions

Commissions will scrutinize alleged financial misconduct, primarily from the previous presidency.

Senegal's National Assembly has initiated 11 parliamentary inquiry commissions, spurred by Ousmane Sonko. These commissions aim to investigate potential financial irregularities, largely dating back to the presidency of Macky Sall.

The commissions possess significant investigative powers, including summoning individuals, conducting sworn testimonies, and requisitioning documents under the criminal procedure code. However, their authority is limited; they cannot replace the judiciary or issue condemnations, and cannot investigate cases already under judicial inquiry.

If infractions are found, the commissions can refer cases to the public prosecutor for prosecution. The National Assembly can vote to publish all or part of an inquiry commission's report after its review and discussion.

Why it mattersSenegalese citizens are affected by parliamentary oversight of potential financial misconduct by past government officials.

Senegal sends 170 police to DRC mission

The third Senegalese formed police unit, SENFPU-3, will deploy to the DRC with increased personnel and female leadership.

Senegal formally handed over the national flag to its third formed police unit (SENFPU-3) on October 22, 2026, for deployment to the Democratic Republic of Congo under MONUSCO. The ceremony at the GMI camp in Thiès marked the departure of the 6th rotation, which will include 170 personnel, an increase from the previous contingent of 140.

Police Commissioner Jean Mané, deputy director of the GMI, presided over the flag ceremony, emphasizing the flag's symbolism of national sovereignty and history. This rotation is notable for its increased operational strength and for being led by a female Police Commissioner, with 34 other women also serving, aligning with UN Resolution 1325 on women, peace, and security.

The contingent underwent rigorous preparation, including leadership training and tactical exercises, validated by UN experts. Commissioner Mané urged vigilance against armed groups like the M23, health risks such as Ebola, and strict adherence to UN conduct policies, particularly regarding sexual exploitation and abuse.

Why it mattersSenegalese police officers are being deployed to the Democratic Republic of Congo to participate in a UN peacekeeping mission.

Saint-Louis project shares zoonose prevention research

The Africam project shared research on animal and environmental health risks in Saint-Louis.

Researchers and officials met in Saint-Louis on October 21 and 22 to present findings from the Africam project on health risks. The project focused on preventing future epidemics by understanding zoonotic diseases, which spread between animals and people. Dr. Mohamed Moustapha Sarr, regional director of Livestock and Animal Production, stated that research included sampling wild animals like primates and rodents, as well as domestic animals, to identify circulating risks.

The research aimed to improve health risk mapping and prepare contingency plans, especially after the 2025 Rift Valley fever outbreak in the region. Dr. Sarr noted that while samples from rodents did not show the virus, surveillance continues for diseases like Lassa fever. The Africam project also involved community awareness and collaboration between researchers, universities, and technical services, emphasizing a multisectoral approach.

Project coordinator Jean Hugues Caffin explained that Africam aimed to help regional actors detect epidemic risks early through a multidisciplinary and multisectoral approach. The project's legacy includes enhanced collaboration among different sectors. Discussions focused on knowledge management tools to share data and promote integrated health surveillance under the "One Health" initiative.

Why it mattersThe Africam project in Saint-Louis works to prevent zoonotic diseases that affect human and animal health.

FDR backs striking workers, criticizes government

The Front for the Defense of Democracy and the Republic demands immediate social dialogue and respect for union commitments.

The Front for the Defense of Democracy and the Republic (FDR) declared solidarity with mobilized workers on October 21, 2026, denouncing the government's handling of social conflict. The organization called for immediate negotiations and criticized parliamentary inquiry commissions, urging national forces to unite.

The FDR supports the Union Front for the Defense of Labor, citing two general strike calls in three months: a 24-hour strike in July 2026 and a 72-hour strike in October. The group stated that government responses to the social stability pact demands are insufficient and that consensus from tripartite consultations on labor and social security codes was not respected.

The FDR also condemned the government's use of security forces to drive buses and requisition workers, viewing these as coercive measures. The organization demands structured social dialogue, respect for union commitments, and an end to intimidation tactics hindering the right to strike. The FDR expressed concern over tensions between legislative and executive powers regarding finance bills.

Why it mattersSenegalese workers face government pressure and unfulfilled commitments, impacting their right to strike and social dialogue.

Sport · 2026-10-23

refugee sprinters compete at Dakar Youth Olympics

Two teenage athletes from the Democratic Republic of Congo will represent the Refugee Olympic Team in Senegal.

Two teenage sprinters who fled instability in the Democratic Republic of Congo are preparing to compete for the Refugee Olympic Team at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games in Senegal. Florence Masumbe and Rachid Rochene, now living in Uganda, are among six refugee athletes selected for the event. The Games will take place in the Senegalese capital from October 31 to November 13.

For Masumbe, 16, and Rochene, the competition offers a chance to pursue their sporting ambitions and potentially gain access to education and a better future. Rochene expressed his excitement, stating, "This was a dream since I was young. I wanted to compete with people, to see how I can also do, to also achieve medals." Masumbe, who has lived in the Rwamwanja Refugee Settlement, hopes her participation will transform her life and inspire other young refugees.

Their selection follows years of work by the Olympic Refuge Foundation and its partners in Uganda to support displaced people through sport. Masumbe stated that if she continues with athletics, it will transform her life, allowing her to get a good school, and her family members will also have better opportunities.

Why it mattersTwo refugee athletes will compete in the Youth Olympic Games in Dakar, Senegal, offering them a chance at athletic achievement and future opportunities.
