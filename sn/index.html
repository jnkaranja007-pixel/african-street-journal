Dakar Dem Dikk mobilizes 300 buses for Maouloud 2026 pilgrims

The national public transport company has deployed 300 buses and completed over 520 rotations to Tivaouane and Kaolack for the Maouloud 2026 celebration.

Dakar Dem Dikk (DDD) has mobilized 300 buses for Maouloud 2026, facilitating travel for nearly 20,000 pilgrims to Tivaouane and Kaolack. The operation, which began on August 14th, has already seen over 520 rotations, according to Aminata Fall Sarr, DDD's Director of Operations. Services are running from four sites in Dakar: Colobane station, Pikine National Arena, Rufisque's Ngalandou Diouf stadium, and Léopold Sédar Senghor Airport in Yoff.

Ticket prices remain 2,500 CFA francs for Tivaouane and 4,000 CFA francs for Kaolack, with sales directly at departure sites and a baggage limit per traveler. Security forces are managing queues and ensuring smooth operations. The transport system will also be maintained for the pilgrims' return to Dakar, with departures from Tivaouane at Serigne Mansour Sy Djamil site and from Kaolack opposite Valdiodio Ndiaye high school.

Return journeys are scheduled to begin on Wednesday, August 26th, at 5 AM. This special arrangement is part of DDD's ongoing tradition of supporting faithful during major religious events, a directive from General Director Assane Mbengue. Rotations will continue until Tuesday at noon, just 24 hours before the Maouloud celebration.

Why it mattersPilgrims traveling to Tivaouane and Kaolack are directly affected by the availability and cost of public transport for the Maouloud celebration.

Constitutional Council invalidates bill on special funds oversight

Senegal's Constitutional Council ruled that the executive branch exclusively manages special credits, blocking a legislative proposal to increase transparency.

The Constitutional Council invalidated a bill aimed at regulating special funds, stating that the management of these credits falls solely under the executive's authority. The government had referred the matter to the institution, which determined that the legislature cannot impose control over these funds.

The bill, adopted by an Assembly commission, emerged from a confrontation between President Bassirou Diomaye Faye and his former Prime Minister, Ousmane Sonko, who became President of Parliament after their split in May. The PASTEF parliamentary group expressed concern, calling the decision a “presidential blockage” and accusing the president of violating electoral promises.

Despite the setback, the PASTEF-Les Patriotes parliamentary group affirmed its commitment to pursuing the regulation of political funds, asserting that laws promoting transparency in public financial management will eventually be enacted. They claim the president is using “presidential levers of blockage” to prevent reforms.

Why it mattersThe decision affects citizens by maintaining opacity in the management of special public funds, which the PASTEF parliamentary group claims are without control or verification.

Sadio Mané scores two goals, secures Al-Nassr victory

Senegalese forward Sadio Mané scored two decisive goals, including a last-minute winner, to lead Al-Nassr to a 3-2 comeback victory over Al-Ettifaq in the Saudi Pro League.

Sadio Mané delivered a standout performance for Al-Nassr, scoring a brace that secured a 3-2 victory against Al-Ettifaq on the third matchday of the Saudi championship. His goals were crucial after Al-Nassr was reduced to ten players and Cristiano Ronaldo left the game at halftime.

Mané first reduced the deficit in the 74th minute, then clinched the win in the final seconds of added time, confirming his ability to turn a match around. This performance adds to his series of excellent displays, highlighting his importance to the club.

These two goals mark Mané's first of the season, allowing Al-Nassr to maintain a perfect record of three wins in three matches. His decisive actions helped his team overcome a challenging situation, which saw them trailing 2-0 at one point.

Why it mattersSenegalese football fans and followers of the Saudi Pro League will be interested in Sadio Mané's continued strong performance and his team's success.

US praises Senegal for budget transparency efforts in 2025 report

The United States government recognized Senegal's significant progress in fiscal transparency, noting its adherence to minimum requirements and public accessibility of budget.

The US government's Fiscal Transparency Report 2026, covering January 1 to December 31, 2025, highlighted Senegal as one of nine countries making significant strides in budget transparency. The report, compiled from information from US embassies, government agencies, international organizations, and civil society groups, evaluates countries eligible for US foreign aid to ensure rigorous use of public funds.

Senegal's government published audits of its annual budget within a reasonable timeframe, making them publicly accessible, including online. These audits were generally comprehensive and reliable, though they did not detail financial allocations or revenues for major public enterprises. The government also monitored its budget throughout the fiscal year, with revenues and expenditures reasonably matching projections.

The report recommended that Senegal further improve transparency by detailing financial allocations and revenues for major public enterprises in the budget, and by publicizing their debt obligations. Senegal's superior public finance control institution, which meets international independence standards, audited the entire budget and provided substantial findings.

Why it mattersThis report affects the Senegalese government by recognizing its efforts in fiscal transparency, potentially influencing future international aid and financial partnerships.

CNDH condemns Tivaouane murder, urges stronger action against gender violence

The National Commission for Human Rights (CNDH) called for increased efforts to combat violence against women across Senegal following the murder of Ndèye Bigué Sène in Tivaouane.

The CNDH expressed dismay over the death of Ndèye Bigué Sène, found lifeless on August 22, 2026, in the Séico neighborhood of Tivaouane. Police reports indicate a suspect has been apprehended and allegedly confessed, citing mystical practices and the involvement of another individual identified as a marabout. The CNDH emphasizes that these details, while awaiting judicial confirmation, highlight the dangers of exploiting beliefs and vulnerabilities for criminal acts.

The commission firmly condemned the extreme gravity of the act, stating that no belief, mystical practice, or promise of wealth justifies harming a person's life, physical integrity, or dignity. The CNDH also noted that this tragedy underscores the ongoing issue of violence against women and girls in Senegal, citing a national survey that found approximately three out of ten women experienced some form of violence in the past year.

The CNDH urged judicial authorities to thoroughly investigate the case, assign responsibility, and prosecute all involved parties while upholding fair trial guarantees. It also called upon public authorities, religious and community leaders, women's organizations, media, and society at large to enhance prevention efforts against manipulation, coercion, and violence targeting women, stressing that action should not only follow tragedies but also focus on prevention and victim protection.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects women and girls in Senegal by highlighting the persistence of violence against them and the need for stronger protective measures and judicial accountability.
