Senegal parents learn to manage child diabetes

An association organized a conference in Dakar to support parents of children with diabetes, aiming for better care and integration.

The Senegalese Association for the Support of Diabetics (Assad) held a public conference in Dakar on Tuesday, November 10, to educate parents of children with diabetes. The goal is to help children live with their illness without significant problems, under the theme 'Childhood Diabetes: Free Access, the Cdic Program Unveiled'. Organizers emphasized that children with diabetes should have the same opportunities for success as others.

Professor Babacar Niang, coordinator of the 'Change Diabetes in Children' (Cdic) program, reported that as of September 2026, approximately 1,915 children with diabetes were registered across Senegal's regions. To improve care, 273 health professionals have been trained, and six diabetes camps have been organized for 297 children. The mortality rate for children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes has decreased from 6.7% in 2016 to 2.6% in 2022.

Baye Oumar Gueye, president of Assad, highlighted a concerning gap in care continuity between the national children's hospital and another facility, which hinders medical follow-up. Novo Nordisk laboratory has provided Assad with 7 tonnes of insulin stored at the National Pharmacy Supply. Dr. Seynabou Mbow from the Ministry of Health and Public Hygiene pledged to raise awareness about the continuity of care issues for some diabetic children.

Why it mattersParents of the 1,915 registered children with diabetes in Senegal must now engage more actively in their children's treatment to ensure better care and manage the continuity of medical services.

Health · 2026-11-13

Saint-Louis launches week against fake medicines

The Senegalese Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency will raise awareness about the dangers of counterfeit drugs.

The Senegalese Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency (ARSP) will hold a week of awareness activities in Saint-Louis from November 22 to 27 to combat the circulation of counterfeit and substandard medicines. The campaign includes a march, a caravan through neighborhoods and schools, and a panel discussion at the Gaston Berger University (UGB).

Dr. Adiouma Barry, ARSP regional coordinator for Saint-Louis, Louga, and Matam, stated that an estimated one in 10 pharmaceutical products in sub-Saharan Africa is falsified or of poor quality. He highlighted Saint-Louis's border position as a vulnerability to illicit supply chains, emphasizing the need for stronger controls and public information on risks.

Governor Al Hassan Sall praised the efforts of control services like customs and the mixed brigade against pharmaceutical trafficking, calling for increased vigilance and collaboration. The awareness week aims to make public education a key tool to protect consumers and curb the spread of fraudulent medicines, with organizers stressing that pharmaceuticals should only be bought from pharmacies and official channels.

Why it mattersResidents of Saint-Louis will receive information on the risks of buying medicines from informal sellers during a week-long awareness campaign.

Senegal beats Sudan 4-1 in CAN qualifier

The Lions secured a dominant second-half performance to take the lead in Group J.

Senegal defeated Sudan 4-1 at the Léopold Sédar Senghor Stadium to move closer to qualifying for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. Nicolas Jackson opened the scoring for Senegal in the first half, but Sudan equalized through Abdalla. The Lions roared back after halftime, with Boulaye Dia heading in a goal in the 61st minute, followed by a strike from Ibrahima Mbaye.

Pathé Mboup, making his debut for the Lions, scored the fourth goal in the final minutes, capping a strong performance after a labored first half. Coach Patrick Vieira stated that the team had crossed a threshold with this victory. Senegal now leads Group J with seven points, awaiting the result of the Mozambique versus Ethiopia match.

The win brings Senegal closer to the 2027 AFCON tournament, which will be held in Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda. Mboup expressed his determination to prove his worth and his ambition to win the Africa Cup of Nations and other trophies with the national team.

Why it mattersSenegal's victory puts them provisionally at the top of Group J in the CAN 2027 qualifiers, with the next match scheduled in Khartoum.

Senegal beats Sudan 4-1 in AFCON qualifier

Boulaye Dia and Ibrahima Mbaye scored second-half goals as Senegal dominated Sudan.

Senegal defeated Sudan 4-1 in an Africa Cup of Nations qualifier on Friday. After a goalless first half, the Lions of Teranga scored four goals in the second half to secure a convincing victory.

Boulaye Dia opened the scoring in the 64th minute with a header from an El Hadji Malick Diouf cross, making it 2-1. Three minutes later, Ibrahima Mbaye extended Senegal's lead to 3-1. Substitute Pathé Mboup added a fourth goal before the final whistle.

Senegal made several substitutions during the match, including Pape Matar Sarr coming on for Boulaye Dia and Pape Demba Diop replacing Dion Lopy. The team displayed strong offensive performance throughout the second half.

Why it mattersSenegal's national football team secured a 4-1 victory over Sudan in an Africa Cup of Nations qualifier.

Senegal's gendarmerie chief visits Youth Olympics security centre

General Martin Faye urged security teams to stay alert as the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games move towards their close.

General of Division Martin Faye, the national gendarmerie's high commander and director of military justice, visited the operations coordination centre of the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games security committee on November 11, 2026. On arrival he was welcomed by the committee's deputy coordinator and the centre's head, and was shown the centre's various units.

He toured the video surveillance room and the communications service, then met centre staff in the crisis room, where he received an overview of how the centre is organised and a progress report on the security operations for the Games. Speaking after the welcome remarks, he congratulated the teams and told them to increase their vigilance, especially as the Games' activities close.

He also stressed the need to consolidate the integrated organisation and keep up the close cooperation between the security forces.

Why it mattersSecurity staff at the Games must keep their guard up through the closing days, when the final activities are still running.
