Senegal's National Assembly sets budget 2027 review dates

The finance commission will scrutinize all ministries between November 7 and November 16, 2026.

Senegal's National Assembly will examine the 2027 finance bill from November 7 to November 16, 2026. The finance commission will review all government departments during this period, with intense sessions scheduled daily. The process begins on November 7 with general considerations, including public revenue and debt, followed by specific ministries each day.

Alphonse Mané Sambou, president of the finance commission, defended the procedure after disagreements with the Ministry of Economy, Finance, and Planning. He stated the review of the 2027 budget is based on the rejected 2026 rectificative finance bill, making the figures legally inconsistent. Sambou aims for institutional dialogue to facilitate the bill's adoption.

The opposition, led by Takku Wallu Sénégal, has criticized the government's budget sincerity following the rejection of the 2026 rectificative finance bill on November 3. The National Assembly's decision to proceed with the 2027 budget review, despite these tensions, signals an intent to move forward. The opposition may use this forum to challenge the budget figures.

Why it mattersSenegalese lawmakers are reviewing the 2027 national budget, affecting government spending and national finances.

Delta Air Lines flies 1.1 million passengers in Senegal

The US carrier celebrated 20 years of connecting Dakar with New York and its global network.

Delta Air Lines marked two decades of service in Senegal on November 4, 2026, with a celebration in Dakar. The US airline has transported over 1.1 million passengers between Senegal and the United States since launching its first flight on December 4, 2006. The airline now operates direct flights between Blaise-Diagne International Airport and New York's John F. Kennedy Airport.

Ryan Bowman, Delta's international strategy general manager, stated that the 20 years represent "people, partnerships, and the bonds that have shaped our journey." Jennifer Paguada, the US Embassy's charge d'affaires in Senegal, noted the flight's importance in connecting the two countries for people, families, and commerce.

Delta also highlighted community engagement, including a 2016 paid internship program for postgraduate students and support for Junior Achievement Africa's "Innovation Camps." In 2023, an innovation camp with Junior Achievement Africa and Consortium Jeunesse Sénégal involved 69 young people developing skills through design thinking.

Why it mattersDelta Air Lines' 20-year operation in Senegal has facilitated travel for over 1.1 million passengers, strengthening business and cultural ties between the two nations.

Pathé Mboup joins Senegal squad

The 23-year-old Stade Brestois forward previously starred in Magic System's 'Magic in the Air' music video.

Pathé Mboup, a 23-year-old forward for Stade Brestois, has been called up for the first time to the Senegal national team by coach Patrick Vieira. He will join the squad for two upcoming matches against Sudan in the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers. Mboup, who was formed at AS Dakar Sacré-Cœur, has become a regular starter in France's Ligue 1, with nine starts in nine games this season.

12 years ago, a then 11-year-old Mboup was the main actor in the music video for Magic System's hit song 'Magic in the Air'. The video, which has over 564 million views on YouTube, featured him as a young boy wearing a yellow football academy jersey. Mboup, originally from Mbao near Dakar, makes his national team debut.

Mboup's professional journey included spells at Standard Liège and Olympique Lyonnais before playing for Pau FC in Ligue 2. He joined Stade Brestois in the summer of 2025. His performances this season, including two goals and one assist in nine league matches, have caught the attention of coach Vieira.

Why it mattersPathé Mboup, a Senegalese footballer, has been selected to represent his country in upcoming qualifiers.

Politics · 2026-11-06

Senegal opposition vows not to block finance law

Takku-Wallu group president Aïssata Tall Sall urged President Bassirou Diomaye Faye to act responsibly.

The Takku-Wallu Senegal parliamentary group will not obstruct the adoption of the revised finance law, aiming to prevent the country's paralysis and harm to Senegalese citizens. Group president Aïssata Tall Sall stated this position on Thursday, November 5, 2026, at the APR headquarters.

Tall Sall declared a stance of "republican responsibility" while maintaining opposition to what she described as an unorthodox public finance management. She stated that while they refuse to approve a management they deem unorthodox, they also refuse to hold Senegal hostage with Pastef.

The opposition leader emphasized that political disagreement should not lead to institutional gridlock or compromise daily life for citizens. She directly called on President Bassirou Diomaye Faye to take responsibility to avoid national paralysis, particularly concerning public servant payments.

Why it mattersSenegalese citizens and public servants face potential disruption if the finance law is blocked.
