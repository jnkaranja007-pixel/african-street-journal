Doune Pathé Mbengue unveils plan to reshape Senegal's port network

The Port Autonome de Dakar plans to reconfigure the Dakar port, convert Ndayane into a multi-terminal platform and link ports from Saint-Louis to Ziguinchor.

Doune Pathé Mbengue, director general of the Port autonome de Dakar (PAD), has set out a plan to reposition Senegal in African maritime and logistics competition. The plan covers the reconfiguration of the Dakar port, the diversification of the Ndayane terminals, the repositioning of Bargny-Sendou and a port network running from Saint-Louis to Ziguinchor.

Speaking at the Tourism Investment Forum, Mbengue said the aim is to make the Port autonome de Dakar the main gateway into Africa. He said Ndayane must move beyond a container terminal to host several types of terminals. The programme is aligned with the Senegal 2050 Transformation Agenda, and technical teams are still finalising its investment details.

Mbengue said the plan is meant to connect Senegal's ports to global trade routes and to the markets of its hinterland. No timetable for the investment programme was given at the forum.

Why it mattersShipping lines, cargo owners and inland traders will depend on which terminals receive the investment and when the details are fixed.

Senegal wins right to host 2031 Francophonie Games

The Permanent Council of La Francophonie picked Senegal on 13 October 2026, a first for the country, after Dakar hosted the Youth Olympic Games.

Senegal will host the 11th Francophonie Games in 2031, after the Permanent Council of La Francophonie chose it on 13 October 2026 in Paris, during the Youth Olympic Games. The Games combine sport with culture, bringing together athletes and artists from French-speaking countries.

The International Olympic Committee's president, Kirsty Coventry, called Dakar 2026 a "100% success" at Diamniadio on 12 November, and said Dakar had changed the image of Africa. The Youth Olympic Games closed with the flame going out at Diamniadio on 13 November.

Senegal plans to reuse existing venues, including the Dakar Arena, the Léopold Sédar Senghor stadium, the Abdoulaye Wade stadium, the Olympic Village and the Train Express Régional. The article says the Games should also support vocational training, job creation, entrepreneurship and tourism.

Why it mattersDakar must now reuse its Youth Olympic venues and add cultural facilities for the 2031 Games, with five years to prepare.

Guinea seeks Senegal's local development model

A Guinean delegation visits Senegal this week to learn from its community development program.

A Guinean government delegation is in Senegal from November 16 to 21, 2026, to study the country's Programme d’urgence de développement communautaire (PUDC). The visit, supported by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), aims to help Guinea design its own local development program. Senegal is sharing its experience in community development with Guinea.

Led by Ambassador Kabelé Soumah, secretary general of Guinea's ministry of Planning and International Cooperation, the delegation will examine the PUDC's planning, financing, execution, and monitoring. This mission occurs as Guinea prepares a new local development program to support its Simandou 2040 plan, which seeks to improve territorial governance, reduce inequality, and enhance access to basic services. The future Guinean program will focus on vulnerable communes and villages, addressing social infrastructure, income-generating activities, climate resilience, and local capacity building.

Senegalese officials will discuss the PUDC's origins, institutional and financial structures, beneficiary targeting, procurement, project monitoring, and investment sustainability. The delegation will also meet with the Minister of Infrastructure in Diamniadio and visit rural electrification sites, a rural road, post-harvest facilities, and a health post in the Kaolack and Fatick regions. They will also hold working sessions with several Senegalese public bodies, including the Directorate General for Planning and Economic Policies (DGPPE) and the National Agency for Statistics and Demography (ANSD).

Sport · 2026-11-16

Senegal's Lions need one win to edge closer to CAN 2027

Senegal, leaders of Group J with 7 points, face Sudan in Juba on Monday, needing a win and a Mozambique loss to Ethiopia to qualify directly.

Senegal beat Sudan 4-1 in Dakar last Thursday and now face the Nile Crocodiles in Juba, South Sudan, on Monday at 13:00. Coach Patrick Vieira's side have won two and drawn one of their three matches, and are the only unbeaten team in Group J.

Vieira warned that Juba's playing conditions and the match context could make the task harder than the first leg suggested. He said Senegal must match Sudan tactically and physically and win their duels. Mozambique's surprise 1-0 home defeat to Ethiopia has also opened a route to qualification.

Senegal must still win the return match to qualify directly, and Mozambique would need to win in Addis Ababa. The final two qualifying days are scheduled for March 2027, and the tournament will be hosted by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania.

Why it mattersA win in Juba would let Senegal book their place at the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations before the final two qualifying days in March 2027.
