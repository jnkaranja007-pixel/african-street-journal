President Faye Mourns Khadre Leader Cheikhna Cheikh Saadbou Aïdara

President Bassirou Diomaye Faye expressed deep sadness and offered condolences following the passing of Cheikhna Cheikh Saadbou Aïdara, a prominent figure in the Khadre community.

President Bassirou Diomaye Faye announced his profound sorrow on Sunday over the death of Cheikhna Cheikh Saadbou Aïdara, identified as the eldest member of the Cheikh Saadbou family and son of Cheikh Mouhamed Takhiyoullah. The head of state extended his heartfelt condolences to the bereaved family, the Khadre community spanning Senegal and Mauritania, and all the followers guided by the deceased.

In his message, President Faye also offered prayers for the eternal rest of Cheikhna Cheikh Saadbou Aïdara. He invoked divine mercy, asking for God to welcome the departed into paradise. The president's statement, shared on social media, conveyed his deep sadness upon learning of the religious leader's passing.

Cheikhna Cheikh Saadbou Aïdara was recognized as the general khalife of the Khadres. His passing prompted a significant outpouring of grief and remembrance from religious and political figures across the nation, highlighting his influence within the Muslim community.

Why it mattersThe passing of a prominent religious leader affects the spiritual guidance and community cohesion for followers of the Khadre order in Senegal and Mauritania.

Four arrested with 47.76 million CFA in counterfeit bills

Senegalese authorities seized fake US dollars intended for a money laundering operation in Toubab Dialaw.

Dakar’s research brigade arrested four Senegalese nationals on August 29, 2026, suspected of counterfeiting currency. The suspects are in custody for criminal conspiracy, attempted fraud, and possessing materials for creating fake legal tender. The investigation began after receiving information that a group was attempting to "wash" 100-dollar bills for a large fee.

Investigators followed leads to Diamniadio and then Toubab Dialaw, where the suspects had rented an apartment to serve as a laboratory for the illicit operation. A coordinated intervention by gendarmes led to the apprehension of the four individuals and the seizure of counterfeit 100-dollar bills, valued at 47.76 million CFA francs. Two Ford vehicles used by the suspects were also confiscated.

The National Gendarmerie stated that the operation targeted individuals involved in producing and attempting to launder counterfeit currency. The Dakar research brigade is continuing its investigation to fully understand the scope of the counterfeiting ring and identify any other involved parties. The seized bills were intended for circulation as legal tender.

Why it mattersFour individuals were arrested for attempting to launder counterfeit US dollars, impacting financial integrity and potentially defrauding businesses.

Journalist receives death threats after broadcast

Senegal's media union condemns threats against RFM journalist Babacar Fall and calls for authorities to ensure his safety.

The Senegalese Union of Information and Communication Professionals (SYNPICS) has strongly condemned death threats made against RFM journalist Babacar Fall. These threats followed a broadcast on August 27, 2026, during which Fall interviewed Abdou Sy, identified as the brother of a religious leader. The interview sparked controversy, leading Sy's organization to contest statements and announce a defamation complaint.

Fall publicly reported being targeted with insults and death threats the day after the broadcast. SYNPICS described the actions as "liberticidal" and emphasized that journalists should not face intimidation for their work. The union highlighted press freedom as a cornerstone of democracy and the rule of law, stating it cannot be held hostage by attempts to silence media professionals.

SYNPICS urged all parties to act responsibly and respect republican principles, including freedom of expression and the right to information. The union formally called on state authorities to take all necessary measures to guarantee Babacar Fall's security and to fully investigate the origin of the threats, pursuing legal action against those responsible. SYNPICS reaffirmed its solidarity with Fall and all journalists.

Why it mattersJournalists and media professionals in Senegal are directly affected by threats and intimidation that hinder their ability to report freely.

Fatma Diouf Samoura Leads SEED Project Board

The former FIFA secretary general will guide the Thiès-based sports academy toward its 2030 development goals.

Fatma Diouf Samoura, formerly the secretary general of FIFA, has been appointed chairperson of the board for the SEED Project. This sports-study academy, founded by Amadou Gallo Fall and based in Thiès, announced the appointment on August 30.

SEED Project stated that Samoura's appointment marks a significant step for the organization, aligning with its transformation and development objectives for 2030. Her extensive experience and leadership are expected to bolster SEED's governance and amplify its impact on African youth.

Samoura expressed her enthusiasm for collaborating with the founder, board, and the SEED community. She aims to strengthen governance, deepen impact, and foster a model for sports-study institutions across the continent, highlighting SEED's success in combining athletic excellence, education, and values to develop young leaders.

Why it mattersInvestors may benefit from the strengthened governance and accelerated impact of the SEED Project under new leadership, potentially leading to more effective youth development programs.
