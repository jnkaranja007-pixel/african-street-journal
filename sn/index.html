Dakar governor requisitions SENELEC agents during strike

The governor of Dakar has ordered the requisition of specific SENELEC employees from October 21 to 23, 2026, to ensure electricity supply continuity.

The governor of Dakar, Ousmane Kane, issued an order on October 20, 2026, to requisition certain SENELEC agents from October 21 to 23, 2026. This measure follows a planned strike by the agents during those dates. The order, identified as N°00197 / GRD/SP/AS, aims to maintain essential electricity services and secure installations.

According to the decree, affected agents are required to report for duty and perform essential tasks to keep the service operational throughout the specified period. The Director General of SENELEC is responsible for notifying the requisitioned agents and implementing the governor's directive.

This action by the regional executive is intended to prevent disruptions to the electricity supply. The requisition covers agents listed in an annex to the official order, ensuring that critical functions are maintained despite the industrial action.

Why it mattersDakar residents are affected by potential electricity service disruptions due to the SENELEC agents' strike and the subsequent requisition.

Faye links Dakar 2026 Youth Olympics to Africa's global standing

Senegal's president sees the games as a chance to boost the continent's visibility and argue for a permanent UN Security Council seat.

President Bassirou Diomaye Faye views the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games as more than a sporting event, aiming to enhance Africa's global profile and demonstrate its capacity for world-class organization. He noted the historic significance of the games being held in Africa for the first time, calling it both a source of pride and a correction of a long-standing delay. Faye expressed a desire for the games to be so successful that the question shifts from whether Africa can host to when it will host again.

Faye drew parallels between hosting the games and advocating for Africa's representation in international decision-making bodies, specifically mentioning the push for a permanent African seat on the UN Security Council. He argued that Africa, with its large population and significant global impact, deserves a voice in forums that discuss its future, stating that hosting events and fulfilling commitments makes the idea of deciding without Africa increasingly untenable.

The president also emphasized a strategic shift in how Senegal presents itself to international partners, focusing on showcasing the country's strengths and economic cooperation opportunities. He stated that Senegal has waited too long and that simply meeting obligations is insufficient, asserting his role in actively promoting the nation's true image, highlighting its resources like gas, oil, and solar energy for the future.

Why it mattersSenegal's president is using the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympics to advance Africa's diplomatic standing and economic partnerships.

Le Soleil newspaper discusses archives with Dakar archbishop

The national daily's director general met with Archbishop André Guèye to explore covering Catholic events and using church records.

Lamine Niang, director general of the Senegalese newspaper Le Soleil, met with Archbishop André Guèye of Dakar on Wednesday. The discussion focused on improving media coverage of Catholic religious events and the potential use of the Church's archives. Niang expressed a desire to collaborate with the Church to enhance the newspaper's public service mission and ensure balanced reporting, as emphasized by the head of state.

Niang detailed innovations in covering religious observances, such as the pilgrimage to the Holy Land and the Popenguine Marian pilgrimage. These include dedicated magazines and sending special envoys. He also proposed leveraging Le Soleil's archives, which contain historical texts and photographs related to the Senegalese Catholic Church, including the visit of Pope John Paul II, for potential future exhibitions and publications.

Archbishop Guèye commended Le Soleil's work, particularly its coverage of Catholic events and its inclusive approach to information. He described the newspaper as a hub of excellence with talented journalists and expressed his appreciation for their daily efforts. Guèye wished Niang's tenure as director general a lasting impact on the newspaper's history.

Why it mattersSenegalese Catholics may see improved media coverage of their religious events and historical narratives through this collaboration.

Senegal unions report low strike turnout in Tivaouane

Only 29% of education staff and 21% of health workers joined a 72-hour strike in Tivaouane, according to official figures.

A 72-hour strike called by the Front syndical on Wednesday saw low participation in Tivaouane, with official figures indicating only 29.02% of education personnel and 21% of staff at the Mame Abdou Aziz Sy Dabakh hospital observed the action. In education, 438 out of 1509 expected teachers participated, with higher rates in preschool and middle school compared to elementary. The majority of teachers remained at work, ensuring classes continued.

At the Mame Abdou Aziz Sy Dabakh hospital, the 21% of staff who struck included those providing care, leading to the suspension of emergency services like radiology and laboratory tests. Hospital director Dr. Yoro Diagne reported an 80% drop in daily revenue. Similar revenue declines were noted at the Massamba Sall health center, where maternity, emergency, and doctor services were affected.

The Front syndical for the Defense of Labor (FSDT) claimed a 90% national turnout for the strike, denouncing what it called "illegal requisitions" and "intimidation tactics" by authorities. Despite these claims, official figures from Tivaouane suggest a limited impact on the education and health sectors in the department, though some services were disrupted and revenues significantly reduced.

Why it mattersTeachers and healthcare workers in Tivaouane are affected by a low turnout in a strike that disrupted some services and reduced hospital revenues.

DER/FJ disburses 65.5 million CFA to 27 groups in Matam

The General Delegation for Rapid Entrepreneurship for Women and Youth has provided funding to 675 beneficiaries in Matam, urging greater youth involvement in business.

The General Delegation for Rapid Entrepreneurship for Women and Youth (DER/FJ) has announced the disbursement of 65.5 million CFA francs to 675 beneficiaries across 27 groups in Matam. Dr. Abdoulaye Niane, the General Delegate, called for increased youth participation in entrepreneurial initiatives during the announcement.

In a separate but related initiative, the department of Podor is set to receive 76.8 million CFA francs from the DER/FJ. This funding will support 104 groups within the region, further expanding the reach of the entrepreneurship program.

These financial injections aim to foster economic activity and provide resources for emerging businesses, particularly targeting women and young entrepreneurs. The DER/FJ's efforts are designed to stimulate local economies and create opportunities for sustainable development.

Why it mattersYoung entrepreneurs and women in Matam and Podor will receive funding to start or expand their businesses.
