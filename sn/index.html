Senegal doctors see stroke patients after eight-year waits

Saint-Louis health centre offered free consultations for stroke victims on October 29, 2026.

Saint-Louis residents struggling with stroke after-effects received free medical consultations on October 29, 2026, at the Ousmane Ngom health centre. The event, organised by the Senegalese Association for Support to Stroke Patients and Families (ASP-AVC), aimed to address the region's shortage of neurologists. Many patients had not seen a specialist for up to eight years.

Dr. Mame Maimouna Diaw Santos, a neurologist at the Saint-Louis regional hospital, saw about 40 patients during the event. She noted that stroke victims represent four out of every 10 neurology patients she sees. Dr. Diaw Santos stated she has been the sole neurologist at the facility since June 2021, highlighting the need for more specialists to manage patient care.

The free consultations also included screenings for high blood pressure and diabetes, along with medication donations. Community health workers, known as Badianou Gokh, also participated, emphasizing the need for ongoing awareness campaigns beyond the annual World Stroke Day. Dr. Diaw Santos stressed that prevention, including regular physical activity and managing conditions like hypertension and diabetes, is important.

Why it mattersStroke patients in Saint-Louis face long waits for specialist care due to a shortage of neurologists.

Senegal releases 30 detained at MFDC Casamance headquarters

Authorities freed 30 individuals arrested during the Tuesday dismantling of the MFDC political wing's base in Ziguinchor.

30 people arrested during the Tuesday dismantling of the political wing's headquarters of the Movement of the Forces of Casamance (MFDC) in Ziguinchor, southern Senegal, have been released. The security source confirmed the releases on Thursday to AFP. The headquarters, located on the outskirts of Ziguinchor, was the base for the MFDC's independence struggle in Casamance, which began in December 1982.

Gendarmes dismantled the site on Tuesday and detained 30 occupants, according to a security source. The last detainees were freed on Wednesday following instructions from the Ziguinchor public prosecutor. A source close to the MFDC stated that two individuals remained in custody, and reported 33 arrests on Tuesday. Local media also reported the seizure of MFDC flags and mobile phones.

Images showed gendarmes erasing MFDC inscriptions from walls and seizing documents, sound equipment, and chairs. A gendarmerie officer told local media that seized documents are being analyzed for useful information. The operation occurred on the anniversary of the October 27, 2019, death of rebel figure Abdou Elinkine Diatta.

Why it matters30 individuals arrested during a security operation in Ziguinchor have been released.

Business · 2026-10-30

Moody's downgrades Senegal's foreign currency debt rating

The rating agency lowered the long-term foreign currency debt rating from Caa2 to Ca, citing expected higher losses for external private creditors.

Senegal's public debt treatment plan, which excludes local currency debt, has led Moody's to downgrade the country's long-term foreign currency debt rating from Caa2 to Ca. The agency confirmed the local currency rating at Caa2 and short-term ratings at Not-Prime, while changing the outlook from negative to stable.

Moody's explained the foreign currency downgrade by anticipating external private creditors could face losses between 35% and 65% due to the debt treatment. The local currency debt was maintained at Caa2 because it will likely be excluded from the treatment but remains exposed to liquidity risks.

The stable outlook is partly attributed to Senegal's membership in the UEMOA, which helps limit financial risks and allows continued borrowing on the regional market.

Sport · 2026-10-30

Refugee athlete to compete at Dakar Youth Games

Arno Yafunga Botay will represent the Refugee Olympic Team in judo at the 2026 games.

Arno Yafunga Botay, a 15-year-old judoka, will compete for the Refugee Olympic Team at the Dakar 2026 Youth Games. He qualified for the under-55kg category after trials in East Africa. This marks the first time a refugee team will participate in the Youth Games.

Botay, who fled the Democratic Republic of Congo with his family at age six, was surprised by his selection. "I didn't expect to be selected," he told BBC Sport Africa, adding, "I was so excited. I was crying tears of joy." His father, Arnold, also practised judo and taught him the sport.

Botay stated that representing other refugees and his community at the games means a great deal to him. His family settled in Nairobi after living in the Kakuma refugee camp in Kenya, following threats to his father's life and an attack on their home.

Why it mattersRefugee athletes will compete in judo at the Dakar 2026 Youth Games, offering representation to displaced communities.
