Senegal insurance union threatens strike over 14th month pay

Workers demand an extra month's salary as negotiations for a new collective agreement stall.

The National Union of Employees and Managers of Insurance Companies (SNECEA) in Senegal has threatened a general strike if their demand for a 14th month of salary is not met. The union, led by General Secretary Doudou Mané, stated that negotiations for a new collective agreement, which began years ago, have stalled over this specific demand.

SNECEA claims that discussions with the employers' federation, mediated by the Directorate of Labour, had previously agreed on the principle of an additional bonus. However, the federation has now rejected the proposal, causing frustration among workers who feel they are not benefiting from the sector's growth. The union points to the banking sector, where a 14th month salary is common, as a benchmark.

The insurance sector's turnover grew from over 200 billion CFA francs to more than 300 billion CFA francs in 2025, according to the union. SNECEA is calling on the president of the employers' federation, El Hadj Amarkébé, to reconsider the decision, which they view as a setback after years of dialogue. The union is pushing for implementation starting in 2027.

Why it mattersInsurance sector workers are demanding a 14th month salary, threatening a strike if their claim is not met by employers.

Senegal minister open to new legal expert status

Justice Minister Moussa Sarr considers a new professional path for law graduates struggling to enter traditional legal careers.

Senegal's Justice Minister, Moussa Sarr, has expressed openness to creating a new status for legal professionals, dubbed 'agréé expert juridique' (approved legal expert), to address the difficulties faced by law graduates in accessing traditional legal careers. Speaking at a conference at UCAD's Faculty of Law, Sarr acknowledged that existing professions cannot absorb the growing number of graduates.

The proposed status, inspired by an Ivorian model, would allow holders of a master's degree in law to practice after a three-year internship and approval from the Ministry of Justice. This would offer them a state-recognized framework distinct from lawyers, notaries, or judicial officers. Minister Sarr indicated a comparative study mission to Côte d'Ivoire to understand the system better, while also stressing the need to adapt university training to market realities.

The minister also called for modernizing law studies with more English modules, improved IT skills, and new specializations like energy and sports law. He further suggested state support to encourage legal professionals to establish practices in rural regions. The potential reform awaits examination of recommendations and legal feasibility, with Sarr stating that 'no door is closed'.

Senegal, Switzerland discuss stronger partnerships

President Bassirou Diomaye Faye met Swiss Vice-President Ignazio Cassis in Phnom Penh to explore cooperation in vocational training, health, economy, and water management.

Senegalese President Bassirou Diomaye Faye met Swiss Vice-President Ignazio Cassis on Saturday, November 14, 2026, in Phnom Penh to discuss strengthening bilateral cooperation. The leaders explored opportunities in vocational training, health, the economy, and water management, building on existing trust and shared values between Dakar and Bern.

The two officials reviewed their partnership, aiming to give it new momentum, with a focus on technical and professional training to prepare Senegalese youth for in-demand jobs. President Faye expressed interest in Swiss expertise to equip the youth for the economy's needs. Scientific and medical cooperation, economic exchanges, and Swiss private investments in Senegal were also discussed, alongside water management and peace and security issues.

President Faye highlighted reforms to create a stable investment environment and Senegal's ambition to develop skills for economic transformation. The meeting aimed to leverage these relations for job creation, improved health services, and economic development.

Why it mattersPresident Faye is seeking to boost job creation and economic development through enhanced cooperation with Switzerland in key sectors.

Senegal football pitch condition draws criticism

Sports journalist Yoro Mangara slammed the state of the Léopold Sédar Senghor Stadium pitch after Senegal's 4-1 victory over Sudan.

Senegal's Lions secured a decisive 4-1 victory against Sudan on Thursday, November 12, at Dakar's Léopold Sédar Senghor Stadium. This win solidified coach Patrick Vieira's team's leading position in Group J.

Despite the team's success, sports journalist Yoro Mangara described the stadium's pitch as "catastrophic" during a Friday RFI broadcast. He questioned the use of billions of CFA francs for renovations that resulted in such poor playing conditions.

Mangara also highlighted issues with stadium access, noting that supporters who had purchased tickets struggled to enter, with some resorting to breaking down doors. He expressed concern that the situation had narrowly avoided a more serious incident.

Why it mattersSupporters who paid for tickets faced difficulties entering the stadium, with some resorting to force.

Senegal's ENAMC drama students win prizes at festivals in Algiers and Rabat

The troupe took a Special Jury Prize in Rabat in October 2026 and another in Algiers in July, with one student winning the best male performance award.

Students of the National School of Arts and Crafts of Culture (ENAMC) in Senegal have won prizes abroad for their stage production "La Graine de la discorde". The troupe received a Special Jury Prize at the International Festival of Higher Education Schools in Dramatic Art in Rabat, Morocco, in October 2026, and student Abdel Sow won the Best Male Performance award for his role in the play.

The production was directed by Abdel Kader Diarra, a trainer and coordinator of the school's drama division. Earlier, in July 2026, the same troupe won a Special Jury Prize at the Algeria-Africa University Theatre Festival in Algiers, Algeria.

The school's new director general, Aboubekr Thiam, has said he wants to give the institution fresh momentum and raise its profile. The prizes come after a period of slower creative activity that observers had noted at the school.

Why it mattersThe awards give the school's students recognition on the international festival circuit, which the director general says he wants to build on.
