President Faye Secures IMF Deal, Eyes Economic Reforms

Senegal's President Bassirou Diomaye Faye announced a technical agreement with the IMF for a $2.2 billion credit facility to support economic reforms.

President Bassirou Diomaye Faye has secured a technical agreement in principle with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) for a three-year, 1,243 billion FCFA ($2.2 billion USD) Extended Credit Facility. The President commended the Prime Minister and his economic team for their work in achieving this accord, which is intended to bolster ongoing economic and financial reforms. This development signals a commitment to stabilizing the nation's economy and attracting further investment.

In addition to the IMF agreement, President Faye emphasized the importance of preserving household purchasing power and combating the rising cost of living. He called for strict enforcement of consumer protection laws and the implementation of enhanced regulatory measures to control rent prices. The President also highlighted the need for strategic planning to ensure the sustainable financing of universal health coverage, pension revalorization, and social protection institutions.

The President also directed preparations for an upcoming Presidential Council for Investment (CPI) to improve the business climate and announced a special session of COS-PETROGAZ to oversee the energy sector. He stressed priorities for youth, employability, and employment, urging the acceleration of dual training programs and the finalization of the 2027 finance bill. These initiatives aim to foster economic growth and social well-being across the country.

Why it mattersSenegalese citizens are directly affected by the IMF agreement and the President's focus on the cost of living, which aims to improve their purchasing power and access to essential services.

ISO, Partners Boost Menstrual Hygiene Product Standards in Senegal

International and Swedish standards bodies are working with local firms to ensure national adoption of quality regulations for hygiene products.

Senegal's health and industrial sectors are prioritizing the quality and safety of hygiene products, with international organizations now focusing on menstrual hygiene standards. The International Organization for Standardization (ISO), in collaboration with the Senegalese Association for Standardization (ASN) and the Swedish Institute for Standards (SIS), has conducted technical missions to assess local production capabilities and quality control processes.

The initiative aims to bridge the gap between theoretical international standards and their practical application by Senegalese manufacturers. A key visit involved the hygiene product company Softcare, where discussions focused on industrial capacity, quality management, and the challenges faced by local producers. This effort seeks to ensure that ISO standards, once adopted nationally, are directly usable by the industry.

This collaboration highlights the importance of public institutions, international bodies, and the private sector working together. By improving quality requirements, the project extends beyond menstrual hygiene to support broader public health goals and enhance the living conditions of the population through better access to essential products.

Why it mattersLocal manufacturers of menstrual hygiene products are directly affected by the efforts to map existing capacities and ensure future standards are applicable.

Islamic Group Completes Two-Month Quranic Immersion for 169 Children

The Ris Al-Wahda program, organized by the Rassemblement Islamique du Sénégal, concluded its 13th edition, focusing on Quran memorization and religious sciences.

The Rassemblement Islamique du Sénégal has concluded the 13th edition of its Daara Ris Al-Wahda, a two-month intensive program that enabled 169 children from various regions to learn Quran memorization and religious sciences. The initiative aims to instill strong values and protect youth from moral decline. The program, which began in 2009 and runs annually in the summer, provides children with an opportunity to dedicate two months to Quranic study.

Director Thierno Mamoudou Soumano explained the Daara's structured approach, with three student categories overseen by religious instructors and monitors, and specialized divisions for security, catering, hygiene, and laundry. Children traveled from Dakar, Thiès, Kaolack, Mbour, and Sédhiou, with a new branch opened in Bignona due to high demand. Inspector Abdoulaye Aziz Massali highlighted the program's broader educational and civic goals, emphasizing its role in fostering Muslim unity.

Parent feedback indicates a positive impact, with children becoming more disciplined and religiously grounded. However, a lack of facilities limits enrollment to under 200 children. The organization plans to expand its educational offerings with preschool and elementary schools already established, a middle school cycle opening, and a secondary school project underway. The program operated on a budget of 13 million CFA francs, with over 7 million allocated to food.

Why it matters169 children are affected by this program which aims to protect them from moral decline and transmit solid values through Quranic memorization and religious sciences.

Senegal and IMF Agree New $2.2 Billion Loan Program

The International Monetary Fund has reached a staff-level agreement with Senegal on a new 36-month debt program to support economic reforms.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) and Senegal have agreed on a new $2.2 billion debt program, intended to support the country's economic and financial reforms from 2026 to 2029. This agreement follows the suspension of a prior $1.8 billion deal in 2023 due to the discovery of previously unreported debt. The IMF stated that the new arrangement requires "decisive corrective measures" to address the misreporting of data.

The current government, which took office after an opposition electoral victory, accused the previous administration of ex-president Macky Sall of concealing the nation's true budgetary situation. The IMF reported that Senegal's 2024 budget deficit was 12.3 percent of GDP, a significant increase from the 4.9 percent previously declared by the former government. Negotiations for the new program began in mid-October after several IMF visits.

The staff-level agreement must still be approved by the IMF's executive board. The previous deal was suspended pending further information and commitments from Senegal's new authorities. The IMF's findings indicated a substantial discrepancy in the reported budget deficit, prompting the need for a new, conditional program.

Why it mattersSenegalese investors and businesses are affected by the new $2.2 billion loan program which aims to support economic reforms and address past fiscal misreporting.
