Diphtheria Cases Rise to 34, Six Deaths Recorded in Senegal

The Ministry of Health and Public Hygiene reported 34 confirmed diphtheria cases, including six fatalities, across eight health districts since August 1.

Senegal has recorded 34 confirmed cases of diphtheria, with six deaths, since the first case was confirmed on August 1, 2026. The Ministry of Health and Public Hygiene stated that these cases are spread across eight health districts in six regions. Kédougou is identified as the epicenter, with 23 cases, while Dakar has five, Thiès has three, and Louga, Diourbel, and Kaolack each have one case.

In response to the outbreak, the Ministry, in collaboration with local authorities and partners, has intensified control measures. These include patient care, systematic investigations around confirmed cases, and the identification and monitoring of contacts. The response also involves strengthening epidemiological surveillance and implementing catch-up vaccination campaigns for unvaccinated or under-vaccinated children.

The Ministry is urging parents to adhere to the vaccination schedule and check their children's vaccination status, completing any missing doses. Diphtheria is described as an infectious, contagious, and potentially epidemic disease that is preventable through vaccination. Symptoms include sore throat, fever, difficulty swallowing, hoarseness, neck swelling, and grayish membranes in the throat, sometimes with breathing difficulties.

Why it mattersThe outbreak affects the general population by posing a risk of infection, with specific measures targeting children through vaccination and the public through awareness campaigns.

S&P Downgrades Senegal Debt to Speculative Grade

The agency cited an "extremely probable" debt restructuring that could result in creditor losses, following an IMF loan agreement.

Standard & Poor's has downgraded Senegal's long-term foreign currency debt rating from 'CCC+' to 'CC', with a negative outlook, citing an "extremely probable" debt restructuring. The agency also lowered the local currency rating from 'CCC+' to 'CCC'. This move by S&P comes just days after Senegal reached a principal agreement with the International Monetary Fund for a $2.2 billion loan over three years, intended to stabilize public finances.

The downgrade reflects S&P's assessment that a distressed debt exchange or default on the country's commercial debt in foreign currency is highly likely. The agency anticipates that ongoing renegotiations will lead to creditors receiving less than initially promised, through reductions in principal, interest, or altered payment terms. Senegal's public finances have been under significant strain since 2024, following the discovery of billions of dollars in undeclared debt by the previous government.

This rating action echoes similar concerns raised by Moody's in late August, which also highlighted increased refinancing risks for the nation. The financial pressure on Senegal stems from the revelation of substantial hidden debt, creating a challenging environment for its sovereign creditworthiness despite recent efforts to secure international financial support.

Why it mattersInvestors face potential losses due to the "extremely probable" debt restructuring and reduced creditor compensation.

Senegal Police Train Women for Peace Missions

An Elsie Project workshop in Dakar prepared 50 female officers for international peacekeeping roles.

The Senegalese National Police are increasing female participation in international peacekeeping missions through a new initiative. A mentorship and psychosocial support workshop was held on September 4, 2026, as part of the Elsie Project, which designates Senegal as the first African beneficiary country. The program aims to better prepare women officers for international peacekeeping operations by focusing on leadership, stress management, and psychosocial readiness.

Currently, women constitute 10.18% of the National Police, with 29 women holding the rank of Police Commissioner. The workshop included 50 participants: 35 women selected for a Formed Police Unit (FPU) with the UN Mission in the Democratic Republic of Congo (MONUSCO), and 15 Individual Police Officers (IPOs) who passed selection tests. Discussions covered psychological preparation, stress management, leadership development, and available support mechanisms.

Through the Elsie Project, the National Police intend to boost female representation in peace operations and foster a more inclusive security force. The goal is to equip women officers with the necessary skills and support to effectively undertake responsibilities in international peace and security missions, enhancing their capacity for leadership and operational effectiveness.

Why it mattersFemale police officers are being trained for international peacekeeping roles, enhancing their leadership and operational capabilities.

Justice Ministry Creates National Penal Policy Committee

A new consultative body will analyze crime trends and propose updates to the 2019 general penal policy circular.

Senegal has established a National Committee for Penal Policy, a new consultative and technical body tasked with updating judicial guidelines. Justice Minister Moussa Sarr issued a decree creating the committee, which will analyze crime trends and propose revisions to the January 14, 2019, general penal policy circular. The committee will also examine issues such as prison policy, alternatives to prosecution, and the execution of sentences to improve the justice system's effectiveness.

The committee comprises representatives from the Presidency, Prime Minister's office, various ministries, the Supreme Court, the Bar Association, public prosecutors, and the prison administration. It also includes members from academia and civil society, with provisions for including external experts. This broad composition aims to foster inclusive deliberation on the evolution of national penal policy and address challenges within the judicial system.

This initiative comes as Senegal faces complex penal justice challenges, including prison overcrowding, prosecution efficiency, and evolving crime patterns. The committee's mandate is to identify constraints affecting criminal case processing and formulate proposals for a more coherent and effective judicial response. Its final report will guide the development of new penal policy directions.

Why it mattersThe committee's work will affect judicial actors and the public by aiming to improve the justice system's efficiency and responsiveness to current realities.
