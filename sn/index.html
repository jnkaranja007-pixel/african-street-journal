Sport · 2026-11-01

Faye calls for lasting legacy from Dakar 2026 Youth Olympics

Senegal's president urged investment in sport as a tool for economic and social development.

President Bassirou Diomaye Faye advocated on Saturday, October 31, 2026, for lasting investment in sport to drive economic and social development in Africa. Speaking at the Summit on Sport and Sustainable Development in Dakar, ahead of the Youth Olympic Games (Joj) opening ceremony, Faye stressed the need to leverage the international competition's legacy. He stated that the Dakar Joj should foster a strong sporting youth and a sports economy for development.

Faye, addressing heads of state and international delegates, including the IOC president, highlighted the Joj as an opportunity to convert Africa's sporting potential into economic and social gains. He emphasized a sports policy encompassing school and university sport, amateur practice, high-level competition, training, coaching, and infrastructure financing. The president noted that these conditions would encourage talent, attract investment, and create a sports economy.

The Senegalese leader pointed to investments made for Dakar 2026, including new and renovated sports facilities, which he believes will provide spaces for youth and communities, strengthen social cohesion, and foster citizen engagement. Faye assured that the state would mobilize national resources to preserve this legacy and monitor summit conclusions. He also condemned racism in sport and called for peace and cooperation.

Why it mattersPresident Faye's call for a sports economy and lasting infrastructure investment directly impacts Senegal's youth and communities.

Senegal assembly rejects finance bill again

The finance committee voted down the revised 2026 budget for a second time, delaying its passage.

Senegal's National Assembly finance committee rejected the revised 2026 finance bill for the second time on October 30, 2026. The government had previously secured a postponement of the text to the commission on October 27.

The parliamentary group PASTEF-Les Patriotes and the government disagreed on budget allocations, financing methods, and expected guarantees. The committee first rejected the bill and two government amendments on October 23.

The finance bill will now be debated in a plenary session on November 3, 2026, at 10 a.m. This vote follows two committee rejections that have created uncertainty about the bill's final approval.

Why it mattersThe national assembly's repeated rejection of the revised 2026 finance bill creates uncertainty for the state's treasury.

Senegal Christians mark All Saints' Day

The feast honours all saints in the Christian faith, reminding believers of their call to holiness.

Senegalese Christians observed All Saints' Day on Sunday, November 1, commemorating all the saints of the Church. The occasion involved prayers to honour men and women who demonstrated their faith in Jesus Christ during their earthly lives.

Abbé Joseph Tendeng, vicar at the Notre-Dame des Pauvres parish in Tilène, clarified that the feast is not for the dead but for all saints. He urged believers to understand the true meaning of this significant Christian celebration.

The day serves as a moment for reflection and a reminder of each Christian's vocation to holiness. It encourages followers to live out their faith through actions and love for their neighbours, striving for a more just world.

Why it mattersSenegalese Christians are reminded of their faith and encouraged to live a life of holiness.

Louga police seize 7 kg of cannabis

Two men were arrested in Moukh Moukh with 7 kilograms of Indian hemp and other items.

The Louga regional drug brigade arrested two individuals and seized 7 kilograms of Indian hemp in Moukh Moukh, a village 52 kilometers from Louga. The operation followed intelligence about a suspected cannabis trafficking network. Police intercepted the suspects on a dirt road leading to Diewal village, finding five blocks of cannabis in their bag. A motorcycle they used was also impounded.

Further searches at the suspects' homes uncovered two additional blocks of cannabis, each weighing one kilogram, hidden in a plastic bucket at the first suspect's residence. Investigators also found two cartons containing a total of 21 bottles of whisky. The total seizure included seven blocks of cannabis, mobile phones, and a Jakarta-type motorcycle.

The two arrested individuals were taken to the service headquarters for questioning as part of the ongoing investigation. The total weight of the seized cannabis was confirmed at 7 kilograms after weighing.

Why it mattersTwo men were arrested and 7 kilograms of cannabis were seized in Senegal, along with whisky and a motorcycle.

Health · 2026-11-01

Senegal forum links sport, health, and agriculture

African food systems forum in Dakar highlights maternal health and nutrition challenges.

The African food systems forum, held on October 30 at the Museum of Black Civilizations in Dakar, brought together public officials, athletes, and youth to discuss maternal, neonatal, and child health, alongside nutrition.

Amath Pathé Sène, director general of the forum, stressed the connection between agriculture, diet, and athletic performance, stating, "We are what we eat." Cathy Fall Sow of the Gates Foundation noted that nearly 70% of the 700 daily global maternal deaths occur in Africa, urging young people and athletes to become health ambassadors.

Dr. Ibrahima Hathie, representing the agriculture minister, emphasized agriculture's role in improving nutrition and called for climate-resilient food systems. Dr. Amadou Doucouré, director of Mother and Child Health, highlighted persistent challenges and the need for cross-sectoral collaboration, including sports figures raising awareness.

Why it mattersThe forum in Dakar addressed critical maternal and child health issues and nutrition, affecting families across Senegal.
