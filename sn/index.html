Senegal justice staff can apply for reclassification from November 5

Government announces new procedure for court clerks and administrators to move up the A hierarchy after training.

Senegalese court staff can begin applying for reclassification starting November 5, 2026, according to a joint announcement from the ministries of Public Service and Justice. This process allows eligible court clerks and administrators to be appointed or reclassified into Hierarchy A following a six-month training at the Judicial Training Centre (CFJ). The initiative stems from a September 7, 2025, agreement between the government and justice sector unions.

The reclassification is not automatic and requires individual applications. Successful candidates will see improvements in their hierarchical level and salary index, with positive impacts expected on their remuneration and retirement pensions. However, maintaining a previously held grade within the new hierarchy is not possible under current legislation, as it could set a precedent and destabilize career management rules. Applications must be submitted to the Ministry of Public Service with a copy of the CFJ training completion certificate.

A draft decree has been prepared to modify existing regulations on the status of justice officials, following review by the Superior Council of Public Service on July 7, 2026. This step is part of the National Pact for Social Stability for Inclusive and Sustainable Growth. The government stated that while career prospects are improving, agents must actively apply to benefit from the reclassification.

Why it mattersCourt clerks and administrators in Senegal will have improved career and salary prospects through a new reclassification process.

Senegalese teacher arrested for visa scam

Police in Mbacké apprehended a Dakar school supervisor accused of defrauding clients of over 25 million CFA francs.

A school supervisor from a well-known Dakar college, identified as C.Y.T., was arrested in Mbacké on Friday. He is accused of defrauding at least 11 individuals of over 25 million CFA francs in a visa scam. The police apprehended him at the Iba Guèye stadium while he was attending a football match.

The accused allegedly worked as a client recruiter for a group of four, including a Malian marabout who has reportedly fled with the money and two Nigerians not residing in Senegal. C.Y.T. had previously been detained in February for fictitious Spanish work contracts but was released. He sought refuge in Mbacké at his family home after abandoning his teaching post.

Nine of the 11 complainants, all from Dakar, have reported the incident to investigators. The suspect had changed his phone number to avoid detection. This arrest follows a tip-off from Dakar police to their Mbacké counterparts.

Why it matters11 Dakar residents seeking visas were defrauded of over 25 million CFA francs by a school supervisor who has now been arrested.

Senegal aims for 48.5% emissions cut by 2035

The nation needs $24.9 billion for its climate goals, with most expected from international partners.

Senegal plans to reduce its greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions by 48.5% by 2035. Achieving this target will require $24.9 billion in investments, with a significant portion anticipated from financial partners. Minister of Environment and Ecological Transition Aliou Gori Diouf announced these goals in Dakar on Monday during the validation meeting for the third National Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0).

Without international support, Senegal projects emission reductions of only 13.3% by 2030 and 19.4% by 2035. With appropriate international backing, the country aims for 31.6% reduction by 2030 and 48.5% by 2035. The $24.9 billion needed includes $16.9 billion expected from financial partners, with the remainder sought from international cooperation, private investment, and trust funds.

The planned investments will fund initiatives such as renewable energy development, mangrove restoration, reforestation, and improved waste management. The strategy also includes developing less polluting transport like the BRT and TER. NDC 3.0 focuses on both emission mitigation and adaptation to climate change impacts, including measures for water resource management and coastal protection.

Why it mattersSenegal's climate strategy requires substantial international funding to achieve its emission reduction targets, impacting national development and environmental resilience.

Senegal parliament debates budget correction

Lawmakers are set to vote on a revised 2026 budget, with opposition lawmakers expressing concerns.

Senegalese lawmakers will reconvene to vote on a revised 2026 budget, known as the loi de finances rectificative. The finance committee has reviewed the budget, but the opposition Pastef party, holding 130 of the 165 parliamentary seats, remains unconvinced.

The Pastef party's opposition to the revised budget could potentially block the approval of a $2.2 billion loan from the International Monetary Fund. The party's stance has not changed despite a new review by the finance committee.

The revised budget aims to adjust the country's financial plan mid-year. The outcome of the parliamentary vote will determine the next steps for the proposed IMF loan and the government's financial strategy for the remainder of 2026.

Why it mattersThe revised budget and potential IMF loan approval directly affect the country's financial planning and economic stability.

Senegalese migrants arrive in Canary Islands

Two boats carrying 197 people from Senegal landed on El Hierro island in the Spanish Canaries.

Two boats that departed from Senegal have arrived in the Canary Islands with a total of 197 migrants. The first pirogue, carrying 107 people including 99 men, five women, and three minors, reached La Restinga port on Sunday. The second boat, rescued on Monday, brought 90 individuals, including 11 women and 13 children.

Migrants arriving on El Hierro were met by the Red Cross, the Canary Islands Emergency Service, and authorities. Two individuals required hospital treatment, while the others were taken to a temporary reception center. The boats were escorted by the 'Salvamar Navia' rescue vessel.

The arrivals highlight the ongoing dangerous journeys undertaken by migrants attempting to reach Europe. The Spanish Canaries are a common destination for those crossing the Atlantic from West Africa.

Why it mattersSenegalese migrants are undertaking dangerous sea journeys to reach the Spanish Canary Islands.
