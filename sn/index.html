Senegal boat disaster kills 39 migrants

Search operations continue for more victims after a pirogue carrying irregular migration hopefuls sank off Lompoul.

A pirogue carrying hopefuls for irregular migration sank off Lompoul, in the Kébémer department, killing 39 people and leaving one survivor. Four more bodies were found Monday morning in advanced decomposition, bringing the total to 39. Search teams have been mobilized for three days along a 25-kilometer coastal stretch, hampered by difficult access and strong currents that are pushing bodies south.

Of the 39 victims, 24 bodies have been identified and returned to their families, while 11 remain unidentified. A collective funeral prayer was held by firefighters before the burials. The Minister of Interior and Public Security visited the site to assess the situation and the ongoing search operations. An investigation has been opened, and fuel canisters were seized as part of the inquiry.

The Alliance des Forces de Progrès (AFP) party called for a transparent investigation into the circumstances of the shipwreck and demanded a national youth policy with clear funding and evaluation. The party stated that the tragedy highlights persistent gaps in public policies for youth, driven by unemployment and lack of prospects, pushing young people to risk their lives.

Migrant boat sinks off Senegal coast

At least 39 people died when a boat carrying migrants capsized near the Loumpoul dunes.

A boat carrying migrants sank off northwestern Senegal on Saturday, killing at least 39 people. The pirogue-type flat-bottom boat capsized near the Loumpoul dunes between Dakar and Saint-Louis. This route to Spain's Canary Islands has seen thousands die in recent years.

Emergency services initially reported 21 bodies and one survivor, but the toll rose throughout Sunday. The Senegalese Press Agency (APS) and Action for Human Rights and Friendship (ADHA) confirmed 34 deaths. An interior ministry source stated the provisional toll would unfortunately rise further.

An indeterminate number of people remain missing following the incident. The search for survivors and more victims continued on Sunday. This tragedy highlights the ongoing dangers faced by migrants attempting the Atlantic crossing.

Senegal president meets Macron in Paris

President Bassirou Diomaye Faye discussed bilateral cooperation and international issues with French leader Emmanuel Macron.

Senegalese President Bassirou Diomaye Faye met with French President Emmanuel Macron at the Élysée Palace in Paris on Monday, November 9, 2026. The meeting preceded the Paris Peace Forum, an annual gathering focused on global governance and crisis resolution.

During their talks, the two leaders reviewed bilateral issues, including preparations for an intergovernmental seminar and the development of economic and trade exchanges. The Senegalese presidency reported that they also discussed major international matters of common interest.

President Faye also thanked France for its support in organizing the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games. Following the Paris Forum, President Faye is scheduled to travel to Phnom Penh, Cambodia, for the 20th Summit of La Francophonie, themed 'Reinvesting in Peace for Shared Prosperity and Lasting Stability'.

Why it mattersSenegalese President Bassirou Diomaye Faye's discussions with French President Emmanuel Macron directly affect Senegal's international relations and economic partnerships.

Senegal court suspends Starlink licence again

Supreme Court rejects state's appeal, leaving Starlink's internet provider licence in limbo.

The Supreme Court of Senegal rejected the state's request to retract its decision on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, leaving Starlink's internet provider licence suspended for the second time in two months. This ruling follows the court's initial suspension in September. The state's attempt to present the recent publication of Starlink's terms of reference as new evidence was dismissed by the court.

The cost of Starlink's licence, reported as 100 million FCFA, has drawn scrutiny when compared to other operators. In 2007, Sudatel paid 200 million dollars (approximately 90 billion FCFA) for Expresso's licence after a tender, and Sonatel paid 100 billion FCFA in 2017 for its licence renewal and 4G. The publication of Starlink's terms of reference on the ARTP website revealed the licence fee, which is lower than that paid by smaller Senegalese internet providers.

This situation raises questions about the fairness of licensing rules for telecommunications operators in Senegal. Existing providers who paid significantly higher fees and underwent rigorous tender processes are questioning the application of regulations. The Supreme Court is expected to make a final decision on the validity of a licence awarded under these circumstances.

Why it mattersSenegalese internet service providers who paid significantly more for their licences are questioning the regulatory fairness after Starlink received a licence for a much lower fee.
