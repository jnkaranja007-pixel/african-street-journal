Senegal's sanitation office blames roadworks for sewer disruptions

The National Sanitation Office (ONAS) says Dakar municipality repairs have buried or damaged inspection chambers, while the city mayor blames the agency for delayed works.

Sources: Dakaractu and Seneweb · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Senegal's National Sanitation Office (ONAS), the state agency that runs the country's sewer and rainwater networks, rejected Dakar Mayor Abass Fall's accusations that it caused delays in municipal works, in a statement responding to his remarks at a press conference on Friday 20 November. Fall had blamed ONAS for delays to some planned work in the capital.

ONAS said road rehabilitation by the municipality has sometimes buried, damaged or blocked sewer inspection chambers with gravel and rubble. The agency said such damage can disrupt sewer networks and cause wastewater overflows on public roads and in some homes. It said it had repeatedly dealt with these incidents without publicly assigning the municipality full responsibility.

The agency manages 2 227 kilometres of wastewater network and 379,5 kilometres for rainwater, and said parts of Dakar's wastewater system are old and suffer recurring pipe breaks. ONAS said work to renew the Hann-Fann collector was at an advanced stage.

Why it mattersDakar residents can face wastewater overflows on roads and in homes when municipal road repairs damage sewer chambers, while the sanitation office renews the Hann-Fann collector.
