Dakar appeals court delays Abdoulaye Touré case to 10 December

The hearing on the prosecutor’s challenge to provisional releases was postponed at the defence lawyers’ request, with pleadings set for 10 December 2026.

Sources: Le Soleil and Seneweb · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Senegal’s Dakar Court of Appeal postponed the Abdoulaye Touré case to 10 December 2026 for pleadings, after defence lawyers requested more time. The case is before its indictment chamber on an appeal by the public prosecutor against orders granting provisional release to several defendants.

The case concerns Pape Abdoulaye Touré’s allegation that the defendants confined and tortured him. On 22 September, judges charged four people arrested by the gendarmerie’s Colobane research section. Djibril Gaye was remanded in custody; Guindo Traoré and two security agents at Dakar’s university works centre were placed under judicial supervision.

The charges include criminal association, crimes against humanity over the period from 1 February 2021 to 25 February 2024, and torture. Amadou Tidiane Cissé, known as Kabakrou, and Ibrahima Ndiaye are also under judicial supervision; Djibril Diaw is wearing an electronic tag.

Why it mattersThe defendants released under judicial supervision and Djibril Gaye, who remains in custody, await the appeal hearing scheduled for 10 December.
