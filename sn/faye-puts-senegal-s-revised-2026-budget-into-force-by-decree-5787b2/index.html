Faye puts Senegal’s revised 2026 budget into force by decree

The president bypassed repeated parliamentary rejections as the government negotiates a proposed $2.2 billion IMF programme over 36 months.

Sources: Jeune Afrique, Seneweb and Dakaractu · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Senegalese President Bassirou Diomaye Faye put the revised 2026 budget into force by decree on Thursday, after the Pastef majority rejected it in committee twice and once in the National Assembly’s plenary session. The move followed a budget impasse that had persisted since late October.

The government says the revised budget accounts for higher hydrocarbon prices, lower tax revenue expected under its recovery plan and rising debt-service costs. Senegal’s debt was estimated at 132% of gross domestic product at the end of 2024, as the country negotiates with creditors.

The International Monetary Fund announced a preliminary agreement in September for a $2.2 billion aid programme over 36 months, but its board has not approved it. IMF spokeswoman Julie Kozack said on 12 November that Senegal’s adopted budget must be consistent with commitments made in those negotiations.

Why it mattersSenegal’s government must now implement a budget shaped by higher fuel costs, lower expected tax revenue and rising debt service while negotiating with creditors.
