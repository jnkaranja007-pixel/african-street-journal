Europe's development bank opens Abidjan office and pledges 250 million euros

The bank says the new commitments will add to more than 200 million euros it has already invested in Ivory Coast, where it began operating in 2025.

Sources: Afrique Sur 7 and Ivoire Matin · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD), a multilateral lender owned by European and other governments, opened a resident office in Abidjan on Monday 23 November and announced 250 million euros in new commitments for Ivory Coast. Its president, Odile Renaud-Basso, made the announcement during a visit that runs through 25 November.

The bank said it has already invested more than 200 million euros in the country and aims to have more than 500 million euros outstanding by the end of 2026. Earlier financing went to cashew processing, mining services, local banks and the energy transition. Ivory Coast joined the bank in December 2024 and became a country of operations in 2025.

Renaud-Basso and Digital Transition Minister Djibril Ouattara signed an agreement on technical assistance, technology-project financing and support for start-ups, but no amount was announced. A financial and technical support programme for Ivorian small and medium-sized businesses is also due to launch during her visit.

Why it mattersIvorian businesses seeking long-term finance through local banks, and firms in cashew processing and energy, now have a resident bank office and 250 million euros in new commitments.
