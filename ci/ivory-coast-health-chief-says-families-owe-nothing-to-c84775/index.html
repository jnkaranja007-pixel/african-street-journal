Ivory Coast health chief says families owe nothing to receive stillborn babies

Dr Kouakoussui Konan Lambert says hospitals will notify police before transferring each body to the morgue and funeral services.

Sources: Agence Ivoirienne de Presse and Afrique Sur 7 · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

Côte d’Ivoire’s regional health director for Tchologo, Dr Kouakoussui Konan Lambert, said on Monday, 23 November 2026, that families do not have to pay 10 CFA000 to receive stillborn babies at Ferkessédougou General Hospital. He said the procedure is free at health facilities across the region.

The clarification followed a dispute with Pompes funèbres de Côte d’Ivoire (POFCI), the company managing the hospital morgue. Its deputy director in Ferkessédougou, Kouadio Evrard, said the company has charged the fee at all its branches since 2024, but gave no legal or regulatory basis. Lambert said each death must now be reported to police before the body is taken to the city morgue and then handed to funeral services.

The health director said awareness campaigns in Ferkessédougou department will cover prenatal visits, care for pregnant women, free health services and the new procedures. The hospital recorded nearly 250 early neonatal deaths in 2025; an in-depth study is planned for 2027 to identify their causes.

Why it mattersFamilies must follow a police-notification and morgue process that the regional health director says is free, while POFCI says its charge has applied since 2024.
