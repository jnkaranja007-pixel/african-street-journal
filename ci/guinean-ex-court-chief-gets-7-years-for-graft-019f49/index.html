Guinean ex-court chief gets 7 years for graft

Mohamed Lamine Bangoura was also fined 2 billion Guinean francs and banned from office for five years.

Source: Afrique Sur 7 · 2026-10-23 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Former Guinean Constitutional Court president Mohamed Lamine Bangoura was sentenced to seven years in prison on October 21, 2026, in Conakry. The Economic and Financial Crimes Court (CRIEF) found him guilty of embezzlement, illicit enrichment, corruption, and money laundering.

The court, presided over by Alpha Camara, also ordered the confiscation of Bangoura's assets in Guinea and Senegal and imposed a fine of 2 billion Guinean francs and a five-year ban from holding public office. His co-accused, Sidiki Sylla and Amadou Diallo, were sentenced to 10 years each and fined 5 billion Guinean francs, with arrest warrants issued for them.

The case involved the management of approximately 20 billion Guinean francs of public funds allocated to the Constitutional Court. Bangoura's defense argued that the institution received substantial annual funding. The state intends to proceed with enforcing the court's decision, according to state attorney Antoine Pépé Lamah.
