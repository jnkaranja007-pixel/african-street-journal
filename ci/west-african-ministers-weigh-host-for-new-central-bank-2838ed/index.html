West African ministers weigh host for new central bank

At a November 20 meeting in Abidjan, finance ministers also examined the regional payment system, a solidarity fund and a transition framework.

Sources: Afrique Sur 7 and Ivoire Matin · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Ministers responsible for the planned West African currency met in Abidjan on November 20 to decide where the future regional central bank will be based. The meeting was the sixth session of the presidential task force on the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) currency, known as the ECO, which is due to start gradually in 2027.

Ivory Coast's Economy, Finance and Budget Minister, Adama Coulibaly, opened the talks on behalf of President Alassane Ouattara and urged member states to make concrete proposals to clear remaining institutional and political blockages. Ministers were also due to consider the regional payment and settlement system, a Solidarity and Stabilisation Fund, governance rules and a transition framework, based on a technical committee report prepared in Abidjan from November 17 to 19.

The launch was first targeted for 2020, but was postponed after the Covid-19 pandemic and failures to meet convergence criteria. A roadmap adopted in June 2021 set 2027 as the new deadline, and the minister said respect for macroeconomic convergence criteria remains the condition for the currency's credibility.

Why it mattersMember states must settle the central bank's host country and the transition framework before the 2027 launch deadline.
