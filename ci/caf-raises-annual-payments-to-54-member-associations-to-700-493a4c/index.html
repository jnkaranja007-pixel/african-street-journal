CAF raises annual payments to 54 member associations to 700 000 dollars

The increase follows a 31,2 million dollar net profit and a recovery from 16,8 million dollars in reserve deficits to a 14,4 million dollar surplus.

Sources: Agence Ivoirienne de Presse, Linfodrome and Ivoire Matin · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

The Confederation of African Football (CAF) raised its annual allocation to each of its 54 member associations to 700 000 dollars from 200 000 dollars in 2021. The increase took effect in July 2026, according to financial statements presented Saturday in Abuja, Nigeria.

CAF reported a net profit of 31,2 million dollars for the financial year ending in June 2026, with revenue of 284 million dollars, up more than 200 %. Its reserves moved from a deficit of 16,8 million dollars to a positive balance of 14,4 million dollars, and cash stood at 118,8 million dollars. CAF said the allocations are intended to strengthen administration, youth football and women’s football.

The six regional unions’ allocation rose from 500 000 to 900 000 dollars. Investment in clubs increased from 19 to 48 million dollars, while the prize for the winner of the CAF Champions League rose to six million dollars from 2,5 million dollars in 2021.

Why it mattersThe 54 member associations, including Côte d’Ivoire’s, receive 700 000 dollars annually from July 2026 to support administration, youth football and women’s football.
