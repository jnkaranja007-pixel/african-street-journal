ECOWAS ministers weigh host for ECO central bank in Abidjan

The ministers are also considering payment-system components and a transition framework for a gradual launch of the single currency in 2027.

Sources: Afrique Sur 7, Agence Ivoirienne de Presse and Linfodrome · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Ministers responsible for the ECO, the planned single currency of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS), met in Abidjan on Friday, 20 November, to consider where its future central bank will be based and how to prepare a gradual launch in 2027.

Ivorian Finance Minister Adama Coulibaly opened the sixth meeting of the presidential task force on behalf of President Alassane Ouattara. The ministers are due to consider the host country, parts of a regional payment and settlement system, a solidarity and stabilisation fund, and rules for governing the institutions. Coulibaly urged member states to offer concrete proposals to resolve institutional and political obstacles, and said meeting macroeconomic convergence criteria was necessary for the currency's credibility.

The ministers are also reviewing a technical committee report from meetings in Abidjan on 17-19 November and are due to validate a transition framework. The launch was first targeted for 2020; a roadmap adopted in June 2021 set 2027 as the deadline.

Why it mattersECOWAS member states must agree on the currency's host institutions and transition framework against the 2027 launch deadline.
