Cocoa union demands names of beneficiaries of 291 milliards FCFA stock buyout

The Syndicat national agricole pour le progrès de Côte d’Ivoire (SYNAP-CI) says the regulator has not disclosed who received state funds for removing 100 000 tonnes of beans.

Sources: RFI Afrique and Linfodrome · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

The Coffee-Cocoa Council, the regulator of Ivory Coast's cocoa sector, has published a report on a buyout of 100 000 tonnes of beans from the 2025-2026 campaign. The operation was set up to relieve cooperatives where cocoa had piled up as sales slowed.

SYNAP-CI president Koné Moussa told a press conference in Plateau, Abidjan, on Wednesday 25 November 2026 that the operation was poorly managed and demanded the full list of recipients of the 291 milliards FCFA the state made available.

The council said the operation ended in August. Some professionals had complained of a lack of transparency in how it was carried out, which led the council to publish the figures.

Why it mattersCooperatives and traders who held the unsold beans, and the state budget that paid 291 milliards FCFA for them, now depend on the council naming who was paid.
