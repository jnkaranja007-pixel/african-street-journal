Ivory Coast trains 26 experts on Chemical Weapons protection

Specialists from 14 francophone nations are learning detection, decontamination, and victim care at the AILCT.

Sources: Afrique Sur 7 and Linfodrome · 2026-10-30 · 1 min read

26 experts from 14 countries are undergoing advanced training in chemical weapons protection in Ivory Coast since October 27, 2026. The course, organized with the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) at the International Academy for Counter-Terrorism (AILCT), concludes on November 3 with an integrated exercise.

For eight days, trainees are practicing detection, decontamination, emergency management, and victim care, moving from theory to practical application. Loic Luyeye, OPCW representative, noted that while Africa is free of chemical weapons, toxic substances used daily in agriculture, industry, and mining pose constant risks. A factory leak or poorly managed pesticide stock can severely impact populations.

The training, a joint effort by the OPCW, the Permanent Secretariat of the Ivorian Commission for the Prohibition of Weapons of Mass Destruction (SPCIADM-CI), and the AILCT, aims to strengthen regional protection capacities. Participants will return with skills to identify hazardous substances, isolate areas, decontaminate individuals, and organize their care, reinforcing counter-terrorism efforts for exposed states.

Why it matters26 experts from 14 countries are being trained in Ivory Coast to respond to chemical weapon threats and accidents.
