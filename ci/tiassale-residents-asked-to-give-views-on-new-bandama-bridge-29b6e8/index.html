Tiassalé residents asked to give views on new Bandama bridge

A new bridge over the Bandama River will be built as part of a five-kilometre road, and the environmental study is now open to public comment.

Sources: Agence Ivoirienne de Presse and Linfodrome · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

Gisèle Ehouman, secretary general of the Tiassalé prefecture, has invited residents to record their opinions and recommendations in a register at the prefecture on the socio-environmental impact of the new bridge at the entrance to the town. She made the call on Thursday, 12 November 2026, when the environmental and social impact study (IES) was opened.

The bridge will cross the Bandama River and be linked to a five-kilometre road with two carriageways of two lanes each, seven metres wide, separated by a two-metre central strip. Éric Zéan, of the consultancy Centunion, which completed the technical study of the route, said a 315-metre bridge will also cross the rice-growing lowland between the detention centre and the drinking water plant to join national road A2 towards Divo. The old 94-year-old bridge will be rehabilitated and kept.

Marie Prudence Gnoan of the National Environment Agency (ANDE) said the views of residents are "very much awaited" to complete the preliminary studies. Onésime Koffi, head of the prefecture's cabinet, has been named investigating commissioner for 10 working days, from 12 November to 25 November 2026, during working hours.

Why it mattersResidents have until 25 November 2026 to file their views in the prefecture register before the study is completed.
