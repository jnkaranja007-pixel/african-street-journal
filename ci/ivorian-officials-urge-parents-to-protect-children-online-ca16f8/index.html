Ivorian officials urge parents to protect children online

A data-protection official cited cyberbullying, grooming and privacy breaches, and called for a national observatory on minors’ digital safety.

Source: Ivoire Matin · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

The Vitamines Foundation held a conference in Abidjan on Friday, 20 November, urging stronger protection for children using social media. The event focused on online risks to minors’ safety, privacy and mental health.

Linda Yao, a representative of the personal-data protection directorate at the telecommunications regulator, cited cyberbullying, grooming, unsuitable content and privacy violations. She called for a national observatory focused on minors’ digital safety. Benjamin Angaman, a child-protection ministry official, said Côte d’Ivoire’s child-protection committees report dangerous situations and urged digital-risk education. He said adolescents should receive a phone from age 15 with appropriate guidance.

Three panels discussed social media’s effects on children’s development and mental health, Ivorian digital-protection law and ways to make online platforms places for learning and responsibility.

Why it mattersParents are being urged to guide adolescents who receive phones from age 15, while child-protection committees are tasked with reporting dangerous situations.
