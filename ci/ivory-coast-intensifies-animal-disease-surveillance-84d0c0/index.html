Ivory Coast intensifies animal disease surveillance

Health, animal and environmental sectors train in Grand-Bassam on zoonotic disease detection.

Source: Ivoire Matin · 2026-11-03 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Ivory Coast is strengthening its defenses against diseases that can spread from animals to humans. From November 2 to 4, 2026, health, animal, and environmental professionals are attending a training workshop in Grand-Bassam. Dr. Kallo Vessaly, Director of Veterinary Services, emphasized the need for faster detection of zoonotic diseases and better cooperation between sectors.

The initiative uses the "One Health" approach, recognizing the links between people, animals, and the environment. Diseases like Ebola, avian flu, rabies, Mpox, Rift Valley fever, Marburg virus, and bovine tuberculosis are being targeted. Recent health events in Ivory Coast's wildlife highlight the importance of constant monitoring. Participants will learn data collection, transmission, and reporting techniques, as well as early warning systems.

The training is part of the EU-funded ZOOSURSY project, supported by the World Organisation for Animal Health (OMSA). The program includes using OMSA's WildEpi tool and practical exercises. Ivory Coast aims to improve its health surveillance system to anticipate epidemics, protect its population, and conserve biodiversity.

Why it mattersIvory Coast residents face increased risk from diseases like Ebola and rabies transmitted from animals.
