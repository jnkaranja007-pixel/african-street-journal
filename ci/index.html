37 French firms seek Ivory Coast contracts

A delegation of French companies is visiting Nigeria, Senegal, and Ivory Coast to secure business deals.

37 French companies are touring West Africa from October 28 to November 4, 2026, led by Business France. The mission, which includes Nigeria and Senegal, concludes in Ivory Coast with targeted business meetings to find partners, distributors, and clients. Business France described the tour as visiting "three of the continent's most dynamic economies," with Lagos, Dakar, and Abidjan concentrating a large share of West African trade.

In Ivory Coast, the final stop, these companies will participate in a program of individual meetings aimed at accelerating their establishment, partnership, or commercial development projects within the country and the wider sub-region. The targeted sectors include industrial solutions, energy, digital technology, health, and agribusiness. The participating firms aim to offer technologies for modernizing infrastructure, telecommunications, and energy networks, as well as strengthening local production and securing sites.

This mission occurs as competition intensifies, with China, Turkey, the United Arab Emirates, and India gaining ground in West African markets previously dominated by French firms. Business France aims to "strengthen economic relations between France and West Africa." The outcome of the mission, including signed contracts or initial contacts, will be known after the Ivory Coast leg concludes on November 4.

Why it mattersIvory Coast is hosting a French business delegation seeking to establish partnerships and secure contracts in key economic sectors.

FBI shares data on Ivory Coast financial court corruption

Ivorian gendarmes arrested one person after receiving information from the FBI about a corruption network within the financial court.

Ivorian gendarmes are investigating corruption within the Economic and Financial Crimes Division (PPEF) of the Abidjan court, following information from the FBI. One person has been arrested as investigators uncovered a corruption network inside the specialized court. The FBI's involvement stems from American citizens being among the victims.

The PPEF handles sensitive cases including embezzlement, money laundering, large-scale fraud, and cybercrime. This investigation comes shortly after the PPEF issued a harsh sentence on October 22 in a sextortion case involving an American victim. The FBI was present in the courtroom during that sentencing.

The timing of this investigation is difficult for Ivorian authorities, as the country was recently removed from the Financial Action Task Force (GAFI) grey list. A new bill to create an Economic and Financial Penal Court of Appeal was also adopted on October 14.

Why it mattersThe corruption investigation within the financial court could undermine reforms aimed at combating financial crimes and maintaining international trust.

Ivory Coast launches online birth certificate platform

The ONECI introduced a digital system on October 23, 2026, to combat fraud and streamline document issuance.

The Office National de l’État Civil et de l’Identification (ONECI) launched an online platform for birth certificate applications and issuance in Yamoussoukro on October 23, 2026. This digital system, using the CityWeb software, aims to secure official documents and deter forgers, with support from the National Security Council and the Ministry of Interior.

The new platform allows citizens to securely authenticate their identity, access civil status information, and obtain birth certificate extracts protected by an electronic verification certificate (CEV). This digital seal enables verification by administrations, schools, and embassies, drawing on the National Register of Natural Persons (RNPP) as the national identity reference.

ONECI Director General Ago Christian Kodia announced the deployment of CityWeb across all civil status centers nationwide to accelerate the digitization of records. While connectivity issues persist in some areas, the ONECI is working with the Ministry of Digital Transition to ensure service continuity, offering users fewer trips and more secure documents.

Why it mattersIvorian citizens will benefit from a more secure and efficient process for obtaining birth certificates, reducing travel and administrative hurdles.

Ivory Coast exits GAFI's enhanced surveillance list

The nation has completed a 24-month reform process to be removed from the financial watchdog's grey list.

The Financial Action Task Force (GAFI) officially removed Ivory Coast from its grey list of countries under enhanced surveillance on Friday, October 30, 2026. This decision followed a GAFI plenary session held in Paris. The move concludes a 24-month period of scrutiny that began on October 25, 2024.

Ivory Coast has strengthened its financial crime prevention measures. The country completed 24 months of reforms to exit the GAFI's enhanced surveillance.

The removal from the grey list closes a 24-month period of difficulty for the Ivorian economy. The GAFI is an intergovernmental body that sets international standards to combat money laundering and terrorist financing.

Why it mattersIvory Coast's economy will be affected by its removal from the GAFI grey list after a two-year reform period.
