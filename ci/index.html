Ivory Coast simulates major cyberattack

800 participants from energy, telecoms, and finance faced a fictional digital crisis in Abidjan.

The first national cyber crisis simulation, CYBER.EX 2026, began on Wednesday, October 28, in Port-Bouët, Ivory Coast. The National Agency for Information Systems Security (ANSSI) organized the exercise where 800 participants from the energy, telecoms, and finance sectors faced a large-scale, fictional cyberattack. The teams in Abidjan were tasked with managing a crisis, making quick decisions, coordinating efforts, and maintaining services under virtual assault.

ANSSI Director General, Brigadier General Guelpétchin Ouattara, stated that participants would test their collective capabilities in real conditions against a major cyber crisis. The exercise focuses on four skills: piloting, decision-making, coordination, and business continuity. Minister Mamadou Touré, speaking on behalf of the Vice-Prime Minister and Minister of Defence, described cyberspace as a new battlefield and a domain of confrontation, emphasizing that a vulnerability in a neighboring country could become their own.

The simulation aims to improve collaboration among national cybersecurity ecosystem actors, who rarely convene. Minister of Digital Transition, Djibril Ouattara, highlighted cybersecurity as a key state policy instrument. A Senegalese delegation's presence indicated Abidjan's intention to share its experience and contribute to West Africa's collective security, according to Mamadou Touré.

Why it mattersThe simulation tested the response capabilities of 800 participants from Ivory Coast's energy, telecoms, and finance sectors to a large-scale cyberattack.

Ivory Coast mobilizes 4 billion FCFA for mental health

Minister Pierre N’Gou Dimba announced the funds for 2027 at a multisectoral dialogue on mental health.

The Minister of Health, Pierre N’Gou Dimba, announced that 4 billion FCFA will be mobilized in 2027 to strengthen mental health actions in Ivory Coast. This announcement was made on Thursday, October 29, 2026, at the opening of a multisectoral dialogue on mental health held at the Confederation Generale des Entreprise de Côte d’Ivoire (CGECI).

The dialogue aimed to address mental health issues and foster collaboration among different sectors. Minister Dimba highlighted the growing need for mental health services and the government's commitment to addressing them. The CGECI hosted the event, indicating a partnership between the government and the private sector.

The government is increasing its focus on mental well-being as part of broader public health initiatives. This funding is intended to support various programs and interventions aimed at improving mental health care access and quality across the nation.

Why it mattersThe government of Ivory Coast is allocating 4 billion FCFA in 2027 to improve mental health services for its citizens.

Ivory Coast trains 26 experts on Chemical Weapons protection

Specialists from 14 francophone nations are learning detection, decontamination, and victim care at the AILCT.

26 experts from 14 countries are undergoing advanced training in chemical weapons protection in Ivory Coast since October 27, 2026. The course, organized with the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) at the International Academy for Counter-Terrorism (AILCT), concludes on November 3 with an integrated exercise.

For eight days, trainees are practicing detection, decontamination, emergency management, and victim care, moving from theory to practical application. Loic Luyeye, OPCW representative, noted that while Africa is free of chemical weapons, toxic substances used daily in agriculture, industry, and mining pose constant risks. A factory leak or poorly managed pesticide stock can severely impact populations.

The training, a joint effort by the OPCW, the Permanent Secretariat of the Ivorian Commission for the Prohibition of Weapons of Mass Destruction (SPCIADM-CI), and the AILCT, aims to strengthen regional protection capacities. Participants will return with skills to identify hazardous substances, isolate areas, decontaminate individuals, and organize their care, reinforcing counter-terrorism efforts for exposed states.

Why it matters26 experts from 14 countries are being trained in Ivory Coast to respond to chemical weapon threats and accidents.

Health · 2026-10-30

Abidjan hospital performs three arterial surgeries

Institut Cœur de Grâce uses a hybrid operating room and French specialists to treat vascular conditions and train local doctors.

The Institut Cœur de Grâce (ICG) in Abidjan successfully operated on three patients for arterial blockages between October 26 and 28, 2026. Procedures included replacing an aortic valve, performing a coronary bypass, and treating a coarctation of the ascending aorta. The hospital utilized its latest-generation hybrid operating room for these interventions.

French specialists collaborated with ICG to conduct the surgeries and train Ivorian doctors in vascular procedures. Hospital officials stated this mission advances cardiovascular surgery and local medical capabilities. They also stressed the importance of early detection and regular cardiology check-ups for the population.

The ICG aims to improve access to cardiovascular care in Ivory Coast and the sub-region through its facilities and specialized equipment. The development of such interventions is a major challenge for the health system, as cardiovascular diseases require rapid diagnosis and appropriate management.

Why it mattersThree patients in Abidjan received specialized cardiovascular surgery to treat arterial blockages.

Ivory Coast police launch operation Epervier 12

The operation aims to dismantle criminal networks across the nation.

Operation Epervier 12 was launched in Bouaké on Thursday, October 29, 2026, by the Director General of National Police, General Youssouf Kouyaté. The Minister of Interior and Security was present at the launch. This operation involves the National Police, Gendarmerie, FACI, Waters and Forests, Customs, and Civil Protection working together.

The coordinated effort is designed to dismantle criminal networks throughout the country. General Kouyaté leads the national police force in this initiative. The participating agencies will combine their resources for a unified approach against crime.

Separately, in Yamoussoukro, about 30 female agents from the Ministry of Waters and Forests began a leadership training on October 27, 2026. This training aims to strengthen female leadership within the ministry.

Why it mattersOperation Epervier 12 aims to combat criminal networks, impacting public safety and security in Ivory Coast.
