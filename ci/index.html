African Union Head Praises Ouattara's Sahel Peace Efforts

Mahmoud Ali Youssouf commended President Alassane Ouattara's leadership in fostering regional stability and economic integration.

The President of the African Union Commission, Mahmoud Ali Youssouf, lauded Ivorian President Alassane Ouattara's vision and commitment to peace and security in the Sahel region. Youssouf specifically highlighted Ouattara's dedication to economic integration and resolving regional crises during a meeting in Abidjan on Tuesday, October 20, 2026.

The praise, according to a statement from the Ivorian Presidency, underscored Ouattara's significant role in promoting regional stability and strengthening cooperation among African nations. The discussions occurred amidst ongoing security challenges in the Sahel and critical economic integration issues facing the continent, which are key concerns for regional and continental bodies.

The African Union Commission, as the executive arm of the pan-African organization, is responsible for implementing AU decisions and fostering collaboration among member states. Youssouf's remarks emphasized the importance of leadership in addressing these complex challenges.

Why it mattersThe African Union Commission head's praise of President Ouattara's leadership impacts regional political stability and economic cooperation.

Air France, Air Côte d’Ivoire Sign Paris-Abidjan Code-Share Deal

The airlines will sell each other's flights under their own codes starting October 25, expanding options for travelers.

Air France and Air Côte d’Ivoire have formalized a code-sharing agreement for the Paris-Abidjan route, effective October 25. This partnership allows each airline to sell flights operated by the other under its own flight number, effectively combining their schedules between Roissy-Charles de Gaulle and Félix Houphouët-Boigny airport. The agreement, announced in Ajaccio, aims to broaden travel choices for passengers on this busy route.

Loyalty programs will also align, with mutual recognition between Air France's Flying Blue and Air Côte d’Ivoire's sMiles expected by year-end. This means members of both programs will be able to earn and redeem miles on each other's flights between Paris and Abidjan. Air France, a strategic partner and shareholder since Air Côte d’Ivoire's 2012 inception, sees this as a step to leverage its global network for the Ivorian carrier.

The code-share deal, which does not involve changes to the aircraft used on the route, intensifies competition with Corsair, the only other direct carrier on the Paris-Abidjan axis. Both airlines plan to extend this commercial alliance to other destinations within Air France's network before the end of the first half of 2027, potentially creating single tickets for Ivorian travelers to other cities via Paris.

Why it mattersThe agreement expands travel options and loyalty program benefits for passengers flying between Paris and Abidjan.

Ouattara Considers Cabinet Reshuffle

President Alassane Ouattara is reportedly contemplating a government overhaul due to dissatisfaction with several key issues.

President Alassane Ouattara is reportedly considering a reshuffling of his government, which has been in place since late January 2026. This potential change comes as the head of state expresses dissatisfaction with the handling of various governmental dossiers. The current administration includes Téné Birahima Ouattara, the 70-year-old younger brother of the president, who serves as Vice Prime Minister and Minister of Defense, a position he has held since March 2021.

Téné Birahima Ouattara, also known by the nickname "Photocopie" due to his striking resemblance to the president, has a background in banking and security before entering politics. He held significant roles in banking institutions, including Société Générale de Banques en Côte d'Ivoire and Banque Atlantique Côte d'Ivoire, before leading the SNG company and the International Institute for Africa. He co-founded the RDR party with his brother.

The potential cabinet reshuffle follows a period where several ministers, including Robert Beugré Mambé and Téné Birahima Ouattara, have been assigned specific missions. The President's dissatisfaction suggests a desire for new approaches or improved performance in critical areas of governance. The exact timing and scope of any changes remain uncertain, but the review indicates a focus on governmental effectiveness.

Why it mattersInvestors may be affected by potential changes in government leadership and policy direction.

Ivory Coast-China Trade Pact Lists 43 Ivorian Goods

A 1984 accord, ratified in 1985, established a trade framework favoring 43 Ivorian exports to China.

A commercial agreement signed in December 1984 between Ivory Coast and China, and later ratified by President Félix Houphouët-Boigny in September 1985, created a specific trade exchange. The pact allowed for 21 Chinese products, including rice, agricultural machinery, and textiles, to enter Ivory Coast. In return, 43 Ivorian products were designated for export to China.

The Ivorian exports covered a range of goods such as cocoa, coffee, fruits, and manufactured products. At the time of the agreement, the Most Favored Nation treatment was applied, with specific exceptions for neighboring countries and customs unions. This established a structured flow of goods between the two nations.

This commercial arrangement aimed to foster economic ties by clearly defining the goods that could be traded. The inclusion of agricultural products like cocoa and coffee, alongside manufactured items, highlighted the diverse economic relationship envisioned by the accord.

Why it mattersIvory Coast farmers are affected by the inclusion of cocoa, coffee, and fruits among the 43 products designated for export to China.

Dengue Outbreak Strains Chad's Health System

An escalating dengue epidemic in Chad has claimed eight lives and revealed critical shortages in intensive care beds and testing kits.

The dengue epidemic continues to spread across Chad, with five provinces now officially affected. The outbreak has resulted in over three thousand suspected cases and eight deaths, according to Dicia Fitouin, coordinator of the Public Health Emergency Operations Center. The province of Wadi Fira alone accounts for two thousand four hundred seventy-two suspected cases. This health crisis highlights the fragility of Chad's healthcare system, particularly its limited number of intensive care beds, estimated at less than one hundred.

Compounding the crisis, laboratories are experiencing a scarcity of essential dengue testing kits, with stocks dwindling. Dr. Bargo, an emergency physician, noted that many patients initially seek treatment at pharmacies for symptoms resembling the seasonal malaria surge, receiving anti-malarial drugs. This delay means patients often arrive at hospitals only when complications arise, such as liver, kidney, or heart failure due to acute dehydration.

Dr. Fitouin urged early consultation and advised the public to avoid anti-inflammatory drugs and stagnant water, which breeds mosquitoes. He expressed confidence that the epidemic can be overcome if the population adheres to guidelines, recalling Chad's successful containment of a similar outbreak in two thousand twenty-three.

Why it mattersThe dengue outbreak directly impacts Chadian citizens by overwhelming the healthcare system, leading to delayed treatment and potential complications.
