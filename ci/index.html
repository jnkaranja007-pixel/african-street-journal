Ouattara and Macron discuss cooperation

Ivorian newspapers report on the Ivorian president's meeting with his French counterpart in Paris.

Ivorian newspapers on Thursday, November 5, 2026, focused on two main events: a meeting between Ivorian President Alassane Ouattara and French President Emmanuel Macron in Paris, and a statement from the Ivorian Bar Association. Several papers, including Fraternité Matin and Le Patriote, highlighted the discussions between Ouattara and Macron, with Le Patriote noting that the two leaders consolidated the strong cooperation between their countries.

The Ivorian Bar Association, led by its bâtonnière Me Florence Loan Messan, issued a statement urging a distinction between political dissent and terrorism. Le Nouveau Réveil quoted Messan stating, "Between a disturbing opinion and an offense, the border must be drawn by law." This statement comes amid reports of anti-terrorism units summoning officials from the PPA-CI political party.

Other news included the approval of two reforms by Ivorian deputies, the banking law and the fourth phase of tariff dismantling with the EU, as reported by Soir Info. L'Inter highlighted Japan's announcement of 44 billion FCFA for three overpasses in Abidjan.

Why it mattersThe meeting between the presidents of Ivory Coast and France directly affects Ivorian political and economic relations with France.

PETROCI ships first crude oil cargo as operator

The national oil company's subsidiary loaded its first shipment from the Espoir field on October 31, 2026.

The Ivorian national oil company, PETROCI, shipped its first crude oil cargo on October 31, 2026, three months after taking over operations of the Espoir field. This marks PETROCI CI-26, a subsidiary fully owned by PETROCI Holding, becoming the direct operator of the offshore field near Jacqueville. This operation signifies the state-owned company's transition from a partner to a direct operator in oil asset exploitation.

This first lifting, or crude oil loading for transport, confirms the operational implementation of PETROCI's new role. Previously, PETROCI Holding primarily held stakes in projects managed by international groups, such as the Baleine field operated by Eni. Now, PETROCI directly manages the Espoir field's production, offshore maintenance, and crude oil loading, a responsibility previously held by Canadian Natural Resources (CNR).

The Espoir field, discovered in 1980 and redeveloped for production in February 2002, is now under the direct management of PETROCI CI-26, led by Mabintou Tatiana Cissé. The next steps for PETROCI involve sustaining production, controlling costs, and building national capacity in oil exploitation. The volume of this initial cargo, the buyer, and its destination were not publicly disclosed.

Why it mattersPETROCI's direct operation of the Espoir oil field means the national company is now responsible for production and revenue, rather than just holding shares.

Cahié Kunta sentenced to three months for public disorder

The social media personality was convicted of disturbing public order, insults, and spreading false news.

The court of first instance in Abidjan-Plateau sentenced Cahié Kakou Guy Joël, known online as Cahié Kunta, to three months in prison on Thursday, November 5, 2026. He was found guilty of disturbing public order, insults, and spreading false news via an information system.

The conviction relates to charges of public disorder and insults. This judicial outcome follows proceedings against the social media personality.

In a separate case in Adjamé, municipal councilor Fousséni Konaté received a one-month prison sentence after a complaint filed by Mayor Farikou Soumahoro.

Amad Diallo nominated for African Player of the Year

The Manchester United winger is the sole Ivorian contender for the 2026 award, to be presented in Lagos.

The Confederation of African Football (CAF) announced its 10 nominees for the 2026 African Player of the Year award on Wednesday, November 4. Amad Diallo of Manchester United is the only Ivorian player on the list, competing against three nominees each from Morocco and Senegal. The winner will be revealed on December 7 in Lagos, Nigeria.

Diallo represents Ivory Coast alone among the 10 selected players, who hail from six different nations. Morocco's Achraf Hakimi, the 2025 titleholder, is nominated alongside Brahim Díaz and Ismaël Saibari. Senegal's contenders are Sadio Mané, Ismaïla Sarr, and Pape Gueye. Other nominees include Yoane Wissa (DR Congo), Antoine Semenyo (Ghana), and Teboho Mokoena (South Africa).

Hakimi is considered a strong contender due to his status as the reigning champion and Morocco's multiple nominations. Diallo faces the challenge of overcoming a difficult season with Manchester United. The CAF Awards ceremony will also announce winners in other categories, such as best goalkeeper, where Morocco's Yassine Bounou is nominated.

Why it mattersAmad Diallo, the only Ivorian nominated for the African Player of the Year award, faces strong competition from titleholder Achraf Hakimi.
