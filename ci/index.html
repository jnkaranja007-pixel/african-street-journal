PDCI cyberactivists defend Tidjane Thiam online amid party tensions

A network of online activists is actively supporting Tidjane Thiam, a presidential candidate for the PDCI, as internal party disputes intensify ahead of a court decision on.

The Parti démocratique de Côte d’Ivoire (PDCI) is increasingly relying on cyberactivists to defend presidential candidate Tidjane Thiam against both rival parties and internal detractors. These online supporters, described as a “galaxy” of volunteers, are active across social media platforms like TikTok, Facebook, and X, according to Jeune Afrique.

This digital mobilization comes as the PDCI faces internal turmoil, with Michel Gueu, a vice-president, urging militants and executives to exercise restraint, engage in dialogue, and respect party institutions. Gueu also called for mediation and advised against escalating tensions, as reported by Linfodrome.

The party's reliance on cyberactivists marks a shift from its traditional support base of established figures. This online influence is proving difficult for the party to regulate, particularly as a court decision regarding the potential removal of the PDCI president is scheduled for October 22.

Why it mattersThis political development affects Ivorian citizens by shaping the public discourse around a major political party and its leadership, potentially influencing future elections and governance.

Ivorian authorities increase security after shots fired near Burkina Faso border

Ivorian authorities have reinforced security in the Doropo department following reports of gunfire heard Sunday evening near the Bodana border post in Burkina Faso.

Security forces in Ivory Coast have increased their presence in the Doropo department after gunshots were heard from Burkina Faso on Sunday evening. The detonations occurred near the Bodana border post, approximately 800 meters from Ivory Coast's Kodiénou post, according to Linfodrome.

The sounds of armed incidents, perceived from Ivorian territory, led to the heightened alert. Ivoire Matin reported that the tranquility of the Doropo department was disturbed by a series of armed incidents at the Burkina Faso border.

The increased security measures aim to address the disturbance caused by the cross-border events. The specific nature of the incidents and the parties involved in the gunfire were not detailed in the reports.

Why it mattersResidents near the border in the Doropo department are directly affected by increased security presence and potential instability from cross-border incidents.

Ivory Coast officials condemn illegal gold mining during Mawlid celebration

During the official Mawlid 1448H celebration in Abidjan, Ivorian authorities called for the preservation of natural resources and a crackdown on illegal gold mining.

The Ivorian government used the official Mawlid 1448H celebration at the Golf mosque in Abidjan to address the issue of illegal gold mining. The Minister in charge of Cults, speaking on behalf of President Alassane Ouattara, emphasized the importance of strengthening fraternity and solidarity, while also highlighting the dangers of illegal gold panning.

The celebration of the Prophet Muhammad's birth served as a platform for a strong appeal to protect natural resources. This message directly targeted illegal gold mining, known locally as "orpaillage illégal," which poses a threat to the country's environmental and economic stability.

The presence of high-ranking state authorities at the event underscored the government's commitment to tackling this issue. The call to action aims to curb unauthorized mining activities that can lead to environmental degradation and economic losses for the nation, as reported by Ivoire Matin.

Why it mattersThe Ivorian government's public condemnation of illegal gold mining directly affects those involved in the practice by signaling increased enforcement and potential disruption to their operations.

BSSI dismantles illegal gold mining sites, seizes equipment in August operations

The Special Surveillance and Intervention Brigade (BSSI) conducted multiple operations in August 2026, dismantling illegal gold mining sites and seizing equipment across several.

The BSSI, under the Ministry of Water and Forests, intensified its crackdown on illegal gold mining throughout August 2026. Operations included the dismantling of sites at Sissingué and Kanakono on August twenty-third, despite difficult access due to rising waters. The brigade also targeted other fronts in the East and Southeast, employing aerial surveillance with drones and helicopters to identify suspicious clearings and eroded riverbanks.

In Zaranou on August twenty-third, fifty agents conducted "Operation Wavie," dismantling a large clandestine complex. Five gold miners, including two Burkinabè and three Togolese nationals, were arrested and referred to the economic and financial penal division. Authorities seized and destroyed 189 motor pumps, 23 crushers, 28 washing plants, 70 washing mats, 243 fittings, 59 makeshift shelters, 8 motorcycles, and 82 B6 gas bottles.

Further operations included "Operation Opo Wavie" in the classified forest of Diambarakro from August twenty-first to twenty-second, where 54 agents arrested five individuals, including three Togolese, one Ivorian, and one Nigerien, and destroyed 22 pieces of equipment. In Nougoua, from August seventeenth to twentieth, 15 agents dismantled several sites and destroyed 120 pieces of equipment, including motor pumps and crushers.

Why it mattersIllegal gold mining operations are being disrupted, affecting those involved in the illicit trade and potentially impacting local communities and the environment.

Cnam caravan brings universal health coverage to Bouaflé residents

The National Health Insurance Fund (Cnam) organized an information and awareness caravan in Bouaflé to promote universal health coverage (CMU) among the local population.

The National Health Insurance Fund (Cnam) held an information and awareness caravan on Saturday, August twenty-second, 2026, at Place Tchin-Tchin in Bouaflé. This initiative aimed to increase understanding of the Universal Health Coverage (CMU) system and encourage enrollment among residents.

The event was part of Cnam's ongoing effort to deploy CMU services closer to the population. By organizing this caravan, Cnam sought to make health services more accessible and better known to the inhabitants of Bouaflé.

The caravan focused on informing the public about the benefits and procedures of the CMU, with the goal of improving health access for a wider segment of the population in the Marahoué region.

Why it mattersResidents of Bouaflé are affected as the caravan aims to increase their understanding and enrollment in universal health coverage, potentially improving their access to healthcare.
