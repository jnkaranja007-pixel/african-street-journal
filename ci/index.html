Ivory Coast MP to honour teachers for peace efforts

Oumé MP Flore Kouassi Lago announced a tribute day for teachers who promoted coexistence during the 2025 presidential election.

Oumé Member of Parliament Flore Kouassi Lago announced on October 30, 2026, that a tribute day will be organised for teachers in the Oumé department. The event will honour educators from all political sides who contributed to promoting peaceful coexistence during the 2025 presidential election. Lago stated that the gathering would include teachers who participated in maintaining peace and social cohesion in Oumé.

The parliamentary initiative also extends to teachers in Kouaméfla, Guépahouo, and Doka sections, as well as other areas within her electoral constituency. Lago credited the victory of the RHDP candidate in the 2025 presidential election to the efforts of individuals who worked towards peace, reconciliation, and social unity. She also acknowledged the mobilisation and contributions of the UE-RHDP militants during the presidential vote.

The RHDP party secured six out of seven seats in the 2026 legislative elections within the concerned electoral district. The meeting was attended by Senator Fatou Sangaré and deputy substitute Sekongo Yéyédé.

Addah village ends 12-year succession crisis

Roger Sangahi installed as chief, aiming to restore community cohesion after years of division.

The village of Addah, near Jacqueville, has ended a 12-year succession crisis with the official installation of Roger Sangahi as chief on Saturday, October 31, 2026. The designation followed a consensus reached on August 19, 2026, among the village's four traditional families, mediated by the Jacqueville prefect. This marks the first such ceremony in Addah in over 50 years, according to the organizing committee president.

The 12 years of disputes, from 2014 to 2026, had impacted community cohesion and village affairs, stated the new chief's spokesperson. Roger Sangahi, an insurance agent born in 1964, has prioritized reconciliation and unity. He urged residents to move past divisions and rebuild trust, emphasizing that trust is not decreed but earned. The ceremony was attended by regional authorities and Bishop Mgr Jean-Salomon Lezoutchié.

Sangahi outlined the village's urgent needs, including potable water, expanded electricity, road paving, improved school and health facilities, and land security against coastal erosion. Addah, located between the Ébrié Lagoon and the Atlantic Ocean, also plans to develop artisanal fishing, cassava processing, and tourism. Minister of Public Service, Anne Désirée Ouloto-Lamizana, advised Sangahi to govern with impartiality and justice, serving the community above all else.

Why it mattersThe village of Addah has resolved a 12-year leadership dispute, affecting community cohesion and development, with the installation of Roger Sangahi as chief.

Ivory Coast improves governance ranking

The nation rose 10 places in the Ibrahim Index of African Governance 2026, reaching 17th position.

Ivory Coast ranked 17th out of 54 African nations in the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, released on October 31. The country scored 58.1 points, surpassing the continental average of 50.1 and West Africa's average of 52.9. The nation gained 4 points since 2016.

The country's progress was driven by strong gains in economic opportunities, which rose 10.8 points to 64.5, and human development, up 6.8 points. Security and rule of law remained stagnant at 54.1, and participation, rights, and inclusion saw a slight decline to 56.8.

Ivory Coast was removed from the Financial Action Task Force's grey list on October 30, 2026, after 24 months. This removal followed a plenary session of the GAFI in Paris. The country had been placed on the enhanced surveillance list on October 25, 2024.

Why it mattersIvory Coast citizens are directly affected by improvements in governance, economic opportunities, and human development, as well as by the country's removal from a financial surveillance list.

Ivory Coast potato imports climb 45% to $12.5 million

Demand for fresh and frozen potatoes rises as domestic production meets only a fraction of consumption needs.

Ivory Coast spent US$12.5 million on fresh or chilled potato imports in the first nine months of 2026, a 45% increase from US$8.64 million in the same period of 2025. This spending is nearing the US$12.58 million total for all of 2025. Domestic production is less than 100 tons annually, while consumption is estimated between 60,000 and 80,000 tons.

Imports of frozen potato products, including French fries, grew by approximately 78% from US$1.52 million in 2020 to US$2.70 million in 2025. Egypt has become a significant supplier, with its potato shipments to Ivory Coast rising from US$14,000 in 2020 to US$1.68 million by 2025, supported by strong logistics. Mali's exports to Ivory Coast fell sharply by nearly 92% to US$37,000 in the first nine months of 2026.

Urbanization, the expansion of fast-food businesses, and changing diets are driving increased demand for potatoes. African suppliers must compete on delivery consistency, product quality, and price to succeed in the Ivorian market. Senegal is also looking to increase its regional potato exports, identifying Ivory Coast as a potential market.

Why it mattersIvorian consumers and businesses face higher costs for potatoes due to increased import spending and reliance on foreign supply.
