Singer A’Salfo Buys Racing Club de Koumassi

The lead singer of Magic System has officially entered Ivorian football by acquiring a third-division club in Abidjan.

Ivorian singer A’Salfo, leader of the group Magic System, has purchased the Racing Club de Koumassi, a club that competes in the Ivorian third division. This acquisition, announced in late August 2026, marks the artist's official entry into the local football scene.

Known as the "Gaou magicien," A’Salfo is now among prominent Ivorian figures investing in domestic football. The move follows a trend of ministers, deputies, and businesspeople entering the sport, which is described as an arena of power and influence in Côte d’Ivoire.

The purchase of the Abidjan-based club by the Magic System frontman signifies a new venture for the artist. Jeune Afrique reported the transaction, highlighting the growing trend of influential individuals acquiring football clubs within the country.

Why it mattersThe acquisition of a football club by a prominent artist affects business investment in the sport.

Socoprim Secures 200 Billion FCFA Abidjan 6th Bridge Project

The toll bridge, intended to connect Abatta to Koumassi and Port-Bouët, is reportedly awarded to Socoprim over Chinese firm CCECC, pending official government confirmation.

The planned 6th bridge in Abidjan, an infrastructure project estimated at nearly 200 billion FCFA, is reportedly set to be built by Socoprim. This toll road is designed to link Abatta with Koumassi and Port-Bouët. Africa Intelligence reported the potential award, noting that Socoprim would undertake the project instead of the Chinese group CCECC. The government of Ivory Coast has not yet officially confirmed these details.

This new structure is expected to span approximately 1.5 kilometers and will be constructed in two phases: Abatta-Koumassi and Koumassi-Port-Bouët. The project's financing, construction, operation, and maintenance are planned under a public-private partnership. Socoprim, which already manages the Henri-Konan-Bédié bridge, could be taking on another significant infrastructure venture. Its shareholders include the Ivorian state, Ivoire Infrastructures Routières, Africa Finance Corporation, PAIDF, BNI, and TotalEnergies Côte d’Ivoire.

The Chinese company CCECC had previously been involved, signing a memorandum of understanding with the Ivorian state for studies and financing mobilization. This agreement, valid until September 2024, did not lead to the commencement of works, which were initially slated for 2024. The start of construction is now tentatively suggested for 2026, though an official schedule remains unannounced. The government must still confirm Socoprim's selection and the project's final execution terms.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the potential award of a major infrastructure project valued at nearly 200 billion FCFA, which could impact Socoprim's financial standing and future projects.

SODECI announces water outage in Bouaflé due to electrical issue

The water supply company cited a technical problem on the electrical network powering its production and distribution facilities as the cause for the interruption.

The Société de distribution d'eau de la Côte d'Ivoire (SODECI) announced a halt in potable water delivery across several neighborhoods in Bouaflé on Thursday, August 27, 2026. The interruption stems from a technical fault directly impacting the electrical grid that supplies the city's water production and distribution infrastructure.

This situation affects residents who rely on the municipal water system for their daily needs. The problem with the electrical network is the sole reason provided by SODECI for the disruption in service, leaving the duration of the outage unspecified.

While the primary evidence focuses on Bouaflé, additional reporting indicates that residents in Abidjan South may also face electricity supply disruptions over the weekend due to ongoing work on their electrical network. This suggests potential broader infrastructure challenges affecting essential services.

Why it mattersResidents of Bouaflé are directly affected by the water outage due to a technical problem with the electrical supply to water facilities.

Ministry Sets Skirt Length for Girls' School Uniforms

The Ministry of National Education, Literacy and Technical Education has issued a directive specifying that girls' skirts must be 11 centimeters above the ankle for the upcoming.

The Ministry of National Education, Literacy and Technical Education has mandated a specific skirt length for female students ahead of the 2026-2027 school year. A directive from the Directorate of Schools, Lycées and Colleges (DELC), dated August 26, 2026, states that the hem of girls' skirts must be precisely 11 centimeters above the ankle. This instruction, signed by DELC Director Zamblé Bi Zamblé Germain, has been communicated to regional directors and heads of educational institutions for immediate implementation.

According to the ministry, the new regulation aims to balance appropriate attire with practical considerations for students. The DELC believes that setting the skirt length at 11 centimeters above the ankle will prevent excessively long skirts that could impede movement or participation in school activities. The directive emphasizes a pedagogical approach, encouraging dialogue with students and families to ensure necessary adjustments are made before the start of the academic year.

School heads are responsible for informing students and their parents about the new uniform requirement. The DELC stressed the importance of strict adherence to this measure from the very first days of the 2026-2027 school year. The ministry expects educational institutions to enforce the rule with a focus on constructive communication and respect for students.

Why it mattersFemale students will need to adjust their school skirts to comply with the new length regulation, affecting their daily school attire.

Debt Service Consumes 58% of Ivory Coast Budget

National debt servicing costs represent 58% of the national budget, despite a significant decrease in the overall debt-to-GDP ratio from 59.5% in 2024 to 25% by the end of 2025.

Ivory Coast's debt servicing expenditure has reached 58% of the national budget, a figure revealed by Sansan Hien, Director General of Economy at the Ministry of Economy, Finance, and Budget. This substantial portion of the budget is allocated to managing the country's debt obligations.

Despite the high servicing costs, the country has seen a marked reduction in its overall debt burden. The debt-to-GDP ratio has fallen significantly from 59.5% in 2024 to an estimated 25% by the close of 2025, indicating a successful effort in reducing the total amount owed relative to economic output.

These figures were presented during the 6th African Conference on Debt and Development (AfCODD VI) in Nairobi on Wednesday, August 26, 2026. The conference provided a platform for discussions on fiscal challenges and strategies across the continent.

Why it mattersInvestors are directly affected as the high proportion of the national budget dedicated to debt servicing may limit public spending on infrastructure and economic development projects.
