Ivory Coast secures 639 million euros from Italy

The funds will finance 10 projects in priority sectors, following a forum on Italian investments in Abidjan.

Ivory Coast is set to receive 639 million euros, approximately 420 billion FCFA, from Italy to fund about 10 projects. The announcement came on Thursday, October 15, 2026, at the opening of the Italian Investment Forum in Abidjan. The Centre for Promotion of Investment in Ivory Coast (CEPICI) played an active role in the forum.

The investment aims to bolster key sectors of the Ivorian economy. CEPICI's involvement signifies a concerted effort to attract and facilitate foreign direct investment. The forum provided a platform for dialogue between Ivorian authorities and Italian investors to explore opportunities.

This financial package represents a significant boost for Ivory Coast's development agenda. The specific projects to be funded are expected to be in areas deemed critical for the nation's economic growth and diversification.

Why it mattersIvory Coast businesses and the economy will benefit from new investment capital and project funding.

Ivory Coast completes 220 child labor actions

A joint initiative by Solidaridad West Africa and Olam Food Ingredients has reported an 88% execution rate on planned actions against child labor.

Solidaridad West Africa and Olam Food Ingredients have completed 220 of 251 planned actions against child labor in Ivory Coast's cocoa sector. This represents an 88% execution rate for the initiative, which concluded its activities with a workshop in Cocody on Wednesday, October 21, 2026. The program, known as the Child Labor Monitoring and Remediation System (CLMRS), was deployed in July 2025 and covered 10 cooperatives within Olam Food Ingredients' supply chain.

The CLMRS initiative aimed to monitor and address instances of child labor within the cocoa farming communities. The system's implementation involved working directly with cooperatives to identify and remediate issues related to child labor, ensuring compliance with ethical sourcing standards. The workshop served as a platform to present the final results and discuss the impact of the program.

Olam Food Ingredients, a global leader in food and beverage ingredients, partnered with Solidaridad West Africa to implement this system. The focus on the cocoa supply chain highlights the sector's vulnerability to child labor and the efforts being made by major industry players to combat it. The program's success is measured by the number of actions taken and the coverage of cooperatives within the supply chain.

Why it mattersCocoa farmers are affected by actions taken to monitor and remediate child labor in their supply chains.

Camara elected Ivory Coast Senate president

Kandia Kamissoko Camara, a former English teacher and minister, has been elected president of the Ivorian Senate.

Kandia Kamissoko Camara, born in Abidjan in 1959, assumed the presidency of the Senate of Ivory Coast on October 12, 2023. She is also the mayor of Abobo. Before her current role, Camara had a career as an English teacher for nearly 20 years and served as the minister of National Education for a decade. She was also the first woman to lead the country's foreign affairs.

Camara's background includes a notable period as a competitive handball player, winning the Ivorian championship in 1974 and 1980, and the African Clubs Champions Cup in 1981. After her athletic career, she pursued higher education, earning an English degree from the University of Abidjan and a certificate in advanced education studies from the University of Lancaster in England. She began teaching in 1983.

Her political journey began with the Ivorian Popular Front (PDCI) before she joined the Rally of Republicans (RDR) in 1994. Camara held significant positions within the RDR's women's wing and served as a special advisor to the Prime Minister. In 2011, she was appointed Minister of National Education, a position she held until April 2021, overseeing technical education and vocational training during her tenure.

Why it mattersIvorian citizens are affected by the election of a new Senate president who previously held significant ministerial roles.

Politics · 2026-10-22

12 charged over gendarme killing amid election tensions

Authorities have charged 12 people with murder and terrorism in connection with the death of a gendarme last year.

12 people have been charged and remanded in custody over the killing of a gendarme, an incident that occurred one year after deadly election-related violence in Ivory Coast. Prosecutor Oumar Braman Koné announced the charges on Tuesday, stating the suspects admitted involvement in the shooting of Second Lieutenant Daniogo Klênon Lacina on October 20, 2025, while he patrolled near Agboville. The alleged shooter confessed to opening fire on security forces.

The accused face serious charges including murder, terrorist acts, conspiracy against state authority, and participation in an insurrectionary movement. The case has been transferred to Abidjan’s anti-terrorism division. This investigation unfolds against a backdrop of lingering political tensions following President Alassane Ouattara’s disputed fourth-term election victory last year, an event that officially claimed 11 lives.

The opposition party PPA-CI has accused the government of anti-democratic actions, though authorities maintain the prosecutions are not politically motivated. The gendarme’s death and subsequent charges highlight the ongoing security concerns and political instability in the country.

Why it mattersThe charges and ongoing political tensions directly affect Ivorians by raising concerns about security and the rule of law following disputed elections.

Ivory Coast NGO reports dozens harmed by skin-lightening creams

An Ivorian non-governmental organization has documented dozens of victims suffering lasting health damage from unregulated skin-lightening cosmetic products.

An Ivorian non-governmental organization has raised concerns over the health impacts of skin-lightening creams, soaps, and lotions. The group reported identifying dozens of individuals who have suffered severe and sometimes permanent health consequences from using these products in recent months.

The NGO stated that while many cosmetic products are part of daily life, some do not meet safety standards and can lead to serious health issues. The organization is now calling for stricter regulatory oversight and increased controls on the sale and distribution of such cosmetics within the country.

The victims' ailments range from skin conditions to other lasting effects, highlighting a significant public health challenge. The NGO's alert underscores the hidden dangers associated with beauty products that promise lighter skin but deliver detrimental health outcomes.

Why it mattersDozens of people in Ivory Coast are suffering lasting health damage from using unregulated skin-lightening cosmetic products.
