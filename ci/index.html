Ivory Coast demolishes 176 risky sites in Abidjan

Thousands of families are homeless after Abidjan authorities cleared 176 sites deemed at risk.

Abidjan's bulldozers have cleared 176 sites identified as risky, displacing thousands of families. Dwellings in neighborhoods from Adjamé to Yopougon, Port-Bouët, and Koumassi have been demolished since the start of the year. Many residents lost their homes and possessions, with some sleeping in markets, bus stations, or cemeteries.

The government plan aims to improve safety by removing homes from unstable slopes, flood-prone areas, and sites near high-tension power lines. Minister-Governor Cissé Ibrahim Bacongo oversees the implementation of this plan, with President Alassane Ouattara reportedly informed of each demolition. However, the prosecutor stated that demolitions, like one in Koumassi Campement, occurred without a court order, leading to an ongoing investigation.

The operation has also affected schools, with several educational facilities destroyed before alternative arrangements were made for students. This forces parents, already displaced, to find new schools for their children, often without a stable address. The scale of displacement in peacetime Abidjan is unprecedented, according to the report.

Why it mattersThousands of families in Abidjan are left without shelter and schools are being destroyed, impacting children's education and stability.

Ivory Coast seeks 984 billion FCFA for climate-sensitive disease projects

The delegation at COP 31 in Antalya, Turkey, is requesting funds for six projects to make the health system more resilient to climate risks.

Ivory Coast is seeking 984 billion FCFA to finance six projects aimed at improving the resilience of its health system to climate risks. The request is being made by its delegation at the 31st Conference of the Parties (COP 31), held in Antalya, Turkey.

Vice-President Tiémoko Meyliet Koné is representing President Alassane Ouattara at the conference, which runs from November 9 to 20, 2026. The projects focus on adapting to and preventing health problems linked to climate change.

The figure of 984 billion FCFA is the total the delegation is looking for across the six projects. The packet does not say which funders have been approached or when a decision on the funding is expected.

Why it mattersThe projects would strengthen the health system against climate risks, which affects the population Ivory Coast's health services serve.

Two men charged with murder near Korhogo

The victim's cousin allegedly planned the killing due to jealousy over a relationship.

Two men, including the victim's cousin, face charges for the suspected premeditated murder of S. S. near Korhogo. The victim's body was found on September 30, 2026, near the village of Waraniéné with a suspicious mark on his neck.

Investigations by the Korhogo Brigade de recherche et d’intervention (BRI) revealed prior tensions between the cousins. An anonymous tip on November 4 led to the arrest of a second suspect in Kafonon, who reportedly confessed and implicated the victim's cousin as the mastermind.

The victim's cousin allegedly admitted to planning the murder, citing jealousy and frustration over the victim's relationship with his own partner. Both suspects have been presented to judicial authorities for prosecution.

Bouna mosque restored after two weeks of work

Artisans from several countries worked to save the mud-brick structure from time and climate change.

A mosque in Bouna, Ivory Coast, has been restored after a two-week project that concluded in late October. The mud-brick structure, located near the borders of Ghana and Burkina Faso, was weakened by age and climate change. Workers from various countries participated in the restoration effort and trained local artisans.

This project is part of a wider initiative to preserve Sudanese-style mosques across West Africa, many of which are not UNESCO World Heritage sites and face degradation. The Bouna mosque's restoration aimed to save the fragile building and ensure its longevity.

Similar Sudanese-style mosques exist in Mali, Burkina Faso, and Ghana, with some recognized for their global heritage value. The Bouna restoration highlights the ongoing challenges faced by less protected historical structures in the region.

Why it mattersThe restoration of the Bouna mosque preserves a piece of cultural heritage for Ivorians and visitors.
