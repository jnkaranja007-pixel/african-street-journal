Dabou prefect calls for collective action against illegal gold mining

Prefect Michèle Hortense Amani Loboué urged community leaders and residents to join the fight against illegal gold mining.

Dabou prefect Michèle Hortense Amani Loboué called for collective action to combat illegal gold mining in the department on October 22, 2026. The prefect stated that the illegal activity is causing significant water pollution, forest destruction, and land degradation. This illegal mining also disrupts local economies, threatens social cohesion, and results in lost state revenue.

Mme Amani Loboué emphasized that prefectural authorities and security forces alone cannot win the fight against illegal gold mining. She specifically requested the engagement of village chiefs, community leaders, landowners, officials, guides, youth, and women. The prefect encouraged sub-prefects of Dabou, Lopou, and Toupah to continue awareness campaigns in their areas.

Discussions during the meeting covered the legal framework for artisanal mining, environmental impacts, and ways to strengthen trust between security forces and the public. While Dabou has no reported cases of illegal gold mining, the department of Grand Lahou does. The prefect urged constant vigilance and reporting of suspicious activities to authorities to preserve the department and ensure sustainable development.

Why it mattersIllegal gold mining is causing environmental damage and economic disruption in the Dabou department.

Ivory Coast court postpones Tidjane Thiam PDCI leadership hearing

A court in Abidjan adjourned a case challenging Tidjane Thiam's election as PDCI president until November 19.

A court in Abidjan postponed a hearing on Tidjane Thiam's election as president of the Democratic Party of Ivory Coast (PDCI). The hearing was adjourned until November 19 after the judge decided to consolidate additional legal actions.

Two party members brought the case claiming the party's rules were not followed during the electoral process. These include a challenge by another member seeking to annul resolutions from last year's party congress and appoint an interim president. The first two suits are against the PDCI, while a third case targets Thiam and his vice president directly.

In May 2025, an Ivorian court dismissed a previous legal challenge against Thiam's leadership of the PDCI, which argued he was ineligible due to acquiring French citizenship in 1987.

Why it mattersThe election of Tidjane Thiam as president of the PDCI is being challenged in an Ivorian court.

Ivory Coast opposition proposes new election authority

Opposition parties want an independent body to manage polls, excluding political representatives.

Opposition parties and movements in Ivory Coast are advocating for a reformed electoral governance structure, proposing the creation of an independent National Electoral Authority (AENI). This new body would be responsible for organizing elections and referendums, aiming to strengthen the credibility of polls by operating on principles of independence, neutrality, impartiality, transparency, and professionalism.

The proposed mechanism includes a complementary structure, the Permanent Election Observatory (OPE), tasked with monitoring the electoral process, evaluating its regularity, and issuing alerts and recommendations. A National Selection Committee (CNS) would also be established to recruit members for both bodies, without direct involvement in the practical organization of the votes. The AENI would be an autonomous public institution with administrative, financial, technical, and operational independence.

The AENI's mandate would cover the entire electoral cycle, from voter list establishment and revision to the proclamation of provisional results. Its leadership would be a college of 11 members from diverse professional backgrounds, excluding political party and government representatives. The OPE would independently observe and evaluate, with its recommendations reviewed by the AENI, which must provide reasoned responses and publish a follow-up report.

Why it mattersOpposition parties in Ivory Coast are proposing a new independent body to organize elections, aiming to restore citizen and political actor confidence in the electoral process.
