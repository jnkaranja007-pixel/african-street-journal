Ministry Validates Professional Qualification Standards

Ivory Coast's employment ministry launched technical workshops to ensure new professional certification frameworks meet regulatory and methodological requirements.

The Ministry of Employment, Social Protection, and Vocational Training has begun a technical validation process for several professional qualification certificate (CQP) frameworks. This initiative aims to ensure the certifications align with methodological and regulatory standards. The process was officially launched in Abidjan on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, during workshops that brought together industry professionals, methodologists, and other relevant stakeholders.

The Director General of Apprenticeship and Continuing Education, Kakou Yao Rita, emphasized that CQPs should serve as effective tools for matching training with labor market needs, stating, "We are no longer training for the sake of training." She noted that the initial four CQPs are designed for a maximum training duration of eleven to twelve months and aim for at least an 80% professional integration rate for participants.

N’Dri Koffi, president of the National Council of Professional Branches, praised the ministry’s effort to strengthen public-professional dialogue in qualification development. He affirmed the professional branches' commitment to supporting CQP implementation to address evolving job requirements. Technical advisor Nour Diarrassouba, speaking for the Minister of Employment, highlighted that the workshops seek to involve professionals in creating accessible, relevant, and recognized qualifications, expecting conclusions that will enhance the national training system.

Why it mattersIvory Coast's employment ministry is working to ensure professional certifications align with market needs, potentially improving job prospects for individuals seeking employment.

Gendarmerie Destroys 109 Dredges, Arrests Three in Illegal Mining Crackdown

Authorities dismantled clandestine mining sites and seized equipment during operations in the Indénié-Djuablin region from August 16 to 27.

The Gendarmerie legion in Abengourou, supported by the Special Group for the Fight Against Illegal Mining (GSLOI), conducted operations between August 16 and 27 in the Indénié-Djuablin region. These actions targeted illegal mining activities, resulting in the dismantling of clandestine sites and the destruction of 109 dredges. Three individuals were apprehended during these interventions.

The operations aimed to curb illegal gold mining, a practice that often leads to environmental degradation and can disrupt local economies. The seized equipment, primarily dredges, are tools commonly used in alluvial gold extraction, often in riverbeds or areas with gold deposits. The scale of the destruction indicates a significant effort to disrupt these illicit operations.

This crackdown is part of a broader offensive against illegal mining in the region. Similar operations have been reported, such as one in the Worodougou region on August 29, where security forces dismantled two clandestine mining bases in Kouégo. The consistent enforcement aims to restore legality and protect natural resources.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the disruption of illegal mining operations, which can impact the supply chain and potentially lead to increased regulatory scrutiny in the mining sector.

Heavy Rains Flood Ivory Coast Homes, Destroy Property

More than one hundred households in Bagohouo are displaced after rivers overflowed, submerging homes and causing structures to collapse.

Heavy rains on the night of August 29, 2026, caused rivers Bah and Sonhon to overflow in the sub-prefecture of Bagohouo, near Duékoué. The flooding inundated residential areas, including Nidrou and the Havre neighborhood of Bagohouo. Preliminary reports indicate over one hundred households have been affected, with more than twenty houses collapsing under the force of the water. The situation was exacerbated in the Havre neighborhood where about ten homes gave way.

Marius Kouamé, the sub-prefect of Bagohouo, alerted civil firefighters from Guémon upon learning of the disaster. Responders were deployed to secure families in exposed areas and conducted awareness campaigns. Firefighters urged residents to exercise extreme caution due to the rising water levels, warning of drowning, building collapse, and electrocution risks.

Residents in at-risk zones were advised to evacuate immediately if water levels rose rapidly and to seek safe locations. They were also instructed to follow directives from administrative authorities and emergency services. The flooding occurred approximately 28 kilometers from Duékoué.

Why it mattersOver one hundred households in Bagohouo are displaced and have lost homes due to severe flooding caused by heavy rains.

National Committee Mobilizes 500 Cocoa Farmers Against Child Labor

Over 500 cocoa producers, children, and community leaders gathered in Ananguié to commit to ending child labor in cocoa farming.

More than 500 cocoa producers, children, customary chiefs, and religious leaders mobilized on Friday, August 28, 2026, in Ananguié, Adzopé sub-prefecture. They united under a shared commitment to end child labor in cocoa cultivation. This awareness campaign, initiated by the National Committee for Monitoring Actions to Combat Trafficking, Exploitation, and Child Labor (CNS), aims to address issues during school holidays.

The CNS, chaired by First Lady Dominique Ouattara, organized the event as part of ongoing efforts to combat child labor in the cocoa sector. The mobilization in Ananguié underscores the committee's strategy to engage local communities directly in awareness and prevention activities. This initiative seeks to foster a collective responsibility among all stakeholders involved in cocoa production.

The campaign's focus on community involvement highlights a shift towards grassroots solutions in tackling complex social issues within the agricultural sector. By bringing together producers, children, and local leaders, the CNS aims to create a sustainable framework for eliminating child labor and ensuring the well-being of children in cocoa-growing regions.

Why it mattersCocoa producers are directly involved in a campaign to end child labor, which could affect their labor practices and community relations.

Ivory Coast Tobacco Packaging Standards Lag

Civil society groups report only four cigarette brands fully comply with new plain packaging rules.

Ivory Coast's new plain packaging rules for tobacco products are showing initial effects, with standardized cigarette packs appearing on the market since August 3, 2026. The measure aims to reduce the appeal of cigarettes and improve public health by removing attractive colors, logos, and other designs from packaging. The Committee/Club UNESCO for the Fight Against Drugs and Other Pandemics (Clucod) has observed the first compliant packs, viewing this as a significant step in the national anti-smoking policy and Côte d'Ivoire's commitment to WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control.

However, Clucod warns that vigilance is still needed, as only four cigarette brands fully adhere to the new regulations. The organization noted that non-compliant products and even new brands not meeting the plain packaging requirements continue to be sold in various retail locations. Clucod is urging public authorities to intensify controls across the entire distribution chain, from importers to wholesalers and retailers, to ensure full compliance.

To address the ongoing issues, Clucod recommends implementing sanctions against violators, including confiscations, fines, and potentially license revocations. The group also calls for the prompt removal of non-compliant packaging from the market. While the adoption of plain packaging is seen as a crucial advancement, its ultimate effectiveness hinges on the rigorous enforcement of these regulations on the ground.

Why it mattersTobacco companies and distributors are affected by the partial implementation of new packaging regulations, with some facing potential sanctions for non-compliance.
