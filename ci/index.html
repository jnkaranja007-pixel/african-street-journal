Deputy Explains National Assembly Functions to Guitry Residents

Kouassi Koffi Kra Paulin, a deputy from Sorobango and Tagadi, detailed the National Assembly's roles and operations to the people of Guitry during a parliamentary outreach session.

Deputy Kouassi Koffi Kra Paulin explained the National Assembly's four main missions: representing citizens, voting on laws, overseeing government actions, and evaluating public policies. This session, held at the Lorka Dadié cultural center in Guitry on Saturday, August twenty-second, two thousand twenty-six, was part of a parliamentary restitution initiative championed by the institution's president, Patrick Achi.

The deputy also outlined the process for adopting laws, from committee review to parliamentary approval and promulgation, including the Senate's role when required. He emphasized the deputies' function in examining and amending government-initiated bills. The event was organized by Deputy Yao Patricia Sylvie, the first vice-president of the National Assembly and deputy for Guitry and Dairo-Didizo.

Looking ahead, Kra Paulin discussed planned investments in the Lôh-Djiboua region, focusing on renewable energy, biomass, road infrastructure, and agriculture. He announced the establishment of an agropole in Lôh-Djiboua to promote agricultural mechanization, local product processing, and food sovereignty. He also urged residents to secure land rights to prevent conflicts and promoted the social and solidarity economy for income-generating activities.

Why it mattersResidents of Guitry and the Lôh-Djiboua region are directly affected by the deputy's explanations of legislative processes and the announced investments in their area.

Abobo Church Council President Pledges Christian Unity

Bishop Lougah Mabé, the new president of the Abobo High Church Council, committed to strengthening Christian unity and cohesion during his investiture ceremony on Saturday.

Bishop Lougah Mabé assumed leadership of the Abobo High Church Council, promising to foster unity among Christians in the commune. His investiture took place at ADO Square, where he also thanked municipal authorities, including Abobo Mayor Kandia Camara.

Mayor Camara urged the Christian community to utilize state-provided opportunities for employment, entrepreneurship, and project financing. She encouraged religious leaders to share this information with their congregants to promote participation in empowerment and integration programs.

The new president stated that this drive for unity would enable member churches of the High Council to contribute more effectively to Abobo's social and community development. The ceremony was attended by administrative and customary authorities, alongside numerous faithful from various member churches.

Why it mattersChristians in Abobo are affected as their religious leaders commit to unity and encourage participation in government programs for employment and financing.

PDCI Vice-President Urges Restraint Amidst Internal Party Tensions

Michel Gueu, a vice-president of the PDCI-RDA, has called for dialogue and respect for party institutions as internal disputes escalate.

The Parti démocratique de Côte d'Ivoire (PDCI-RDA) is experiencing internal turmoil, prompting Michel Gueu, a vice-president, to urge party members to exercise restraint. Gueu specifically called for dialogue and respect for the party's established bodies, according to Linfodrome on August 24, 2026.

This appeal comes as the party faces significant internal challenges, including a court decision expected on October 22 regarding the potential removal of the PDCI president. Jeune Afrique reported on August 25, 2026, that numerous cyberactivists are actively defending Tidjane Thiam, a presidential candidate for the PDCI in 2025, across social media platforms like TikTok, Facebook, and X.

Gueu also advised party executives to avoid escalating rhetoric and to prioritize mediation as a means to resolve the ongoing internal disagreements. The party's reliance on cyberactivists to defend its candidate highlights a shift in political engagement strategies within the PDCI.

Why it mattersThe internal tensions and calls for restraint affect party members and candidates by shaping the political landscape and potentially influencing future leadership decisions within the PDCI-RDA.

SODECI schedules water service interruption in Abobo

Four neighborhoods in Abobo will experience a temporary potable water service interruption on Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, 2026, starting at eight o'clock in the morning.

The Société de distribution d'eau de Côte d'Ivoire (SODECI) announced a planned water outage affecting four neighborhoods in the commune of Abobo. This interruption is scheduled for Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, 2026, beginning at eight o'clock in the morning.

The temporary service cut is part of scheduled maintenance work by SODECI. The company aims to optimize its distribution network through these efforts. Dokui A is among the sectors that will be affected by the outage.

This maintenance is intended to improve the overall efficiency of the water distribution system in the long term. Residents in the affected areas should prepare for the temporary lack of potable water during the specified period.

Why it mattersResidents in the four affected Abobo neighborhoods will be without potable water for a period due to scheduled maintenance.

Diogo Bamba assumes leadership of Dozo traditional hunters in Ivory Coast

Diogo Bamba was inaugurated as the national chief of the Dozo traditional hunters in Tokala, succeeding Fadjouma Bamba and committing to unity and collaboration with defense.

Diogo Bamba was installed as the national chief of the Dozo traditional hunters of Ivory Coast on Thursday, August 20, 2026, in Tokala, within the sub-prefecture of Mahadianan-Sokourani. The ceremony, sponsored by the chief of the Vandougou canton and observed by military authorities, saw Bamba pledge to unify the hunters and harmonize their actions with the defense and security forces (FDS). He also proposed a national identification card for members to improve organization and state recognition.

Bamba emphasized that the Dozos' role in security is a civilian support to the FDS, not a replacement for their sovereign missions, stating that a Dozo hunter is not an FDS agent but collaborates to enhance security across Ivory Coast. He urged members to act exemplarily and respect national institutions, viewing security as a collective responsibility.

Noumoutié Koné, president and national spokesperson for the National Council of the Dozo Binkadi Vandougou Traditional Hunters Brotherhood of Ivory Coast (CNCCTBV-CI), criticized the proliferation of traditional hunter associations, which he believes causes disorder. Koné called on Dozos to avoid illegal activities like selling weapons or drugs and to promote social cohesion, inviting all associations to join the national council.

Why it mattersThis event affects Ivorian citizens by clarifying the role of traditional hunters in national security and addressing concerns about their organization and potential for illegal activities.
