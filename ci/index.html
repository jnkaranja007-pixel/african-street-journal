Bonoua forms local security committee

A new committee aims to improve safety by involving residents in prevention efforts.

A local security committee was established in Bonoua on November 3, 2026, to boost public participation in preventing and managing safety issues. The initiative, led by the sub-prefect Kra Kouadio Maïzan, aims to adapt security measures to the town's needs and challenges.

This committee, chaired by the sub-prefect, extends the work of the Grand-Bassam departmental security committee. It includes the district police chief as vice-president, the Bonoua police station chief as secretary, and the local gendarmerie commander as vice-secretary, along with representatives from other security forces and community groups.

The committee's mission is to foster dialogue and cooperation between the public, authorities, and security forces to maintain public order and social peace. Initial discussions identified recurring crimes and sensitive areas, with strategies like awareness campaigns proposed, while operational interventions remain with security forces.

Oumar Diakité called up after scoring twice

The striker replaces Ange-Yoan Bonny who withdrew from the Ivory Coast squad for medical reasons.

Ivory Coast striker Oumar Diakité scored two goals for Sturm Graz on Sunday, November 8, 2026, in a 3-4 loss to Red Bull Salzburg. This performance came three days after he was omitted from Hervé Renard's 25-player squad for upcoming matches.

Ange-Yoan Bonny of Inter Milan will miss the third and fourth matches of the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers due to a medical issue. The Ivorian Football Federation announced Diakité's recall on Monday, November 9, to join the Elephants for their double-header against Gambia.

Diakité, previously left out by Renard who questioned the Austrian league's level, has been scoring regularly for Sturm Graz in the Austrian Bundesliga and Europa League. His last national team appearance was at the 2026 World Cup. The Elephants will play Gambia on November 12 in Bouaké and again on November 17 in Bakau.

Politics · 2026-11-10

Blaise Lasm trial delayed again

Ivory Coast court postpones hearing for detained PPA-CI official until November 16

The trial of Blaise Lasm, deputy secretary-general of the PPA-CI party, was postponed again on Monday, November 9, 2026. The Abidjan-Plateau Court of First Instance rescheduled the hearing for November 16, extending the detention of the political official who has been held since September 30, 2025.

The prosecution requested the delay, stating they were not ready to proceed, despite Lasm having been detained for over 13 months. Lasm's defense had argued for his release, citing a procedural delay where his case dossier was sent to the prosecution on September 14 and he was not presented to judges until October 19, exceeding the one-month legal limit for detained defendants.

Lasm, originally charged with terrorism-related offenses, received a partial dismissal on those charges in September. He now faces charges including disturbing public order, conspiracy against state authority, and inciting insurrection, yet his case is still being heard by the anti-terrorism chamber. Another PPA-CI official, Dahi Nestor Douhouré, also detained since September 30, 2025, has his case set for November 16.

Why it mattersBlaise Lasm, a political figure in Ivory Coast, remains in detention as his trial is repeatedly postponed.

Tiken Jah Fakoly fled Ivory Coast after death threats

The reggae star left Abidjan in 2002 following political unrest and criticism of leaders.

Reggae artist Tiken Jah Fakoly, born Doumbia Moussa Fakoly on June 23, 1968, fled Ivory Coast for Mali in 2002 after death threats emerged during political unrest.

Fakoly, who has released 16 albums and won a Victoire de la musique in 2003, used his music to criticize Ivorian leaders and the state.

He settled in Bamako in 2003, continuing his career and releasing albums like Françafrique, which earned him the reggae award in 2003.

Why it mattersTiken Jah Fakoly's music and criticism of leaders led to him fleeing Ivory Coast and living in exile.
