Ouattara, Macron discuss cooperation

The Ivorian and French presidents met for two hours at the Élysée Palace on November 3, 2026.

Ivorian President Alassane Ouattara met with French President Emmanuel Macron on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, for discussions at the Élysée Palace. Several Ivorian newspapers reported on the meeting, with headlines focusing on cooperation between Côte d’Ivoire and France. Fraternité Matin highlighted "Cooperation Côte d’Ivoire-France. Ouattara-Macron: the subjects at the heart of the exchanges," while L’Intelligent d’Abidjan stated "Côte d’Ivoire-France: Ouattara and Macron at the table."

L’Essor reported that Ouattara and Macron "consolidate the Abidjan-Paris partnership," and Le Patriote emphasized the "still very solid" relations between the two countries. L’Expression detailed "Ouattara-Macron meeting at the Élysée: here are the subjects discussed," and Le Matin noted a "2h one-on-one at the Élysée." L’Avenir also covered the exchange under the title "Alassane Ouattara exchanges with Macron."

The press review also noted other news, including US midterm election results where Donald Trump reportedly faced a "stinging setback." Nationally, rumors of a ministerial reshuffle were circulating, with Le Jour Plus reporting that "the noise is growing, growing, growing!" The Ivorian bar association's stance on distinguishing political protest from terrorism was also a topic, with Le Temps stating "the bar refuses to equate political protest with terrorism."

Why it mattersThe meeting between President Ouattara and President Macron directly involves the Ivorian head of state and discusses bilateral cooperation.

Ivory Coast trains rice processors to boost local quality

A four-day workshop in Yamoussoukro aims to improve milled rice quality and competitiveness against imports.

The Agency for the Development of the Rice Sector (ADERIZ), through the REWARD-CI project, organized a four-day capacity-building workshop in Yamoussoukro starting November 4, 2026. The training targets managers of mini rice mills, processing one to two tons per hour, to enhance the quality of local milled rice and its competitiveness against imports.

According to Brou Jérémy Kouadio, technical operations chief for REWARD-CI, the initiative addresses paddy supply shortages, quality issues, and inadequate equipment. Upon completion, participants will better understand their equipment's operation and how to improve rice quality to meet market demands. A diagnostic mission will follow to identify specific needs for factory upgrades and environmental compliance, facilitating bank financing.

The REWARD-CI project, launched in June 2026 and ending April 2028, aims to strengthen food sovereignty in West Africa by providing quality milled rice and increasing private investment. The project is active in seven to eight regions of Ivory Coast and is part of a national strategy to make rice a competitive sector by 2030.

Why it mattersRice processors in Ivory Coast are being trained to improve their product quality and competitiveness, which will help them meet market demands and access bank financing.

Health · 2026-11-05

Abidjan hosts second vascular surgery mission

French and Ivorian medical teams treated four patients for vascular diseases between October 26 and 28.

The Institut Cœur de Grâce (ICG) in Abidjan held its second vascular surgery mission from October 26 to 28, 2026. The mission, part of a collaboration with French specialized hospitals, aimed to combat cardiovascular diseases in Ivory Coast.

Led by French vascular surgeon Dr. Aurélien Besancenot and including ICG specialist Dr. Séka Antoine, the team examined four patients. Three patients with lower limb arterial disease underwent procedures like angioplasty and a right carotid operation.

Dr. Besancenot highlighted the growing public health challenge of vascular diseases due to aging populations and obesity. ICG's scientific director, Professor Iklo Coulibaly, praised the mission for advancing specialized care and improving access to treatment in Ivory Coast.

Why it mattersFour Ivorian patients received specialized vascular surgery to treat serious arterial conditions.

Amad Diallo nominated for African Player of the Year

The Confederation of African Football revealed 10 nominees for the 2026 award, with the winner announced on December 7 in Lagos.

Ivory Coast international Amad Diallo is among 10 nominees for the 2026 African Player of the Year award, announced by the Confederation of African Football (CAF) on Wednesday, November 4. The Manchester United winger is the sole Ivorian representative in this category. The winner will be revealed on Monday, December 7, 2026, in Lagos, Nigeria.

The CAF unveiled the 10 contenders for the 2026 African Player of the Year award, presenting a list that includes some surprises. Amad Diallo, an winger for Manchester United, is the only Ivorian player nominated for this prestigious title.

The award ceremony is scheduled to take place in Lagos, Nigeria. This nomination highlights Diallo's performance and recognition on the continental stage.

Why it mattersAmad Diallo, an Ivorian footballer playing abroad, has been nominated for a major African football award.

Health · 2026-11-05

Yamoussoukro hosts neurology congress

Experts gather from October 28-30, 2026, to improve care for neurological disabilities in French-speaking Africa.

The Saint-Joseph Moscati Catholic Hospital in Yamoussoukro will host the third Ordinary Congress of the Society of Francophone Neurorehabilitation (SAFNeR) from Wednesday, October 28 to Friday, October 30, 2026.

This scientific meeting brings together specialists from multiple medical disciplines to address the major challenge of improving the management of people living with neurological disabilities in French-speaking Africa.

The congress aims to enhance the accessibility of care for individuals affected by neurological conditions across the region.

Why it mattersPeople living with neurological disabilities in French-speaking Africa will benefit from improved care following this congress.
