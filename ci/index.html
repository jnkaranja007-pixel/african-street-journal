Ministry of Higher Education publishes university placement results

New baccalaureate holders can access their university and higher education school placements online starting Thursday, August twenty-seventh, 2026, at 10 AM.

The Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research has made the placement results for 2026 baccalaureate holders available. Students can now check their assignments on the official platform and begin the enrollment process for their assigned institutions.

This online release streamlines the process for new baccalaureate graduates, allowing them to quickly determine their next steps for higher education. The system aims to provide clear guidance for students transitioning from secondary school.

The publication of these results is a key step for thousands of students across the country who are seeking to continue their studies in universities and other higher education establishments for the upcoming academic year.

Why it mattersThousands of baccalaureate holders are directly affected by these results, as they determine their access to higher education and future career paths.

Ivory Coast, Mali define border, adopt delimitation treaty draft

Experts from both nations met in Bamako to agree on the definitive border demarcation and an initial draft of a delimitation treaty, aiming to enhance security and development.

Ivory Coast and Mali have taken a significant step in clarifying their shared border. Experts from both countries convened in Bamako from August twenty-first to twenty-fifth, 2026, to adopt the definitive border demarcation and the preliminary draft of a delimitation treaty, according to Linfodrome.

This agreement marks a decisive move towards territorial clarification, as reported by Ivoire Matin. The mutually agreed-upon border tracing and treaty draft are expected to contribute to improved security in border areas.

The initiative aims to strengthen the development of these regions, benefiting the populations residing along the frontier. This collaboration is intended to foster stability and progress in the shared border zones.

Why it mattersPeople living near the border between Ivory Coast and Mali will experience increased security and potential development opportunities due to the clarified territorial lines.

Ivory Coast Minister Proposes African Coalition Against Illegal Mining

Ivory Coast's Minister of Environment and Ecological Transition, Abou Bamba, presented a plan for a coalition to combat illegal mining and land degradation at COP 17 in Mongolia.

Ivory Coast has identified illegal gold panning as a major environmental, social, and economic problem, bringing it to the forefront at the COP 17 conference on desertification in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. The conference, running from August seventeen to August twenty-eight, focuses on the global issue of desertification and land degradation, which affects forty percent of the Earth's surface.

Minister Bamba introduced a project for an African coalition aimed at addressing illegal mining and land degradation. This initiative was presented to official and international partners on Thursday, August twenty-seventh, during the conference.

Globally, land degradation impacts billions of people and incurs an annual cost of up to nine hundred billion US dollars, highlighting the significant economic and societal consequences that states are urged to address at COP 17.

Why it mattersThe proposed coalition directly affects those involved in mining and land use across Africa, potentially altering economic activities and environmental regulations.

Ivory Coast researchers launch Nectar Project to find plant-based mosquito insecticides

Researchers in Abidjan have initiated the Nectar Project to identify new plant molecules capable of combating insecticide-resistant mosquitoes, marking a new phase in malaria.

Ivory Coast has launched the Nectar Project, a scientific initiative focused on discovering new plant-based molecules to fight mosquitoes resistant to current insecticides. The project was introduced on Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, 2026, during a workshop in Abidjan.

This new effort aims to address the growing challenge of insecticide-resistant mosquitoes, which complicate malaria control efforts across the country. Researchers are now looking to plants as a source for effective solutions.

The Nectar Project represents a new step in the fight against malaria in Ivory Coast, with scientists specifically seeking plant-derived compounds that can combat these resilient mosquito populations, according to Linfodrome and Ivoire Matin.

Why it mattersThe Nectar Project directly affects Ivorians by seeking new ways to combat malaria-carrying mosquitoes, potentially reducing disease incidence and improving public health.

Teachers' Union Seizes Bar on Politically Engaged Lawyer

The Union of Teachers of Preschool and Primary Militants of the Pdci-Rda (Ueppm/Pdci-Rda) has asked the Ivory Coast Bar Association to examine the case of lawyer Gueu Deuyoueu.

The Ueppm/Pdci-Rda announced on Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, 2026, that it has formally requested the Bar Association to review the role of lawyer Gueu Deuyoueu Patrice. The organization is questioning the compatibility of his political activities as a leader of the Rhdp in Danané with his legal work on cases directly involving the Pdci-Rda and its president, Tidjane Thiam.

Gossé Mathias, national president of the Ueppm/Pdci-Rda and a member of the Pdci-Rda Political Bureau, stated that the move is based on "ethics, law, and professional deontology." The union seeks clarification and a reminder of professional conduct, not a condemnation, regarding potential conflicts of interest and independence.

The union cited several cases involving individuals like Mey Jean-Charles and Gba Golo Patrick, who are contesting Tidjane Thiam's leadership. They also mentioned statements attributed to Me Gueu Deuyoueu Patrice on July thirty, 2026, where he allegedly announced new legal actions against Tidjane Thiam and Georges Philippe Ezaley.

Why it mattersThis situation affects political actors and legal professionals by scrutinizing the ethical boundaries of their dual roles, potentially impacting their careers and the outcomes of legal and political disputes.
