Ouattara discusses Francophonie with Macron

Ivory Coast President Alassane Ouattara met with French President Emmanuel Macron in Paris to discuss international cooperation and bilateral relations.

Ivory Coast President Alassane Ouattara met with French President Emmanuel Macron in Paris on November 4, 2026, for discussions that included the Francophonie, the Abidjan metro project, and Macron's upcoming visit to Ivory Coast. The meeting followed Ouattara's participation in the 8th WTO Presidential Conference in Geneva. President Ouattara stated that Ivory Coast has demonstrated that progress is achievable.

The Ivorian press reported a mixed assessment of the nation's situation, highlighting international standing alongside economic challenges and political rivalries. While the country's performance in the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance showed it advancing five times faster than the continent, and significant progress was noted in combating money laundering by the FATF, concerns remain about the social impact of economic growth.

Journals also covered shifts in local commerce, with a move from basic stalls to supermarkets, and new money transfer regulations within the UEMOA zone. These changes, particularly the end of free transfers by the BCEAO, mean consumers now face charges. Questions were raised about why many young Ivorians continue to leave the country despite reported economic growth.

Why it mattersIvorian citizens face new charges on money transfers within the UEMOA zone and question the social benefits of economic growth.

Ivory Coast seeks 265 billion FCFA for climate plan

The nation will defend its development priorities at the COP31 climate conference in Turkey.

Ivory Coast aims to raise 265 billion FCFA through a pilot phase of the African Climate Pact and Ecological Transition. The country will present its National Development Plan (PND 2026-2030) priorities at the 31st Conference of the Parties on Climate Change (COP31). The conference is scheduled to take place from November 9 to 20, 2026, in Antalya, Turkey.

In parallel, the government has maintained the price of first-choice cotton at 310 FCFA per kilogram for the 2026-2027 agricultural campaign. Ivory Coast has also been designated a pilot country for Italy's Mattei Plan, hosting the African headquarters of the Italian development bank, CDP.

The COP31 conference will serve as a platform for Ivory Coast to advocate for its development agenda. The nation's participation in the pilot phase of the African Climate Pact signifies a commitment to ecological transition and climate action.

Why it mattersIvory Coast's participation in the COP31 climate conference and its climate pact aims to secure funding and advance its national development plan.

Cédric Diarra named Ivory Coast National Assembly vice-president

The 45-year-old RHDP deputy for Bako, Bougousso and Dioulatiédougou assumed the role on February 5, 2026.

Cédric Tidiane Diarra, a RHDP deputy for Bako, Bougousso and Dioulatiédougou, became vice-president of the Ivory Coast National Assembly on February 5, 2026. Diarra, who is 45 years old in 2026, also served as a special advisor to Prime Minister Patrick Achi.

Diarra, son of former Prime Minister Seydou Diarra, previously worked in the cabinet of Hamed Bakayoko and later in Patrick Achi's Prime Minister's office. He entered state service at age 35 after a career in communication and private investment, including work with Johnson Investments on renewable energy projects.

He studied at the European Communication School in Brussels, the New York Institute of Technology, and Harvard Business School. Diarra was elected as a deputy in the December 2025 legislative elections for the 121st constituency.

Why it mattersCédric Tidiane Diarra, son of a former Prime Minister, has been appointed vice-president of the National Assembly, a significant political role.

Ouattara meets Macron in France on November 3

Ivory Coast's president will discuss the Abidjan metro project and the OIF with his French counterpart.

Ivory Coast President Alassane Ouattara will meet French President Emmanuel Macron in France on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. The leaders are expected to discuss the Abidjan metro project. They will also talk about the renewal of the secretary-general position at the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF).

The meeting is scheduled to take place in France. Preparations for the upcoming French presidential election are also on the agenda for the strategic discussion.

The discussions will cover key bilateral and international issues, including the metro project and the OIF secretariat general.

Why it mattersThe meeting will address the Abidjan metro project and the renewal of the OIF secretary-general position.
