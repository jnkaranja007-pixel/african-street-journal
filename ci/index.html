Ivory Coast loses $88.6 billion to illicit financial flows

This sum, equivalent to 3.7% of Africa's GDP, could fund numerous schools and hospitals.

Africa loses $88.6 billion annually to illicit financial flows, according to a 2020 UNCTAD report. This amount, which includes funds from commercial misinvoicing, tax evasion, and corruption proceeds, represents 3.7% of the continent's GDP. In Ivory Coast, this sum could finance the construction of between eight and fifteen school buildings, each costing between 20 and 37 million dollars.

These illicit outflows, estimated at $836 billion between 2000 and 2015, exceed the continent's external debt of $770 billion in 2018. The UNCTAD report concludes that Africa is a net creditor to the rest of the world, financing other economies. A significant portion of these flows, an estimated $40 billion in 2015 alone, is linked to raw material exports, often facilitated by foreign companies with local facilitators involved in negotiating contracts and customs controls.

The report highlights that while external cooperation is not inherently negative, the pursuit of international recognition can overshadow the defense of national interests. This can lead to situations where essential public services, such as hospital equipment, are neglected while officials seek favor abroad. The debate on corruption and development in Africa should consider the impact of such financial losses on families and communities.

Why it mattersIllicit financial flows of $88.6 billion annually mean hundreds of students in Ivory Coast study in overcrowded classrooms or remain at home, as school construction is hampered.

Ahmadiyya mission in Bondoukou urges justice as pillar of peace

At a symposium on Sunday, 15 November 2026, the local Islamic mission argued that fair treatment for all is essential to lasting peace in Ivory Coast.

The Ahmadiyya Islamic Mission in Bondoukou held a symposium on Sunday, 15 November, to mark National Peace Day under the theme "Justice without borders, a pillar of lasting peace". Administrative, traditional and religious authorities, community leaders and educators gathered at the mission's headquarters.

Its regional missionary, Shahid Ahmed, said justice must not be swayed by wealth, poverty, religious affiliation or personal ties. Citing the message of the worldwide head of the Ahmadiyya community, Mirza Masroor Ahmed, he said Islam calls for justice even when it means testifying against one's own people, and urged that the same standards apply to allies and opponents alike.

The principal of the Lycée moderne de Bondoukou, Akpa Séraphin, said fights and violence involving some young people in and around his school disrupt lessons and force police to commit large numbers of officers to keep order there. He called on families to start peace education at home.

Why it mattersPolice time spent on school disturbances in Bondoukou is time not spent on other security needs in the town, according to the principal.

Abidjan hosts African oil producers' executive council meeting

Ivory Coast is hosting the 29th ordinary meeting of the Executive Council of the African Petroleum Producers' Organization (APPO) until Monday, 16 November.

Ivory Coast is hosting the 29th ordinary meeting of the Executive Council of the African Petroleum Producers' Organization (APPO) from 15 to 16 November 2026. The opening ceremony took place on Sunday, 15 November, at the Azalaï hotel in Marcory, Abidjan.

The meeting brings together representatives of APPO member countries. It takes place as the organisation seeks to strengthen energy cooperation across the continent and to make oil resources a lever for economic and social development.

The APPO, which groups African countries that produce oil, is meeting in Abidjan over two days to discuss the future of the continent's oil industry.

Why it mattersOil-producing member states now have a two-day forum in Abidjan to set their shared approach to cooperation.

Ivory Coast can qualify for CAN 2027 this week

Cameroon, Senegal, Morocco and DR Congo also have a chance to book their places in the continental tournament during the fourth matchday of qualifying.

The fourth round of qualifiers for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (CAN) runs over Monday, 16 November, and Tuesday, 17 November. Ivory Coast is among several major African nations that can secure a place in the finals if results go their way.

Cameroon, Senegal, Morocco and the Democratic Republic of Congo are also in a position to qualify during the same round. RFI Afrique reports that each team's route to qualification depends on specific results in its matches.

The report sets out the conditions under which each of these nations can qualify, so supporters can follow what each side needs.

Why it mattersSupporters of the national team can follow the matchday results that decide whether it books a place in the finals.

Politics · 2026-11-16

Achi seeks parliamentary models abroad

Ivory Coast's National Assembly president Patrick Achi visited the UK, Paris, and met Canadian senators to modernize legislative processes.

Patrick Achi, president of Ivory Coast's National Assembly, led a delegation to the UK Parliament from November 4-7, meeting with Speaker Sir Lindsay Hoyle. The group, including Vice-President Cédric Diarra and RHDP group president Adama Koné, observed parliamentary sessions and visited universities in London, Oxford, and Cambridge. Discussions focused on digitizing procedures, using artificial intelligence in legislative work, training elected officials, and overseeing government actions, reflecting Achi's goal for a more modern assembly since his election on January 17, 2026.

From November 10-11, Achi represented President Alassane Ouattara at the 9th Paris Peace Forum, addressing the theme "From Sovereignty to Cooperation" amid global turbulence. Upon returning to Abidjan on November 13, he received the Canada-Africa Parliamentary Association, led by Senator Amina Gerba. Achi presented Ivory Coast's 2026-2030 National Development Plan, highlighting opportunities in agricultural processing, food security, industry, infrastructure, and digital development, while also advocating for improved access to Canadian universities for Ivorian students.

Achi, who previously served as Prime Minister and Minister of Economic Infrastructure, is leveraging parliamentary diplomacy to advance his agenda. His delegations consistently include Cédric Diarra and Adama Koné. Patrick Achi will turn 71 on November 17.
