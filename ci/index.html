Cahié Kunta jailed for three months

The social media personality was convicted of disturbing public order and spreading false news.

Abidjan-Plateau's court of first instance sentenced Cahié Kakou Guy Joël, known online as Cahié Kunta, to three months in prison on Thursday, November 5, 2026. He was convicted of disturbing public order, using insults, and disseminating false news via an information system.

The court found Cahié Kunta guilty of actions that disrupted public order and spread misinformation. This judicial outcome follows charges related to his online activities.

In a separate case, Adjamé municipal councilor Fousséni Konaté received a one-month prison sentence following a complaint by Mayor Farikou Soumahoro.

Business · 2026-11-07

PETROCI ships first crude oil cargo as operator

The national oil company's subsidiary loaded its first shipment from the Espoir field on October 31, 2026.

The Ivorian national oil company, PETROCI, shipped its first crude oil cargo on October 31, 2026, three months after taking over operations of the Espoir field. This marks PETROCI CI-26, a subsidiary fully owned by PETROCI Holding, becoming the direct operator of the offshore field near Jacqueville. This operation signifies the state-owned company's transition from a partner to a direct operator in oil asset exploitation.

This first lifting, or crude oil loading for transport, confirms the operational implementation of PETROCI's new role. Previously, PETROCI Holding primarily held stakes in projects managed by international groups, such as the Baleine field operated by Eni. Now, PETROCI directly manages the Espoir field's production, offshore maintenance, and crude oil loading, a responsibility previously held by Canadian Natural Resources (CNR).

The Espoir field, discovered in 1980 and redeveloped for production in February 2002, is now under the direct management of PETROCI CI-26, led by Mabintou Tatiana Cissé. The next steps for PETROCI involve sustaining production, controlling costs, and building national capacity in oil exploitation. The volume of this initial cargo, the buyer, and its destination were not publicly disclosed.

Why it mattersPETROCI's direct operation of the Espoir oil field means the national company is now responsible for production and revenue, rather than just holding shares.

Sport · 2026-11-07

Hervé Renard calls up four new players for Ivory Coast

Defender Oumar Solet and midfielder Abdoulaye Kanté are among the new faces for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers.

Ivory Coast coach Hervé Renard has named four new players for the upcoming 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers against Gambia. Among the newcomers are 21-year-old defensive midfielder Abdoulaye Raslan Kanté of Saudi club Al-Ettifaq and 26-year-old Udinese centre-back Oumar Solet. The squad also includes debutants Abdoul Ouattara, a full-back with Ipswich Town, and midfielder Yaya Kader Fofana, who plays for Reims.

Solet, who stands 1.92m tall, brings power and speed to the defence and has established himself as a starter for Udinese in Serie A since joining in October 2024. Born in 2000, he previously played for Stade Lavallois and Olympique Lyonnais before developing his career in Austria with RB Salzburg, winning league titles and playing in the Champions League. He has chosen to represent the Elephants despite having played for France's youth teams and having a Central African father.

The Elephants have won their first two matches in Group C, defeating Ghana and Somalia 2-0. Gambia also has six points. Ivory Coast will host Gambia on November 12 in Bouaké, with the return match on November 17 in Bakau. Amad Diallo has been declared fit to play, while Hassane Kamara and Martial Godo return to the squad. Ghislain Konan, Odilon Kossounou, and Guéla Doué are absent, as are Sébastien Haller, Simon Adingra, Seko Fofana, and Wilfried Singo.

Why it mattersFootball fans in Ivory Coast will be interested in the new talent selected for the national team's upcoming matches.

Tech · 2026-11-07

Ivory Coast opens regional cybersecurity centre

The new facility, managed by US firm Cybastion, will train personnel and operate as a command centre.

Ivory Coast has inaugurated a new cybersecurity centre for West, Central, and East Africa, one week after hosting a national cyberattack exercise. The centre aims to bolster the nation's cybersecurity ambitions.

Managed by the American technology company Cybastion, the facility will serve as a training hub and an operations centre. This move confirms Ivory Coast's growing role in regional digital security.

The centre's establishment follows a national cyberattack drill. Cybastion's involvement highlights international partnerships in developing African cybersecurity capabilities.

Why it mattersIvory Coast is establishing itself as a regional hub for cybersecurity training and operations.
