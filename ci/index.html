Ivorian Deputy Explains New Laws to Constituents

N’Gata Brié Joseph detailed the benefits of recent parliamentary acts, including the 2026-2030 development plan, to residents of Sikensi.

Sikensi, Ivory Coast – N’Gata Brié Joseph, the deputy for the Sikensi-Gomon constituency, held a public meeting on August 28, 2026, to explain the anticipated advantages of approximately ten laws passed by the National Assembly. The session, titled "Tribune du député," took place at the Sikensi town hall and was attended by local leaders and village chiefs.

Key legislation discussed included the National Development Plan 2026-2030, which aims to boost infrastructure, agriculture, and industrial growth, alongside laws on national detention monitoring, construction insurance, Ivorian nationality acquisition through marriage, and the social and solidarity economy. The deputy emphasized that the development plan seeks to reduce poverty and inequality through investments in roads, energy, water, and digital services.

The meeting also served to educate attendees on the functioning of the National Assembly and the role of parliamentarians. Local officials expressed a desire for these information sessions to continue, highlighting their importance in informing the public about legislative outcomes. Discussions also touched upon national television broadcasts of parliamentary sessions and addressing the rising cost of living.

Why it mattersResidents of Sikensi will benefit from infrastructure improvements, agricultural development, and social programs outlined in the new National Development Plan.

Sant'Egidio Community Educates Youth on Corruption's Dangers

A meeting in Abidjan highlighted the story of Floribert Bwana Chui, a martyr against corrupt practices.

The Sant'Egidio Community in Ivory Coast held a meeting on August 29, 2026, at the Center for Peace Research and Action (CERAP) in Abidjan-Cocody. The event focused on Blessed Floribert Bwana Chui, aiming to educate young people about the perils of corruption and the value of integrity. Attendees included members of the Sant'Egidio Community, religious leaders, and individuals from various backgrounds.

The gathering introduced the life and testimony of Floribert Bwana Chui, a young Congolese man who became a martyr for refusing to yield to corruption. Bwana Chui, an official at the Congolese Control Office (OCC), died in 2007 after refusing to allow spoiled food into the Democratic Republic of Congo, despite facing pressure and bribery attempts. His story was central to a panel discussion on integrity, civic responsibility, and youth engagement against corruption.

Alphonse Krecoum, the head of Sant'Egidio in Ivory Coast, stated that Bwana Chui's fate demonstrates how an individual's choice can have a lasting historical impact. He urged youth to value integrity over material possessions, warning that the relentless pursuit of wealth can normalize societal-weakening practices. Father Franck Zohouri, another speaker, emphasized the role of education and consistent dialogue in fostering behavioral change, advocating for more awareness events.

Why it mattersYoung people are affected by learning about the consequences of corruption and the importance of integrity through the example of Floribert Bwana Chui.

NGO donates sanitation equipment to Bondoukou village

The Group of Experts for Sustainable Development, Environment and International Solidarity (GEDES) provided 10 brooms, 10 wheelbarrows, and other tools to the community.

The village of Bondoukou, in the Adiaké sub-prefecture, received a donation of sanitation equipment on Saturday, August 29, 2026, from the NGO GEDES. The handover ceremony took place in the village square, attended by local administrative, municipal, and customary leaders. The donation included items such as 10 brooms, 10 wheelbarrows, 10 bins, 10 shovels, five machetes, and 10 garbage bags, aimed at bolstering community environmental protection efforts.

The president of GEDES stated that the initiative seeks to strengthen community actions for environmental preservation, emphasizing that environmental protection begins at the local level. He urged residents to adopt practices that promote cleanliness and highlighted the technical support from the National Waste Management Agency. Representatives from the Adiaké mayor's office and the regional directorate of the National Waste Management Agency also spoke, encouraging proper use of the equipment and stressing collective responsibility for sanitation.

The equipment was formally presented by the sub-prefect's representative to the village chief, who then passed it to the head of the local sanitation committee. The event concluded with a cleanup operation at the village's health center. Bondoukou, a village with an estimated population of two thousand inhabitants across three main neighborhoods and several hamlets, received these resources to improve its living conditions and environmental health.

Why it mattersThe populations of Bondoukou village are affected by the donation of sanitation equipment which aims to improve environmental protection and cleanliness in their community.

Cocoa Sector Actors Hold Sports Event Ahead of Price Announcement

The 10th National Cocoa and Chocolate Days event included sports activities as the sector awaits the new campaign's kilogram price.

The 10th edition of the National Cocoa and Chocolate Days (JNCC) featured a significant sports segment on Saturday, August 29, 2026, at the National Institute of Youth and Sports. Participants from the cocoa sector engaged in a cross-country run and a maracana football tournament.

This gathering occurred just days before the official opening of the event and the announcement of the cocoa kilogram price for the upcoming season. The sports activities provided a platform for industry stakeholders to connect ahead of key commercial decisions.

Additionally, the Council of Coffee and Cocoa announced the list of 68 packaging units for the 2026-2027 marketing campaign. This development clarifies the sector's conditioning framework as the new commercialization period approaches.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by the upcoming announcement of the cocoa kilogram price for the new campaign, which will influence market activity.

Basketball Federation cites administrative and career hurdles for Mo Bamba's absence

FIBB president Moussa Diarra explained that FIBA qualification issues and the player's NBA contract priorities prevented Mo Bamba from joining the national team.

The Ivory Coast national basketball team, the Elephants, will not feature NBA player Mo Bamba due to administrative and career-related obstacles, according to FIBB president Moussa Diarra. Bamba, a Utah Jazz center, had obtained an Ivorian passport but his prior participation with the United States in the U19 World Cup complicated his eligibility with FIBA, requiring a 45-day qualification period. The international federation did not validate the submitted documents, including his father's passport showing him as a child.

Diarra stated that the FIBB faced a choice between Bamba and Matt Costello for the Afrobasket tournament, ultimately selecting Costello due to the quota for naturalized players and the administrative delays. Beyond these regulatory issues, Bamba also prioritized his professional career, informing the federation that securing an NBA contract and preparing for the season were his immediate concerns, leading him to decline the call-up for the summer international window.

Despite Bamba's absence, the Elephants are continuing their 2027 World Cup qualifying campaign under coach Miguel Hoyo. The team secured an 83-77 victory against Mali in Dakar, with American point guard Kobi Simmons, selected as a 'star' player, contributing 17 points and 3 assists. The Ivorian squad is moving forward with its current roster as they pursue qualification, awaiting any potential resolution on Bamba's eligibility.

Why it mattersBasketball players and administrators are affected by FIBA's player qualification rules and the demands of professional leagues like the NBA.
