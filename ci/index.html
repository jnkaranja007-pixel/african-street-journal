Ivory Coast sends minister to Russia-Africa summit

Minister Nialé Kaba led Ivory Coast's delegation to the October 28-29, 2026, Moscow summit.

Ivory Coast's Minister of State for Foreign Affairs, Nialé Kaba, represented the nation at the third Russia-Africa summit in Moscow on October 28 and 29, 2026. President Alassane Ouattara had chosen to speak at the WTO forum in Geneva on October 29, the same day Kaba arrived in Moscow on October 27. Russia's President Vladimir Putin hosted 48 African delegations at the summit.

Kaba was welcomed at Domodedovo Airport by Russian foreign ministry officials and the Russian ambassador to Abidjan, Alexey Saltykov. She also met with members of the Ivorian community in Moscow at her hotel. Bilateral exchanges between Ivory Coast and Russia reached 273.4 billion CFA francs in 2025, with Russia seeking to increase sales of grain and fertilizers to Africa.

Putin addressed "unprecedented Western pressure" on African nations and pledged to expand Russian agricultural exports, stating 13 million tonnes had been sent to Africa in 2026. The summit also planned to sign 33 documents, including economic cooperation agreements, and discussed producing Russian medicines and vaccines locally. Amnesty International raised concerns about the forced recruitment of Africans for combat in Ukraine.

Why it mattersIvory Coast maintained diplomatic ties with Russia while its president focused on trade discussions in Geneva.

Ivory Coast trains 26 experts on chemical weapons protection

Specialists from 14 countries are learning detection, decontamination, and victim care at Abidjan's counter-terrorism academy.

26 specialists from 14 countries are undergoing advanced training in chemical weapons protection in Ivory Coast. The course, held at the International Academy for Counter-Terrorism (AILCT) in Abidjan, began on October 27, 2026, and concludes on November 3 with an integrated exercise. Participants are learning detection, decontamination, emergency management, and victim care over eight days.

The training, organized with the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW), focuses on assistance and protection following chemical attacks or accidents. Loic Luyeye, an OPCW representative, noted that while Africa is free of chemical weapons, toxic substances used in agriculture, industry, and mining pose constant risks. The program aims to equip first responders, as a poorly prepared firefighter or doctor can become a victim.

The session is a joint effort by the OPCW, Ivory Coast's Permanent Secretariat of the Commission for the Prohibition of Weapons of Mass Destruction (SPCIADM-CI), and AILCT. General Kouamé Joseph Allah, AILCT's Director General, urged participants to show unwavering moral and intellectual commitment. This training reinforces Ivory Coast's role as a regional security platform, with delegations returning home with immediately useful skills.

Why it matters26 experts from 14 countries are being trained in chemical weapons protection in Ivory Coast, enhancing regional security capabilities.

Health · 2026-10-29

Abidjan hospital performs three arterial surgeries

Institut Cœur de Grâce specialists used a hybrid operating room to treat patients with heart conditions.

The Institut Cœur de Grâce (ICG) in Abidjan successfully performed three surgeries between October 26 and 28, 2026, to address arterial blockages. These procedures included replacing an aortic valve, performing a coronary bypass, and treating an ascending aorta coarctation. The hospital utilized its latest generation hybrid operating room for these interventions.

French specialists collaborated with Ivorian doctors at the facility during this specialized medical mission. ICG officials stated the mission advances cardiovascular surgery development and local medical capabilities. They also emphasized the importance of early detection and regular consultations for heart conditions.

The institute aims to improve access to cardiovascular care in Ivory Coast and the wider sub-region. This development is a major issue for the health system, as heart diseases require prompt diagnosis and appropriate management.

Why it mattersThree patients in Abidjan received specialized cardiovascular surgery to treat arterial obstructions.

Ivory Coast sets 2026-2027 technical education calendar

The Ministry of National Education, Literacy and Technical Education released the schedule for the upcoming academic year.

The Ministry of National Education, Literacy and Technical Education in Ivory Coast announced the 2026-2027 technical education training calendar on October 29, 2026. The schedule details the dates for the two semesters, internship periods, school holidays, and the start date for the next academic year.

Separately, inscriptions for the 2027 school examinations opened on October 12 and will close on December 18, 2026. Students and independent candidates must complete their registration according to the timeline set by the Directorate of Examinations and Competitions (DECO).

The DECO's timeline ensures that all candidates are aware of the examination registration deadlines for the 2027 session.

Why it mattersStudents in Ivory Coast need to be aware of the registration deadlines for the 2027 school examinations and the upcoming 2026-2027 technical education calendar.

Ivory Coast tests cyber defenses with 800 participants

The nation's first national cyber crisis simulation, Cyber.Ex 2026, involved public and private sectors to assess response capabilities.

Ivory Coast launched its first national cyber crisis simulation, Cyber.Ex 2026, in Abidjan on Wednesday, October 28, 2026. The exercise brought together 800 participants from government, private companies, cybersecurity experts, and students to test the nation's ability to withstand cyberattacks. Organized by the National Agency for the Security of Information Systems (ANSSI-CI), the event focused on national digital resilience and collective response.

The simulation, themed 'National digital resilience: anticipate, react, bounce back together to ensure security,' specifically involved the energy, information and communication technology (ICT), and finance sectors. Its aim was to evaluate the effectiveness of prevention measures and digital incident management, including attack detection, decision-making speed, response team coordination, and business recovery after intrusions. General Guelpetchin Ouattara, director general of ANSSI-CI, stated that collective preparation is vital for unpredictable threats.

Cyber.Ex 2026, concluding on Thursday, October 29, included executive, technical, and talent development components. The 'Cyber Talents' program featured a master class on cybersecurity law and a 'Capture The Flag' competition for young participants. A 'Cyber Village' also showcased digital protection solutions, highlighting local innovations. This exercise supports the National Cybersecurity Strategy implemented by ANSSI-CI, established in 2024.

Why it mattersThe simulation tests the nation's ability to protect its digital infrastructure, affecting energy, finance, and ICT sectors.
