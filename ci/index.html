Minister Announces Textile Factory Resumption Early 2027

The Industrielle textile de Côte d’Ivoire (ITCI) in Dimbokro will restart operations in early 2027 with modern equipment and aims to supply local and regional markets, creating.

The Industrielle textile de Côte d’Ivoire (ITCI) factory is set to resume operations in early 2027, according to an announcement by Minister of Commerce, Industry, and Crafts, Kalil Konaté. The minister stated that the facility will be equipped with modern machinery and production lines to cater to both standard and luxury markets. This relaunch is expected to contribute to supplying the Ivorian market as well as neighboring countries.

The revival of ITCI, located on a 43-hectare industrial site in Dimbokro, is being undertaken by Société des textiles de Côte d’Ivoire (Sotexi), a group of private Ivorian shareholders. Sotexi plans to invest approximately twelve million euros over five years for modernization and reactivation. The project is anticipated to create around one thousand jobs in its initial phase, boosting the local economy and providing new employment opportunities.

ITCI, originally established in 1973, ceased operations following the 2002 politico-military crisis and significant financial difficulties, including debts exceeding thirteen million euros. Its relaunch occurs within a national textile sector that has faced challenges from political instability, cotton sector issues, smuggling, and counterfeit imports from Asia. Experts believe the factory's revival could help restart local production of "made in Côte d’Ivoire" fabrics and fibers.

Why it mattersFarmers may benefit from increased demand for cotton if the textile factory's revival leads to greater local processing of raw materials.

Ivory Coast, Mali Experts Finalize Border Treaty Draft

Officials from Ivory Coast and Mali have agreed on the definitive border demarcation and a draft treaty, aiming to enhance security and development in shared frontier regions.

Experts from Ivory Coast and Mali have reached a significant agreement on their shared border, finalizing its definitive route and drafting a delimitation treaty. The joint meeting, held in Bamako from August 21 to 25, 2026, concluded with mutual consent on the territorial demarcation.

This accord marks a crucial step in clarifying the territorial boundaries between the two West African nations. The finalized border alignment and the preliminary treaty are expected to foster improved security within the border areas.

Beyond security enhancements, the agreement is anticipated to stimulate development in the frontier zones. This collaborative effort aims to bring tangible benefits to the populations residing in these regions, strengthening regional integration.

Why it mattersThe finalized border between Ivory Coast and Mali will affect populations by improving security and fostering development in shared frontier regions.

Ivory Coast Researchers Seek Plant-Based Insecticides for Mosquitoes

The Nectar project aims to identify new plant molecules to combat insecticide-resistant mosquitoes, a critical step in fighting malaria.

Abidjan researchers are turning to plants for solutions to the growing problem of mosquitoes resistant to conventional insecticides. The Nectar project, launched on Wednesday, August 26, 2026, focuses on identifying novel plant-derived molecules that can effectively control mosquito populations. This initiative represents a new phase in the country's efforts to combat malaria and other mosquito-borne diseases.

The project's launch was marked by a workshop where scientists discussed the potential of African flora in developing new pest control agents. The goal is to find natural alternatives that can overcome the resistance that current chemical treatments face, thereby improving public health outcomes. The research will explore various plant species for their insecticidal properties.

This scientific endeavor seeks to provide sustainable and effective methods for mosquito control, particularly in areas where resistance to existing insecticides is high. By harnessing the power of local plant resources, the Nectar project aims to contribute significantly to public health strategies against malaria and other vector-borne illnesses.

Why it mattersThis initiative affects public health by seeking new ways to combat mosquitoes that carry diseases like malaria, which are becoming resistant to current treatments.

Ivory Coast Minister Highlights Illegal Mining as Environmental Crisis

Abidjan is calling for an African coalition against illegal mining and land degradation at the COP 17 summit in Mongolia.

Ivory Coast's Minister of Environment and Ecological Transition, Abou Bamba, has declared illegal gold mining the "environmental calamity of the moment" at the COP 17 summit in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. The minister presented a proposal for an African coalition to combat illegal mining and land degradation, emphasizing the global scale of desertification and land degradation, which affects 40% of the Earth's land surface.

The COP 17 conference, focused on combating desertification, is taking place from August 17 to 28. Minister Bamba addressed official and international partners, highlighting the environmental, social, and economic impacts of illicit mining activities. He stressed the need for collective action to address these pressing issues affecting ecosystems and livelihoods across the continent.

Globally, soil degradation impacts up to 40% of the planet, affecting billions and costing an estimated $900 billion annually. The COP 17 summit calls on nations to strengthen their understanding of ecosystem links to mitigate these consequences, which have profound effects on economies and societies worldwide.

Why it mattersIllegal mining and land degradation directly impact farmers through environmental damage and reduced land productivity.

PSG Pursues Ivorian Defender Guéla Doué for Transfer

Paris Saint-Germain is actively negotiating for Strasbourg's right-back Guéla Doué as the summer transfer window nears its end.

Paris Saint-Germain is reportedly intensifying efforts to sign Ivorian right-back Guéla Doué from Strasbourg before the summer transfer window closes. Transfer specialist Ekrem Konur reported on August 26 that the French club, through its sporting advisor Luis Campos, is pushing for the transfer.

The negotiations come as Guéla Doué is a target for the Parisian club. Strasbourg is reportedly seeking approximately 26.2 billion FCFA for the player. The transfer window is in its final stages, adding urgency to the discussions.

This potential move highlights significant activity in the European football transfer market involving Ivorian talent. The final decision on Doué's transfer will likely be made before the window officially shuts.

Why it mattersInvestors may be affected by the potential transfer fee of approximately 26.2 billion FCFA for Guéla Doué, impacting club finances and player valuations.
