Ivory Coast anti-corruption court investigated for corruption

The FBI alerted Ivorian gendarmes to a corruption network within the economic and financial crimes court in Abidjan.

Ivorian gendarmes are investigating the economic and financial crimes court (PPEF) in Abidjan for corruption, following information provided by the FBI. One person has already been arrested as investigators uncover a network within the specialized court, which handles cases of embezzlement, money laundering, and large-scale fraud. The FBI's involvement stems from American citizens being among the victims of the alleged crimes.

The PPEF is responsible for sensitive cases, including those involving public funds and cybercrime. This investigation comes shortly after the court handed down a 20-year prison sentence and a 100 million CFA franc fine to four young Ivorians in a sextortion case linked to an American woman's suicide in 2022. The convicted individuals are appealing their sentences, and FBI agents were present during that trial.

This development occurs as Ivory Coast was removed from the Financial Action Task Force (GAFI) grey list, a move that acknowledged reforms against money laundering and terrorism financing. The Ministry of Justice has denied accusations of corruption and extortion within the PPEF, stating that the justice system is sound.

Why it mattersThe investigation into the anti-corruption court affects the integrity of the justice system in Ivory Coast.

Ivory Coast removed from FATF grey list

The Financial Action Task Force removed Ivory Coast from its list of countries under enhanced surveillance.

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) has removed Ivory Coast from its list of countries under enhanced surveillance. The international organisation, which fights money laundering and terrorist financing, made the decision at its meeting in Paris.

The FATF announced the decision on Friday, October 30, 2026, following 24 months of scrutiny that began on October 25, 2024. This marks the end of a two-year period of difficulty for the Ivorian economy.

Ivory Coast has now completed two years of surveillance by the FATF. The country has strengthened its measures against financial crime.

Ivory Coast links climate policy and health for COP31

The health ministry organised a national dialogue on climate and health in Abidjan on October 30, 2026.

Ivory Coast intends to strengthen the consideration of health issues in its climate change policy. The Ministry of Health, Public Hygiene and Universal Health Coverage organised a national dialogue on climate and health in Abidjan on Friday, October 30, 2026. This meeting is in preparation for the 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Turkey.

The Directorate of Public Hygiene and Environmental Health led the workshop. The aim was to integrate health concerns into the country's climate change response strategies. The dialogue brought together national stakeholders to discuss the link between climate and public health.

The COP31 conference is scheduled to take place in Antalya, Turkey. Ivory Coast's preparation focuses on ensuring health impacts are a central part of climate change discussions at the international meeting.

Why it mattersIvory Coast is preparing its national position on climate and health for an international conference.

37 French firms seek deals in West Africa

A delegation led by Business France is visiting Nigeria, Senegal, and Ivory Coast to secure partnerships and clients.

37 French companies are touring West Africa from October 28 to November 4, 2026, aiming to secure business contracts. Led by Business France, the mission includes stops in Nigeria and Senegal before concluding in Ivory Coast with targeted business meetings. The agency described the tour as visiting "three of the continent's most dynamic economies," with Lagos, Dakar, and Abidjan chosen for their significant share of West African trade.

In Ivory Coast, the final stop, French companies will engage in a focused program of individual meetings to accelerate their establishment, partnership, or commercial development projects within the country and the wider sub-region. The targeted sectors include industrial solutions, energy, digital technology, health, and agribusiness. These companies aim to offer technologies for modernizing infrastructure, telecommunications, and energy networks, boosting local production, and securing sites.

This mission occurs as competition intensifies in West African markets, with China, Turkey, the UAE, and India gaining ground. Business France aims to "strengthen economic relations between France and West Africa." The results of the mission, including signed contracts or initial contacts, will be known after the Ivory Coast leg concludes on November 4.

Why it mattersFrench companies are seeking to establish partnerships and secure contracts in Ivory Coast's dynamic economy.

Ivory Coast potato imports climb 45% to $12.5 million

Egypt's potato exports to Ivory Coast rose to $1.68 million in 2025, up from $14,000 in 2020.

Ivory Coast spent $12.5 million on fresh or chilled potato imports in the first nine months of 2026, a 45% increase from $8.64 million in the same period of 2025. The country imports an average of 62,000 tonnes of potatoes annually to meet demand that far outstrips its domestic production of less than 100 tons. Imports of processed potato products, including French fries, also rose by 78% from $1.52 million in 2020 to $2.70 million in 2025.

Egypt has become a significant supplier, with its potato shipments to Ivory Coast growing from $14,000 in 2020 to $1.68 million in 2025. This growth is attributed to Egypt's organized logistics network, which includes irrigation, sorting, packaging, phytosanitary inspections, and refrigerated transport. These systems ensure consistent deliveries and minimize losses from farm to market.

Other West African producers show mixed results, with Mali's fresh potato exports to Ivory Coast falling by nearly 92% to $37,000 in the first nine months of 2026, down from $461,000 a year earlier. Senegal is exploring ways to boost its regional potato exports, forecasting over 255,000 tonnes in January 2026. African suppliers must compete on delivery consistency, product quality, and final price to succeed in the Ivorian market.

Why it mattersIvory Coast's reliance on imported potatoes affects consumers and businesses like fast-food restaurants and delivery services.
