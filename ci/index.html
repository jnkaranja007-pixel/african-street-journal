Ivory Coast's Abidjan district funds 60 young entrepreneurs with 300 million FCFA

The funds went to winners of the 2026 Alassane Ouattara Prize for Emerging Young Entrepreneurs, handed over at the Sofitel Hotel Ivoire in Cocody.

60 young entrepreneurs received their funding cheques on Monday, 16 November, at the indoor arena of the Sofitel Hotel Ivoire in Cocody, Abidjan. The District Autonomous of Abidjan allocated a total of 300 million FCFA to them.

The money is meant to help the winners of the 2026 Alassane Ouattara Prize for Emerging Young Entrepreneurs turn their projects into working businesses and join the economy. The district is the body that provided the funding.

The handover comes as Botswana's president, Duma Gideon Boko, makes a state visit to Ivory Coast aimed at giving new momentum to economic cooperation between the two countries.

Why it matters60 young entrepreneurs gain 300 million FCFA in start-up funding to bring their projects into operation.

Sport · 2026-11-17

Ivory Coast can qualify for CAN 2027 on Tuesday

The Elephants lead their group with 9 points and can secure a spot with a win against Gambia.

The Ivory Coast national football team, the Elephants, can qualify for the Africa Cup of Nations (CAN) 2027 tournament on Tuesday, November 17. They will play Gambia at the Independence Stadium in Bakau. The match is part of the fourth day of CAN 2027 qualifiers.

Currently leading Group C with 9 points, the Elephants, coached by Hervé Renard, need a victory to guarantee their place. A win would put them at 12 points, a total neither Gambia nor Ghana can reach. This would ensure Ivory Coast finishes at least second in the group with two matches remaining.

The Elephants have won all three of their previous qualifying matches under Renard, scoring six goals and conceding only one. Gambia, with 6 points, is seeking to keep its qualification hopes alive and will play at home in front of their supporters.

Yamoussoukro students and officials plant trees to mark peace day

Forestry officials say planting trees together builds social cohesion and restores the country's forest cover.

The regional directorate of Waters and Forests in Yamoussoukro organised a sports walk followed by a tree planting drive on Friday, 13 November 2026, to mark the National Day of Peace. Pupils, students, administrative authorities, service heads and residents of the capital took part.

Lieutenant-Colonel Florent Bato, the regional head of Waters and Forests, said planting trees together cultivates cohesion, solidarity and hope. He urged Ivorians to restore the forest cover of a country that was once heavily forested. Sub-prefect Yéo Dohogninnina said peace is built daily through dialogue, mutual respect and tolerance, and asked young people and students to look after the seedlings they planted.

In a separate contribution, Minister of Waters and Forests Jacques Assahoré Konan called for collective action to protect the national forest heritage during the 30th National Day of Peace, celebrated on 15 November in Guiglo. In Séguéla, pupils and residents also replanted one hectare at the Lycée moderne Dominique Ouattara.

Why it mattersYoung people who planted the seedlings are now responsible for watering and protecting them, a task the sub-prefect said continues after planting.

Politics · 2026-11-17

Achi seeks parliamentary models abroad

Ivory Coast's National Assembly president Patrick Achi visited the UK, Paris, and met Canadian senators to modernize legislative processes.

Patrick Achi, president of Ivory Coast's National Assembly, led a delegation to the UK Parliament from November 4-7, meeting with Speaker Sir Lindsay Hoyle. The group, including Vice-President Cédric Diarra and RHDP group president Adama Koné, observed parliamentary sessions and visited universities in London, Oxford, and Cambridge. Discussions focused on digitizing procedures, using artificial intelligence in legislative work, training elected officials, and overseeing government actions, reflecting Achi's goal for a more modern assembly since his election on January 17, 2026.

From November 10-11, Achi represented President Alassane Ouattara at the 9th Paris Peace Forum, addressing the theme "From Sovereignty to Cooperation" amid global turbulence. Upon returning to Abidjan on November 13, he received the Canada-Africa Parliamentary Association, led by Senator Amina Gerba. Achi presented Ivory Coast's 2026-2030 National Development Plan, highlighting opportunities in agricultural processing, food security, industry, infrastructure, and digital development, while also advocating for improved access to Canadian universities for Ivorian students.

Achi, who previously served as Prime Minister and Minister of Economic Infrastructure, is leveraging parliamentary diplomacy to advance his agenda. His delegations consistently include Cédric Diarra and Adama Koné. Patrick Achi will turn 71 on November 17.

Politics · 2026-11-17

Ivorian minister Nassénéba Touré receives Makeda Honorary Prize in Paris

Touré was honoured for her political career and her work on women's leadership and economic empowerment.

Ivory Coast's Minister of Women, Family and Children, Nassénéba Touré, received the 2026 Makeda Honorary Prize on Saturday, 14 November, at the second edition of the award, held at the Paris city hall. The honour was presented by the High Commission of African Diasporas of France, which recognised her political career and her commitment to women's leadership and empowerment.

According to a ministry statement carried by the Agence Ivoirienne de Presse, the prize credits her experience gained abroad and in Ivory Coast, and her work to bring women into economic, social and political development. Touré spent several years in the United States before working at the World Bank, and returned to Ivory Coast in 2006. She became the first woman elected mayor of Odienné and later held government posts and a seat on the directorate of the RHDP party.

Six other Ivorian women were also honoured at the ceremony. Touré dedicated the prize to women who work every day for the progress of their communities.

Why it mattersHer ministry now oversees public policy on protecting women and children, economic empowerment of women, and their place in decision-making bodies.
