Abidjan Hosts African Freedom and Development Debate

Think tanks and foundations gathered in Abidjan to discuss rethinking freedom and development based on the continent's realities.

The question of how to rethink freedom and development in Africa, grounded in the continent's human, social, and cultural realities, was central to the "African Freedom Encounters." The event took place on Thursday, September 3, 2026, in Cocody Riviera 4. It was organized by Audace Institut Afrique in partnership with Atlas Network, the Friedrich Naumann Foundation for Freedom, and the Center for Research and Action for Peace (Cerap).

The International Book Fair of Abidjan (SILA) also served as a venue for significant political reflection on Thursday, September 3, 2026, in Cocody. This event focused on reinventing democracy in Africa beyond voting, drawing from an essay by Ousmane Ndiaye.

These discussions aimed to foster a deeper understanding of the challenges and opportunities facing the continent in its pursuit of progress and self-determination, bringing together diverse perspectives on governance and societal advancement.

Ivory Coast Project Extends Six Months to Consolidate Climate Gains

Stakeholders met to review technical recommendations and discuss extending the "Transition bas carbone" project until June 2027.

Actors involved in combating climate change are aiming to solidify Ivory Coast's achievements in its low-carbon transition. On Wednesday, September 2, 2026, participants gathered in Cocody for the sixth steering committee meeting of the "Transition bas carbone" (Tbc) project. They examined technical recommendations and the conditions for a six-month extension of the initiative.

The Tbc project, initially planned for a shorter duration, is now expected to continue until June 2027. This potential extension aims to build upon the progress made and ensure the sustainability of the project's outcomes in the country's efforts towards a low-carbon economy.

The discussions focused on consolidating the gains achieved so far and exploring the feasibility of prolonging the project's activities. This strategic move underscores a commitment to long-term environmental goals and the integration of sustainable practices within the nation's development framework.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected because the project's extension signals continued focus and potential for further investment in low-carbon initiatives.

Ivory Coast Basketball Climbs to 35th Globally

The national team gains two spots in the FIBA rankings following strong performances in World Cup qualifiers.

The Ivorian men's national basketball team, known as the Elephants, has advanced two positions to reach 35th place globally in the latest FIBA rankings released in September 2026. This marks an improvement from their previous 37th position in July. The Ivorian Basketball Federation (FIBB) announced the update, attributing the rise to recent successes in international windows, particularly during the 2027 World Cup qualifiers.

With 413.8 points, the team solidifies its position within Africa's top three, building on a record of seven wins and two losses in qualification matches. This climb follows crucial victories against Mali (83-77) and Egypt (72-66) during the fourth qualifying window in Dakar, despite a subsequent loss to Angola (91-70). The team is currently second in Group F with 16 points, trailing Senegal.

The Elephants are well-positioned to secure a spot in the 2027 FIBA World Cup. The final African qualifiers will take place in Abidjan from February 25-28, 2027, at the Palais des sports de Treichville. The national team aims to clinch their qualification in front of their home crowd during this decisive fifth window.

Why it mattersThe national basketball team's improved global ranking and strong performance in World Cup qualifiers directly impacts national pride and potential future international sporting events hosted in the country.

Ivory Coast Aims for 700 Digital Public Services by 2028

The government launched a national workshop to prioritize digital services, targeting 700 coordinated online offerings within three years.

The Ivorian government has initiated a national workshop to accelerate the digital transformation of public administration. The goal is to identify and prioritize approximately one hundred public services for digitization. This initiative aims to achieve a total of 700 coordinated digital services by the end of 2028.

This effort is part of a broader strategy to enhance public administration through technology. The workshop, held on September 3rd, brought together stakeholders to define the roadmap for this digital transition. The government is committed to modernizing its service delivery mechanisms to improve efficiency and accessibility.

The push for digital services is expected to streamline bureaucratic processes and improve citizen engagement with government functions. By digitizing a significant portion of public services, Ivory Coast seeks to align its administrative capabilities with global digital trends and foster a more efficient public sector.

Why it mattersThe government's plan to digitize 700 public services by 2028 will affect businesses by potentially streamlining interactions and reducing administrative burdens.

Yohou's Speech Sparks Row Between Ivory Coast Opposition Parties

An address by a former Yopougon deputy led to controversy between allied opposition parties, prompting official responses to quell the dispute.

Dia Houphouët Augustin Yohou, a vice-president of the PDCI and former deputy, is at the center of a dispute with Laurent Gbagbo's PPA-CI party. His remarks about the failure of the opposition's Common Front have created tensions between the PDCI and the PPA-CI, two key allied opposition groups.

The PDCI-RDA has since reminded its members of the rules governing public statements made on behalf of the party. This action followed the PPA-CI's reaction to Yohou Dia Houphouët Augustin's declarations concerning the opposition's electoral front.

The controversy has forced both allied opposition parties to issue statements to de-escalate the situation. The incident highlights internal friction within the opposition alliance as they navigate their political strategy.

Why it mattersThe political maneuvering and internal disputes among opposition parties affect the stability and unity of the political landscape.
