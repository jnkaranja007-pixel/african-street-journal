Ivory Coast court postpones PDCI leadership election challenge

The Abidjan court will hear further proceedings on November 19 regarding Tidjane Thiam's PDCI leadership.

The Abidjan Court of First Instance postponed a hearing on Thursday, October 22, 2026, concerning legal challenges to Tidjane Thiam's election as head of the PDCI-RDA party. Two party militants filed the action, questioning the legality of his election.

The court rescheduled the hearing for November 19 to incorporate another legal procedure into the case. This decision follows the initial examination of the appeals filed against Thiam's leadership.

The PDCI-RDA is a significant political party in Ivory Coast. The outcome of these legal challenges could impact the party's internal dynamics and future political activities.

Why it mattersThe court's decision affects the leadership of the PDCI-RDA party in Ivory Coast.

CANAL+ opens Studios Mancala in Abidjan

The new audiovisual production complex aims to create international standard content and support local talent.

Studios Mancala, a new audiovisual production complex, opened on October 20 in Abidjan's Riviera Golf area. The facility, backed by CANAL+ Afrique, aims to boost film and television production capacity in Ivory Coast. After two years of construction, the studios were inaugurated by government ministers and industry figures.

The Studios Mancala feature three sound stages with dedicated control rooms, advanced acoustic design, and high-performance post-production equipment. CANAL+ Afrique's Chief Content Officer, Fabrice Faux, stated the investment continues the group's 30-year presence in Ivory Coast. Michel Mutombo-Cartier, director general of Studios Mancala, emphasized the need for structures, talent, and resources to foster creativity.

The complex is open to all audiovisual producers and creators, not just CANAL+ channels, potentially offering new opportunities for Ivorian companies. Communication Minister Amadou Coulibaly highlighted the importance of training and professionalization for the audiovisual industry. He also mentioned plans for a national studio catalog to position Abidjan as an audiovisual and film hub.

Why it mattersIvorian audiovisual professionals and companies can now access modern production facilities and training.

FECOMCI proposes 10 actions for artisanal mining by 2030

The Federation of Mining Cooperatives of Ivory Coast aims to formalize actors and improve gold traceability.

The Federation of Mining Cooperatives of Ivory Coast (FECOMCI), led by Koné Seydou, has put forward 10 proposals for its 2026-2030 action plan. This plan seeks to sustainably transform artisanal mining operations across Ivory Coast. Key objectives include combating illegal gold panning, formalizing industry participants, enhancing gold traceability, and protecting the environment.

In parallel, authorities continue to fight illegal gold extraction. 19 illegal mining sites were dismantled recently in the Yamoussoukro autonomous district and the Bélier region. These actions are part of efforts against illicit gold exploitation in these areas.

FECOMCI's plan targets the period up to 2030 for these transformations. The federation's proposals aim to create a more structured and regulated artisanal mining sector.

Why it mattersArtisanal miners in Ivory Coast face efforts to formalize their activities and combat illegal operations.

Guinean ex-court chief gets 7 years for graft

Mohamed Lamine Bangoura was also fined 2 billion Guinean francs and banned from office for five years.

Former Guinean Constitutional Court president Mohamed Lamine Bangoura was sentenced to seven years in prison on October 21, 2026, in Conakry. The Economic and Financial Crimes Court (CRIEF) found him guilty of embezzlement, illicit enrichment, corruption, and money laundering.

The court, presided over by Alpha Camara, also ordered the confiscation of Bangoura's assets in Guinea and Senegal and imposed a fine of 2 billion Guinean francs and a five-year ban from holding public office. His co-accused, Sidiki Sylla and Amadou Diallo, were sentenced to 10 years each and fined 5 billion Guinean francs, with arrest warrants issued for them.

The case involved the management of approximately 20 billion Guinean francs of public funds allocated to the Constitutional Court. Bangoura's defense argued that the institution received substantial annual funding. The state intends to proceed with enforcing the court's decision, according to state attorney Antoine Pépé Lamah.

Why it mattersFormer Guinean officials face prison and asset confiscation for corruption charges.

Ivory Coast completes 220 child labor actions

Solidaridad West Africa and Olam Food Ingredients reported 88% completion of planned actions against child labor.

Solidaridad West Africa and Olam Food Ingredients completed 220 actions against child labor in Ivory Coast's cocoa sector. This represents an 88% execution rate of the 251 planned interventions.

The two organizations presented this report on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, in Cocody during a workshop. This initiative is part of their Child Labor Monitoring and Remediation System (CLMRS), deployed in July 2025.

The CLMRS covered 10 cooperatives within Olam Food Ingredients' supply chain. The system aims to address and prevent child labor in cocoa farming.

Why it mattersChild labor actions were carried out in Ivory Coast's cocoa plantations.
