Mali street drug sales endanger health

Health professionals warn of treatment failures and poisonings from unregulated medicines sold without prescriptions.

In Bamako and other major Malian cities, the high cost of healthcare and medicines drives some people to buy unregulated drugs from street vendors. This practice, known as "pharmacie par terre," exposes consumers to serious health risks, including ineffective treatments and poisonings, according to health professionals.

General practitioner Harouna Doumbia stated that financial hardship, vendor proximity, and easy, prescription-free purchases are key reasons for this trend. Pharmacist Bréhima Sidibé agreed, citing high drug prices and a lack of public awareness about the dangers. Street vendor Aly Keita confirmed that antibiotics like amoxicillin are frequently requested, with prices ranging from 25 to 500 CFA francs.

Dr. Doumbia warned that unknown drug compositions can cause poisoning and organ damage, while under-dosed or unsuitable medications can lead to treatment failure and delayed diagnosis. He noted particular concern for malaria and infection treatments, where substandard drugs could worsen conditions and lead to severe illness.

Why it mattersMalian citizens are risking their health by purchasing unregulated medicines on the street due to the high cost of formal healthcare.

Health · 2026-10-31

Mali launches solidarity month activities

Mali's solidarity month included training for cancer patients and disabled women, and delivery of maternal health kits.

Mali launched its 31st Solidarity Month with several events from October 26 to 29, 2026, focusing on vocational training, maternal health, economic empowerment, and partner mobilization. On October 27, ministers Oumou Sall Seck and Assa Badiallo TOURÉ co-chaired a 10-day traditional and modern beadwork training for the Association Solidarité et Soutien aux Malades de Cancers, supported by FAFPA to boost their economic autonomy.

The Ministry of Health and Social Development also received clean delivery kits to improve care for pregnant women and newborns, presided over by Dr. Abdoulaye Guindo. In Niamakoro, ministers Oumou Sall Seck and Assa Badiallo TOURÉ launched a semi-industrial soap-making training for women with disabilities and widows, also backed by FAFPA for professional integration.

Additionally, 220 widows and orphans of military personnel received self-employment kits for trades like tailoring and solar energy installation after receiving training. Moov Africa Malitel supported vulnerable populations with food, medical supplies, solar equipment, and funding for income-generating activities. Kénéya Foot 2026 also met with the Health Minister to promote sports for health and civic engagement.

Why it mattersVulnerable populations in Mali, including cancer patients, disabled women, widows, orphans, pregnant women, and newborns, received training and essential supplies.

Multiple bus robberies on Gao–Intilit road

Travelers report being robbed and their belongings ransacked on the route between Gao and Intilit.

Bus hijackings are increasing on the Gao–Intilit road, causing concern for travelers. Following an attack last Tuesday, another bus was reportedly robbed on Thursday along the same route. Passengers allegedly had their belongings stolen, and their luggage and packages were searched and damaged.

Travelers are calling for increased security on the Gao–Intilit road due to repeated attacks. Passengers have been robbed of their possessions and their baggage has been ransacked.

The repeated attacks have prompted passengers to call for a reinforcement of security on this stretch of road.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Mali's M5-RFP leaders sidelined by military junta

Former key figures in the ousting of President IBK are now imprisoned, in exile, or out of public life.

Leaders of the M5-RFP coalition, who were instrumental in the downfall of President IBK, are now largely out of power in Mali. Many of these figures are currently facing imprisonment, have gone into exile, or have returned to civilian life.

The M5-RFP, a broad opposition movement, had previously organized large demonstrations that weakened IBK's administration. Today, the military leaders who took power have sidelined these former allies.

Prominent M5-RFP figures like Issa Kaou Djim, Imam Oumarou Diarra, and Choguel Kokalla Maïga are experiencing vastly different fates. Some are in prison, while others have left the country or are no longer active in public affairs.

Why it mattersThe leaders of the M5-RFP coalition, who were key actors in removing former President IBK, are now facing imprisonment, exile, or a return to civilian life.
