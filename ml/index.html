Mali weather events kill thirteen, affect nearly three thousand

Since the start of the rainy season, storms have caused thirteen deaths and seventeen injuries across Mali, impacting 355 households.

Weather events in Mali have resulted in thirteen deaths and seventeen injuries since the beginning of the rainy season. As of September 2, nearly two thousand, eight hundred people, comprising 355 households, have been affected by floods, lightning, and violent winds. Authorities are intensifying prevention and intervention measures, including rapid response teams and canal cleaning operations, alongside public awareness campaigns via media and the SOS Sécurité app.

The reduced impact this year is attributed by urban planner Ibrahim Abdrahamane Kounta to lower rainfall compared to the previous five years, particularly outside Bamako. In the capital, interventions on watercourses and stormwater drainage systems, along with the clearing of canals, have also contributed to limiting damage by ensuring smoother water flow towards the Niger River.

Despite a generally weaker rainy season, the situation remains precarious, with official forecasts indicating moderate thunderstorm activity throughout September, potentially bringing intense rain, strong winds, and lightning. River levels are rising, and while major riverine threats are not currently observed, localized flooding remains a risk, prompting government calls for vigilance, especially in flood-prone areas.

Why it mattersThirteen people have died and nearly two thousand, eight hundred individuals are affected by weather-related incidents, including floods and lightning strikes.

Malian Colonel Sentenced to 20 Years for State Security Offenses

Former Gendarmerie Colonel Alpha Yaya Sangaré received a firm 20-year prison sentence for charges including undermining state security.

Malian justice has sentenced former Gendarmerie Colonel Alpha Yaya Sangaré to twenty years in prison. The verdict was delivered by the National Pole for the Fight Against Cybercrime's criminal chamber on Thursday, September 3, 2026. The charges stemmed from the publication and dissemination of content, including an essay on terrorism and its management, which the court found constituted an attack on the external security of the state.

The court also found Sangaré guilty of publishing and disseminating false news likely to disturb public order and undermining the state's credit. While the public prosecutor had sought the death penalty for treason, the criminal chamber ultimately imposed the twenty-year sentence. The judgment also includes the confiscation of seized copies of his book and a symbolic payment of one franc to the Malian state.

The case drew significant attention due to Sangaré's high rank and the sensitive nature of the charges. The court's final determination of offenses, particularly the charge of attacking the external security of the state, reflects its legal assessment of the evidence presented during the trial and debates.

Why it mattersFormer Colonel Alpha Yaya Sangaré is affected by a 20-year prison sentence for offenses related to state security and the dissemination of content.

NGO PRESU-Sahel Launches Remedial Classes in Koro

The organization aims to support struggling students and integrate refugee children into local schools and centers.

The NGO PRESU-Sahel has initiated remedial classes in Koro to assist students facing academic difficulties. This initiative was formalized during a general assembly of the Siaka Dama D school's School Management Committee, where the NGO is actively involved.

The program's objectives include implementing targeted remedial education and ensuring the enrollment of refugee children within schools and SSAP centers. This effort is designed to provide equitable educational opportunities for all children.

The NGO's intervention at the Siaka Dama D school signifies a commitment to improving educational outcomes for vulnerable students in the Koro region. The focus on remedial support and inclusion aims to address existing educational disparities.

Why it mattersChildren in Koro, including refugees, will receive additional academic support to improve their chances of accessing education.

Malian Pharmacists Mobilize for Responsible and Innovative Practices

The 25th Day of the Order of Pharmacists in Mali convened in Bamako, bringing together health and justice ministers to discuss quality, training, and drug availability.

The 25th Day of the Order of Pharmacists of Mali took place in Bamako on September 3, 2026, under the patronage of the Minister of Health and Social Development. The event, themed 'Pharmacy in Mali: strengthening good practices for a responsible, innovative profession serving public health,' addressed critical issues including practice quality, training, ethics, and medication accessibility. The President of the Order called for enhanced initial and continuous training, professional oversight, and regulation of training institutions.

The Minister of Justice affirmed the judiciary's commitment to combating fraudulent practices within the pharmaceutical sector, instructing public prosecutors to take necessary actions. The Minister of Health acknowledged the pharmacists' role in healthcare continuity, the success of the health insurance scheme (AMO), and their philanthropic contributions through medication donations to vulnerable populations and free consultations. Several pharmacists were recognized for their outstanding contributions to the profession.

During the ceremony, the Minister of Health urged participants to use the event as a platform for reflection and action, assuring that recommendations would be reviewed by her department to foster a more responsible and innovative pharmacy sector dedicated to public health. The event was attended by the Minister of Justice, a representative from the Ministry of Higher Education, and numerous Malian and invited pharmacists.

Why it mattersMalian citizens are affected by the focus on improving pharmaceutical practices, training, and drug availability, which directly impacts public health services and access to medication.

Business · 2026-09-05

Mali Raises 60.5 Billion FCFA in Public Debt Sale

The West African Monetary Union's debt market saw strong investor demand, though at a significant cost to public finances.

Mali's Treasury successfully raised 60.5 billion FCFA on September 2 from the West African Monetary Union's public securities market, exceeding its initial target of 55 billion FCFA. The operation involved one-year Treasury bills and three- and five-year bonds. Investor demand was robust, with proposals reaching nearly 68 billion FCFA, indicating continued confidence in Malian securities from regional banks and investors.

Despite strong demand, the cost of this liquidity is substantial, reflected in the yields required by investors. The weighted average yield for bills maturing in September 2027 was 4.89%, while bonds due in September 2029 yielded 7.69% and those maturing in August 2031 yielded 7.40%. These yields are higher than the nominal coupons, which were set at 6% for three-year bonds and 6.20% for five-year bonds, due to the price at which investors purchased the securities.

In a separate transaction on the same day, the Treasury repurchased 9.05 billion FCFA of a bond maturing in late September at a weighted average yield of 2.5%. This move aims to reorganize upcoming debt repayments rather than secure new funds. These two operations, the debt issuance and the early redemption, serve complementary goals by providing fresh liquidity for budgetary needs while restructuring near-term obligations.

Why it mattersThe Malian government is borrowing money at a high cost, which will impact its finances.
