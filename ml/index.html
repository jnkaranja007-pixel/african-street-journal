Women Leaders Convene in Bamako to Boost Sahel Peace Efforts

A regional forum in Bamako brings together women from seven countries to enhance their participation in conflict prevention, mediation, and peacebuilding, addressing a critical.

A regional forum focused on women's leadership for peace in the Sahel and coastal countries opened Wednesday, August 26, in Bamako. The meeting, running until August 28, aims to strengthen women's involvement in conflict prevention, mediation, and peace consolidation. It gathers representatives from women's peace organizations, women mediators, national and regional authorities, and UN agencies, alongside actors from defense, security, academia, and community leadership.

Six countries, including Burkina Faso, Niger, Benin, Chad, Mauritania, and Ivory Coast, are represented alongside Mali. This mobilization occurs amidst a concerning humanitarian situation, with nearly 20 million people forcibly displaced or stateless in West and Central Africa as of April 2026, according to the UNHCR. Women and children constitute 80% of refugees and asylum seekers in the region, highlighting their vulnerability and essential role in peace processes.

The forum addresses the persistent issue of conflict-related violence and underrepresentation of women in decision-making, over twenty-five years after UN Security Council Resolution 1325. Key priorities include ensuring women's participation at all peace process stages, enhancing protection for women mediators, facilitating access to flexible funding for women's organizations, and establishing a follow-up mechanism for recommendations. The event seeks to accelerate the implementation of the women, peace, and security agenda in the region.

Why it mattersWomen leaders from Mali and six neighboring countries are meeting to improve their involvement in peace and security efforts, directly impacting regional stability and humanitarian responses.

Mali Meat Prices Rise Amidst Cattle Supply Shortages

Butchers and consumers face difficulties accessing affordable meat in Bamako and regional capitals due to high livestock costs and supply chain issues.

Meat is becoming increasingly scarce and expensive in Malian markets, with Bamako, Bafoulabé, and Koulikoro experiencing difficulties in finding affordable cuts. Butchers report less stocked stalls and high livestock prices, which significantly reduce profit margins and can lead to losses after slaughter. One vendor shared an experience where a purchased ox resulted in a loss of forty-five thousand FCFA after butchering, highlighting the financial strain on the sector.

Industry stakeholders are proposing solutions to improve market supply and stabilize prices. Mamoudou Diallo, president of the livestock-meat sector interprofession, believes that an increase in supply, potentially through the return of transhumant animals, could lower meat costs. He also advocates for the creation of secure livestock selling points near major urban centers to facilitate access to animals and reduce reliance on intermediaries, which could streamline supply to Bamako and regional capitals.

While awaiting potential price reductions, some households are adjusting their diets, with chicken and fish becoming more prominent in meals. Separately, meteorologists predict rains will continue until October, which could benefit some crops but poses risks to maize and cotton, prompting agricultural specialists to advise producers to adapt their practices.

Why it mattersConsumers in Mali are finding it harder to buy meat due to rising livestock costs and supply chain problems, forcing some to change their eating habits.

Mali Basketball Team Loses FIBA World Cup Qualifier to Ivory Coast

The Eagles of Mali fell to the Elephants of Ivory Coast 77-83 in the opening game of the fourth window of the FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifiers in Group F.

The Malian national basketball team, the Eagles, lost their first match of the fourth window of the FIBA World Cup 2027 qualifiers. They were defeated by Ivory Coast's Elephants with a final score of 77-83 in Group F. This loss marks a setback for Mali's campaign to secure a spot in the upcoming World Cup.

The Eagles had arrived in Dakar, Senegal, with a strong record of five wins and one loss from the previous three qualifying windows. This performance had placed them among the top teams in Group F, alongside Senegal and Ivory Coast, who shared the same win-loss record. The team's next match is scheduled against the Democratic Republic of Congo on Saturday, August 29, 2026, at 15:30 GMT.

Mali's squad includes key players like Siriman Kanouté, the team's leading scorer with an average of 19.2 points per game, Oumar Ballo contributing 13.5 points and 9.2 rebounds, and Aliou Fadiala Diarra with 11.3 points and nine rebounds. Despite individual strengths, the team's collective performance will be crucial in upcoming games against strong opponents like the DRC and Senegal.

Why it mattersMali's basketball team's loss impacts national pride and their qualification chances for the FIBA World Cup 2027.

Mali, Ivory Coast Experts Agree on Border Demarcation

Technical experts from Mali and Ivory Coast have reached a consensus on the definitive alignment of their shared border, a crucial step that still requires governmental approval.

Experts from Mali and Ivory Coast have agreed on the final route of their 532-kilometer shared border, marking a significant technical advancement after a joint meeting in Bamako. The agreement, reached by consensus, includes validation of the draft delimitation treaty and a geodesic framework for future demarcation. This technical accord follows a joint reconnaissance mission in the Pogo-Zégoua area, which reconciled old maps with current terrain realities to resolve outstanding uncertainties.

While the technical agreement is a long-awaited step, it must be formalized by a treaty signed by both governments to gain full legal standing. The agreed geodesic framework is slated for deployment by the end of 2026, paving the way for the installation of border markers. The process of demarcation, which makes the border visible with markers and recognized coordinates, involves further consultations, topographical work, and funding, alongside informing local communities.

For residents in the border zone, the demarcation is vital for daily life, impacting administrative authority, land identification, tax collection, and dispute resolution. The physical marking of the border aims to prevent conflicts without hindering legitimate cross-border activities like those of families, herders, farmers, and traders who maintain long-standing relationships. The Pogo-Zégoua crossing, a key route to Ivorian ports, may see improved coordination between customs and police, though it won't immediately resolve logistical delays or security concerns.

Why it mattersFarmers and traders in the border region are affected by the potential for clearer land identification and the preservation of cross-border usages, while investors may see improved coordination at key crossings.

Mali Farmers Face Planting Delays Amid Late, Heavy Rains

The agricultural season's delayed start and persistent rainfall are raising concerns about input delivery and crop yields for the 2026-2027 farming year.

Farmers in Mali are expressing significant worry over the current agricultural season, which began late and is now experiencing sustained heavy rainfall. This combination of factors is disrupting the timely delivery of essential agricultural inputs, directly threatening the planned production targets for the 2026-2027 campaign. While early-maturing crop varieties may offer some mitigation, the overall outlook remains uncertain.

The distribution of rainfall has become a major point of concern across several agricultural regions. The season's delayed onset meant that planting periods, crucial for optimal crop development, were missed. Now, the persistent and heavy precipitation is creating further challenges, compounding the difficulties faced by farmers who are already grappling with delayed access to necessary supplies.

The situation highlights the vulnerability of Mali's agricultural sector to climatic variations and logistical challenges. The reliance on timely rainfall and input availability means that any disruption can have a cascading effect on food security and rural livelihoods. Authorities are monitoring the situation, but the immediate impact on the ground is one of apprehension among those dependent on the harvest.

Why it mattersFarmers are directly affected by delayed inputs and unfavorable rain patterns, which threaten their crop production and livelihoods for the 2026-2027 agricultural season.
