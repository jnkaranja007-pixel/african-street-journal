Climate · 2026-11-10

Mali aims to cut emissions by 25.22% by 2035

The nation presented its updated climate targets at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, on November 9, 2026.

Mali is participating in the 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31) in Antalya, Turkey, which began on November 9, 2026. The country's government delegation will present its new national climate contribution, CDN 3.0, which was approved on October 1, 2026. Mali aims to reduce its greenhouse gas emissions by 25.22% by the year 2035.

Ahmed Sékou Diallo, president of the Climate Platform of Mali, stated that achieving this goal is ambitious but possible with sufficient financial resources. He emphasized that African nations prioritize access to international funding for climate adaptation and managing losses and damages. Diallo noted that the continent seeks a significant increase in financial resources to support its climate efforts.

The conference, running until November 20, 2026, is a platform for Mali to communicate its updated climate commitments. The nation's participation occurs as securing international finance is seen as crucial for translating climate pledges into concrete actions. Africa is particularly interested in a massive increase in funding for adaptation and loss and damage.

Why it mattersMali's commitment to reducing greenhouse gas emissions by 25.22% by 2035 directly affects its environment and future development.

Politics · 2026-11-10

Mali's prime minister calls for security innovation

Abdoulaye Maïga opened the Bamako International Defence and Security Exhibition 2026, focusing on modern threats.

Mali's Prime Minister Abdoulaye Maïga opened the Bamako International Defence and Security Exhibition (BAMEX) 2026 on November 9, 2026, at the Parc des Expositions. The event, running until November 13, focuses on defence and security under the theme "Innovation, intelligence and reactivity: Africa facing new global security challenges."

Maïga paid tribute to Malian security forces, the unified force of the Confederation of Sahel States (AES), and partners. He highlighted current threats including terrorism, violent extremism, transnational crime, trafficking, cyber threats, and disinformation, noting these are complicated by porous borders and vast territories. The Prime Minister stressed the need for anticipation, knowledge, coordination, and adaptation over purely reactive responses.

The Prime Minister stated that innovation, intelligence, and reactivity are complementary pillars of modern security. Innovation involves leveraging technologies like artificial intelligence, drones, and satellite imagery, adapted to local realities. Intelligence requires strengthening information collection, verification, analysis, and sharing. Reactivity means rapidly converting information into decisions and actions, emphasizing cooperation as no single country can defeat terrorism alone.

Why it mattersThe Prime Minister of Mali addressed security challenges and called for innovation and cooperation to combat terrorism and transnational crime.

Culture · 2026-11-10

Lamine Goro honored for newspaper distribution

The 5th media Salon in Bamako recognized his daily work delivering Malian newspapers to readers.

Lamine Goro was honored on November 8, 2026, at the Palais de la Culture Amadou Hampâté Ba in Bamako. The award recognized his daily commitment to distributing several Malian newspapers.

The ceremony marked the closing of the 5th edition of the Bamako Media Salon. Boubacar Yalkoue, president of the Association of Private Press Publishers (ASSEP), attended with a delegation.

This distinction highlights the essential role of distributors in the print media chain. Goro's work ensures information reaches the public daily.
