Mali farmers discuss agroecology transition

Over 100 farmers and sector actors gathered in Ségou for a three-day national event focused on agroecology and organic farming.

A national week dedicated to peasant agroecology began on October 20 in Ségou, Mali. More than 100 farmers and sector stakeholders from various districts within the region are participating in the event.

For three days, attendees will engage in discussions centered on the transition to agroecology and organic agriculture. The primary objective of these exchanges is to bolster food sovereignty across Mali.

The regional platform for agroecology and organic agriculture is the organizing body behind this national initiative, bringing together key figures in the agricultural sector to share knowledge and strategies.

Malian cooperative members receive governance training

Over 180 members of six cooperatives in Macina and Kokry are attending a training program to improve their economic management and market access.

More than 180 members from six cooperative societies in the communes of Macina and Kokry began a series of training sessions on October 20. The initiative, part of the Tisal project funded by BMZ, aims to enhance participants' skills in governance, economic management, and commercial negotiation.

The training is being implemented by the NGO Donilab. Participants will also learn strategies to improve their access to markets, a key component of strengthening their cooperative operations and economic viability.

This program is designed to equip cooperative members with essential tools for better business practices and market engagement, ultimately contributing to their livelihoods and the local economy.

Why it mattersFarmers in Macina and Kokry are affected by gaining skills to improve their economic management and market access.

Mali traffic alternates on RN27 from October 19

The Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure has reintroduced alternating traffic on a section of Bamako's Route Nationale 27 to allow for roadworks.

Alternating traffic has resumed on the urban stretch of Route Nationale 27 in Bamako since October 19, 2026. The Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure announced the measure, citing ongoing road development works. This system aims to facilitate the continuation of construction while maintaining vehicle passage as much as possible, requiring increased vigilance from all road users.

The decision follows a site visit on October 16, 2026, by ministry officials, national police, and Bamako city representatives. They assessed the progress of the roadworks and determined the necessity of temporary lane closures to allow construction teams to work. The alternating traffic is intended to balance the needs of the construction site with the daily travel requirements of the population.

This measure is designed to manage traffic flow during urban road construction, where sections of the road must be temporarily closed. The principle of alternating traffic on RN27 involves organizing vehicle passage according to conditions set on the ground, accounting for areas closed for construction purposes. This approach seeks to limit difficulties for commuters.

Malian fashion show highlights traditional clothing

The sixth Koko Fashion Show in Bamako will showcase traditional Malian attire and craftsmanship.

The sixth Koko Fashion Show is scheduled for Saturday, October 24, at the Bamako International Conference Centre. The event, themed "Preserving Traditions," aims to highlight Malian clothing heritage, local craftsmanship, and foster connections with the sub-region and diaspora. Organized by KOKO Fashion, the show will feature traditional fabrics like bogolan and bazin, emphasizing their cultural significance and commercial potential in contemporary fashion.

Fatoumata Sanogo, the general coordinator, stated the event seeks to delve deeper into Malian cultural roots and celebrate tradition. The fashion show is intended to increase the visibility of Malian creations internationally, potentially boosting sales and creating opportunities for artisans, dyers, and designers. Organizers also stressed the importance of passing down this heritage to younger generations, viewing traditional clothing as suitable for both daily wear and formal occasions.

The event will include a red carpet inspired by the Met Gala, encouraging guests to wear outfits reflecting local traditions and skills. The red carpet begins at four o'clock, with a television broadcast starting at five thirty. For the first time, the show will be broadcast live on TM1, allowing a wider audience to follow the event, especially via social media. Delegations from Guinea, Ivory Coast, Mauritania, Gabon, the United States, and France are expected to attend.

Why it mattersMalian artisans and designers will gain visibility for their traditional clothing and craftsmanship.
