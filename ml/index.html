Moroccan ambassador highlights autonomy plan in Bamako

Driss Isbayène presented Morocco's autonomy proposal for the Sahara region, emphasizing its legal and institutional framework.

Morocco's Ambassador to Mali, Driss Isbayène, organized a celebration in Bamako on October 31, 2026, for the first anniversary of Morocco's Fête de l’Unité. The event highlighted the strong relationship between Morocco and Mali, with Ambassador Isbayène discussing the historical significance of the Moroccan territorial unity holiday, established in 2025. He detailed Morocco's proposed Plan d’Autonomie for the Sahara, describing it as a comprehensive legal and institutional framework.

Ambassador Isbayène explained that the autonomy plan includes a regional parliament with legislative powers, an executive led by a government chief appointed by the King, and regional courts. He also noted provisions for preserving Hassani identity and culture, including an institute, and facilitating the return of populations from Tindouf camps. The ambassador stated that the plan is a serious, realistic basis that aligns with international standards and recent UN Security Council resolutions.

During the event, Daouda Tékété spoke about the deep historical ties between Bamako and Rabat, referencing trans-Saharan caravans and current Moroccan investments in Mali across sectors like banking, telecommunications, agriculture, and insurance. Souleymane Satigui Sidibé also addressed territorial integrity and called for African unity. The celebration reaffirmed the strong bonds between Morocco and Mali, united by shared history and a vision for South-South partnership.

Why it mattersMalians are informed about Morocco's proposed autonomy plan for the Sahara region and the historical ties between the two nations.

Mali targets first medal at Dakar Youth Olympics

Five Malian athletes aged 15-17 compete in athletics, fencing, judo, swimming, and taekwondo.

Mali is participating in the Youth Olympic Games (YOG) in Dakar, Senegal, from October 31 to November 13, 2026, aiming to win at least one medal. The Malian delegation includes five athletes, two of whom are women, competing in athletics, fencing, judo, swimming, and taekwondo. The team is led by Abdoul Wahab Zéromé, vice-president of the Malian National Olympic and Sports Committee.

Taekwondo is seen as a strong possibility for a medal, with past successes by athletes like Daba Modibo Keïta and Ismaël Coulibaly. Bintou Diakité's gold medal at the 2026 African Taekwondo Championships in Bamako further fuels these hopes. The young athletes have pledged to perform their best to represent Mali with honor.

This is the first edition of the YOG to be held on the African continent. The athletes are determined to achieve their goal of bringing a medal back to Mali.

Why it mattersMalian athletes are competing in the Youth Olympic Games in Dakar with the goal of winning a medal.

Mali police arrest suspects in three murders

Authorities in Bamako have apprehended individuals in connection with three killings that occurred in October.

Bamako, Mali – Three murders have shaken the Malian capital in October, with police announcing the arrest of suspects in all three cases. The Director General of the National Police, Youssouf Koné, stated on October 28, 2026, that his services had solved three assassinations committed within the month. The cases occurred in the Sébénikoro, Djélibougou, and Lafiabougou neighborhoods.

The first victim, 23-year-old Broulaye Doumbia, a hairdresser, was found on October 14 in ACI 2000, allegedly shot in an apartment before his body was dumped. Six people were arrested, including a former soldier accused of the crime, with the motive reportedly a sexual assault attempt gone wrong. The second case involved 53-year-old shopkeeper Moctar Fofana, found on October 18 in Djélibougou. An 18-year-old student was detained, claiming the victim was blackmailing him over intimate videos.

The third victim, 23-year-old merchant Boubacar Ly, disappeared on October 23 and his body was found the next day in Sébénikoro. The investigation, aided by a social media appeal, led to the arrest of five youths on October 26, including a close friend identified as the main suspect. The police are continuing their investigations into these incidents.

Why it mattersResidents of Bamako are affected by a series of violent crimes that have occurred in their city.

Mali marks 13 years since RFI journalists' murder

Family and colleagues mark the International Day for the End of Impunity for Crimes against Journalists.

13 years ago, on November 2, RFI reporter Ghislaine Dupont and technician Claude Verlon were assassinated in northern Mali. Their deaths, which occurred on the day the UN established the international day to end impunity for crimes against journalists, remain a stark reminder of the dangers faced by media professionals.

Family and colleagues continue to honor their memory and seek administrative clarity in their pursuit of truth. The ongoing quest for answers highlights the persistent challenges in achieving justice for crimes committed against journalists.

The November 2 commemoration serves as a focal point for those demanding accountability and an end to impunity for journalists targeted in conflict zones and elsewhere.

Why it mattersThe assassination of two RFI journalists in Mali 13 years ago remains unresolved, impacting those seeking justice.

Mali police arrest three suspected armed robbers in Bamako

Police seized two motorcycles, ammunition, and mobile phones during the operation in the Hippodrome district.

Police in Bamako arrested three men aged 24, 26, and 38 between October 27 and 28, 2026, in the Hippodrome neighborhood. The arrests followed an anonymous tip about armed robberies targeting motorcycle users in Commune II of the Bamako District. Officers from the research brigade conducted the operation under the direction of Adjudant-chef Rassoi Habib DAKOUO.

A search of the suspects' apartment yielded two motorcycles, a 9mm cartridge, two radios, and five mobile phones. Investigators believe the arrested individuals are part of a network that operates at night. The suspects have been transferred to judicial authorities, and investigations are ongoing.

The operation, overseen by Police Commissioner Acherif AG AKLY, demonstrates the National Police's commitment to combating crime. Director General Youssouf KONÉ and the Minister of Security and Civil Protection support these efforts. The public is encouraged to report information to the police hotline at 101.

Why it mattersResidents of Bamako, particularly motorcycle users, are directly affected by the arrests of suspected armed robbers.
