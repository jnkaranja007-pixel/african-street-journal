Inheritance disputes leave widows and orphans in Mali without their share

Relatives of deceased heads of family are reportedly sharing out property in disregard of the rights of widows and children.

In Mali, the death of a family head often leaves a painful gap, and in some households it brings a further hardship: widows and their children are denied their share of the inheritance. Relatives of the deceased are reportedly dividing his property while ignoring the rights of the widow and the orphans.

Studio Tamani asked why these practices are spreading and what remains of the values of solidarity, honour and responsibility that families have long relied on. The question was put to the traditionalist Sidiki Kouyaté on the Club Tamani programme, broadcast on 13 November 2026.

The same report, carried by Bamada.net, said the programme examined why inheritance has become a source of family division. It gave no figures on how many families are affected.

Why it mattersWidows and children who are shut out of an estate lose the property that was meant to keep them housed and fed.

Niger bans peanut exports, with exceptions possible for Mali

The ban, set by an order dated 11 November, is meant to supply Niger's cooking-oil processing plants with local raw material.

Niger has banned the export of peanuts under order No. 05-18, dated 11 November 2026. Commerce and Industry Minister Abdoulaye Seydou announced the measure on Friday 13 November, saying it is intended to support local processing units, especially those that make cooking oil.

Exports to Mali remain possible in principle. Exceptional authorisations may be granted for shipments to countries of the Confederation of Sahel States (AES), but these follow a defined procedure and are not automatic. Seydou said illegal export attempts will lead to the seizure of the quantities concerned for the state, under the existing judicial procedure.

Peanuts join millet, paddy rice, sorghum and maize, whose exports were already banned. Seydou also reminded officials that public administrations must buy Nigerien-made products when they are available on the national market.

Why it mattersTraders who want to ship peanuts out of Niger must now apply for an exceptional authorisation, and anyone exporting without one faces seizure of the goods.

Mali names police commissioner Moussa Ag Infahi inspector general of security services

The appointment places a veteran national police officer in charge of security and civil protection services, a post Mali's Council of Ministers filled on Friday.

Mali's Council of Ministers appointed Police Commissioner General Moussa Ag Infahi as Inspector General of Security and Civil Protection Services at a meeting on Friday, 13 November 2026, chaired by Transition President Assimi Goïta at the Koulouba Presidential Palace. The appointment was made under the Ministry of Security and Civil Protection.

Ag Infahi, born on 20 July 1965 in Anderamboukane in Ménaka region, has spent decades in public security, investigation and security sector reform. His career includes posts in police stations, the national police's general directorate, investigation units and international cooperation. He trained at the National School of Administration (ENA) and, from February to September 1996, at the special platoon of the Inter-Armed Forces Military School (EMIA) in Koulikoro.

The same day, a decree signed by Goïta named Mouhamed B. Diarra director general of the National Institute of Social Security (INPS), succeeding Idrissa Bakary Diarra.

Why it mattersThe post puts a senior police officer in charge of security and civil protection services, the body that oversees public protection across the country.

Mali: 50 women farmers get agricultural kits

The initiative in Yélimané aims to improve production techniques and farm management for market gardeners.

50 market gardeners in the Yélimané district received agricultural training and kits on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. The handover ceremony took place at the Guidimé town hall.

The project is supported by the Mirya Gnoumaba cooperative, ADEC, and ARDL, with funding from PSAAF IV. This initiative is designed to enhance production methods and the operational management of market gardens.

The goal is to improve the skills and resources available to these women farmers, directly impacting their ability to cultivate and manage their produce effectively.

Mali beats Rwanda 3-2 to take sole lead of CAN 2027 qualifying Group K

Mali came from behind twice at the 26-Mars Stadium in Bamako, with Kamory Doumbia equalising after a Rwandan goalkeeping error and Camara's 84th-minute strike sealing the result.

The Malian national team, the Eagles, beat Rwanda's Wasps 3-2 on Friday at the 26-Mars Stadium in Bamako in the third round of qualifiers for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. Rwanda led through Kwizera, who headed in from a cross in the 29th minute. Mali came from behind twice to win the match.

Tatou had crossed for Kwizera to score the opening goal. Kamory Doumbia equalised in the 43rd minute after the Rwandan goalkeeper failed to hold a free kick from Guindo. Camara's goal in the 84th minute gave Mali the win and sole leadership of Group K.

Mali had now won two of its three matches in the group, with the victory taking it ahead of Rwanda, which had also won its first two games.

Why it mattersThe win puts Mali alone at the top of Group K, with the qualifying race for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations still open.
