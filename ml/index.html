Mali allocates 212 hectares for 100 MW solar plant

The Gongasso solar farm will include a 100 MWh storage system to integrate renewable energy into the national grid.

Mali has designated 212 hectares of land in Gongasso, Sikasso region, for a new 100 MWc solar power plant. The decision was made during a Council of Ministers meeting on October 23, 2026. This project aims to boost the country's renewable energy capacity.

The solar farm will feature a 100 MWh storage system to help integrate solar power into the national grid. An engineering firm, ICRED, previously conducted a feasibility study for a 100 MWc solar plant with storage. The final land allocation is slightly smaller than the 240 hectares initially considered.

The project aims to diversify Mali's energy sources and strengthen electricity availability. The combination of solar production and storage can contribute to better integration of renewables into the national electricity network.

Why it mattersMali is developing renewable energy infrastructure to increase electricity availability for its citizens.

CANAM uses gala match to promote health access

Mali's national health insurance body partners with Keneya Foot for a solidarity match on October 31, 2026.

The Caisse nationale d’assurance maladie (CANAM) is supporting a gala football match scheduled for October 31, 2026, to raise awareness about public health issues. The initiative, organized by Keneya Foot, aims to use football as a tool to connect with citizens and promote access to healthcare across Mali. Mamadi Coulibaly, CANAM's Director of Administration and Human Resources, spoke at a press conference on October 23, 2026, on behalf of the Director General.

Coulibaly stated that CANAM's fundamental mission is to ensure all Malians have access to healthcare services throughout the country. He emphasized that this goal aligns with the national priority placed on public health by the highest authorities of the Transition. The partnership with Keneya Foot seeks to bridge health concerns with the population through the universal language of football.

This collaboration provides CANAM with an opportunity to increase the visibility of its services and promote health coverage to a wider audience. Former players and supporter representatives also attended the press conference, expressing their commitment to the initiative and its values of solidarity and fair play.

Why it mattersMali's citizens will be made more aware of their access to healthcare services through a football match.

Mali army drone strikes kill civilians

An investigation found at least a quarter of drone strikes by the Malian army and its Russian partners caused civilian casualties.

An investigation into drone attacks in Mali revealed that at least one quarter of the strikes conducted by the Malian army and its Russian partners, Africa Corps, resulted in civilian casualties. The investigation, which spanned six months from September 2023 to September 2026, meticulously verified 60 drone strikes.

Journalist Matteo Maillard stated that drones have profoundly transformed the war in Mali, with civilian targets frequently hit. This high number of collateral victims may stem from a lack of precise intelligence, a flawed target identification process, or less restrictive rules of engagement. It is possible Malian crews pilot drones with Turkish assistance, striking targets identified or approved by Russian command structures.

The use of drones has also become a media weapon for the Malian army, showcasing state power and modern technology. However, the human cost includes polluted fields, killed livestock, and entire villages displaced due to fear of nighttime attacks.

Why it mattersCivilians in Mali are being killed and displaced by drone strikes.

Mali distributes 200 food kits to vulnerable people

The kits, part of the 31st Solidarity Month, contain rice, maize, and oil for those in need.

Colonel Cheick Mamadou Chérif TOUNKARA, representing the Special Advisor to the President of the Transition, distributed 200 food kits to vulnerable people in the Ségou region on Sunday, October 25, 2026. The distribution took place at the Mali Agricultural Products Office (OPAM) in the Somonosso neighborhood. Each kit contained 50 kg of rice, 50 kg of maize, and 20 litres of oil.

This initiative is part of the 31st Solidarity Month and fight against exclusion, reflecting the commitment of President Assimi GOÏTA to promote sharing and mutual aid. Ségou's mayor, Nouhoun DIARRA, praised the gesture, noting the President's dedication of sovereignty funds to social works. Beneficiary spokesperson Ismaël TRAORÉ expressed gratitude and wished for the initiative's continuation.

Governor Chiaka MAGASSA stated that solidarity is a daily duty and urged Malians to follow the President's example. Colonel TOUNKARA assured that generosity would continue in various forms, recalling past actions like distributing school kits and building infrastructure. The distribution aims to support the most fragile populations in the region.

Why it mattersVulnerable people in the Ségou region received food kits containing rice, maize, and oil.
