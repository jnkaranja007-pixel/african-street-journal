Health · 2026-11-04

Bamako health centers get solar power

Three community health centers in Bamako received solar installations on October 16, 2026, as part of a larger program.

Solar power installations were officially activated on October 16, 2026, at the Dar Salam, Médina Coura, and Bagadadji community health centers in Bamako. These three facilities were equipped as part of social initiatives by the President of the Transition, General Assimi Goïta.

The initiative aims to equip 50 community health centers in Bamako with solar power to improve their operations and the quality of care. Minister of Health and Social Development, Colonel-Major Assa Badiallo Touré, praised the effort as valuable support for her department, noting significant energy access challenges.

Colonel Cheick Mamadou Chérif Tounkara, representing the special advisor to the President for social works, urged the center managers to use the equipment wisely and maintain it regularly. The presidents of the management committees expressed gratitude to the head of state for the support.

Why it mattersThree community health centers in Bamako received solar power installations, improving their operational capacity and the quality of care for local populations.

Agriculture · 2026-11-04

Mali farmers face varied harvest yields

Rainfall interruptions and high input costs impact crops across different regions of Mali.

Mali's 2026 agricultural harvest is yielding mixed results across the country. In Bankass, farmers are still in the fields harvesting mil, sorghum, fonio, and peanuts, but report lower yields than last year. This is attributed to a nearly three-week break in rainfall during a critical growth period, affecting crop development.

In contrast, the mil and sorghum harvest is complete in Douentza, with farmers finishing in October 2026. The Sikasso region reports significant progress, with 90% of maize and 80% of rice harvested, and 70% of cotton collected. Producers there are now focused on bagging and storing maize and rice.

Further north, producers in Tombouctou's Amadia plain are awaiting their harvest this month, facing challenges with water supply due to expensive diesel for irrigation and degraded infrastructure. High fertilizer costs also impacted their campaign, though rice crops are now showing promise.

Why it mattersFarmers across Mali are experiencing varied harvest outcomes due to rainfall patterns and the cost of agricultural inputs like fuel and fertilizer.

Politics · 2026-11-04

Mali state security holds lawyer for six months

Mountaga Tall, a former minister, was abducted from his home by State Security agents.

Malian lawyer and former minister Mountaga Tall has been held by State Security for six months. He was abducted from his home by hooded men on November 2, 2026. Other pro-democracy activists taken by State Security in May have recently reappeared, raising concerns about their health.

Amnesty International is urging the transitional authorities to investigate the disappearance of Mountaga Tall. He was seized from his residence six months ago. The abduction occurred during the country's harvest season.

The transitional authorities have not provided an update on Tall's whereabouts or condition. Human rights groups are calling for transparency and accountability in this case.

Why it mattersMountaga Tall, a lawyer and former minister, has been detained by state security forces for six months, with other activists reappearing in poor health.

Education · 2026-11-04

Mali minister inspects new Kayes university campus

Minister Bouréma Kanssaye visited provisional sites for January 2027 classes.

Mali's Minister of Higher Education and Scientific Research, Pr Bouréma Kanssaye, inspected the future university campus in Kayes on Sunday, November 1, 2026. Accompanied by the governor of Kayes, the minister examined temporary facilities intended for the first courses starting in January 2027.

These provisional sites include the ANPE trades workshop, the Massa Makan Diabaté performance hall, and the former circle council building. The inspection aimed to assess the readiness for the start of educational activities at the new university.

The minister's visit on November 1, 2026, focused on evaluating the conditions for commencing academic programs. The university is scheduled to begin classes in January 2027.

Why it mattersMalian students will soon have access to a new university campus in Kayes, with classes starting in January 2027.

Mali journalists' murder case advances with document release

French investigators received partial declassification of a military note from April 2024 in the 2013 Ghislaine Dupont and Claude Verlon case.

13 years after the murders of journalists Ghislaine Dupont and Claude Verlon in Kidal, Mali, the judicial investigation in France has moved forward. On April 15, 2026, the French Commission for National Defence Secrecy approved the partial declassification of a note from the French Ministry of Armed Forces dated April 24, 2024. Judge Valéry Muller, leading the investigation into kidnapping, sequestration, and murder linked to terrorism, requested the document.

This decision marks the fourth declassification procedure in the case, following similar actions in 2016, 2017, and 2023. While some technical details remain classified, other information can now be shared with the magistrate and civil parties. No trial has yet established full responsibility for the abduction and assassination of the two Radio France Internationale reporters, who were found dead shortly after being kidnapped in Kidal on November 2, 2013.

Seidane Ag Hitta is named in the investigation as a suspected instigator, a qualification pending judicial confirmation. Malian authorities also seek Ag Hitta, identifying him as a leader for the Group for the Support of Islam and Muslims in the Kidal region, offering a 500 million FCFA reward for information leading to his arrest in 2026. Civil parties are requesting international arrest warrants for him and Hamadi Ag Mohamed, cited as a suspected member of the commando.

Why it mattersThe investigation into the 2013 murders of two RFI journalists in Mali is progressing with new document access, though no one has been convicted.
