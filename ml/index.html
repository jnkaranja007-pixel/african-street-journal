Education · 2026-11-08

Mali universities announce regional opening dates

Bandiagara will recruit students in November 2026, and Kayes will start classes in January 2027.

Mali's Council of Ministers, meeting on October 16, 2026, announced plans for regional universities. The University of Bandiagara will open applications for its Materials Science program in November 2026, with classes beginning in the 2026-2027 academic year. The University of Kayes will launch its Mines and Geology department courses in January 2027.

These timelines were presented by the Minister of Higher Education and Scientific Research as part of a gradual rollout for universities in Sikasso, Gao, Timbuktu, Bandiagara, and Kayes. The goal is to provide education and research tailored to local needs. The University of Sikasso's rural development institute has been operational since the 2023-2024 academic year, currently serving 325 students in two programs.

The Council of Ministers, chaired by Transition President Assimi Goïta, also adopted three draft decrees, made appointments, and heard communications on social dialogue, sport, climate, and health. The minister aims for these universities to eventually offer all planned courses and establish research laboratories meeting international standards.

Why it mattersMalian students in Bandiagara and Kayes will have new higher education opportunities starting in late 2026 and early 2027.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Mali's Goïta observes Russian military exercise

Malian President Assimi Goïta attended a live-fire drill during his visit to Moscow for the Russia-Africa summit.

Malian President Assimi Goïta observed a live-fire military exercise on a Russian army base during his visit to Moscow. The event occurred on the sidelines of the Russia-Africa summit held on October 28 and 29. Images of Goïta firing a weapon four times were broadcast on Malian national television, ORTM.

The Malian leader attended the exercise after watching local soldiers demonstrate their skills. The footage showed Goïta himself participating in the shooting drill. This observation took place during his trip to Moscow for the third Russia-Africa Summit.

Goïta also met with Niger's President Abdourahamane Tiani in Niamey on November 5. They discussed security within the Sahel States Confederation and the fight against armed groups, claiming success. They also addressed diplomatic coordination, free movement of people and goods, transport infrastructure, and resource exploitation.

Why it mattersMalian President Assimi Goïta's participation in a Russian military exercise and his diplomatic meetings with regional leaders affect Mali's security and international relations.

Business · 2026-11-08

Malians remember Madou Dakolo 12 years after his death

The entrepreneur, known for his generosity, built a significant business in the hydrocarbon sector.

Malians are remembering Mamadou Lah, known as Madou Dakolo, on the twelfth anniversary of his death on November 8, 2026. Dakolo, who passed away on November 8, 2014, is remembered for his entrepreneurial success and generosity. His legacy continues to inspire admiration and recognition.

Madou Dakolo, a prominent businessman in the hydrocarbon sector through his company Hydro-Mali SARL, was known for his significant investments and wealth. He was also recognized for his charitable contributions and support for those in need, embodying human and social values.

His life serves as an example for young Africans seeking success and determination. Dakolo's business acumen and philanthropic spirit left a lasting impression on Mali and its people.

Mali receives thousands of gold miners expelled from Guinea

Malian authorities are providing emergency support and repatriation for thousands of gold miners expelled from Guinea.

Tens of thousands of gold miners expelled from Guinea have arrived in Mali in recent days. This mass arrival follows measures taken by Conakry on November 2, 2026, to end illegal gold mining operations.

Malian transitional authorities, with international partners, have established emergency systems to support these returning miners. The primary destination for repatriation is Burkina Faso.

The expulsion order in Guinea was issued on November 2, 2026. The Malian government is coordinating efforts to manage the influx of its citizens.

Health · 2026-11-08

Mali allocates 125 billion FCFA for hospitals

The government is also strengthening border controls to manage health risks and recent dengue cases.

Mali's government has allocated 125 billion FCFA through an advance decree to urgently fund hospital construction, salary adjustments, and priority development projects. This financial boost aims to modernize healthcare services and improve patient care across the country. The decision was made during a ministerial meeting on Friday, November 6, 2026.

In parallel, Mali is reorganizing its border management to enhance territorial security and health protection. The National Directorate and National Border Commission's responsibilities are expanding to improve coordination among control forces. This move is a response to a recent increase in dengue cases and regional health threats.

These measures reflect a commitment to improving citizens' daily lives through significant investment in the hospital sector and border health security. The government also plans to launch a livestock vaccination campaign for nearly 89 million animals on December 12, 2026.

Why it mattersMalians will benefit from improved hospital infrastructure and better containment of disease outbreaks at their borders.
