Mali State Protects 260 Vulnerable Children

Two government decrees grant national or state ward status to children orphaned by terror attacks or abandoned by parents.

The Malian government has declared 260 children as wards of the nation or state, providing them with institutional protection and support. This decision, adopted by the Council of Ministers on August 28, 2026, aims to safeguard children affected by the country's ongoing crisis and situations of abandonment. The measures are intended to ensure these minors receive appropriate care and guidance in essential areas like health and education.

A total of 247 children have been recognized as Wards of the Nation. These are minors whose fathers were killed in terrorist attacks, ambushes, or by improvised explosive devices. The Malian state's recognition of their status offers a formal response to the challenges these children face following the loss of their fathers, providing them with access to a support system.

Additionally, 13 abandoned minors without a valid parental substitute have been declared Wards of the State. This legal status allows the government to intervene and guarantee the necessary conditions for their development, education, and protection. The initiative seeks to offer these children a more favorable environment for building their future, preventing their vulnerability from permanently hindering their life paths.

Why it mattersChildren orphaned by terrorist attacks or abandoned by parents will receive state support for their health and education.

Mali Ministry Schedules Special Exam for Kidal Students

Sixty-four students in Kidal will have a second chance to take their diploma exams in September due to security concerns that prevented their participation in June.

The Malian Ministry of National Education has announced a special examination session for sixty-four students from Kidal who were unable to sit for their Diploma of Fundamental Studies (DEF) exams in June. The special session is scheduled to take place from September 14 to 16, offering these candidates a crucial opportunity to complete their education.

These students missed the ordinary examination period because of insecurity that led to the displacement of many residents from Kidal following violence on April 25. Examination centers were relocated to Gao and Ménaka, but most Kidal candidates could not reach these alternative sites. For instance, only 28 out of 80 students managed to take the exam in Ménaka.

The decision to hold a special session follows appeals from civil society organizations and the Kidal branch of the National Federation of Education, Sports, Arts, Research, and Culture (FENAREC-Kidal). These groups had advocated for a special examination opportunity for the affected students, a request now being addressed by the ministry.

Why it mattersSixty-four students in Kidal are affected because they will now have an opportunity to take their diploma exams after being unable to do so in June due to security issues.

Insecurity Halts Mali's Meat Supply Chain

Malian livestock and meat industry professionals met to address disruptions caused by insecurity, transport issues, and reduced supply.

The difficult journey of meat from pasture to market in Mali is being severely hampered by insecurity, transport challenges, and a shrinking supply, according to industry leaders. The Interprofession of the Livestock and Meat Sector of Mali (IFBV-Mali) convened its general assembly in Bamako to discuss these critical issues. Mamoudou Abdoulaye Diallo, president of IFBV-Mali, noted the strong regional participation, highlighting the sector's vitality despite the difficulties.

In regions like Bandiagara, the impact is stark. Amadou Traoré, president of the butchers' association, reported a significant slowdown in activity. Where previously up to 20 cows, plus sheep and goats, could be slaughtered daily, now only three cows are processed. Many butchers have ceased operations. The region's abattoir is insufficient, and a lack of preservation equipment further complicates meat sales. Cattle traders like Boubacar Koïta pointed to the insecurity on routes like Bandiagara-Fatoma as a major blockage, cutting the traditional supply chain.

Similar concerns were raised in the Mopti region, a traditional livestock hub. Alboucar Bocoum, secretary-general of the regional livestock merchants' union, stated that insecurity has led some herders to abandon their usual grazing areas, resulting in stolen animals and making travel to markets perilous. Even with efforts to bring livestock markets closer, traders and herders remain hesitant to travel due to the risks involved, further constricting the meat supply.

Why it mattersInsecurity and transport issues are disrupting the livestock and meat supply chain, leading to reduced availability and higher prices for consumers.

Malian Army Strikes Terrorist Camps, Seizes War Materials

Malian armed forces targeted terrorist gathering points in Tessalit and Koutiala regions, destroying significant war materiel.

Malian armed forces, supported by partners, conducted operations in the Tessalit and Koutiala regions on August 28, 2026. Intelligence led to the detection and successful targeting of terrorist group encampments. In the Tessalit area, these positions contained substantial war materials, including armed vehicles and fuel reserves. Simultaneously, operations west of Koutiala identified and struck other terrorist strongholds where leaders were present.

These strikes are part of Operation Dougoukoloko, aimed at securing national territory. The Malian Army's General Staff reported that surveillance and reconnaissance efforts are ongoing to assess the full impact of the operations and neutralize any remaining threats. The military reaffirms its commitment to rigorous and professional security operations across the country and asks for continued public support.

The operations follow a series of strikes against armed groups in northern and central Mali. On August 24 and 25, 2026, aerial attacks targeted regroupments in Abéïbara and northeast of Sévaré, destroying war materiel and armed vehicles. The full human and material toll from these recent actions is still under evaluation, with military communications serving as the primary source of information.

Why it mattersThe Malian armed forces are directly affected by these operations as they work to neutralize terrorist threats and secure national territory.

Mali Eagles Schedule Three International Matches

The Malian national football team, the Eagles, will play against Cape Verde, Liberia, and Tunisia in September and October.

The Malian national football team, known as the Eagles, is preparing for three significant international fixtures. Their first match is scheduled for September 25 in Bamako, where they will face Cape Verde. This will be followed by an away game on September 29 in Monrovia against Liberia.

The team's international schedule concludes with a friendly match on October 3 in Tunis, where they will play against Tunisia. These upcoming games are considered crucial for the team's development and their preparation for future competitions.

Supporters are eagerly anticipating the Eagles' performances and are hopeful for strong showings in these encounters. The team's participation in these matches is aimed at enhancing their overall play and readiness for subsequent tournaments.

Why it mattersFootball fans in Mali and abroad will be interested in the national team's performance in these international matches.
