Regional Women Leaders Convene in Bamako to Strengthen Peace Efforts

A regional forum in Bamako has gathered women leaders from seven West African nations to boost female participation in conflict prevention and peacebuilding.

A regional forum focused on women's leadership for peace in border regions of the Sahel and coastal countries opened Wednesday, August 26, in Bamako. The meeting, scheduled until August 28, 2026, aims to increase women's involvement in conflict prevention, mediation, and peace consolidation. Representatives from women's peace organizations, female mediators, national authorities, regional institutions, and UN agencies are attending.

The forum includes participants from Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Benin, Chad, Mauritania, and Côte d’Ivoire, alongside defense and security actors, researchers, community and religious leaders, and youth organizations. This mobilization occurs as nearly 20 million people were forcibly displaced or stateless in West and Central Africa by April 2026, with women and children accounting for 80% of refugees and asylum seekers, according to the UN High Commissioner for Refugees.

Discussions have highlighted four key priorities: ensuring women's participation in all peace processes, strengthening protection for mediators, facilitating flexible funding for women's organizations, and establishing a monitoring mechanism for recommendations. Mali's Minister of Women, Children, and Family, Diarra Djénéba Sanogo, noted that a meeting with Ambassador Liberata Mulamula, the African Union Commission's Special Envoy for Women, Peace, and Security, is intended to accelerate this agenda's implementation.

Why it mattersThis forum directly affects women and children in West and Central Africa by seeking to enhance their role in peace processes, which could lead to improved security and stability in conflict-affected regions.

CNDH urges Mali to adopt specific law against female genital mutilation

The National Commission for Human Rights (CNDH) has called on Malian authorities to enact a specific law addressing female genital mutilation (FGM), including excision.

The CNDH issued a statement on Wednesday, August 26, 2026, responding to recent debates surrounding FGM. The commission views these practices as traditional acts that harm the physical, moral, psychological integrity, and dignity of women and young girls.

Citing national, African, and international legal instruments concerning women's and children's rights, the CNDH emphasized that FGM constitutes a violation of human rights. The institution highlighted the need for stronger awareness campaigns by authorities and civil society organizations to shift societal attitudes.

The CNDH's appeal underscores that protecting human rights is a shared responsibility, requiring collective action from all societal stakeholders. This call aims to provide a dedicated legal framework to combat FGM in Mali, as reported by Kémoko Diabaté for Le PAYS.

Why it mattersThis affects women and young girls in Mali by seeking to provide them with specific legal protection against harmful traditional practices like female genital mutilation.

Mali and Côte d'Ivoire experts agree on final border demarcation

Experts from Mali and Côte d'Ivoire have adopted a definitive border line and validated a draft treaty, moving closer to physical demarcation.

Malian and Ivorian experts reached a consensus on the final route of their shared 532-kilometer border during a meeting in Bamako from August 21 to 25. This technical agreement also included the validation of a draft delimitation treaty and a geodetic framework for future demarcation and boundary marker installation. The decision follows a joint reconnaissance mission in April to the Pogo-Zégoua area, where old maps were compared with current ground realities to resolve remaining uncertainties.

The adopted technical agreement requires further steps before full legal effect and physical manifestation. Both governments must sign the delimitation treaty, and the geodetic framework needs to be deployed by the end of 2026 to allow for the start of boundary marker placement. This process aims to clarify the border, which is crucial for administrative authority, land identification, tax collection, and dispute resolution in affected communities.

The materialization of the border will involve local explanations and accessible recourse mechanisms to prevent conflicts and ensure the preservation of cross-border activities for families, herders, farmers, and traders. While clearer territorial lines can improve coordination among customs, police, and gendarmerie, they alone will not resolve issues like corridor delays, harassment, or security risks, particularly along the Pogo-Zégoua axis, a key route to Ivorian port infrastructure.

Why it mattersThe agreement on the border demarcation directly affects communities living along the 532-kilometer frontier by clarifying land ownership, administrative authority, and dispute resolution mechanisms.

Malian farmers face production threats from late rains and input delays

Late and sustained rainfall, coupled with delayed input deliveries, are raising concerns among Malian farmers for the 2026-2027 agricultural season, potentially impacting.

Malian farmers are expressing concern over the 2026-2027 agricultural campaign due to late-starting, yet sustained, rainfall and delays in receiving essential inputs. These factors threaten to undermine production objectives, although the use of early-maturing crop varieties could offer some mitigation.

The rainy season began later than recommended for planting in several agricultural zones, according to reports. Despite the initial delay, the season is now characterized by continuous, heavy precipitation, which presents its own set of challenges for crop development and management.

The combination of a late start to the rains and the hold-up in input distribution creates a difficult situation for farmers aiming to meet their agricultural goals. The potential for reduced yields could affect food security and the livelihoods of those dependent on farming in the region.

Why it mattersFarmers in Mali are directly affected by the late rains and input delays, which threaten their ability to meet production targets for the 2026-2027 agricultural season.

Banamba authorities select local mining fund projects

Local authorities in Banamba held a meeting to select projects for the Local Mining Development Fund, aiming to improve access to water, health, and education.

On Thursday, August 27, 2026, Banamba authorities convened to select projects for the Local Mining Development Fund. The meeting was chaired by Hama Fofana, the first deputy to the prefect, and included sub-prefects, mayors, village chiefs, and technical services.

The chosen projects are intended to be integrated into the communal PDSEC plans. Their primary goal is to enhance access to essential services such as water, health, and education for the local population.

This initiative reflects a concerted effort by various local leaders and technical departments to channel mining revenues into tangible community development, addressing critical needs identified within the communes.

Why it mattersResidents of Banamba will benefit from improved access to water, health, and education facilities as a result of these selected projects.
