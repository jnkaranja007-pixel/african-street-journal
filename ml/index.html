Education · 2026-11-09

Malian academics extend strike indefinitely

University teachers and researchers are entering their third week of industrial action over unpaid research bonuses.

Malian university teachers and researchers began their third week of strike action on Monday, November 9. The industrial action, called by the National Union of Higher Education and Scientific Research (Snesup) on October 26, continues indefinitely. The union demands the payment of a research bonus stipulated in law nearly a decade ago but never disbursed.

The strike has halted university activities across Mali. Snesup stated that their sole demand is the payment of the research bonus. This bonus has been legally mandated for almost 10 years, yet educators have not received it.

The educators' strike began on October 26. The union has not yet achieved satisfaction regarding its demand for the research bonus. The movement is now extended without a set end date.

Why it mattersMalian university teachers and researchers are affected by the non-payment of a research bonus.

Business · 2026-11-09

Malians remember Madou Dakolo 12 years after his death

The entrepreneur, known for his generosity, built a significant business in the hydrocarbon sector.

Malians are remembering Mamadou Lah, known as Madou Dakolo, on the twelfth anniversary of his death on November 8, 2026. Dakolo, who passed away on November 8, 2014, is remembered for his entrepreneurial success and generosity. His legacy continues to inspire admiration and recognition.

Madou Dakolo, a prominent businessman in the hydrocarbon sector through his company Hydro-Mali SARL, was known for his significant investments and wealth. He was also recognized for his charitable contributions and support for those in need, embodying human and social values.

His life serves as an example for young Africans seeking success and determination. Dakolo's business acumen and philanthropic spirit left a lasting impression on Mali and its people.

Politics · 2026-11-09

Mali ex-detainee recounts torture and deaths

El Bachir Thiam, a former member of the now-dissolved Yelema party, describes five months of illegal detention and the deaths of dozens of fellow prisoners.

El Bachir Thiam, a former member of Mali's now-dissolved Yelema party, has spoken out about his detention by state security agents. Thiam was abducted on May 8, 2025, in Kati by masked intelligence service officers and held for nearly five months without legal process. He described atrocious detention conditions and the deaths of several dozen co-detainees during his imprisonment.

Thiam, who is now in exile outside Mali, stated he stopped counting the deaths of fellow prisoners. He was released after a period of detention that coincided with the swift suppression of a brief pro-democracy protest movement. Agents threatened him with death to ensure his silence about the events.

Thiam's testimony details the abduction of several pro-democracy activists under similar circumstances. The Yelema party, like all political organizations in Mali, has been dissolved. Thiam was held in state security custody for approximately five months before his release.

Why it mattersEl Bachir Thiam and other pro-democracy activists were illegally detained and subjected to harsh conditions, with many dying in custody.
