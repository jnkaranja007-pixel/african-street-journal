Mali journalists' murder case sees partial declassification

A French court seeks to declassify a note from April 2024 related to the 2013 killings.

13 years after the murder of journalists Ghislaine Dupont and Claude Verlon in Kidal, Mali, the judicial investigation in France continues. The case involves kidnapping, sequestration, and assassination linked to terrorism. On April 15, 2026, the French Commission for National Defence Secrecy partially approved declassifying a note from the French Ministry of Armed Forces dated April 24, 2024, following a request from investigating judge Valéry Muller.

This decision marks the fourth declassification procedure in the case, with previous ones in 2016, 2017, and 2023. No trial has yet established full responsibility for the deaths. Seidane Ag Hitta is named in the investigation as a suspected instigator, identified by Malian authorities as a leader in the Group to Support Islam and Muslims in the Kidal region. A reward of 500 million FCFA was offered in 2026 for information leading to his arrest.

Civil parties in the case are seeking international arrest warrants for Seidane Ag Hitta and Hamadi Ag Mohamed, also cited as a suspected member of the commando unit. The ongoing investigations aim to determine the individual roles of those implicated in the kidnapping and assassination of the two journalists.

Why it mattersThe investigation into the 2013 murders of two journalists in Mali is progressing with partial declassification of documents, potentially leading to accountability.

Malian women's group collects over 58 million FCFA

The Femmes solidaires et autonomes du Mali announced their 2026 financial contributions at their annual general assembly.

The Femmes solidaires et autonomes du Mali (FSAM) announced over 58 million FCFA in contributions for 2026 at their ninth annual general assembly on October 31, 2026, in Bamako.

FSAM international president Aïché Baba Keïta reported the figures at the gathering, which was attended by thousands of women from across Mali. The organization, founded in 2017, supports women's economic initiatives and financial autonomy through trade, agriculture, and local product transformation.

Past achievements highlighted included the establishment of soap manufacturing units, sachet water production, and peanut paste processing, alongside financing for income-generating activities. The FSAM aims to empower women economically and increase their participation in societal decisions.

Why it mattersThousands of Malian women are directly affected by the FSAM's focus on economic empowerment and financial autonomy.

Mali state security holds lawyer for six months

Mountaga Tall, a former minister and democracy advocate, was abducted from his home by state security agents.

Malian state security agents abducted lawyer and former minister Mountaga Tall from his home six months ago, on November 2, 2026. Tall, an advocate for democracy, was taken by hooded individuals identified as belonging to state security. Other pro-democracy activists seized by state security in May have recently reappeared, raising concerns about their health.

The abduction of Mountaga Tall occurred at his residence, according to reports. The involvement of state security in his disappearance and the reappearance of other activists raise serious questions about their current well-being. The health of these recently reappeared activists is a significant concern for their supporters and human rights organizations.

The case highlights ongoing concerns regarding the actions of state security forces in Mali. The reappearance of other detained activists, whose health is now a worry, suggests a pattern of detentions. Further information on the condition of Tall and the other activists is awaited.

Why it mattersMountaga Tall, a former minister and democracy advocate, has been held by state security for six months, raising concerns for his well-being and that of other detained activists.

Mali distributes aid to vulnerable populations

Rice, oil, and school kits were given to people with disabilities in Kadiolo, Bafoulabé, and Mahina.

During the Month of Solidarity and Fight Against Exclusion, aid was distributed in several Malian locations. In Kadiolo, the FELAPH provided 23 sacks of 50kg rice, 23 five-litre oil containers, six tricycles, a wheelchair, and school kits to individuals with disabilities. People with disabilities in Bafoulabé and Mahina received aid kits containing rice, oil, sugar, mosquito nets, and a symbolic cash envelope.

In Gao, 50 households of internally displaced and vulnerable people received food donations. These actions aim to support those facing hardship and exclusion within these communities.

The distribution is part of a national solidarity initiative. Further details on the total number of beneficiaries or the duration of the solidarity month were not provided in the report.

Why it mattersVulnerable people in Kadiolo, Bafoulabé, Mahina, and Gao received essential food items and mobility aids.

Kayes associations fight drug proliferation

Community groups in Kayes held a debate on October 31, 2026, to raise awareness about drug dangers.

Associations in Kayes organised a debate on October 31, 2026, to address the rise of narcotics in the city. The event aimed to inform attendees about the risks associated with drug use and trafficking.

The initiative, led by the Collectif des Associations, seeks to boost the involvement of community groups and citizens in preventing and combating drug-related issues. Participants were made aware of the dangers of consuming and circulating illegal substances.

The Collectif des Associations plans to continue its efforts to engage the community in the fight against drug proliferation. The debate focused on the immediate dangers and the need for collective action.

Why it mattersResidents of Kayes are directly affected by the proliferation of dangerous drugs in their city.
