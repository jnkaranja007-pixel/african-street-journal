Mali Eagles Schedule Three International Matches

The Malian national football team will play against Cape Verde, Liberia, and Tunisia in September and October.

The Malian national football team, known as the Eagles, is preparing for three significant international fixtures. The first match is scheduled for September 25 in Bamako, where the team will face Cape Verde. This game is part of the team's international engagements and preparation for upcoming competitions.

Following the match against Cape Verde, the Eagles will travel to Monrovia on September 29 to play against Liberia. The team's schedule concludes with a friendly match against Tunisia, set to take place in Tunis on October 3. These upcoming games are considered crucial for the team's development.

Supporters are eagerly anticipating the Eagles' performances in these upcoming matches. The team's participation in these international games aims to foster growth and readiness for future tournaments. The schedule highlights a busy period for the Malian squad as they aim for high-level performances.

Why it mattersThe Malian national football team's upcoming international matches may attract attention and potential revenue from fans and associated businesses.

Malian Forces Neutralize Over One Hundred Terrorists in Dogoni

Malian armed forces successfully targeted and destroyed three terrorist gathering points in the Sikasso region.

Malian armed forces have reported the neutralization of over one hundred terrorists in the Dogoni area, Sikasso region, following a failed attack on the Kignan police station. During the Kignan incident, two police motorcycles were burned, and a kamikaze drone was shot down. Terrorists abandoned eight motorcycles as they retreated.

Intelligence gathering and pursuit of the assailants led to the discovery of three armed group gathering points in Dogoni, estimated to hold more than one hundred individuals and significant war materiel. These positions were successfully engaged. Additional terrorist regroupment sites with war materiel were also detected and struck southeast of Macina.

Surveillance and reconnaissance operations are ongoing in the affected sectors to assess the impact of the strikes, establish a consolidated toll, and neutralize any residual threats. The General Staff of the Armed Forces reiterated its commitment to national security operations and called for continued public support.

Why it mattersMalian armed forces engaged and destroyed terrorist gathering points, impacting security in the Sikasso region.

Mali Prosecutor Becomes Judiciary's Most Feared Instrument

Adama Coulibaly, appointed in January 2024, leads the national anti-cybercrime unit, now a potent tool against critics of the ruling junta.

The national anti-cybercrime unit in Mali has become the judiciary's most feared instrument, particularly targeting voices critical of the ruling junta. Adama Coulibaly was officially installed as the prosecutor for this unit in early January 2024, though he had been acting in the role for several months prior.

Coulibaly's appointment marks a significant shift in how judicial power is wielded against dissent. The unit, under his leadership, is perceived as a primary tool for enforcing the junta's agenda and silencing opposition through legal means. The formal installation ceremony took place in a packed press conference room.

This development places Coulibaly at the center of a judicial apparatus that critics argue is being used to suppress free expression and political opposition within Mali. The unit's growing influence and perceived intrusiveness are a cause for concern among civil society and opposition groups.

Why it mattersCritics of Mali's junta are affected by the anti-cybercrime unit, which is used as a judicial instrument to silence dissenting voices.

News · 2026-08-31

Turkey Mobilizes Resources After Ship Sinks Off Cyprus

President Erdogan confirmed 237 rescued and seven dead, with search efforts for missing individuals continuing.

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan announced that Ankara has deployed all available resources following the capsizing of a passenger ship traveling between Girne and the port of Tasucu in Mersin. The president stated that extensive search and rescue operations were launched immediately after the accident in cooperation with the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus authorities.

Erdogan reported that 237 passengers and crew members were rescued safely, but sadly, seven people lost their lives. He affirmed that the efforts to locate those still missing are ongoing without interruption and expressed hope for encouraging news from the search operations. The state's full resources have been mobilized to monitor the situation closely.

The Turkish president extended his wishes for a speedy recovery to the survivors and offered condolences to the families of the deceased. He also conveyed support to the Turkish nation and the Turkish Cypriot people in the wake of the tragic incident.

Why it mattersThis event directly impacts Turkish and Turkish Cypriot communities through loss of life and rescue operations, affecting families and national response efforts.

Malian pro-FGM videos spark regional online debate

Social media posts of girls undergoing female genital mutilation in Mali have ignited a cross-border digital conflict between proponents and feminists.

Videos depicting girls undergoing female genital mutilation in Mali, posted on social media, have triggered a trans-border mobilization. Influential men in Mali have openly supported female genital mutilation, leading to a coordinated response from West African female activists. This digital confrontation has reportedly caused some of the most vocal opponents, based in Europe, to back down.

The controversy has led to a notable silence from the governments of Bamako and Conakry, who have not publicly addressed the issue. The online debate highlights a deep division on the practice of female genital mutilation within the region and its diaspora, with activists on both sides leveraging social media platforms to advance their arguments.

This digital clash underscores the growing influence of social media in shaping public discourse on sensitive cultural practices across West Africa. The coordinated efforts by activists demonstrate a new form of engagement in advocating for or against deeply entrenched traditions, with implications for policy and social change.

Why it mattersMalian proponents of female genital mutilation are engaged in a digital conflict with West African feminists, affecting the online discourse around the practice.
