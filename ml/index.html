Mali army destroys terrorist vehicles and camp

Malian armed forces reported destroying a terrorist vehicle near Yenchechi and a camp southeast of Kidal on Friday, November 13, 2026.

Malian armed forces (FAMa), supported by partners, destroyed a terrorist vehicle 12 kilometers north of Yenchechi on Friday, November 13, 2026, during Operation Dougoukoloko.

The same day, FAMa struck a terrorist camp 105 kilometers southeast of Kidal, destroying vehicles and military equipment, and also destroyed a camouflaged terrorist vehicle three kilometers north of Ingouzma.

The Malian army's General Staff stated that surveillance operations continue to assess the impact of these actions and to neutralize any remaining threats, urging public support.

Why it mattersMalian armed forces conducted operations to secure the national territory on November 13, 2026.

Mali joins UEMOA farm program for 2026-2030

Ministers approved regional plans for cotton, fertilizer, and rice, aiming for 90% rice self-sufficiency.

Ministers from the West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA) approved regional agricultural programs for 2026-2030 in Ouagadougou on October 23, 2026. Mali participated in the meeting where plans for cotton-textile, phosphates-fertilizer, and rice were endorsed. The communiqué from Mali's Council of Ministers, published on November 13, 2026, reported these decisions.

The approved programs set union-wide targets, including transforming at least 50% of cotton fiber produced and increasing fertilizer consumption from 13 to 50 kg per hectare. A key objective is to raise rice self-sufficiency from 58% to 90% across the UEMOA member states. Mali's Minister of Agriculture informed the council of the country's involvement in these initiatives.

These regional programs are part of implementing the UEMOA Commission's 'IMPACT 2030' strategic plan. The orientations for the cotton, fertilizer, and rice sectors are set for the long term, aiming for 2040. The meeting also saw ministers acknowledge white papers dedicated to these three agricultural sectors.

Security · 2026-11-15

Mali closes defense expo, plans biennial event

The Bamako International Defense and Security Exhibition (BAMEX’26) concluded with plans for future editions and intellectual property registration.

The second Bamako International Defense and Security Exhibition (BAMEX’26) closed on Friday, November 13, 2026, in Koulikoro, Mali. The event showcased technological innovations in defense and security, bringing together official delegations from 19 countries and 24 exhibiting companies. Demonstrations of equipment and exchanges of expertise aimed to strengthen operational capabilities.

Minister of Security and Civil Protection, General Daoud Aly MOHAMMEDINE, announced two key decisions at the closing ceremony: registering the BAMEX brand with the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) and making the exhibition a biennial event. Mali aims to establish BAMEX as a significant platform for defense and security cooperation on the African continent.

The next edition of BAMEX is scheduled for 2028, with ambitions to be even more impactful. This year's exhibition featured a more diverse industrial offering compared to 2025, with participation from countries including Türkiye, Russia, China, Germany, and Sudan, reflecting Mali's efforts to diversify its military partnerships and enhance its autonomy in defense.

Why it mattersMali plans to register its defense exhibition brand and hold it every two years, aiming to boost its security sector.
