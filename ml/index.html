Sport · 2026-11-01

Mali targets first medal at Dakar Youth Olympics

Five Malian athletes aged 15-17 will compete in athletics, fencing, judo, swimming, and taekwondo.

Mali is participating in the Youth Olympic Games (YOG) held in Dakar, Senegal, from October 31 to November 13, 2026. The Malian delegation, comprising five athletes including two women, aims to win at least one medal at this first edition organized on the African continent. The young athletes, aged between 15 and 17, will compete in athletics, fencing, judo, swimming, and taekwondo.

The delegation is led by Abdoul Wahab Zéromé, vice-president of the Malian National Olympic and Sports Committee. Taekwondo is seen as a strong contender for a medal, with past successes from athletes like Daba Modibo Keïta and Ismaël Coulibaly, and Bintou Diakité's recent gold at the African Taekwondo Championships in Bamako. The athletes have expressed their determination to perform well for Mali.

This participation aligns with the Malian authorities' efforts to support sports development. The athletes are focused on giving their best to represent Mali with dignity and potentially secure a medal. The YOG in Dakar represent an opportunity for these young Malian talents to showcase their skills on an international stage.

Why it mattersFive Malian athletes are competing in the Youth Olympic Games in Dakar, Senegal, with the hope of winning a medal.

Education · 2026-11-01

Mali's ISPRIC signs first deal with Russian university

The Malian institute will offer joint engineering programs and double degrees with Russia's Kalashnikov State Technical University.

Mali's ISPRIC signed its first agreement with Russia's Kalashnikov State Technical University on October 28, 2026. The partnership aims to boost academic exchanges, focusing on engineering training and potential double degrees.

The deal was finalized during the third Russia-Africa summit, with support from the Public Diplomacy Center (CDP). ISPRIC's Ali Kane highlighted the institute's interest in technical education and other disciplines with Russian universities.

Kalashnikov State Technical University Rector Mikhail Pisarev noted his institution's strengths in engineering and IT, expressing a desire to quickly move from the agreement to concrete joint projects.

Health · 2026-11-01

Malians buy street drugs amid high pharmacy costs

Doctors warn of poisoning and treatment failure from unregulated medicines sold without prescriptions.

In Bamako and other Malian cities, people are buying medicines from street vendors, known as 'pharmacie par terre,' because pharmacy prices are too high. This practice exposes buyers to health risks like treatment failure and poisoning, according to health professionals.

General practitioner Harouna Doumbia stated that financial hardship, vendor proximity, and easy purchase without a prescription drive people to street drugs. Vendor Aly Keita confirmed that amoxicillin is a popular item, sold between 25F CFA and 500F CFA, often without proper refrigeration.

Dr. Doumbia warned that unknown drug compositions can cause poisoning and organ damage, while under-dosed products may lead to treatment failure and disease progression, delaying proper diagnosis and care.

Why it mattersMalian citizens are risking their health by self-medicating with unregulated street drugs due to the high cost of formal healthcare.

Climate · 2026-11-01

Mali meteorite yields early findings

A space rock found in the Sahara desert in 2022 has revealed its first scientific results.

A meteorite that originated from Mars has completed its journey in Mali's Sahara Desert. Scientists discovered the rock four years ago, in 2022. Initial studies of this Martian meteorite have now been published in the journal Science.

The meteorite's arrival in the Sahara marks the end of a long voyage from the Red Planet. Researchers have begun to analyze its composition and history. These findings offer new insights into planetary science.

The publication in Science provides the first detailed look at this extraterrestrial object. Further research is expected to reveal more about its origins and the conditions on Mars.

Why it mattersA meteorite from Mars was found in Mali's Sahara Desert in 2022, and scientists have now published initial findings from its study.

Culture · 2026-11-01

Malian artist Pakenzy blends genres to reach young audiences

Rapper Moriba Coulibaly, known as Pakenzy, mixes hip-hop, afrobeat, and R&B influences to create accessible music.

Malian musician Pakenzy, whose real name is Moriba Coulibaly, began his music career in 2016 and launched a solo career in 2019. He releases singles like "Société" in March 2024 and "Loloni" in October 2026, blending hip-hop, rap, and afrobeat to appeal to a young audience while retaining Malian references. His song "Oh My Baby" reached the Shazam charts in Ivory Coast in October.

Pakenzy works independently without a label, collaborating with beatmaker Wippa Beat and his team. He aims for accessible and danceable songs that reflect the realities of his generation, focusing on themes of youth, love, and betrayal. He emphasizes patience, perseverance, and consistency in building his career, stating that it requires time, passion, and a connection with people.

The artist carefully manages his image through elegant appearances, clips, and social media performances to establish a recognizable identity. He has collaborated with artists like Sadio Sidibé, Dr Keb, and MS2 on tracks such as "Diarabi" in July 2024 and "FAMA" in January 2025. Pakenzy believes his music can bring happiness, dreams, and life lessons.

Why it mattersMalian youth are affected by Pakenzy's music which discusses their generation's realities, relationships, and contradictions.
