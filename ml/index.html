Mali Council Approves State Protection for 260 Vulnerable Children

New decrees grant 'Pupils of the Nation' and 'Pupils of the State' status to children affected by crisis and abandonment.

The Malian Council of Ministers has adopted two decrees to enhance the protection of vulnerable children. The first decree declares 247 children as 'Pupils of the Nation,' specifically those whose fathers died due to terrorist attacks, ambushes, or improvised explosive devices. This status aims to provide institutional support for these children, covering essential areas like healthcare and education, acknowledging the significant challenges they face after losing a parent.

A second decree designates 13 abandoned children without parental substitutes as 'Pupils of the State.' This measure ensures state intervention to guarantee these minors the necessary conditions for their development, education, and protection. The government's intention is to offer these children a more favorable environment for building their future, preventing their vulnerability from permanently compromising their life paths.

These decisions, presented by the Minister of Health and Social Development, reflect a commitment to prioritizing child protection within social policies, especially for those impacted by violence and familial crises. The measures aim to provide a structured support system, moving beyond symbolic gestures to offer tangible assistance and opportunities for children facing difficult circumstances across Mali.

Why it mattersChildren orphaned by terrorist attacks or abandoned are directly affected by these new state protection measures, receiving institutional support for their well-being and future.

BMS-SA Deposits Exceed One Thousand Billion FCFA

The Malian bank climbed to 13th place regionally for savings collection in 2025.

The Banque Malienne de Solidarité (BMS-SA) has solidified its position in the West African financial landscape, collecting one thousand thirteen billion FCFA in deposits in 2025. This achievement moved the bank from 18th to 13th place regionally. The bank's total balance sheet also grew to two thousand thirty-six billion FCFA, maintaining its seventh position within the UEMOA.

BMS-SA's financial performance was highlighted in the UMOA Banking Commission's annual report for 2025. The bank's total assets increased from one thousand seven hundred twelve billion FCFA in the previous year. In terms of lending, BMS-SA maintained its twelfth position in the UEMOA, with financial commitments totaling nine hundred ninety-six billion FCFA.

Malian banking sector strength is further demonstrated by other institutions' performance. BDM-SA ranked eleventh in the UEMOA with a balance sheet of one thousand seven hundred twenty-four billion FCFA, and BNDA ranked twenty-third with one thousand forty-seven billion FCFA. These collective results indicate the Malian banking system's capacity to provide liquidity and support development projects.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by BMS-SA's increased deposit collection and overall balance sheet growth, indicating financial stability and capacity for lending.

Malian Army Strikes Armed Groups Near Abéïbara and Sévaré

Malian armed forces conducted airstrikes on August 24 and 25 against armed groups in Abéïbara and northeast of Sévaré.

Malian armed forces, supported by partners, carried out airstrikes on August 24 and 25 targeting armed groups in Abéïbara and northeast of Sévaré. The operations, part of the Dougoukoloko operation, followed intelligence gathering that identified armed groups' positions in Abéïbara, Kidal region, and near Sévaré, Mopti region. These positions reportedly held significant war materials and armed vehicles. The military command stated that surveillance and reconnaissance continue to assess the impact of the strikes.

The strikes aimed to neutralize armed groups active in the country, with specific targets identified through precise intelligence. In Abéïbara, positions holding war materials and armed vehicles were successfully engaged on August 24. The following day, a similar gathering of armed elements and equipment was targeted approximately 34 kilometers northeast of Sévaré. The General Staff of the Armed Forces is currently evaluating the results and monitoring for residual threats.

These military actions occur amidst ongoing attacks against military positions, including a recent assault on the 23rd Infantry Regiment camp in San on August 9, which was claimed by the Group for Support of Islam and Muslims. The General Staff of the Armed Forces has also issued a call for irregular military personnel to rejoin their units by September 6, emphasizing discipline and cohesion within the national forces.

Why it mattersThe Malian armed forces are conducting airstrikes against armed groups, impacting security and potentially leading to further military operations.

Mali Imam's Arrest in Algeria Unconfirmed by Officials

A viral TikTok video claims Imam Mahmoud Dicko was arrested in Algiers, but no official sources or close associates confirm the report.

A widely shared TikTok publication alleges that Imam Mahmoud Dicko was arrested upon arrival at Algiers' Houari Boumédiène International Airport and placed under house arrest by Algerian authorities. The video, which had over one hundred thirty-seven thousand views and thousands of interactions at the time of verification, also suggested a possible deportation of the imam back to Mali. However, Studio Tamani's editorial team has found no official confirmation of these claims.

Following inquiries and verification efforts, no official sources consulted by our newsroom have corroborated the arrest of Mahmoud Dicko in Algiers. Contact with individuals close to the imam also yielded no confirmation of his arrest or placement under surveillance. Consequently, there is currently no evidence to substantiate the assertions made in the social media post.

The report emphasizes that the number of views or interactions on social media does not validate the information presented. In the absence of official confirmation or reliable sources to support the allegations, the publication should be approached with caution. Verifying the origin of viral information and seeking confirmation from credible sources is advised before sharing.

Why it mattersThe unconfirmed arrest of Imam Mahmoud Dicko in Algeria could affect Malian citizens' perceptions of regional stability and travel.

Kolokani Prefecture Issues First Motorcycle License Plates

Authorities in Mali's Kolokani circle have begun distributing official license plates for two- and three-wheeled vehicles to improve traffic management.

The Prefecture of Kolokani hosted a ceremony on Wednesday, August 26, 2026, marking a significant step in the identification and regularization of two- and three-wheeled vehicles within the circle. The first license plates were officially handed over to recipients in the presence of administrative and communal authorities, technical services, defense and security forces, and civil society representatives. This operation aims to enhance vehicle identification and improve traffic organization.

Presided over by Kolokani Prefect TOURÉ Fanta SAMAKÉ, the event emphasized the importance of user responsibility and adherence to administrative procedures and traffic rules. The license plates are intended not only as an administrative formality but also to facilitate better identification of circulating vehicles and contribute to more efficient traffic management. The initiative seeks to raise awareness among owners and drivers about their responsibilities on the road.

Two- and three-wheeled vehicles are crucial for daily transportation in many localities, facilitating mobility across districts and villages. Their widespread use necessitates administrative oversight for better visibility of circulating vehicles. The introduction of license plates provides competent services with an additional tool for their missions, contributing to a more organized traffic system within the circle.

Why it mattersResidents of Kolokani circle who own two- or three-wheeled vehicles are directly affected by this new licensing requirement, which aims to improve road safety and administrative oversight.
