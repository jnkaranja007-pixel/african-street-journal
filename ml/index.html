Education · 2026-10-30

Malian higher education paralysed by strike

Teachers demand a research bonus promised nearly a decade ago, halting classes nationwide.

Higher education institutions across Mali are paralyzed by a strike of teacher-researchers. The industrial action began on October 26 and is scheduled to continue until the end of this week. The national union for the sector, CEN-SNESUP, called the strike.

CEN-SNESUP is demanding the payment of a research bonus that was promised almost 10 years ago but has never been disbursed. This has led to the complete shutdown of academic activities in universities and other higher learning facilities.

The strike affects students and faculty in all higher education institutions throughout Mali. The union's action highlights a long-standing grievance regarding unfulfilled financial promises to academic staff.

Why it mattersStudents and faculty in Mali's higher education system are directly affected by the strike over unpaid research bonuses.

Bamako families fear for missing relatives amid rising murders

Police investigate three murder cases while families search for missing loved ones, with one student missing since October 12.

Vivid concern grips Bamako as disappearances and murders become almost weekly occurrences, with social media relaying search notices. While some missing individuals are found alive, others are discovered dead, leaving families distressed and searching for answers.

Habibatou Boncana Maïga, a 21-year-old student, vanished from her home on October 12, and her father reports no progress from police in locating her phone. Separately, Bocar Ly, a young man reported missing, was found dead, and another young person's body was discovered in Sébénikoro days earlier.

Authorities have arrested suspects in the murders of Broulaye Doumbia, Moctar Fofana, and Abocar Ly, with motives reportedly including homosexuality, attempted rape, and a vehicle sale dispute. Sociologist Mohamed Eglass suggests improved education and family vigilance could help prevent such tragedies.

Why it mattersFamilies in Bamako face distress and uncertainty due to the ongoing disappearances and murders of their relatives.

Sport · 2026-10-30

AS Réal Bamako draws CAF Confederation Cup group stage opponents

The Malian club will face USM Alger, CS Sfaxien, and Black Bulls in group D.

AS Réal of Bamako learned its opponents for the 2026-2027 CAF Confederation Cup group stage after a draw on Wednesday, October 28, in Cairo, Egypt. The Scorpions were placed in Group D, where they will compete against two former competition winners and a Mozambican team. Their adversaries are USM Alger, CS Sfaxien, and the Black Bulls.

This draw marks AS Réal's return to this stage of the competition for the first time since the 2022-2023 season. They will face defending champions USM Alger, who also won in 2023, and three-time winners CS Sfaxien (2007, 2008, 2013). The Black Bulls of Mozambique complete the group. AS Réal qualified by eliminating ES Zarzis of Tunisia with a 5-3 aggregate score after a 3-1 away win and a 2-2 home draw.

The group stage matches are scheduled to take place from November 27, 2026, to January 24, 2027. AS Réal aims to finish in the top two of Group D to advance to the quarterfinals, a step up from their third-place finish in the 2022-2023 season. The club will need consistent performances in their six matches, three at home and three away, to achieve this goal.

Why it mattersAS Réal of Bamako will compete in the group stage of the CAF Confederation Cup, facing experienced continental clubs.

Agriculture · 2026-10-30

Mali army and Russian corps kill over 200 livestock

More than 200 cattle and sheep were killed by the Malian army and Africa Corps near Diéma on October 28.

Malian soldiers and their Russian Africa Corps partners killed over 200 cattle and sheep on Wednesday, October 28, in Mangara, Dioumara commune, Diéma district, Nioro du Sahel region.

Habitations were also burned in the incident, the second of its kind in eight days in an area known for jihadist presence. The livestock represented the sole livelihood for local herding communities.

The jihadists of Jnim, linked to al-Qaïda, are known to operate in the region.

Why it mattersHerding communities in the Nioro du Sahel region lost their sole means of subsistence when over 200 livestock were killed.
