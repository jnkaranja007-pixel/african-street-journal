Power cuts persist in Mali’s Douentza and Macina despite Bamako improvement

Fuel shortages have plunged Douentza into darkness since Monday, forcing artisans to spend more on diesel generators while food sellers lose stock.

Source: Studio Tamani · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Electricity supply remains unstable in several Malian regions even as conditions improve in the capital, Bamako. In Douentza, the town has been without power since Monday, 23 November, due to a fuel shortage, according to the local head of the energy agency. Similar persistent outages affect Macina in Ségou region and Ménaka in the north, where access has been irregular for over two years because of generator failures and supply difficulties.

The blackouts are disrupting economic activity and raising costs for small businesses. Oumar Ouologuem, a welder in Douentza, said buying diesel for his generator is financially difficult, though any electricity would help. Hawa Cissé, who sells traditional drinks like yogurt and ginger juice, reported losing merchandise that requires refrigeration. Moussa Dem, a member of civil society in Macina, warned that the lack of cold storage threatens food security and increases insecurity for fishmongers and butchers.

Authorities in Douentza stated that measures are underway to resupply the town with fuel in the coming days. Residents in these areas continue to call for durable solutions to the energy crisis, noting that women in rural areas rely heavily on electricity for income-generating activities.

Why it mattersTraders and artisans in affected towns must now pay extra for diesel to run generators or accept losses from spoiled goods, impacting their daily income.
