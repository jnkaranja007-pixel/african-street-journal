Mali's SNESUP split deepens as faction keeps university strike going

Teachers rejecting the stability pact say they will continue the walkout until the government settles their research-bonus claim.

Sources: Studio Tamani and Journal du Mali · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

A faction of Mali’s National Union of Higher Education and Scientific Research (SNESUP) is continuing its strike over research bonuses, while another faction has urged teachers to resume classes. The split was made public on Thursday, November 19, as the government said the claim should be addressed through a review of the 1975 ordinance governing bonuses and allowances.

Dr Oumar Coulibaly, who leads the faction that did not sign the stability pact, said its members would maintain the strike until they receive the research bonus. Public-service workers in other groups had received bonuses since 2020, he said, while teachers were being directed to the legal review. The government says that review is needed to establish a legal basis for bonuses.

The separate union faction that signed the pact rejected the strike and called for classes to resume. The walkout has affected teaching, assessments and scientific work at public higher-education and research institutions, while students nearing graduation face disruption to university calendars.

Why it mattersTeachers and students at public higher-education institutions face continued disruption to classes, assessments and research while the union remains divided.
