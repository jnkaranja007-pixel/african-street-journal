Mali eye hospital treats more than 300 displaced people for cataracts

The Bamako hospital also received specialist equipment worth more than CFA250 million, funded by Spain through its development agency and a foundation partner.

Source: Mali Actu · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

The University Hospital of the Institute of Tropical Ophthalmology of Africa (CHU-IOTA) in Bamako has operated on more than 300 internally displaced people with cataracts, against an initial target of 500 patients, health officials and technical partners said at a ceremony on Thursday 26 November 2026.

The ceremony, chaired by Abdoulaye Guindo, secretary general of the Ministry of Health and Social Development, was attended by the hospital's director general, Adama Issaka Guindo. Support came from Spain's development agency, the Spanish Agency for International Development Cooperation (AECID), and the Les Yeux du Monde Foundation, a partner since 2008. The equipment, valued above CFA250 million, includes 8 000 surgical kits, a 90 kVA standalone solar system, 10 slit lamps and an anaesthesia ventilator with a multiparameter monitor.

Spain mobilised one million euros in total, and the partnership trained around 15 practitioners. Public health specialists quoted in the report say the free procedures for the most disadvantaged patients integrate displaced people into the wider health system.

Why it mattersDisplaced cataract patients gained free surgery, and the hospital gained solar power for its operating theatres, which it previously lacked.
