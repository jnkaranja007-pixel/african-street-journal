Mali journalists' murder case sees partial declassification

A French court seeks to declassify a note from April 2024 related to the 2013 killings.

Source: Journal du Mali · 2026-11-03 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

13 years after the murder of journalists Ghislaine Dupont and Claude Verlon in Kidal, Mali, the judicial investigation in France continues. The case involves kidnapping, sequestration, and assassination linked to terrorism. On April 15, 2026, the French Commission for National Defence Secrecy partially approved declassifying a note from the French Ministry of Armed Forces dated April 24, 2024, following a request from investigating judge Valéry Muller.

This decision marks the fourth declassification procedure in the case, with previous ones in 2016, 2017, and 2023. No trial has yet established full responsibility for the deaths. Seidane Ag Hitta is named in the investigation as a suspected instigator, identified by Malian authorities as a leader in the Group to Support Islam and Muslims in the Kidal region. A reward of 500 million FCFA was offered in 2026 for information leading to his arrest.

Civil parties in the case are seeking international arrest warrants for Seidane Ag Hitta and Hamadi Ag Mohamed, also cited as a suspected member of the commando unit. The ongoing investigations aim to determine the individual roles of those implicated in the kidnapping and assassination of the two journalists.

Why it mattersThe investigation into the 2013 murders of two journalists in Mali is progressing with partial declassification of documents, potentially leading to accountability.
