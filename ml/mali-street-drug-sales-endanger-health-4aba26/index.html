Mali street drug sales endanger health

Health professionals warn of treatment failures and poisonings from unregulated medicines sold without prescriptions.

Source: Studio Tamani · 2026-10-31 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

In Bamako and other major Malian cities, the high cost of healthcare and medicines drives some people to buy unregulated drugs from street vendors. This practice, known as "pharmacie par terre," exposes consumers to serious health risks, including ineffective treatments and poisonings, according to health professionals.

General practitioner Harouna Doumbia stated that financial hardship, vendor proximity, and easy, prescription-free purchases are key reasons for this trend. Pharmacist Bréhima Sidibé agreed, citing high drug prices and a lack of public awareness about the dangers. Street vendor Aly Keita confirmed that antibiotics like amoxicillin are frequently requested, with prices ranging from 25 to 500 CFA francs.

Dr. Doumbia warned that unknown drug compositions can cause poisoning and organ damage, while under-dosed or unsuitable medications can lead to treatment failure and delayed diagnosis. He noted particular concern for malaria and infection treatments, where substandard drugs could worsen conditions and lead to severe illness.

Why it mattersMalian citizens are risking their health by purchasing unregulated medicines on the street due to the high cost of formal healthcare.
