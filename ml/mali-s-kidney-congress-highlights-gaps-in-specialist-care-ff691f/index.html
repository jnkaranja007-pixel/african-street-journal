Mali’s kidney congress highlights gaps in specialist care and prevention

The meeting cited five dialysis centres but said the country lacks operational peritoneal dialysis and kidney-transplant services.

Source: Mali Actu · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Mali’s second International Congress of Nephrology opened in Bamako on Thursday, 26 November, bringing together African experts, researchers and technical partners to discuss chronic kidney disease. The meeting highlighted five dialysis centres in Bamako and the regions, alongside a dedicated master’s programme, and called for health services to adapt to rising non-communicable diseases.

Mali’s nephrology society president, Magara Samaké, said 60% of people in the country with chronic kidney failure are aged 20 to 40. The Global Kidney Health Atlas 2025 reports shortages of paediatric nephrologists and specialist surgeons, and says Mali has no operational peritoneal dialysis or kidney-transplant pathways.

The African nephrology association president cited a World Health Assembly resolution adopted on 10 February 2025 that calls for wider early screening, including creatinine and albuminuria tests, at local health facilities. The congress also identified organ transplantation and stronger national registers as priorities.

Why it mattersHealth planners must address specialist shortages and expand early screening as 60% of Mali’s chronic kidney failure patients are aged 20 to 40.
