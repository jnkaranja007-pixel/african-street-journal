Mali activist Clément Dembélé marks three years in jail despite being cleared

A judge ordered his release several times, but the anti-cybercrime prosecutor opposed each order and the Supreme Court refused release in August.

Source: RFI Afrique · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Clément Dembélé, an anti-corruption activist, has been in prison in Mali for exactly three years as of Tuesday, 17 November. He was accused of threatening Transition President General Assimi Goïta in a recording that circulated on social media.

A technical expertise quickly cleared Dembélé of the charge. A judge ordered his release on several occasions, but the prosecutor of the anti-cybercrime unit, which has a feared reputation, opposed each decision. The Supreme Court itself rejected his release request in August.

Eduardo Gonzalez, the independent expert appointed by the United Nations on human rights in Mali, has taken up the case.

Why it mattersHis continued detention means a man the expert evidence has cleared stays behind bars, and the courts have not yet set him free.
