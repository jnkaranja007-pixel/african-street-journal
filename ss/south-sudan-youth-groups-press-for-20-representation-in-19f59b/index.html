South Sudan youth groups press for 20% representation in government

An assessment found little improvement from last year, while the country has no legislature in place for a new parliamentary count.

Source: Radio Tamazuj · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

Youth-led organisations in South Sudan called on Friday for greater representation in government, saying young people remain underrepresented in leadership and decision-making. Florence Agiba, executive director of youth organisation Markaz Al-Salam, said a recent assessment found little improvement from last year, despite progress in some states.

Agiba cited Unity State, where four young people had been appointed ministers. The latest assessment excluded parliament because South Sudan has no legislature in place; in the previous assessment, young people made up about 4.81% of parliamentarians, or approximately 23 of 550 members. Markaz Al-Salam is preparing a fact sheet with support from Norwegian People’s Aid and the National Youth Union.

Agiba called for youth representation to reach at least 20%. Michael Gorjin Kuol of Norwegian People’s Aid proposed a dialogue with political parties and election bodies, while counsel Izale Benjamin said many young people do not know the National Youth Policy provides for 20% representation.

Why it mattersYouth organisations must take their case to political parties and government institutions, with Agiba calling for representation to reach at least 20%.
