South Sudan adviser says civil servants work unpaid for months or years

Joseph Ngere Paciko said workers continue to report for duty and cover daily expenses themselves despite prolonged salary delays.

Sources: Eye Radio and Radio Tamazuj · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

South Sudan’s presidential adviser on governance, Joseph Ngere Paciko, said civil servants continue reporting to work despite going months or even years without salaries. He spoke on Wednesday, November 25, at the close of a two-day China-South Sudan Governance Roundtable in Juba.

Paciko said workers use their own money for daily expenses while they keep working. He contrasted their experience with neighbouring countries, saying three months without pay there would bring widespread unrest. He also cautioned that workers’ attendance did not mean they were satisfied with their circumstances.

Paciko said governance is a system of rules, processes and structures for directing and holding a group accountable, and that the principles apply in settings including families, churches and communities.

Why it mattersCivil servants continue to spend their own money to meet daily expenses while working through salary delays that can last months or years.
