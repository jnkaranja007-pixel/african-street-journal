South Sudan state seeks leads after more than 200 cattle go missing

The herd was attacked near Torit after being moved from Kapoeta to escape drought and food shortages in their grazing areas.

Source: Radio Tamazuj · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

More than 200 cattle belonging to Eastern Equatoria caretaker governor Louis Lobong Lojore remain missing after a raid near Torit at about 4 p.m. on Tuesday. The animals had been brought from Kapoeta in search of better pasture, after drought and food shortages in their original grazing areas.

Siro Silvio Ewak, the state government's deputy secretary-general, said the herd had once numbered more than 1,000, but many animals died from poor health and inadequate pasture. Suspected raiders drove the cattle towards Osile Hill, and investigators have followed their tracks towards Lolianga village, though it is unclear whether they entered it. Ewak urged residents to give security authorities credible information, saying fear of retaliation could deter reporting.

Margaret Itiara, a civil society representative in Eastern Equatoria, said preliminary information indicated the attackers ambushed the herd and injured one person. She urged cattle owners to invest proceeds from livestock sales in small businesses.

Why it mattersThe state government must obtain information from surrounding villages to locate the missing cattle and identify those responsible.
