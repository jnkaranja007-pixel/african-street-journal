South Sudan rehabilitates 44-kilometre road linking Magwi and Palotaka

The project began in October, with completion expected in December, to improve farmers’ access to markets in Eastern Equatoria State.

Sources: Eye Radio and Sudans Post · 2026-11-24 · 1 min read

South Sudan began rehabilitating a 44-kilometre feeder road linking Magwi and Palotaka in Eastern Equatoria State in October 2026, with completion expected in December. The project is financed by the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD) and the Government of the Netherlands, with implementation support from the United Nations Office for Project Services (UNOPS).

The road’s progress was assessed on November 21 by a government committee and national and state officials alongside IFAD and UNOPS. Agriculture ministry undersecretary Anthony Majak Achuoth said better road links would help farming communities transport produce to markets. IFAD Country Director Caroline Mwongera said rural roads would also help farmers reach services and expand their livelihoods.

The road is part of the government-led South Sudan Livelihoods Resilience Program, which targets 61,808 vulnerable households, or about 370,848 people, across eight counties. Government and development partners urged communities to protect and maintain the rehabilitated road.

Why it mattersFarmers along the route are expected to gain market access when the 44-kilometre road is completed in December.
