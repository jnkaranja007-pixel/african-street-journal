Doctors' charity spotlights four-hour walk for South Sudan rape survivor

A woman identified as Nyawura said she walked to a health facility the morning after armed men raped her on her way home.

Source: Eye Radio · 2026-11-27 · 1 min read

Médecins Sans Frontières, the international medical charity known as MSF, has highlighted the obstacles survivors of sexual violence face in reaching care in South Sudan. In testimony published on Thursday for its 16 Days of Activism campaign against gender-based violence, a woman identified by the pseudonym Nyawura said armed men raped her as she returned home after collecting firewood.

Nyawura said she could not find transport after the attack, so she left home at around 4 a.m. the next morning and walked four hours to a health facility. There, health workers gave her medication to help prevent infections, and she said she received good care.

The charity says its health facilities offer medical services to survivors of sexual and gender-based violence. Its testimony was released as part of the campaign.
