South Sudan rules out forced evictions as it maps six national parks

The government will consult residents, verify land claims and negotiate any relocation under proposed guidance for protected areas.

Sources: Radio Tamazuj and Sudans Post · 2026-11-25 · 1 min read

South Sudan will negotiate with communities living inside its six national parks before considering relocation, the Ministry of Wildlife Conservation and Tourism said. The government is developing guidance to set park boundaries and address settlement expansion into protected areas.

Martin Ring Malek, the ministry’s assistant director general for administration and finance, said authorities would first map boundaries and check whether residents had historical land claims. People who say they inherited land before a park was established must provide evidence, he said. The government has not established how many people live in the parks.

The verification will involve the ministries responsible for wildlife, land and environment, as well as the Land Commission, which will provide ownership documents and maps. Officials may adjust boundaries where evidence supports communities’ claims.

Why it mattersResidents claiming inherited land must provide evidence, and officials will use Land Commission records and maps before deciding whether relocation is needed.
