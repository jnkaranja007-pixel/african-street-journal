Norway urges South Sudan leaders to pursue dialogue and better governance

Ambassador Roar Haugsdal said emergency aid alone cannot resolve a crisis driven largely by political choices and conflict.

Source: Eye Radio · 2026-11-23 · 1 min read

Norway urged South Sudan’s political leaders to pursue dialogue, strengthen accountability and improve governance as Norwegian People’s Aid (NPA) marked 40 years of work in the country. Norwegian Ambassador Roar Haugsdal said humanitarian assistance alone cannot resolve the crisis, which he attributed largely to political decisions and conflict.

Haugsdal said the organisation, which began operating in South Sudan in 1986, had supported communities through humanitarian aid, development and peace-building. He called for political solutions, trusted public institutions and better public services, including payment of salaries to nurses, doctors, teachers and other civil servants.

He also urged leaders to ensure inclusive governance, respect for the rule of law and civic participation, saying South Sudan’s future should be shaped by its own people. NPA has also supported local organisations working on equal rights, housing, land and property access, and resource management.

Why it mattersNurses, doctors, teachers and other civil servants are among those whose salaries Haugsdal says must be paid as leaders address governance and public services.
