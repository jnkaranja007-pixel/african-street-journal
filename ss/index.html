South Sudan struggles with voluntary blood donations

The World Health Organization warns that low voluntary blood donation rates risk patient lives due to unstable supply.

South Sudan faces significant challenges in transitioning from family and replacement blood donors to voluntary contributions, according to the World Health Organization (WHO). Low voluntary non-remunerated blood donations, ranging from 7.6 to 34 percent, force health facilities to rely on family members during urgent patient needs. This dependency creates an unstable supply, potentially endangering lives, especially for women in childbirth, anemic patients, trauma victims, and those undergoing surgery.

A national assessment identified critical issues hindering blood transfusion services, including inadequate infrastructure, poor storage capacity, unreliable electricity, and weak cold-chain systems. Shortages of essential equipment, insufficient trained personnel, weak quality management, and fragmented paper-based data systems further complicate efforts. The Muslim community in Juba has been noted as a primary source of voluntary donations, with mosques being the most reliable source for restocking the national blood bank.

The National Blood Transfusion Service (NBTS) aims to collect 160,000 units annually, but current voluntary donations only meet about 34 percent of this target. This deficit means lives are lost due to insufficient blood supply. Health officials are urging all faith groups, civil society, and citizens to participate in regular voluntary blood drives to bridge the gap and ensure adequate blood availability for medical needs.

Why it mattersPatients requiring blood transfusions in South Sudan are at risk of death due to the insufficient and unstable supply of voluntary donations.

Communal clashes kill 49 in South Sudan cattle camp

Renewed fighting erupted Thursday morning at a cattle camp in Tonj North County, leaving dozens dead and wounded, according to local authorities.

At least forty-nine people were killed and thirty-three others injured in renewed communal clashes in Tonj North County, Warrap State, local officials reported. The violence occurred around 5:00 AM on Thursday at the Machar-Pakuek cattle camp and Mading Juer Boma in Majak Payam. William Wol Mayom of the Warrap State Ministry confirmed the casualties.

In a separate incident, two people were killed in an ambush on the outskirts of Jebel Boma County, according to the Minister of Information for the Greater Pibor Administrative Area. Unidentified attackers ambushed and killed the two individuals before fleeing the scene. Security forces have been dispatched to investigate the attack and identify the perpetrators.

Preliminary investigations into the Jebel Boma ambush suggest the attackers may have come from the direction of Kuron in Kapoeta East County, Eastern Equatoria State. Authorities in Kapoeta East County and the Eastern Equatoria State government have not yet commented on the incident. The circumstances surrounding both attacks remain unknown.

Why it mattersCommunal clashes and ambushes in Tonj North and Jebel Boma counties have resulted in deaths and injuries, disrupting peace and safety.

South Sudan Minister Attends AU Security Summit in Luanda

Foreign Affairs Minister Dr. James Pitia Morgan represents South Sudan at a two-day summit focused on conflict prevention and continental security.

Foreign Affairs Minister Dr. James Pitia Morgan departed Juba on Saturday for Luanda, Angola, to attend an African Union summit on conflict prevention and continental security. He will represent South Sudan at the gathering, which includes over 10 African heads of state and runs from August 29 to 30.

The summit is part of the 21st Extraordinary Session of the Assembly of the African Union, convened by Angolan President João Manuel Gonçalves Lourenço. Delegates will review the continent’s political, legal, and financial dispute resolution systems, aiming to agree on practical measures to enhance emergency response capabilities. Discussions will cover early warning systems, funding for peace operations, and strategies for combating terrorism.

The meeting takes place amid ongoing instability across various regions, including the Sahel, the Great Lakes, Sudan, the Horn of Africa, and parts of southern Africa. The Executive Council of ministers met on August 29, preceding the main Heads of State and Government session on August 30.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan's participation in the AU security summit directly affects its engagement with continental efforts to resolve disputes and improve emergency response capabilities.

Prosecutors Deny Singling Out Machar in Special Court Trial

The prosecution team at the Special Court in Juba stated their counter-application aims for procedural clarity, not unequal treatment of defendants.

Prosecutors in Juba denied on Friday that their counter-application in the ongoing Special Court trial seeks to single out suspended First Vice President Dr. Riek Machar. Lead prosecutor Ajo Ony’Ohisa argued at the 113th proceeding that the application aims to clarify and apply correct statutory procedures equally to all remaining defendants. The prosecution stated it does not intend to invalidate or reopen completed examinations, nor subject Machar, the fifth accused, to different treatment.

Ohisa cited Section 197(1) and Section 224(2)(e) of the law, which allow the court to question an accused person on circumstances appearing in evidence. The prosecution's request, he explained, is to ensure these procedures are followed prospectively for defendants yet to undergo this stage. The prosecution asked the court to reject the defence's interpretation, emphasizing that equality under the law, not repetition of error, is the relevant standard.

Machar and seven other defendants are on trial for violence in Nasir, Upper Nile State, in March 2025, which resulted in heavy casualties for government forces. The defence, led by Dr. Geri Raimondo Legge, acknowledged receiving the prosecution’s counter-application but objected to its use. Judge Stephen Simon adjourned proceedings until September 7, 2026, to rule on the defence’s application for clarification on legal representation and the prosecution’s counter-application.

Why it mattersThe legal proceedings affect suspended First Vice President Dr. Riek Machar and seven co-accused, potentially impacting their trial process and outcomes.

South Sudan Mothers Breastfeed Babies at 71% Rate

UNICEF and health-sector data indicate that only 71% of South Sudanese mothers exclusively breastfeed their infants for the first six months.

Only 71% of mothers in South Sudan exclusively breastfeed their babies during the first six months of life, according to UNICEF and health-sector data. The 2025 MICS survey found that 76% of mothers initiate breastfeeding within one hour of birth.

This rate of exclusive breastfeeding falls short of optimal infant nutrition targets. While a high percentage of mothers begin breastfeeding promptly, maintaining exclusivity for the full six months presents a challenge.

The data highlights a gap in sustained exclusive breastfeeding practices, which are crucial for infant health and development. Further efforts may be needed to support mothers in continuing this practice throughout the critical early months.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan's mothers are affected by a 71% rate of exclusive breastfeeding, impacting infant health outcomes.
