Red Cross Distributes Aid to Displaced South Sudanese

Humanitarian assistance reaches 1,700 households in Kajo-Keji County, providing food and support to those displaced by recent border clashes and returnees from Uganda.

The International Committee of the Red Cross and South Sudan Red Cross began distributing humanitarian aid in Kajo-Keji County on Wednesday. The assistance targets returnees from Ugandan refugee camps and individuals displaced by recent fighting between South Sudanese and Ugandan armies. A total of 1,700 out of 2,600 targeted households have received food donations, with aid deliveries completed in Mangalatore, Keriwa, and Sokare.

The distribution follows mid-July clashes where UPDF troops reportedly crossed into South Sudan, leading to fatalities and the displacement of an estimated 16,000 civilians. Humanitarian agencies are also rehabilitating water boreholes and offering protection and family-linking services, including phone airtime for separated families. Community leaders expressed gratitude for the relief efforts, noting significant logistical delays due to poor road conditions.

Beneficiaries highlighted that current aid allocations per family often fall short for larger households, with one recipient noting a family of 14 receiving only one carpet. They called for aid distribution to be adjusted based on verified family sizes. The support is seen as timely, especially for vulnerable children, women, and the elderly, bringing hope and dignity to the affected communities.

Why it mattersDisplaced individuals and returnees in Kajo-Keji County are receiving essential food aid and support services, addressing immediate needs following conflict and displacement.

Foreign Envoys Urge Dialogue; South Sudan Summons Ambassadors

Diplomats called for revived political dialogue and accountability, prompting the foreign ministry to summon the envoys over an "inappropriate and divisive" statement.

Four foreign ambassadors have urged South Sudan's leaders to restart political dialogue and strengthen accountability, following a meeting with Vice President Rebecca Nyandeng. The diplomats from Germany, Japan, Norway, and the United States stated that the country's path to peace requires "urgent action" and the abandonment of violence for resolving political differences. They also emphasized that public revenues should be directed towards services and accountability, which they believe would foster private investment, security, and the rule of law.

The South Sudanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation has summoned these four ambassadors, labeling their joint statement "inappropriate and divisive." Minister Dr. James Pitia Morgan expressed concern that the statement was particularly troubling as the nation prepares for elections. He stressed the importance of national ownership and adherence to diplomatic norms, including non-interference in internal affairs, suggesting sensitive political matters should be handled through proper diplomatic channels.

The diplomatic exchange follows an unusual public dispute between Vice President Nyandeng and President Salva Kiir's office. Nyandeng had publicly questioned the current leadership's oversight and the impact of senior government positions on ordinary citizens. The presidency responded by defending Kiir's role in the liberation struggle and suggesting that those dissatisfied with the government should present their case to voters in the upcoming elections.

Why it mattersInvestors may be affected as the call for dialogue and accountability, alongside a public dispute between top officials, could impact stability and the conditions for private investment ahead of elections.

South Sudan Promises Probe After UN Convoy Ambush

Two Ethiopian peacekeepers were killed and seven injured in an attack on a UN peacekeeping convoy in Jonglei State on Monday, August 24.

The South Sudanese government has pledged to investigate and prosecute those responsible for an ambush on a United Nations peacekeeping convoy in Jonglei State. The attack resulted in the deaths of two Ethiopian peacekeepers and injuries to seven others. The convoy, consisting of eight vehicles, was part of the UN's peacekeeping mission in the region.

The incident occurred on Monday, August 24, as the UN force-protection convoy traveled through Jonglei State. Details regarding the specific armed actors involved remain unclear, but the government has committed to identifying and bringing them to justice. This event highlights ongoing security challenges in the area.

In a separate incident in Warrap State, 49 people were killed and 33 wounded in a cattle raid in Tonj North County. The Warrap State government blamed armed criminals from Unity State for the attack, which targeted a cattle camp. Authorities are appealing for humanitarian aid to address the displacement caused by the violence.

Why it mattersThe attacks on the UN convoy and the cattle raid in Warrap State create insecurity and humanitarian crises, affecting livelihoods and potentially disrupting aid delivery.

Japan, WFP Sign $2.2 Million Food Aid Deal for South Sudan

The agreement aims to provide critical nutrition assistance to nearly 40,000 vulnerable women and children facing acute malnutrition.

Japan has provided 350 million yen, equivalent to $2.2 million, to the UN World Food Programme (WFP) for lifesaving food and nutrition aid in South Sudan. The funding will support approximately 40,000 women and children in areas severely affected by acute malnutrition, including Greater Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap, and parts of Greater Bahr el Ghazal.

The contribution comes as over 7.8 million people in South Sudan face acute food insecurity, with a warning that over 222,000 children and pregnant or breastfeeding women could lose access to humanitarian aid from October without urgent support. WFP Country Director Mutinta Chimuka stated that rising needs are stretching the agency's resources, highlighting the critical concern of malnutrition among women and children.

The Japanese government urged South Sudan's transitional government and all parties to prioritize food and nutrition security, ensure peace, and guarantee safe humanitarian access. The Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management welcomed the Japanese contribution while appealing for additional funding from other donors to prevent a lapse in essential food assistance for vulnerable populations.

Why it mattersThe funding will help provide critical nutrition assistance to nearly 40,000 women and children suffering from acute malnutrition.

Machar defence disputes prosecution's questioning method

Lawyers for the suspended First Vice President and co-defendants argue that direct questioning could prevent them from fully explaining evidence in court.

The defence team for suspended First Vice President Dr. Riek Machar and seven co-accused argued on Wednesday that prosecutors' proposed method of questioning witnesses could limit the defendants' ability to fully explain evidence. The argument took place during the 112th session of the special court in Juba, where prosecutors had filed a counter-application seeking to question defendants on prosecution evidence before they submit written defence statements.

Lead defence lawyer Dr. Geri Raimondo Legge told the court that defendants should have ample opportunity to present their evidence without restrictive questioning. He noted that four defendants had already submitted written testimonies and urged the court to dismiss the prosecution's application. Legge also clarified that a previous defence application seeking safeguards for legal representation was not intended to challenge the court's authority but to ensure clarity and prevent mistrust.

Lead prosecutor Ajo Onyi’ Ohisa acknowledged receiving the defence's submissions and requested more time to prepare a written response. The court, presided over by Judge Stephen Simon Binjigwa, adjourned proceedings until Friday, August 28, to allow the prosecution to respond. Machar and the others face charges including treason and murder stemming from violence in Nasir in March 2025.

Why it mattersThe legal proceedings could impact the political stability and future of suspended First Vice President Riek Machar and his co-defendants, who face serious charges.
