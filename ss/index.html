Red Cross distributes aid to Kajo-Keji returnees and displaced residents

The International Committee of the Red Cross and South Sudan Red Cross began distributing humanitarian aid to 1,700 households in Kajo-Keji County, targeting returnees.

The International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) and South Sudan Red Cross (SSRC) started distributing humanitarian assistance in Kajo-Keji County’s Liwolo County on Wednesday. This aid targets returnees from Ugandan refugee camps and locals displaced by clashes between the armies of South Sudan and Uganda last month. The distribution has reached Mangalatore, Keriwa, and Sokare, with 1,700 of 2,600 targeted households receiving food donations.

Renewed fighting in mid-July 2026, when UPDF troops reportedly crossed into South Sudanese territory, attacked positions in Merwa, Gorbeleng, and the Andejoga region. These skirmishes, along with prior 2025 incidents, caused multiple fatalities, including SSPDF soldiers and local security personnel, and displaced an estimated 16,000 civilians who sought refuge in schools, churches, and areas like Sokare.

Joseph Lukak, SSRC Head of Branch in Juba, confirmed the ongoing distribution. Beneficiaries like Lokole Chaplain noted that flat-rate allocations often fall short for large households, with families of up to 14 members receiving inadequate shelter and supplies. Humanitarian agencies are also rehabilitating critical infrastructure like broken water boreholes and offering protection and family-links services, including free mobile phone airtime to help separated families reconnect across borders.

Why it mattersThe distribution of aid directly affects the 1,700 households receiving assistance, providing essential food and supplies to those displaced by conflict and those returning from refugee camps.

South Sudan government promises probe after deadly attack on UN convoy

The South Sudan government has pledged to investigate and prosecute those responsible for an ambush on a United Nations peacekeeping convoy in Jonglei State.

The South Sudan government has promised to track down and prosecute armed actors behind a deadly ambush on a United Nations peacekeeping convoy in Jonglei State. The attack, which occurred on Monday, August twenty-fourth, left two Ethiopian peacekeepers dead and seven others injured.

The incident involved an eight-vehicle force-protection convoy belonging to the United Nations. This commitment to investigate comes as the government addresses security concerns in various regions.

Separately, in Warrap State, 49 people were killed and 33 wounded in a cattle raid in Tonj North County on Thursday at about 5:00 am. William Wol Mayom, the State Minister of Information and Communication, attributed the attack on a cattle camp in Juer Boma to armed criminals from Unity State, though Unity authorities have not responded to the accusation. The Warrap State government has appealed for humanitarian support for those displaced by the conflict.

Why it mattersResidents in Jonglei State face increased insecurity due to armed attacks, while communities in Warrap State are experiencing displacement and loss of life from cattle raids.

Foreign Envoys Urge South Sudan Leaders to Revive Political Dialogue

Ambassadors from Germany, Japan, Norway, and the United States called for urgent inter-party dialogue and an end to political violence after meeting Vice President Rebecca.

Foreign envoys have pressed South Sudan's leaders to restart political dialogue and abandon violence as a tool for political competition. This call came from the ambassadors of Germany, Japan, Norway, and the United States following their August twenty-fifth meeting with Vice President Rebecca Nyandeng de Mabior. In a joint statement issued on Wednesday, the diplomats emphasized that South Sudan's path to peace requires "urgent action," including the initiation of what they termed authentic inter-party dialogue.

The envoys also urged political leaders to use public revenues to provide services and strengthen accountability, stating that such measures would foster conditions for private investment, security, and the rule of law. Their statement follows a public dispute between Vice President Nyandeng and President Salva Kiir's office, which emerged after Nyandeng questioned the current leadership's oversight and the value of senior government positions if they do not improve citizens' lives.

In response to the diplomats' appeal, South Sudan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation issued a statement on Thursday, urging regional and international partners to support peaceful elections scheduled for December. The government reiterated its commitment to implementing the Revitalized Peace Agreement and preparing for the upcoming polls, emphasizing its value for continued international support in advancing peace and the electoral process.

Why it mattersThe call for renewed political dialogue and accountability directly affects South Sudanese citizens by influencing the stability of their government and the potential for improved public services and security.

Japan funds WFP with $2.2 million for South Sudan food aid

The Japanese government and the United Nations World Food Programme signed an agreement to provide lifesaving food and nutrition assistance to vulnerable people in South Sudan.

Japan has contributed 350 million Japanese Yen, equivalent to about $2.2 million, to the World Food Programme (WFP) to deliver nutrition assistance to vulnerable women and children in South Sudan. This funding aims to support nearly 40,000 women and children in areas severely affected by acute malnutrition, including Greater Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap, and parts of Greater Bahr el Ghazal.

The contribution comes as more than 7.8 million people in South Sudan face acute food insecurity, with over 222,000 children and pregnant and breastfeeding women at risk of losing humanitarian assistance from October without urgent support. Conflict, displacement, flooding, rising food prices, and disease outbreaks continue to worsen the humanitarian situation, according to Hiromoto Oyama, Chargé d’Affaires of the Japanese Embassy in South Sudan.

Mutinta Chimuka, WFP Country Director in South Sudan, stated that the agency's resources are increasingly stretched due to rising humanitarian needs. The Japanese support will help WFP provide critical nutrition assistance and focus on preventing malnutrition among mothers and young children. Since 2021, Japan has provided over $37.7 million to WFP's operations in South Sudan.

Why it mattersNearly 40,000 vulnerable women and children in South Sudan will receive critical nutrition assistance, preventing further suffering from acute malnutrition.

Yambio traders decry high taxes, rising commodity prices

Traders in Yambio County expressed concerns over high taxes, a weak local currency, and a lack of affordable trading spaces during a market assessment by state officials.

Traders in Yambio County, Western Equatoria State, reported that high taxes, poor roads, insecurity, and the rising value of the US dollar are hindering their businesses and making it difficult to keep commodity prices affordable. Emmanuel Obama, an Ethiopian trader, stated that high taxes on goods from Nimule contribute to increased prices in Yambio, and the high exchange rate of the US dollar affects businesses nationwide.

A local food vendor, speaking anonymously, noted that declining purchasing power has reduced sales, with many people unable to afford food items due to unpaid salaries and the dollar's impact. Faiza Manrate added that the lack of affordable trading spaces, with some costing up to SSP 700,000 for a table, particularly affects women and traders from rural areas.

Nimeri Tobo of the Yambio Chamber of Commerce welcomed the government's visit, highlighting challenges such as poor roads, roadblocks, high taxes, insecurity, and limited trading spaces. Governor Badagbu Daniel Rimbasa pledged to review the concerns and improve security, cleanliness, market organization, and the overall business environment, urging traders to form cooperatives for support.

Why it mattersTraders in Yambio are directly affected by increased operational costs and reduced sales due to high taxes, currency depreciation, and lack of affordable spaces, impacting their livelihoods.
