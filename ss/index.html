South Sudan's electoral commission moves December vote to February 2027

The National Elections Commission has pushed polling day to February 11, 2027, nearly two months past the December 2026 deadline set in law.

South Sudan's National Elections Commission (NEC) has set February 11, 2027, as the new polling day for the country's general elections, replacing December 22, 2026. The revised calendar, issued by the commission, moves the whole electoral timetable back by nearly two months.

Radio Tamazuj reports that the timetable raises questions about compliance with the National Elections Act, which requires the voter register to be published at least three months before elections. Voter registration runs from November 14 to November 28, with the provisional register due on November 29 and final publication on January 10, 2027.

The campaign period is set to run for 45 days, from December 27 to February 9, 2027, with February 10 as a campaign-free day. Candidate lists are due to be published on January 18, 2027. The timetable also allows 148 days for resolving election disputes, from February 17 to July 15, 2027.

Why it mattersCandidates and voters now face a campaign that starts December 27 and a final candidate list due January 18, 2027.

South Sudan Bar Association to sue Interior Ministry

The association plans legal action over traffic police demanding US dollar fines and high license plate fees for commercial vehicles.

The South Sudan Bar Association will sue the Ministry of the Interior and the Traffic Police Department for illegally demanding US dollar fines from private vehicle owners. Stephen Martin Nyichar, Secretary General of the Bar Association, told Radio Tamazuj on Wednesday that the traffic department's fines were not approved by the Ministry of Finance or the Revenue Authority. He stated that requiring citizens to pay fines in US dollars violates directives from the Bank of South Sudan.

Nyichar said the association contacted the Caretaker Interior Minister Lt. Gen. Rizik Zachariah Hassan and informed the Ministry of Justice about the traffic police's unilateral decisions. The Bar Association has given the Ministry of Interior a seven-day deadline to reverse these decisions. An official announcement of the lawsuit will be made at a press conference soon.

The traffic department is also requiring commercial vehicle owners to replace license plates at a cost of USD 400, a move the Bar Association deems illegal as no agreement was reached. These actions occur as South Sudan faces an economic downturn, with widespread financial hardship among the population.

Why it mattersThe South Sudan Bar Association has given the Ministry of Interior a seven-day deadline to reverse decisions on traffic fines and license plate fees before filing a lawsuit.

Gunmen kill journalist Veronica Gordon in Western Equatoria ambush

Gordon's killing came as delegates returning from SPLM primaries were ambushed in Miri Loko village, leaving two others wounded, while the attackers' identities and motive remain.

Veronica Lucy Gordon, a veteran journalist and recent parliamentary candidate, and Justin Akim, a former teacher and church elder, were killed on Thursday evening, November 12, when unidentified gunmen attacked a vehicle carrying Sudan People's Liberation Movement (SPLM) delegates on the Lui-Mundri road in Western Equatoria State. The attack occurred at about 7 p.m. in Miri Loko village as the delegates returned from SPLM primaries in Lui Payam to Mundri West. Two other people were wounded, and several delegates fled into nearby bushes.

Eye Radio reported that the attackers targeted the last vehicle in the convoy. Western Equatoria Caretaker Minister of Information Noah Issa told Eye Radio that the identities of the attackers and their motive remain unknown. Gordon, a former South Sudan Broadcasting Corporation (SSBC) journalist, had been nominated to contest a women's seat in the National Legislative Assembly.

The Female Journalists Network (FJN), the Union of Journalists of South Sudan (UJoSS) and the Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) Alliance on Elections condemned the killing in separate statements issued on Friday and called for an urgent investigation and accountability. The CSOs Alliance said violence against women in political life undermines their constitutional rights and equal participation in governance.

Why it mattersFemale candidates and journalists in Western Equatoria now face the same road threats that killed Gordon, and the attackers remain unidentified with no motive given.

Two killed in attack on SPLM delegates in South Sudan

Gunmen ambushed a vehicle carrying ruling party delegates in Western Equatoria State, leaving two dead and others missing.

At least two people were killed and one critically injured when gunmen attacked a vehicle carrying ruling Sudan People’s Liberation Movement (SPLM) delegates in Miri Loko village, Western Equatoria State, on Thursday night. The delegates were travelling from Lui Payam to Mundri West County after party primaries and congresses when the ambush occurred around 9 p.m. Several other delegates reportedly fled into nearby bushes during the attack.

Veronica Lucy Gordon, a former journalist nominated for a women’s seat in the National Legislative Assembly, and Justin Akim, a former teacher and elder, were among those killed. The critically injured delegate is receiving treatment at Lui Hospital. Deng Dau Deng, chairperson of the SPLM High-Level Committee in Western Equatoria, confirmed the incident and extended condolences to the families. The state’s Minister of Information, Noah Issah Philip, condemned the violence and urged an end to attacks on roads.

The attack occurred amid SPLM primaries and county congresses ahead of South Sudan’s general elections scheduled for December 22, 2026. Commissioner of Mundri East County, Daite Matthew Bakhieth, confirmed two deaths and other injuries, stating the situation was normal for now. The Western Equatoria State caretaker government expressed concern over the safety of political delegates and road users.

Why it mattersPolitical delegates travelling for party primaries were attacked, resulting in two deaths and raising security concerns ahead of the December 22, 2026 general elections.

South Sudan mourns General Joseph Lagu Yanga

A tribute remembers the late General Joseph Lagu Yanga, who died in 2026, for his courage and call for peace.

South Sudan is remembering General Joseph Lagu Yanga, who died in 2026. A tribute published by Radio Tamazuj on November 13, 2026, honors his life and service.

The tribute describes General Yanga as a hero who walked where maps end and taught others to stand for survival. It highlights his courage and duty, noting that war's cost is too high and its glory purchased with broken bodies.

The writer calls for an end to bloodshed that profits the powerful and empties the poor, urging a collective work for peace. The tribute concludes by stating that General Yanga's name moves like a star in the nation's darkness and he will not be forgotten.

Why it mattersThe nation remembers General Joseph Lagu Yanga, a hero who called for peace and an end to bloodshed.
