South Sudan Blood Supply Lacks Voluntary Donors

The World Health Organization warns that low voluntary blood donations and reliance on family donors create an unstable supply, risking patient lives.

South Sudan faces significant challenges in transitioning from family and replacement blood donors to voluntary donations, according to the World Health Organization (WHO). Pontius Bayo, WHO's Reproductive Health Officer in South Sudan, stated that voluntary non-remunerated blood donations remain critically low, fluctuating between 7.6 and 34 percent. This forces health facilities to depend heavily on relatives to find donors during urgent patient needs, a situation the WHO warns could jeopardize patient safety.

A national assessment by the Ministry of Health, supported by WHO, identified numerous obstacles hindering blood transfusion services. These include insufficient blood-bank infrastructure and storage, unreliable electricity, weak cold-chain systems, a shortage of essential equipment and supplies, and a lack of trained personnel. Fragmented, paper-based data systems and weak quality management also contribute to the problem, making a stable blood supply difficult to achieve.

The WHO is collaborating with the Ministry of Health and the National Blood Transfusion Services to address these systemic weaknesses. Efforts include supporting the National Blood Transfusion Services Strategic Plan 2026–2030, installing 13 solar-powered blood-bank refrigerators across the country, training health workers in donor management, and engaging community leaders to encourage voluntary donations.

Why it mattersPatients requiring blood transfusions, especially women during childbirth, anemic individuals, trauma victims, and surgical patients, are at risk due to the unstable blood supply.

Prosecutors Deny Singling Out Machar in Special Court Trial

Lead prosecutor Ajo Ony’Ohisa stated the prosecution's counter-application seeks procedural clarity for all remaining defendants, not unequal treatment for the suspended First.

Prosecutors at South Sudan's Special Court in Juba denied on Friday that their counter-application in the ongoing trial targets suspended First Vice President Dr. Riek Machar for different treatment. Lead prosecutor Ajo Ony’Ohisa argued during the 113th proceeding that the application seeks to clarify and apply correct statutory procedures prospectively and equally to all remaining defendants. The prosecution stated it does not aim to reopen or invalidate examinations already completed for any accused person.

Ony’Ohisa cited legal provisions, including Section 197(1), to support the prosecution's request for the court to question remaining defendants on circumstances appearing in evidence. The prosecution emphasized that its application is intended to ensure proper legal procedure is followed for those defendants who have yet to reach that stage of the proceedings. The defence, led by Dr. Geri Raimondo Legge, acknowledged receipt of the counter-application but objected to its use.

The trial involves Machar and seven other defendants facing charges related to violence in Nasir, Upper Nile State, in March 2025, which resulted in significant casualties for government forces. Judge Stephen Simon adjourned the proceedings until Monday, September 7, 2026. The court is expected to rule on the defence's application concerning constitutional rights to legal representation and the prosecution's counter-application.

Why it mattersThe ongoing trial of Dr. Riek Machar and seven co-accused affects political stability and the rule of law in South Sudan.

Communal Clashes Kill 49 in South Sudan Cattle Camp

Renewed violence erupted at dawn in Tonj North County, leaving dozens dead and wounded in a cattle camp and nearby village.

At least 49 people were killed and 33 wounded in renewed communal clashes in Tonj North County, Warrap State, local authorities reported. The violence occurred around 5:00 AM on Thursday at the Machar-Pakuek cattle camp and Mading Juer Boma in Majak Payam.

William Wol Mayom, from the Warrap State Ministry, confirmed the casualties resulting from the clashes. The specific details regarding the perpetrators or the immediate cause of the renewed conflict were not immediately available.

The incident highlights ongoing inter-communal tensions in the region, which often involve disputes over resources and livestock. The affected areas are primarily rural, with a strong reliance on cattle for livelihood.

Why it mattersFarmers in Tonj North County are directly affected by the communal clashes which resulted in deaths and injuries at a cattle camp and nearby village.

Red Cross delivers aid to 1,700 households in South Sudan

Humanitarian assistance reaches returnees and displaced persons in Kajo-Keji County following recent border clashes.

The International Committee of the Red Cross and South Sudan Red Cross began distributing humanitarian aid on Wednesday to 1,700 households in Kajo-Keji County. The assistance targets individuals who returned from refugee camps in Uganda and those displaced by fighting between South Sudanese and Ugandan armies last month. Aid deliveries have been completed in Mangalatore, Keriwa, and Sokare, with distribution ongoing across multiple locations.

The aid distribution aims to support an estimated 16,000 civilians displaced by renewed fighting that broke out in mid-July 2026. Local authorities reported Ugandan troops crossing into South Sudanese territory, leading to clashes. Humanitarian agencies are also rehabilitating infrastructure, such as water boreholes, and providing protection and family-linking services to help separated families reconnect.

Beneficiaries expressed gratitude for the timely support, noting its importance for vulnerable groups including children, women, and the elderly. However, some highlighted concerns about aid allocation ratios not matching household sizes, with larger families receiving insufficient supplies. Community leaders also called for continuous registration to accommodate new arrivals and for improvements in road conditions that have caused logistical delays.

Why it mattersReturnees and internally displaced persons in Kajo-Keji County are receiving essential food and supplies, addressing immediate needs following displacement.

South Sudan Mothers Breastfeed Babies at 71% Rate

A recent UNICEF and health-sector report indicates that only 71% of mothers in South Sudan exclusively breastfeed their infants for the first six months of life, with 76%.

Exclusive breastfeeding rates among mothers in South Sudan stand at approximately 71%, according to recent data from UNICEF and health sector analysis. This figure reflects the percentage of mothers who exclusively provide breast milk to their infants during the initial six months of life, a critical period for infant nutrition and development.

The findings, derived from the 2025 MICS survey, also highlight that a significant majority, 76%, of mothers in South Sudan begin breastfeeding their newborns within the first hour after birth. However, the data indicates a drop-off in exclusive breastfeeding, with 71% continuing this practice until their children reach two years of age.

These statistics provide insight into infant feeding practices across the nation. The data underscores the importance of continued support and education for mothers to maintain exclusive breastfeeding for the recommended duration, contributing to improved child health outcomes in South Sudan.

Why it mattersLow exclusive breastfeeding rates affect the health and nutritional status of infants in South Sudan.
