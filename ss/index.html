Amnesty: South Sudan failed to protect Abiemnom residents

Over 200 killed in March massacre despite prior warnings to government and UNMISS.

Amnesty International reported that South Sudanese authorities and the UN Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) failed to protect residents of Abiemnom town from a massacre on March 1. A group of 1,000 to 1,500 armed men from Unity State’s Mayom County killed over 200 people and destroyed homes during a four-hour attack. This event is one of South Sudan’s largest documented massacres since 2020.

Tigere Chagutah, Amnesty International’s Regional Director for East and Southern Africa, stated that both the government and UNMISS had two weeks' advance knowledge of the likely attack. Survivors reported warnings about Mayom County men mobilizing weapons and fighters. UNMISS acknowledged high tensions and that around 1,000 people sought protection at its base before the violence escalated.

Amnesty International found that the massacre may have been triggered by unresolved killings in Abiemnom County and fueled by impunity for a prior attack. The organization called for an urgent, independent investigation to hold perpetrators accountable and provide justice for victims and their families.

Why it mattersOver 200 people were killed and their property destroyed in a massacre that authorities and the UN knew was coming.

South Sudan confirms yellow fever death in Morobo County

The National Public Health Institute says a 48-year-old farmer from Okaba village died of the disease, which is spread by mosquitoes.

South Sudan has confirmed a yellow fever infection in Morobo County, Central Equatoria State, after the death of a 48-year-old man. The National Public Health Institute (NPHI) announced the case in a press release issued on Tuesday.

NPHI Acting Executive Director Dr Ader Macar Achiek said the patient was a farmer from Okaba village in Morobo County. The institute's statement confirms the infection and the death, and the health authorities are responding to the case.

The packet does not report further cases, vaccination plans or a timeline for the response. Readers in Morobo County and nearby areas should follow updates from the NPHI and local health services.

Why it mattersResidents of Morobo County and nearby areas face a confirmed fatal case of yellow fever, as reported by the National Public Health Institute.

SSPDF deploys troops for disarmament in Wau

South Sudan's army sent soldiers to Wau County to collect illegal firearms.

Heavily armed South Sudan People’s Defense Forces (SSPDF) troops were dispatched to Wau County on Tuesday to begin a disarmament operation ordered by President Salva Kiir.

Major General Wol Dhel Theip, the SSPDF’s Fifth Infantry Division Deputy Commander, stated that a six-month state of emergency will run alongside the disarmament in Western Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, Lakes, and Unity states.

The operation aims to improve security and prepare for peaceful elections by removing illegal firearms from civilians, who are warned that the SSPDF will deal with them without lenience if they do not surrender their guns.

Why it mattersThe disarmament operation and state of emergency directly affect civilians in four states by targeting those carrying illegal firearms.

South Sudanese women present 13 recommendations on gender violence

A four-year initiative supporting prevention and survivor services closed in Juba on Wednesday, with women's groups seeking firmer protections.

Women's organizations in South Sudan presented 13 recommendations on Wednesday in Juba to strengthen protection for women and girls and improve responses to sexual and gender-based violence. The proposals were made as the German and EU-supported ResPEct project, which covered prevention and survivor services, officially closed after four years.

At the closing ceremony at the University of Juba's UNIPOD, government representatives, women's groups and international partners called for sustained action on gender discrimination and better access to justice. Hellen Awate, a widow and mother of three from Yei River County, said counselling and business training helped her regain confidence after her husband's death left her isolated and struggling to provide for her children.

Among the demands are legal protection that is stronger, accountability for perpetrators and better access to reproductive healthcare. Women's groups also want the right to own, use and inherit land and property protected, and more participation in governance from women, young women, survivors and women with disabilities.

Why it mattersWomen and girls in South Sudan, including widows who lose land and property rights, are the people the recommendations would directly affect.

South Sudan makes Friday a public holiday for Lagu's state funeral

Civil servants and staff of international organisations get the day off, and the burial begins with a 6 a.m. requiem at All Saints Cathedral in Juba.

South Sudan has declared Friday, November 13, 2026, a public holiday so citizens can attend the state funeral of veteran liberation leader General Joseph Lagu Yanga. The State Funeral High-Level Committee says there will be no work that day, including for civil servants and employees of international organisations.

Committee spokesperson Ambassador John Andruga Duku said people may attend the church service and burial, or follow the proceedings from home. The requiem starts at 6 a.m. at All Saints Cathedral of the Episcopal Church of South Sudan in Mobil, Juba, before the remains go to the burial site east of the Pope Francis Pavilion at the Dr. John Garang Mausoleum. Attendees must arrive early for strict security screening.

President Salva Kiir Mayardit opened public viewing of Lagu's body at the national parliament in Juba on Wednesday. Lagu, who led the Anyanya movement and negotiated the 1972 Addis Ababa Agreement, died in Juba on October 27, 2026, aged 94. The holiday declaration still awaits official confirmation from the Ministry of Labour.
