South Sudan closes airline offices over $5 passenger levy

Authorities in Wau ordered Kush Airlines and Arojet Airlines to shut down for refusing to collect a stamp duty on tickets.

Authorities in Wau, South Sudan, have ordered the closure of Kush Airlines and Arojet Airlines offices due to their refusal to collect a $5 stamp duty on air passengers. The state Revenue Authority insists the levy is constitutional and payable by individual travellers, not the companies.

Ishaq Mohammed Mustafa, a consultant with the State Revenue Authority, stated on Saturday that the stamp duty is a legally mandated fee for all travellers, with Article 179 of the constitution providing the basis for state revenue collection. He added that Kush Airlines had been instructed to collect the levy through ticket sales but refused to comply.

Makuec Deng Madut, Commissioner of the State Revenue Authority, clarified that the $5 charge is for individual passengers, particularly foreigners, and not the airline companies. Caretaker Finance Minister Brigadier General Julio Velario Osman noted that Kush Airlines had previously collected the duty but stopped, warning companies against non-compliance with government revenue policies.

Why it mattersTwo airline offices in Wau have been shut down by state authorities for failing to implement a $5 passenger levy, impacting air travel services.

Nurse shot in suspected revenge attack at Cueibet hospital

Unidentified armed men shot a nurse on duty in what Lakes State officials suspect is retaliation for a patient's death.

A nurse was critically wounded after armed men opened fire at Cueibet County Hospital in South Sudan’s Lakes State on Thursday evening. Authorities suspect the attack was revenge for a patient's death at the facility. The victim, Santino Mayom Kau, was shot in the chest.

Lakes State Caretaker Minister of Information William Koji Kerjok stated that the health worker, identified as Mayom Majok, was targeted while on duty. Kerjok condemned the shooting, calling Majok an innocent person not involved in any violence. The minister confirmed Majok sustained injuries and is receiving treatment at Rumbek Hospital.

Kerjok urged security forces to arrest the suspects, who remain at large, and called on Lakes State residents to avoid revenge attacks and legal disputes. He stressed that criminal cases should be handled through police and courts rather than retaliatory violence.

South Sudanese urged to recall peaceful dispute resolution history

A letter argues that traditions of settling quarrels through elders and compensation are as much a part of heritage as past wars.

A letter to South Sudanese citizens recalls that their history includes rich traditions of peacefully settling disputes, not just years of war since 1955.

The author cites examples like Nuer communities using a 'leopard-skin chief' to mediate feuds and negotiate cattle compensation, and Dinka values of 'cieng' for mutual care and 'dheeng' for dignified conduct.

Oral histories from Kakwa communities around Yei also value chiefs for "good speech" in negotiating with armed groups, suggesting leaders have an obligation to hear the people affected by their decisions.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens are reminded of their cultural heritage of peaceful conflict resolution to inform how they live together as a nation.

South Sudan murder trial adjourned after bail absconders

The Juba court postponed the case of six accused of killing Reverend Christopher Maring until November 19.

A court sitting at Juba Central Prison adjourned the hearing for six individuals accused of killing Reverend Christopher Maring, a senior cleric of the African Inland Church, to November 19. The adjournment occurred because two of the accused, who had been released on bail, did not appear for the proceedings on Thursday, November 5.

Prosecutor Lilian Gorge informed Eye Radio that Judge Peter Mazen postponed the trial due to the absence of the two bail recipients. Gorge stated the prosecution asked the court to examine the conditions under which bail was granted. The six accused face charges including murder, robbery, and use of firearms under the South Sudan Penal Code Act, 2008.

Reverend Maring was killed at his Juba residence in January 2026 after armed men reportedly entered his home. Police used mobile phone tracking to arrest the suspects, with one having fled to the Democratic Republic of Congo before his apprehension. The trial is expected to resume on November 19.

Politics · 2026-11-08

China urges Sudan, South Sudan to resolve Abyei dispute

Beijing warns that worsening security and unilateral actions could derail peace talks over the disputed territory.

China's UN ambassador, Sun Lei, urged Sudan and South Sudan on Thursday, November 5, to resolve their dispute over Abyei through dialogue. He warned that deteriorating security and unilateral actions could undermine efforts to reach a political settlement. Sun called on the two nations to build on recent progress in their joint political and security mechanisms to settle Abyei’s final status.

Tensions have risen over South Sudan's plan to include Abyei in its December 2026 general elections, a move Sudan rejects as a violation of agreements. UN Secretary-General António Guterres cautioned that the plan could significantly increase political tensions before the elections. Sudan's representative, Al-Harith Idriss Al-Harith Mohamed, stated the decision undermined existing agreements, while South Sudan's Sabino Edward Nyawella Amaikwey argued the political rights of the Ngok Dinka could not be ignored.

Sun welcomed the resumption of joint political and security meetings, noting positive outcomes. He condemned a recent drone attack on Amiet market that caused civilian casualties and urged the Rapid Support Forces and other armed groups to withdraw from Abyei. Delays in resolving Abyei’s political status have hindered development and weakened its economic capacity, Sun added.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens in the Abyei region face increased political tensions and potential disruption to their rights due to the unresolved dispute.
