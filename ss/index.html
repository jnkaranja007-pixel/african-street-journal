Riek Machar praises late General Joseph Lagu

Machar, detained in a cell, called Lagu a selfless leader who prioritized peace and self-determination.

Former First Vice President Riek Machar on Wednesday described the late General Joseph Lagu as a leader who was not greedy for power. Machar, currently detained, conveyed his condolences in a message during the 125th session, praising Lagu's commitment to peace and self-determination for Southern Sudan.

Machar stated that Lagu deserved recognition for his contributions to the liberation struggle and for prioritizing the welfare of the people. He noted that Lagu left leadership to someone not originally part of the movement, highlighting his selfless nature. Lagu was a key figure in the Anyanya movement and helped negotiate the 1972 Addis Ababa Agreement.

The Addis Ababa Agreement ended the first Sudanese civil war and granted Southern Sudan regional autonomy for a decade. Lagu, born in 1931 in Eastern Equatoria, became leader of the Southern Sudan Liberation Movement in 1968. He later served as president of the High Executive Council and Sudan's second vice-president before retiring from public service.

Why it mattersDetained former First Vice President Riek Machar is speaking about the legacy of General Joseph Lagu, a significant figure in South Sudan's history.

South Sudan secures $267 million for Ugandan electricity

The funding will extend power from Nimule to Juba and other major towns, aiming to boost reliability and affordability.

South Sudan's Ministry of Finance and Planning and the African Development Bank (AfDB) have agreed on $267 million to extend electricity from Uganda into the country. The project, launched on Wednesday, will bring power from Nimule to Juba and other major towns, improving supply reliability and affordability. This initiative aims to integrate South Sudan's power grid with Uganda's through a new interconnection.

Benjamin Ayali Koyongwa, the Finance Ministry's Undersecretary for Planning, thanked the AfDB and partners for their support during the launch. Fouzia Haji of the AfDB stated that electricity is vital for households and businesses and will support economic development in both nations. The launch was attended by officials from the Ministries of Energy and Dams, and Finance, along with Ugandan representatives.

The project follows the recent commissioning of electricity supply from Uganda to Nimule in Eastern Equatoria State. This extension is part of wider efforts to increase access to reliable electricity in South Sudan via regional power connections. The AfDB noted that electricity is essential for economic growth once the project is completed.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese households and businesses will benefit from improved electricity supply and affordability.

South Sudan elections face credibility risks

RJMEC warns political disputes and incomplete reforms threaten the December 22 general elections.

The Reconstituted Joint Monitoring and Evaluation Commission (RJMEC) has warned that South Sudan's planned December 22 general elections face credibility risks. Political disputes, insecurity, and delays in implementing key reforms could undermine public confidence in the vote, according to RJMEC's third-quarter report released on Wednesday.

RJMEC noted that disagreements among political parties over the legal process and the government's amendment of the peace agreement without proper approval could affect the elections. Security concerns, including armed clashes and intercommunal violence, also pose a threat to voter registration, campaigning, and the safety of candidates and voters.

The report also highlighted delays in constitutional reforms, with no progress in the permanent constitution-making process during the reporting period. RJMEC urged South Sudan's political leaders to resolve differences through dialogue and fulfill their commitments under the peace agreement for a peaceful end to the transitional period.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens face potential risks to the credibility of their upcoming general elections due to unresolved political disputes and ongoing insecurity.

Juba mayor suspends brokers union

All offices of the union representing trade intermediaries, real estate, and market brokers must close.

Juba Mayor Christopher Serefino Wani ordered the suspension of all activities for the Brokers Union and the closure of its offices within Juba City Council. The order, effective October 21, 2026, targets bodies representing trade intermediaries, real estate, and market brokers.

Mayor Wani stated that any violation of the order would result in punitive measures under South Sudan's laws. He directed Juba City Council authorities, law enforcement, and relevant institutions to enforce the closure of all union offices.

The Brokers Union operates as a body representing trade intermediaries, real estate, and market brokers.

Why it mattersThe activities of trade intermediaries, real estate agents, and market brokers in Juba have been halted.

South Sudan Revenue Authority collects SSP 138 billion

The Domestic Tax Revenue Division gathered the funds in the first 15 days of October.

The South Sudan Revenue Authority collected SSP 138 billion in the first 15 days of October 2026. The authority is increasing efforts to register more taxpayers and improve compliance with tax laws.

Angelo Aguot, acting commissioner for Domestic Tax Revenue, stated that the division has recently seen improvements in its revenue collection. He noted these gains occurred as the authority works to expand taxpayer registration and enhance compliance.

The collected revenue is part of the authority's ongoing efforts to boost domestic tax collection. Further details on the specific improvements in compliance and registration were not provided in the initial report.

Why it mattersThe South Sudan Revenue Authority collected SSP 138 billion in 15 days, impacting national revenue collection.
