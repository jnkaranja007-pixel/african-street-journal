South Sudan vows probe after two UN peacekeepers killed in Jonglei ambush

South Sudan's government condemned an attack on a United Nations patrol convoy in Jonglei state that killed two Ethiopian peacekeepers and wounded seven people, calling for a.

South Sudan’s government has pledged a joint investigation into an ambush on a United Nations patrol convoy in Jonglei state that resulted in the deaths of two Ethiopian peacekeepers and injuries to seven other personnel. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, through its spokesperson Ambassador Apuk Ayuel, stated on Tuesday that the government “unequivocally condemns this attack” and seeks to identify those responsible for the incident.

The attack occurred on Monday as the UNMISS convoy, comprising eight vehicles and 38 people including Ethiopian peacekeepers, UN civilian and military personnel, and four South Sudan National Police Service officers, traveled from Bor towards Pajut, approximately 10 km from Jalle. The seven injured individuals, including peacekeepers, South Sudanese police, and UN civilian staff, are currently receiving medical treatment.

The government has expressed condolences to Ethiopia and the families of the deceased peacekeepers, affirming its commitment to collaborate with UNMISS and national authorities to enhance security coordination. This incident highlights ongoing insecurity and armed violence in Jonglei state, a region contested by government and opposition forces, which continues to challenge the implementation of South Sudan’s peace process.

Why it mattersThe attack directly affects the safety of international personnel and local law enforcement, impacting security efforts and the peace process in South Sudan.

Juba City Council proposes property tax cut to 6%

The Juba City Council has proposed reducing the property tax from 8.3% to 6% of annual rental income to ease the burden on property owners and boost compliance.

Juba City Council Mayor Christopher Wani Sarafino announced a proposal to cut the rental value tax from 8.3% to 6% of annual rental income. This reduction aims to provide relief to property owners and encourage more people to pay their taxes, potentially increasing government revenue by expanding the tax base.

The proposed tax cut is part of amendments to the relevant law, according to Mayor Sarafino, who spoke at a media and opinion leaders’ networking program. He emphasized that lower, more affordable taxes could lead to higher compliance rates among property owners, as the council can only collect taxes authorized by law.

Sarafino also called for tax harmonization across national, state, and local governments to simplify payments for businesses. He noted that traders currently face multiple collections from different authorities, and a coordinated mechanism would allow them to pay once annually, reducing repeated visits from tax collectors.

Why it mattersProperty owners in Juba will directly benefit from a lower tax burden, and businesses will experience a more streamlined tax payment process.

South Sudan graduates 50 rangers to combat Badingilo poaching

Fifty wildlife rangers completed a two-month training program to strengthen anti-poaching efforts and protect wildlife in South Sudan’s Badingilo National Park.

Fifty wildlife rangers have graduated from an intensive two-month training program aimed at strengthening efforts to combat poaching and protect wildlife in South Sudan’s Badingilo National Park. This third batch of rangers received training in wildlife management, law enforcement, tactical skills, weapons handling, discipline, and operational readiness, according to the Sudans Post.

The Ministry of Wildlife Conservation and Tourism passed out the rangers, with African Parks facilitating the training in partnership with the ministry. The curriculum also covered patrol procedures, weapons safety, appropriate use of force, law enforcement standards, and anti-poaching operations, as reported by Eye Radio.

The new rangers are expected to increase patrol coverage and strengthen the park’s response to illegal wildlife activities, including the commercial bushmeat trade. Badingilo National Park, part of the Great Nile Migration Landscape, is South Sudan’s first UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Why it mattersThe deployment of these rangers directly affects local communities and wildlife by aiming to reduce illegal activities like poaching and the commercial bushmeat trade within a World Heritage Site.

South Sudan Immigration suspends 'silent search' operations on migrants

South Sudan's Directorate of Immigration, Passport, and Citizenship has halted controversial 'silent search' operations targeting foreign nationals following concerns over their.

The Directorate of Immigration, Passport, and Citizenship in South Sudan has suspended its 'silent search' operations aimed at verifying migrants' credentials and immigration status. This decision comes after concerns were raised regarding the treatment of foreign nationals within the country.

The South Sudan National Police Service issued a directive on August 25, 2026, ordering all immigration authorities nationwide to cease the 'Silence Search' exercise. The police stated that such operations require broader consultation and coordination with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, foreign embassies, and other relevant authorities.

According to Lt. Gen. Achol Cyier Rehan, Director General of the Directorate of Civil Registry, Nationality, Passports and Immigration, no official approval or authorization for the exercise had been issued. The police instructed authorities to ensure no unauthorized actions are taken, with further instructions to follow after consultations and approvals are complete.

Why it mattersForeign nationals in South Sudan are directly affected by the suspension of these operations, as it addresses concerns about their treatment and the legality of credential verification.

Central Equatoria lifts Juba sports ban, mandates security for tournaments

The Central Equatoria State Ministry of Youth and Sports has ended its suspension of community sports tournaments in Juba, requiring organizers to obtain official approval and.

Central Equatoria State has lifted its suspension on community sports tournaments in Juba, effective Monday, according to State Minister for Youth and Sports Bush Buse. The decision, announced after a meeting with tournament organizers, mandates that all community tournaments must secure approval from the state Ministry of Youth and Sports before resuming activities.

The lifting of the ban comes with new conditions aimed at improving security and discipline at sporting events. Buse directed organizers to provide adequate security for referees, technical staff, players, and spectators, warning that harassment, violence, or misconduct will not be tolerated. Teams or tournaments failing to meet these security and disciplinary requirements could face suspension or expulsion.

Buse also urged organizers of the Bahr el Ghazal Counties Tournament to peacefully resolve a dispute involving Cueibet and Twic Mayardit to avoid further disruption. Bahr el Ghazal Tournament President Majok Ayar Chieny apologized for an incident between supporters from Cueibet and Twic in Munuki, describing it as unplanned and affirming the communities' peaceful intentions.

Why it mattersThe resumption of community sports directly affects players, organizers, and spectators in Juba, allowing them to participate in and attend tournaments under new security guidelines.
