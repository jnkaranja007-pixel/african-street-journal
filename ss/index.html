Juba City Council digitizes revenue collection

The council arrested 32 employees and launched a public website to improve financial transparency.

Juba City Council has launched a new digital financial payment system and a public website to improve transparency in revenue collection and fund management. Caretaker Mayor Christopher Sarafino Wani Swaka announced on Friday that the council has migrated all revenue operations from manual forms to a digitized system over the past 10 days.

Investigations uncovered financial malpractices, with Mayor Swaka stating that 50 percent of the council’s resources were lost. This led to the arrest of 32 revenue collectors who are facing disciplinary action, including potential dismissal. The reforms follow complaints from residents and business owners about the transparency of taxes and fees.

The council also launched a website, www.jccgov.ss, to provide public access to information like the annual budget and tax schedules. Mayor Swaka said this aims to address public uncertainty about fees and improve communication between the city and its residents.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese residents and business owners will have clearer information on taxes and fees collected by Juba City Council.

UK gives South Sudan $2.8 million for flood preparedness

Funding will support early warning systems and aid for communities in Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile states.

The United Kingdom has provided 2.8 million US dollars to the UN Food and Agriculture Organization and the World Food Programme to help vulnerable communities in South Sudan prepare for climate-related disasters. The funding will support early flood preparedness and response activities in Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile states. The programme will provide early-warning information, cash assistance, livelihood support, community public works, and emergency supplies.

UK Development Director Sebastian Fouquet stated that climate shocks are worsening the country’s humanitarian situation, and acting on early warnings helps families protect their assets. WFP Country Director Mutinta Chimuka said early action is essential to prevent predictable climate shocks from becoming humanitarian emergencies. FAO Acting Representative Felix Dzvurumi added that the support will help farmers, livestock keepers, and fishing communities protect their livelihoods and improve food security.

The programme will run until March 2027 and will also strengthen community-based early-warning systems and coordination with government authorities. This contribution brings the UK’s total funding for El Niño-related early action in South Sudan to over £2.85 million. The initiative aims to reduce losses and protect livelihoods before disasters strike.

Why it mattersCommunities in Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile states will receive support to prepare for and respond to climate-related disasters like floods and drought.

SSPDF reunites two abducted children with parents in Juba

Two-year-old Abi Gatwich Tuach and five-year-old Simon Gattiek Tut Kai were recovered from Gumuruk Town.

The South Sudan People’s Defense Forces (SSPDF) reunited two abducted children with their parents in Juba on Thursday. The handover ceremony occurred at the army headquarters in Bilpam. The children, Abi Gatwich Tuach, aged two, and Simon Gattiek Tut Kai, aged five, were recovered from Gumuruk Town in the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA).

SSPDF spokesperson Major General Lul Ruai Koang stated Abi was abducted on December 5, 2025, from Suk Darfur, and Simon on June 13, 2026, from Suk Arnab in Juba. He identified Juma Logocho, Ayella Dominic Onono, and Angok Ayok as alleged traffickers who sold the children to Mary Amei and Thomei Agany. The operation involved SSPDF intelligence and GPAA authorities.

Gen. Ruai reported that 32 missing children have been registered, with only three recovered so far. He appealed to the public to report missing children and provide information on suspected traffickers. The GPAA authorities were urged to intensify efforts to arrest remaining suspects and find other abducted children.

Kiir returns to Juba after Russia summit

Russia increased scholarship places for South Sudanese students to 40 for the 2026-2027 academic year.

South Sudanese President Salva Kiir returned to Juba on Saturday after attending the third Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow. During the summit, Russian President Vladimir Putin announced an increase in scholarship allocations for South Sudanese students to 40 places for the 2026-2027 academic year.

Putin stated that Russia would continue to assist in training personnel for South Sudan, noting that the two countries marked 15 years of diplomatic relations this year. He also mentioned exploring ways to strengthen bilateral trade and economic engagement, acknowledging that the full potential of their relationship had not yet been realised. The summit, which concluded on Thursday, October 29, saw participation from 48 African countries.

On the sidelines of the summit, South Sudan and Russia signed a Memorandum of Understanding and a Letter of Acceptance for the transfer of former Petronas shares in the Dar Petroleum Operating Company and Greater Pioneer Operating Company to Russia's Zarubezhneft. President Kiir had earlier invited Russian and other foreign investors to South Sudan, highlighting opportunities in agriculture, petroleum, mining, and tourism.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese students will have more opportunities for higher education in Russia.

Stephen Dhieu wins SPLM gubernatorial primaries in Upper Nile

Dhieu secured 120 votes, defeating former governor Jacob Dollar and becoming the party's flag bearer for December 2026 elections.

Stephen Dhieu Dau won the SPLM gubernatorial primaries in Upper Nile State with 120 votes, becoming the party’s candidate for the December 2026 elections. SPLM Secretary-General Dr Akol Paul Kordit also named Dhieu the party’s chairperson for Upper Nile State. 13 candidates competed for the nomination.

Dhieu defeated former Upper Nile Governor Jacob Dollar, who received 12 votes, and East African Legislative Assembly Member of Parliament Kim Gai, who got three votes. The sole female aspirant, Advocate Ferida Philip Nyanan, received two votes. Gabriel Isaac came second with 38 votes and was confirmed as the deputy SPLM chairperson.

The primaries were supervised by a high-level SPLM National Leadership delegation led by Third Deputy Chairperson Simon Kun Puoch and Secretary-General Dr Akol Paul Kordit. The congress aimed to elect new state leadership and organise party structures ahead of the general elections.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese voters in Upper Nile State will choose between party-nominated candidates in the December 2026 general elections.
