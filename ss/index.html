South Sudan standards body commends Juba arrests over expired flour

Eight employees of H Brothers General Trading and Investment Company Limited were detained for allegedly repackaging expired maize flour.

The South Sudan National Bureau of Standards (SSNBS) praised Juba County authorities for arresting eight employees of H Brothers General Trading and Investment Company Limited on October 25. The employees are accused of repackaging expired maize flour for resale in Gumbo. County authorities conducted an operation on Saturday and found employees transferring the flour into new sacks.

The company’s storage facilities were closed and the eight employees detained pending legal proceedings, according to a statement from the Office of the Caretaker Commissioner. A company manager disputed the allegations, claiming the products were unexpired wheat flour affected by heat and had been previously inspected by the SSNBS. The manager stated that videos from the operation did not clearly show expiry dates.

The SSNBS urged businesses to comply with food safety regulations and improve stock management, emphasizing the need to segregate expired products. The Bureau also called for strengthened cooperation between county authorities, public health institutions, and law enforcement to prevent unsafe products from circulating. Consumers were advised to check product labels and expiry dates before purchasing.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese consumers are at risk of purchasing and consuming expired maize flour.

South Sudanese urged to demand better governance

A letter calls for a national dialogue where citizens' experiences shape how the country is governed.

A letter addressed to South Sudanese citizens argues that a child's life should be valued above political ambition.

The author recalls a Nuer woman challenging men at the 1999 Wunlit peace conference to account for children killed in conflict, stating the SPLA/M did not liberate the country to kill.

The letter advocates for a national dialogue where the experiences of those who have lost loved ones or fled their homes can shape governance, asserting that South Sudan belongs to its people, not its leaders.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens are urged to demand a national dialogue to shape their governance.

Judiciary to rule on deadly clashes at Akol Koor's residence

A South Sudanese court will deliver a judgment on Monday regarding a lawsuit filed by victims of a November 2024 gunfight.

The Judiciary is set to deliver a judgment on Monday in a lawsuit filed against the Ministry of Defence concerning the November 2024 armed confrontation at General Akol Koor’s residence in Juba. The case originates from a deadly gunfight on November 21, 2024, involving South Sudan People’s Defence Forces (SSPDF) guarding the former intelligence chief. The confrontation erupted after a disagreement between security personnel guarding the residence and forces tasked with relocating General Koor.

The incident resulted in four deaths, including two soldiers and two civilians, according to army spokesperson Major General Lul Ruai Koang. Two civilians were reportedly crushed by an armoured personnel carrier, and two law students were killed in the crossfire, stated Member of Parliament Mary Nyarieka Lorjok. Juba Teaching Hospital confirmed treating 10 wounded civilians, some with bullet injuries, who were later transferred to Juba Military Hospital.

President Salva Kiir established an investigation committee to assess casualties and damage following the confrontation. The army also requested injured civilians, families of the deceased, and those with damaged property to report to military headquarters with documentation for the inquiry. The lawsuit against the Ministry of Defence is expected to address claims stemming from the incident.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens were killed and injured during armed clashes at a former intelligence chief's residence.

10 killed in Tonj North communal violence

Attackers from Akop Payam killed eight residents of Mading Juer Boma and two of their own in Friday night fighting.

10 people died and three were injured in communal violence in Alabek Payam, Tonj North County, on Friday night. The attackers came from neighboring Akop Payam and targeted residents of Mading Juer Boma.

Agany Lok Agany, the Tonj North County caretaker commissioner, confirmed the deaths, stating eight people were killed from Mading Juer and two from the attacking side, with three others wounded. Residents reported that feuding sections from the same larger lineage within Alabek Payam have been fighting for years over a local administrative unit, leading to revenge killings.

Mercenaries from neighboring villages, including Akop Payam and Mayom County in Unity State, have been brought in by both sides. The violence has created a dangerous situation for residents in the area.

Why it matters10 people were killed and three injured in communal violence in Tonj North County.
