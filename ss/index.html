South Sudan President Assents to Election Rule Changes

Constitutional amendments remove prerequisites for a permanent constitution and census ahead of December 2026 elections.

South Sudan's President Salva Kiir has signed constitutional amendments that separate the holding of national elections from the completion of a permanent constitution and a national census. The Transitional National Legislative Assembly approved the changes on Tuesday, with the president's office stating the move is necessary to remove legal obstacles to the December 2026 vote. These requirements were originally stipulated in the 2018 peace deal as prerequisites for the election.

The amendments follow earlier changes to the 2018 peace agreement, also approved by parliament and assented to by Kiir in July, which similarly removed the census and constitution-making process as preconditions for the election. The government argues these adjustments are essential to allow the National Elections Commission and political parties to accelerate their preparations for the vote, which has already been postponed multiple times since the country gained independence in 2011.

While the constitutional amendment addresses legal hurdles, significant challenges remain for the electoral process, including preparations by electoral institutions and securing necessary funding. The government's decision to defer the census and constitution-making until after the elections raises questions about whether these reforms will be implemented or indefinitely postponed, potentially impacting the credibility and sustainability of the democratic transition.

Why it mattersThe constitutional changes affect the country's political leadership by altering the legal framework for upcoming elections, potentially impacting the timeline and credibility of the democratic transition.

Juba County Commissioner Suspends Illegal Boda-boda Associations

Samuel Emiliano Wani ordered all unregistered motorcycle taxi groups to cease operations immediately due to public order and security concerns.

Juba County Commissioner Samuel Emiliano Wani has ordered the immediate suspension of all illegal, unregistered, and unauthorized Boda-boda associations. This directive follows a resolution by the Central Equatoria State Security Committee and concerns from the Juba County Security Committee regarding public order and unauthorized activities. The Commissioner's office stated that affected associations must stop collecting fees, registering members, or issuing identification cards.

The order mandates that any individual or group found violating or obstructing its implementation will face legal consequences. Boda-boda associations seeking to operate legally are instructed to submit their registration and relevant documents to the competent authorities for verification and authorization. Riders, association leaders, and stakeholders are urged to remain calm and cooperate with the authorities during this regulation process.

This action aims to tighten regulation within the commercial motorcycle transport sector by targeting associations that lack proper authorization or structures recognized by local authorities. The suspension affects the collection of fees and the issuance of permits by these unauthorized groups, impacting their operational capacity and revenue streams.

Why it mattersBoda-boda associations and their members are directly affected by the suspension, which prohibits them from collecting fees and registering new members.

Juba shoppers flock to Chinese stores for cheaper goods

South Sudanese consumers are increasingly turning to Chinese-owned retail outlets for affordable household items as soaring prices strain household budgets.

As soaring prices continue to erode purchasing power in South Sudan, consumers in the capital, Juba, are finding relief at low-cost Chinese-owned retail shops. These businesses, expanding from wholesale into neighborhood outlets, offer a wide range of products including clothing, kitchenware, and school supplies at prices significantly lower than other retailers. At Suk Libya market, shoppers noted that essential goods have become more accessible despite worsening economic conditions.

Residents report substantial price differences, with items like a hair straightening comb costing around SSP 15,000 at Chinese shops compared to SSP 60,000-70,000 elsewhere. Similarly, a box of tea glasses priced at SSP 90,000 elsewhere was found for half that amount. While some associate Chinese goods with lower quality, affordability is now the primary concern for many families managing rising living costs.

Chinese retailers have expanded consumer options during a financially challenging period. While prices are a major draw, the popularity of these stores has led to overcrowding and long queues. Economist Abraham Maliet stated that consumer choices are increasingly driven by affordability, with purchasing decisions largely determined by income levels in the current economic climate.

Why it mattersJuba residents are affected by the rising cost of living, finding more affordable household goods at Chinese-owned shops.

Acholi community demands return of four abducted children

The Acholi community in Eastern Equatoria has written to county officials urging intervention to find four children allegedly taken by armed elements.

The Acholi community in South Sudan’s Eastern Equatoria state has called on Magwi County authorities to investigate the abduction of four children and secure their safe return. In a letter to Magwi County Commissioner Francis Lam Gabriel, Acholi Community Chairperson Otim David Okot requested urgent government and security intervention following a complaint from the Omeo community. The children, identified as Jonathan Oloya Wani, 6; Stella Anyio Wani, 8; Ageno Ruth Wani, 4; and Achan Monday Wani, 4, have caused "untold pain and suffering to the families."

A preliminary report suggests the children were abducted by armed elements from the Murle community in the Pibor Administrative Area. Okot urged Magwi authorities to coordinate with the Pibor Administrative Area government to facilitate the children’s recovery and reunification. The interim Omeo Community chairman, Jacob Onek, also condemned the abduction, stating it caused fear and distress, and appealed for all efforts to locate the children and prosecute those responsible.

The letters did not specify when or how the abductions occurred. No immediate response was available from authorities in the Pibor Administrative Area. The Omeo community is seeking the identification and prosecution of those responsible for the alleged abductions in accordance with the law.

Why it mattersThe Acholi and Omeo communities are affected by the abduction of four children, causing them pain, suffering, fear, and distress.

South Sudan Elections Commission Rejects Biometrics for Voter Registration

The National Elections Commission cited low literacy levels as the reason for opting for manual voter registration over technology.

South Sudan's National Elections Commission (NEC) has decided against using biometric technology for the upcoming voter registration, opting instead for a manual process. NEC Chairperson Prof. Abednego Akok Kacuol announced the decision during a voter registration workshop in Juba on Wednesday, stating that the country's low literacy rates made the technological approach unsuitable.

The commission's choice to proceed manually means that voter registration will rely on traditional paper-based methods rather than digital identification systems. This decision comes as the nation prepares for elections, with the NEC aiming to ensure accessibility and participation across the population.

This move by the NEC highlights a practical challenge in implementing advanced technological solutions in regions with varying levels of educational attainment. The focus remains on a registration system that can accommodate all potential voters, regardless of their literacy skills.

Why it mattersThe decision affects potential voters by using a manual registration process instead of biometrics, which could impact their ability to register if they are not familiar with manual systems.
