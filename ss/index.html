Yambio infant needs surgery after baboon attack

A three-month-old baby girl in Yambio suffered severe facial injuries from a baboon attack and requires specialized reconstructive surgery.

A three-month-old baby girl in Yambio, Western Equatoria State, is in stable condition after a baboon attack severely damaged her nose. Health officials stated the infant was brought to Yambio State Hospital on Thursday morning with injuries that left only small openings for breathing. Medical staff inserted a nasogastric tube to assist feeding while the child receives treatment.

The infant requires specialized reconstructive surgery to repair her damaged nasal tissue, a procedure unavailable at the local hospital due to a lack of expertise and facilities, according to Dr. Kinawo Richard of the Western Equatoria State Ministry of Health. The baboon, which belonged to a relative, reportedly left its feeding area and entered the house before attacking the child. The Director of wildlife in the state, Joseph Dau Deng, urged residents to stop keeping dangerous wild animals in residential areas, calling it illegal.

Brigadier Justin Waiwai, Western Equatoria State Police Commissioner, condemned the incident and stated the baboon's owner will be held accountable. Deng warned that such animals pose a significant risk to children and the community. The exact circumstances of the attack remain unclear.

Special Court rejects Riek Machar's video evidence request

The court ruled Machar's application to play video footage supporting his arguments was premature.

The Special Court in Juba rejected former First Vice President Riek Machar's request to present video footage during his trial on Friday, October 23, 2026. Presiding Judge Dr. James Alala Deng stated the application was premature as the proceedings had not yet reached the stage for considering such evidence. Machar, 74, had sought to play videos of senior government officials to support his defence.

Judge Deng explained that Machar should first focus on understanding and responding to the specific charges against him, as the court had not yet framed the charges. The lead prosecutor had also opposed the application, arguing it was too early for video evidence. The court adjourned the proceedings to October 26, 2026, for the examination of the fifth accused to continue.

Separately, the Special Court announced it had completed an investigation into alleged threats against Machar, his defence team, and co-accused. Judge Deng said individuals identified in the investigation have been referred to the attorney general and police for further action. The defence team had raised concerns about the safety of the accused and their lawyers last month.

GIZ gives 12 grinding machines to South Sudan farmers

The German agency aims to boost income and reduce processing time for cereal farmers in Yei River and Magwi counties.

The Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit (GIZ) GmbH provided 12 cereal grinding machines to 12 farmer groups in Yei River and Magwi counties on Tuesday. Each group, comprising 25 members with 60 percent females, received one machine through GIZ's Improved Living Conditions and Psychosocial Support (ILiPS) project.

GIZ South Sudan stated the initiative aims to support income-generating activities by improving access to processing equipment, reducing time and effort for cereal processing, and increasing product value. Ramson Duku, Agricultural Advisor, emphasized the equipment's role in promoting self-reliance and strengthening household livelihoods.

The project will offer follow-up training and technical support to ensure sustainable use of the machinery, which is part of broader efforts to strengthen agricultural extension services and promote climate-smart farming. The ILiPS project also supports returnees, internally displaced persons, and host communities.

Why it mattersSmallholder farmers in Yei River and Magwi counties will benefit from reduced processing costs and increased income opportunities.

News · 2026-10-24

AU condemns deadly drone attack on Abyei market

The African Union Commission Chairperson expressed concern over civilian casualties and suffering caused by the October 19 attack.

The Chairperson of the African Union Commission, Mahmoud Ali Youssouf, condemned a drone attack on Amiet Market in the Abyei Administrative Area on October 19. Local authorities reported 220 people killed in the attack.

Youssouf expressed concern over civilian casualties and suffering, stating that civilians continue to pay an "intolerable price" through loss of loved ones and disruption of essential services. He called on all parties to halt attacks affecting civilians and protect humanitarian personnel.

The attack triggered a humanitarian crisis, displacing more than 8,000 people to Abyei town and surrounding areas. The AU Commission Chairperson urged parties to avoid actions that could fuel further conflict and reaffirmed AU support for mediation efforts.

Why it mattersCivilians in the Abyei Administrative Area suffered loss of life, displacement, and disruption of essential services due to the drone attack.

South Sudan shortens school year for elections

Primary and secondary examinations moved to November to allow for December 22, 2026 general elections.

South Sudan has reduced its academic calendar by one month to ensure national examinations conclude before the general elections on December 22, 2026. The Council of Ministers approved changes to the education schedule in September, advancing primary and secondary exams to November.

Hussein Abdelbagi Akol, the Presidential Advisor on Foreign Affairs and International Relations, and former Minister of Cabinet Affairs Dr Martin Elia Lomuro are among political leaders whose parties have joined newly registered coalitions. The Political Parties Council announced the registration of several political alliances on Wednesday as parties organize for the upcoming vote.

Dr Lomuro's South Sudan Democratic Forum is part of the National Agenda Coalition of Political Parties, which includes 10 political parties. Abdelbagi leads the South Sudan Patriotic Movement under the South Sudan Opposition Alliance. Two other coalitions, the Coalition of National Alliance of Political Parties and the National Alliance of Political Parties, have also been registered.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan's students will complete their academic year earlier than usual due to the upcoming general elections.
