Kiir Dismisses Deputy Justice Minister Over Abyei Map Dispute

President Salva Kiir removed Joseph Malek Arop from his post amid controversy surrounding a map of the disputed Abyei area.

South Sudanese President Salva Kiir has dismissed Deputy Minister of Justice and Constitutional Affairs Joseph Malek Arop with immediate effect. The removal was announced via a presidential decree read on state broadcaster SSBC. No specific reason was given for Arop's dismissal, though it follows a dispute over maps of the Abyei region.

Malek Arop, who is from Twic in Warrap State, had recently published a map he claimed showed the boundaries of Abyei as determined by the Permanent Court of Arbitration. Representatives from Abyei accused him of altering the court's 2009 ruling, while Twic representatives defended the map as reflecting the court's findings. This dispute highlights competing interpretations of the 2009 arbitration decision and boundary claims between Abyei and Twic Mayardit communities.

In a separate decree, President Kiir appointed William Othwon Awer as the new deputy justice minister. Awer is a lawyer and former governor of Upper Nile State, with prior involvement in constitutional review processes and parliamentary human rights work. Malek Arop had served as deputy justice minister since March 2020, appointed as part of the Revitalised Transitional Government of National Unity.

Why it mattersThe Deputy Minister of Justice and Constitutional Affairs was dismissed due to a controversy over maps of the disputed Abyei area, affecting the interpretation of legal boundaries.

Political Parties Council Opens Coalition Registration

South Sudan's Political Parties Council has begun accepting applications for parties wishing to form pre-election or post-election coalitions.

The Political Parties Council (PPC) in South Sudan has officially opened the registration process for political parties seeking to form coalitions. This initiative commenced on Tuesday, September 1, 2026, allowing all registered political parties to submit applications for pre-election or post-election alliances.

According to PPC Chairperson Hon. Eng. James Akol Zakayo Dhiak, registration forms are available at the council's offices in Hai Malakal, Juba. The deadline for submitting coalition applications intended for participation in the upcoming elections is strictly set for October 22, 2026, which is 60 days prior to the elections.

The council is urging all relevant political parties and stakeholders to take note of the registration requirements and the firm deadline. This process is a crucial step for parties intending to contest in the upcoming electoral period and requires adherence to the specified procedures and timelines.

Why it mattersPolitical parties and their members are affected by the opening of coalition registration, as it directly impacts their ability to organize and participate in upcoming elections.

Parliament Passes Constitutional Amendments to Clear Election Path

South Sudan's parliament unanimously approved amendments to the transitional constitution, removing legal barriers to the December 2026 general elections.

South Sudan’s parliament on Tuesday passed constitutional amendments aimed at removing legal obstacles to general elections scheduled for December 22, 2026. The Transitional National Legislative Assembly (TNLA) approved the Transitional Constitution of South Sudan, 2011 (Amendment) No. 13 Bill 2026 in a closed-door session citing national security concerns. Parliamentary spokesperson Oliver Mori Benjamin confirmed the unanimous passage, stating the bill paves the way for elections by removing standing obstacles.

The bill, first tabled by Justice and Constitutional Affairs Minister Wek Mamer Kuol, separates the election process from constitution-making and census prerequisites. It affects several provisions of the transitional constitution, including Articles 53, 104, and 202. The cabinet, chaired by President Salva Kiir Mayardit, had approved the bill on August 7, deeming the changes necessary for the planned elections.

Lawmakers, however, rejected cabinet proposals that would have reduced the number of seats in the national legislature, specifically those affecting Articles 56, 58, and 94. Opposition member Bol Joseph Agau stated that the retained provisions, which govern parliament's composition under the 2018 peace agreement, do not hinder the December elections. The 2018 agreement outlines 550 seats allocated among various political parties.

Why it mattersThe amendments remove legal obstacles to general elections, directly impacting the political process and the timeline for democratic transition.

Juba road project advances despite supply chain delays

Central Equatoria State officials inspect the 5.6-kilometer Lou–Referendum–Bilpam road, with 1.5 kilometers of asphalt laid, as bitumen shipment issues persist.

Construction of the 5.6-kilometer Lou–Referendum–Bilpam road in western Juba is progressing, with officials reporting 1.5 kilometers of asphalt surfacing completed on one side of the dual carriageway. The project, funded by Presidential Special Programmes, aims to improve urban connectivity and offer alternative routes for residents. State Governor Emmanuel Adil Anthony, alongside local officials, inspected the site to assess progress and address logistical challenges.

Key hurdles include delays in bitumen shipments from Iran due to Middle East supply chain disruptions, as well as administrative and clearance issues at the Mombasa port and Nimule border post. Governor Adil is engaging national authorities to streamline these processes and ensure project support. The road is planned for extension to 11.3 kilometers, eventually connecting Lou directly to Gabat as part of a wider ring road.

Mayor Christopher Sarafino Wani Swaka commended residents for their cooperation and urged continued community support as contractors work to finish the vital thoroughfare. The project is being implemented under the Office of the Senior Presidential Envoy on Special Programmes, with the aim of enhancing transit and urban mobility in densely populated western Juba.

Why it mattersThe road project, funded by presidential programs, is intended to improve urban connectivity and provide alternative transit routes for residents in western Juba.

South Sudan Pursues Global Tobacco Control Treaty Ratification

The Ministry of Health, with WHO support, is convening national stakeholders to advance the ratification of the World Health Organization’s Framework Convention on Tobacco Control.

South Sudan is intensifying efforts to ratify the World Health Organization’s Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (WHO FCTC). The move aims to bolster measures against tobacco use and mitigate its health consequences. The Ministry of Health, in collaboration with the WHO, is organizing a meeting in Juba on September 1, bringing together approximately 50 national stakeholders to discuss the ratification process.

This initiative signifies a commitment to implementing international standards for tobacco control within the country. The WHO FCTC provides a framework for countries to adopt comprehensive policies aimed at reducing tobacco consumption, including measures related to taxation, advertising, and public awareness campaigns. The engagement of national stakeholders is crucial for ensuring broad support and effective implementation of the treaty's provisions.

The ratification process is a key step for South Sudan in its public health agenda, demonstrating a proactive approach to addressing the significant health burdens associated with tobacco-related diseases. The Ministry of Health's efforts, backed by the WHO, are expected to lead to strengthened national policies and programs designed to protect citizens from the harms of tobacco.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens are affected by strengthened measures aimed at reducing tobacco use and its health impacts.
