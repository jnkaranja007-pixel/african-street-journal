South Sudan clashes kill soldiers, civilians in Kapoeta gold mine

Eastern Equatoria State government confirms clashes and deployed forces to restore order after armed attacks.

Clashes erupted over the weekend in the Nakeke gold mining area of Kapoeta South County, Eastern Equatoria State, resulting in the deaths of soldiers and civilians, with several others wounded. The Eastern Equatoria State Government confirmed the violence and stated that the security situation is gradually normalizing. The government deployed security forces to restore law and order following armed attacks, road ambushes, and criminal activities around the mining area.

Caretaker Minister of Information Akileo Mboya Peter Anyanga said authorities are engaging local communities for a peaceful resolution to the problem. He noted that insecurity along the Kapoeta-Ngauro road, marked by attacks and blockages, is a particular concern. An incident on September 18, 2026, saw two people killed after 12 motorcycle riders were attacked, and a bodyguard was wounded during a visit to Nakeke on October 21. Further violence wounded a Chinese national and a driver, and a vehicle was looted and burned.

The opposition National Salvation Front (NAS) accused government forces of a reprisal attack against Toposa youth, claiming dozens of military casualties and civilian deaths. NAS spokesperson Suba Samuel Manase condemned the use of force and called for an investigation into alleged human rights violations. The government's intervention aimed to restore security and ensure legal mining activities, urging community members to use peaceful dialogue for grievances.

Why it mattersClashes in the Nakeke gold mining area of Kapoeta South County have led to civilian deaths and wounded others, disrupting economic activities and security.

South Sudan Ebola preparedness exercise reveals gaps

A simulation exercise in Juba, Yei, and Yambio identified shortcomings in emergency referrals and sample handling.

South Sudan's readiness for a potential Ebola virus disease outbreak shows operational gaps in emergency referrals, sample handling, safe burials, and essential supplies. These issues were revealed by a national simulation exercise conducted from October 19 to 24 in Juba, Yei, and Yambio. Over 100 participants from government, UN agencies, and health partners tested the country's ability to detect, contain, and respond to an outbreak across 10 sites.

Dr. Moses Ongom, WHO Representative (OIC) for South Sudan, stated that while much has been done, more is needed to close remaining gaps in outbreak management. The exercise assessed early detection, notification, case management, laboratory operations, and coordination. The findings highlight the need to strengthen coordination and operational capacity before a potential outbreak. South Sudan's national readiness level stands at 72.5 percent across 11 preparedness pillars, according to Dr. Ader Macar Aciek, Executive Director of NPHI.

Between October 22 and 27, nine new Ebola virus disease alerts were reported nationwide, bringing the cumulative total to 81. Of these, 32 tested negative, 47 were discarded, and two cases in Morobo awaited results. WHO reported over 5.4 million screenings at points of entry since May 2026, with 196,713 screenings conducted between October 22 and 28 at specific locations.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens face potential risks from an Ebola outbreak due to identified gaps in the country's emergency response capacity.

Politics · 2026-11-03

Rival SPLA-IO factions clash in Leer, killing five soldiers

Fighting erupted between forces loyal to Nathaniel Oyet and Regina Joseph Kaba, following Kaba's appointment as acting chairperson.

At least five soldiers died and nine were wounded in clashes between rival SPLA-IO factions in Leer County's Adok Payam last Friday. The fighting occurred between groups loyal to Nathaniel Oyet, who has been acting as SPLM/A-IO chairman, and those supporting Regina Joseph Kaba, recently appointed acting chairperson.

James Yoach Bading, a military commissioner loyal to Oyet, told Radio Tamazuj that Kaba's group attacked their positions in the Nordhiech area. Bading reported two soldiers killed and five wounded from his side, with the injured taken to Leer County Hospital. Dhoal Koryom Lingling, a rival SPLM/A-IO military administrator, confirmed the clashes and stated three soldiers were killed and four injured from his side.

The SPLM-IO splintered after Regina Joseph Kaba's appointment as acting chairperson on August 31, 2026, which was endorsed by the military command on September 3, 2026. Former First Vice President Dr. Riek Machar confirmed on November 2, 2026, during a court session, that Kaba is the substantive acting chairperson, further deepening the group's disarray.

Why it mattersThe clashes between rival SPLA-IO factions directly impact the soldiers involved and contribute to the ongoing instability within the opposition group.

Agriculture · 2026-11-03

South Sudan governor receives three tractors for farming

The equipment, donated by the Presidency, aims to boost food production and improve produce transport to markets.

Western Bahr el Ghazal State governor Sharif Daniel Sharif on Monday received three tractors and six tricycles from the Office of the President. The equipment is designated for agricultural use in Jur River, Raja, and Wau counties.

Teresa Stephen Uding, the state minister for agriculture, stated the machinery will enhance food production and security. She urged county commissioners to ensure the tractors are used only for their intended purpose of food production and the tricycles for transporting harvests to markets.

Jur River County Commissioner Santino Manut Akech thanked President Salva Kiir and the governor for the donation, anticipating a bumper harvest next year. This follows a 2015 donation of 1,000 tractors by President Kiir to all states, whose whereabouts remain unknown.

Why it mattersFarmers in Jur River, Raja, and Wau counties will benefit from new equipment to increase food production and transport harvests.

Culture · 2026-11-03

Derik Cultural Festival returns to Juba

The event will run from November 7 to 14 at the University of Juba’s Main Campus.

The sixth Derik Cultural Festival is scheduled to take place in Juba from November 7 to 14. Organisers aim to foster peace, reconciliation, and civic engagement through various cultural activities at the University of Juba’s Main Campus.

The festival is being held under the theme “Advancing Peace Through Culture.” It honors the late Derik Uya Alfred, who was known for championing cultural development across Sudan.

This event seeks to use cultural expression as a tool for building bridges and understanding within South Sudan.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens attending the festival can engage in activities promoting peace and reconciliation.
