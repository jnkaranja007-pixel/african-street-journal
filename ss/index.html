Türkiye's ambassador to South Sudan dies

Metin Ergin, who represented Türkiye in Juba since 2024, died on November 2 at age 59.

Türkiye’s ambassador to South Sudan, Metin Ergin, died on November 2, 2026, at the age of 59. The Turkish Foreign Ministry announced his passing, with Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan expressing condolences.

South Sudan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation called Ergin a great friend of South Sudan and noted his death as a profound loss to the diplomatic community. The ministry recognized his dedication to strengthening relations between the two countries.

Ergin joined the Turkish Foreign Ministry in 1992 and previously served as consul general in Bosnia and Herzegovina and Belgium. A memorial ceremony is scheduled for November 5 in Ankara, with burial on November 6 in Istanbul.

Why it mattersThe death of Türkiye's ambassador affects diplomatic relations and the diplomatic community in South Sudan.

South Sudan candidate quits governorship race for parliamentary seat

Modi John Molla, a candidate for Central Equatoria State governor, will now seek a parliamentary seat in the December 22 elections.

Central Equatoria State governorship aspirant Modi John Molla has withdrawn from the gubernatorial race to contest a parliamentary seat. Molla, representing Terekeka West Constituency No. 12, announced his decision to seek election to South Sudan’s National Legislative Assembly on December 22. He is a member of the ruling Sudan People’s Liberation Movement (SPLM).

In Jonglei State, veteran politician Kuony War Jock lost his bid for the SPLM nomination for Uror North Constituency No. 8 in the National Legislative Assembly. Gatdet Bayak Riek won the primary election with 152 votes, defeating Jock who received 70 votes. Peter Nin Tut Kuei secured 80 votes in the primary.

Civil society activist Edmund Yakani warned that poor information management could fuel misinformation ahead of the December 22 elections. Yakani, Executive Director of the Community Empowerment for Progress Organization, called on the National Elections Commission to establish media centres to verify information and provide accurate updates on the voter registration process.

Why it mattersVoters in Central Equatoria State and Jonglei State will have different candidates for parliamentary seats, while election credibility is threatened by misinformation.

South Sudan plans 94km dyke for flood protection

The dyke will protect communities in Jonglei State from recurrent flooding while preserving access to water and livelihoods.

The government of South Sudan is planning to construct a 94-kilometre flood-protection dyke in Jonglei State. The dyke will be built between Bor South and Twic East counties. This project aims to shield communities from recurring floods.

A delegation from the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation visited the area on November 2, 2026. The dyke is intended to preserve access to water sources and safeguard the livelihoods of local residents. The plan seeks to mitigate the impact of seasonal flooding.

The project will help protect communities from recurrent flooding. It also aims to ensure continued access to vital water resources for residents and their livestock. The initiative is designed to support the local economy by protecting agricultural activities.

Armed opposition detains eight MTN workers in South Sudan

Workers installing fibre-optic network ambushed and detained in Western Equatoria State

Eight MTN fibre-optic workers were detained by armed opposition forces in Bandala, Western Equatoria State, on October 29, 2026. The workers were travelling in four vehicles expanding the MTN network when they were ambushed between Tambura and Nagero counties.

Western Equatoria State Minister of Information and Communications Noah Philip Isaah stated that one vehicle was later released and its driver sent to Wau to collect an unspecified amount of money demanded for the workers' release. Tambura County Commissioner James Mupaya reported that the armed opposition forces allegedly demanded 35 million South Sudanese pounds (SSP) in compensation for a child's death, claiming the child drank diesel from one of the vehicles.

Isaah condemned the detention, questioning the action despite a reported announcement of resumed free movement for civilians. He urged the immediate and unconditional release of the workers, who are providing essential network services. The minister also advised travellers to seek security clearance before using the Nagero-Tambura-Wau road due to ongoing security concerns.

Why it mattersEight workers installing telecommunications infrastructure were detained by armed opposition forces in South Sudan, disrupting essential services.
