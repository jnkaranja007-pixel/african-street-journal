South Sudan government vows probe after two UN peacekeepers killed

An attack on a UN patrol convoy in Jonglei state killed two Ethiopian peacekeepers and wounded seven personnel, prompting calls for a joint investigation.

South Sudan's government has condemned an attack on a United Nations patrol convoy in Jonglei state that killed two Ethiopian peacekeepers and wounded seven people. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson, Ambassador Apuk Ayuel, stated that the government calls for a joint, thorough investigation to identify those responsible for the incident.

The convoy, operated by the U.N. Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS), came under attack on Monday while traveling from Bor towards Pajut, about 10 km from Jalle. The convoy comprised eight vehicles carrying 38 people, including Ethiopian peacekeepers, U.N. civilian and military personnel, and four South Sudan National Police Service officers.

The government expressed condolences to the Ethiopian government and the families of the two peacekeepers killed. It also affirmed its commitment to working with UNMISS and national authorities to strengthen security coordination and operational cooperation, amid ongoing insecurity and armed violence in the country.

Why it mattersThis event directly impacts the safety and security of international personnel and local law enforcement operating in South Sudan, affecting their ability to maintain peace and order.

Juba City Council proposes property tax cut to 6%

Juba City Council has proposed reducing the property tax rate from 8.3% to 6% of annual rental income to ease the burden on property owners and encourage compliance.

Juba City Council is considering a property tax reduction from 8.3% to 6% of annual rental income, according to Mayor Christopher Wani Sarafino. This proposal aims to provide relief to property owners and potentially increase government revenue by broadening the tax base through improved compliance. The change would be part of amendments to the relevant law.

Mayor Sarafino also advocated for greater coordination of taxes across national, state, and local governments. He stated that businesses currently face multiple collections from different authorities, creating a burden beyond just the number of taxes. The proposed harmonization would streamline payments through a coordinated mechanism, reducing repeated visits from tax collectors.

Currently, Juba City Council collects about seven of the eighteen taxes and fees authorized under Section 74 of the Local Government Act. Sarafino noted that the council limits its collections to avoid excessive pressure on residents and businesses, emphasizing that taxation decisions should consider economic conditions and the public's ability to pay.

Why it mattersProperty owners in Juba could see a direct reduction in their tax obligations, and businesses might experience a more streamlined tax collection process, affecting their operational costs and compliance efforts.

South Sudan graduates 50 rangers to boost anti-poaching efforts

Fifty wildlife rangers completed a two-month training program to strengthen protection for Badingilo National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Fifty wildlife rangers have graduated from an intensive two-month training program aimed at strengthening efforts to combat poaching and protect wildlife in South Sudan’s Badingilo National Park. This group represents the third batch of rangers to complete the training, which covered wildlife management, law enforcement, tactical skills, and weapons handling.

The Ministry of Wildlife Conservation and Tourism, in partnership with African Parks, facilitated the training. The curriculum focused on patrol procedures, weapons safety, appropriate use of force, law enforcement standards, and anti-poaching operations, alongside discipline, professional conduct, restraint, and respect for local communities.

These new rangers are expected to increase patrol coverage and strengthen the park’s response to illegal wildlife activities, including the commercial bushmeat trade. Badingilo National Park is part of the Great Nile Migration Landscape, which became South Sudan’s first World Heritage Site after its inscription by UNESCO.

Why it mattersThe deployment of 50 new rangers directly affects local communities and those involved in the commercial bushmeat trade by increasing enforcement against illegal wildlife activities in Badingilo National Park.

Central Equatoria lifts Juba sports ban with new security rules

The Central Equatoria State Ministry of Youth and Sports has ended its suspension of community sports tournaments in Juba, requiring organizers to obtain official approval and.

Community sports tournaments in Juba can now resume after Central Equatoria State lifted a suspension that began on August 17, 2026. State Minister for Youth and Sports Bush Buse announced the decision on Monday, following a meeting with tournament organizers. The lifting of the ban comes with new conditions aimed at improving security and discipline at sporting events.

Organizers must now obtain approval from the state Ministry of Youth and Sports before holding any competitions. Buse directed them to provide adequate security for referees, technical staff, players, and spectators. He warned that any harassment, violence, or misconduct would not be tolerated, and violations could lead to disciplinary action, including suspension or expulsion of teams or tournaments.

Buse also urged organizers of the Bahr el Ghazal Counties Tournament to peacefully resolve a dispute between Cueibet and Twic Mayardit communities to avoid further disruption. Majok Ayar Chieny, President of the Bahr el Ghazal Tournament, apologized for an incident involving supporters from Cueibet and Twic in Munuki, describing it as unplanned and asserting the communities remain peaceful.

Why it mattersResidents of Juba who participate in or attend community sports tournaments are directly affected by the lifting of the suspension and the new security requirements.

South Sudan Immigration suspends 'silent search' operations on migrants

The Directorate of Immigration, Passport, and Citizenship has halted its controversial “silent search” operations aimed at verifying the credentials of foreign nationals.

South Sudan’s Directorate of Immigration, Passport, and Citizenship has suspended its “silent search” operations targeting migrants, according to the Sudans Post. This decision follows concerns regarding the treatment of foreign nationals within the country. The operations were designed to verify the credentials and immigration status of migrants.

The South Sudan National Police Service ordered all immigration authorities to suspend the exercise, stating it is a national initiative requiring broader consultation. This includes discussions with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, foreign embassies, and other relevant authorities, as reported by Eye Radio.

Lieutenant General Achol Cyier Rehan, Director General of the Directorate of Civil Registry, Nationality, Passports and Immigration, confirmed that no approval or authorization for the exercise had been issued. The police directive, dated August 25, 2026, mandates that any such operation must first be approved by the Minister of Interior and the Inspector General of Police.

Why it mattersMigrants in South Sudan are directly affected as the suspension of these operations changes how their immigration status will be verified and reduces immediate scrutiny.
