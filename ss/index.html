Health · 2026-11-11

South Sudan confirms fatal yellow fever case

A 48-year-old farmer died after travelling from the Democratic Republic of Congo.

South Sudan's health ministry confirmed a fatal yellow fever case in Morobo County, Central Equatoria State. The deceased was a 48-year-old farmer from Okaba village who had travelled to Ariwara in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) approximately two weeks before falling ill.

The farmer developed symptoms including fever, headache, and nosebleeding on October 24, with a body rash appearing later. Blood samples initially tested negative for Ebola but confirmed yellow fever virus infection via RT-PCR. The patient died on November 1, and the ministry has activated an emergency response with the WHO and Africa CDC.

A multidisciplinary team is investigating the case in Morobo County, conducting active case searches and contact tracing. The investigation includes reviewing health facility records and collecting samples to assess local transmission. The ministry also plans to assess community knowledge and strengthen cross-border surveillance with DRC health authorities.

Why it mattersA fatal yellow fever case has been confirmed in South Sudan, prompting an emergency response and investigations into potential local transmission.

South Sudan ranked worst-governed in Africa in 2026 index

The Mo Ibrahim Foundation's 2026 index scores South Sudan 20.4 out of 100, placing it last of 54 countries.

The Mo Ibrahim Foundation has placed South Sudan at the bottom of its 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, ranking it 54th of 54 countries with a score of 20.4 out of 100. The October report puts Somalia one place above it, on 26.6 points, and Sudan above that, on 27.4.

The index assesses governance across security and the rule of law, participation, rights and inclusion, foundations for economic opportunity, and human development. South Sudan gained 0.5 points between 2016 and 2025, which the report labels "Increasing Improvement", but the gain was not enough to lift it off the bottom. The findings point to weak public institutions and poor service delivery.

The index covers all 54 African countries over 2016 to 2025. The South Sudanese government has not yet responded to the report.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens are affected by weak public institutions and service delivery, the report's main concerns.

Bor police release brothers who killed sister over marriage

Two men accused of beating their teenage sister to death for rejecting an arranged marriage have been freed without charges.

Police in Bor, Jonglei State, released two men on Sunday who allegedly beat their 18-year-old sister, Ajok Kelei, to death on November 5. Ajok had rejected two previous engagements and was killed during her third attempt to marry a suitor preferred by her family. Forced marriage, often involving significant bride wealth, is common in South Sudan's pastoralist communities.

The caretaker commissioner of Bor County, Daniel Deng Panchol, stated the suspects' release was painful and that an investigation would still occur. He explained Ajok had twice escaped her family's preferred suitor before being forcibly returned and beaten. The suspects, identified as the girl's brother and cousin, were released after the deceased's family demanded it, citing kinship. Commissioner Deng expressed shock at the release, noting the justification given was that one suspect needed to write secondary education exams.

Bol Deng Bol, executive director of INTREPID South Sudan, condemned the incident, calling it unjustifiable and evidence of a weak justice system, noting two similar cases in Jonglei last year. He argued the police should have forwarded the case to court as a matter of public interest, even without an official complaint. Under South Sudan's Penal Code, murder carries a potential death penalty or life imprisonment.

Why it mattersThe release of suspects in a murder case, particularly one involving a young woman killed for rejecting an arranged marriage, highlights concerns about the justice system and the protection of girls' rights in South Sudan.

South Sudan starts reservoir construction in Eastern Equatoria

Preliminary works have started on three new water reservoirs meant to ease water shortages for people and herds during dry spells.

The government of South Sudan has begun constructing three water reservoirs in Eastern Equatoria State, aimed at giving communities and livestock better access to water as dry spells keep returning. The Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation said in an update on Monday that preliminary works have started.

The ministry is pointing to rising pressure on existing water sources in the state, where dry spells are becoming more frequent. The reservoirs are meant to store water for both household use and herds, so that people and animals are less dependent on the sources already under strain.

The packet does not give a completion date, a budget or a cost for the reservoirs, and it does not say how many people or animals each reservoir will serve.

Why it mattersHouseholds and herders in Eastern Equatoria are set to get more storage for water during dry periods, once the three reservoirs are finished.

South Sudan joins global telecom talks in Qatar

The nation aims to shape international ICT policy and boost digital development.

South Sudan joined over 190 nations at the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) conference in Doha, Qatar, which opened on Monday, November 9. The meeting, themed “Together for a Connected Digital Future,” discusses global telecommunications policies and digital development strategies.

The South Sudanese delegation includes Minister of Information, Communication Technology and Postal Services Ateny Wek Ateny, National Communication Authority (NCA) Board Chairman Engineer Tejwok Ajak, and Director General Engineer Gieth Kon Mathiang. The conference, held every four years, runs until November 27.

The NCA stated the conference offers South Sudan a chance to influence international ICT policy, forge partnerships, and explore cooperation for its digital advancement. The country seeks to expand connectivity, improve affordable access, and promote inclusive digital transformation.

Why it mattersThe nation's participation aims to advance its digital development and ensure its priorities are represented in global decisions.
