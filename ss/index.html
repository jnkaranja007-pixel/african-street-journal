News · 2026-10-28

Nine killed in Bor County communal fighting

Renewed clashes between Abang and Malual communities over land dispute result in nine deaths and a curfew.

At least nine people died in renewed fighting between the Abang and Malual communities in Bor County, Jonglei State, on Monday. Bor County Commissioner Daniel Deng Panchol stated that seven of the deceased were from the two warring communities, with five from Malual and two from Abang. Two additional individuals from neighbouring communities, one woman from Gwala and one man from Deer, also lost their lives, bringing the total to nine.

Commissioner Deng announced that state and county authorities have imposed a curfew from 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. to curb the violence. Joint security forces, including CID and National Security Service personnel, have been deployed to de-escalate tensions and protect civilians. The government also plans to engage youth and elders from both communities to foster restraint and peaceful coexistence.

The recent clashes are rooted in a long-standing territorial dispute between the Abang and Malual communities concerning the Malual-Chaat area. Tensions have been high since late April, following the establishment of a new administrative boma, which exacerbated disagreements over local boundaries and administrative authority.

Why it mattersCommunal fighting in Bor County has resulted in nine deaths and the imposition of a curfew.

UN warns South Sudan president's actions risk conflict

A UN commission says President Salva Kiir's concentration of power amounts to a 'constitutional coup'.

A United Nations human rights commission warned on Tuesday that South Sudan's 2018 peace deal is being dismantled, calling President Salva Kiir's concentration of power a “constitutional coup”. The UN Commission on Human Rights in South Sudan stated that this action risks renewed conflict ahead of elections scheduled for December.

Yasmin Sooka, chair of the commission, told the UN General Assembly that unilateral legislative reforms have displaced the negotiated settlement. She explained that President Kiir dissolved the transitional government, parliament, and state legislatures on September 22, removing vice-presidents and appointing a caretaker administration answerable only to him. First Vice-President Riek Machar remains detained and excluded from the electoral process.

Commissioner Barney Afako told the General Assembly that South Sudan needs a credible political process to address grievances and manage differences peacefully, rather than just elections. He noted that the current political trajectory promotes exclusion and risks engendering disaffection and destruction. The commission urged member states to attach consequences to violations and commitments to diplomacy.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese people face renewed conflict and exclusion from political processes due to President Kiir's actions.

South Sudan launches $267 million Uganda power project

The Ministry of Finance and Planning and the African Development Bank launched the initiative in Juba on Tuesday.

South Sudan has started a $267 million project to import electricity from Uganda, aiming to reduce reliance on diesel and improve power for homes and businesses. The Ministry of Finance and Planning and the African Development Bank launched the initiative in Juba on Tuesday.

This project advances a cross-border connection designed to supply power to South Sudan. The initiative seeks to improve the nation's power infrastructure and reduce the costs associated with current energy generation methods.

The African Development Bank is a key partner in financing this significant infrastructure development. The project aims to provide a more stable and affordable electricity supply across the country.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan will import electricity from Uganda through a $267 million project to improve power supply for homes and businesses.

Central Equatoria schools ordered to use South Sudanese pounds

Schools in Central Equatoria State must charge fees in local currency or face closure, education ministry warns.

Central Equatoria State authorities have ordered all schools to collect tuition and related fees exclusively in South Sudanese pounds, effective from the 2027 academic year. The directive, issued by the Ministry of General Education and Instruction, aims to curb the practice of charging fees in foreign currencies like the US dollar.

Lupai Satimon Andrew, the caretaker Education Minister, stated that schools violating this order risk losing their operating licenses or facing closure. The ministry also cited concerns over exorbitant fees, poor management, inadequate learning environments, and insufficient lesson coverage as reasons for the new regulations.

The ministry also mandated that private school land in urban areas must measure 400 by 400 meters and that schools must adhere to official working hours, with classes starting at 8:00 a.m. and ending at 4:00 p.m. Institutions failing to meet these standards will not be permitted to operate in the state from 2027.

Why it mattersSchools in Central Equatoria State are being compelled to use South Sudanese pounds for fees, affecting parents and students.

Health · 2026-10-28

5,000 enter South Sudan from DRC amid Ebola fears

The UN reports over 5,000 people have crossed into Western Equatoria state fleeing an Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

More than 5,000 people have entered South Sudan's Ibba County in Western Equatoria state from the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) between July and mid-October. The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) stated that nearly 380 arrivals are Congolese nationals, with the rest being South Sudanese returning home. The arrivals are reportedly fleeing an Ebola outbreak in the neighbouring DRC.

During a recent visit to Nabanga Payam, OCHA and partners identified urgent needs for medicines, food, and hygiene kits for women and girls among the new arrivals. Agencies also called for an operational Ebola isolation centre at the Nabanga border crossing to improve screening and disease surveillance. The UN warned that continued Ebola transmission in the DRC poses a risk of the disease spreading into South Sudan.

South Sudan’s Ministry of Health, National Public Health Institute, and UN partners recently conducted a simulation exercise in Juba, Yei, and Yambio counties to test the country's preparedness for suspected Ebola cases. Separately, the UN Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) expressed concern over escalating violence in Warrap state, where clashes on October 26 left 72 dead and 38 wounded.

Why it mattersOver 5,000 people have entered South Sudan from the DRC, requiring humanitarian aid and raising concerns about disease spread.
