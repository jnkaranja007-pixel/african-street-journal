US demands peaceful, self-funded elections in South Sudan

Washington recognizes South Sudan's sovereign right to decide election timing and scope but expects peaceful conditions and domestic funding.

The United States has stated its expectations for South Sudan to hold peaceful elections funded by domestic public resources, while acknowledging the nation's sovereign right to determine the timing and scope of any vote. The U.S. embassy in Juba issued the statement, emphasizing that Washington does not aim to impose a political or ideological model on the country. This position comes amid recent discussions by South Sudan's transitional government regarding Western countries' policies on elections.

The U.S. government's stance reiterates its policy priorities in South Sudan, which include supporting peace and preventing the misuse of public revenue, asserting that such funds should be allocated for appropriate public purposes. While the transitional government has set a December 2026 election date, concerns have been raised by opposition groups and some international actors regarding security, the implementation of the 2018 peace agreement, and the readiness for a credible vote.

South Sudan has postponed elections since gaining independence in 2011, with leaders citing the need to fulfill key aspects of the peace agreement and prepare the nation for balloting. The United States, a significant provider of humanitarian aid, has consistently urged South Sudanese leaders to implement the peace accord, improve governance, and ensure public resources are used as intended.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese leaders are affected by the US expectation that elections must be peaceful and funded domestically, impacting their ability to proceed with the planned vote.

Deputy Speaker Kon Challenges Kiir for Presidency

South Sudan's third deputy speaker, Kornelio Kon, has declared his intention to run for president against Salva Kiir in December 2026 elections.

Kornelio Kon Ngu, South Sudan’s third deputy speaker of parliament, announced his bid to challenge President Salva Kiir in the upcoming December 2026 elections. Kon, who also leads the National United Democratic Front (NUDF), made the declaration at a summit of the National Alliance of Political Parties (NAOPP) in Juba. He criticized the concentration of services and opportunities in the capital, stating that political change is necessary to address disparities across the country.

Kon's announcement has led to a split within the NAOPP, with eight party leaders withdrawing their confidence and accusing him of violating alliance regulations, abusing his position, and making unilateral decisions. They also alleged favoritism in appointments and the promotion of his presidential ambitions without proper endorsement. Kon has not publicly responded to these allegations.

The upcoming elections are intended to end South Sudan’s prolonged transitional period, marking the country's first presidential vote since independence. However, opposition groups have raised concerns about whether the necessary political, security, and institutional conditions are in place for a credible election, especially given previous postponements.

Why it mattersThe presidential election bid by Kornelio Kon challenges the incumbent Salva Kiir, potentially altering the country's political direction and leadership.

Kiir mourns Norway's King Harald as friend and supporter

South Sudan's President Salva Kiir Mayardit remembered King Harald V as a personal friend and a strong supporter of the nation's independence.

President Salva Kiir Mayardit of South Sudan has expressed his condolences following the death of Norway's King Harald V. The President described the late monarch as a personal friend and a significant supporter of South Sudan's journey to freedom.

King Harald V passed away on Friday, August 28, 2026, at Oslo University Hospital. He was 89 years old at the time of his death. His passing marks the end of a long reign and a period of diplomatic relations between Norway and South Sudan.

The President's remarks highlight the historical ties and personal connections that have existed between the leadership of South Sudan and the Norwegian monarchy. King Harald's support is noted as having been instrumental during the nation's struggle for independence.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese leaders are affected by the death of a foreign head of state who was described as a personal friend and supporter of the nation's independence.

Former Lainya County Commissioner Eresto Taban Luate Dies

Central Equatoria State government announced the death of former Lainya County commissioner Eresto Taban Luate, citing his dedication to public service.

Eresto Taban Luate, former commissioner of Lainya County, has died, the Central Equatoria State government announced. The office of Governor Emmanuel Adil Anthony issued a condolence statement but did not specify the cause of death. Luate previously served as commissioner during the defunct Yei River State and as Security Coordinator for the Greater Yei Counties. He was also a member of the State Land Commission and the SPLM Veterans League in Lainya County at the time of his passing.

Governor Adil described Luate as a courageous and committed public servant who dedicated his service to his people and Central Equatoria State. The governor extended his sympathies to Luate’s family, friends, and colleagues. Luate's tenure as commissioner occurred during the period when South Sudan operated under a system of 32 states, a structure that was later abolished in 2020.

The announcement of Luate's death comes as Yei River County commemorated the International Day of the Disappeared, an event focused on raising awareness for missing persons and supporting their families. Speakers at the event called for peace to prevent future disappearances and urged the public to report cases of missing individuals.

Why it mattersThe death of a former county commissioner and security coordinator affects political leadership and public service continuity in Central Equatoria State.

South Sudan Nutrition Outreach Offers Lifeline Amidst Crisis

Temporary health services in Tonj provide critical support to mothers and children facing severe acute malnutrition due to widespread insecurity and poor access.

In Machar Ayuel village, mothers gather under a tree for nutrition screenings, as Amref Health Africa provides essential services in the absence of a permanent health center. Wut Akol, whose nine-month-old daughter Abuoch Bak was diagnosed with severe acute malnutrition, found the outreach program a vital resource. Previously, reaching a clinic meant a three-hour journey, a daunting prospect when her daughter fell ill with severe symptoms. The program has since enrolled Abuoch and provided necessary supplements, leading to a visible improvement in her health.

Wut's situation reflects a broader nutrition crisis in South Sudan, where an estimated 7.8 million people faced severe food insecurity between April and July 2026. Over two million children under five and one million pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to need treatment for acute malnutrition. This crisis is exacerbated by conflict, displacement, rising prices, disrupted markets, disease outbreaks, flooding, and limited humanitarian access, turning common illnesses into life-threatening emergencies.

For pregnant women like 19-year-old Yom Akol, accessing care is further complicated by distance and seasonal flooding that makes travel impossible. Discovered to be malnourished by Amref's community volunteers, Yom received nutrition supplements and a ration card. However, she highlights the ongoing need for medicine and general healthcare for the sick in her community, underscoring the multifaceted challenges faced by those in remote areas.

Why it mattersMothers and young children in rural South Sudan are affected by a severe malnutrition crisis, facing life-threatening conditions due to lack of access to healthcare and essential supplies.
