Drone attack displaces 8,000 in Abyei

More than 8,000 people fled their homes after a drone strike killed over 220 civilians at Amiet Market, according to local authorities.

Over 8,000 people have been displaced from northern Abyei following a drone attack on Amiet Market that killed more than 220 individuals. The incident occurred on Monday, October 19, in the disputed area between South Sudan and Sudan. Local authorities reported that 220 people were killed and dozens injured, with some victims buried in mass graves. The South Sudanese government has attributed the attack to the Sudan Armed Forces and summoned the Sudanese ambassador.

Caretaker Minister of Information and Spokesperson for Abyei, Yohana Akol, stated that the displaced are seeking refuge in Abyei town and surrounding areas, with many sheltering under trees. He appealed to humanitarian organizations and United Nations agencies for urgent assistance. Residents of the Abyei Administrative Area staged a peaceful protest on Tuesday to condemn the strike and demand better civilian protection. They submitted a petition to the UN Interim Security Force for Abyei (UNISFA) urging an investigation and referral to the UN Security Council.

The attack has prompted condemnation from the South Sudanese government, with President Salva Kiir's office calling for an investigation and accountability. The government has summoned the Sudanese ambassador to discuss the incident. The protest, involving community leaders, civil society, youth, and women, highlighted concerns over civilian safety and the impact of the attack.

Why it mattersOver 8,000 people are displaced and seeking urgent humanitarian aid following a deadly drone attack.

South Sudan parties register election coalitions

The Political Parties Council has approved four pre-election coalitions, comprising 25 parties, ahead of the December 2026 general elections.

South Sudan’s Political Parties Council (PPC) has set Thursday, October 22, as the deadline for political parties to register pre-election coalitions for the December 2026 elections. The council announced Wednesday that four coalitions, made up of 25 parties, had met the legal and regulatory criteria for registration. PPC Chairperson James Akol Zakayo stated in Juba that these coalitions were officially entered into the council’s register after an extraordinary session.

The four approved coalitions are the National Agenda Coalition of Political Parties (NAC), the Coalition of National Alliance of Political Parties (CNA), the South Sudan Opposition Alliance (SSOA), and the National Alliance of Political Parties (NAPP). Zakayo detailed that NAC includes 10 parties, CNA has six, SSOA has five, and NAPP has four. The council urged the 25 constituent parties to finalize outstanding internal documentation, secure signatures, and make required legal deposits before the deadline.

Registration certificates are expected to be issued by Monday, October 26. Zakayo emphasized that the deadline adheres to a legal requirement for coalitions to register at least 60 days before the election, which is scheduled for December 22, 2026. Parties submitting registrations after Thursday will not be considered.

Why it matters25 political parties are affected as they have registered pre-election coalitions to participate in the upcoming general elections.

Kiir removes longtime minister Lomuro from cabinet

President Salva Kiir has replaced Martin Elia Lomuro, who served for nearly two decades, with Bol Wek Agoth as minister of cabinet affairs.

South Sudanese President Salva Kiir Mayardit has removed Martin Elia Lomuro, the minister of cabinet affairs, after nearly two decades in government. Lomuro was first appointed to a ministerial role in 2005. Bol Wek Agoth, the former governor of Warrap State, has been appointed to replace him. The decrees were broadcast on Wednesday night by the South Sudan Broadcasting Corporation.

Hussein Abdelbagi Akol, formerly a vice president, was appointed presidential adviser for international relations and foreign affairs, replacing Costello Garang Riiny. Angelo Ngot Mayar was named caretaker governor of Warrap State, succeeding Agoth. Lomuro's removal follows his October 20 order assigning acting ministers to national ministries lacking substantive caretaker ministers, a move intended to maintain administrative oversight.

Lomuro began his ministerial career in October 2005 in the autonomous Government of Southern Sudan. He served as minister of agriculture and forestry, then minister of animal resources and fisheries, before taking over the cabinet affairs portfolio in July 2013. He retained this position through successive administrations and in the caretaker government formed in September 2026.

Why it mattersThe removal of a long-serving minister and other appointments affect the coordination and administration of government business ahead of scheduled elections.

UK gives £2.35 million for El Niño risks in South Sudan

The United Kingdom has announced additional funding to help mitigate climate shocks that threaten food production and vulnerable communities.

The United Kingdom has pledged an additional £2.35 million to South Sudan to address the risks posed by El Niño. The funding aims to protect food production and the livelihoods of communities vulnerable to climate shocks. UK Ambassador Rebecca Fabrizi announced the new financial support during the start of her term in the country.

El Niño is a climate pattern that can cause extreme weather events, including droughts and floods, which have a significant impact on agricultural output and food security in South Sudan. This new funding is intended to bolster resilience against these anticipated climate challenges.

The UK's contribution is designed to support interventions that will help safeguard essential resources and reduce the impact of climate-related disasters on the population. Ambassador Fabrizi's statement highlighted the UK's commitment to supporting South Sudan's efforts to manage climate change effects.

Why it mattersFarmers in South Sudan are affected by the threat of climate shocks to their food production and livelihoods.

Revenue authority pledges to cut customs delays

The South Sudan Revenue Authority will simplify procedures and speed up goods clearance to reduce business costs, the commissioner general announced.

The South Sudan Revenue Authority (SSRA) has pledged to streamline customs procedures and address delays affecting clearing agents and traders nationwide. Commissioner General Rizig Dominic Samuel assured stakeholders during a Tuesday meeting with the South Sudan Freight Forwarders Association that the authority is committed to simplifying processes and ensuring the timely release of goods once requirements are met.

The meeting, which included discussions on challenges at the Nimule border and Juba International Airport, highlighted how border delays and complex clearance procedures increase business costs. The freight forwarders association called for measures to improve efficiency and facilitate legitimate trade. The SSRA also noted ongoing training initiatives aimed at improving clearing agents' understanding of customs laws and procedures to curb unlicensed intermediaries.

Rizig stressed the importance of accurate customs declarations and complete documentation, warning against under- or over-declaration of goods. He directed the Customs Compliance and Enforcement Division to strengthen efforts against smuggling and illicit trade to protect government revenue and secure the country’s borders.

Why it mattersTraders and clearing agents are affected by increased business costs due to border delays and complex customs procedures.
