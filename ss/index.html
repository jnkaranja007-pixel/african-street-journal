Amnesty says South Sudan and UNMISS failed to prevent Abiemnom massacre

Amnesty International says more than 200 people were killed in Abiemnom in March despite warnings given at least two days before the attack.

Amnesty International has accused South Sudan's government and the United Nations Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) of failing to prevent the Abiemnom massacre in March, despite receiving warnings of an impending attack. The human rights organisation said both had information about the planned assault but did not take adequate steps to protect civilians. Between 1,000 and 1,500 armed men from Mayom County attacked Abiemnom town on March 1, killing more than 200 people, including children.

South Sudanese authorities reported at least 213 deaths, including 130 civilians, 76 security personnel and seven foreign nationals. Amnesty said the attackers burned homes, looted property and damaged parts of the local hospital during the four-hour assault. About 1,000 people had sought protection at the UNMISS base on the outskirts of the town after tensions escalated 48 hours before the attack.

Amnesty questioned why UNMISS deployed 40 additional Formed Police Unit personnel after the massacre rather than before it. It also documented seven killings outside the base's rear gates, and satellite images showed more than 230 structures destroyed by fire. Amnesty has called for an independent investigation, and the government has not replied to its written requests for comment.

Why it mattersSurvivors and the families of the dead are still waiting for an investigation, and Amnesty is urging authorities to provide medical, psychosocial and livelihood support.

Security · 2026-11-13

Gunmen kill two in ambush on SPLM delegates in Mundri

Gunmen attacked a vehicle carrying SPLM delegates returning from party primaries in Mundri East County, killing a former journalist and a former teacher.

Two people, including former South Sudan Broadcasting Corporation (SSBC) journalist Veronica Lucy Gordon, were killed when gunmen ambushed a vehicle carrying SPLM delegates in Mundri East County on Thursday evening. The attack happened around 7:00 PM in Miri Loko village as the delegates travelled from Lui Payam to Mundri West.

The attackers targeted the last vehicle in the convoy, killing Gordon and former teacher Justin Akim on the spot. Gordon had recently been nominated to contest a women's seat in the National Legislative Assembly. Two other victims suffered gunshot wounds and are being treated at Lui Hospital, according to caretaker Minister of Information Noah Issa.

Issa described the attackers as bandits and said their identity and motive remain unclear. Several delegates reportedly fled into nearby bushes during the attack and are still unaccounted for, according to Sudans Post.

Why it mattersFamilies of the dead and wounded, and the SPLM delegates still unaccounted for, face an unresolved attack with no named suspects or motive.

South Sudan ministry demands 70% local staff on donor-funded projects

The agriculture ministry wants UN agencies such as FAO and UNOPS to fill most project jobs with South Sudanese graduates, leaving 30 percent for foreigners.

The Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security has told international development agencies that at least 70 percent of employees on donor-funded projects should be South Sudanese. The ministry's undersecretary, Morris Aduol Dut, named the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the United Nations Office for Project Services (UNOPS), urging them to prioritise local hiring in their programmes.

Aduol made the call on Thursday at the annual SSHARP Resilience Learning Fair in Juba, organised by FAO, the World Food Programme and the THRIVE Consortium. He said many young graduates had come to his office seeking work, and he criticised delays in projects where communities were still waiting for services despite available funding. He cited sites he had visited in Renk and Malut in Upper Nile State.

The UK's Deputy Development Director at the British Embassy in Juba, Duncan Overfield, said UK-funded work through the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) is partnering with local organisations to help vulnerable households diversify livelihoods and adapt to climate change. He reported progress in savings and lending, with communities using loans to start and expand businesses.

Why it mattersGraduates who cannot find work in aid-funded projects would gain the first claim on roughly seven in 10 of those jobs if the ministry's quota is adopted.

Politics · 2026-11-13

Machar asks court to hear urgent complaint over his defence team

Former First Vice President Riek Machar raised the matter during the Special Court's 128th session, saying the defence needed the judges to act.

Former First Vice President Riek Machar raised another security concern on Wednesday before the Special Court trying him and seven co-defendants. He asked the judges to consider an urgent complaint involving his defence team during the court's 128th session at Freedom Hall in Juba.

Machar said the defence had a complaint it needed the court to address. The report does not give the nature of the security concern or the details of the complaint involving the defence team.

The trial is continuing at Freedom Hall in Juba, with Machar and his seven co-defendants still before the court.

Why it mattersThe judges must now decide whether to take up the complaint, which concerns the defence team handling Machar and his seven co-defendants.
