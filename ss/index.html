Riek Machar praises late General Joseph Lagu

Machar, detained in a cell, called Lagu a selfless leader who prioritized peace and self-determination.

Former First Vice President Riek Machar on Wednesday described the late General Joseph Lagu as a leader who was not greedy for power. Machar, currently detained, conveyed his condolences in a message during the 125th session, praising Lagu's commitment to peace and self-determination for Southern Sudan.

Machar stated that Lagu deserved recognition for his contributions to the liberation struggle and for prioritizing the welfare of the people. He noted that Lagu left leadership to someone not originally part of the movement, highlighting his selfless nature. Lagu was a key figure in the Anyanya movement and helped negotiate the 1972 Addis Ababa Agreement.

The Addis Ababa Agreement ended the first Sudanese civil war and granted Southern Sudan regional autonomy for a decade. Lagu, born in 1931 in Eastern Equatoria, became leader of the Southern Sudan Liberation Movement in 1968. He later served as president of the High Executive Council and Sudan's second vice-president before retiring from public service.

Why it mattersDetained former First Vice President Riek Machar is speaking about the legacy of General Joseph Lagu, a significant figure in South Sudan's history.

South Sudan secures $267 million for Ugandan electricity

The funding will extend power from Nimule to Juba and other major towns, aiming to boost reliability and affordability.

South Sudan's Ministry of Finance and Planning and the African Development Bank (AfDB) have agreed on $267 million to extend electricity from Uganda into the country. The project, launched on Wednesday, will bring power from Nimule to Juba and other major towns, improving supply reliability and affordability. This initiative aims to integrate South Sudan's power grid with Uganda's through a new interconnection.

Benjamin Ayali Koyongwa, the Finance Ministry's Undersecretary for Planning, thanked the AfDB and partners for their support during the launch. Fouzia Haji of the AfDB stated that electricity is vital for households and businesses and will support economic development in both nations. The launch was attended by officials from the Ministries of Energy and Dams, and Finance, along with Ugandan representatives.

The project follows the recent commissioning of electricity supply from Uganda to Nimule in Eastern Equatoria State. This extension is part of wider efforts to increase access to reliable electricity in South Sudan via regional power connections. The AfDB noted that electricity is essential for economic growth once the project is completed.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese households and businesses will benefit from improved electricity supply and affordability.

South Sudanese children demand climate action

Young participants in a Juba consultation called for climate-resilient schools and early warning systems.

Children in South Sudan have urged the government and world leaders to invest in practical measures against climate change impacts like flooding and drought. During a climate consultation in Juba on Thursday, young participants adopted a declaration demanding climate-resilient schools, accessible early warning systems, and emergency shelters. They described climate change as a child-rights crisis threatening education, food, water, and healthcare, especially for vulnerable communities.

Climate advocate Siama Santino, 17, shared how heat waves in 2024 and 2025 disrupted learning, and flooding in Bor made school travel difficult. The children's declaration stated that COP31 must deliver climate justice and action, not just promises. Some families in affected communities are reportedly eating only once a day due to crop failures and flooding.

The young participants also called for increased investment in climate education, safer schools, clean water access, and emergency services tailored to children's needs. They highlighted the heightened risks for children with disabilities, displaced children, and those in remote or conflict-affected areas. The demands come ahead of the COP31 climate conference in Türkiye.

Why it mattersChildren in South Sudan face disruptions to their education and daily lives from climate-related hazards like flooding and extreme heat.

South Sudan agrees to transfer Petronas oil shares to Russia

Zarubezhneft will take over stakes in DPOC and GPOC following Petronas' withdrawal.

South Sudan has agreed in principle to transfer former Malaysian oil company Petronas' shares in two oil consortiums to Russia's state-owned Zarubezhneft. The preliminary agreement was formalized through a Memorandum of Understanding and a Letter of Acceptance signed in Moscow on October 29. This move follows Petronas' official withdrawal from South Sudan's oil industry on August 7, 2024, after a previous sale attempt to Savannah Energy failed.

The shares are in the Dar Petroleum Operating Company (DPOC) and Greater Pioneer Operating Company (GPOC). South Sudan's Minister of Petroleum, Awow Daniel Chuang, and Russian Energy Minister Sergey Tsivilev were present at the signing. Nile Petroleum Corporation (NilePet) described Zarubezhneft as a suitable partner with experience in increasing production and improving infrastructure.

The transfer, discussed on the sidelines of the third Russia-Africa Summit, is expected to strengthen South Sudan's oil partnership with Russia. Zarubezhneft has over 50 years of international experience in developing oil and gas fields, particularly challenging ones. This agreement aims to create opportunities for greater economic cooperation and technical development in South Sudan's oil sector.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan's oil production and infrastructure development will be impacted by the transfer of shares to a new Russian operator.

South Sudan to hold state funeral for General Joseph Lagu in Juba

The family of the late General Joseph Lagu Yanga has accepted President Salva Kiir's offer of a state funeral and burial in the capital.

The family of the late General Joseph Lagu Yanga has welcomed President Salva Kiir Mayardit's decision to grant him a state funeral, confirming his burial will be in Juba. Ambassador John Andruga, speaking for the family, stated that consultations with close relatives confirmed their agreement with the state funeral in Juba, as a village ceremony was not feasible. Andruga noted that Lagu was a national figure whose legacy belonged to all of South Sudan, not just his Madi community.

General Joseph Lagu Yanga died in Juba earlier this week at the age of 95. His death prompted tributes for his role in South Sudan’s liberation struggle, including leading the Anyanya One movement in 1963. Under his command, the movement gained momentum, leading to the 1972 Addis Ababa Agreement that granted regional autonomy to Southern Sudan.

Lagu later served as president of the High Executive Council of the autonomous Southern Sudan region from 1978 to 1980 and as Sudan’s second vice president. He also represented Sudan at the United Nations in New York from 1990 to 1992. Lagu remained influential in South Sudan’s political history, supporting self-determination before the 2011 independence referendum.
