UK gives South Sudan $2.8 million for flood preparedness

Funding will support early flood preparedness and response activities in Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states.

The United Kingdom has provided 2.8 million US dollars to the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the World Food Programme (WFP) for flood preparedness in South Sudan. This funding targets vulnerable communities in Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states, which face increasing threats from climate-related disasters like floods and drought.

The programme will deliver early-warning information, cash assistance, livelihood support, and emergency supplies to help families safeguard their food, livestock, and assets. Sebastian Fouquet, the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office’s Development Director in South Sudan, stated that climate shocks worsen the country’s humanitarian situation, and acting on early warnings helps families protect their resources.

The initiative, running until March 2027, will also strengthen community-based early-warning systems and coordinate with government authorities. This contribution brings the UK's total funding for El Niño-related early action in South Sudan to over £2.85 million, aiming to reduce losses and protect livelihoods before disasters strike.

Why it mattersVulnerable communities in Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile states will receive support to prepare for and respond to climate-related disasters like floods.

South Sudan police arrest two drivers after pedestrian death

Two drivers are in custody following a fatal accident on the Juba-Nimule highway, with investigations ongoing.

Police in Nimule, Eastern Equatoria State, have arrested two drivers in connection with a fatal road accident on the Juba-Nimule highway. Investigators are working to determine which vehicle struck and killed a pedestrian near the Moli area. The driver of a clearing and forwarding company vehicle initially reported finding the body and was subsequently detained on suspicion of involvement.

Lieutenant Colonel David Khasmiro confirmed that multiple truck drivers were detained as authorities struggled to identify the vehicle responsible. The drivers of a small car and a truck are currently in custody. Second Lieutenant Ochaya James, a police investigator in Magwi County, is also investigating a report of a truck involved in a fatal accident in Nimule and another incident in Jokoki Village.

Ohide Gabriel, an activist with the Women Initiative for Humanitarian Development Organization, condemned the accidents and urged law enforcement to ensure accountability. The incidents have renewed concerns about pedestrian safety on the busy highway, which connects South Sudan to Uganda and is used by heavy trucks, commercial vehicles, and passenger transport.

Why it mattersTwo drivers are being investigated for a fatal accident on a major South Sudan highway, raising concerns for pedestrian safety.

South Sudan seeks Russian investment in key sectors

President Salva Kiir invited Russian investors to develop agriculture, oil, mining, and tourism during the Russia-Africa Summit.

South Sudanese President Salva Kiir urged Russian and foreign investors to fund projects in agriculture, petroleum, mining, and tourism. Speaking at the Russia-Africa Summit in Moscow on Wednesday, October 28, Kiir stated that the nation's abundant natural resources present investment opportunities. He expressed support for the Russia-African Partnership Forum Action Plan 2027-2029 to foster mutual benefits.

President Kiir met with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow, where Putin announced an increase in the scholarship quota for South Sudanese students to 40 for the 2026-2027 academic year. The two leaders discussed expanding bilateral trade and economic cooperation, with Putin noting that the potential of their relationship had not been fully realized. South Sudan's Petroleum Minister, Awow Daniel Chuang, also met with Russian Energy officials regarding the transfer of Petronas' oil shares to Zarubezhneft.

The Russia-Africa Summit, held on October 28-29, brought together African and Russian leaders to discuss trade, energy, technology, peace, and development. President Kiir advocated for a fair international order where global institutions serve all nations equally. He emphasized strengthening economic ties through mutual investment and fostering global economic and physical security.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens will have increased opportunities for higher education in Russia, and the nation aims to attract foreign investment to develop its natural resources.

South Sudanese children demand climate action

Young participants adopted a declaration in Juba calling for climate-resilient schools and early warning systems.

Children in South Sudan have urged the government and world leaders to invest in measures protecting their education, health, and livelihoods from climate change impacts like floods and extreme heat. During a consultation in Juba on Thursday, young participants adopted a declaration demanding climate-resilient schools, accessible early warning systems, and emergency shelters ahead of the COP31 climate conference.

The children described climate change as a child-rights crisis, warning that disasters threaten access to education, food, water, and healthcare, especially for displaced communities. Climate advocate Siama Santino, 17, shared how heat waves in 2024 and 2025 disrupted learning and flooding in Bor made school travel difficult, stating, “Every morning when you wake up, you have to think of how much you’re going to move in water or first remove your shoes to reach school.”

The declaration stated that COP31 must deliver climate justice and action to every child globally, not just promises. Children also reported that climate shocks damage homes and crops, worsen food insecurity, and expose families to health risks, with some eating only once a day due to crop failures or flooding.

Why it mattersChildren in South Sudan face disruptions to their education, health, and livelihoods due to climate-related disasters like floods and heatwaves.

Renk depot explosions injure civilians and soldiers

South Sudan's army blames high temperatures for blasts at ammunition depots in Upper Nile State.

Explosions at the South Sudan People’s Defence Forces (SSPDF) First Division ammunition depots in Renk town, Upper Nile State, injured civilians and soldiers on Thursday night. The blasts occurred around 9 p.m. at the Jamus headquarters, starting at the armoured unit’s storage facility and spreading to the artillery depot.

Major General Kulang Mayen, commander of the SSPDF First Division, stated that high temperatures, reaching 43 degrees Celsius on Thursday, may have triggered the explosions, ruling out external interference. Renk Civil Hospital treated civilians for shrapnel wounds, and an investigative committee has been formed to determine the exact cause.

The successive blasts caused panic among residents near the military headquarters, with explosions heard across the town. Local authorities reported that the situation had calmed, and patrols were assessing conditions, though the exact number of injured and the extent of damage were not yet officially confirmed.
