South Sudan and Sudan clash at UN over Abyei election plan

Khartoum warns Juba's move to include the disputed region in December 2026 polls violates agreements.

South Sudan and Sudan clashed at the UN Security Council over Juba's plan to include the disputed Abyei region in its December 2026 general elections. Sudan's UN representative, Al-Harith Idriss Al-Harith Mohamed, stated the move violated agreements and could undermine Abyei's final status resolution. South Sudan's representative, Sabino Edward Nyawella Amaikwey, defended the plan, arguing the rights of the Ngok Dinka population must be considered within the context of Abyei's unresolved status.

UN Secretary-General António Guterres warned in an April 16 to October 1 report that including Abyei in South Sudan's electoral districts could significantly increase political tensions. The final status of Abyei has remained unresolved since a 2005 peace agreement, which planned for a referendum that has not occurred due to disagreements over voter eligibility.

The Security Council also discussed the presence of South Sudanese forces south of the River Kiir and Rapid Support Forces in the north. Sudan raised concerns over both forces and the RSF's naming of a representative to the Abyei Joint Oversight Committee. South Sudan defended its forces' deployment as stabilizing and protecting civilians.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens and the Ngok Dinka population in Abyei are directly affected by the dispute over election inclusion and the unresolved status of their region.

South Sudan launches cancer registry

The new registry aims to improve data collection and analysis of cancer cases nationwide.

South Sudan inaugurated its first National Population-Based Cancer Registry at Juba Teaching Hospital on Thursday. The registry will help health authorities collect, analyze, and report information on cancer cases, including types, distribution, and treatment outcomes. This initiative aims to address the long-standing struggle with a lack of reliable national cancer data, which has made understanding the disease's scale difficult.

Caretaker health minister Dak Doup Bichok stated that previous cancer figures, estimated at 8,124 new cases and 5,128 deaths within five years, were modelled and largely based on data from neighboring countries. Dr. Albino Amum, Director of the Cancer Registry at Juba Teaching Hospital, explained that the registry will gather information from public and private clinics through trained staff, following recommendations from the African Cancer Registry Network.

The registry, initially based at Juba Teaching Hospital, faces challenges including limited funding, a shortage of trained registrars, fragmented health information systems, and poor communication. The initiative is supported by the World Health Organization, UNICEF, Concern for Women, and the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, which are providing essential equipment.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens affected by cancer will benefit from improved data collection, leading to better policy decisions, planning, resource allocation, and treatment.

Aid cuts risk cholera for 150,000 in South Sudan

Médecins Sans Frontières warns of disease outbreaks in Bentiu and Malakal camps due to reduced water, sanitation, and healthcare services.

Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) warned on Wednesday that about 150,000 internally displaced people in South Sudan's Bentiu and Malakal camps face increased risks of cholera and other waterborne diseases.

Funding cuts have reduced access to clean water, sanitation, and primary healthcare, with Bentiu camp hosting 111,000 people facing sanitation challenges and Malakal's water supply for 40,000 people at risk. Healthcare services in Malakal have also declined after the International Medical Corps withdrew.

MSF is calling for urgent funding to maintain essential services and prevent a public health emergency, noting South Sudan recorded nearly 110,000 cholera cases and 1,970 deaths since 2024.

Why it mattersDisplaced people in Bentiu and Malakal camps are at risk of cholera and other waterborne diseases due to reduced access to essential services.

South Sudan finance minister pledges reforms

UK reaffirms support for financial reforms during the 104th Public Financial Management Oversight Committee meeting in Juba.

South Sudan's caretaker finance minister, Athian Diing Athian, pledged to improve oversight, transparency, and accountability in public fund management. This occurred during the 104th meeting of the Public Financial Management Oversight Committee in Juba, which he co-chaired with British Ambassador Rebecca Fabrizi.

Minister Athian called for fiscal discipline and efficient use of public resources to enhance service delivery, stating the committee would provide strategic oversight for tangible progress. Ambassador Fabrizi reaffirmed the UK's commitment to supporting credible public financial management reforms in South Sudan.

Updates presented included the integration of the Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS) and the Real-Time Gross Settlement System (RTGS) between the Finance Ministry and the Bank of South Sudan. The meeting also covered public sector salary arrears as of September 2026 and the 2026/2027 budget estimates.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens will benefit from improved service delivery through better management of public funds.

South Sudan dialogue ends without consensus on elections

Political parties challenged the government's plans for December polls, citing absent conditions for credible elections.

South Sudan's inter-party dialogue concluded last month in Juba without reaching consensus, as political parties questioned the government's push for December elections. Analyst Abraham Awolich stated that participants argued conditions for credible polls were absent and called for postponing the December 22 elections and negotiating a new political arrangement.

Awolich, a former managing director of the Sudd Institute, said the talks failed because the government sought endorsement of President Salva Kiir's decisions rather than consensus on the nation's future. Government representatives insisted the elections should proceed regardless of the prevailing political and security environment.

The dialogue was abandoned without publicly agreed resolutions after attempts to draft a final document without all parties' agreement were dropped. Awolich also linked tensions to the recent firing of Martin Elia Lomuro as cabinet affairs minister, suggesting it would further weaken the government.

Why it mattersThe failure of inter-party dialogue means South Sudanese citizens will not have consensus on the conditions for upcoming elections.
