DAFI Scholarship Intake Surpasses Previous Nine-Year Total

The DAFI scholarship program selected 650 students in South Sudan for 2026, exceeding the total number of beneficiaries from 2016 to 2025.

The DAFI scholarship program has selected 650 new students in South Sudan for 2026, a figure that surpasses the total number of beneficiaries supported by the program in the country over the previous nine years. This year's intake includes refugees, returnees, and for the first time, students from host communities. The program, implemented by the Lutheran World Federation in partnership with UNHCR, aims to build human capital for reconstruction and development.

Officials stated that the 2026 cohort was selected from approximately 1,400 applications. Kenyi Beneth Emmanuel, Education Project Manager at the Lutheran World Federation, noted that the program supported 414 students between 2016 and 2025, making the current intake a significant expansion. The inclusion of host-community students, a long-standing request from the government, is also intended to foster peaceful coexistence.

The scholarship provides comprehensive support, including tuition fees, transport, food, medical care, and housing, for students pursuing diploma-level courses at recognized institutions within South Sudan. Priority is given to fields such as education, social work, and medical-related courses that can improve employment prospects. UNHCR Protection Officer Tabasum Noor Jamal emphasized that the scholarship is a partnership requiring students to work hard and complete their studies.

Why it mattersStudents from refugee, returnee, and host communities in South Sudan are directly benefiting from expanded educational opportunities and financial support for their studies.

Governor Declares Saturday Cleaning Day in Juba

Central Equatoria State Governor Emmanuel Adil Anthony has mandated weekly city-wide cleaning exercises to improve Juba's environment and urban management.

Governor Emmanuel Adil Anthony has designated every Saturday as a city-wide cleaning day in Juba, aiming to enhance the capital's cleanliness, greenery, and safety. These weekly exercises, scheduled from 7:00 a.m. to 9:00 a.m., will involve government institutions, communities, businesses, schools, religious bodies, and development partners. The announcement was made during a briefing to the Juba City Legislative Council, where the governor presented a plan for improved urban management and municipal services.

The governor's roadmap prioritizes making Juba clean, green, and safe, with specific calls for better solid waste management systems covering collection, transport, and disposal from markets, streets, and public areas. He also emphasized increased tree planting along major roads and around public facilities, advocating for a "One Citizen, One Tree" initiative to ensure Juba remains liveable for future generations. Stronger urban planning, enforcement of development standards, and protection of public land were also highlighted.

Further initiatives include improving roads and drainage to reduce transport costs and mitigate flooding, simplifying business licensing, and implementing digital tax collection. The governor also stressed the need for enhanced sanitation in markets, better traffic management, designated parking, pedestrian-friendly streets, and long-term public transport planning. Security improvements through closer cooperation among agencies and community policing were also part of the plan.

Why it mattersThis initiative affects businesses by potentially simplifying licensing and tax collection, and by improving market sanitation and infrastructure, which can reduce operational costs.

Armed Youth Kill 26 at South Sudan Barracks

An early morning attack on a military installation in Warrap State resulted in the deaths of soldiers, police, and civilians.

At least twenty-six people, including ten soldiers, six police officers, and four civilians, died after armed youth attacked a South Sudan People’s Defence Forces (SSPDF) barracks in Acong-cong, Warrap State. Eight others were wounded in the incident, which occurred around 5 a.m. The state government stated that the barracks were established to maintain order between the Marial Wau and Apuk Gii communities. An eleven-year-old boy was among the civilians killed.

The Warrap State Ministry of Information and Communication Services reported that six attackers were also killed during the confrontation. The motive behind the attack on the military facility remains unknown, and investigations are underway to establish the cause. Authorities have directed security agencies to identify and apprehend those responsible for the violence and bring them to justice.

The Warrap State government, led by Governor Bol Wek Agoth, extended condolences to the families of the deceased, acknowledging the security personnel who lost their lives while protecting civilians. The government affirmed its commitment to maintaining peace, enforcing the rule of law, and ensuring the safety of civilian lives within the state.

Why it mattersThe attack on the SSPDF barracks in Warrap State directly resulted in the deaths of soldiers, police, and civilians, impacting law enforcement and community safety.

News · 2026-09-05

Ambush kills one traveler, wounds two in South Sudan

Armed elements from Mayom County are accused of attacking motorcyclists on Thursday evening in Abiemnhom County.

A 32-year-old man was killed and two others wounded in a road ambush in Abiemnhom County, Ruweng Administrative Area, on Thursday evening. The victims were traveling by motorcycle between Majak and Awila Payams when they were attacked at 6 p.m. The deceased was identified as Kur Deng Manyror, with the wounded being Mayai Arop Machar, 29, and Kong Mut Majak, 21.

Local authorities expressed surprise at the incident, noting the presence of two military detachments near the ambush site. Police Inspector Captain Majok Machar questioned the army's failure to protect the travelers. Efforts are underway to pursue the assailants, who are believed to be armed criminals from Mayom County in Unity State.

The two injured travelers were initially hospitalized in Abiemnhom before being transferred to Turalei in Warrap State’s Twic County for advanced medical care. One of the wounded requires surgery due to a bullet lodged in his body, necessitating the referral for intensive treatment.

Why it mattersOne traveler was killed and two were injured, affecting their livelihoods and families due to armed criminal activity.
