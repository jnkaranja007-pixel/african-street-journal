MSF warns 150,000 displaced face disease risk

Funding cuts threaten water, sanitation, and health services for people in Bentiu and Malakal camps.

Around 150,000 displaced people in South Sudan's Bentiu and Malakal camps face increased risk of cholera and waterborne diseases due to funding cuts and aid withdrawals. Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) warns that essential water, sanitation, and health services are at risk of collapse in the coming months.

MSF deputy head of operations in South Sudan, Foued Gamoudi, stated that humanitarian needs persist for residents of Bentiu and Malakal, even after the camps were transferred to government authority. In Bentiu, an assessment found approximately 60 people share one functioning latrine, with widespread open defecation.

Displaced woman Nysunday Dobuol, living in Bentiu camp, shared that residents have already endured conflict, flooding, and cholera, and many cannot return home. South Sudan recorded nearly 110,000 cholera cases and 1,970 deaths nationwide since 2024, with Rubkona County accounting for a significant portion of recent cases.

Why it mattersAround 150,000 displaced people in South Sudan's Bentiu and Malakal camps are at risk of cholera and other waterborne diseases due to the withdrawal of aid organizations and reduced access to basic services.

Rumbek North bans alcohol after fatal clashes

Three people died and two were injured during a peace dialogue in Lakes State when armed youths attacked.

Authorities in Rumbek North County, Lakes State, have banned the brewing and sale of local alcohol following clashes that killed three people and wounded two. The violence erupted Tuesday evening at Malueth Payam headquarters during a peace dialogue facilitated by Caretaker County Commissioner Buoi Mayek.

Commissioner Mayek stated that armed youths, reportedly under the influence of alcohol, initiated the conflict between the Pan Adieng and Payueth sub-clans. He has given local chiefs seven days to identify and hand over those responsible for the attacks for investigation. Offenders caught brewing alcohol or possessing ingredients face three months in detention.

Daniel Laat Kon, a coordinator for the Community Empowerment for Progress Organization (CEPO) in Lakes State, condemned the killings and urged the government to disarm armed youths and regulate alcohol. He warned that the combination of firearms and alcohol contributes to killings as South Sudan approaches general elections.

Why it mattersThe ban on alcohol brewing and sale in Rumbek North County follows clashes that killed three people and wounded two.

South Sudan launches mobile courts to clear case backlog

The 30-day initiative in Western Bahr el Ghazal aims to speed up justice and reduce detention times.

The Judiciary of South Sudan, supported by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), has started a 30-day mobile court initiative in Wau and Raja Counties. This program aims to speed up the resolution of pending criminal cases and reduce prolonged pre-trial detention in Western Bahr el Ghazal State.

Justice Andrew Joshua Lado, President of the Court of Appeal for the Bahr el Ghazal Circuit, stated that over 200 remand cases are awaiting trial in prisons across the state. He explained that the mobile court is a practical response to challenges like limited judicial personnel and case backlogs, making justice more accessible.

The initiative, implemented with the Directorate of Legal Administration and Public Prosecution, also seeks to strengthen victim rights and accountability. UNDP representative Felix Cassim reaffirmed the organization's commitment to supporting national justice institutions in improving access to judicial services for all citizens.

Why it mattersPeople in Wau and Raja Counties will experience faster resolution of their criminal cases and reduced pre-trial detention periods.

South Sudan NEC appoints election officials

Returning officers and assistants named for all 10 states and three administrative areas for December 2026 polls.

South Sudan’s National Elections Commission (NEC) has appointed returning officers and their assistants across all 10 states and three administrative areas. NEC Chairperson Gabriel Bol Deng signed the order on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, to prepare for the general elections scheduled for December 2026. Specific appointments include Emmanuel Angelo Maame for Central Equatoria State and Taban Clement Oliver for Western Equatoria State.

The NEC also named officials for Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, Lakes, Jonglei, Upper Nile, and Unity states, as well as for Pibor, Ruweng, and Abyei administrative areas. These officials are responsible for coordinating and supervising electoral activities, including voter registration and polling arrangements, under the National Elections Act, 2012.

Voter registration materials have begun to be distributed, with Unity State receiving 3,300 books and Northern Bahr el Ghazal receiving 6,864. Greater Pibor received 1,397 books, Ruweng 1,364, and Abyei 781. The NEC aims to make registration accessible to all eligible citizens and encourages them to follow official announcements for details.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens will participate in general elections in December 2026, with election officials now appointed and voter registration materials being distributed.

South Sudan plans 94km dyke to stop floods

The government will build a barrier between Bor South and Twic East counties to protect communities.

The government of South Sudan is planning to construct a 94-kilometre dyke to protect communities in Jonglei State from recurring floods. The project aims to preserve access to water and safeguard local livelihoods in the flood-prone areas between Bor South and Twic East counties.

A delegation from the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation visited the region on November 2, 2026, to advance these plans. The dyke is intended to mitigate the impact of seasonal inundation on the affected populations.

This initiative seeks to provide a lasting solution to the challenges posed by flooding in Jonglei State. The government's intervention is designed to offer security to residents and their economic activities.

Why it mattersCommunities in Jonglei State will be protected from recurrent flooding.
