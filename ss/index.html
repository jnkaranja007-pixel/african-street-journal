Health · 2026-11-07

Doctor shot in revenge attack at Cueibet hospital

Relatives of a patient who died at the facility allegedly attacked a doctor on duty on Thursday.

A doctor was critically wounded in a suspected revenge attack at Cueibet County Hospital in South Sudan’s Lakes State on Thursday. Santino Mayom Kau was shot in the chest at about 5 p.m. while on duty. The attackers fled and remained at large.

Lakes State caretaker minister of information William Koji Kirjok stated on Friday that preliminary investigations suggested the attack was linked to revenge. The doctor was transferred to Rumbek State Hospital for treatment. Kirjok said authorities are working with traditional leaders to curb revenge killings.

Simon Makoi Malek, the medical officer in charge of Cueibet County Hospital, said the attack occurred because relatives of a man who died at the hospital believed the attackers came from the same area as the doctor. Daniel Laat Kon, coordinator of CEPO in Lakes State, condemned the attack and urged the state government to address revenge killings and disarm civilians.

Why it mattersA health worker was critically wounded in a suspected revenge attack, highlighting ongoing community disputes and the need for authorities to address violence.

Politics · 2026-11-07

Machar details Nasir clashes from 2021-2024

Former First Vice President Riek Machar testified about violence triggered by arrests, fishing net theft, and firewood collection.

Former First Vice President Dr. Riek Machar detailed deadly clashes in Nasir County, Upper Nile State, between 2021 and 2024. The violence, involving South Sudan People’s Defence Forces (SSPDF) soldiers and local youth, resulted in civilian deaths, property destruction, and displacement. Machar testified during the 127th session of his trial, describing confrontations stemming from disputes over arrests, stolen fishing nets, firewood collection, and child custody.

Machar recounted clashes in February-March 2021 following the arrest of two youth leaders, which led to a standoff between police, chiefs, the commissioner, and the SSPDF over custody. He also described fighting from February 10-12, 2024, triggered by the theft of fishing nets, resulting in casualties on both sides. A separate incident involved soldiers collecting firewood, leading to one youth dead and four civilians wounded, with two children drowning.

The violence forced civilians to flee Nasir town and surrounding villages, with homes looted and burned. Machar stated a helicopter gunship flew over Nasir as a show of force. The Ceasefire and Transitional Security Arrangements Monitoring and Verification Mechanism (CTSAMVM) investigated the February 2024 incident and recommended redeploying SSPDF forces and deploying the Necessary Unified Forces.

Why it mattersCivilians in Nasir County, Upper Nile State, faced deaths, injuries, displacement, and property destruction due to clashes between soldiers and youth.

Dry spells destroy South Sudan crops

Farmers in Eastern Equatoria report maize and sorghum withered by extreme heat and lack of rain.

Prolonged dry spells and extreme heat are destroying crops across several South Sudanese states, threatening harvests and increasing food insecurity. Farmers in Eastern Equatoria, Warrap, Lakes, and Northern Bahr el Ghazal are reporting crop failures as erratic rainfall patterns damage agricultural production. Communities dependent on rain-fed farming and pastoralism face growing worries about their next meal.

In Magwi County, Eastern Equatoria State, cooperative Ochano, head of the farmers’ association, stated that the intense sun had withered maize and sorghum crops. This situation is particularly concerning for households with limited resources to cope with repeated climate shocks. The Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET) noted in September 2026 that widespread rainfall deficits persisted, with some areas receiving less than 60 per cent of normal rainfall.

El Niño, a natural climate pattern involving warming Pacific Ocean waters, is influencing global weather, including South Sudan's rainfall and temperatures. This phenomenon, combined with other climate-related shocks, adds pressure to communities already facing humanitarian and economic challenges. The below-average rainfall predicted by FEWS NET could further reduce crop production and worsen food insecurity across the nation.

Why it mattersFarmers in South Sudan are facing crop failures due to dry spells and extreme heat, directly impacting their food security and livelihoods.

Politics · 2026-11-07

China urges Sudan, South Sudan to resolve Abyei dispute

Beijing warns that escalating security issues and unilateral actions could derail peace talks over the disputed territory.

China's UN ambassador, Sun Lei, called on Sudan and South Sudan to resolve their Abyei dispute through dialogue on Thursday, November 5. He warned that worsening security and unilateral actions could undermine efforts for a political settlement. Sun urged the nations to build on progress in their joint political and security mechanisms to settle Abyei’s final status.

Tensions have risen over South Sudan’s plan to include Abyei in its December 2026 general elections, a move Sudan rejects as a violation of agreements. UN Secretary-General António Guterres warned this plan could significantly increase political tensions. Sudan’s representative, Al-Harith Idriss Al-Harith Mohamed, stated the decision undermined existing agreements, while South Sudan’s representative, Sabino Edward Nyawella Amaikwey, argued the political rights of the Ngok Dinka should not be disregarded.

Ambassador Sun condemned a recent drone attack on Amiet market in Abyei that caused civilian casualties, urging an end to violence and respect for international humanitarian law. He also called for the implementation of the 2011 temporary arrangements for Abyei, including its demilitarised status, and urged the Rapid Support Forces to withdraw. Delays in resolving Abyei’s political status have hindered development and weakened its economic capacity, Sun noted.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens in the Abyei region face increased political tensions and potential insecurity due to unresolved territorial disputes and election plans.

Tech · 2026-11-07

South Sudan central bank warns of digital finance risks

Governor Addis Ababa Othow stated stronger cybersecurity measures are needed as digital infrastructure expands.

South Sudan's central bank governor warned that increasing cybersecurity threats could compromise financial institutions, mobile money, and electronic transactions. Bank of South Sudan (BoSS) Governor Dr. Addis Ababa Othow made this statement on Thursday.

Dr. Othow said stronger cybersecurity measures are needed to safeguard the nation's expanding digital financial infrastructure. The warning comes as South Sudan increases its use of digital financial services.

The central bank's alert points to potential weaknesses in the country's financial technology sector.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan's financial institutions and electronic transactions face growing cybersecurity threats.
