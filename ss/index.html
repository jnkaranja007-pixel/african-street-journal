SSPDF begins six-month disarmament in Wau

Operation aims to remove illegal firearms and improve security, but civil society warns of looting and civilian safety concerns.

The South Sudan People’s Defence Forces (SSPDF) has started a six-month operation in Wau County to disarm civilians and remove illegal firearms. Major General Wol Dhel Theip, the SSPDF’s Fifth Infantry Division Deputy Commander, confirmed the operation began to improve security in the region.

Civil society groups in Western Bahr el Ghazal State are calling for oversight of the disarmament forces due to reports of looting. Stephen Robo Musa, regional coordinator for the Community Empowerment for Progress Organization (CEPO), stated that soldiers found abusing their authority should face legal action. The Paramount Chief of Greater Bagari Payam reported looting, including from hospitals and a court.

Concerns have also been raised about the impact of the insecurity on pupils preparing for Primary Eight examinations, scheduled to begin on Monday. Robo Musa urged the state Ministry of Education to assess examination centres in Wau County and ensure they are safe, suggesting candidates be moved to safer venues within Wau Municipality if necessary.

South Sudan begins voter registration amid logistical strain

The National Elections Commission must deliver materials to remote areas within a two-week window before the February 11, 2027 vote.

South Sudan began voter registration on Saturday, November 14, ahead of general elections scheduled for February 11, 2027. The National Elections Commission (NEC) will run the exercise until November 28 across the country's 10 states and three administrative areas, using 3,161 registration centres.

In Malakal, Upper Nile State, Peter Mayom, chairman of the High Election Committee, said the final consignment of materials arrived on Saturday, but some remote areas were still waiting and expected to receive them within two to three days. He cited difficulties transporting heavy equipment and said the announcement, made only a day before, had left little preparation time. Upper Nile State has 237 centres and 1,659 officials.

In Yambio, Western Equatoria State, Radio Tamazuj observed low turnout at the launch. Simon Bakama Mande, chairperson of the State High Elections Commission, urged eligible citizens to register in large numbers, while Gabriel Bol Deng, chairperson of the NEC, called registration the foundation of a credible electoral process.

Politics · 2026-11-15

South Sudan's elections moved to February 11, 2027

The National Elections Commission's revised calendar sets polling nearly two months past the December 2026 deadline in the election law.

South Sudan's general elections have been rescheduled to Thursday, February 11, 2027, replacing a December 22, 2026 polling date. The National Elections Commission (NEC) issued the revised calendar, which Radio Tamazuj saw on Saturday, and it runs beyond the December 2026 deadline provided for in the law.

Manual voter registration was scheduled to run from November 14 to November 28, with the provisional register due on November 29 and public inspection from December 6 to December 12. Candidate nominations were due to open on November 29, with formal submissions from December 14 to December 20. The final candidate list is scheduled for January 18, 2027.

The 45-day campaign is scheduled to run from December 27 to February 9, with February 10 as a campaign-free day. Results are due to be declared on February 15 and published on February 16. Radio Tamazuj reports that the election law requires the voter register to be published at least three months before polling.

Why it mattersVoters will cast ballots on February 11, 2027, and candidates must file nominations by December 20 under the revised calendar.

Politics · 2026-11-15

South Sudan delays elections to February 2027

The delay was blamed on ongoing political strife and flare-ups of violence, with voter registration complicated because many South Sudanese lack state-recognized identification.

South Sudan announced on Saturday that its first national elections will be delayed from December to February 11, 2027, due to ongoing political strife and flare-ups of violence.

Josephine Achiro, spokeswoman for the National Elections Commission, stated that the election timelines were adjusted according to Article 57 of the commission's law to allow for voter registration and civic education processes.

The World Bank notes that a large portion of South Sudanese lack state-recognized identification, complicating voter registration efforts, with thousands of centers established nationwide for the process.

Why it mattersPresidential and legislative elections, originally planned for December, are now scheduled for February 11, 2027.

Business · 2026-11-15

South Sudan, Sudan sign oil deal

The two nations agreed to reconcile crude oil volumes and boost cooperation in the petroleum sector by June 2027.

South Sudan and Sudan signed a Memorandum of Understanding in Khartoum on Sunday to reconcile crude oil volumes and strengthen cooperation in the petroleum sector. The agreement covers pipeline inspections, oil volume audits, and environmental clean-up operations, with activities expected to conclude by June 2027.

Engineer Lual Chol Dak, South Sudan’s Petroleum Authority Director-General, led the delegation that met Sudan’s Minister of Energy and Petroleum, Engineer Al-Mutasim Ibrahim. The talks focused on shared pipeline infrastructure and the crude oil transit agreement, ensuring the flow of South Sudanese crude oil to international markets.

The deal also includes technical support, emergency response, cross-border equipment movement, and improvements to financial systems for oil transit fees. Sudan reaffirmed its commitment to supplying electricity to Renk town in South Sudan, as both countries aim to maintain oil production and transport despite operational challenges.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan will maintain oil flow to international markets through Sudan, with cooperation on pipeline inspections and financial systems for transit fees until June 2027.
