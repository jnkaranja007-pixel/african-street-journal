Western Equatoria trains election officials for polls

Officials in Yambio received induction on coordinating electoral activities and supporting voter registration.

Western Equatoria State government and the State High Elections Committee (SHEC) launched a two-day training for returning officers in Yambio on Monday. The induction aims to equip officials with skills to coordinate electoral activities and prepare for upcoming elections across the state's 10 counties.

Caretaker Governor Badagbu Daniel Rimbasa pledged government security and logistical support, including electricity for the committee's operations. SHEC Chairperson Simon Bakama stated the training would prepare officers for responsibilities like supporting registration and ensuring personnel are ready for their duties.

The trained officials will serve in Yambio, Nzara, Ibba, Maridi, Mvolo, Mundri East, Mundri West, Ezo, Tambura, and Nagero counties. This initiative is intended to strengthen electoral personnel capacity and improve coordination ahead of the polls.

Why it mattersResidents of Western Equatoria State will participate in upcoming elections after officials received training to manage the process.

EAC adopts regional plan for El Niño

The East African Community will coordinate member states' efforts to address anticipated El Niño effects until February 2027.

The East African Community (EAC) adopted a regional emergency preparedness and response plan on November 6 to coordinate member states' efforts against anticipated effects of a strong El Niño weather event.

The plan aims to address the potential impacts of the El Niño event, which is expected to persist through February 2027, according to the EAC's Joint Sectoral Council.

This coordinated approach seeks to prepare for and manage the consequences of the weather phenomenon across the region.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan, as an EAC member, will be involved in coordinated efforts to prepare for the impacts of El Niño.

South Sudan Media Authority warns against hate speech

The Authority has observed increasing signs of harmful and inflammatory communication across digital platforms.

South Sudan’s Media Authority has warned politicians, their supporters, and social media users against spreading hate speech and inflammatory messages ahead of the December polls. Ajuo Luol Akuai, the managing director, stated on Monday in Juba that unlawful conduct could lead to legal action. The Authority has registered cases of hate speech primarily from online pages linked to political parties in Jonglei, Upper Nile, and Equatoria regions.

Luol Akuai said the Authority has observed increasing signs of harmful and inflammatory communication across digital platforms, raising concerns about its potential impact on public peace and national cohesion. He reminded candidates and media practitioners of their responsibilities in promoting peaceful electoral discussions and responsible communication, urging them to respect opposing views and refrain from statements that could threaten public peace.

The Media Authority plans to launch a public awareness campaign to combat hate speech, misinformation, and disinformation during the electoral period, but this is subject to the availability of necessary resources and support. They are appealing to the government and development partners for financial and technical assistance to conduct this nationwide initiative.

Why it mattersPoliticians, their supporters, and social media users in South Sudan face potential legal action for spreading hate speech ahead of the December elections.

Juba County talks with six markets on administration handover

County authorities met market representatives to discuss strengthening local administration and improving market organisation.

Juba County authorities met with representatives from six markets on Monday to discuss plans for strengthening local administration and improving market organisation. Caretaker County Commissioner Kalisto Lado Foustino Nyigilo Wani engaged leaders from Referendum, Jondoki, Longe, Mangaten, Newside, and Suk Darfur markets regarding a proposed handover of management responsibilities.

The meeting, the first of its kind for these markets, aimed to facilitate dialogue and peacefully resolve issues surrounding market organisation, according to a statement from the Commissioner’s Press Unit. Maj. Gen. Luka Kuot, speaking for the market delegation, welcomed the initiative and acknowledged the county's commitment to taking over administration and management.

Commissioner Kalisto Lado emphasised his administration's commitment to promoting peace and unity among Juba County's 64 tribes, stating that all citizens have the right to conduct business if they respect local laws. Local chiefs of Luri Payam will oversee courts in these areas. Specific details on the handover timeline and arrangements were not provided.

Why it mattersMarket leaders and county officials in Juba are discussing administrative responsibilities and management of trading areas.

South Sudan government and African Parks eye wildlife for economic growth

Officials and conservationists see potential for tourism, jobs, and investment beyond oil resources.

South Sudan's government and African Parks are highlighting wildlife's economic value as a sustainable alternative to oil. Pierre-Armand Roulet has started as the new Regional Operations Manager for African Parks in the country, focusing on protecting Boma and Badingilo National Parks. Wildlife Conservation and Tourism Minister Denay Jock Chagor stated that the nation's vast animal migrations are a significant asset for future prosperity if managed effectively.

Minister Chagor explained that protecting wildlife can create jobs and attract investment, offering long-term benefits through tourism. African Parks Chief Operations Officer Charles Wells echoed this, noting that wildlife and protected areas provide continuous economic advantages, unlike finite resources like oil. The organisation is partnering with the Ministry to enhance management of Boma and Badingilo National Parks, a collaboration that began in August 2022.

Presidential Advisor on Governance Joseph Ngere described wildlife protection as an investment in the nation's future, capable of boosting economic development and national pride. Turning wildlife into a revenue source will require investment in tourism infrastructure, roads, and services to draw visitors, according to the report. African Parks reaffirmed its commitment to supporting South Sudan's conservation efforts.
