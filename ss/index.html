Gunmen kill veteran journalist Veronica Lucy Gordon in Mundri road ambush

Two people were killed and several others reported missing when unidentified gunmen ambushed a vehicle on the road between Mundri East and Mundri West.

Veteran journalist Veronica Lucy Gordon was killed on Thursday night when unidentified gunmen ambushed her vehicle on the road between Mundri East and Mundri West. Two people died in the attack, and several others were reported missing.

Gordon was one of South Sudan's pioneering journalists and a champion of media freedom and women's rights. She co-founded the Association for Media Women in South Sudan (AMWISS), which works to elevate female journalists, and in 2014 became the first woman elected Deputy Chairperson of the Association for Media Development in South Sudan (AMDISS) board.

Colleagues describe her as a trailblazer and mentor. Her career included work as a news anchor, senior editor and columnist, and she later became Director of the English News Department at SSBC Radio.

Why it mattersMedia women in South Sudan have lost a senior figure who opened board and leadership positions to female journalists.

South Sudan's Cairo embassy opens registration for voluntary return

The government has approved more than 26.5 million US dollars to bring home about 20,000 vulnerable citizens from Egypt.

The South Sudanese Embassy in Cairo has opened registration for nationals who want to return home voluntarily from Egypt. Ambassador Kuol Nyok Kuol Arop announced the start of the exercise on Thursday, November 12, and urged citizens to cooperate with the embassy and relevant authorities.

The registration was organised by the South Sudanese Community in Egypt, led by Engineer Santino Akot Deng, and attended by community leaders, chiefs and citizens. It is meant to identify those willing to return before the government's repatriation programme begins, which will cover about 20,000 vulnerable citizens.

On September 4, the Council of Ministers, chaired by President Salva Kiir Mayardit, approved the funding for transport, travel documents, reception, humanitarian aid and reintegration. Stranded students, the elderly, people needing medical help, women and children, persons with disabilities and those displaced by conflict are prioritised.

Why it mattersEgyptian-based citizens who lack valid documents or face hardship can register now for transport and reintegration support.

South Sudan begins voter registration for 2027 elections

The National Elections Commission will run the exercise until November 28 at 3,161 centres across the country.

South Sudan began registering voters on Saturday, November 14, 2026, for general elections planned for February 11, 2027. The National Elections Commission (NEC) stated the registration process will continue until Saturday, November 28. This exercise is taking place at 3,161 centres spread across the nation's 10 states and three administrative areas.

Eligibility for voting is defined by the National Elections Act 2012, requiring individuals to be South Sudanese citizens aged 18 or older. Applicants must meet residency requirements or provide proof of origin, present an acceptable identification document, and not be registered elsewhere. Citizens living abroad with a South Sudanese passport can also register for presidential elections or referendums.

The official election campaign is set to start on December 27, 2026, and will last for 45 days. Polling is scheduled for February 11, 2027, with final election results expected to be declared on February 15, 2027.

Why it mattersVoter registration for the February 11, 2027 general elections began on November 14, 2026, affecting all eligible citizens.

Politics · 2026-11-16

South Sudan Supreme Court disposes of 317 cases

The court handled 317 cases in 15 months, but faces funding and staffing challenges.

South Sudan’s Supreme Court disposed of 317 cases in the 15 months since the new administration began, according to Deputy Chief Justice Laku Tranquilo Nyombe. Speaking on Sunday in Juba, Nyombe stated that the court had made progress in handling cases, with efforts underway to improve judicial efficiency nationwide.

Nyombe announced that previously unassigned cases were distributed to judges, and lower courts were directed to improve their processes, with a judicial statistics system reactivated to monitor performance. However, he identified inadequate funding, a shortage of judges, and limited infrastructure outside Juba as major challenges.

The Judiciary’s five-year plan prioritises building more High Courts and county courts. Nyombe also appealed to the Central Bank Governor regarding several months of outstanding salary payments for Judiciary employees, noting that delayed salaries could affect staff performance, especially at the newly launched Commercial Court.

Why it mattersJudiciary employees have faced several months of outstanding salary payments, affecting their working environment and performance.

Raga leaders urge youth to return to school

Officials and traditional chiefs met to address low enrolment, which the students' association says leaves the county's future at risk.

Leaders in Raga County, Western Bahr el Ghazal State, have called for collective action to improve education and encourage young people to return to school. The appeal came at a meeting organised by the Raga County Students Association in Universities and Higher Institutions at the county headquarters hall on Wednesday morning.

Abdul-Rahman Gasim Jalab, the county's Executive Director, said education was the top priority and that everyone should work together to raise its level. Lily Rajab Hussein, chairperson of the students' association, said low enrolment and participation pose a serious challenge, pointing to a gap between the area's youth population and the number of students in universities and higher institutions. She said she is the only female student in her college.

Paramount Chief Sultan Issa Ramadan Dongo thanked the young people behind the initiative, calling it one of the first programmes of its kind at county level. He said traditional leaders must encourage young people who have left school to return. The meeting ended with a call for administrators, traditional authorities, student bodies and families to cooperate on expanding educational opportunities.

Why it mattersGirls and young people who have dropped out of school are the ones whose return the county's leaders and student group are now asking families and chiefs to press for.
