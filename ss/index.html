Rumbek Central paramount chief Madol Mathok dies at 87

The influential leader, known for supporting the SPLA, died in Juba after a battle with illness.

Prominent Rumbek Central Paramount Chief Madol Mathok Agolder, 87, died in Juba on Wednesday after battling illness. Born in 1939, Mathok succeeded his brother as chief in 1976 and was known for his contributions to the SPLM/A liberation struggle.

Rumbek Central County Commissioner Manyiel Riak Manyiel confirmed the chief's death, stating it caused shock across Lakes State. Emmanuel Mading Manon, chairperson of the Nyang Community, said Mathok died from a heart attack and was a "dictionary and a Bible" for the community.

Arrangements are underway to transport the body to Rumbek for burial, with a grave being prepared near Bros Hotel. Mathok was a strong supporter of the liberation struggle, mobilizing youth and contributing food to the SPLA.

Why it mattersParamount Chief Madol Mathok Agolder, 87, died in Juba after a prolonged illness.

South Sudan parties demand probe into peacekeeper killings

CTSAMVM representatives called for justice after two Ethiopian peacekeepers died in an August ambush in Jonglei State.

Senior representatives on South Sudan’s Ceasefire and Transitional Security Arrangements Monitoring and Verification Mechanism (CTSAMVM) urged an investigation into an August ambush that killed two Ethiopian peacekeepers in Jonglei State. The attack near Pajut also wounded seven others, including three peacekeepers, two police officers, and two civilian staff. Calls for accountability were made during a CTSAMVM meeting in Juba on Thursday.

SPLM-IO representative Maj Gen. Dr. Chuol Ruey urged the government and CTSAMVM to identify and prosecute the perpetrators, stating the ceasefire remains shaky. Revitalized Transitional Government of National Unity representative Maj Gen. Wuoi Mayom Deng condemned the attack, calling for cooperation between CTSAMVM, the UN Mission, and national authorities. He warned that armed mobilization and attacks threaten the December 22, 2026 elections.

Other Political Parties (OPP) representative Maj Gen. Bior Leek also demanded clear information from the SSPDF and government about responsibility for the ambush. He questioned the peace agreement's implementation and expressed concern over movement restrictions impacting political campaigns before the elections. CTSAMVM chairperson Maj. Gen. Dr. Hailu Endashaw Atomsa noted continued armed clashes and intercommunal violence across the country.

Why it mattersTwo Ethiopian peacekeepers were killed and seven others wounded in an ambush in Jonglei State, South Sudan.

South Sudan government to boost flood response in Unity State

The government will strengthen coordination with partners to address displacement and flood risks, focusing on preparedness and recovery.

The South Sudan government is enhancing collaboration with humanitarian and development partners to tackle the needs of displaced people and communities affected by floods in Unity State. The focus will be on preparedness, recovering livelihoods, and finding lasting solutions.

Speaking at a meeting with United Nations agencies on Thursday in Bentiu, Kuyok Abol Kuyok, caretaker Minister of General Education and Instruction, stated the government's commitment to protecting those impacted by displacement and environmental shocks. He highlighted that the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management is working with institutions and partners to coordinate emergency aid, including food, shelter, and healthcare, depending on available resources.

The government acknowledges challenges such as recurrent flooding, limited finances, poor infrastructure, and logistical problems that hinder humanitarian efforts. Minister Kuyok called for increased investment in drainage systems, raised roads, and flood-protection structures, alongside support for agriculture and small businesses to help displaced families rebuild their lives and reduce reliance on aid.

Why it mattersThe government is strengthening its response to displacement and flood risks in Unity State, affecting communities and their livelihoods.

ex-commissioner arrested after airstrip shooting

Former Rubkona commissioner Stephen Salam Maluit allegedly fired shots at Bentiu airstrip when denied entry.

Former Rubkona County commissioner Stephen Salam Maluit was arrested on Thursday morning after allegedly firing shots at Bentiu airstrip. The incident occurred as gubernatorial candidate Michael Chanjiek Geay arrived at the location. Maluit arrived with over 20 armed individuals but was stopped by security forces.

Unity State Information Minister Nyakenya Johannes Keah stated that Maluit opened fire when security personnel advised him to leave. The confrontation led to an exchange of gunfire between Maluit's armed group and security forces. No injuries or property damage were reported following the incident.

Minister Keah urged political aspirants to reject violence ahead of the country's elections, emphasizing the need for peaceful participation and stability in Unity State. She called on community members to promote peace and coexistence amid current challenges.

Why it mattersThe arrest of a former commissioner and gunfire at an airstrip disrupt political campaigning and threaten stability in Unity State.

UK gives £2.35 million to South Sudan for El Niño risks

The funding aims to protect food production and vulnerable communities from climate shocks.

The United Kingdom has announced an additional £2.35 million to help mitigate El Niño-related risks in South Sudan. This funding will assist communities facing threats to their food production and livelihoods from climate shocks. UK Ambassador Rebecca Fabrizi announced the new support during the start of her tenure in the country.

Ambassador Fabrizi stated that the funding is intended to address the impacts of climate shocks. The support targets vulnerable communities whose livelihoods are at risk. The announcement was made in a statement released in Juba.

The additional funding from the UK comes as El Niño weather patterns can disrupt agricultural cycles. This support aims to build resilience against such climate-related challenges.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese farmers and vulnerable communities will receive support to mitigate the risks of El Niño.
