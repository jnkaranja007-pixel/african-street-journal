Juba authorities review market security operations

Caretaker Mayor Christopher Serafino Wani Swaka chaired a meeting to address unauthorised brokering and improve public order.

Juba authorities are reviewing security operations in neighbourhoods and markets to tackle unauthorised brokering by young people and improve public order. The review took place during a high-level strategic meeting on Friday, November 6, led by Caretaker Mayor Christopher Serafino Wani Swaka. Senior police generals, security commanders, and heads of security agencies attended the meeting.

Discussions focused on assessing neighbourhood security performance, strengthening community safety, and enhancing cooperation between law enforcement and residents. The authorities also examined security challenges in markets, specifically unauthorised brokering activities involving young people. Mayor Swaka urged security personnel to act professionally and build public trust through responsible conduct.

Swaka stated that lasting security and public order depend on mutual trust between law enforcement and the communities they serve. He reaffirmed the city council’s commitment to working with security agencies to address operational gaps and strengthen coordination. The goal is to make Juba safer, cleaner, and more peaceful.

Why it mattersJuba residents are affected by security operations in their neighbourhoods and markets, with a focus on public order and unauthorised brokering.

South Sudan dialogue resumes to finalise resolutions

Political parties will meet in Juba on Monday, November 9, 2026, to discuss proposed resolutions from a two-week dialogue.

South Sudan's inter-party dialogue is set to resume in Juba on Monday, November 9, 2026, to finalise proposed resolutions. Senior SPLM officials met to prepare for the plenary session, which will discuss a joint communiqué. The date for a closing ceremony will be set after President Salva Kiir approves the adopted resolutions.

The dialogue, launched on September 24, aimed to discuss preparations for South Sudan’s first general elections since 2011. However, some parties criticised the process, citing government control and the detention of former First Vice-President Riek Machar. President Kiir had warned participants against renegotiating the electoral timetable during the discussions.

Political analyst Abraham Awolich stated that the dialogue failed last month because the government insisted on scheduled elections while others called for postponement and new political arrangements. Awolich described the dialogue as a "banquet among people who had already agreed to violate the constitution," noting that attempts to prepare resolutions without all parties' agreement were abandoned.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese political parties and stakeholders are affected by the outcome of the inter-party dialogue regarding election preparations and political arrangements.

South Sudan named vice-chair for Africa at solar alliance

The nation will join regional discussions on solar energy development and cooperation among African member states.

South Sudan has been appointed Vice-Chair of the International Solar Alliance’s Regional Committee for Africa. The appointments were announced during the Alliance’s Seventh Assembly held in New Delhi, India, in November 2026. This role gives South Sudan a platform in regional discussions on solar energy development and cooperation among African member states.

The country was represented at the assembly by a delegation led by the Undersecretary of the Ministry of Energy and Dams, Madhieu Achuil Mawien. During the assembly, South Sudan highlighted its electricity challenges, reporting approximately 20 megawatts of solar generation capacity and electricity access of around five percent. The nation called for technical and institutional support from the Alliance to expand solar energy projects.

The Ninth Assembly also discussed the proposed US$200 million Africa Solar Facility to mobilise financing and accelerate solar energy investments across the continent. For South Sudan, such initiatives could provide opportunities to attract investment in solar power generation and rural electrification.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan's appointment could provide opportunities to attract investment in solar power generation and rural electrification.

South Sudan NEC identifies 3,161 voter registration centres

The National Elections Commission will deploy 22,127 personnel for the exercise ahead of the December 2026 polls.

South Sudan's National Elections Commission (NEC) has identified 3,161 voter registration centres for the December 2026 general elections. The commission plans to deploy 22,127 personnel to support the nationwide exercise, with each centre staffed by seven individuals. NEC Chairperson Gabriel Bol Deng announced this in Juba on Sunday, November 8, during a training for returning officers. The commission has also prepared 82,962 registration books for the process.

Bol urged the 26 returning officers to enhance coordination and accountability while mobilising eligible voters and ensuring accessible registration centres. The NEC pledged to provide support, including internet services, addressing concerns raised about transport, equipment, accommodation, medical services, and security. The training was conducted under Constitutional Amendment No. 13 and the National Elections Act 2012, as amended in 2026.

The returning officers are expected to return to their areas on Monday to continue preparations for voter registration. South Sudan is scheduled to hold its first general elections since independence on December 22, 2026. The NEC has begun distributing election materials, with a first batch sent to Central Equatoria State on November 2. The commission also warned the public about a fake online registration link, stating that registration must be done physically at designated centres.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens will need to register physically at designated centres for the upcoming December 2026 general elections.

Western Equatoria elects new athletics leaders

Four officials will promote athletics and build sports structures across the state’s 10 counties.

Western Equatoria State has elected a new leadership for its athletics federation, dissolving the Yambio Local Athletics Association. The election on Thursday brought in four new officials to promote athletics development and create opportunities for young talents across the state’s 10 counties.

The new officials are President Jackson Gaaniko, Deputy President Mbabazi Desire, Secretary-General Angel Tata, and Finance Secretary Malesh Ramadan. Col. Beatrice Safari of the National Athletics Federation announced the dissolution and urged the new leadership to represent all 10 counties and prepare athletes for the Third Republican Athletics Championships in Juba.

President Gaaniko pledged to identify and nurture local talent, organize competitions, and strengthen state-level structures. He also aims to establish an inclusive leadership structure involving coaches and advisers to promote transparency and accountability, calling for support from government institutions and development partners.

Why it mattersAthletes and young people in Western Equatoria will benefit from new leadership focused on talent development and inclusive participation in sports.
