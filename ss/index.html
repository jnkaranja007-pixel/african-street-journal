South Sudan protests exclusion from UNISFA mandate drafting

Juba says its concerns were overlooked when the Security Council renewed the peacekeeping mandate for the disputed Abyei area.

South Sudan has protested that it was left out of consultations on a United Nations Security Council resolution renewing the mandate of the UN Interim Security Force for Abyei (UNISFA), the peacekeeping force in Abyei. The council adopted Resolution 2838 (2026) on November 13.

The South Sudanese government said its concerns were overlooked and not adequately reflected in the decision. The report does not say what changes Juba is seeking or how it intends to respond.

The renewal extends the mandate of the force operating in Abyei, the area named in the resolution. The report does not say what the protest will lead to.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan says its concerns were not adequately reflected in the mandate decision that governs the UN force in Abyei, a disputed border area, and no changes to the resolution have been requested.

South Sudan Supreme Court directs Ajing to seek release from prosecutor

The court said it cannot order temporary release while the former presidential office director’s criminal case remains under investigation.

South Sudan’s Supreme Court on Tuesday, November 17, declined to grant temporary release to Ajing Deng Ajing, a former Executive Director in the Office of the President, while his criminal case remains under investigation. The Constitutional Panel directed him to apply to the Office of the Public Prosecutor.

The judges said Ajing faces charges in Criminal Case No. 710 of 2026. His lawyers alleged that his detention violated his rights and that he lacked access to legal counsel, family members and medical care; the respondents argued the detention was lawful and release requests must follow criminal procedures.

The ruling addresses only temporary release, not the legality of his arrest or detention. Ajing’s constitutional petition against the National Security Service and the Ministry of Justice and Constitutional Affairs remains pending, with proceedings scheduled to resume on December 15, 2026.

Why it mattersAjing’s release request must now go to the Office of the Public Prosecutor, while his constitutional case is scheduled to resume on December 15, 2026.

Juba council sets 10 million SSP fine for selling alcohol to minors

The order also fines bars and shops for mixing alcohol with food and took effect on Monday after the caretaker mayor signed it.

Juba City Council has imposed fines of up to 10 million South Sudanese pounds on bars and shops that sell alcohol to minors or mix it with food. Local Order No. 04/2026 was signed on Monday by Caretaker Mayor Christopher Serafino Wani Swaka and took effect that day.

The order makes knowingly supplying alcoholic drinks to minors, or mixing alcohol with foodstuffs, an offence. Offenders may also face penalties under council by-laws and other South Sudanese laws, according to the order.

Juba, Kator and Munuki blocks, the Department of Environment and Sanitation and other council zones are to oversee enforcement with local chiefs, quarter councils, government institutions, security agencies and law enforcement authorities. Swaka issued the order under Gubernatorial Decree No. 02/2026 and Section 57 of the Local Government Act 2009.

Why it mattersBars and shops found committing either offence face a 10 million South Sudanese pound fine, with further penalties under council by-laws and other laws.

Jonglei plans to move 135 Akobo exam candidates to Bor

The state says it will cover food and accommodation, but opposition officials and parents are still deciding whether to accept the plan.

Jonglei State is consulting opposition officials and parents about moving 135 candidates from Akobo County to Bor after insecurity stopped them sitting national primary and secondary examinations in Akobo. The candidates are registered for both levels.

Moses Majok Gatluak, Jonglei’s caretaker governor, told Radio Tamazuj on Tuesday evening that the state would pay for their transport, food and accommodation. He said the National Examinations Council had cancelled exams in the opposition-controlled area over security concerns, and that the national examinations ministry would make separate arrangements for candidates who had missed the start.

John Wiyual Lul, the opposition-appointed Akobo commissioner, said officials would meet candidates and parents on Wednesday to discuss relocation and decide whether to go to Bor. Primary examinations began on November 16; secondary exams are scheduled to start on November 23.

Why it mattersThe candidates and their families must decide on relocation, while the state has offered to cover food and accommodation and opposition officials are due to meet them on Wednesday.

Education · 2026-11-18

Emmanuel Christian University graduates 131 in Yei

The graduates included 60 from Business Administration, 47 from Education and 24 from Theology at the university’s 18th ceremony.

Emmanuel Christian University in Yei Town, Central Equatoria State, graduated 131 students last Friday, awarding qualifications across three faculties at its 18th graduation ceremony.

Vice Chancellor Dr. Daniel Ohide said the university had grown from three faculties to five and offered about 13 programmes. It has added Agriculture and Social Work, including Public Administration, and plans to establish an Information Technology faculty in 2027 focused on Artificial Intelligence and digital skills.

Yei River County caretaker commissioner Emmanuel Taban Seme said the county government was working to secure more scholarships and improve access to higher education. Central Equatoria education minister Lupai Satimon cited inadequate funding and teacher shortages at primary, secondary and technical schools across the state.

Why it mattersStudents seeking higher education in Yei have new Agriculture and Social Work faculties, while the county government is working to secure more scholarships.
