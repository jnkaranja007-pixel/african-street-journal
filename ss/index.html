Abyei Residents Protest Drone Strike on Amiet Market

Abyei residents, including community leaders and civil society, protested a drone strike attributed to Sudanese forces that killed dozens at a market.

Residents of the Abyei Administrative Area protested on Tuesday against a drone strike on Amiet Joint Market, which reportedly killed dozens of people on Monday. The market is situated in the disputed territory between Sudan and South Sudan. The South Sudanese government has condemned the attack and summoned the Sudanese ambassador. The protest, which included community leaders, civil society, youth, and women, called for enhanced civilian protection.

Demonstrators submitted a petition to the United Nations Interim Security Force for Abyei (UNISFA), urging an investigation into the attack and its referral to the UN Security Council. Rose Monytoc Dau, leader of Abyei Civil Society, stated the attack, attributed to drones operated by the Sudanese Armed Forces, violated international humanitarian law and UN Security Council resolutions. The petition cited resolutions 1990 (2011) and 2445 (2018).

UNISFA Acting Head of Mission and Force Commander, Lieutenant General Ganesh Kumar Shrestha, acknowledged the community's concerns and pledged to forward the petition to relevant authorities. He welcomed the peaceful expression of opinions and assured the protesters that their message had been heard and would be transmitted.

Why it mattersAbyei residents are affected by a deadly drone strike on a market, prompting protests and calls for protection and investigation.

Juba County Bans Alcohol, Restricts Market Vending

Central Equatoria State authorities have banned a popular alcoholic drink and prohibited wheelbarrow sales to improve public safety and tax collection.

Juba County in Central Equatoria State has banned the sale of the red-capped alcoholic drink known as “Jena Fa’ar” and prohibited wheelbarrow vending in markets and along roadsides. These measures, issued by the Central Equatoria State Security Committee, aim to enhance public safety, strengthen tax compliance, and reorganize commercial activities in Juba. The directives were announced during a weekly meeting chaired by Caretaker Governor Augustino Jadalla Kamilo Wani.

The ban on the alcoholic drink is intended to protect citizens, particularly young people, from harmful substances. The prohibition of wheelbarrow vending targets shop owners who allegedly abandoned business premises to sell goods through mobile vending, thereby evading taxes. The committee also mandated the inspection of electrical wiring in markets to mitigate fire risks and directed the replacement of temporary market structures with permanent concrete buildings.

The Central Equatoria State Security Committee reported that the overall security situation remains calm, with isolated incidents being addressed. The implemented measures are designed to protect public health, prevent market fires, and promote orderly commercial activities. The state government has reiterated its commitment to maintaining peace and stability ahead of scheduled elections.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected by new regulations on alcohol sales and market vending that aim to improve tax compliance and commercial order.

Caretaker Government Assigns Acting Ministers to Vacant Posts

South Sudan's caretaker government has appointed acting ministers to oversee ministries left vacant following the dissolution of the transitional administration.

South Sudan's caretaker government has assigned acting ministers to national ministries that currently lack caretaker ministers, aiming to ensure continued supervision. Caretaker Minister of Cabinet Affairs Martin Elia Lomuro issued Ministerial Order No. AM-01/2026 on Tuesday, October 20, assigning responsibilities across governance, economic, service, and gender and youth clusters. The order directs under-secretaries to manage daily administration under the supervision of these acting ministers, emphasizing that under-secretaries are civil servants and cannot exercise ministerial powers.

The appointments follow President Salva Kiir's dissolution of the transitional government on September 22 and the establishment of a caretaker administration. The order details numerous cross-assignments, for example, the minister of Cabinet Affairs will act for Presidential Affairs, while the minister of Petroleum will act for Finance and Planning. Several second-alternate positions remain to be determined, and the order takes effect immediately until amended or revoked.

Under-secretaries are instructed to recognize the authority of their assigned acting ministers and implement the order, with non-compliance potentially leading to disciplinary consequences. The order aims to maintain administrative continuity amid preparations for elections scheduled for December 22, though concerns exist about the progress of these preparations.

Why it mattersThe appointments ensure administrative continuity in government ministries, impacting the functioning of state institutions.

South Sudan Officials Face New Travel Curbs

Presidential order requires senior officials to seek approval for foreign and domestic travel, raising rights concerns.

President Salva Kiir has issued an order requiring serving and former senior government officials to obtain approval for foreign travel and notify authorities before domestic journeys. The directive, Republican Order No. 36/10/2026, dated October 15, mandates that serving officials need ministerial and presidential endorsement for international trips, while former high-ranking officials require presidential or ministerial permission. For travel within South Sudan, officials must provide written notification detailing their destination, purpose, duration, and mode of transport at least 48 hours in advance.

The order, which revokes earlier general travel restrictions, cites Article 101(t) of the Transitional Constitution as its legal basis. However, legal experts and policy analysts have raised questions about the order's constitutional validity and its potential impact on political competition ahead of upcoming elections. Concerns include the lack of defined grounds for approval or refusal, no specified response deadlines for applications, and no clear mechanism for challenging decisions, potentially preventing travel without a formal rejection.

Dr. Remember Miamingi, an international human rights lawyer, noted that the order's wording creates ambiguity between simple notification and requiring permission, which could grant the government power to halt journeys. The requirements apply to both official duties and private travel, and the order does not define 'other senior government officials' or outline exceptions for emergencies.

Why it mattersSenior government officials and former officials are directly affected by new travel approval requirements.

Germany Funds Nutrition Aid for Vulnerable South Sudanese

Emergency services will target women and children in Unity State facing acute malnutrition and food insecurity.

Germany has provided funding for emergency nutrition services in Guit County, Unity State, to assist vulnerable communities grappling with a severe and prolonged nutrition crisis. The support will enable the Coalition for Humanity to deliver aid to underserved populations in Kuach, Kuerguiny, Wathnyotna, and Chotyiel, where access to essential services is limited. German Ambassador Sebastian Brökelmann stated the funding aims to help those affected by acute malnutrition, with a focus on the most vulnerable groups.

The intervention will prioritize young children, pregnant women, and breastfeeding mothers, who are at heightened risk. Displaced individuals, returnees, and households impacted by conflict and flooding that have disrupted access to food, healthcare, and nutrition services will also benefit. The program will concentrate on hard-to-reach areas that become isolated due to flooding or insecurity, often lacking permanent nutrition facilities.

Guit County is experiencing an emergency-level nutrition crisis, classified under Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Phase 4 for both acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition. This situation is exacerbated by ongoing food insecurity, recurrent flooding, displacement, localized insecurity, disease risks, and disruptions to humanitarian aid. Germany, South Sudan's second-largest bilateral donor, continues its commitment to supporting the nation's population.

Why it mattersVulnerable women and children in Unity State will receive emergency nutrition services to combat acute malnutrition.
