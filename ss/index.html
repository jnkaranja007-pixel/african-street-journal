Juba City Council digitizes revenue collection

The council has arrested 32 collectors and launched a new website to improve transparency.

Juba City Council has launched a new digital financial payment system and public website to address concerns over transparency in revenue collection. Caretaker Mayor Christopher Sarafino Wani Swaka announced on Friday that the council completed the transition from manual revenue collection to the digital system in the last 10 days, migrating all revenue operations from manual forms like Form 15.

Mayor Swaka stated that investigations uncovered financial malpractices, leading to the arrest of 32 rate collectors who are facing disciplinary action. He reported that 50 percent of the council’s resources were lost to malpractice, and the new system aims to improve accountability and public confidence. The council also launched a website, www.jccgov.ss, for public access to information on budgets and tax schedules.

The reforms followed complaints from residents and business owners about the transparency of JCC’s revenue collection operations. A digitalization law was passed by the legislative council after four months of deliberations. The website aims to address public uncertainty about fees and improve communication between the city and its residents.

Why it mattersResidents and business owners in Juba will now have clearer access to information about taxes and fees, and improved transparency in how their money is collected and managed.

One killed in clashes near South Sudan-Uganda border

A 45-year-old South Sudanese refugee travelling to her farm was killed by a stray bullet.

Clashes between suspected opposition armed groups in Morobo County, Central Equatoria State, killed one South Sudanese woman and injured three others. The fighting began around nine o'clock on Thursday, near the border with Uganda. Some injured individuals crossed into Uganda for medical treatment.

The deceased, Irena Roba Jeina, 45, was a South Sudanese refugee in Uganda travelling to her farm in South Sudan when she was killed by a stray bullet. Gabriel Rang, 30, sustained a serious leg injury, and Tik Rial, 40, was shot in the hand. Jehina Audi is receiving care at Busia Health Centre in Uganda, while others are at Koboko Hospital. Two arrests have been made.

Ugandan authorities described the border security situation as fragile, with concerns over armed group movements. Security forces have increased patrols and are coordinating with South Sudanese counterparts. Residents are advised to avoid crossing into South Sudan for farming or grazing until the security improves.

Why it mattersA South Sudanese woman was killed and three others injured in fighting near the Ugandan border, affecting civilian safety and cross-border movement.

UAE investor takes South Sudan to arbitration over oil deal

Aquilon FZCO seeks completion of a March 2025 agreement or compensation for over $300 million in potential losses.

A United Arab Emirates investor, Aquilon FZCO, has initiated international arbitration against South Sudan and its state-owned Nile Petroleum Corporation (Nilepet). The dispute centers on a March 2025 agreement for the transfer of oil production assets previously held by Malaysia's Petronas, which Aquilon claims has not been finalized.

Aquilon states it has already paid 50 million US dollars for the deal and faces potential losses exceeding 300 million dollars if the agreement is not honored. The International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes registered the case on October 16, 2026, while Mauritius' Supreme Court issued a temporary injunction on October 29, 2026, preventing Nilepet from selling the disputed assets until February 5, 2027.

This arbitration adds to South Sudan's legal and financial challenges in its oil sector, following a separate case initiated by Petronas after a reported 1.25-billion-dollar deal collapse in 2024. Separately, South Sudan reached a preliminary agreement with Russia on October 29 for the transfer of former Petronas shares in DPOC and GPOC to Zarubezhneft.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan's government and Nilepet face international arbitration and asset sale restrictions impacting foreign investment in its oil sector.

Five killed in Pochalla cattle raid

Armed assailants attacked Otallo, killing five civilians and wounding four others, according to local officials.

Five civilians died and four were injured in a cattle raid in Otallo, Pochalla County, on Friday and Saturday. Armed assailants attacked the area, which hosts the palace of King Akway Agada Akway Cham of the Anyuak Nyiodolla Royal Dynasty. Lt Col Amat Ochan, the county police inspector, accused armed men from the neighbouring Murle community of carrying out the attack.

The attackers stole cattle, but local youth recovered all the stolen animals and a mobile phone believed to belong to an assailant. Ochan stated that names saved in the phone suggest the attackers are Murle. Three of the five killed were members of the same family. Two of the four wounded received initial treatment at Pochalla Primary Health Care Centre.

Two of the injured are in critical condition and were evacuated to neighbouring Ethiopia for further treatment after efforts to move them to Juba were unsuccessful. Othow Okoti, a former local official, condemned the attack. The recovered mobile phone will be investigated to establish further details about the assailants.

Why it mattersThe attack resulted in five civilian deaths and four injuries, with two critically wounded individuals requiring cross-border medical evacuation.

South Sudanese girl married at 14 for cows

Nyanbuot, now 17, is a mother of one and pregnant again after her family arranged her marriage in January 2024.

Nyanbuot was married at 14 in January 2024 in Rumbek, Lakes State, South Sudan, in exchange for cows and other wealth. She was studying in Kenya at the time and was unaware of the arrangement until her family called her home for Christmas. She is now 17, a mother, and six months pregnant with her second child.

The girl, who had dreamed of becoming a doctor, said her family arranged the marriage to a man she had never met. Nyanbuot reported experiencing difficult pregnancies and physical abuse from her husband, stating her family refused to support her when she sought help. Her brothers, who received the cows, would not accept her back from the marriage.

Nyanbuot’s education has been interrupted, and her aspirations are on hold as she faces the responsibilities of motherhood and marriage. She expressed suffering and concern for her young child, noting her husband struggles to provide for the family and only possesses the cows given to her parents.

Why it mattersNyanbuot, a 17-year-old girl, is suffering from an interrupted education and an abusive marriage arranged by her family in exchange for cows.
