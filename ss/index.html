Professor Duany: South Sudan Fails to Build State Institutions

Weak public administration hinders governance and service delivery, according to the Civil Service Commission chairperson.

South Sudan has not yet built functioning state institutions, declared Prof. Julia Aker Duany, chairperson of the Civil Service Commission. Speaking in Juba, she questioned the nation's progress since independence, stating that institutions remain too weak to address political, economic, and social challenges. Duany argued that the country carried the "tools and mindset of liberation" into state-building, which requires different approaches like stability, effective laws, and responsible resource management.

The inadequacy of public administration is evident, with some institutions lacking basic facilities, including the Civil Service Commission itself, which reportedly has no office. Prof. Duany highlighted the government's struggle to provide essential services such as education, health, and clean water, attributing the failure not to a lack of laws but to institutional inability to implement them. She called for a return to basics and a focus on building institutions capable of enforcing existing legislation.

Despite the criticisms, Duany noted South Sudan's considerable human resources, including many educated citizens, questioning why their potential is underutilized. She suggested learning from other countries while adapting experiences to local contexts and emphasized the need for active citizen participation in strengthening institutions, urging cooperation between government and civil society.

Why it mattersSouth Sudanese citizens are affected by the weak state institutions through the government's inability to deliver essential services like education, health, and clean water.

Central Equatoria Cabinet Approves SSP 108.2 Billion Draft Budget

The proposed budget aims to tackle economic challenges and improve public services, while a separate donation enhances rural development efforts.

The Central Equatoria State Council of Ministers has approved a draft budget of 108.2 billion South Sudanese Pounds for the 2026-2027 financial year. Presented by the Minister of Finance, Planning and Investment, the budget is intended to address economic challenges, enhance transparency, and strengthen public service delivery. The Council approved the budget with the understanding that amendments may be made.

In a related development, the Ministry of Cooperatives and Rural Development received a Toyota Hilux four-wheel-drive vehicle from the Government of Turkey. This donation aims to bolster the ministry's operational capacity and support its field activities, particularly in engaging with rural communities and assisting cocoa and coffee farmers.

Governor Emmanuel Adil Anthony urged officials and citizens to maintain peace during the election period and protect government property. A security report indicated a calm and stable situation in the state over the past three months, with isolated incidents being contained through coordinated efforts by security organs.

Why it mattersThe approved budget and the vehicle donation are intended to improve economic conditions, public services, and support for rural farmers in Central Equatoria.

South Sudan President Signs Election Law Amendments

President Salva Kiir has assented to constitutional amendments, removing legal barriers for the December 2026 general elections.

South Sudan has advanced its electoral process with President Salva Kiir signing constitutional amendments on Wednesday. This action removes significant legal obstacles to holding the planned general elections in December 2026.

The amendments, designated as Transitional Constitution of the Republic of South Sudan, 2011 (Amendment No. 13), were formally approved by the president. This move is seen as a crucial step towards fulfilling the electoral timeline previously established.

The assent signifies a commitment to proceeding with the democratic process, clearing the path for the electoral commission to prepare for the upcoming polls. The amendments address key legal requirements necessary for the conduct of national elections.

Why it mattersThe constitutional amendments directly affect the political landscape by enabling the upcoming December 2026 general elections to proceed.

Kiir Orders Action on State Broadcaster Challenges

President Salva Kiir has directed urgent solutions for the South Sudan Broadcasting Corporation amid a leadership dispute and ahead of general elections.

President Salva Kiir has ordered an immediate resolution to the operational challenges facing the South Sudan Broadcasting Corporation (SSBC). The directive was issued during a meeting with the Minister of Information, Ateny Wek Ateny, at the State House. The President stressed the broadcaster's critical role in informing the public, especially with the upcoming December general elections.

The meeting followed reports of the SSBC managing director, John Madol Panther, being suspended by the board of directors. Panther, however, dismissed these reports, asserting his continued authority and questioning the board's power to remove him. The Office of the President stated that Ateny presented a progress report on the broadcaster's operational issues.

Kiir emphasized the strategic importance of SSBC and called for coordinated efforts to enhance its capacity, professionalism, and effectiveness. He instructed relevant institutions to collaborate on resolving the identified challenges, ensuring the broadcaster is well-equipped to fulfill its mandate of informing, educating, and entertaining the citizens of South Sudan.

Why it mattersThe President's directive aims to ensure the South Sudan Broadcasting Corporation is functional and effective in informing the public, particularly concerning the upcoming general elections.

Refugees Return to South Sudan, Embrace Farming

More than 6,000 South Sudanese refugees have returned from Uganda and the DRC and are now cultivating land to rebuild their lives.

Over 6,000 South Sudanese refugees have registered their return to Yei River County since January, according to the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission. The returnees, who came from refugee camps in Uganda and the Democratic Republic of Congo, are now integrating into communities and engaging in farming and other activities. Women and children constitute 70 percent of the arrivals, with men making up the remaining 30 percent.

The returnees cited difficult conditions in refugee camps, including restrictions on accessing land, water, and firewood, as well as hostility from some host communities. Many expressed relief at being back in South Sudan, where they feel safer and have the freedom to cultivate land to feed their families. Despite finding some of their homes and structures destroyed upon arrival, they are focused on rebuilding their livelihoods.

The Relief and Rehabilitation Commission is appealing for increased support from humanitarian partners and well-wishers. Assistance is needed for hospitals, schools, and farms to create a conducive environment for the returnees and to encourage others still in refugee camps to come home. Some returnees are already renting land for cultivation and are determined not to return to the camps.

Why it mattersOver 6,000 refugees have returned and are now engaged in farming, contributing to local agriculture and food security.
