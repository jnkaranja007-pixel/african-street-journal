South Sudan government and African Parks eye wildlife for economic growth

Officials and conservationists see potential for tourism, jobs, and investment beyond oil resources.

Source: Eye Radio · 2026-11-10 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

South Sudan's government and African Parks are highlighting wildlife's economic value as a sustainable alternative to oil. Pierre-Armand Roulet has started as the new Regional Operations Manager for African Parks in the country, focusing on protecting Boma and Badingilo National Parks. Wildlife Conservation and Tourism Minister Denay Jock Chagor stated that the nation's vast animal migrations are a significant asset for future prosperity if managed effectively.

Minister Chagor explained that protecting wildlife can create jobs and attract investment, offering long-term benefits through tourism. African Parks Chief Operations Officer Charles Wells echoed this, noting that wildlife and protected areas provide continuous economic advantages, unlike finite resources like oil. The organisation is partnering with the Ministry to enhance management of Boma and Badingilo National Parks, a collaboration that began in August 2022.

Presidential Advisor on Governance Joseph Ngere described wildlife protection as an investment in the nation's future, capable of boosting economic development and national pride. Turning wildlife into a revenue source will require investment in tourism infrastructure, roads, and services to draw visitors, according to the report. African Parks reaffirmed its commitment to supporting South Sudan's conservation efforts.
