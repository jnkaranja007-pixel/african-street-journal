Yei records 1,918 primary candidates despite insecurity

Yei River County's Primary Eight candidates rose from 1,825 last year to 1,918 this year, even as insecurity forces some learners to sit exams elsewhere.

Sources: Radio Tamazuj, Sudans Post and Eye Radio · 2026-11-17 · 1 min read

Monday saw 1,918 Primary Eight candidates in Yei River County, Central Equatoria State, begin their Certificate of Primary Education examinations, up from 1,825 last year. Philip Taban Issa, the county education director, said 892 girls and 1,025 boys are sitting the tests across 59 schools, including four Alternative Learning Programme Centres.

Insecurity has moved some candidates: those from Lasu, Tore and Otogo payams are sitting in Yei Town, while Mugwo Payam candidates are at Payawa Centre. Mary Ding Foster, the chief examination field monitor, warned candidates and invigilators against malpractice, and Emmanuel Taban Seme, the caretaker commissioner, urged learners to stay committed to their studies.

Elsewhere, 393 Primary Eight students in Panyijiar County, Unity State, have missed their national exams after the government cancelled tests in opposition-controlled areas. In Jonglei State, the government will transport 534 Akobo candidates to Bor, where it says they will be housed until the exams end.

Why it mattersCandidates in Panyijiar and Akobo have lost or are being moved from their usual exam centres because of security concerns, and Akobo pupils will be housed in Bor until the exams end.
