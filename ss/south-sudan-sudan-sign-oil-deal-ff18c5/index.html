South Sudan, Sudan sign oil deal

The two nations agreed to reconcile crude oil volumes and boost cooperation in the petroleum sector by June 2027.

Source: Eye Radio · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

South Sudan and Sudan signed a Memorandum of Understanding in Khartoum on Sunday to reconcile crude oil volumes and strengthen cooperation in the petroleum sector. The agreement covers pipeline inspections, oil volume audits, and environmental clean-up operations, with activities expected to conclude by June 2027.

Engineer Lual Chol Dak, South Sudan’s Petroleum Authority Director-General, led the delegation that met Sudan’s Minister of Energy and Petroleum, Engineer Al-Mutasim Ibrahim. The talks focused on shared pipeline infrastructure and the crude oil transit agreement, ensuring the flow of South Sudanese crude oil to international markets.

The deal also includes technical support, emergency response, cross-border equipment movement, and improvements to financial systems for oil transit fees. Sudan reaffirmed its commitment to supplying electricity to Renk town in South Sudan, as both countries aim to maintain oil production and transport despite operational challenges.

Why it mattersSouth Sudan will maintain oil flow to international markets through Sudan, with cooperation on pipeline inspections and financial systems for transit fees until June 2027.
