Armed opposition detains eight MTN workers in South Sudan

Workers installing fibre-optic network ambushed and detained in Western Equatoria State

Sources: Radio Tamazuj and Eye Radio · 2026-11-04 · 1 min read

Eight MTN fibre-optic workers were detained by armed opposition forces in Bandala, Western Equatoria State, on October 29, 2026. The workers were travelling in four vehicles expanding the MTN network when they were ambushed between Tambura and Nagero counties.

Western Equatoria State Minister of Information and Communications Noah Philip Isaah stated that one vehicle was later released and its driver sent to Wau to collect an unspecified amount of money demanded for the workers' release. Tambura County Commissioner James Mupaya reported that the armed opposition forces allegedly demanded 35 million South Sudanese pounds (SSP) in compensation for a child's death, claiming the child drank diesel from one of the vehicles.

Isaah condemned the detention, questioning the action despite a reported announcement of resumed free movement for civilians. He urged the immediate and unconditional release of the workers, who are providing essential network services. The minister also advised travellers to seek security clearance before using the Nagero-Tambura-Wau road due to ongoing security concerns.
