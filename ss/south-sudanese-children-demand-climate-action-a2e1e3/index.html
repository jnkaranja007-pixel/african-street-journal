South Sudanese children demand climate action

Young participants adopted a declaration in Juba calling for climate-resilient schools and early warning systems.

Source: Radio Tamazuj · 2026-10-31 · 1 min read

Children in South Sudan have urged the government and world leaders to invest in measures protecting their education, health, and livelihoods from climate change impacts like floods and extreme heat. During a consultation in Juba on Thursday, young participants adopted a declaration demanding climate-resilient schools, accessible early warning systems, and emergency shelters ahead of the COP31 climate conference.

The children described climate change as a child-rights crisis, warning that disasters threaten access to education, food, water, and healthcare, especially for displaced communities. Climate advocate Siama Santino, 17, shared how heat waves in 2024 and 2025 disrupted learning and flooding in Bor made school travel difficult, stating, “Every morning when you wake up, you have to think of how much you’re going to move in water or first remove your shoes to reach school.”

The declaration stated that COP31 must deliver climate justice and action to every child globally, not just promises. Children also reported that climate shocks damage homes and crops, worsen food insecurity, and expose families to health risks, with some eating only once a day due to crop failures or flooding.

Why it mattersChildren in South Sudan face disruptions to their education, health, and livelihoods due to climate-related disasters like floods and heatwaves.
