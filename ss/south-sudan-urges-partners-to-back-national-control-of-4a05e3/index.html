South Sudan urges partners to back national control of peace process

Foreign Affairs Minister Cecilia Adut Manyok said the African Union, the Intergovernmental Authority on Development and the UN task force should help end the transition, not.

Source: Radio Tamazuj · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

South Sudan's Foreign Affairs Minister Cecilia Adut Manyok urged international partners to respect national ownership of the peace process as a joint task force began work in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, on Friday. Speaking for President Salva Kiir Mayardit, she said the government and South Sudanese people hold primary responsibility for peace, security, reconciliation and democratic governance.

The task force brings together the African Union (AU), the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD), a regional bloc of East African states, and the United Nations. Adut said its role should be to help conclude the transitional period, which she said the country is approaching, rather than make the process indefinite. She also called for adequate financial and technical support from partners.

Adut cited resolutions from an inter-party dialogue concluded by Kiir in Juba on Thursday. They address political freedoms, campaign security, party financing and a 35% affirmative-action provision for women. The dialogue included Juba-based parties but excluded opposition groups operating abroad.

Why it mattersThe government and political parties must coordinate with regional and international partners to complete the transition, while the task force is being asked to provide financial and technical support.
