Lesotho seeks compensation from South Africa for lost territories

Prime Minister Matekane raised historical land grievances and compensation with President Ramaphosa, exploring access to the sea and employment opportunities.

Source: Sunday Express Lesotho · 2026-10-22 · 1 min read

The Matekane administration has initiated discussions with South Africa regarding compensation for territories lost during the nineteenth century. Prime Minister Sam Matekane reportedly presented the issue to South African President Cyril Ramaphosa during a bilateral meeting on August 16, 2026. This move marks a determined effort to address a long-standing historical grievance that has impacted Lesotho's economic prospects and development resources.

While reclaiming the land itself faces significant diplomatic and legal challenges, including the African Union's stance on inherited borders, the administration is exploring alternative forms of redress. Proposals include seeking access to the sea, which would enhance Lesotho's trade capabilities as a landlocked nation heavily reliant on South Africa for port access and international markets. Discussions also encompass improving employment opportunities for Basotho citizens in South Africa through better work permit arrangements and structured labor agreements.

These practical discussions aim to provide tangible economic benefits and address immediate concerns like unemployment among Basotho. A stronger economic relationship between the two nations, with a more prosperous Lesotho, could also foster increased trade and investment opportunities for South African businesses, suggesting mutual benefits from resolving these historical and economic issues.

Why it mattersBasotho citizens are affected by the potential for improved access to the sea for trade and increased employment opportunities in South Africa.
