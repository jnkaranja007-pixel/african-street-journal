Western Cape water allocations cut as dam storage drops

Water and Sanitation Minister Pemmy Majodina announced a 7% cut for domestic and industrial users and 15% for irrigation.

Source: Sunday Express Lesotho · 2026-11-18 · 1 min read

Water allocations in South Africa's Western Cape are being reduced because dam storage has fallen under the preferred operating benchmark set by the Department of Water and Sanitation. Majodina announced a 7% reduction for domestic and industrial users and a 15% reduction for irrigation on Monday, measures expected to save 56.8 million cubic metres a year.

The Western Cape Water Supply System, which serves Cape Town, the West Coast and agricultural users, held 72.5% of capacity on November 2, or 646.5 million cubic metres, against a benchmark of 80%. A year earlier it stood at 84.4%. Below-normal winter rainfall has cut storage, and a forecast strong to extreme El Niño could mean hotter weather, more evaporation and less runoff.

Majodina urged municipalities, businesses, farmers and households to save water and keep clear of their full allocations. The department says the system remains stable and can meet current needs, but it will keep tracking dam levels, climate and demand, and may take further steps if conditions worsen.

Why it mattersIrrigation users in the Western Cape must now draw 15% less water, a cut worth 29.6 million cubic metres a year.
