Lesotho authority trains local teams as it develops national cyber response

The interim response team is running a three-day programme after years of reliance on international specialists, the authority’s chief executive said.

Source: Lesotho Times · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Lesotho’s communications regulator is using a three-day cybersecurity training programme to build local capacity, as its interim response team works to strengthen the country’s ability to handle digital threats. The National Cybersecurity Capacity Building Week began at Mpilo Boutique Hotel and brings together local and international experts.

Authority chief executive Nizam Goolam said Lesotho had relied on international specialists for years and that the programme was intended to develop local expertise. The authority invited experts from the Cyber Capacity Centre for Southern Africa to help train Basotho. Cybersecurity manager Tumane Mahloane said the interim team, first established within the regulator under the Communications Act of 2012, now serves as the country’s interim computer emergency response team.

The government is working to establish a permanent national cybersecurity body. The week also included a two-day hackathon in which students from four universities and colleges competed to develop cybersecurity solutions; winners were due to be announced on Friday.

Why it mattersThe government’s work to establish a permanent national cybersecurity body follows the interim team’s expanded role in responding to threats.
