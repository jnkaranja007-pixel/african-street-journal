Man Sentenced to 20 Years or M15,000 Fine for Fatal Food Dispute

A Leribe man convicted of culpable homicide must also pay M30,000 compensation to the victim's family after a fatal altercation.

Masupha Katse has been sentenced to 20 years in prison or a M15,000 fine for killing ’Mane Mothatjaneng during a village celebration in August 2019. Acting High Court Judge ’Manapo Motebele also ordered Katse to pay M30,000 compensation to the victim’s family. The fatal assault occurred at Ha Seetsa following a dispute over food served at a community event.

Justice Motebele rejected Katse’s self-defense claim, finding he inflicted the fatal injury during a heat of passion after sudden provocation. The incident began when Katse, arriving from an initiation ceremony, demanded meat and papa, remarking that only dogs ate meat without the staple. A subsequent intervention by Mothatjaneng escalated into violence, with the Crown asserting Katse struck Mothatjaneng on the forehead with a stick.

Although no witnesses saw the fatal blow, prosecutors presented circumstantial evidence and alleged informal admissions. A post-mortem confirmed a severe head injury as the cause of death. The defense argued Katse struck Mothatjaneng on the chest, believing he was reaching for a concealed knife, and highlighted the deceased's violent reputation and a prior serious head injury.

Why it mattersA man was convicted of culpable homicide and ordered to pay a fine and compensation, affecting his livelihood and freedom.

Matekane Demands Tougher Drug War as M5bn Case Grips Nation

Prime Minister Sam Matekane urged a crackdown on drug dealers, citing the M5 billion Khubetsoana case involving Chinese nationals and a senior official.

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has called for a more aggressive approach to combating drug trafficking, stating that narcotics are increasingly harming the youth and communities of Lesotho. He emphasized that individuals involved in the drug trade should face severe penalties from the justice system. Matekane's remarks follow the high-profile M5 billion Khubetsoana drug case, which has led to charges against three Chinese nationals, their company Stan Dar (Pty) Ltd, a senior government official, and a political party leader.

During a visit to Mabote Police Station, Matekane highlighted the pervasive threat of drug use, which he noted affects both young people and adults. He commended the police for dismantling a drug warehouse in Berea, expressing hope for stringent court punishments for those responsible. The Prime Minister questioned the adequacy of the recently approved 500 additional police officers in addressing the escalating drug problem, particularly in light of the corruption of school-going youth.

The M5 billion case involves allegations of a clandestine drug-manufacturing operation. Police reportedly discovered approximately 7.5 tonnes of chemicals used in the production of crystal methamphetamine, ecstasy, and khat-related substances at a Khubetsoana workshop. Those charged, including Chen Wei, Chen Minglevu, Yang Xiong Kang, Kemong Masupha, and Machesetsa Mofomobe, face multiple charges and deny any wrongdoing, contesting the accusations in court.

Why it mattersPrime Minister Matekane's call for a tougher drug war directly impacts citizens by highlighting the justice system's response to drug-related crime and the potential for increased law enforcement presence.

Mofolo convicted of Khetheng murder after decade-long trial

Senior Inspector Mathibeli Mofolo was found guilty of intentionally causing the death of Police Constable Mokalekale Khetheng, who disappeared after being taken into custody.

High Court Judge Charles Hungwe convicted Senior Inspector Mathibeli Mofolo of the murder of Police Constable Mokalekale Khetheng, who died after being taken into police custody on March 26, 2016. The judge stated that Mofolo unlawfully and intentionally caused Khetheng’s death. Three co-accused, including former Hlotse Police Station commander Senior Superintendent Thabo Tšukulu, were acquitted of murder, though Tšukulu was convicted of being an accessory after the fact for attempting to cover up the killing.

The judgment concludes nearly ten years of legal proceedings following Khetheng's disappearance after his arrest and the discovery of his body the next day in a remote gorge. Justice Hungwe noted that Khetheng was taken alive into police custody, deliberately omitted from official records, and found dead with signs of violence. The court found the circumstantial case against Mofolo, including his exclusive custody of Khetheng, failure to explain the circumstances, and subsequent false accounts, to be overwhelming.

While the court found that Khetheng met an unlawful death, the precise cause, place, and time could not be established due to a lack of direct evidence of the killing itself. Inspector Mabitle Matona and Sub-Inspector Haleokoe Taasoane were acquitted and released. The court relied on circumstantial evidence against Mofolo, including his use of handcuffs and gloves, and his attempts to suppress evidence.

Why it mattersSenior Inspector Mofolo's conviction for murder affects the Lesotho police force by holding an officer accountable for the death of a detainee and the subsequent cover-up.

Matekane Denies Links to Crime Boss Cat Matlala

Prime Minister Sam Matekane denies knowing South African crime boss Vusimusi “Cat” Matlala, but confirms a former aide dealt with him.

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has denied any association with South African crime figure Vusimusi “Cat” Matlala, who claimed to have been negotiating VIP protection services in Lesotho. Matekane stated he has never met, known, or conducted business with Matlala, nor authorized any company linked to him for security services. This statement addresses prior allegations by Basotho National Party leader Machesetsa Mofomobe suggesting a business partnership between the two.

The Prime Minister’s Office acknowledged that a former member of Matekane’s private security detail engaged with Matlala regarding the establishment of a VIP security company in Lesotho. However, the office insisted these dealings were in the former aide’s private capacity, without the Prime Minister’s knowledge or authorization. Matlala had testified before South Africa’s Madlanga Commission of Inquiry about his company’s registration in Lesotho and ongoing negotiations for security services.

Matlala’s testimony included claims that he had been in negotiations to provide services, including presidential protection, in Lesotho. He also confirmed working with General Lesetja Senona, provincial head of the Hawks in KwaZulu Natal, who had expressed interest in the venture, though Senona was ultimately not hired. Matlala confirmed the company was registered in Lesotho but could not recall the proposed timeline for the arrangement.

Why it mattersPrime Minister Sam Matekane is affected by allegations of association with a crime boss, which could impact his public image and political standing.

Nine Basotho Students Depart for China on Scholarships

The students will pursue studies in fields including artificial intelligence, agriculture, and medicine as bilateral educational ties deepen.

Nine Basotho students are en route to China to undertake studies in artificial intelligence, agriculture, medicine, engineering, and finance. The departure marks an expansion of educational cooperation between Lesotho and China. Chinese Ambassador to Lesotho Yang Xiaokun bid farewell to the students at a reception held at the Chinese Embassy this week, encouraging them to maximize the opportunity and represent their country.

Ambassador Yang emphasized that education is a long-term investment in national development, fostering not only knowledge and skills but also character and responsibility. He noted that China's own progress is a testament to sustained investment in education, science, technology, and human capital. Over the past three years, one thousand six hundred eighty-nine Basotho youths have participated in study and training programs offered by China, either in person or online.

The ambassador highlighted that education and skills development are crucial pillars of China-Lesotho relations, with thousands of Basotho benefiting from various programs. More than two hundred fifty Basotho have traveled to China this year for educational purposes or are scheduled to do so. China announced sixty thousand training opportunities for Africans at the 2024 Forum on China-Africa Cooperation Beijing Summit, underscoring a commitment to closer people-to-people ties.

Why it mattersNine Basotho students will gain specialized education in China, potentially enhancing their future career prospects and contributing to national development.
