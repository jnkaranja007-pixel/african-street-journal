Western Cape water allocations cut as dam storage drops

Water and Sanitation Minister Pemmy Majodina announced a 7% cut for domestic and industrial users and 15% for irrigation.

Water allocations in South Africa's Western Cape are being reduced because dam storage has fallen under the preferred operating benchmark set by the Department of Water and Sanitation. Majodina announced a 7% reduction for domestic and industrial users and a 15% reduction for irrigation on Monday, measures expected to save 56.8 million cubic metres a year.

The Western Cape Water Supply System, which serves Cape Town, the West Coast and agricultural users, held 72.5% of capacity on November 2, or 646.5 million cubic metres, against a benchmark of 80%. A year earlier it stood at 84.4%. Below-normal winter rainfall has cut storage, and a forecast strong to extreme El Niño could mean hotter weather, more evaporation and less runoff.

Majodina urged municipalities, businesses, farmers and households to save water and keep clear of their full allocations. The department says the system remains stable and can meet current needs, but it will keep tracking dam levels, climate and demand, and may take further steps if conditions worsen.

Why it mattersIrrigation users in the Western Cape must now draw 15% less water, a cut worth 29.6 million cubic metres a year.

Khutlisi elected Maseru Mayor

Councillor ’Malerato Khutlisi defeated her predecessor Thabo Sefuthi in a vote at the Maseru City Council Chambers.

’Malerato Khutlisi, a councillor for the Revolution for Prosperity (RFP) party, has been elected the new Mayor of Maseru. She secured 11 votes against her predecessor Thabo Sefuthi’s eight in Friday’s election held at the Maseru City Council (MCC) Chambers. Khutlisi, who won her council seat in 2023, becomes the third woman to lead the capital’s council since September 2020.

Mahlohola Phalatsi, the councillor for Majoe-a-li-tšene, was elected deputy mayor unopposed. Following the election, Ha Leqele Chief Tšosane Mputlane appealed to councillors to unite and focus on improving service delivery. He urged them to put public interests above personal gain.

The new mayor acknowledged the significant responsibilities of her position and expressed readiness to tackle the challenges ahead. Deputy Mayor Phalatsi pledged his full support to Khutlisi and encouraged councillors to move past previous disagreements. Both leaders emphasized the importance of working together for the benefit of the people.

Why it mattersCouncillor ’Malerato Khutlisi was elected Maseru Mayor on Friday, with the new administration expected to focus on improving service delivery.

Sport · 2026-11-17

Molapo wins November Golf Corporate League tourney

Mathaleha Molapo secured first place in the Maseru Golf Club Corporate League November tournament with 39 points.

Mathaleha Molapo won the Maseru Golf Club Corporate League November tournament on Friday, scoring 39 points. She tied with Mphaphathi Moshoeshoe, who also scored 39 points, but Molapo won on countback due to her lower handicap of 22. Setšabe Lefosa came in third place with 37 points.

The tournament was sponsored by Momentum Health Lesotho, which recently transitioned from Metropolitan Health Lesotho. Momentum Health Lesotho Finance Manager Semethe Lephosa stated the event was a success and pledged future sponsorships. He noted the tournament provided an opportunity to showcase the company's new identity.

Momentum Health Lesotho offers healthcare and insurance solutions in Lesotho and South Africa. Lephosa encouraged the public to obtain insurance for life protection. The Maseru Golf Club Corporate League holds monthly tournaments sponsored by various corporations.

Why it mattersGolfers in the Maseru Golf Club Corporate League will continue to compete in monthly tournaments sponsored by companies like Momentum Health Lesotho.
