Victims sue police chief for M825 000

Two men allege abduction, torture and broken bones by officers during investigations.

Two men are suing Police Chief Advocate Borotho Matsoso for M825 000 in damages, claiming police officers subjected them to severe torture. Nthoes??? Mohapi and Sechaba Senoko allege abduction, unlawful detention, and brutal abuse including suffocation and broken bones. The lawsuits filed in the High Court accuse officers of violating their constitutional rights.

Mr Mohapi, seeking M450 000, alleges he was tortured after reporting to Roma Police Station on September 18, 2025, regarding a robbery. He claims officers Rathulo Mojaki, Maoela, and Pokothoane beat him with a rubber tube and a steel bar, breaking both his arms. Mr Senoko, 67, is also seeking damages for alleged torture.

The lawsuits name Advocate Matsoso, Attorney General Advocate Rapelang Motsieloa, and the involved police officers and stations. Both men detail horrific physical abuse in their court papers, seeking compensation for the alleged violations.

Why it mattersTwo Basotho men are seeking compensation after alleging severe physical abuse and torture by police officers.

Lesotho plans mini off-road motorcycle event

The Lesotho Off Road Association will host a shorter, less technical race on November 21 at Ha Molengoane.

The Lesotho Off Road Association (LoRA) is organizing a Mini-Roof of Africa event on November 21 at Ha Molengoane, near Maseru. This event aims to provide riders with another chance to experience Lesotho's off-road terrain after the main Roof of Africa race in September.

LoRA public relations officer Lehlohonolo Taela stated that the association received requests from riders who missed the main event due to school commitments. The Mini-Roof will focus on the Bronze and Iron categories, omitting the more demanding Silver and Gold classes.

The main Roof of Africa race, now part of the Hard Enduro World Championship series, has had its dates shifted from their traditional late November or early December slot. This change has impacted participation, particularly for riders from South Africa.

Why it mattersMotorcycle riders and enthusiasts in Lesotho will have another opportunity to compete in an off-road event.

Maluti Lager hosts MPs for brand experience

Brewers aim to educate lawmakers on product heritage and economic contribution.

Maluti Mountain Brewery (MMB) hosted Members of Parliament (MPs) on Friday at its Maseru premises for an event focused on Maluti Premium Lager. The MPs, part of the Portfolio Committee on Economic and Development Cluster, learned about the beer's heritage and drinking rituals as part of MMB’s Brew Across the World programme.

MMB Managing Director ’Mapeete Ntjana stated the initiative aimed to help MPs understand the product's potential contribution to economic growth and employment. She expressed hope that the lawmakers would share this knowledge with the public, emphasizing the lager as a unique Lesotho product. MMB also highlighted its partnership with the Lesotho Mounted Police Service to combat drunk driving through donated breathalysers.

Qeme Constituency MP Sello Hakane, who chairs the Economic and Development Cluster, thanked MMB for the insightful experience. He noted that while Maluti Premium Lager is manufactured locally, some ingredients are still imported, urging for greater use of local sourcing. Hakane also acknowledged MMB's significant contribution to Lesotho's economy and employment.

Why it mattersLesotho MPs were educated on a local product's heritage and economic impact, with concerns raised about imported ingredients.

Tšolo declines to testify in M856m solar case

Former minister Temeki Tšolo will not present his defence in the M856 million Frazer Solar scandal.

Former Minister in the Prime Minister’s Office, Temeki Tšolo, has chosen not to testify in his own defence regarding the M856 million Frazer Solar scandal. The High Court will now decide his fate based on the prosecution and defence's presented evidence. Mr Tšolo faces accusations of exposing Lesotho to a £50 million (approximately M856 million) legal claim through a deal with German businessman Robert Frazer.

Mr Tšolo declined to take the witness stand after the prosecution closed its case on Thursday, having presented four witnesses. His lawyer, Adv Nkola Masoabi, informed the court of the decision after consultations. The prosecution alleges Mr Tšolo signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with Frazer without Cabinet approval and beyond his ministerial authority, breaching government financial procedures.

Former Prime Minister Dr Moeketsi Majoro testified that Mr Tšolo lacked authority to sign agreements with financial implications. Former Secretary in the Prime Minister’s Office, ‘Masentle Ntobaki, detailed Frazer's close contact with Mr Tšolo between September 2017 and May 2018. Dr Majoro also confirmed the MoU was signed before Cabinet approval and that other ministers were not consulted.

Why it mattersFormer Minister Temeki Tšolo is accused of exposing Lesotho to a M856 million legal claim through a controversial solar deal.
