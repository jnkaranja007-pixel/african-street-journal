China reaffirms ties and multilateralism with Lesotho

Ambassador Yang Xiaokun urged local media to respect China's sovereignty claims over Taiwan.

Chinese Ambassador to Lesotho, Yang Xiaokun, met with local media in Maseru this week to reaffirm China's commitment to multilateralism and strengthening relations with Lesotho. The meeting marked the 55th anniversary of the restoration of the People’s Republic of China’s (PRC) seat at the United Nations (UN) and Commemoration Day of Taiwan’s Restoration.

Ambassador Yang stated that history shapes international relations and offers lessons for safeguarding peace and nations' legitimate rights. He recalled that the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 2758 on 25 October 1971, which restored the PRC's rights at the UN and recognised its representatives as the sole legitimate representatives of China.

The ambassador also highlighted China's contributions to international development and peace efforts, including its Global Development Initiative which has mobilised over US$23 billion. China has implemented zero-tariff treatment for 53 African countries, a measure from which Lesotho is expected to benefit.

Why it mattersLesotho is expected to benefit from China's implementation of zero-tariff treatment for 53 African countries with which it maintains diplomatic relations.

Lesotho to chair SADC security body

The nation takes over leadership of the SADC Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation after a decade under scrutiny.

Lesotho has been elected the incoming chair of the Southern African Development Community (SADC) Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation. Prime Minister Sam Matekane accepted the position following an extraordinary summit of SADC heads of state and government. This appointment follows years of Lesotho being under regional observation for political instability and security-sector failures.

Foreign Affairs Minister Limpho Tau described the appointment as an honour for all Basotho, stating it is not a victory for one party but for the nation. The country was removed from the Organ’s agenda in August 2025 after being placed on it in 2014 due to an attempted military coup and subsequent political troubles. The SADC Preventive Mission was deployed to Lesotho in 2017 after the killing of Lieutenant General Khoantle Motšomotšo.

The election signifies a turnaround in Lesotho’s regional standing, suggesting the region believes the country has made progress. However, the government must continue reforms, including independent institutions and security agencies operating within the law. The Tenth Amendment to the Constitution passed in 2025 was a step towards addressing governance weaknesses, but the broader reform process remains unfinished.

Why it mattersLesotho's citizens are directly affected by the nation's new leadership role in regional security, which requires continued domestic reforms and stability.

Mafeteng Hospital sued M2 million for alleged negligence

A patient claims faecal matter leaked into his body after a seven-day wait for medical attention post-surgery.

Mafeteng Hospital faces a M2 million lawsuit for alleged medical negligence after a patient claimed a botched surgery left faecal matter leaking from his intestines. Teboho Rammusi alleges he received no medical attention for seven days following stomach surgery in December 2024, before being transferred to Tšepong Hospital and later Bloemfontein, South Africa.

In court papers filed in the High Court, Rammusi accuses the hospital of failing to provide adequate care and qualified practitioners. He cites the Principal Secretary in the Ministry of Health, the Minister of Health, the Medical Superintendent of Mafeteng Hospital, and the Attorney General as respondents. Rammusi claims the hospital's medical superintendent failed to ensure a skilled practitioner was always available.

Rammusi claims the alleged negligence resulted in additional medical expenses, a severe lung infection, and pain and suffering. He argues the government is liable as Mafeteng Hospital is state-owned and its officers acted within their employment scope. Doctors at Tšepong Hospital reportedly discovered faecal matter had spilled into his abdominal cavity.

Why it mattersA patient is seeking M2 million from Mafeteng Hospital due to alleged medical negligence following surgery, resulting in additional medical expenses and suffering.

Politics · 2026-10-31

Lebona Lephema considers new party formation

Disgruntled tycoon Lebona Lephema may form a new political party if his bid to oust RFP deputy leader fails.

Business tycoon Lebona Lephema is considering forming a new political party and leaving the Revolution for Prosperity (RFP). Lephema, a founding member of the RFP, has been consulting with disgruntled members of the All Basotho Convention (ABC), including former minister Chalane Phori and ex-secretary-general Lebohang Hlaele.

Lephema fell out with Prime Minister Sam Matekane after resisting a ministry split and refusing redeployment to the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Business Development in April. He was dismissed from Cabinet in May. Lephema also believes he is entitled to the RFP deputy leadership, which he sees as a step to succeeding Matekane.

His ambition to oust current RFP deputy leader Nthomeng Majara has reportedly divided the party. Majara has the backing of Prime Minister Matekane. Lephema's potential departure follows his earlier dismissal from Cabinet after resisting redeployment.

Why it mattersLebona Lephema, a founding member of the ruling RFP and former minister, is considering forming a new political party.
