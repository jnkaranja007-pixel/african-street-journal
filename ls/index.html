Lesotho earmarks M50 million for unfinished royal palace

The project's cost has risen to M461 million, nearly four times the original estimate, after 15 years of delays.

Lesotho's government has allocated an additional M50 million to resume construction on the new Royal Palace in Maseru, a project that has been delayed for over 15 years. Construction began in 2011 with a planned completion in May 2013, but remains unfinished. This new funding brings the total projected cost to M461 million, significantly exceeding the initial M136.7 million budget.

An audit report from May 2025, reviewed by the Senate Committee on Government Assurances, revealed overpayments of M74 million to consultants and contractors. Payments were reportedly made without clear contractual justification or proof of work completed. The ministry also lacked documentation for the appointment of Nyolohelo Mohale as Clerk of Works, despite M5.49 million in payments to him.

The government aims to ensure transparent procurement and accountability for the M50 million allocation as construction restarts. Their Majesties King Letsie III and Queen ’Masenate Mohato Seeiso await a proper official residence, and the country seeks to close this prolonged and costly chapter.

Why it mattersLesotho taxpayers are facing a nearly half-billion maloti expenditure on a royal residence that has been under construction for 15 years due to government inefficiency.

Harrison targets CAF group stage for Lijabatho

New coach Mark Harrison aims to make Lijabatho the first Lesotho club to reach the Confederation of African Football Champions League group stages.

Lijabatho's new head coach, Mark Harrison, has set winning the league title and advancing to the CAF Champions League group stages as his primary goals. Harrison has guided the team to two wins in his first two matches since taking over from Kabelo Malapane. Lijabatho currently leads the league standings with 23 points after defeating LCS 3-0 on Sunday.

Harrison stated his immediate aim is to defend the league title and secure a return to continental competition, with a specific ambition to reach the CAF Champions League group stages. This achievement would make the club the first Lesotho side to reach this stage. Lijabatho previously exited the competition in the preliminary round after a loss and a draw against CFFA of Madagascar.

The coach expressed satisfaction with the squad's progress and his intention to implement a passing and possession-based style of play. Harrison noted that while Lesotho has football talent, infrastructure, particularly playing surfaces, presents a challenge, a common issue across Africa. He believes the team is currently at 70-75 percent of its potential.

Why it mattersLesotho football fans will be affected by the potential historic achievement of their club reaching the CAF Champions League group stages.

Business · 2026-11-08

Johane Garments becomes Johane Industrials

Mosotho entrepreneur Tšepo Johane celebrated his factory's transition from SME to industrial operation in Ha Thetsane.

Johane Garments, a textile factory owned by Tšepo Johane, has officially become Johane Industrials, marking its transition from a small and medium-sized enterprise to a full-scale industrial operation. The factory, which began at a skills development centre, celebrated this milestone in Ha Thetsane after years of overcoming debt and compliance issues.

Trade, Industry and Business Development Minister Motlatsi Maqelepo attended the launch, calling it a testament to Mosotho entrepreneurial determination. He noted that Johane Industrials had faced significant challenges, including outstanding debt that nearly led to closure, but acknowledged Mr. Johane's resilience. The minister stated the government is committed to supporting business growth through funding and infrastructure.

Mr. Maqelepo emphasized manufacturing's central role in Lesotho's economic development, calling for greater respect for the sector and highlighting the need for homegrown industrialists. Mr. Johane expressed gratitude for the journey, which started at the Station skills development centre, and pledged to double his efforts for the enterprise's lasting success, focusing on local job creation and sustainability.

Why it mattersThe transition of Johane Garments to Johane Industrials signifies a step towards industrialisation and local economic development in Lesotho.
