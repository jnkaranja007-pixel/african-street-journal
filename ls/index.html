Lesotho elected chair of SADC security organ

Prime Minister Sam Matekane will lead the regional body's politics, defence, and security cooperation.

Lesotho has been elected the incoming chair of the Southern African Development Community’s Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation. Prime Minister Sam Matekane was elected to the position during a virtual summit on October 21, 2026, ending a deadlock with Angola. This appointment places Lesotho in a leadership role within the regional bloc's most influential security structure.

Foreign Affairs Minister Limpho Tau stated the appointment reflects the region's confidence in Lesotho's political stability, noting the country was removed from the SADC agenda in 2025 after years of monitoring its domestic instability. The Organ Troika will now include Lesotho, alongside Eswatini and Malawi.

The organ is responsible for supporting democratic governance, conflict prevention, and regional security cooperation. Lesotho's new role means it will directly engage in discussions on regional challenges such as the conflict in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo and violent extremism in northern Mozambique.

Why it mattersLesotho's election to lead the SADC Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation gives the country a direct role in addressing regional security challenges.

Green City estate guards paid with food parcels

Lesotho residents guarding Green City Estate receive monthly food parcels for 12-hour shifts.

Residents of Ha Abia are guarding the Green City Estate for up to 12 hours daily in exchange for monthly food parcels, a practice condemned by Labour Minister Tšeliso Mokhosi and MP Thuso Makhalanyane. The residents patrol the estate, prevent livestock from entering, and stop unauthorised people from accessing the property. Green City director Mahesh Patel stated the arrangement is voluntary community policing, not employment.

The food parcels provided to 10 selected residents include 12.5kg of maize meal, 12.5kg of bread flour, two litres of cooking oil, 2kg of beans, and 10 cartons of 500ml milk. Some residents feel the groceries are inadequate compensation for their demanding duties and have expressed dissatisfaction. An attempt to negotiate cash payments resulted in an offer of M550 per month.

The estate management initially arranged for a heater in a guardroom but removed it after residents raised concerns about their compensation. The arrangement was facilitated through local chiefs who identified residents willing to monitor the vast development's boundaries at Ha Pena-Pena.

Why it mattersResidents of Ha Abia are receiving inadequate compensation in the form of food parcels for their security work at the Green City Estate.

Law minister blamed for M80 million election fund misprocurement

Richard Ramoeletsi allegedly authorised irregular purchase of IEC headquarters using unspent election money.

Law and Justice Minister Richard Ramoeletsi is implicated in the irregular purchase of the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) headquarters for M80 million. The Public Accounts Committee (PAC) is questioning his role in authorising the acquisition using unspent funds from the 2023 Local Government Elections.

The Maseru West property purchase from Homestead Holdings was not budgeted for in the 2023/24 financial year, yet the IEC used election money. Auditor General Mathabo Makenete flagged the M80 million expenditure, noting the IEC’s failure to remit unspent allocations to the government’s Consolidated Fund by March 31, 2024.

IEC executives admitted to the PAC that the commission acted wrongly in diverting funds. Finance Manager Mokheseng Mofolo stated the purchase was approved by former Director of Elections Mpaiphele Maqutu via a memorandum and routing slip.

Why it mattersMinister Richard Ramoeletsi is accused of authorising the irregular purchase of the IEC headquarters using M80 million in unspent election funds.

Activists sue Lesotho education minister over school fees

SECTION 2 argues barring learners from exams for parental debt violates their right to education.

Human rights activists, SECTION 2, have taken Education and Training Minister Professor Ntoi Rapapa to the Constitutional Court. They seek to prevent schools from barring learners from final examinations due to unpaid school or examination fees. SECTION 2 argues this practice punishes children for parental debts, violating their constitutional right to education.

SECTION 2 Coordinator Morapeli Roman Masoto stated in an affidavit that excluding learners over unpaid fees interferes with their right to education. The group is seeking urgent court orders against the practice. The central question is whether Lesotho law permits educational consequences for children based on their parents' debts.

Mr Masoto argues that the law does not permit such exclusions, deeming the practice unlawful and discriminatory based on socio-economic status. He cited Section 25 of the Constitution, which supports the progressive realisation of state policy principles, asserting that this does not make Chapter III rights unenforceable.

Why it mattersLearners in Lesotho may be barred from final examinations if their parents owe school fees.

Lesotho minister urges innovation for jobs

Minister Nthati Moorosi called for research to become businesses and practical solutions for national challenges.

Lesotho must transform its science and innovation efforts into tangible businesses and jobs, according to Minister of Information, Communications, Science, Technology and Innovation Nthati Moorosi. Speaking at the third National Science, Technology and Innovation Week 2026, she urged the country to move beyond annual discussions and apply research to solve national problems and drive economic growth.

The event, held at the Lehakoe Recreation Centre, aims to promote the practical application of science, technology, and innovation (STI) to improve the lives of Basotho. Minister Moorosi stated that nations investing in knowledge and innovation are better positioned to industrialise and compete globally, emphasizing that Lesotho needs to become a producer of knowledge and solutions, not just a consumer of technology.

The government is strengthening policy frameworks, including the upcoming Research and Innovation Policy, to support this transition. This policy seeks to turn research into commercially viable products and services by fostering collaboration between universities, research institutions, entrepreneurs, investors, and industry, ensuring research benefits factories, farms, businesses, and communities.

Why it mattersLesotho's citizens are affected by the call to translate research into practical solutions that can create businesses and jobs.
