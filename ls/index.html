two men sentenced for 2024 border murder

Basotho nationals received life sentences in South Africa for a double murder at Maseru Bridge.

Two men, Tumelo Tsiu, 32, and Lineo Walatsa, 30, have been sentenced to life imprisonment by the Ficksburg High Court in South Africa for a double murder at the Maseru Bridge border post in June 2024. The court also sentenced them to nine months for illegal presence in South Africa. They were acquitted of charges related to an unlicensed firearm and conspiracy to commit murder.

The victims, Moholi “Bohome” Mohapi, 31, and Rebone David Sephaka, 40, were shot dead around 4am on June 11, 2024, shortly after arriving from Johannesburg. SAPS stated that the gunmen arrived in a white Isuzu bakkie, opened fire on passengers, and fled. Police intercepted the bakkie, recovering a 9mm firearm, and arrested the driver, Seun Gert Moloi, who was later granted R8000 bail and subsequently died before the trial.

The extradition of Tsiu and Walatsa from Lesotho followed cooperation between the SAPS and the Lesotho Mounted Police Service. The accused faced charges including murder, attempted murder of Mr Sekantsi and Mr Pitso, and contravening South Africa’s Immigration Act. The sentences were handed down on Thursday, October 23, 2026.

Lesotho parliament lags on cybercrime law

Southern African nations struggle to update laws against online gender-based violence despite a model law.

Lesotho's laws have not kept pace with technology-facilitated gender-based violence (TFGBV), according to a policy brief from the SADC Parliamentary Forum and Equality Now. While 11 of the 16 SADC member states have cybercrime laws, only five specifically address TFGBV, leaving women vulnerable to online abuse like image sharing without consent and blackmail. Lesotho's Counter Domestic Violence Act of 2022 does define technological abuse and allows courts to prevent electronic contact.

The region faces a significant gap in legal protections against online harms, with many countries lacking specific prohibitions on sharing intimate images without consent or on online coercion. Traffickers exploit social media to lure victims, yet anti-trafficking laws often ignore technology. A lack of dedicated data collection systems hinders governments' understanding of the problem's scale. Even existing laws can be difficult to apply, as seen in Malawi where intent to cause fear for safety is required, overlooking the humiliation of image-based abuse.

Lesotho hosted the SADC Parliament Forum in December 2021, where the SADC Model Law on Gender-Based Violence, including online cybercrimes, was adopted. However, nearly five years later, none of the 16 member states have fully implemented this model law, with most rated as 'partial' and some still drafting legislation. This slow progress leaves women across the region exposed to online abuse with inadequate legal recourse.

Why it mattersWomen and girls in Lesotho and across Southern Africa are at risk of technology-facilitated gender-based violence due to outdated laws.

Lesotho probes oil firms for alleged fraud

Companies accused of claiming fees for fuel never offloaded at Maseru depot, government to launch audit.

The Lesotho government is launching an investigation into oil companies accused of defrauding the state by claiming storage and handling fees for fuel that was never delivered to the Maseru depot. Local fuel transporters raised the allegations, suggesting that public funds were paid for services not rendered, contrary to the Petroleum Policy which mandates companies to maintain 1.5 million litres of fuel for national security.

Energy and Mining Minister Lejone Mpotjoane has pledged an audit by the Office of the Auditor-General to verify the claims. Transporters state that some companies deliver fuel directly from South Africa to districts, bypassing the Maseru depot entirely, yet continue to charge government for offloading and storage.

The Petroleum Fund, established in 1955, regulates fuel prices and Lesotho imports all its petroleum products from South Africa. The alleged scheme involves companies collecting full transport reimbursements and then forcing local operators to accept lower rates, impacting their operating costs.

Why it mattersLesotho citizens are paying for fuel storage and handling services that oil companies allegedly never provided.

Lesotho government launches Tebellong bridge project

Unik Construction will build the Senqu River bridge, expected to improve safety and tourism in Qacha’s Nek.

The Lesotho government has appointed Unik Construction to build a bridge across the Senqu River at Tebellong and will tar eight kilometres of road linking Sehlabathebe to Thamathu. The M3 billion infrastructure push in Qacha’s Nek aims to improve connectivity and safety, with the bridge project expected to take 24 months and the road project 18 months.

Roads Directorate Director General Linehella Ntšere stated the bridge project addresses longstanding community concerns, while Public Works and Transport Minister Matjato Moteane expressed delight that the project is finally underway. Zutari will serve as the supervising consultant for the bridge, which will also include an access road to Tebellong village.

Minister of Tourism, Sports, Arts and Culture Stephen Mputi said the bridge will enhance tourism and local attractions, making travel easier for Basotho and international visitors. Principal Secretary Tšepang Koele announced that recruitment information for the project would be published the following week.

Why it mattersThe Tebellong bridge and road project will improve connectivity and safety for residents in Qacha’s Nek.

Mafeteng Hospital sued M2 million for alleged negligence

A patient claims he received no medical attention for seven days after a stomach operation in December 2024.

Mafeteng Hospital faces a M2 million lawsuit for alleged medical negligence after a patient claimed a botched surgery left faecal matter leaking into his body. Teboho Rammusi alleges he received no medical attention for seven days following a stab wound operation in December 2024 before being transferred to Tšepong Hospital and later Bloemfontein, South Africa. He is suing the Ministry of Health, its principal secretary, the hospital's medical superintendent, and the Attorney General.

Mr Rammusi's court papers state he was admitted on December 27, 2024, and accuses the hospital of failing to provide adequate care and ensure qualified practitioners were available. He claims the medical superintendent failed to ensure a doctor was present at all times and that he did not receive timely assessment or appropriate attention. Faeces were observed coming from tubes on his body during his stay.

Doctors at Tšepong Hospital discovered faecal matter had spilled into his abdominal cavity through punctured intestines after his transfer on January 2, 2025. Mr Rammusi claims the alleged negligence resulted in additional medical expenses, a severe lung infection, pain, and suffering. He argues the government is liable as Mafeteng Hospital is state-owned and operated.

Why it mattersA patient in Lesotho suffered severe complications and incurred additional expenses due to alleged medical negligence at Mafeteng Hospital.
