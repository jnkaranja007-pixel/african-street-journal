Lesotho launches M547.4 million airport-Mafeteng road project

The 64-kilometre road rehabilitation is expected to take 36 months and create temporary employment.

Public Works and Transport Minister Matjato Moteane launched a M547.4 million project to rehabilitate the 64-kilometre road connecting Moshoeshoe I International Airport to Mafeteng Traffic Circle. The project, awarded to Sino Hydro Corporation Limited with Mafube Consulting Engineers as consultants, is scheduled for completion within 36 months.

Minister Moteane urged the contractor to ensure quality workmanship and local community involvement, including sourcing equipment locally and allowing villages access to surplus quarry materials for their own road improvements. He stressed the importance of worker welfare and adherence to specifications, warning against substandard materials.

The road project is part of the government's strategy to enhance national road infrastructure, boost trade, and stimulate economic activity by creating temporary employment. Similar road upgrades are underway, including repairs on Masianokeng road and reconstruction between Mohale’s Hoek and Quthing.

Mahlala urges Lesotho to exploit local resources for jobs

Basotho Development Front leader Bothata Mahlala says the nation must harness its own capabilities instead of relying on foreign markets.

Basotho Development Front (BDF) leader Bothata Mahlala stated that Lesotho must fully exploit its competitive advantages to create jobs, rather than relying on foreign markets and outside assistance. The Quthing-born businessman and philanthropist advocates for supporting Basotho-driven projects and generating employment from the country’s existing resources. Mr Mahlala, who previously contested the Mount Moorosi constituency in 2022, formed the BDF after leaving the Democratic Congress.

Mr Mahlala believes Lesotho has the capacity to produce and export on its own terms, suggesting the nation can produce goods for the South African market due to lower production costs and wages. He also pointed to the potential for job creation through agriculture and bottling clean water for export. These initiatives, he argued, do not require external help.

The BDF leader’s message comes amid ongoing economic difficulties, including instability in the textile and apparel sector, which is Lesotho’s largest source of formal jobs. The African Growth and Opportunity Act (AGOA) was extended to December 31, 2028, but Mr Mahlala feels the country should not depend on such agreements for its economic future.

Why it mattersLesotho's citizens face persistent unemployment and economic challenges, and the BDF leader proposes internal solutions.

Naledi Funeral Planners earns tax compliance award

Revenue Services Lesotho recognised the company for adhering to tax laws amid a boardroom dispute.

Revenue Services Lesotho (RSL) recently presented Naledi Funeral Planners (Pty) Ltd with a Tax Compliant Taxpayer Award. The award honours the funeral services company for its adherence to Lesotho’s tax laws. Naledi Funeral Planners, incorporated in 2012, operates across northern, central, and southern Lesotho, offering funeral policies and related services.

The recognition comes as the company navigates a boardroom dispute that has reached the High Court’s Commercial Division. Acting Managing Director Tšolo Seutloali stated the award reflects the company's commitment to good governance and compliance, noting that clients entrust Naledi with their money. He praised the finance department for ensuring compliance with VAT obligations.

Seutloali described tax payment as a way of giving back to the country and a testament to integrity, institutional discipline, and fiduciary responsibility. The company's operations include providing funeral services, coffins, and cashback to clients who pay premiums.

Lesotho U-15 team learns from FIFA World Cup

The national squad finished 116th out of 189 countries in Baku, Azerbaijan, in their first FIFA World Cup appearance.

Lesotho's Under-15 men's football team participated in the FIFA U-15 World Cup and Festival in Baku, Azerbaijan, from October 24 to 30. The team, largely drawn from the Lesotho Football Association (LeFA) Academy in Leribe, finished 116th out of 189 participating nations. This marked Lesotho's debut at any FIFA World Cup event across all age categories. The squad recorded three wins and five defeats in eight matches during the tournament.

Coach Mahlomola Matšoara described the experience as a learning curve, highlighting the need for local players to improve speed, physical strength, and bravery. He noted that the eight-a-side format, played in 20-minute halves, accelerated tactical decision-making under pressure. Matšoara expressed confidence that the LeFA Academy, supported by FIFA's Talent Development Scheme since 2023, would help develop more competitive players.

FIFA has supplied the academy with a development coach, Sam Smith from the United Kingdom, to nurture players born between 2011 and 2012. Matšoara advocates for the adoption of the eight-a-side format in schools to enhance players' technical, tactical, and physical growth. The tournament exposed the team to experimental rule changes and demanding conditions, with teams playing approximately two matches daily at a high tempo.

Why it mattersLesotho's U-15 football team gained international experience and identified areas for player development at the FIFA World Cup.

Lesotho pension fund reports M2 billion returns

The Public Officers’ Defined Contribution Pension Fund achieved M2 billion in investment returns for the 2025/26 financial year.

The Public Officers’ Defined Contribution Pension Fund (PODCPF) in Lesotho achieved M2 billion in investment returns for the 2025/26 financial year. Finance Minister Retšelisitsoe Matlanyane praised the fund for its resilience against global economic shocks and for upholding good governance. The fund's assets under management increased from M14.1 billion in 2025 to M16.4 billion in 2026.

These results were shared at the Fund’s fourth Annual Member Engagement Forum, themed “Be informed, Prepare for the future, We are with you.” Minister Matlanyane noted the fund's strong performance was significant given global challenges like geopolitical fragmentation and trade tensions. She added that global equities produced double-digit returns, with developed markets, particularly the United States, making substantial contributions.

Lesotho's domestic economy experienced modest growth, supported by construction linked to the Lesotho Highlands Water Project Phase II. Government finances benefited from Southern African Customs Union (SACU) transfers and water royalties. However, underlying economic growth drivers remain weak, with limited private sector expansion and persistent unemployment impacting the textile industry, exports, and household demand.

Why it mattersPublic officers in Lesotho can expect their pension funds to remain resilient and well-managed, despite global economic instability.
