Lesotho elected to lead SADC security organ

Prime Minister Sam Matekane took over the chair of the Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation following a virtual summit yesterday.

Lesotho has been appointed the incoming chair of the Southern African Development Community (SADC) Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation. Prime Minister Sam Matekane was elected to the position during an Extraordinary Summit of SADC Heads of State and Government held virtually yesterday. This appointment concludes a deadlock between Lesotho and Angola that had delayed the decision since August 2026.

South African President Cyril Ramaphosa confirmed Mr Matekane’s election, placing Lesotho on the Organ Troika alongside Eswatini and Malawi. Foreign Affairs Minister Limpho Tau stated the appointment signifies regional confidence in Lesotho’s political stability, urging citizens to celebrate. Mr Tau noted this comes after Lesotho was removed from the SADC agenda in 2025, having been on it since 2014.

The summit addressed ongoing instability in the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo and the need for continued support for Madagascar's political stability. President Ramaphosa emphasized that peace, security, and political stability are foundational for regional development and prosperity, highlighting the need for continued cooperation to resolve disputes and strengthen democratic governance.

Why it mattersLesotho's government has been entrusted with a leadership role in regional security, reflecting a shift in international perception of the country's stability.

Two men sentenced for double murder at Maseru Bridge

Basotho nationals Tumelo Tsiu and Lineo Walatsa received life sentences for a 2024 shooting that killed two famo musicians.

Two Basotho men, Tumelo Tsiu, 32, and Lineo Walatsa, 30, have been sentenced to life imprisonment by the Ficksburg High Court in South Africa for a double murder. The court convicted them for the June 2024 killings of famo musicians Moholi “Bohome” Mohapi and Rebone David Sephaka, also known as Serurubele, near the Maseru Bridge border post. They were also sentenced to life for two attempted murder counts and nine months for illegal entry into South Africa.

The South African Police Service (SAPS) stated that the two accused were arrested in Lesotho in July 2024 and extradited to South Africa. The shooting occurred around 4am on June 11, 2024, when an unknown number of men in a white Isuzu bakkie opened fire on passengers alighting from a taxi from Johannesburg. Bohome, 31, and Sephaka, 40, both associated with the Terene ea Chakela group, died at the scene. Police recovered a 9mm firearm from the bakkie.

The extradition was a result of collaboration between SAPS and the Lesotho Mounted Police Service (LMPS). The driver of the bakkie, Seun Gert Moloi, 30, was arrested and granted R8000 bail but later died before the trial. Tsiu and Walatsa were acquitted of charges related to firearm possession and conspiracy to commit murder.

Why it mattersTwo Basotho nationals were convicted and sentenced in South Africa for murders that occurred at a border post between the two countries.

Law minister implicated in M80 million election fund misprocurement

Lesotho's Public Accounts Committee questions minister Richard Ramoeletsi's role in irregular purchase of IEC headquarters.

Minister of Law and Justice Richard Ramoeletsi faces scrutiny over the M80 million irregular purchase of the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) headquarters in Maseru West. The Public Accounts Committee (PAC) is questioning his authorisation of the acquisition using unspent funds from the 2023 Local Government Elections, which were not budgeted for in the 2023/24 financial year.

Auditor General Mathabo Makenete flagged the M80 million expenditure in her audit of the IEC’s 2023/24 financial statements, noting the commission’s failure to remit unspent budget allocations to the government’s Consolidated Fund by March 31, 2024. IEC executives, including Director of Legal Services Advocate Lehloholo Suping, struggled to explain the diversion of funds, which had been invested with Stanlib.

IEC Finance Manager Mokheseng Mofolo stated that the acquisition was approved by former Director of Elections Mpaiphele Maqutu, citing a memorandum, routing slip, and payment voucher as supporting documents. Advocate Suping admitted the commission acted wrongly in handling the unspent election funds.

Why it mattersLesotho citizens are affected by the potential misuse of M80 million in public funds allocated for elections, impacting government accountability.

Lesotho urged to turn innovation into jobs

Minister Nthati Moorosi called for research to become practical solutions, businesses, and jobs for Basotho.

Lesotho must transform its science and innovation discussions into tangible businesses and jobs, according to Minister of Information, Communications, Science, Technology and Innovation Nthati Moorosi. Speaking at the third National Science, Technology and Innovation Week 2026, she challenged the nation to use science and innovation to solve national problems and drive economic growth.

The event aims to promote the practical use of science, technology, and innovation to improve Basotho lives and address development challenges. Ms Moorosi stated that nations investing in research and innovation are better positioned for industrialisation and global competition, urging Lesotho to become a producer of knowledge rather than a consumer of foreign technologies.

The government is strengthening policy frameworks, including the upcoming Research and Innovation Policy, to support this transition. This policy seeks to commercialise research findings and foster collaboration between universities, industry, and investors, ensuring research benefits factories, farms, businesses, and communities.

Why it mattersBasotho citizens are directly affected by the minister's call to translate research into practical solutions that can improve their lives and create employment.
