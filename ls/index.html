Man Sentenced to 20 Years or M15,000 Fine for Fatal Food Dispute

A Leribe man convicted of culpable homicide must also pay M30,000 compensation to the victim's family after a fatal altercation.

Masupha Katse has been sentenced to 20 years in prison or a M15,000 fine for killing ’Mane Mothatjaneng during a village celebration in August 2019. Acting High Court Judge ’Manapo Motebele also ordered Katse to pay M30,000 compensation to the victim’s family. The fatal assault occurred at Ha Seetsa following a dispute over food served at a community event.

Justice Motebele rejected Katse’s self-defense claim, finding he inflicted the fatal injury during a heat of passion after sudden provocation. The incident began when Katse, arriving from an initiation ceremony, demanded meat and papa, remarking that only dogs ate meat without the staple. A subsequent intervention by Mothatjaneng escalated into violence, with the Crown asserting Katse struck Mothatjaneng on the forehead with a stick.

Although no witnesses saw the fatal blow, prosecutors presented circumstantial evidence and alleged informal admissions. A post-mortem confirmed a severe head injury as the cause of death. The defense argued Katse struck Mothatjaneng on the chest, believing he was reaching for a concealed knife, and highlighted the deceased's violent reputation and a prior serious head injury.

Why it mattersA man was convicted of culpable homicide and ordered to pay a fine and compensation, affecting his livelihood and freedom.

Matekane Denies Links to Crime Boss Matlala

Prime Minister Sam Matekane stated he never met South African crime boss Vusimusi “Cat” Matlala, but confirmed a former aide dealt with him.

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has denied any association with South African crime figure Vusimusi “Cat” Matlala, who claimed to be negotiating VIP protection services in Lesotho. Matekane stated he has never met, known, or conducted business with Matlala, nor authorized any company linked to him for security services. This statement addresses prior allegations of a business partnership between the two.

The Prime Minister’s Office acknowledged that a former member of Matekane’s private security detail engaged with Matlala regarding the establishment of a VIP security company in Lesotho. However, the office insisted these discussions were in the former aide’s private capacity and without the Prime Minister’s knowledge or authorization. Matlala had testified before South Africa’s Madlanga Commission about these negotiations.

Matlala told the commission his company was registered in Lesotho and he was in talks for security services, including presidential protection. He also confirmed working with General Lesetja Senona, a Hawks provincial head, who was interested in the Lesotho venture but was ultimately not hired. The Prime Minister’s clarification aimed to distance himself directly from Matlala’s alleged dealings.

Why it mattersPrime Minister Sam Matekane is affected by allegations of association with a crime boss, which could impact his public image and political standing.

Mofomobe challenges drug case joinder

Basotho National Party leader Machesetsa Mofomobe asked the Maseru Magistrate’s Court to drop him from a M5 billion drug case.

Basotho National Party leader Machesetsa Mofomobe has asked the Maseru Magistrate’s Court to remove him from the M5 billion Khubetsoana drug case. Mofomobe’s lawyer, Advocate Christopher Lephuthing, argued that his client was unlawfully joined as the sixth accused while an application by his five co-accused to quash the charges was still pending. The MP is accused of facilitating premises used by Chinese nationals for a clandestine drug laboratory.

Advocate Lephuthing told Magistrate Lebusa Tšosane that the court lacked jurisdiction to join Mofomobe while the charges' validity was under challenge. He urged the prosecution to withdraw the charges, warning of a formal application if they refused. The prosecution rejected this, citing the law allowing additions at any stage before proceedings commence. Magistrate Tšosane ordered Mofomobe to file a formal challenge by August 31, 2026, to be heard with the co-accused’s pending bid.

The defence argued that prosecutors sought to join Mofomobe while pleadings for the quashing application were being finalized. They described the joinder as an abuse of court process and problematic for synchronizing remand dates. However, Crown prosecutor Advocate Keketso Motiki maintained that the Criminal Procedure and Evidence Act permits adding charges and accused persons before a case proceeds. The magistrate suggested the challenge should have been raised formally earlier.

Why it mattersMachesetsa Mofomobe, a Member of Parliament, is affected by this legal challenge as he seeks to be removed from a drug case where he is accused of facilitating premises for a clandestine drug laboratory.

Prime Minister Demands No Mercy for Drug Lords

Prime Minister Sam Matekane called for a tougher crackdown on drug dealers, citing the M5 billion Khubetsoana drug case.

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has demanded a more aggressive approach to combating drug trafficking, stating that narcotics are increasingly harming Basotho youth and communities. He emphasized that individuals involved in distributing drugs should face severe penalties from the justice system. Matekane's call comes amid the ongoing M5 billion Khubetsoana drug case, which involves three Chinese nationals, their company, a senior government official, and a political party leader charged with operating a clandestine drug-manufacturing facility.

During a visit to Mabote Police Station, Matekane highlighted the pervasive threat of drug use, which affects both young people and adults. He praised the police for dismantling a drug warehouse in Berea, expressing hope for strong court punishments for those responsible. The Prime Minister also questioned the adequacy of the recently approved 500 additional police officers in addressing the escalating drug problem, urging the Commissioner of Police to assess the situation.

The M5 billion case involves charges against Chen Wei, Chen Minglevu, Yang Xiong Kang, their company Stan Dar (Pty) Ltd, Kemong Masupha, and Machesetsa Mofomobe. They face accusations related to drug manufacturing, illegal importation of medicines, and environmental pollution. The accused deny the charges and are contesting them. The police had previously discovered approximately 7.5 tonnes of chemicals suspected for manufacturing crystal methamphetamine, ecstasy, and khat-related substances at a Khubetsoana workshop.

Why it mattersThe Prime Minister's call for a tougher crackdown on drug lords directly impacts the justice system and law enforcement's efforts to combat drug proliferation.

Inspector Mofolo convicted of Constable Khetheng murder

A High Court judge found Senior Inspector Mathibeli Mofolo guilty of intentionally causing the death of Police Constable Mokalekale Khetheng after he was taken into custody.

Senior Inspector Mathibeli Mofolo has been convicted of the murder of Police Constable Mokalekale Khetheng, concluding a nearly decade-long trial. High Court Judge Charles Hungwe ruled on Monday that Mofolo unlawfully and intentionally caused Khetheng’s death after taking him into police custody on March 26, 2016. Mofolo's co-accused, Senior Superintendent Thabo Tšukulu, Inspector Mabitle Matona, and Sub-Inspector Haleokoe Taasoane, were acquitted of murder charges.

While acquitted of murder, SSP Tšukulu was convicted of being an accessory after the fact for his role in an elaborate cover-up of PC Khetheng’s killing. Inspectors Matona and Taasoane were released. PC Khetheng disappeared after being taken into police custody and his body was found the next day in a gorge with signs of violence. Justice Hungwe stated that Khetheng was taken alive into custody, deliberately omitted from records, and found dead the following day.

The court found the circumstantial case against Snr Insp Mofolo overwhelming, noting he took exclusive custody, circumvented detention records, borrowed handcuffs, and received gloves. He failed to explain these actions and provided false, evasive accounts. Justice Hungwe relied on alleged admissions of Khetheng's death and attempts to suppress evidence, stating the case did not depend on excluded interrogation material.

Why it mattersSenior Inspector Mofolo has been convicted of murder, and three other officers faced charges related to the death and cover-up of Police Constable Khetheng.
