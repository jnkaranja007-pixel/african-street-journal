WASCO begins Mafeteng drought relief plan

The water utility will dredge Rasebale Dam and drill boreholes to secure district water supplies.

The Water and Sewage Company (WASCO) has started a drought response plan in Mafeteng, including water tanks, boreholes, and dredging Rasebale Dam. This multi-layered approach aims to prevent water shortages like those in 2015 and 2024.

WASCO Project manager Lehlohonolo Ncheke stated the plan has three phases, beginning with 29 water tanks for immediate relief. The second phase will drill boreholes as an alternative water source, while the third involves dredging Rasebale Dam to increase its storage capacity, a first for the utility.

WASCO CEO Lebohang Peke Makara called the dredging a defining moment for the company's engineering capabilities, noting Rasebale Dam has had water collection problems since 2012. Natural Resources Minister Mohlomi Moleko confirmed the project fulfills a 2024 commitment by Prime Minister Sam Matekane, but traditional leaders warned erosion control is needed in the catchment area.

Why it mattersMafeteng communities will receive emergency water supplies and improved long-term water security.

Lesotho Correctional Service promotions challenged in court

Assistant Superintendent Moji Zakea questions the January promotion of five officers to Senior Superintendent.

Assistant Superintendent Moji Zakea has filed a court application challenging the January promotion of five officers to Senior Superintendent within the Lesotho Correctional Service (LCS). The application names acting Chief of Corrections Matingoe Phamotse and Minister of Law and Justice Richard Ramoeletsi as respondents.

Zakea argues the promotions violated the LCS Promotion Guidelines of 2024, citing a lack of preliminary interviews and the shortlisting process being handled by Phamotse’s office instead of Human Resources. He specifically questions if Senior Superintendent Samson Rasehloho met the degree and disciplinary case experience requirements.

Zakea, who has acted as Superintendent since May 2021, applied for promotion after an internal notice on January 9 advertised vacant positions. His application was unsuccessful, prompting this legal challenge to the process he claims lacked transparency and fairness.

Why it mattersAssistant Superintendent Moji Zakea and other applicants for promotion within the Lesotho Correctional Service are directly affected by a process that may not have followed its own guidelines.

Lesotho police risk allowance dispute continues

Officers are seeking an increase from M100 to M1500 monthly, a proposal facing bureaucratic delays.

A long-running dispute over a proposed M1500 monthly risk allowance for Lesotho Mounted Police Service (LMPS) members highlights officers' difficult work. The proposed increase, which would replace the M100 allowance in place since 1994, has been caught in bureaucratic delays and union pressure.

Police officers routinely confront dangerous situations, including armed criminals and volatile public gatherings, putting their lives at risk. The LMPS members' demand for a higher allowance is seen as a recognition of these occupational hazards, which many public servants do not face.

While acknowledging shortcomings like corruption and incompetence in some cases, the report suggests that improvements in crime reduction, such as fewer vehicle hijackings and drunk driving accidents, deserve police recognition. The government is urged to address the risk allowance issue despite ongoing security challenges like stock theft and drug trafficking.

Why it mattersPolice officers in Lesotho are affected by the protracted administrative process delaying a proposed increase in their monthly risk allowance.

Standard Bank plants 2000 trees in Mafeteng

The bank partnered with the Ministry of Environment and Forestry to boost conservation and biodiversity in Ha-Khojane.

Standard Bank (SLB) and the Ministry of Environment and Forestry planted 2000 trees in Ha-Khojane, Mafeteng District on Thursday. This initiative is part of SLB's commitment of M750,000 over three years to Lesotho's reforestation efforts, which began in 2024.

Makeke Bereng from the Ministry of Environment stated that trees play a vital role in maintaining environmental balance, regulating climatic conditions, and improving air quality. Keketso Makara of SLB added that the planting is part of the bank's Blue Roots programme, aiming to plant one million trees across Africa, building on previous plantings of 54,000 trees in Mohale’s Hoek.

Ha-Khojane headwoman ’Makhojane Khojane believes the trees will help restore the area’s environment, create a habitat for biodiversity, and bring back traditional medicinal plants for future generations.

Why it mattersThe tree planting aims to improve the local environment, potentially reintroducing medicinal plants and attracting wildlife.

Heirs fight over M450,000 Leribe property

Deceased businessman's family claims ownership of commercial plots sold in 2000.

Heirs of businessman Aslam Abubaker are suing Tau Mokhofu in the Leribe Magistrate’s Court over commercial property in Maputsoe allegedly purchased for M450,000 in 2000. The dispute involves two plots, with the heirs seeking transfer of portions they say their father bought. Mr Mokhofu allegedly sold portions of the plots but later transferred one to his son.

Mr Mokhofu, 88, testified that he sold Aslam Abubaker half of Plot No. 147 and half of Plot No. 153, receiving M450,000. However, he only secured a lease for Plot No. 153 in 2015, 15 years later, and transferred it to his son, Morapeli Mokhofu, in May 2016. Regarding Plot No. 147, Mr Mokhofu claimed his signature on a deed of transfer presented by Abubaker was forged.

The case adds to ongoing land disputes involving the Abubaker family, some dating back decades. The heirs are represented by Advocate Motiea Teele KC, while Resident Magistrate Bokang Makhaketso is presiding. Mr Mokhofu had previously sought a High Court order in 2010 to declare a transfer invalid.

Why it mattersThe heirs of a deceased businessman are in court seeking ownership of commercial property, impacting rental businesses and a butchery.
