Lesotho, Angola Chairmanship Bid Deferred to October Summit

Regional leaders could not agree on who should succeed Eswatini as the incoming chair of the SADC Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation, postponing the decision.

Lesotho's bid to secure a leadership role within the Southern African Development Community (SADC) has been postponed, as regional leaders deferred a decision on the incoming chairship of the Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation. The 46th SADC Ordinary Summit in Durban, South Africa, could not resolve the contest between Lesotho and Angola, who both declared interest in the position. The matter will now be addressed at an extraordinary summit scheduled for October 20, 2026.

The Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation is a key SADC institution responsible for steering the bloc's response to regional peace and stability threats. The chair rotates annually on a troika basis, with the incoming chair chosen by consensus. Lesotho, which has not held a SADC troika seat for 12 years due to past political instability, declared its interest in March, while Angola submitted a competing bid in June. Eswatini formally took over the current chairship from Malawi.

Lesotho's Minister of Foreign Affairs and International Relations, Limpho Tau, confirmed that the country formally expressed its interest in March. He stated that the summit of all 16 member states could not reach a consensus, leading to the deferral. This decision comes after Lesotho was removed from the SADC agenda in August 2025, allowing it to pursue leadership positions within SADC structures again.

Why it mattersLesotho's political standing and ability to influence regional security decisions are directly impacted by the unresolved SADC chairship bid.

SA men seek M202,000 return in drug case bail bid

Two South African men charged with cannabis possession and money laundering have applied for bail, demanding the return of M202,000 cash and a seized vehicle.

Nash Pillay and Thabiso Ngidi, facing cannabis possession and money laundering charges, have filed a fresh bail application in the Maseru Magistrate’s Court. Their initial bid was rejected on July 9, 2026, leading to their remand. Pillay, who identifies as a herbal doctor and entrepreneur with a South African company, claims cannabis is a vital medicinal herb in his Muslim tradition. The pair are jointly seeking M1,000 bail each.

The proposed bail conditions include Ngidi reporting to Maseru Central Police Station weekly and surrendering his passport. They also demand the return of M202,000 confiscated from Pillay on July 7, 2026, or that it be held in trust by their lawyers. They also seek the release of a seized motor vehicle. The accused state a seized pill is a dental painkiller, not an illicit drug, and request its return for Ngidi’s urgent medical treatment.

The defense argues the accused are presumed innocent and not flight risks due to their South African ties and lack of prior convictions. They challenge the seizure of cash and vehicle, claiming police lacked reasonable suspicion and that the funds were for legitimate business. The State opposes the application, citing a lack of valid licenses and flight risk concerns, as detailed by investigating officer Detective Police Constable No. 10709 Paki.

Why it mattersTwo South African men are seeking the return of M202,000 and a vehicle seized in a drug case, impacting their business funds and assets.

Refugee Documents Freeze Strands Hundreds in Lesotho

A year-long government freeze on identity and travel documents leaves refugees unable to access essential services, including healthcare and banking.

Hundreds of refugees in Lesotho are effectively trapped, unable to travel or access basic services due to a government-imposed freeze on identity and travel documents. This administrative crisis, now entering its second year, stems from an investigation into alleged corruption in the issuance of refugee certificates. Refugees report being unable to access healthcare, banking services, and professional opportunities, while critically ill patients struggle to get referrals to South Africa.

The suspension of document issuance began in July last year, following allegations of corruption against the former Acting Commissioner for Refugees and the Director of Passports. Both officials were suspended and later charged with corruption and abuse of power. Although investigations by the Directorate on Corruption and Economic Offences have concluded, the case awaits reallocation in the Magistrates Court after a magistrate's retirement.

Despite the completion of investigations, refugees remain without the fundamental documents required for daily life, work, and study. The continued freeze raises questions about compliance with Lesotho's refugee laws and international obligations, which mandate the issuance of identity cards and resident permits to recognised refugees. The situation leaves families stranded and bank accounts frozen, impacting the most vulnerable.

Why it mattersRefugees are unable to access essential services like healthcare and banking, and are unable to travel for treatment or opportunities.

Princess Senate's charity drive raises M1 million

The Shoeshoe ea Moshoeshoe Trust and Foundation aims to support 100 schools by 2028, focusing on youth development and cultural preservation.

Princess Senate Mohato Seeiso's new charitable initiative, the Shoeshoe ea Moshoeshoe Trust and Foundation, has raised over M1 million at its launch event. The foundation has set an ambitious goal to reach 100 schools across Lesotho by 2028, aiming to invest in young people while preserving national heritage. The launch, held on Thursday at AVANI Maseru, was attended by King Letsie III, Prime Minister Sam Matekane, government officials, and development partners.

The foundation's work will be guided by six strategic pillars: youth leadership and entrepreneurship; culture, heritage and identity; mental health and youth well-being; education and social support; community development and environmental stewardship; and responsible youth lifestyle campaigns. By 2028, the foundation intends to directly engage over 20,000 young people, support thousands of learners, and strengthen community development and cultural preservation efforts nationwide.

Significant financial backing was secured at the launch, including a M500,000 donation from the Sam Matekane Foundation, presented by Prime Minister Matekane. Pheello Mochaba, speaking for the foundation, described the initiative as a national movement inspired by Lesotho's founding father, Morena Moshoeshoe I, and the nation's bicentennial celebrations. The foundation seeks partnerships from government, the private sector, development partners, civil society, and citizens to achieve its goals.

Why it mattersThe initiative's fundraising and stated goals for school and youth engagement directly impact educational opportunities and community development within Lesotho.

Young Entrepreneurs Drive Lesotho's Economic Future

With formal employment lagging, Basotho youth are increasingly vital for creating jobs and unlocking untapped economic potential.

Entrepreneurship has become a crucial pillar for Lesotho's economic future as formal employment fails to keep pace with the aspirations of young people. Economic uncertainty, rising living costs, and limited job opportunities necessitate a new generation of business owners to drive prosperity. The nation's future prosperity hinges on empowering young Basotho to create jobs, not just fill them.

Governments and large businesses cannot absorb the growing labor market, making a thriving private sector essential for sustainable growth. This shift presents an opportunity for small businesses to act as engines of innovation and job creation. Every successful enterprise contributes to economic activity by generating income, creating employment, and supporting local communities.

Identifying problems and finding practical solutions, rather than relying on capital or connections, defines entrepreneurship. Opportunities exist across various sectors, including agriculture, tourism, and renewable energy, addressing unmet community needs. Young entrepreneurs possess the energy, creativity, and ambition to transform local challenges into economic opportunities, unlocking untapped value.

Why it mattersYoung Basotho entrepreneurs are essential for addressing unemployment and driving economic growth by creating jobs and innovative solutions.
