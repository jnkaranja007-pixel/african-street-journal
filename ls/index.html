Makhutle seeks bail on third attempt

A man accused of murder has filed an urgent review application challenging a High Court ruling that denied him bail.

Hopolang Mokhopi FOLIKE Makhutle, accused of murdering Lesotho Nurses Association Secretary-General Morephe Santi, is seeking release on bail for a third time. He has filed an urgent review application after the Court of Appeal declined jurisdiction over his previous appeal. Makhutle argues that the High Court judge failed to consider exceptional circumstances and relied on hearsay evidence when denying his bail.

Makhutle contends that his continued detention threatens his construction business, potentially leading to lost contracts, lawsuits, and distress for his family and employees. He denies posing a risk to witnesses and is prepared to comply with stringent bail conditions, stating that the charge does not involve premeditation. He cites previous court decisions to support his arguments.

The accused is challenging the High Court's finding that his release would not be in the interests of justice due to fears of witness intimidation. Makhutle argues there was no evidence presented to support this allegation. He maintains he has demonstrated exceptional circumstances warranting his release and denies any intention to interfere with witnesses or obstruct investigations.

Why it mattersHopolang Mokhopi FOLIKE Makhutle's business and family are affected by his continued incarceration and inability to meet contractual obligations.

Leribe man to serve one year for culpable homicide

A High Court in Lesotho sentenced a man to one year in prison for the 2020 fatal shooting of a livestock herder, ordering him to compensate the victim's family.

Kabelo Masoabi KOAKOA Sefale has been sentenced to one year in prison for the culpable homicide of livestock herder Fihlang Nkopane. The High Court at Tšifa-li-Mali handed down a 15-year sentence, with 14 years suspended, for the 2020 killing. Sefale must also pay Nkopane's family M104,000 within a year of his release and provide two cows within one month.

Nkopane, 24, was shot dead at a cattle post in Ha Nkopa, Leribe, on Christmas Day in 2020. The Crown argued that the use of a firearm and the nature of the two wounds to the deceased's back demonstrated a serious disregard for human life and a high degree of cruelty. The defence highlighted Sefale's youth, his role as a sole breadwinner, and his guilty plea as mitigating factors.

The court considered that Sefale was 22 at the time of the offence and is the sole provider for his unemployed mother. His defence lawyer argued that his guilty plea showed remorse and that he was willing to pay compensation, offering 10 cattle. The court ordered monthly payments of M8,600 towards the M104,000 compensation after Sefale's release.

Why it mattersA Leribe man will serve one year in prison and pay compensation for the fatal shooting of a livestock herder, impacting his family and the victim's family.

FNB Lesotho launches funeral cover plan

The new FNB Funeral Plan offers up to M100,000 in cover for as many as 21 family members under a single policy.

FNB Lesotho has introduced a new funeral plan in partnership with LNIG Hollard, providing up to M100,000 in cover for up to 21 family members. The product, launched in Maseru, aims to offer financial assistance during times of bereavement, with premiums starting at M1.63 per day. FNB Lesotho Chief Executive Officer Delekazi Mokebe highlighted the importance of partnerships in developing solutions that meet customer needs and support Basotho families.

According to FNB Lesotho Insurance Manager Gloria Senatsi, the plan reflects the bank's dedication to creating products that adapt to evolving customer requirements. She stated that the FNB Funeral Plan, an extension of the bank's 'Help Changes Everything' philosophy, includes inclusive features designed for the current market. FNB Lesotho Retail Banking Executive Nthabeleng Khoali added that the plan helps customers manage significant life events with greater confidence.

LNIG Hollard Chief Executive Officer Mpho Vumbukani emphasized that collaborations between financial institutions and insurers can broaden service accessibility. He noted that FNB's existing customer base and distribution networks would allow LNIG Hollard to reach more clients through familiar channels. Vumbukani also stressed that such partnerships should prioritize delivering tangible benefits to customers and stakeholders beyond commercial interests.

Why it mattersBasotho families are affected by the new funeral plan which provides financial protection during bereavement.

Lesotho seeks compensation from South Africa for lost territories

Prime Minister Matekane raised historical land grievances and compensation with President Ramaphosa, exploring access to the sea and employment opportunities.

The Matekane administration has initiated discussions with South Africa regarding compensation for territories lost during the nineteenth century. Prime Minister Sam Matekane reportedly presented the issue to South African President Cyril Ramaphosa during a bilateral meeting on August 16, 2026. This move marks a determined effort to address a long-standing historical grievance that has impacted Lesotho's economic prospects and development resources.

While reclaiming the land itself faces significant diplomatic and legal challenges, including the African Union's stance on inherited borders, the administration is exploring alternative forms of redress. Proposals include seeking access to the sea, which would enhance Lesotho's trade capabilities as a landlocked nation heavily reliant on South Africa for port access and international markets. Discussions also encompass improving employment opportunities for Basotho citizens in South Africa through better work permit arrangements and structured labor agreements.

These practical discussions aim to provide tangible economic benefits and address immediate concerns like unemployment among Basotho. A stronger economic relationship between the two nations, with a more prosperous Lesotho, could also foster increased trade and investment opportunities for South African businesses, suggesting mutual benefits from resolving these historical and economic issues.

Why it mattersBasotho citizens are affected by the potential for improved access to the sea for trade and increased employment opportunities in South Africa.

Khubetsoana hardware donates food and medical aid

More than 100 households received food hampers and free medical services from Khubetsoana Building World in its second annual corporate social responsibility initiative.

Khubetsoana Building World (KBW) provided food hampers and free medical services to over 100 households along the Maseru-Berea border. The initiative, now in its second year, involved a Chinese medical team from Maseru District Hospital offering basic healthcare to vulnerable residents identified with local chiefs. Communities including Naleli, Ha Mabote, Sekamaneng, Khubetsoana, and Koalabata benefited from the program.

KBW Assistant General Manager Motloli Mothepu stated the initiative is an expression of gratitude to communities that have supported the business. "We want to communicate that we value their livelihoods and well-being," Mothepu said, emphasizing the company's integration within these communities. The medical team conducted health checks, administered medication, and referred patients needing further treatment to medical facilities. Food hampers contained essential supplies like maize meal, bread flour, and cooking oil.

Local chiefs and headmen assisted in selecting beneficiaries, prioritizing vulnerable households and individuals with disabilities. Sekamaneng headman Tsooang Molata confirmed that recipients included about 100 representatives from households in Naleli, Khubetsoana, Sekamaneng, Koalabata, and Ha Mabote. Molata noted that individuals receiving old-age pensions were excluded to ensure aid reached those most in need, despite some feeling unfairly left out.

Why it mattersVulnerable households in Lesotho received essential food supplies and basic medical check-ups, improving their immediate well-being.
