News · 2026-09-03

Taxi fire kills community leader, sparks safety probe

A woman died from burns sustained in a taxi fire, prompting calls for an investigation into public transport safety standards.

Hundreds gathered to mourn Malefu Sephula, 45, who died on August 14, 2026, two weeks after a taxi she boarded caught fire. The blaze erupted inside the vehicle minutes after she boarded, trapping passengers as flames spread. Sephula suffered fatal burns and died in hospital. Two other passengers, including a child, were hospitalized but survived.

Eyewitness accounts and a widely shared social media video of the incident have raised questions about bystander conduct and the taxi's condition. A family member thanked one individual who attempted to fight the flames but criticized another for filming the event instead of assisting. A local councillor questioned whether the taxi carried a fire extinguisher.

The Minister of Sports, speaking for the government, called Sephula's death a devastating loss and stated the government's responsibility for public transport safety. He called for a formal investigation into the taxi's condition and safety compliance. Allegations that the owner, reportedly driving the vehicle, added petrol directly to the engine before departure could not be independently verified.

Why it mattersThe death of a community member in a taxi fire prompts an investigation into public transport safety, affecting all who use these services.

Econet, GEM Institute Plant 500 Fruit Trees at Lesotho Schools

The 2026 ACT 4 Climate Change Fruit-Up School Drive aims to educate over 3,000 learners in practical climate action.

Telecommunications firm Econet Telecom Lesotho, in partnership with GEM Institute and UNESCO, launched the 2026 ACT 4 Climate Change Fruit-Up School Drive, planting over 500 fruit trees at 14 schools across six districts. The initiative, which began Friday at Qhomane LECSA High School, is part of a five-year collaboration to promote climate action among young Basotho. Learners will participate in tree planting and care as part of environmental education and skills development.

Econet's Public Relations Specialist, Retšelisitsoe Nkhahle, stated that the company's role extends beyond connectivity to meaningful community contribution. The Fruit-Up program moves climate education from theory to practice, fostering greener school environments and providing long-term resources. Since 2021, the partnership has planted 3,397 fruit trees at 54 schools, involving over 3,000 learners.

GEM Institute's Tšepang Khau highlighted the program's focus on environmental and entrepreneurial skills, encouraging recognition of indigenous plants' potential. UNESCO representative Mankopane Nkhono reaffirmed support for these initiatives, noting their success in building environmental awareness and encouraging youth participation in addressing climate change. Qhomane LECSA High School received 50 apple and peach trees.

Why it mattersYoung Basotho learners are directly involved in practical climate action and environmental education through tree planting and care initiatives.

Politics · 2026-09-03

Rights Group Sues Judiciary Over Combined Sheriff Powers

Section 2 argues the merging of Registrar and Sheriff roles within the Judiciary violates separation of powers and risks public abuse.

Human rights organization Section 2 has filed a Constitutional Court challenge against Lesotho's Judiciary and other state bodies, aiming to dismantle a system that consolidates the powers of Registrar, Sheriff, and Deputy Sheriff.

The group's president, Kananelo Boloetse, contends in a founding affidavit that this "three-in-one" system infringes upon the doctrine of separation of powers and judicial independence. He argues that combining these roles places executive functions within the Judiciary, potentially exposing vulnerable individuals facing evictions or property seizures to constitutional rights violations.

At the core of the legal challenge are the definitions and functions of the Registrar as outlined in the Administration of Justice Act of 2011 and High Court Civil Litigation Rules of 2024. Section 2 asserts that the Registrar, defined as a judicial officer and chief administrator, should not also assume the duties of the Sheriff, which involves enforcing the law.

Why it mattersThe lawsuit by Section 2 could affect Basotho citizens by potentially leading to abuses of power during property enforcement actions if the current system is maintained.
