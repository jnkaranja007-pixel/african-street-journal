Lesotho cricket team loses final match in Abuja

The national side finished fourth in the Africa Cricket Association regional qualifying tournament after a three-run defeat to Gambia.

Lesotho's campaign in the Africa Cricket Association regional qualifying tournament in Abuja ended with a three-run defeat by Gambia. The national side was bowled out for 109 in 19.5 overs while chasing Gambia's 112 for nine. This result left Lesotho fourth in the final standings with one win from five matches.

Nigeria and Uganda secured the two places in the Africa Regional Final, according to the Lesotho Cricket Association (LCA). Coach Dzikamai Mavhiko cited a lack of a dedicated cricket ground and competing against full-time players as team limits. He said sustained investment in facilities and player development is needed.

The LCA said the team showed promise during the campaign. The team will need to build on the experience as it prepares for future international fixtures.

WASCO begins Mafeteng drought relief plan

The water company will supply tanks, drill boreholes, and dredge Rasebale Dam to secure water.

The Water and Sewage Company (WASCO) has started a drought response plan in Mafeteng, Lesotho, to address water shortages. The plan includes delivering 29 water tanks for immediate relief and drilling boreholes as a secondary water source. The company will also dredge Rasebale Dam to increase its storage capacity, a project described as a first for WASCO.

WASCO Project manager Lehlohonolo Ncheke stated the intervention aims to prevent a repeat of severe shortages seen in 2015 and 2024. WASCO CEO Lebohang Peke Makara called the dam dredging a defining moment for the company's engineering capabilities, noting Rasebale Dam has had collection problems since 2012. He also highlighted that delays have increased costs.

Natural Resources Minister Mohlomi Moleko said the dredging fulfills a 2024 commitment by Prime Minister Sam Matekane. Traditional leaders, however, warned that dredging alone is insufficient without addressing erosion in the dam's catchment area. Chief Fako Leshoro Seiso stated sand will continue to accumulate if underlying causes are not fixed.

Why it mattersMafeteng residents will receive emergency water supplies and improved long-term water security through new infrastructure.

Lesotho Correctional Service promotions challenged in court

Assistant Superintendent Moji Zakea questions the legality of five promotions made in January.

Assistant Superintendent Moji Zakea has filed a court application challenging the January promotions of five officers to Senior Superintendent within the Lesotho Correctional Service (LCS). The application names acting Chief of Corrections Matingoe Phamotse and Minister of Law and Justice Richard Ramoeletsi as respondents.

Zakea argues the promotions violated the LCS Promotion Guidelines of 2024, citing the absence of preliminary interviews and the shortlisting process being handled by Phamotse's office instead of Human Resources. He specifically questions if Senior Superintendent Samson Rasehloho met the required bachelor’s degree and disciplinary case experience.

This legal challenge comes as a separate case questioning earlier LCS promotions, filed in August, remains unresolved in the High Court. Zakea, who has served since 2007 and acted as Superintendent since May 2021, applied for promotion after Internal Circular Notice No. 1 of 2026 was issued on January 9.

Why it mattersLesotho citizens employed by the Correctional Service are directly affected by the fairness and legality of promotion processes.

Politics · 2026-11-02

Lesotho police risk allowance dispute continues

Officers have waited since 1994 for an increase from M100 to M1500 monthly.

Members of the Lesotho Mounted Police Service (LMPS) are awaiting a proposed M1500 monthly risk allowance, a dispute that has faced bureaucratic delays. The current allowance of M100 has been in place since 1994, failing to reflect the dangers officers face daily.

Police officers routinely confront armed criminals, dangerous traffic, and volatile situations, often at personal risk. The proposed increase aims to better recognise the occupational hazards inherent in their unpredictable work environments.

While crime statistics are not provided, public perception suggests a decline in vehicle hijackings and drunk driving incidents. The LMPS faces ongoing challenges with organised crime, illegal firearms, and drug trafficking, but the risk allowance debate continues.

Why it mattersPolice officers in Lesotho are affected by the protracted administrative process delaying their risk allowance increase.

Climate · 2026-11-02

Standard Bank plants 2000 trees in Mafeteng

The bank's Blue Roots programme aims to plant one million trees across Africa, with 54,000 planted previously in Mohale's Hoek.

Standard Bank (SLB) and the Ministry of Environment and Forestry planted 2000 trees in Ha-Khojane, Mafeteng District on Thursday. This initiative is part of SLB's commitment of M750,000 over three years to Lesotho's reforestation efforts, which began in 2024.

Ministry representative Makeke Bereng stated that trees help maintain environmental balance, regulate climate, and improve air quality. SLB's Head of Business and Commercial Banking, Keketso Makara, added that the planting is part of the bank's Blue Roots programme, which seeks to plant one million trees continent-wide.

Ha-Khojane headwoman ’Makhojane Khojane expressed hope that the trees would restore the environment, create habitats for biodiversity, and bring back traditional medicinal plants. SLB previously planted 54,000 trees in Mohale's Hoek as part of the same programme.

Why it mattersThe tree planting initiative aims to improve environmental conservation and biodiversity in Mafeteng.
