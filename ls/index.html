Makhutle Pursues Third Bail Bid Over Business Concerns

An accused man argues his continued detention threatens his construction business and risks lawsuits.

Hopolang Mokhopi FOLIKE Makhutle, accused of murdering Lesotho Nurses Association Secretary-General Morephe Santi, is seeking bail for a third time. He has filed an urgent review application challenging a High Court ruling that denied him release. Makhutle argues that his ongoing incarceration is jeopardizing his construction business, potentially leading to lost contracts, lawsuits, and distress for his family and employees.

Makhutle contends that the High Court judge failed to properly consider exceptional circumstances for his release, relying on hearsay evidence and wrongly concluding he posed a risk to witnesses. He asserts that no evidence was presented to support claims of witness intimidation. Makhutle maintains he would not interfere with investigations and is prepared to face trial under strict bail conditions.

This latest application follows a Court of Appeal decision to decline jurisdiction over a previous bail appeal. The court agreed that Makhutle should have sought a review of the High Court decision rather than a direct appeal. Makhutle's legal team cites prior cases to argue that fears of witness interference must be supported by specific evidence linking the accused to the alleged risk.

Why it mattersMakhutle's continued detention is presented as a threat to his construction business, potentially causing financial distress and legal issues.

Leribe man sentenced to one year for fatal shooting

Kabelo Masoabi Sefale will serve one year of a 15-year sentence for culpable homicide and must compensate the victim's family.

A Leribe man, Kabelo Masoabi Sefale, has been sentenced to one year in prison for the culpable homicide of livestock herder Fihlang Nkopane. The High Court ordered Sefale to pay Nkopane's family M104,000 within one year of his release, in monthly installments of M8,600, and to provide two cows within one month. Nkopane, 24, was fatally shot on Christmas Day in 2020 at a cattle post in Ha Nkopa, Leribe.

The Crown prosecutor highlighted the use of a firearm and the nature of the injuries, noting two open wounds to the back, as aggravating factors that demonstrated a disregard for human life. The prosecution argued that the victim's young age and the lasting grief caused to his family warranted a significant sentence to deter similar acts and uphold public safety.

In mitigation, the defense emphasized Sefale's status as a first offender, his youth at 22 when the offense occurred, and his role as the sole breadwinner for his unemployed mother. The defense also pointed to Sefale's guilty plea as a sign of remorse and his willingness to pay compensation, including ten head of cattle.

Why it mattersA Leribe man will serve one year in prison and pay significant compensation for a fatal shooting, impacting his family and the victim's relatives.

Lesotho Seeks Compensation From South Africa For Lost Territories

Prime Minister Matekane has raised the issue of historical land loss and compensation with President Ramaphosa, exploring access to the sea and employment opportunities.

Lesotho's administration is pursuing compensation from South Africa for territories lost during the nineteenth century, a move previously unaddressed by prior governments. Prime Minister Sam Matekane reportedly discussed the issue with South African President Cyril Ramaphosa during a bilateral meeting on August 16, 2026. The loss of fertile western lands has been a significant historical grievance, impacting Lesotho's economic prospects and development resources.

While reclaiming land faces diplomatic and legal challenges, including the African Union's stance on inherited borders, the administration is exploring alternative forms of redress. Access to the sea is being considered as a potential compensation, which could reduce trade barriers and improve the movement of goods for the landlocked nation. Discussions also encompass enhancing employment opportunities for Basotho citizens in South Africa through improved work permit arrangements and structured labor agreements.

These practical discussions aim to provide tangible economic benefits for Basotho, addressing unemployment concerns while the complex issue of historical compensation is navigated. A stronger economic relationship between the two nations could foster increased trade and investment, potentially benefiting South African businesses as well.

Why it mattersLesotho's pursuit of compensation and improved trade access directly impacts its economic development and the livelihoods of its citizens.

FNB Lesotho Launches Funeral Plan with M100,000 Cover

The new FNB Funeral Plan, developed with LNIG Hollard, offers up to M100,000 in coverage for 21 family members, with premiums starting at M1.63 daily.

FNB Lesotho has introduced a new funeral insurance product designed to support families during times of bereavement. The FNB Funeral Plan, a collaboration with LNIG Hollard, provides coverage of up to M100,000 for a maximum of 21 family members under a single policy. Premiums for this plan begin at M1.63 per day, making it accessible to a wide range of customers.

The bank stated that the product aims to offer practical support and financial protection, reflecting FNB Lesotho’s commitment to developing solutions that meet evolving customer needs. FNB Lesotho Chief Executive Officer Delekazi Mokebe highlighted the importance of partnerships in creating customer-centric offerings, while Insurance Manager Gloria Senatsi emphasized the plan's inclusive features. Retail Banking Executive Nthabeleng Khoali added that the plan is an answer to the bank's ongoing question of how it can help customers navigate significant life events.

LNIG Hollard Chief Executive Officer Mpho Vumbukani underscored the value of collaboration between financial institutions and insurance providers, noting that FNB's extensive customer base and distribution networks will help extend services to more people. He stressed that such partnerships should extend beyond commercial interests to deliver tangible benefits to customers and stakeholders, focusing on insurance solutions that positively impact the well-being of those they serve.

Why it mattersThe FNB Funeral Plan offers financial protection to Basotho families during bereavement, with coverage for up to 21 members.

Khubetsoana hardware donates food, medical aid

More than 100 households received food hampers and free medical services from Khubetsoana Building World's second annual corporate social responsibility initiative.

Khubetsoana Building World (KBW) provided food hampers and free medical services to over 100 households in communities along the Maseru-Berea border. The initiative, now in its second year, involved a Chinese medical team from Maseru District Hospital offering basic healthcare checks. Beneficiaries were identified in collaboration with local chiefs and headmen from areas including Naleli, Ha Mabote, Sekamaneng, Khubetsoana, and Koalabata.

KBW Assistant General Manager Motloli Mothepu stated the initiative is an expression of appreciation for community support over the years, emphasizing the company's value for local livelihoods and well-being. The food hampers contained essential supplies such as 25kg bags of maize meal, 12.5kg bags of bread flour, and cooking oil. Patients needing further treatment were referred to medical facilities.

Sekamaneng headman Tsooang Molata, representing Area Chief Bereng Majara, conveyed gratitude and urged those not selected not to lose hope. He explained that the selection process prioritized the most vulnerable households, excluding individuals receiving old-age pensions to ensure aid reached those most in need. KBW plans to continue the initiative in the coming years.

Why it mattersOver 100 households received essential food supplies and basic medical services.
