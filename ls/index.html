Lesotho and Angola vie for SADC security chair, deferring decision to October

Regional leaders at the 46th SADC Ordinary Summit in Durban could not agree on whether Lesotho or Angola should be the incoming chair of the Organ on Politics, Defence.

Lesotho's bid to secure a leadership position within the Southern African Development Community (SADC) has been postponed, as regional leaders failed to reach a consensus on the incoming chair of the Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation. The decision, which also saw Eswatini formally take over the current chairship, has been deferred to an extraordinary summit scheduled for October 20, 2026.

The impasse arose after Lesotho declared its interest in the incoming chair position in March, followed by a competing bid from Angola in June. This contest remained unresolved at the summit held on August 17, 2026. The Organ is crucial for steering the 16-member bloc's response to regional peace and security threats, with its chair rotating annually among an outgoing, current, and incoming chairperson.

Lesotho has not held a seat on any SADC troika structure for the past 12 years due to recurring political instability, having been under the bloc's observation. Foreign Affairs and International Relations Minister Limpho Tau confirmed Lesotho's formal expression of interest through a verbal note to the SADC Secretariat in March, aiming to succeed Eswatini as incoming chair.

Why it mattersThe deferral of the SADC Organ chair decision directly affects Lesotho's political standing and its ability to influence regional security matters after a decade of being sidelined.

South African Duo Renews Bail Bid, Seeks Return of Seized Assets

Two South African men, facing charges of cannabis possession and money laundering, have filed a new bail application in Maseru Magistrate’s Court, also demanding the return.

Nash Pillay and Thabiso Ngidi submitted their fresh bail application this past week, after their initial request was denied on July nine, 2026. They are jointly seeking bail of M1,000 each and have proposed Palo Ngoae and Nataline Pillay, both based in Lesotho, as sureties.

Pillay, identifying himself as a certified herbal doctor and entrepreneur, claims the M202,000 was legitimate business funds for commercial purposes in Lesotho, and the seized vehicle was a mode of transport. He also states cannabis is an important medicinal herb in his Muslim tradition. The defense challenges the seizure, arguing police lacked reasonable suspicion.

The accused also want a seized pill, which they claim is a dental painkiller, returned for Ngidi's urgent medical treatment or verification by a pharmacist. Ngidi seeks permission to consult a dentist at his own expense. The State opposes the application, citing a lack of valid licenses and flight risk concerns, according to Detective Police Constable Paki.

Why it mattersThe accused face continued detention and loss of significant assets, impacting their personal freedom and financial standing.

Princess Senate's charity raises M1 million for 100 schools

The Shoeshoe ea Moshoeshoe Trust and Foundation, launched by Her Royal Highness Princess Senate Mohato Seeiso, secured over M1 million to support education and youth development.

Princess Senate Mohato Seeiso's new charity, Shoeshoe ea Moshoeshoe Trust and Foundation, raised more than M1 million at its launch event at AVANI Maseru. The foundation aims to reach at least one hundred schools across Lesotho by 2028, directly engaging more than twenty thousand young people and supporting thousands of learners.

Pheello Mochaba, speaking for the foundation, stated that the initiative is a national movement inspired by the spirit of Lesotho’s bicentennial celebrations. The foundation focuses on six strategic pillars, including youth leadership, education and social support, and mental health and youth well-being, over the next three years.

The launch event, attended by King Letsie III and Prime Minister Sam Matekane, received significant financial backing, including a M500,000 donation from the Sam Matekane Foundation. The charity seeks partnerships from government, the private sector, development partners, civil society, and citizens to achieve its goals.

Why it mattersThis event directly impacts young people and learners in Lesotho by providing financial support and educational initiatives through the new foundation.

Lesotho government freezes refugee documents, stranding hundreds

A government-imposed freeze on identity cards and travel documents for refugees has entered its second year, leaving hundreds unable to access essential services.

Hundreds of refugees in Lesotho are unable to access banking, healthcare, education, and professional opportunities due to a prolonged government freeze on issuing identity cards and travel documents. The suspension, initiated in July last year, followed corruption allegations against then Acting Commissioner for Refugees, Nthatisi Thabane, and Director of Passports, Mpiko Rafono.

The Directorate on Corruption and Economic Offences (DCEO) spokesperson, Matlhokomelo Senoko, stated yesterday that investigations are complete and the case awaits reallocation in the Magistrates Court. This reallocation is necessary because Magistrate Peter Murenzi, who previously handled the case, retired in February this year. Despite the conclusion of investigations, the freeze on documents continues.

Refugees report that families are stranded, bank accounts frozen, and critically ill patients cannot obtain referrals for specialized treatment in South Africa. Children have also missed opportunities to study outside Lesotho. The ongoing suspension raises questions about its compatibility with Lesotho’s Refugees Act, 1983, and its international obligations under the 1951 Convention and 1967 Protocol relating to the Status of Refugees.

Why it mattersHundreds of refugees are directly affected by the inability to access basic services like healthcare, banking, and education due to the government's freeze on their identity and travel documents.

Business · 2026-08-27

Basotho youth shift focus to entrepreneurship as job market shrinks

Lesotho's economic future increasingly relies on young entrepreneurs creating jobs, as formal employment opportunities fail to keep pace with the growing number of young people.

The traditional path of education leading to employment is changing in Lesotho, with entrepreneurship now seen as an essential pillar for the nation's economic future, according to Moeketsi Motšosi. This shift comes as economic uncertainty, rising living costs, and limited job opportunities affect families across the country.

Motšosi highlights that governments and large businesses cannot absorb the increasing number of young people seeking employment. Sustainable economic growth requires a thriving private sector supported by innovative entrepreneurs, who can transform local challenges into economic opportunities in sectors like agriculture, tourism, and manufacturing.

Young Basotho entrepreneurs are crucial for unlocking untapped economic value, bringing energy, creativity, and a willingness to embrace change. They are often the first to adopt new technologies and explore emerging industries, contributing to job creation and economic activity across communities.

Why it mattersThis story matters to young Basotho as it directly addresses the changing economic landscape and highlights entrepreneurship as a viable path for creating their own employment and contributing to the national economy.
