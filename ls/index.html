Victims sue police chief for M825 000

Two men allege abduction, torture and broken bones by officers during investigations.

Two men are suing Police Chief Advocate Borotho Matsoso for M825 000 in damages, claiming police officers subjected them to severe torture. Nthoes??? Mohapi and Sechaba Senoko allege abduction, unlawful detention, and brutal abuse including suffocation and broken bones. The lawsuits filed in the High Court accuse officers of violating their constitutional rights.

Mr Mohapi, seeking M450 000, alleges he was tortured after reporting to Roma Police Station on September 18, 2025, regarding a robbery. He claims officers Rathulo Mojaki, Maoela, and Pokothoane beat him with a rubber tube and a steel bar, breaking both his arms. Mr Senoko, 67, is also seeking damages for alleged torture.

The lawsuits name Advocate Matsoso, Attorney General Advocate Rapelang Motsieloa, and the involved police officers and stations. Both men detail horrific physical abuse in their court papers, seeking compensation for the alleged violations.

Why it mattersTwo Basotho men are seeking compensation after alleging severe physical abuse and torture by police officers.

Lesotho earmarks M50 million for unfinished royal palace

The project, started in 2011, has seen its cost balloon to M461 million from an initial M136.7 million.

The construction of Lesotho's new Royal Palace in Maseru has been allocated an additional M50 million to resume work. This brings the total projected cost for the palace to M461 million, a significant increase from its original M136.7 million budget. Construction began in 2011 and was initially scheduled for completion in May 2013.

An audit report from May 2025, reviewed by the Senate Committee on Government Assurances, revealed overpayments of more than M74 million to consultants and the contractor. Payments were reportedly made without sufficient contractual justification or proof of work completed. The ministry also lacked documentation for the appointment of Nyolohelo Mohale as Clerk of Works/Principal Agent, despite M5.49 million in payments.

The government stated that the M50 million allocation must be properly accounted for with transparent procurement procedures and clearly defined contractual obligations for all parties. Their Majesties King Letsie III and Queen ’Masenate Mohato Seeiso have been waiting for an official residence for over 15 years.

Why it mattersLesotho taxpayers face a nearly half-billion maloti bill for a royal residence that has been under construction for over 15 years due to administrative failures.

Lesotho plans mini off-road motorcycle race

The Lesotho Off Road Association will host a Mini-Roof of Africa event on November 21.

The Lesotho Off Road Association (LoRA) is organising a Mini-Roof of Africa event on November 21. This race will take place at Ha Molengoane, near Maseru, offering riders a chance to compete on challenging off-road terrain. The event aims to provide an opportunity for riders who missed the main Roof of Africa race held in September.

LoRA public relations officer Lehlohonolo Taela stated that the association received requests from riders and enthusiasts who wanted an event on the traditional late-year dates. "Now that we no longer have the Roof of Africa on our traditional dates, we opted for a Mini-Roof to give riders and spectators something on the dates when we used to have the Roof of Africa," Taela said. The Mini-Roof will likely feature the Bronze and Iron race categories, omitting the more technical Silver and Gold classes.

The main Roof of Africa race, now part of the Hard Enduro World Championship series, had its dates shifted from its traditional late November or early December slot. This change meant some riders, particularly from South Africa, could not participate due to school and work commitments. LoRA decided to organise the Mini-Roof to cater to these riders and enthusiasts.

Why it mattersMotorcycle riders and enthusiasts in Lesotho will have another opportunity to compete in an off-road event.

Lesotho politician faces treason charge

Dr Tšepo Lipholo, a politician, reverend, and doctor, is accused of attempting to establish himself as King of Basutoland.

Dr Tšepo Lipholo, who is also a reverend and national doctor, has allegedly sought to crown himself King of Basutoland. This move has led to claims that young men were recruited for military training on farms in South Africa.

South African intelligence and police structures reportedly found no evidence to support these recruitment claims. The Lesotho Mounted Police Service (LMPS) is involved, and a treason case related to Dr Lipholo's alleged actions is currently before the courts.

Dr Lipholo originates from Qoaling, a neighbourhood known for high crime rates, including housebreaking and gender-based violence. The LMPS established a police post in Qoaling in 2020 due to the concerning crime situation.

Why it mattersDr Tšepo Lipholo's alleged attempt to claim kingship and the subsequent treason case directly impact Lesotho's political stability and legal proceedings.

Business · 2026-11-09

Maluti Lager hosts MPs in Maseru

Brewery aims for lawmakers to understand product's economic contribution and local sourcing challenges.

Maluti Mountain Brewery (MMB) hosted Members of Parliament (MPs) on Friday at its Maseru premises for an event focused on its flagship Maluti Premium Lager. The MPs learned about the beer's heritage and drinking rituals as part of MMB’s Brew Across the World programme.

MMB Managing Director ’Mapeete Ntjana stated the initiative aimed to help MPs understand the product's potential contribution to economic growth and employment. Qeme Constituency MP Sello Hakane thanked MMB for the insightful experience but noted concerns about imported ingredients for locally manufactured products.

Mr Hakane urged that products made in Lesotho should use entirely local ingredients, while also applauding MMB's economic and employment contributions. MMB also highlighted its commitment to responsible trading through partnerships with the Lesotho Mounted Police Service to combat drunk driving.

Why it mattersMembers of Parliament were hosted by Maluti Mountain Brewery to understand its product and its economic impact on Lesotho.
