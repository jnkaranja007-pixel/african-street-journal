Durban court views CCTV of alleged gunmen before AKA, Tibz killings

Footage from 17 locations shows two men approaching Wish restaurant before the February 2023 shooting.

The Durban High Court has viewed CCTV footage showing two men approaching Wish restaurant shortly before rapper Kiernan “AKA” Forbes and his friend Tebello “Tibz” Motsoane were shot dead in February 2023. The footage, presented in the trial of seven men accused of the killings, shows the pair crossing the road as Forbes and his entourage leave the restaurant. One man appears to open fire while the other appears to cover the group with a firearm, according to SABC News.

The two then run towards Gordon Road, with surveillance recordings forming part of the State’s effort to reconstruct movements surrounding the shooting on Durban’s Florida Road. Investigators gathered footage from at least 17 locations, including King Shaka International Airport and the hotel where Forbes stayed. State witness Constable Rajen Sanders is taking the court through the recordings, which are being used to trace people and vehicles allegedly connected to the case.

All seven accused have pleaded not guilty, with the defence challenging aspects of the identification evidence. The CCTV material is evidence being considered by the court.

Why it mattersLesotho readers are interested in the progress of the trial and evidence presented in the high-profile killings of South African artists.

Lesotho elected chair of SADC security organ

Prime Minister Sam Matekane took the helm of the regional body's Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation.

Lesotho has been appointed the incoming chair of the Southern African Development Community (SADC) Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation. Prime Minister Sam Matekane was elected to the position during a virtual summit yesterday, chaired by South African President Cyril Ramaphosa. This appointment follows a period where Lesotho was previously on the SADC agenda until 2025.

Foreign Affairs Minister Limpho Tau stated the appointment reflects regional confidence in Lesotho's political stability. Mr Matekane will serve on the Organ Troika alongside the current chairperson from Eswatini and the outgoing chairperson from Malawi. President Ramaphosa urged continued regional cooperation for peace and development.

The summit addressed ongoing instability in the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo and supported dialogue in Madagascar. Efforts to combat terrorism in Mozambique were also recognized. Minister Tau, speaking from Germany, highlighted this role as an honour for Basotho, demonstrating trust in the country's ability to help resolve regional security challenges.

Why it mattersLesotho's government will now lead regional efforts on politics, defence, and security cooperation.

two men sentenced for Lesotho border murders

Basotho nationals received life sentences in South Africa for a 2024 double murder at Maseru Bridge.

Two Basotho men, Tumelo Tsiu, 32, and Lineo Walatsa, 30, were sentenced to life imprisonment by the Ficksburg High Court on October 23, 2026, for a double murder at the Maseru Bridge border post in June 2024.

The South African Police Service announced that the two men were convicted of murdering famo musician Moholi “Bohome” Mohapi, 31, and Rebone David Sephaka, 40, and also received sentences for attempted murder and illegal immigration.

The pair were extradited from Lesotho to South Africa after their arrest in July 2024, following a shooting that occurred shortly after the victims arrived from Johannesburg.

Why it mattersTwo Basotho nationals were convicted and sentenced in South Africa for murders committed at their country's border.

Lesotho parliament lags on cybercrime law

Southern Africa's laws fail to recognise technology-facilitated gender-based violence, a policy brief finds.

Lesotho's parliament has yet to fully implement a regional model law on gender-based violence, including online crimes, nearly five years after hosting its adoption. The SADC Parliamentary Forum and Equality Now policy brief highlights that while Lesotho's Counter Domestic Violence Act of 2022 defines technological abuse, none of the 16 SADC member states have fully domesticated the model law.

The brief states that across the region, technology-facilitated gender-based violence (TFGBV) is barely recognised by law, with many countries lacking specific prohibitions on sharing intimate images without consent or on online coercion. While Lesotho's 2022 act allows courts to stop electronic abuse, the regional law, adopted at a forum hosted by Lesotho's Parliament in December 2021, remains largely undomesticated.

Most SADC member states are rated as having "partial" implementation of the model law, with some still drafting bills and two rated "low". The lack of comprehensive laws leaves women vulnerable to online harassment, blackmail, and the non-consensual sharing of intimate images, with limited legal recourse.

Why it mattersLesotho's citizens are affected because the country's parliament has not fully enacted laws to protect them from technology-facilitated gender-based violence.

Lesotho launches Tebellong bridge and road project

The government has unveiled Unik Construction to build a bridge over the Senqu River and tar eight kilometres of road.

The Lesotho government has launched a major infrastructure project in Qacha’s Nek to build a bridge across the Senqu River at Tebellong and tar eight kilometres of road linking Sehlabathebe to Thamathu. The Tebellong bridge project is expected to take 24 months to complete, addressing long-standing safety concerns where the river has claimed lives. The road project will be completed over an 18-month construction period.

Roads Directorate Director General Linehella Ntšere stated that the bridge project marks an important step in addressing community concerns, introducing Unik Construction as the appointed contractor with Zutari as the supervising consultant. Minister of Public Works and Transport Matjato Moteane expressed delight that the project is finally underway after years of promises, noting the government has invested more than M3 billion in Qacha’s Nek over the past four years. Recruitment information for the project is expected to be published next week, according to Principal Secretary Tšepang Koele.

Minister of Tourism, Sports, Arts and Culture Stephen Mputi welcomed the development, stating the bridge will improve access and create opportunities for tourism and local attractions. Area Chief Makhaola Makhaola conveyed the community's happiness and gratitude for the development, which he said comes after a long wait for assistance.

Why it mattersResidents of Tebellong and surrounding areas will experience safer travel and improved access to services and tourism opportunities.
