Princess Senate's charity raises M1 million for youth development

The Shoeshoe ea Moshoeshoe Trust and Foundation, launched by HRH Princess Senate Mohato Seeiso, secured over M1 million to support its goal of reaching 100 schools by 2028.

Princess Senate Mohato Seeiso's new charitable initiative, the Shoeshoe ea Moshoeshoe Trust and Foundation, has raised more than M1 million at its launch event. The organization aims to reach at least 100 schools across Lesotho by 2028, directly engaging over 20,000 young people and supporting thousands of learners.

The launch, held at AVANI Maseru, drew attendance from King Letsie III, Prime Minister Sam Matekane, government officials, and development partners. Pheello Mochaba, speaking for the foundation, described the event as the start of a national movement inspired by Lesotho's bicentennial celebrations and the values of Morena Moshoeshoe I.

The foundation seeks to invest in the future of young people by focusing on six strategic pillars, including youth leadership, entrepreneurship, mental health, education, and community development. The Sam Matekane Foundation contributed M500,000 to the initiative, highlighting significant financial support from key stakeholders.

Why it mattersYoung people in Lesotho will benefit from new programs focused on education, mental health, and leadership development, funded by the M1 million raised.

LNWMGA Chairperson Faces Scrutiny Over Alleged Misdirection of Farmers' Funds

Mokuenehi Thinyane, chairperson of the Lesotho National Wool and Mohair Growers Association, faces new questions regarding his relationship with South African wool broker BKB.

Mokuenehi Thinyane, chairperson of the Lesotho National Wool and Mohair Growers Association (LNWMGA), is under renewed scrutiny for allegedly directing farmers' wool and mohair business to South African broker BKB. This follows allegations that BKB appointed his son, Christopher Thinyane, to run its Lesotho subsidiary, Lihoai Consultancy, in 2020. Both BKB and Christopher Thinyane deny any link between the appointment and Mr. Thinyane's influence, stating the job was earned on merit.

The appointment occurred as Mr. Thinyane concluded a campaign against government regulations that had prevented farmers from selling their fibre outside Lesotho, which had impacted BKB's access to the market. These regulations, introduced in 2018, aimed to funnel sales through the Lesotho Wool Centre. Mr. Thinyane's campaign to scrap these rules, which included an appeal to the Court of Appeal, was unsuccessful, leading BKB to establish Lihoai Consultancy locally.

Further allegations suggest that over M13 million in farmers' dipping levies, intended for the Ministry of Agriculture, Food Security and Nutrition to fund livestock medicine, was instead deposited into the LNWMGA's account under Mr. Thinyane's instruction. A source claims these funds were then distributed among Mr. Thinyane's inner circle on the Association's executive committee, leading to a criminal charge against Mr. Thinyane in December 2019, for which he was released on M1,000 bail.

Why it mattersFarmers are affected by alleged misdirection of funds intended for livestock medicine and by potential conflicts of interest in the marketing of their wool and mohair.

Lesotho and Angola vie for SADC Organ chair, delaying decision to October

Regional leaders at the 46th SADC Ordinary Summit in Durban could not agree on whether Lesotho or Angola should be the next incoming chair of the SADC Organ on Politics, Defence.

Lesotho's bid to secure the incoming chair position for the Southern African Development Community (SADC) Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation has been postponed, as SADC leaders failed to reach a consensus between Lesotho and Angola. The impasse occurred at the 46th SADC Ordinary Summit in Durban, South Africa, on August 17, 2026, where Eswatini formally took over the Organ Troika chairship from Malawi.

The decision on the next incoming chair, which rotates annually among member states, has been pushed to an extraordinary summit scheduled for October 20, 2026. Lesotho expressed its interest in March, while Angola submitted a competing bid in June. This marks Lesotho's first opportunity to seek a leadership role within SADC structures in over a decade, following its removal from the SADC agenda in August 2025 after a period of observation due to political instability.

Foreign Affairs and International Relations Minister Limpho Tau confirmed Lesotho's formal expression of interest to the SADC Secretariat. The Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation is a key SADC institution responsible for addressing threats to regional peace, security, and stability across the 16-member bloc. Lesotho last held an overall regional chairmanship in 2006/2007 and briefly secured an incoming chair position in 2014/15 before political instability led to its removal.

Why it mattersThis event directly affects Lesotho's government and its standing within the SADC, impacting its ability to influence regional security and political stability decisions.

Finance Ministry identifies census overpayments, seeks recovery from workers

The Ministry of Finance and Development Planning has discovered overpayments to Census Assistant Supervisors and Enumerators, prompting an investigation and a directive for.

Lesotho's Ministry of Finance and Development Planning has identified overpayments made to Census Assistant Supervisors and Enumerators, marking another instance of financial discrepancies within the public sector. The Bureau of Statistics (BoS) confirmed these overpayments, though the exact number of affected individuals and the total amount involved are still under investigation.

The Ministry of Finance has instructed all census workers who received excess funds to immediately report the matter to their Constituency Supervisors. They have also been warned not to withdraw or spend these funds, as spending money one is not entitled to is illegal, even if deposited into a personal account. The ministry aims to verify payments and facilitate recovery.

This incident follows other reported financial mismanagement cases, including the Central Bank of Lesotho's handling of M524 million in government debt outside the Integrated Financial Management Information System and a duplicate payment of M1.2 million. Another case involved the Tsoha O Iketsetse project, where some beneficiaries received M5,000 instead of M500 due to an extra zero mistakenly added to payment amounts last year.

Why it mattersCensus Assistant Supervisors and Enumerators are directly affected as they are required to report and return overpaid funds, impacting their personal finances.

Lesotho High Court orders psychiatric evaluation for murder suspect

A woman accused of burning a romantic rival alive will undergo a mental health assessment before her murder trial can proceed in Leribe.

The High Court in Leribe has ordered ’Machabalala Chabalala, accused of murder, to undergo a psychiatric evaluation. This assessment will determine her fitness to stand trial and her mental state at the time of the alleged killing in Maputsoe in 2022. The trial, scheduled to begin on Thursday, was interrupted after her defense lawyer, Advocate Mokhachane of Legal Aid, raised concerns about her mental health.

The defense informed the court that Chabalala has a history of depression and had previously been under medical supervision, but had not complied with her doctor’s instructions. This led to concerns about her current mental state and her ability to understand the proceedings. The court’s decision to order the evaluation does not determine her guilt or innocence.

The case involves the alleged burning of Relebohile Letuka, who reportedly died after Chabalala allegedly poured petrol into a locked room and set it alight. This incident reportedly stemmed from a confrontation over a suspected romantic relationship between Letuka and Chabalala’s husband.

Why it mattersThe community in Maputsoe and Leribe is directly affected by the delay in this murder trial, as the psychiatric evaluation will impact the legal process for a serious alleged crime.
