Lesotho's Royal Palace project needs M50 million more to resume

The government has earmarked a further M50 million to restart work on the Maseru palace, lifting its projected cost to M461 million.

The government has earmarked another M50 million to resume construction of the new Royal Palace in Maseru, a building meant to serve as the official residence of King Letsie III and Queen 'Masenate Mohato Seeiso. The additional allocation raises the projected cost of the project to M461 million, up from an original price tag of M136.7 million.

Construction began in 2011, with completion scheduled for May 2013, and the building is still unfinished in November 2026. An audit report by the Auditor-General, published in May 2025 and later scrutinised by the Senate Committee on Government Assurances, found overpayments exceeding M74 million to consultants and the contractor. It also found M5.49 million paid to Nyolohelo Mohale as Clerk of Works/Principal Agent, although the ministry had no official documentation authorising his appointment.

The editorial argues that the M50 million must be properly accounted for and that procurement must be transparent, while warning that accountability should not cause further delays to finishing the building.

Why it mattersTaxpayers face a projected cost of M461 million, far above the original M136.7 million, and the audit findings mean the M50 million will be closely watched.

Lesotho plans Mini-Roof of Africa motorcycle race

The Lesotho Off Road Association will stage a shorter event on 21 November for riders who missed the main race.

The Lesotho Off Road Association (LoRA) plans to host a Mini-Roof of Africa motorcycle event on 21 November. The race will take place at Ha Molengoane, near Maseru, offering riders another chance to compete on challenging terrain. This event aims to accommodate riders who could not participate in the main Roof of Africa race held in September.

Lehlohonolo Taela, LoRA public relations officer, stated that the association received requests for an event on the traditional late-year dates. "Now that we no longer have the Roof of Africa on our traditional dates, we opted for a Mini-Roof to give riders and spectators something on the dates when we used to have the Roof of Africa," Taela said. The Mini-Roof will likely feature the Bronze and Iron race categories, omitting the more technical Silver and Gold classes.

The main Roof of Africa race now follows the Hard Enduro World Championship (HEWC) calendar, which led to its earlier staging this year. This change caused some riders, particularly from South Africa, to miss the event due to school and work commitments. LoRA decided to organize the Mini-Roof to cater to these riders and enthusiasts.

Why it mattersMotorcycle riders and enthusiasts in Lesotho and South Africa will have an opportunity to compete in a new off-road event.

AADFI launches continental development finance journal in Maseru

The Association of African Development Finance Institutions has launched a peer-reviewed journal to guide research and policy on Africa's economic growth.

The Association of African Development Finance Institutions (AADFI) has launched a continental Journal of Development Finance to strengthen research, knowledge generation and policy dialogue in support of Africa's economic development. The launch took place at the AADFI Chief Executive Officers (CEOs) Forum, which is being held in Maseru from 4 to 6 November 2026.

Zeph Nhleko, chairperson of the AADFI Economists Forum, which set up the publication in 2024, said the journal would give Africa a stronger voice in continental and global development policy debates. It will cover development banking, infrastructure finance, industrialisation, climate and green finance, financial inclusion, agricultural finance and risk management, and will use peer review overseen by the forum's executive committee.

Lesotho National Development Corporation Chief Executive Officer Thabo Khasipe welcomed delegates, saying the forum would explore how development finance institutions could become more resilient and contribute to transforming African economies. The forum's theme covers governance, standards, regulation and development impact.

Why it mattersDevelopment finance institutions across Africa, and the researchers and practitioners who work with them, gain a peer-reviewed platform for evidence on infrastructure, industrialisation and green finance.

Man claims kingship in Lesotho

Dr Tšepo Lipholo, a politician and reverend, has allegedly declared himself King of Basutoland.

Dr Tšepo Lipholo, known for his roles as a politician, reverend, and national doctor, has reportedly declared himself King of Basutoland. This claim comes from the neighbourhood of Qoaling, a place known for high crime rates.

The Lesotho Mounted Police Service established a post in Qoaling in 2020 due to concerns over crime. Allegations have surfaced regarding young men being recruited for military training in South Africa, though South African authorities found no evidence. The treason case involving Dr Lipholo is currently before the courts.

Dr Lipholo's ambition to claim a throne while another king already reigns has led to this legal challenge. The Lifaqane era, when kingdoms expanded through conquest, is contrasted with the current situation where supporters have reportedly organised an installation ceremony.

Johane Garments becomes Johane Industrials in Lesotho

Owner Tšepo Johane's textile factory has moved from a small enterprise to full-scale industrial production after years of debt and compliance problems.

Johane Garments, a textile factory owned by Mosotho entrepreneur Tšepo Johane, has been renamed Johane Industrials and is moving into full-scale industrial production. The business, which began at a skills development centre, had struggled for years with debt and compliance hurdles. The change was marked at a launch event in Ha Thetsane.

Trade, Industry and Business Development Minister Motlatsi Maqelepo told the event the milestone showed what a determined Mosotho entrepreneur could achieve. He said Johane had told him months earlier that outstanding debt with the Lesotho National Development Corporation had brought the business close to closure. Maqelepo said the government supports manufacturing through funding and infrastructure for innovation.

Johane said the launch was deeply personal, taking him back to the skills development centre in Station where his journey began. He said he would double his efforts to make the enterprise a lasting success and described the factory as an investment in local job creation. Maqelepo said Lesotho still relies on imported threads and fabrics, which limits the benefits it gets from the textile industry.

Why it mattersJohane Industrials' move into full industrial production is expected to bring its local jobs and business plans into a larger operation, as Johane described it.
