Man Sentenced to 20 Years or M15,000 Fine for Fatal Food Dispute

A Leribe man convicted of culpable homicide must also pay M30,000 compensation to the victim's family after a fatal altercation.

Masupha Katse has been sentenced to 20 years in prison or a M15,000 fine for killing ’Mane Mothatjaneng during a village celebration in August 2019. Acting High Court Judge ’Manapo Motebele also ordered Katse to pay M30,000 compensation to the victim’s family. The fatal assault occurred at Ha Seetsa following a dispute over food served at a community event.

Justice Motebele rejected Katse’s self-defense claim, finding he inflicted the fatal injury during a heat of passion after sudden provocation. The incident began when Katse, arriving from an initiation ceremony, demanded meat and papa, remarking that only dogs ate meat without the staple. A subsequent intervention by Mothatjaneng escalated into violence, with the Crown asserting Katse struck Mothatjaneng on the forehead with a stick.

Although no witnesses saw the fatal blow, prosecutors presented circumstantial evidence and alleged informal admissions. A post-mortem confirmed a severe head injury as the cause of death. The defense argued Katse struck Mothatjaneng on the chest, believing he was reaching for a concealed knife, and highlighted the deceased's violent reputation and a prior serious head injury.

Why it mattersA man was convicted of culpable homicide and ordered to pay a fine and compensation, affecting his livelihood and freedom.

Matekane Demands Tougher Drug War

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has called for a harsher crackdown on drug dealers, citing the M5 billion Khubetsoana case as a serious threat.

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has urged a more aggressive approach to combating drug trafficking, stating that narcotics are increasingly harming the youth and communities of Lesotho. His call comes amid the ongoing M5 billion Khubetsoana drug case, which involves three Chinese nationals, their company, a senior government official, and a political party leader. Matekane emphasized that those involved in peddling drugs should face no leniency from the justice system and challenged the police to intensify their efforts.

During a visit to Mabote Police Station, Matekane highlighted the pervasive nature of drug use, affecting both young people and adults. He noted the police's success in dismantling a drug warehouse in Berea, expressing hope for severe court punishments for the perpetrators. The Prime Minister questioned the adequacy of the recently approved 500 additional police officers in addressing the escalating drug problem, particularly given the rate at which youth are being corrupted.

The M5 billion case involves charges related to drug manufacturing, unlawful importation of medicines, and environmental pollution. The accused, who deny wrongdoing, were linked to approximately 7.5 tonnes of chemicals allegedly used in manufacturing crystal methamphetamine, ecstasy, and khat. Matekane's remarks underscore the government's concern over the significant impact of drug-related activities on national security and the well-being of its citizens.

Why it mattersThe Prime Minister's call for a tougher stance on drug lords and the ongoing M5 billion drug case directly affect the justice system and law enforcement's capacity to combat drug proliferation.

Mofolo convicted of Khetheng murder after decade-long trial

Senior Inspector Mathibeli Mofolo was found guilty of unlawfully causing the death of Police Constable Mokalekale Khetheng, who disappeared after being taken into custody.

Senior Inspector Mathibeli Mofolo has been convicted of the murder of Police Constable Mokalekale Khetheng, ending a nearly decade-long trial. High Court Judge Charles Hungwe ruled on Monday that Mofolo intentionally caused Khetheng’s death after taking him into police custody on March 26, 2016. Khetheng disappeared after being taken into custody and his body was found the next day with signs of violence.

Three co-accused, including former Hlotse Police Station commander Senior Superintendent Thabo Tšukulu, were acquitted of murder. Tšukulu was convicted of being an accessory after the fact for his role in an elaborate cover-up attempt. Inspectors Mabitle Matona and Sub-Inspector Haleokoe Taasoane were acquitted and released. The court found that Khetheng was taken alive into police custody, deliberately kept off records, and found dead the following day.

Justice Hungwe stated that while direct evidence of the killing was absent, the circumstantial case against Mofolo was overwhelming. The court found Mofolo had taken exclusive custody of Khetheng, circumvented detention records, borrowed handcuffs, and failed to explain these actions. Mofolo’s subsequent false accounts and attempts to suppress evidence were also considered by the judge.

Why it mattersSenior Inspector Mathibeli Mofolo has been convicted of murder, affecting the public's trust in law enforcement and the justice system.

Matekane Denies Links to Crime Boss Cat Matlala

Prime Minister Sam Matekane denies knowing South African crime boss Vusimusi “Cat” Matlala but confirms a former aide dealt with him.

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has denied any association with South African crime boss Vusimusi “Cat” Matlala, who claimed to be negotiating VIP protection services in Lesotho. Matekane stated he has never met or conducted business with Matlala, nor authorized any company linked to him for security services. This statement comes after allegations by Basotho National Party leader Machesetsa Mofomobe suggesting a business partnership between Matekane and Matlala.

The Prime Minister’s Office acknowledged that a former member of Matekane’s private security detail had discussions with Matlala regarding the establishment of a VIP security company in Lesotho. However, the office insisted these dealings were conducted in the former aide’s private capacity, without the Prime Minister’s knowledge, authority, or involvement. Matlala had testified before South Africa’s Madlanga Commission of Inquiry about these negotiations.

Matlala told the commission his security company had registered in Lesotho and was in talks for services, including presidential protection. He also confirmed working with General Lesetja Senona, provincial head of the Hawks in KwaZulu Natal, who was considering involvement in the Lesotho venture but was ultimately not hired. The company registration in Lesotho was confirmed by Matlala.

Why it mattersPrime Minister Sam Matekane is affected by allegations of links to a crime boss, which he denies, while his former aide's dealings with the crime boss are confirmed.
