Maseru mother and daughter murdered; relative arrested

A memorial service heard tributes to Hlompho and Liteboho Matsoso, who were allegedly killed by a 23-year-old relative.

Hlompho Matsoso, 66, a retired Deputy Accountant General, and her daughter Liteboho, 27, an Assistant Lecturer at Limkokwing University of Creative Technology, were found murdered inside their Thetsane West home last week. Tlotliso Mohapeloa, 23, a relative whom Hlompho had reportedly been assisting financially, has been arrested and is alleged to have carried out the killings. Police investigations are continuing.

At a memorial service in Maseru on Wednesday, Ministry of Foreign Affairs Principal Secretary Thabang Lekhela urged the public to remember the two for the lives they lived rather than the circumstances of their deaths. Family caregiver Mofihli Matsoso described the scene as horrific, saying the house had been forcibly entered and the victims attacked with a garden rake, a flower vase and a knife. Hlompho sustained 17 stab wounds to the head and Liteboho was stabbed 16 times, and Mr Matsoso alleged Hlompho's body had also been scalded with hot water.

Former Accountant General Likhapha 'Malehlohonolo Mahase recalled the close bond between mother and daughter, saying Hlompho had sometimes missed work to care for Liteboho by phone while she studied in China. Hlompho was posted to China as Third Secretary from 2000 to 2008.

Why it mattersPolice are holding one suspect, and investigations into the killings of the two women are still open.

Lesotho cuts diamond royalties for Letšeng mine

Government approves variable royalty rate of 3% to 6% for the country's sole operating diamond mine.

The Lesotho government has reduced royalties for the Letšeng Diamonds Mine from 10 percent to a variable rate between 3 percent and 6 percent. The decision, approved by the government, aims to support the mine amidst global diamond price slumps and rising fuel costs. Gem Diamonds Limited, which owns 70 percent of Letšeng, announced the change in its third-quarter trading update on November 4, 2026.

Letšeng, located in the Maluti Mountains, is the world's highest dollar-per-carat kimberlite diamond mine and is now the only one still operating in Lesotho. Minister of Energy and Mining, Lejone Mpotjoane, stated that the diamond industry is severely affected by the growing popularity of lab-grown diamonds. He also cited the Middle East conflict, which began on February 28, 2026, as a factor that has worsened the crisis by triggering a sharp rise in fuel prices.

The royalty remission, which temporarily waived payments until September 2026, had previously helped the company reduce costs. Gem Diamonds reported a modest first-half profit in 2026, supported by a 38 percent increase in Letšeng’s average rough diamond price to US$1395 per carat. The other three major diamond mines in Lesotho—Liqhobong, Kao, and Mothae—suspended operations earlier this year.

Why it mattersThe government has approved a variable royalty rate of between 3% and 6% for the Letšeng mine, effective for sales post October 1, 2026.

Business · 2026-11-13

Founder regains control of Naledi Funeral Planners after court ruling

The High Court's judgment on 5 November 2026 ends a 10-month boardroom dispute at the funeral services provider.

Thabiso Madiba, founder of Naledi Funeral Planners, has regained full control of the company after the High Court (Commercial Division) ruled in his favour on 5 November 2026. The judgment settled a 10-month dispute with a faction led by former board chairperson Malefetsane Tlelima, who had sought to take control of the company.

Addressing a press conference in Maseru, Mr Madiba said the dispute had stopped his team from accessing company funds held in banks, because they were not recognised as legitimate directors. He said one mortuary receives more than 100 corpses on a weekend when its capacity is 50, and that the company is now able to expand and invest in its services.

The company, founded in 2013 and operating 25 branches, has appointed Tšolo Seutloali as Chief Sales and Marketing Officer and Dr Mphekeleli Hoohlo as Chief Operations Officer. The dispute began at the company's Annual General Meeting on 16 January 2026, over the appointment of Advocate Peter Matekane as company secretary.

Why it mattersNaledi Funeral Planners can now access the company funds it had been unable to use, so it can expand its 25 branches and its mortuaries.

Health · 2026-11-13

ETL donates M400 000 medical equipment to Loretto Health Centre

Econet Telecom Lesotho's donation aims to improve maternal healthcare services with new equipment for the Qoaling facility.

Econet Telecom Lesotho (ETL) has donated medical equipment valued at M400 000 to the maternity ward of Loretto Health Centre in Qoaling. The donation, which includes delivery beds, ultrasound scanners, and vital signs monitors, was handed over this week by ETL Chief Executive Officer Dennis Plaatjies. This initiative was a collaboration between ETL, its sister company Sasai Econet Financial Services, and the Higherlife Foundation.

Mr Plaatjies stated that the donation is intended to enhance healthcare delivery, particularly for expectant mothers and their newborns. He emphasized the importance of health centres in providing hope and confidence to mothers, and expressed pride in supporting the improvement of health services in Lesotho. The equipment will enable healthcare workers to conduct more accurate examinations and provide better care.

ETL also announced a new working agreement with St Joseph Hospital in Rome, which will offer practical experience opportunities for Basotho medical students abroad. This partnership is expected to benefit 19 students currently enrolled in the Moshoeshoe I Scholarship Programme, who are studying medicine in Zimbabwe, Zambia, and South Africa.

Why it mattersThe donation of M400 000 worth of medical equipment directly improves care for mothers and babies at Loretto Health Centre.
