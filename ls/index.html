Man jailed 20 years or fined M15,000 for fatal food dispute

A Leribe man convicted of culpable homicide must also pay M30,000 compensation to the victim's family after a fatal row over food at a village celebration in August 2019.

Masupha Katse has been sentenced to 20 years in prison or a M15,000 fine for fatally assaulting ’Mane Mothatjaneng during a village celebration. Acting High Court Judge ’Manapo Motebele ordered Katse to also pay M30,000 compensation to the victim’s family. The conviction followed a dispute over food at Ha Seetsa in August 2019, where Katse inflicted a fatal head injury.

Katse denied murder, claiming self-defence after believing Mothatjaneng was reaching for a knife. However, Justice Motebele found the Crown proved beyond reasonable doubt that Katse inflicted the fatal injury. The court convicted him of culpable homicide, noting he acted in the heat of passion following sudden provocation. The incident began when Katse objected to being served meat without the staple food, papa, at a community gathering.

The altercation escalated when Katse allegedly struck Mothatjaneng with a stick after Mothatjaneng intervened in a separate dispute. While no witnesses saw the fatal blow, prosecutors used circumstantial evidence and informal admissions. A post-mortem identified a severe head injury as the cause of death. The defence argued Mothatjaneng had a history of violence and had previously sustained a head injury from a sword attack.

Why it mattersThis court ruling directly affects the convicted man and the victim's family through imprisonment, fines, and compensation payments.

Prime Minister Demands Tougher Drug War Amid M5bn Case

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has urged a more aggressive approach to combating drug trafficking, citing the ongoing M5 billion Khubetsoana case as a stark example of the threat.

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has called for a more stringent fight against drug dealers, stating that narcotics are increasingly harming communities and the future of young Basotho. His remarks follow the M5 billion Khubetsoana drug case, which involves three Chinese nationals, their company, a government official, and a political leader charged with operating a clandestine drug-manufacturing site. Matekane emphasized that perpetrators of drug crimes deserve no leniency from the justice system.

During a visit to Mabote Police Station, Matekane highlighted the pervasive issue of drug use affecting both children and parents. He acknowledged the police's efforts in dismantling a drug warehouse in Berea, expressing hope for severe court punishments. The Prime Minister also questioned the adequacy of the recently approved 500 additional police officers in addressing the escalating drug problem, particularly concerning the corruption of school-going youth.

The M5 billion Khubetsoana case involves charges against Chen Wei, Chen Minglevu, Yang Xiong Kang, their company Stan Dar (Pty) Ltd, senior official Kemong Masupha, and Machesetsa Mofomobe. They face accusations including drug manufacturing, unlawful importation of medical items, and environmental pollution. The accused deny the charges and are contesting them. The police had previously discovered approximately 7.5 tonnes of chemicals, believed to be for manufacturing crystal methamphetamine, ecstasy, and khat-related substances, with an estimated street value of M5 billion.

Why it mattersThe Prime Minister's call for a tougher stance on drug trafficking and the ongoing M5 billion drug case directly impact the safety and future prospects of Basotho youth.

Mofolo convicted of Khetheng murder after decade-long trial

A senior inspector was found guilty of intentionally causing the death of a police constable taken into custody in 2016, while co-accused were acquitted or convicted of cover-up.

Senior Inspector Mathibeli Mofolo has been convicted of the murder of Police Constable Mokalekale Khetheng, who disappeared after being taken into police custody on March 26, 2016. High Court Judge Charles Hungwe ruled that Mofolo unlawfully and intentionally caused Khetheng’s death. The judgment concludes nearly a decade of legal proceedings surrounding the case.

While Mofolo was found guilty, his three co-accused, Senior Superintendent Thabo Tšukulu, Inspector Mabitle Matona, and Sub-Inspector Haleokoe Taasoane, were acquitted of murder. However, Tšukulu was convicted of being an accessory after the fact for his role in an alleged cover-up. Matona and Taasoane were released.

The court found that PC Khetheng was taken alive into police custody, deliberately omitted from official records, and discovered dead the following day with signs of violence. Justice Hungwe stated that while direct evidence of the killing was absent, the circumstantial case against Mofolo, including his exclusive custody of the deceased and subsequent evasive accounts, was overwhelming.

Why it mattersThis conviction affects law enforcement personnel and brings closure to a case involving the death of a police officer while in custody.

Matekane Denies Links to South African Crime Boss

Prime Minister Sam Matekane stated he has never met or done business with Vusimusi “Cat” Matlala, despite a former aide’s dealings with the crime boss over a VIP protection.

Prime Minister Sam Matekane has denied any association with South African crime figure Vusimusi “Cat” Matlala, who claimed to be negotiating VIP protection services in Lesotho. Matekane’s office confirmed that a former member of his private security detail engaged with Matlala regarding a potential VIP security company in Lesotho. However, the office stressed these discussions occurred in the aide’s private capacity, without the Prime Minister’s knowledge or authorization.

The Prime Minister’s statement directly addresses allegations that he and Matlala were business partners, a notion he emphatically rejects. Matekane stated he has never met, known, or conducted business with Matlala, nor authorized any company linked to him for security services. This clarification follows Matlala’s testimony before South Africa’s Madlanga Commission of Inquiry.

Matlala had told the commission that his company was registered in Lesotho and that he was in negotiations to provide security services, including presidential protection. He also confirmed working with General Lesetja Senona on the venture, though Senona was ultimately not hired. The Prime Minister’s Office issued the statement to distance Matekane from Matlala’s alleged dealings and testimony.

Why it mattersThe Prime Minister's denial of association with a South African crime boss involved in a potential security venture affects perceptions of governance and stability within Lesotho.

Nine Basotho Students Depart for China on Scholarships

Nine Basotho students are traveling to China to study artificial intelligence, agriculture, medicine, engineering, and finance as educational cooperation between the two nations.

Nine Basotho students departed this week for China to pursue higher education in fields including artificial intelligence, agriculture, medicine, engineering, and finance. The students were bid farewell at a reception hosted by the Chinese Embassy in Lesotho, where Ambassador Yang Xiaokun encouraged them to utilize the opportunity and represent their country.

Ambassador Yang highlighted that education is a long-term investment in national development, fostering not only knowledge and skills but also character and responsibility. He noted that China's own progress is linked to sustained investment in education, science, technology, and human capital, offering lessons that could be adapted for Lesotho's development path.

This initiative is part of a broader expansion of China-Lesotho educational ties, which has seen thousands of Basotho benefit from scholarships, vocational training, and professional exchanges. Over the past three years, one thousand six hundred eighty-nine Basotho youths have participated in study and training programs, with more than two hundred fifty traveling to China this year for similar opportunities.

Why it mattersNine Basotho students will gain specialized education in China, potentially impacting their future careers and contributions to the country.
