Lesotho cuts diamond mine royalties to 6%

Government reduces payments for Letšeng mine due to global price drops and fuel costs.

The Lesotho government has reduced the royalty rate for Letšeng Diamonds Mine from 10 percent to a variable rate between 3 percent and 6 percent. This decision, approved on October 1, 2026, follows a global slump in diamond prices and increased fuel costs impacting operations. Letšeng is currently the only diamond mine operating in Lesotho, located in the Maluti Mountains of Mokhotlong District.

Gem Diamonds Limited, which owns 70 percent of Letšeng, announced the change in its third-quarter trading update on November 4, 2026. The variable rate will be determined by the six-monthly average dollar per carat. Minister of Energy and Mining, Lejone Mpotjoane, stated that the rise of lab-grown diamonds has driven down prices, while the war in the Middle East has increased fuel expenses.

The other three major diamond mines in Lesotho—Liqhobong, Kao, and Mothae—suspended operations earlier in 2026 due to high costs and weak market conditions. Gem Diamonds reported a modest first-half profit in 2026, partly supported by a 38 percent increase in Letšeng’s average rough diamond price to US$1395 per carat in the same period.

Why it mattersThe government's decision directly impacts the revenue generated from Lesotho's sole operating diamond mine.

Ha Belo factories near operational after power project completion

Unfinished industrial buildings in Botha-Bothe could soon create jobs as electrification nears completion.

The unfinished industrial buildings at Ha Belo in Botha-Bothe, along with incomplete electricity infrastructure, are nearing operational status after years of delays. The Lesotho National Development Corporation began construction of the M800 million Ha Belo Industrial Estate in 2016, completing 16 factory shells by April last year. The project stalled due to delays in constructing the Ha Belo 33kV substation and associated power lines.

The electrification project, part of the World Bank-funded Lesotho Renewable Energy and Energy Access Project, aims to stimulate economic growth by expanding electricity access. The Ministry of Energy awarded Phaks JV a contract on 18 August 2021 to construct the substation and power line, with an 18-month completion timeframe. The project cost escalated to M121,577,004.21, yet remained incomplete.

Former Ministry of Energy Principal Secretary Themba Sopeng was charged in January 2025 with corruption, embezzlement, and money laundering related to the project. In June 2025, the government refunded M22 million to the World Bank for unaccounted funds for the Ha Belo substation. Current Principal Secretary Tankiso Phapano stated he took office in January 2024, after the contract was awarded.

Why it mattersThousands of residents in Botha-Bothe who anticipated jobs and rental income from the Ha Belo industrial estate can now hope for economic recovery.

Naledi Funeral Planners founder regains control

High Court judgment ends a 10-month power struggle threatening the funeral services provider.

Thabiso Madiba, founder of Naledi Funeral Planners, has regained full control of the company after a High Court judgment on 5 November 2026. This ruling settled a 10-month boardroom dispute involving a faction led by former chairperson Malefetsane Tlelima. Founded in 2013, Naledi Funeral Planners operates 25 branches across Lesotho.

Mr Madiba stated that the dispute prevented his team from accessing company funds, as they were not recognised as legitimate directors. The court ruling allows them to access these funds and resume expansion plans. He announced new appointments: Tšolo Seutloali is now Chief Sales and Marketing Officer, and Dr Mphekeleli Hoohlo is Chief Operations Officer.

The dispute began at a 16 January 2026 AGM over Advocate Peter Matekane's dual role as company secretary and employee of the Directorate on Corruption and Economic Offences. Shareholders holding over 70 percent of the company's shares voted to dissolve the existing board, including Mr Tlelima, and remove him as chairperson.

Why it mattersLesotho's Naledi Funeral Planners can now access funds to expand services and improve operations after a court ruling ended a leadership dispute.

Maseru memorial urges remembrance of mother and daughter's lives

Principal Secretary Thabang Lekhela asked the public to remember Hlompho and Liteboho Matsoso for their contributions, not their deaths.

The public has been urged to remember Hlompho and Liteboho Matsoso for the lives they lived and their contributions to society. Ministry of Foreign Affairs Principal Secretary Thabang Lekhela said this at a memorial service in Maseru on Wednesday, where family, former colleagues and public figures paid tribute to the two women, who were allegedly killed last week by their 23-year-old relative, Tlotliso Mohapeloa.

Lekhela described Hlompho, 66, as a dedicated public servant who retired as Deputy Accountant General in August and had earlier been posted to China as Third Secretary between 2000 and 2008. Former Accountant General Likhapha 'Malehlohonolo Mahase recalled that Hlompho often missed work to care for Liteboho, who was studying in China, and that she wanted to travel there during the Covid-19 lockdown.

Liteboho, 27, was an Assistant Lecturer at Limkokwing University of Creative Technology. Family caregiver Mofihli Matsoso said the two were found murdered in their Thetsane West home, with the house forcibly entered. Tlotliso Mohapeloa, a relative Hlompho had reportedly been helping financially, has been arrested, and police investigations are continuing.

Why it mattersHlompho Matsoso's former colleagues and the family of Liteboho Matsoso are the people most directly affected by the memorial and the ongoing police investigation.

Lesotho rugby president to serve as secretary-general of new regional body

Fetang Selialia, head of the Federation of Lesotho Rugby, has been appointed secretary-general of COSARU, a newly endorsed Southern African body.

Fetang Selialia, president of the Federation of Lesotho Rugby, has been appointed secretary-general of the Council of Southern Africa Rugby Unions (COSARU). The body was established in 2023 but received official endorsement from Rugby Africa and World Rugby at its inaugural virtual meeting. Namibia's Petrie Theron, the chairperson, leads the organisation.

Selialia said COSARU's mandate is to build a framework for rugby development in Southern Africa, with a focus on youth and women's rugby. Rugby Africa has approved plans for a strategic partnership with the African Union Sports Council and an application to add rugby to the AUSC Region 5 Youth Games. Selialia said rugby will not feature at this year's edition in Mozambique but is expected at the next one.

The organisation will also run capacity-building programmes for coaches, referees, match officials and administrators, and explore technical cooperation and investment. Selialia said Africa has considerable rugby talent but struggles to turn it into international success because of inadequate structures, investment and athlete development pathways.

Why it mattersLesotho's rugby federation gains a seat in a regional body that will coordinate youth, women's and coaching programmes across Southern Africa.
