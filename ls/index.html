Lesotho to chair SADC security organ

Prime Minister Sam Matekane's election follows a decade of regional scrutiny for instability.

Lesotho will chair the Southern African Development Community (SADC) Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation starting in 2026. Prime Minister Sam Matekane was elected to the position at an extraordinary SADC summit, marking a significant diplomatic achievement for the nation.

This appointment signifies a turnaround in Lesotho's regional standing after years of observation due to political instability and security-sector failures. The country was placed under the Organ’s agenda in 2014 following an attempted military coup and subsequent political troubles, including assassinations and troop deployments.

Foreign Affairs Minister Limpho Tau described the appointment as an honour for all Basotho, emphasizing it is a national responsibility. The role requires continued reform and accountability at home to credibly address regional security challenges.

Why it mattersLesotho's election as chair of the SADC security organ means the nation must continue its own reforms to address governance weaknesses.

Mafeteng Hospital sued for M2 million over alleged negligence

A patient claims he received no medical attention for seven days after a stomach operation in December 2024.

Mafeteng Hospital faces a M2 million lawsuit for alleged medical negligence after a patient claimed a botched surgery left faecal matter leaking into his body. Teboho Rammusi alleges he received no medical attention for seven days following surgery for a stab wound in December 2024 before being transferred to Tšepong Hospital and later Bloemfontein, South Africa. He is suing the Ministry of Health, its Principal Secretary Matšoanelo Monyobi, Minister Mamokete Ntšekhe, the hospital's Medical Superintendent, and the Attorney General.

In court papers, Rammusi accuses Mafeteng Hospital of failing to provide adequate care and qualified practitioners, stating that tubes on his body showed faeces leaking. Doctors at Tšepong Hospital reportedly discovered faecal matter had spilled into his abdominal cavity through punctured intestines. Rammusi claims this negligence resulted in additional medical expenses, a severe lung infection, pain, and suffering.

The lawsuit alleges the hospital's medical superintendent agreed to perform surgery with reasonable professional skill and care but failed to ensure suitably qualified practitioners were available. Rammusi claims the government is liable as Mafeteng Hospital is state-owned and its officers were acting within their employment scope.

Why it mattersA patient in Lesotho is suing the government for M2 million due to alleged medical negligence following a surgery at Mafeteng Hospital.

Lebona Lephema considers new party after RFP leadership bid fails

Business tycoon Lebona Lephema may form a new political party if his attempt to unseat Nthomeng Majara as RFP deputy leader fails.

Disgruntled business tycoon Lebona Lephema is considering forming a new political party and leaving the Revolution for Prosperity (RFP). Lephema, a founding member of the RFP, believes he is entitled to the deputy leadership position currently held by Nthomeng Majara.

Lephema fell out with Prime Minister Sam Matekane after resisting a ministry split and his subsequent redeployment. He was dismissed from Cabinet in May after refusing to accept a new portfolio, citing potential conflicts of interest with the Lesotho National Development Corporation.

If his bid to wrestle the RFP deputy leadership from Majara fails, Lephema plans to form his own party. He has reportedly consulted with disgruntled members of the All Basotho Convention (ABC), including former minister Chalane Phori and ex-secretary-general Lebohang Hlaele, though both deny the claims.

Why it mattersLebona Lephema's potential departure from the ruling RFP and formation of a new party could affect political stability.

Sport · 2026-10-30

Likuena coach warns of player fitness slump

Bob Mafoso reports aerobic fitness has dropped significantly ahead of November AFCON qualifiers.

Lesotho's national football team, Likuena, is facing a significant drop in player fitness, according to coach Bob Mafoso. Locally based players reported for a non-residential training camp on Monday, where physical assessments revealed a decline in aerobic capacity and endurance. The team is preparing for back-to-back 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) qualifiers against Gabon next month.

Mafoso stated that approximately 80 percent of players who previously completed the Bronco Test in four minutes and 28 seconds are now taking four minutes and 52 seconds. He also expressed concern over players' strength levels, noting that physical conditioning requires urgent attention. Despite the findings, Mafoso insisted the technical team has three weeks to improve player condition before the crucial matches.

Likuena lost their opening two AFCON qualifiers to Niger and Morocco, and positive results against Gabon are needed to revive qualification hopes. The team will face Gabon's Panthers in Libreville on November 13 and in Polokwane, South Africa, on November 17. Mafoso stressed that player pressure should be reflected in preparation, not just on match day.

Why it mattersLikuena players' declining fitness levels directly impact their ability to perform in upcoming AFCON qualifiers.
