Princess Senate's charity raises M1 million for education goals

The Shoeshoe ea Moshoeshoe Trust and Foundation, launched by Princess Senate Mohato Seeiso, secured over M1 million to support its goal of reaching 100 schools by 2028.

Princess Senate Mohato Seeiso's new charitable initiative, Shoeshoe ea Moshoeshoe Trust and Foundation, has raised more than M1 million at its launch event. The foundation aims to reach at least 100 schools across Lesotho by 2028, directly engaging over 20,000 young people and supporting thousands of learners.

The launch, held at AVANI Maseru, was attended by King Letsie III, Prime Minister Sam Matekane, government officials, and development partners. Pheello Mochaba, speaking for the foundation, stated that the initiative is a national movement inspired by Lesotho's bicentennial celebrations and dedicated to investing in youth while preserving Basotho heritage.

The foundation outlined six strategic pillars, including youth leadership, culture, mental health, education, community development, and responsible youth lifestyles. The Sam Matekane Foundation contributed M500,000 to the initiative, which seeks partnerships across government, private sector, and civil society to achieve its goals.

Why it mattersThis initiative directly affects young people in Lesotho by providing educational support, mental health resources, and opportunities for youth leadership and entrepreneurship.

Lesotho, Angola vie for SADC Organ chair, decision deferred to October

Regional leaders at the 46th SADC Ordinary Summit in Durban could not agree on whether Lesotho or Angola should be the next incoming chair of the Organ on Politics, Defence and.

Lesotho's bid to secure the incoming chair position for the Southern African Development Community (SADC) Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation has been delayed, as member states failed to reach a consensus with Angola's competing bid. The decision has been postponed to an extraordinary summit scheduled for October 20, 2026, according to the Sunday Express Lesotho.

The impasse occurred at the 46th SADC Ordinary Summit in Durban, South Africa, where Eswatini formally took over the Organ Troika chairship from Malawi. Lesotho declared its interest in March, while Angola lodged its bid in June, creating a contest that leaders could not resolve during the August 17 summit.

This marks Lesotho's first opportunity to seek a leadership role within SADC structures in over a decade, having been removed from the SADC agenda in August 2025 after a period of observation due to recurring political instability. Foreign Affairs and International Relations Minister Limpho Tau confirmed Lesotho's formal expression of interest to the SADC Secretariat.

Why it mattersThis event directly impacts Lesotho's political standing and its ability to influence regional security and stability decisions within SADC.

Business · 2026-08-25

Finance Ministry uncovers census payroll overpayments

The Ministry of Finance and Development Planning has identified overpayments to Census Assistant Supervisors and Enumerators, prompting an investigation into the extent of the.

The Ministry of Finance and Development Planning has found overpayments made to Census Assistant Supervisors and Enumerators, according to the Sunday Express Lesotho. The Bureau of Statistics (BoS) confirmed the overpayments, though its Public Relations Officer, Neo Mokatsa, stated the investigation is in its early stages and the total amount or number of affected individuals is not yet known.

This incident adds to a series of financial discrepancies in government, including the Central Bank of Lesotho’s handling of M524 million in government debt outside the Integrated Financial Management Information System. Another case involved the Tsoha O Iketsetse project, where some beneficiaries received M5,000 instead of M500 due to an extra zero mistakenly added by Disaster Management Authority officials.

In an August 10, 2026 letter, the Ministry of Finance instructed those who received excess payments to report to their Constituency Supervisors immediately and not to withdraw or spend the funds. The ministry warned that spending money one is not entitled to is illegal, even if it has been deposited into a bank account, and urged cooperation for a smooth recovery process.

Why it mattersThis news affects census workers who received overpayments, requiring them to report the discrepancy and retain the excess funds, and it impacts public trust in government financial management.

Lesotho High Court orders psychiatric evaluation for murder accused

A woman accused of burning a romantic rival to death will undergo a mental health probe before her murder trial can proceed in Leribe.

The High Court in Leribe has ordered ’Machabalala Chabalala, accused of murder, to undergo a psychiatric evaluation. This decision came after her defense lawyer, Advocate Mokhachane of Legal Aid, raised concerns about her mental health, stating she had a history of depression and had not complied with medical instructions.

The evaluation will determine if Chabalala is mentally fit to stand trial and if she was suffering from a mental disorder at the time of the alleged killing in Maputsoe in 2022. The defense argued that her current mental state might prevent her from understanding the proceedings or appreciating the nature of the trial.

Chabalala is accused of pouring petrol into a locked room and setting it alight, leading to the death of Relebohile Letuka, following a confrontation over a suspected romantic relationship with Chabalala’s husband. The court's order does not determine guilt or innocence, but addresses her capacity to participate in the legal process.

Why it mattersThe accused, ’Machabalala Chabalala, faces a psychiatric evaluation, which will directly impact the legal proceedings of her murder trial and determine her fitness to stand trial.

Lesotho Correctional Service faces court challenge over officer promotions

A senior officer has filed a High Court application to overturn the April 2026 promotions of three colleagues, alleging they lack required academic qualifications.

Assistant Superintendent (AS) Karele Poqa is challenging the promotions of Assistant Commissioner (AC) Neo Moramotse, Senior Superintendent (SSP) Ramaketekete Rasehloho, and Superintendent (Supt) Sidwell Mothea. Poqa claims these officers were promoted despite not meeting the academic qualifications outlined in the Lesotho Correctional Service (LCS) promotion guidelines.

According to Poqa's court papers, the promotions process, initiated by LCS Acting Commissioner Matingoa Phamotse in February 2026, was procedurally flawed. Poqa alleges that the academic credentials of candidates were not verified, and the Senior Officers Appointment Board was improperly constituted, lacking a public officer nominated by the Minister of Public Service.

Poqa specifically states that AC Moramotse and SSP Rasehloho did not possess the degrees required for their new ranks. He seeks to have the court demote the three officers to their previous ranks of Senior Superintendent, Superintendent, and Assistant Superintendent, respectively.

Why it mattersLesotho Correctional Service officers are affected by potential demotions and a challenge to the integrity of their promotion system.
