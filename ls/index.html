China reaffirms ties and multilateralism with Lesotho

Ambassador Yang Xiaokun urged local media to respect China's sovereignty claims over Taiwan.

Chinese Ambassador to Lesotho, Yang Xiaokun, met with local media in Maseru this week to reaffirm China's commitment to multilateralism and strengthening relations with Lesotho. The meeting marked the 55th anniversary of the restoration of the People’s Republic of China’s (PRC) seat at the United Nations (UN) and Commemoration Day of Taiwan’s Restoration.

Ambassador Yang stated that history shapes international relations and offers lessons for safeguarding peace and nations' legitimate rights. He recalled that the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 2758 on 25 October 1971, which restored the PRC's rights at the UN and recognised its representatives as the sole legitimate representatives of China.

The ambassador also highlighted China's contributions to international development and peace efforts, including its Global Development Initiative which has mobilised over US$23 billion. China has implemented zero-tariff treatment for 53 African countries, a measure from which Lesotho is expected to benefit.

Why it mattersLesotho is expected to benefit from China's implementation of zero-tariff treatment for 53 African countries with which it maintains diplomatic relations.

WASCO begins Mafeteng drought relief plan

The water company will supply tanks, drill boreholes, and dredge Rasebale Dam to secure water.

The Water and Sewage Company (WASCO) has started a drought response plan in Mafeteng, Lesotho, to address water shortages. The plan includes delivering 29 water tanks for immediate relief and drilling boreholes as a secondary water source. The company will also dredge Rasebale Dam to increase its storage capacity, a project described as a first for WASCO.

WASCO Project manager Lehlohonolo Ncheke stated the intervention aims to prevent a repeat of severe shortages seen in 2015 and 2024. WASCO CEO Lebohang Peke Makara called the dam dredging a defining moment for the company's engineering capabilities, noting Rasebale Dam has had collection problems since 2012. He also highlighted that delays have increased costs.

Natural Resources Minister Mohlomi Moleko said the dredging fulfills a 2024 commitment by Prime Minister Sam Matekane. Traditional leaders, however, warned that dredging alone is insufficient without addressing erosion in the dam's catchment area. Chief Fako Leshoro Seiso stated sand will continue to accumulate if underlying causes are not fixed.

Why it mattersMafeteng residents will receive emergency water supplies and improved long-term water security through new infrastructure.

Lesotho to chair SADC security organ

The nation takes on leadership of the Southern African Development Community Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation.

Lesotho will lead the Southern African Development Community (SADC) Organ on Politics, Defence and Security Cooperation starting in 2025. Prime Minister Sam Matekane was elected to the position by SADC heads of state and government. This role signifies a major shift for Lesotho, which was under regional scrutiny for political instability for over a decade.

The country was removed from the Organ’s agenda in August 2025 after years of observation following events like an attempted military coup in 2014 and the assassinations of two army commanders in 2015 and 2017. Foreign Affairs Minister Limpho Tau called the appointment an honour for all Basotho.

This leadership role requires Lesotho to continue its reform efforts, including independent institutions and security agencies operating within the law. The country's constitutional and institutional reforms, though slow, must become a lived reality to credibly address regional challenges.

Why it mattersLesotho's election as chair of the SADC security organ means the country must continue its own reforms to address political and security challenges.

Lesotho police risk allowance dispute continues

Officers have waited since 1994 for an increase from M100 to M1500 monthly.

Members of the Lesotho Mounted Police Service (LMPS) are awaiting a proposed M1500 monthly risk allowance, a dispute that has faced bureaucratic delays. The current allowance of M100 has been in place since 1994, failing to reflect the dangers officers face daily.

Police officers routinely confront armed criminals, dangerous traffic, and volatile situations, often at personal risk. The proposed increase aims to better recognise the occupational hazards inherent in their unpredictable work environments.

While crime statistics are not provided, public perception suggests a decline in vehicle hijackings and drunk driving incidents. The LMPS faces ongoing challenges with organised crime, illegal firearms, and drug trafficking, but the risk allowance debate continues.

Why it mattersPolice officers in Lesotho are affected by the protracted administrative process delaying their risk allowance increase.

Gambia beats Lesotho by three runs in Abuja

Lesotho finished fourth in the Africa Cricket Association tournament after losing their final match.

Lesotho's campaign in the Africa Cricket Association regional qualifying tournament in Abuja ended with a three-run defeat to Gambia. The national side was bowled out for 109 in 19.5 overs while chasing Gambia's 112 for nine. The loss concluded Lesotho's participation in the tournament.

The result left Lesotho fourth in the final standings with one win from five matches and two points. Nigeria and Uganda secured the two places in the Africa Regional Final, according to the Lesotho Cricket Association (LCA). Coach Dzikamai Mavhiko highlighted the lack of a dedicated cricket ground and players' inability to commit full-time as limitations.

Mavhiko stated that sustained investment in facilities and player development is needed to close the gap. The LCA said the team showed promise and must build on the experience for future international fixtures.

Why it mattersLesotho's national cricket team concluded its tournament participation with a loss, highlighting challenges in facilities and player commitment.
