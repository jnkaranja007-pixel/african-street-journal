Taxi fire kills community leader, sparks safety probe

Malefu Sephula, 45, died from burns sustained in a taxi blaze two weeks prior, prompting calls for an investigation into public transport safety.

Hundreds gathered to mourn Malefu Sephula, 45, who died on August 14, 2026, from burns sustained in a taxi fire two days earlier. The blaze erupted inside the vehicle minutes after Sephula boarded it on her way to work. Two other passengers, including a child, were hospitalized but survived. The incident has raised questions about bystander conduct, with a family member criticizing someone for filming the event instead of assisting.

Community councillor Lekhooa Molibetsane criticized the misuse of social media for popularity and questioned if the taxi was equipped with a fire extinguisher. Minister of Sports Mputi Stephen Mputi, speaking for the government, called Sephula's death a devastating loss and stated the government's responsibility for public transport safety. He called for a formal investigation into the taxi's condition and the circumstances of the fire.

Allegations suggest the taxi owner, who was reportedly driving, added petrol directly to the engine's carburettor before starting the vehicle. These claims could not be independently verified. Sephula's death is the third tragedy involving fire or road accidents to affect her family, who previously survived a near-fatal incident where she was dragged by a truck.

Why it mattersMalefu Sephula, a community servant, died from burns sustained in a taxi fire, prompting an investigation into public transport safety.

Man sentenced to 20 years for fatal food row

A Leribe man convicted of culpable homicide must pay M30,000 compensation to the victim's family or serve two decades in prison.

Masupha Katse has been sentenced to 20 years in prison or a M15,000 fine for fatally assaulting ’Mane Mothatjaneng during a village celebration in August 2019. Acting High Court Judge ’Manapo Motebele also ordered Katse to pay M30,000 in compensation to the victim’s family. The altercation began over a dispute about serving meat with papa, a staple food, at a community event in Ha Seetsa.

Justice Motebele rejected Katse’s claim of self-defence, finding that he inflicted the fatal injury during a heat of passion following sudden provocation. While none of the prosecution’s five witnesses saw the fatal blow, the Crown presented circumstantial evidence and informal admissions. The post-mortem identified a severe head injury as the cause of death, though Katse’s defence argued he struck the deceased on the chest and that the victim had a history of violence.

The incident occurred on August 3, 2019, when Katse and another man arrived at a celebration where sheep had been slaughtered. After being served mutton, Katse allegedly commented that only dogs ate meat without papa. A dispute over food escalated when Katse reportedly struck a witness’s hand with a stick, leading to Mothatjaneng’s intervention and the fatal assault. Mothatjaneng died the day after the confrontation.

Why it mattersA man is affected by a prison sentence and compensation order following a fatal dispute over food during a community gathering.

Econet, GEM Institute Plant 500 Fruit Trees at Lesotho Schools

The 2026 ACT 4 Climate Change Fruit-Up School Drive aims to promote practical climate action and environmental education among young Basotho.

Telecommunications company Econet Telecom Lesotho, in partnership with GEM Institute and UNESCO, launched the 2026 ACT 4 Climate Change Fruit-Up School Drive, which will see over 500 fruit trees planted at 14 schools across six districts. The initiative, launched at Qhomane LECSA High School, is part of a five-year collaboration focused on environmental education and climate action for young people. Learners will participate in tree planting and tree care, fostering a hands-on approach to conservation.

Econet's Public Relations Specialist, Retšelisitsoe Nkhahle, stated that the company's commitment extends beyond telecommunications, emphasizing a responsibility to contribute to the communities and the future of Lesotho. The Fruit-Up program aims to move climate change education from theory to practice, creating greener school environments and providing long-term benefits for schools and surrounding communities. Since 2021, Econet and GEM Institute have planted 3,397 fruit trees at 54 schools, involving over 3,000 learners.

GEM Institute, represented by Tšepang Khau, highlighted the program's role in building environmental and entrepreneurial skills since 2021, encouraging Basotho to recognize the potential of indigenous plants for economic opportunities. UNESCO representative Mankopane Nkhono reaffirmed the organization's support for these initiatives, noting their success in engaging youth in environmental awareness and climate action. The Qhomane LECSA High School principal welcomed the program, with the school receiving 50 apple and peach trees.

Why it mattersYoung Basotho learners are directly involved in practical climate action through tree planting and care, fostering environmental awareness and skills.

Mofomobe asks South Africa to question PM over security links

An opposition leader has petitioned South Africa's Madlanga Commission to seek evidence from Prime Minister Sam Matekane regarding alleged connections to a controversial.

Opposition politician Machesetsa Mofomobe has asked South Africa's Madlanga Commission of Inquiry to question Prime Minister Sam Matekane about his alleged links to businessman Vusimuzi “Cat” Matlala. Matlala testified before the commission, stating he was negotiating to provide VIP protection services to the Lesotho Prime Minister through a company registered in the country. The commission, established in July 2025, is investigating criminality and corruption within South Africa's criminal justice system.

Prime Minister Matekane has denied any knowledge of or business dealings with Matlala, stating he has never met the businessman. However, the Prime Minister's office acknowledged that a former member of his private security detail had engaged with Matlala regarding the establishment of a VIP security company in Lesotho. These dealings, the office stated, were conducted in the former aide's private capacity without the Prime Minister's authorization.

Matlala, who faces criminal charges in South Africa, also testified that General Lesetja Senona, a provincial head of the Hawks, had expressed interest in joining his Lesotho venture, though he was ultimately not hired. Matlala's company was involved in a controversial R228 million police healthcare tender in South Africa, which was later canceled due to corruption allegations. He has also been linked to cartels accused of stealing billions from South African hospitals.

Why it mattersPrime Minister Matekane is affected by allegations of links to a controversial businessman, which could impact his reputation and political standing.

Politics · 2026-09-04

ABC leaders fight, party implodes

Party leader Kabi accuses deputy and SG of insubordination amid deepening crisis.

The once-dominant All Basotho Convention (ABC) party is in crisis as its leadership is at war. Party leader Nkaku Kabi issued a circular accusing his deputy, Samonyane Ntsekele, and Secretary General Thebe Mokoatle of deliberately destabilizing the party. This internal conflict follows an incident where Mr. Mokoatle was reportedly attacked by Mr. Kabi’s bodyguards at a National Executive Committee meeting.

Founded in 2006, the ABC previously dominated Lesotho’s political landscape, winning 51 seats in 2017 and leading the governing coalition. The party suffered a significant loss in the October 2022 polls, securing only eight Proportional Representation seats after losing all 51 constituencies. Mr. Kabi took leadership in February 2022, succeeding Tom Thabane, and has been pursuing a revitalization strategy.

In his circular, Mr. Kabi alleged that Messrs. Mokoatle and Ntsekele have repeatedly disobeyed NEC decisions and worked against his leadership. The party has been plagued by deep internal factionalism since its electoral defeat, with this latest dispute further intensifying the ongoing crisis within the ABC.

Why it mattersThe internal conflict within the ABC party leadership affects its ability to govern and potentially destabilizes the political landscape for all Basotho citizens.
