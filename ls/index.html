World Vision unveils five-year plan for 650,000 vulnerable Lesotho children

The 'BOLD HOPE' National Strategy 2026-2030 covers children from early childhood to adolescence, with work on health, nutrition and child protection.

World Vision International Lesotho launched its 'BOLD HOPE' National Strategy 2026-2030 last Friday, aimed at improving the wellbeing of 650,000 vulnerable children from early childhood to adolescence.

National Director James Chifwelu said the launch reaffirmed the charity's commitment to letting every child in Lesotho live life in its fullness, whatever their circumstances. The strategy promises agile, low-cost, high-impact programmes.

World Vision has worked in Lesotho since 1987 across six Area Development Programmes, and Chifwelu cited progress on child protection systems, water, sanitation and hygiene, and menstrual health, which led to a National Menstrual Health Strategy.

Why it mattersVulnerable children in the most remote and hard-to-reach areas of Lesotho are the people the strategy is designed to reach.

Lesotho ranks 16th of 54 in African governance index

The country scored 58.3 out of 100, above both the African and Southern African averages, in the 2026 Ibrahim Index.

Lesotho has placed 16th out of 54 African countries in the 2026 Ibrahim Index of African Governance, scoring 58.3 out of 100. The Mo Ibrahim Foundation released the index on October 31, covering governance from 2016 to 2025. Lesotho's score is above the African average of 50.1 and the Southern African regional average of 56.1, a gain of 1.9 points over the decade.

The index grades four areas: security and rule of law, participation, rights and inclusion, economic opportunity, and human development. Lesotho scored highest on participation and rights, and every area improved over the period. The country scored 100 on the absence of armed conflict, laws against violence toward women, and civil registration.

The index also flags weak spots. The transport network scored just 16.8, and public perception of economic opportunity stood at 18.8. Law enforcement fell 33.7 points to 25.8, and perceptions of anti-corruption dropped 21.5 points.

Why it mattersTraders and households that depend on Lesotho's transport network face its lowest-scoring area, 16.8 out of 100, in a index released on October 31.
