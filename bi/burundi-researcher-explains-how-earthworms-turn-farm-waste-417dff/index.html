Burundi researcher explains how earthworms turn farm waste into compost

Eric Kazitsa says the process uses agricultural and food-processing residues, with earthworms producing more than 20 offspring monthly when well raised.

Source: Iwacu · 2026-11-21 · 1 min read

Eric Kazitsa, a researcher at Burundi's Ecole normale supérieure, says vermicomposting uses earthworms and microorganisms to break down organic matter into compost. The process has three stages: pre-composting, active decomposition and maturation.

Agricultural and food-processing residues form the starting material. Kazitsa says drying them raises their carbon content, while mixing them with cow dung adds nitrogen. Acidic waste, including citrus residues, should be avoided or treated first, because worms prefer a pH between 6 and 8.

In the active stage, worms consume and break down the material, releasing castings that mature into a stable, nutrient-rich compost ready for harvest. Kazitsa says a well-raised worm can lay eggs two or three times a week and produce more than 20 individuals by month's end.

Why it mattersFarmers can turn their crop and food-processing residues into usable compost rather than discarding them, and a well-raised worm stock can multiply quickly.
