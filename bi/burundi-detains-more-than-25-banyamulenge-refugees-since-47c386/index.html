Burundi detains more than 25 Banyamulenge refugees since October

Security services accuse the refugees of spying for Rwanda and collaborating with M23 and its allies, while families seek information about detainees.

Source: SOS Medias Burundi · 2026-11-20 · 1 min read

Burundi’s intelligence services have arrested more than 25 Banyamulenge refugees since October, in Bujumbura and refugee camps, according to SOS Médias Burundi. Authorities accuse them of spying for Rwanda and collaborating with M23 and its allies, grouped in the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, a politico-military coalition led by former Congolese president Joseph Kabila.

Community sources told the outlet that some arrests took place in churches, homes, streets and at wedding ceremonies. Some people detained early in the wave were transferred to Bujumbura’s central prison, while families are seeking the reasons for the arrests and the locations of their relatives. On Friday 9 October, a security ministry spokesperson presented 13 people accused of the same offences to journalists; most were Banyamulenge refugees.

Authorities also suspended at least two churches managed by refugees or led by members of the Banyamulenge community during the same month. Community members link the arrests to security in Minembwe, in Congo’s South Kivu province, and say the authorities have not clarified the accusations against them.

Why it mattersFamilies of Banyamulenge refugees are seeking the reasons for relatives’ detention and their locations, and some detainees have been transferred to Bujumbura’s central prison.
