Burundi educator presses for Braille access and school exams for blind pupils

Jean-Marie-Vianney Zingiza says abbreviated Braille would make books received from abroad usable and help blind students keep pace in class.

Source: Yaga Burundi · 2026-11-22 · 1 min read

Jean-Marie-Vianney Zingiza, a former teacher and Braille educator in Burundi, is advocating for blind pupils to have accessible books, trained teachers and the chance to continue to university. He says many books received from abroad use abbreviated Braille, which many Burundian teachers and pupils cannot read.

Abbreviated Braille uses shorter signs for words or groups of letters than full Braille, taking less space and allowing faster reading and note-taking with practice. Zingiza says pupils writing letter by letter can fall behind as a teacher dictates. He returned to teaching in 2008 at the Rumuri Centre, after losing his sight in 1991.

From 2008 to 2012, blind pupils were not allowed to take the national examination at the end of sixth year, according to the account. After Zingiza pressed for access, the education minister authorised six pupils to sit it in 2012; two passed.

Why it mattersBlind pupils and teachers need abbreviated-Braille skills to use books received from abroad, while access to the sixth-year examination led six pupils to sit it in 2012.
