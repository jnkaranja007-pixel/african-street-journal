Rumonge commune threatens 130 families with eviction from 600 hectares

Residents of Mutambara hill say the commune wants to reclaim farmland they have worked since the rural development programmes of past decades.

Source: SOS Medias Burundi · 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

About 130 families, including roughly 10 widows, living on Mutambara hill in the Gatete zone of Rumonge commune in Burunga province, south-west Burundi, say local authorities are threatening to expropriate their land. The residents have been in a dispute with the administration since 2023 over parcels they have worked for several decades.

The core of the disagreement is where the boundary lies between state land and the plots the families occupy and cultivate. The area involved is estimated at about 600 hectares. The families are asking the authorities to clarify the limits of public land and of the land they claim, saying the dispute threatens their livelihoods.

The residents say their plots were allocated under rural development programmes, including the PIA-RU.BU.BU, and that cession contracts were issued although the state kept ownership. After the programme was dissolved, they kept farming, growing food crops and planting oil palms, and some plots have passed by inheritance or been sold between private individuals.
