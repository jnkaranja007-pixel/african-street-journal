Burundi demolishes market stalls, traders fear ruin

Ruyigi officials say stall demolitions prevent fires, but small business owners face lost income and debt.

Source: SOS Medias Burundi · 2026-10-24 · 1 min read

WhatsApp X Telegram

Small traders in Burundi's Ruyigi market face ruin after authorities began demolishing their kiosks and selling points on September 16. The communal administration in Buhumuza province claims the operation aims to prevent fires and clear access for firefighters. Affected vendors, including palm oil, food, and fruit sellers, fear losing their primary income and being unable to repay loans taken for their businesses.

Traders reported having official authorization for their stalls and expressed surprise at the demolitions, stating they had recently spent capital on school expenses and restocking. Many had taken loans from local banks. They worry that relocation outside the market will reduce customers and income. Communal administrator Diane Niyibitanga stated the decision was made with trader representatives, citing fire risks and the need for clear access routes for emergency services, adding that traders were informed three months prior.

Niyibitanga warned that opposition to the demolitions could lead to sanctions, including reimbursement of demolition costs, administrative fines, or legal action. Local police are prepared to support the operation. The demolitions are impacting vendors of palm oil, cotton, food products, fruits, vegetables, and tailors using irons.
