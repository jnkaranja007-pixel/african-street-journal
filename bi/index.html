Meheba refugees face new gate pass rules

Camp officials announced gate passes will now take one week to issue and last three months.

The new head of Meheba refugee camp in Zambia met with residents on November 4, 2026, to address security concerns. Issues discussed included difficulties obtaining exit permits, known as gate passes, stray animals, and a rise in banditry. Measures were announced to improve safety and community relations within the camp.

Refugees reported that delays in receiving gate passes hinder their movement, leading to arrests for lacking identity documents. The camp head committed to issuing gate passes within one week, extending their validity from one month to three months. Concerns were also raised about stray pigs destroying crops and dogs posing a biting risk, with owners given two weeks to regularize their animals or face potential culling.

The administration also addressed unauthorized construction and a reported increase in banditry. Residents were reminded to seek permission before building and to report suspicious individuals. The goal is to enhance security through better collaboration between residents and camp administration.

Why it mattersBurundian refugees in Zambia are directly affected by new administrative procedures for exit permits and security measures in their camp.

Burundi engineer disappears, three SOSUMO officials questioned

Ismaël Mugisha, an engineer at the Moso Sugar Company, has been missing since October 30, prompting an investigation.

Ismaël Mugisha, an engineer and deputy head of agricultural equipment maintenance at SOSUMO, disappeared on October 30 from his residence in Rutana province, southern Burundi. Three company officials, Arnaud Nikuze, Jean Claude Ntwari, and Aimé Clovis Irakoze, were questioned by the public prosecutor in Makamba on November 3 regarding his unexplained absence. Mugisha was last seen heading to work on the morning of October 30, stating he needed to complete biometric check-ins.

Mugisha, who had worked at SOSUMO for four years, reportedly had disagreements with management over equipment maintenance, advocating for repairs over new purchases. Internal sources suggest suspicions of mismanagement and potential fraud in equipment acquisition and upkeep, though these claims remain unproven by the justice system. Colleagues reported that Mugisha had expressed feeling threatened by some individuals within the company prior to his disappearance.

SOSUMO announced Mugisha's disappearance in local churches on November 1, two days after he went missing. The circumstances surrounding his vanishing remain unclear, with conflicting accounts from colleagues about whether he was seen at his office that morning. Heavy rains were reported in the region on the day he disappeared.

Why it mattersThe disappearance of engineer Ismaël Mugisha and the questioning of three SOSUMO officials raises concerns about potential mismanagement and threats within the company.

Burundi families sell land for stroke treatment

Stroke patients' relatives face financial ruin and disrupted lives due to costly care.

Stroke victims' families in Burundi are making significant sacrifices, including selling land, to cover medical expenses and caregiving responsibilities. Gisèle Kaneza, a student at the University of Burundi, has had her studies interrupted to care for her mother, who suffered a stroke. She is the eldest sibling and must manage household duties while her younger siblings attend secondary school.

Gérard Nibigira's wife is hospitalized after a second stroke, forcing him to sell their rural land to pay for her treatment and medication. The family has been at the hospital for nearly a month, highlighting the severe financial strain caused by the illness.

Stroke, also known as a cerebral attack, occurs when a brain blood vessel is blocked or ruptures, depriving brain tissue of oxygen. This medical emergency can lead to significant disabilities and destabilizes the economic and social well-being of entire families.

Why it mattersFamilies in Burundi are forced to sell assets and disrupt education to afford stroke treatment and care for affected relatives.

Burundi's sole referee selected for CAF matches

Pacifique Ndabihawenimana will represent Burundi as the only official at continental club football tournaments.

Pacifique Ndabihawenimana is Burundi's only referee chosen for the TotalEnergies CAF Champions League and Confederation Cup group stages. 35 referees from 12 African nations will officiate these matches, beginning November 18 in Cairo.

This selection marks a significant moment for Burundi's football officials. Unlike other nations, Burundi's referees will not use Video Assistant Referee (VAR) technology during this phase, focusing instead on traditional officiating.

Ndabihawenimana joins an elite group of 76 officials selected by the Confederation of African Football. His presence ensures Burundi remains represented in high-level continental competitions.
